## 3. Obstacles and revision

**Obstacle 1 (pure-function collapse of process-matrix residual).** The complete configuration at the crossing, any process-matrix generating circuit of polynomial size, the purifiable process description, the unitary channel evaluations, the local recovery predicates, the decoder states, and any verification predicate are pure functions of the original ordered input blocks \( L_\pi, R_\sigma \) and the fixed deterministic machine A. By PURE-FUNCTION-UNIVERSAL-COLLAPSE1 and prior post-crossing residual collapses (including Hall-slack and unshardable-code), the entire process-matrix residual is jointly recoverable by free local solves and deterministic whole-run replay within a single authentication-and-comparison pass. No information outside the original input and A is introduced; the process matrix re-encodes existing map information; it does not create new information outside pure functions. The quantum/process-matrix formalism is classicalized here as a free local function evaluation.

**Obstacle 2 (charged transport of residual symbols).** Realizing the local recovery or verification of residual components still requires the head (or message) to transport the residual map-difference information (or the process-matrix description / channel samples that encode it) across the cut. Because the maps are incompressible, the information length remains \( \Theta(r) \) (or \( \Theta(N) \) in the encoding) in the worst case; the number of crossings needed to deliver or verify the residual is asymptotically the same as the classical argument of CHARGED-UNSAT-TRANSPORT1. Free process-matrix evaluation improves local NP solving under indefinite causal order but does not eliminate the need to co-locate or transport the incompressible information. Free random access removes only local charge. Indefinite causal order in the process matrix does not reduce the asymptotic charged transport of the global residual map across the cut in the single-tape model.

**Obstacle 3 (static ceiling and matching upper bound).** Theorem 2 continues to bind: \( Dh \le N^2/8 \) and \( Dh^2 \le N^3/27 \). The matching upper bound (eq. 41) realizes the quadratic/cubic orders on the family by authenticating and comparing the maps in one pass. Process-matrix residual encodings do not force additional independent work beyond what is already paid; they re-encode the comparison differently.
