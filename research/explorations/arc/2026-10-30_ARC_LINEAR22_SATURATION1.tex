\begin{abstract}
We attempt the current ARC/FLMV construction using exact Davis--Putnam variable elimination,
proper-3CNF re-entry, shared AND/OR circuits, and bounded two-variable lookahead. An explicitly
constructible connected, linear, signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF family defeats every one-variable
pivot for both declared output languages: proper-3CNF re-entry adds three variables and four
clauses, while a common-factored circuit adds one DAG node. This failure is representation-
and algorithm-relative, not decision hardness. In fact, by reducing signed variable relabeling
to bounded-degree colored graph isomorphism, the entire signed-isomorphism orbit of this
family has polynomial-time witness recovery. Finite tests include all 1,089 two-pivot
candidates at three sizes and signed scrambling controls. The full all-input, same-machine
resource-contraction arrow remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Context, prior corpus, and exact target}
The current root \texttt{main.tex} gives the ARC/FLMV simultaneous time--space
separation target, with \texttt{main4.tex} and \texttt{main5.tex} retaining the ARC
and WRAPPER drivers. The earlier project established: (i) the fully charged
substituted-child recurrence; (ii) a local ten-variable example where every
single CNF elimination grows but a two-pivot sequence shrinks; (iii) parity
projection where auxiliary-free CNF is exponentially longer than a circuit;
(iv) checkpoint/replay and streaming-join alternatives that defeat apparent
memory lower bounds; and (v) the SHORT-QUERY-QUARANTINE3 conditional short-SAT-query
composition. We adopt these rather than rebrand them as new. The underlying
knowledge-compilation transformations are classical\cite{dechter,dm}; SAT
lower bounds from a particular language must not be transferred to all algorithms.

The target remains a uniform, answer-preserving replacement for \emph{every}
ordinary SAT input, with all reducer work, child writes, source access,
intermediate live state and repeated attempts charged in one time--space model.
This experiment probes whether a modest enlargement of the source-normalizing
portfolio, from CNF elimination to explicit shared-circuit projection, produces
a universally favorable first step. The answer is \emph{no for the declared
portfolio}, as demonstrated by an infinite family.

\section{An infinite connected signed exact-$(2,2)$ family}
Let $k\ge7$ be odd. Indices are elements of $\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z$.
Introduce $A_i,B_i,C_i$ for $i\in\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z$ and define
\[
F_k=\bigwedge_{s=0}^3\ \bigwedge_{i=0}^{k-1}
 \bigl(\ell_s(A_i)\lor\ell_s(B_{i+s})\lor\ell_s(C_{i+2s})\bigr),
\quad
\ell_s(v)=\begin{cases}v&s=0,1,\\ \neg v&s=2,3.\end{cases}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Syntax, incidence, and easy witness]
Every $F_k$ is a connected proper signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF with $n=3k$
variables and $m=4k$ distinct clauses. Every pair of variables occurs together
in at most one clause. The assignment $A_i=1$, $B_i=C_i=0$ satisfies $F_k$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each $s$, every variable in each of the three parts occurs in one
clause, so each participates once for each $s=0,1,2,3$, twice with each
polarity. All three names in a clause lie in different parts. For an
$A$--$B$ pair, the shift is uniquely $s=j-i$; for an $A$--$C$ pair,
$2s=j-i$ has at most one permitted $s$ since $k$ is odd and the four
shifts are distinct; for a $B$--$C$ pair, $s=j-i$ is unique. Thus no
variable pair recurs. The $s=0$ clauses connect $A_i$ to $B_i,C_i$;
$s=1$ connects $A_i$ to $B_{i+1}$, so all indices are connected.
For $s=0,1$ the literal $A_i$ is true, while for $s=2,3$ the negative
$B$ and $C$ literals are true. Hence the displayed global witness works.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact ordinary CNF elimination and re-entry}
Suppose $x$ occurs positively in $x\lor P_1,x\lor P_2$ and negatively
in $\neg x\lor N_1,\neg x\lor N_2$; let $H$ contain all other clauses.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dp}
\exists x\,F
\equiv H\land\bigwedge_{i,j\in\{1,2\}}(P_i\lor N_j)
\equiv H\land\left[(P_1\land P_2)\lor(N_1\land N_2)\right].
\end{equation}
The identity is pointwise on every surviving assignment, not just a SAT-bit
equivalence. It follows from the two choices $x=0,1$ and distributivity.

\begin{lemma}[One-pivot width-four firewall]
For every variable $x$ of $F_k$, the four resolvents in \eqref{eq:dp}
are distinct, non-tautological, width-four clauses. No unaffected source
3-clause subsumes one of them. In the elementary proper-3CNF re-entry in
which each width-four clause is replaced with its two-clause Tseitin
expansion using one fresh auxiliary, the result has
\[
\boxed{n'=n+3,\qquad m'=m+4.}
\]
Under the declared fixed-width and Elias-gamma indexed clause-list
encodings, the resulting child is longer. No claim is made for arbitrary
semantic compression formats.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The four clauses containing $x$ are pairwise disjoint outside $x$ by
linearity of the clause hypergraph; hence the four two-literal remnants
use eight distinct variable names. Each $P_i\lor N_j$ has four distinct
literals, no complementary pair, and distinct variable support for distinct
$(i,j)$. An unaffected clause shares at most one variable with each
parent of such a resolvent; it cannot have three variables inside the
four-variable union, so it cannot subsume the resolvent.
For a width-four disjunction $a\lor b\lor c\lor d$, introduce fresh $z$:
\[
\exists z\,[(a\lor b\lor z)\land(\neg z\lor c\lor d)]
\equiv a\lor b\lor c\lor d.
\]
There are four independent resolvents and hence four new auxiliaries,
eight new clauses, and four deleted clauses, while $x$ disappears.
Thus the variable count changes by $-1+4=+3$ and the clause count by
$-4+8=+4$. More clauses and nondecreasing variable-ID width force a
strict increase in the specified full-width clause-list encoding.
\end{proof}

\section{Shared-circuit representation and its exact cost}
Use one canonical node for each distinct signed literal, one $m$-input
AND node, and one 3-input OR node per clause. The native source DAG has
$2n$ literal nodes, $m+1$ internal gates and $4m$ edges.
The compiler factors $H$ before eliminating $x$, using the rightmost
form of \eqref{eq:dp}. It hash-conses identical nodes and simplifies only
Boolean constants, idempotence, literal complements, and associative
flattening. It does not call a semantic minimizer.

\begin{theorem}[Exact factored first-step DAG growth]
On each $F_k$, for every pivot $x$, this compiler creates exactly
three additional internal gates, removes two signed-literal nodes, and
removes one edge. Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{V'=V+1,\qquad E'=E-1.}
\]
In the project's declared common-index-width test format with an eight-bit
node tag, the source/child circuit payload difference is exactly eight bits.
The result makes no assertion about a minimum equivalent circuit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The old four touched clauses give four 3-input OR gates. The new
expression has four 2-input OR gates for the $P_i,N_j$, two 2-input AND
gates for $P_1\land P_2$ and $N_1\land N_2$, and one 2-input OR gate
joining them. There are no repeated literal pairs or local identities
that trigger the stated simplifier. The untouched clauses are preserved.
The old $x,\neg x$ literal nodes disappear, while every other signed
literal still occurs among untouched clauses or local remnants because
its variable had four source occurrences and shared no pair of $x$-clauses.
Thus gates change by $+3$, literal nodes by $-2$, and total nodes by $+1$.
Four old clause edges into the root are replaced by one, a change of $-3$;
twelve old local clause input edges become $8+4+2=14$, a change of $+2$.
Hence total edge count decreases by one. For a common reference width
$w$, total encoded bits are
\(H+(8+w)V+wE\), so $\Delta=8$; here $H$ is an equally sized,
explicit parent-scale header. The actual test encoder chooses a local
width and returns the same width for these odd-$k$ source/child pairs.
\end{proof}

\section{Polynomial-time signed-isomorphism escape}
Associate to each proper signed exact-$(2,2)$ formula a vertex-colored
undirected graph with one center vertex for each variable, two undistinguished
literal-port vertices for that variable, and one vertex for each clause.
Join the center to its two ports, and join a clause vertex to the appropriate
positive/negative port for each literal. Colors distinguish centers,
ports and clauses, but do not distinguish the two ports of a variable.
Every center has degree two, every port degree three, and every clause
degree three. There are $n$ centers, $2n$ ports, and $m$ clause vertices.

\begin{theorem}[Scrambled-template recognition; known theorem transfer]
The class of all signed-variable permutations, independent polarity
complementations, clause permutations, and literal reorderings of the
family $\{F_k:k\ge7\text{ odd}\}$ has a deterministic polynomial-time
recognition and satisfying-assignment algorithm.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For an input with $n$ variables and $m$ clauses, first validate the
proper signed exact-$(2,2)$ syntax and dimension conditions. Construct
the colored degree-three incidence graph of the input and the corresponding
template $F_{n/3}$. The two graphs are color-preserving isomorphic exactly
when the formulas differ by those allowed transformations: variable centers
and their two attached ports determine a variable renaming and possible
polarity flip, while clause vertices determine clause permutation.
Luks's polynomial-time bounded-valence graph-isomorphism theorem applies
to these colored constant-degree graphs\cite{luks}. A concrete isomorphism
can be recovered by the standard polynomial search-to-decision procedure.
Transport the known template witness via that isomorphism, complementing
each mapped variable when its ports are swapped, and check it against the
input. The result is a correct polynomial-time witness algorithm on the
promised signed-isomorphism class. Outside the class, the recognizer returns
``not recognized,'' not an UNSAT assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Canonical promise contraction; exact restricted]\label{cor:canonical}
For the canonically indexed clause stream $F_k$ in $(s,i)$ order, a fixed
deterministic one-pass recognizer checks the entire source with
$O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)$ bit work and $O(\log N)$ writable bits.
On $F_k$ for $k>7$, it may output the fixed satisfiable $F_7$ as one
strict-$(2,2)$ recursively terminal child; $F_7$ is a directly checked
base case. On other inputs it returns \emph{unresolved}, not a SAT answer.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Read and validate the indexed header, then maintain $(s,i,k)$ and
recompute the three expected literal IDs and signs for each next source
clause. Each index occupies $O(\log N)$ bits; modulo-$k$ addition, comparison,
and index update use polynomially many such bit operations per clause.
No clause table or SAT oracle is required. A complete match certifies
membership in this all-satisfiable family, so producing the already known
$F_7$ preserves the decision bit. The accepted promise is recursively
closed with one terminal step. The argument does not handle an arbitrary
CNF and does not give a favorable point for an NP-complete class.
\end{proof}

Six finite scrambled inputs were independently checked with a colored
GraphMatcher and witness transport. This \emph{finite} checker uses
backtracking VF2; its run times do not establish Luks's complexity bound.
The theorem's polynomial claim follows from the known theorem and the
explicit graph reduction, not from the measurements.

\section{Implication for the ARC/FLMV proof spine}
The candidate policy ``choose the smallest output among every single
DP/re-entry pivot and every factored-circuit pivot'' fails on the infinite
family above: every candidate increases the physical representation cost.
Finite two-pivot search fails for three sizes. Yet the family admits an
unconditional global source-recognition terminal. Thus the relevant
proof target cannot be an algorithm-independent cost derived solely from
local failure, incidence expansion, or an exhaustive pivot menu.

A concrete next construction would combine (i) certified recognizers and
native Horn/2SAT/affine closures; (ii) source-authenticated exact projection
on surviving interfaces; (iii) size-controlled circuit/CNF switching with
both physical bytes and discovery charged; and (iv) a single unresolved
ordinary-SAT child. The key unanswered assertion is that this portfolio
provides a power-smaller or time--space-contracting successor \emph{on every
input} before an expensive parent-scale SAT call. Under $P=NP$, the older
SHORT-QUERY-QUARANTINE3 lemma permits a hypothetical polynomial SAT solver
to resolve polylogarithmic-length helper questions at an $N^{o(1)}$ cost
per call, but no current theorem obtains all-input recursive closure from
those helpers.

The present report neither implements that missing universal compiler nor
identifies a nonrelativizing/nonalgebrizing hinge. A recursion with a
same-size fallback, or an uncharged semantic selector, is not the
sought simultaneous ARC point. The forbidden SAT time--space region
remains a valid independent barrier\cite{flmv} and does not itself
supply the needed transformation.
