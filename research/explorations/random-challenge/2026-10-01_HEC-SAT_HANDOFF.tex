\section*{HEC-SAT handoff to LSPV team}
\textbf{Status:} restricted-model theorem package proved; static-SAT transfer open.

The project now has an exact online/no-reread state/result separation theorem. For $K$ independent payload bits and an $r$-bit free semantic side channel,
\[
\boxed{s\ge K-r}
\]
for the worst semantic fiber whenever any payload coordinate may be challenged later and the old payload cannot be reread.

Consequences include the free-SAT-answer bound $s\ge K-1$, explicit witness-side-information bound $s\ge K-w$, and multi-epoch direct accumulation
\[
s\ge \sum_e K_e-r.
\]

Adaptive masking
\[
Y_t=R_t\oplus b_t
\]
with fresh independent uniform $R_t$ preserves perfect randomness conditioned on the past, so heterogeneous gate problems may control timing/routing without manufacturing entropy.

The model is compatible with quantum random-access-code lower bounds, but none of these facts yet implies a lower bound for ordinary static SAT.

The single load-bearing handoff is:
\[
\boxed{
\text{static SAT decider}
\Longrightarrow
\text{HEC/chronogram solver with controlled state, rereads, and post-challenge work}.
}
\]

The team should treat this as a candidate universal-normalization theorem and attack it with static full-input visibility, rereading, recomputation, preprocessing, pointer jumping, compression, wrappers, semantic/algebraic oracles, and easy tractable controls.

Do not promote the HEC lower bound itself into the main P-versus-NP proof spine unless this compiler is proved.
