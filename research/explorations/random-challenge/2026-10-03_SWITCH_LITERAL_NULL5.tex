\section*{SWITCH-LITERAL-NULL5: complement contacts, null chains, and the failure of raw cut width}

\textbf{STATUS: PROVED/EXACT finite certificate facts plus COMPUTATIONAL exhaustive verification. P versus NP remains OPEN.}

\paragraph{Literature model.}
Jukna's switching-and-rectifier model permits directed acyclic networks whose controlled
edges are labelled by variables or their negations.  This is the natural full-literal version
of the contact DAG.  The literature distinguishes syntactic read restrictions from semantic
ones precisely because inconsistent paths, or \emph{null chains}, can merge otherwise
different subcomputations.  Exact Perfect Matching is a canonical example: Jukna and later
Cook--Edmonds--Medabalimi--Pitassi use it to demonstrate large separations between syntactic
and semantic read-once nondeterministic branching programs.

\paragraph{Wrapper failure of physical cut width.}
Let $N$ be any correct literal-labelled switch DAG.  Add a new source with two parallel
contacts $x_i$ and $\neg x_i$, both feeding the old source.  On every input exactly one
contact is live, so the computed function is unchanged.  Nevertheless the new source cut has
only two controlled edges.

Hence raw physical cut size and the associated raw Menger path count are not
wrapper-invariant in the full literal model.  The monotone cut argument of
SWITCH-DAG-NROBP4 cannot simply be transferred after complement contacts are admitted.

\paragraph{Exact D(4,8) certificate census.}
Identify the 32 clause bits with the 32 edges of $Q_4$.  UNSAT promised inputs are exactly
the 272 perfect matchings.  Exhaustive enumeration of inclusion-minimal safe literal
conjunctions of length at most four gives:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{literal type}&\text{number}\cr
\hline
1\text{ literal}&0\cr
2^+,0^-&96\cr
3\text{ literals}&0\cr
0^+,4^-&16\cr
1^+,3^-&192\cr
2^+,2^-&768\cr
3^+,1^-&1120\cr
4^+,0^-&480.
\end{array}
\]
The 96 two-literal certificates are exactly the positive conflict pairs.  The 16 purely
negative four-literal certificates are exactly the four incident edges at each cube vertex.

Thus negation creates no shorter local certificate than the existing positive two-contact
witness.  Any improvement below the current 48-contact construction must come from DAG
sharing/null-chain structure rather than certificate shortening.

\paragraph{A second exact 48-contact construction.}
On an eight-edge subgraph of $Q_4$, total degree is 16.  If no cube vertex is isolated, all
16 vertices have degree at least one and therefore exactly one, so the edges form a perfect
matching.  Consequently
\[
\mathrm{SAT}\iff\text{some cube vertex is isolated}.
\]
This yields a complement-only read-once contact DAG.

Order the four cube dimensions.  Share the dimension-0 negative contact between the two
endpoint-star paths, duplicate the dimension-1 and dimension-2 contacts per cube vertex, and
share the dimension-3 contact between the two endpoint-star paths.  The controlled-contact
count is
\[
8+16+16+8=48.
\]
The DAG has exactly 16 accepting paths, one for each four-negative vertex star.

Exhaustive verification over all
\[
\binom{32}{8}=10{,}518{,}300
\]
promised inputs accepts all 10,518,028 SAT instances and rejects exactly the 272 perfect
matchings.

\paragraph{Optimality inside the negative-star architecture.}
There are 64 path/contact incidences.  A contact can be shared only between the two endpoint
stars of its cube edge.  If a shared occurrence is at positions $p,q$ on the two length-four
paths, path splicing gives controlled lengths $p+4-q$ and $q+4-p$.  Since no safe
all-negative certificate has size below four, $p=q$.  If this common position is internal
(position 2 or 3), the spliced four-negative set is not a vertex star; the exact certificate
census shows that it is unsafe.  Therefore sharing is possible only at the first or last
position.  Each star path can share at most two of its four incidences, saving at most 16
contacts overall.  Hence
\[
\boxed{C_{\rm negative\mbox{-}star}(D(4,8))=48}.
\]

\paragraph{Current status.}
The literal model therefore gives
\[
C_{\rm literal\ DAG}(D(4,8))\le48,
\]
but this run does not prove the unrestricted literal-DAG lower bound 48.  The monotone
$2M-16$ lower bound is broken as a proof method by the $x_i/\neg x_i$ wrapper even though
the best tested literal construction still has 48 contacts.

A further important correction is that output inversion is not free in this model:
nondeterministic read-once branching-program classes can behave very differently under
complement, with Exact Perfect Matching providing the classical example.

\paragraph{Next target.}
Replace physical cut width by a semantic resource that ignores tautological/null-chain
wrappers.  The most direct candidates are consistent-path cut capacity and continuation
incompatibility across a cut.  These should be tested against the exact $D(4,8)$ certificate
census before any asymptotic claim is attempted.
