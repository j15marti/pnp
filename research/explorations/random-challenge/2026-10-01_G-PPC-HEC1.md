# G-PPC-HEC1 — Exogenous True-Random Source for Pointer-Pebbling Chronogram HEC

**Timestamp:** 2026-10-01 09:49 PT  
**Status:** CANDIDATE architecture with several PROVED/EXACT model facts. P versus NP remains OPEN.

## 1. Motivation

An algorithmic pseudorandom generator is a poor load-bearing source for PPC-HEC because its entire output is a deterministic function of a short seed and may admit compact prediction/reconstruction. Replace it by an exogenous ideal random source G.

G is not an algorithm and does not have a seed.

At discrete clock tick t,

R_t <- Bernoulli(1/2),

independently of all previous bits, the SAT instance, deterministic machine state, and nondeterministic choices.

A fixed rate lambda > 0 means that by computational time T only

R_1,...,R_floor(lambda T)

have been physically/causally released.

Future bits do not belong to the observable machine state and cannot be queried early.

This is a mathematical idealization of a true-random source, not a claim about a physical device.

## 2. Causal filtration

Let F_t denote the information available by time t:

F_t = sigma(x, R_1,...,R_floor(lambda t), machine history through t).

Every deterministic state transition, pointer update, challenge, or witness action at time t must be F_t-measurable.

No operation may depend on R_j with j > floor(lambda t).

This rule is the essential content of G.

## 3. Exact constant-rate entropy budget

By time T, G has released exactly floor(lambda T) independent random bits. Therefore

H(G_{<=T}) = floor(lambda T).

Thus a T-step computation cannot have received more than O(T) independent random bits from G.

This repairs the earlier accounting problem in which an exponentially large random payload could be injected for free.

It also imposes a strict guard:

**G cannot by itself yield a superlinear information lower bound in elapsed time.**

If the lower bound is obtained solely by counting random bits released by time T, it is at most lambda T.

## 4. Nondeterministic clairvoyance trap

True randomness does not automatically defeat standard nondeterministic semantics.

For one future bit R:

Pr_R[ exists b : b = R ] = 1.

But if b must be committed before R is released,

max_b Pr_R[b = R] = 1/2.

For m future bits,

Pr_R[ exists b in {0,1}^m : b = R ] = 1,

whereas a precommitted b succeeds with probability exactly

2^{-m}.

Therefore an existential branch selected after the full random transcript is known is effectively clairvoyant.

This is a quantifier-order issue, not a weakness of true randomness.

## 5. Causal witness commitment

To preserve the intended separation between NP witness and deterministic state, G-PPC-HEC adopts the following rule.

A witness/certificate W used to represent the NP/NDTM semantic channel must be committed before the G-bits that select its later challenges are released.

For a single commitment phase the success condition has MA-like order:

exists W  Pr_R[ Verify(x,W,R)=1 ] >= p.

It must not be replaced by

Pr_R[ exists W Verify(x,W,R)=1 ],

because the latter allows the witness to depend on the complete random transcript.

If witnesses are allowed to change after each newly revealed public random block, the model becomes an interactive public-coin protocol rather than ordinary NP. That may be useful as an auxiliary model, but it must not be silently identified with NP.

## 6. How G feeds PPC-HEC

G should not continually change the semantic SAT instance. Instead it should select future challenge/address structure over a fixed static semantic object.

At epoch e:

1. A fixed SAT-derived computation/witness object is already defined.
2. PPC-HEC has a dependency DAG D_e.
3. Newly released G-bits select child edges, challenge indices, or salts used to determine later pointer addresses.
4. The current address is updated causally:
   a_{t+1} = F_t(a_t, R_t, local state).
5. A future challenge asks for information attached to the reached node or to a derived proof/computation location.

This keeps SAT witness existence conceptually separate from G.

G chooses what is audited or what becomes relevant; it does not create truth of the SAT instance.

## 7. Why this helps compared with an algorithmic generator

### PROVED/EXACT in the model

- There is no short deterministic seed encoding future G-bits.
- Future G-bits cannot be computed from past G-bits.
- A precommitted witness cannot predict m fresh future bits with probability greater than 2^{-m} if exact matching is required.
- The number of random bits injected by time T is automatically charged as O(T).
- Repeated simple shift histories are still compressible, so G does not rescue the retired cyclic-shift mechanism by itself.

### What G does not solve

- An NDTM evaluated ex post on the entire random transcript can still choose a matching branch unless causal commitment is enforced.
- G does not turn a restricted online lower bound into a static SAT lower bound.
- G does not cross relativization or algebrization.
- G does not create more than O(T) independent information in T time.
- If G-bits are part of the final static input, the chronology disappears and ordinary NP may choose its witness after seeing them.

## 8. Pointer/pebbling use

The best use of G is to prevent preprocessing of the **future path**, while a pebbling-hard dependency DAG forces STORE-or-RECOMPUTE behavior.

Desired resource inequality:

S_data
+ S_pointer
+ w Q_reread
+ C_recompute
+ C_sequential
>= L(D,T).

G should select among future edges only after the machine has crossed the relevant state cut.

The dependency graph, not randomness alone, must supply the expensive store/recompute structure.

## 9. Randomness can be eliminated from a final lower-bound proof if used probabilistically

For a fixed deterministic algorithm A, suppose one proves

Pr_R[ Cost(A,x,R) >= L ] > 0.

Then there exists a finite random transcript r such that

Cost(A,x,r) >= L.

Therefore G can serve as a probabilistic-method device during the lower-bound argument and the final hard schedule can be fixed afterward.

However, this only helps if the cost theorem continues to hold after r is fixed and staticized. If fixing r reveals an easy preprocessing shortcut, the random online theorem has not transferred to ordinary SAT.

This gives a concrete derandomization/fix-transcript target rather than requiring a physical random generator in the final theorem.

## 10. Relation to interactive-proof literature

The distinction between witness commitment before randomness and witness adaptation after public randomness is the same fundamental order-of-information issue formalized in Merlin-Arthur / Arthur-Merlin and private/public-coin interactive proof systems.

Goldwasser-Sipser show equivalence in language-recognition power between private-coin and public-coin interactive proofs with appropriate protocol transformation. That does **not** mean the chronology is irrelevant for this resource lower-bound construction; resource preservation must be proved separately.

## 11. Relation to delay-function literature

Larsen-Simkin study F^T when an adversary has bounded advice, oracle access, parallelism, and seeks to use fewer sequential queries. Their lower bounds and attacks make explicit the tradeoff between preprocessing/advice and sequential evaluation.

This is structurally close to G-PPC-HEC, but remains an oracle/delay-function theorem, not an unrestricted SAT lower bound.

## 12. Exact toy test

Let G release m bits after a commitment point.

- Ex-post existential branch:
  success probability = 1.
- One precommitted m-bit branch:
  success probability = 2^{-m}.
- At unit G rate, generating those m bits requires m ticks.

Values:

| m | precommit exact-match probability | ex-post existential |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1/2 | 1 |
| 2 | 1/4 | 1 |
| 4 | 1/16 | 1 |
| 8 | 1/256 | 1 |
| 16 | 1/65536 | 1 |
| 32 | 2^-32 | 1 |
| 64 | 2^-64 | 1 |

This is an exact quantifier calculation, not an empirical claim.

## 13. New theorem targets

### Target G1 — Causal Result/State Separation

Strengthen HEC's state/result theorem with a random filtration and a precommitted witness channel.

### Target G2 — G-PPC Store-or-Recompute

For a pebbling-hard DAG whose future edge choices are revealed by G only causally, prove a model-matched time-space/recovery tradeoff.

### Target G3 — Fix-the-Transcript Lemma

Show that a positive-probability hard G transcript can be fixed without opening a preprocessing or random-access shortcut that destroys the lower bound.
