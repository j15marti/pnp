\section*{LINEAR-DENSITY-3SAT1: letting the clause count grow with the variable count}

\textbf{STATUS: COMPUTATIONAL. P versus NP remains OPEN.}

To leave the fixed-eight-clause regime, random simple genuine 3-CNFs were generated with
\[
m=\lfloor \alpha n\rceil
\]
for densities \(\alpha\in\{1,4/3,2,3,4,4.25,4.5,5\}\). Formulas contain no repeated
clauses. An exact DPLL implementation with unit propagation and max-occurrence branching
was run with deterministic seed 20261003. There were 100 samples per point through
\(n=64\) and 50 samples per point for \(n=80,96,112,128\).

At \(\alpha=3\), every sampled formula through \(n=128\) was satisfiable and median
search nodes grew only from 12 at \(n=36\) to 34 at \(n=128\). The difficult band moved
to densities near four to five clauses per variable. At \(n=128\), SAT fractions were
\[
1.00, 0.98, 0.52, 0.12, 0.00
\]
for \(\alpha=3,4,4.25,4.5,5\), respectively.

Median DPLL nodes at \(\alpha=4.25\) were
\[
35,73,132,389,1040,2851,5699
\]
for \(n=36,48,64,80,96,112,128\), while at \(\alpha=4.5\) they were
\[
36,77,194,504,1209,2982,5590.
\]
On the finite window \(36\le n\le128\), least-squares fits of \(\log_2\) median nodes
against \(n\) give bases approximately 1.05794 and 1.05733 with \(R^2\) values
0.9967 and 0.9976. Simple power-law fits are worse on the same data window. This is only
algorithm-specific finite evidence; it is not an asymptotic lower bound and cannot exclude
a different polynomial-time algorithm.

The experiment therefore exposes two distinct notions of growth. The previously certified
all-pairs witness theorem gives
\[
B(n,m)\ge 8\binom n3-1
\]
for the global clause-query DAG model across the entire nonconstant fixed-\(n\) density
range. For any fixed \(\alpha>0\) with \(m=\alpha n\), this remains only
\(\Theta(n^3)\). In contrast, one concrete DPLL solver shows a rapidly growing hard band
near the random 3-SAT transition. The latter cannot be promoted from solver behavior to
arbitrary-algorithm hardness.

\paragraph{Hostile interpretation control.}
The easy-hard-easy curve is compatible with the classical random-3-SAT phase-transition
literature, but random-average behavior is not worst-case complexity, DPLL is not an
arbitrary algorithm, and a finite exponential-looking fit does not prove exponential
asymptotics.

\paragraph{Next target.}
Repeat the density panel with a structurally different exact solver or representation
and compare whether the hard-band exponent survives. A representation-invariant lower bound
would require an argument that survives such solver changes.
