\section*{RESWIDTH-RANDOM3SAT1: exact bounded-width resolution on linear-density random 3-SAT}

\textbf{STATUS: COMPUTATIONAL finite experiment plus KNOWN THEOREM proof-complexity context. P versus NP remains OPEN.}

\paragraph{Generator correction.}
LINEAR-DENSITY-3SAT1 used a base-64 integer key to reject duplicate clauses. This is
injective only through \(n=64\). Above 64 it can collide on distinct clauses, so the
previous \(n>64\) samples were valid 3-CNFs but slightly biased relative to the intended
uniform simple-CNF distribution. The generator has been corrected to base \(n\), and all
reported \(n>64\) scaling points were rerun. The easy-hard-easy pattern survives. On
\(36\le n\le128\), corrected finite-window median DPLL fits are approximately
\[
1.05650^n\quad(\alpha=4.25),\qquad
1.05875^n\quad(\alpha=4.5),\qquad
1.04864^n\quad(\alpha=5).
\]
These remain algorithm-specific finite fits, not asymptotic lower bounds.

\paragraph{Literature target.}
Ben-Sasson and Wigderson make resolution width a certified proof resource by relating
width to proof size. Classical random-CNF work of Chvatal--Szemeredi and successors gives
exponential resolution lower bounds in fixed-density unsatisfiable regimes. Carenini
(ECCC TR26-158, 2026) strengthens the current random-3-CNF benchmark to exponential
lower bounds for \(k\)-DNF resolution for \(k=O(\log n)\) at every constant density
above the elementary first-moment UNSAT bound. For random 3-SAT that elementary density is
\[
\alpha_{\rm FM}=\frac{\ln2}{\ln(8/7)}\approx5.190893.
\]

\paragraph{Exact finite test.}
For each sampled formula, bounded-width resolution saturation exhaustively adds every
non-tautological resolvent of width at most \(W\). If saturation reaches a fixed point
without the empty clause, then no width-\(W\) resolution refutation exists.

At \(n=64,\alpha=5.25\), 12 of 40 UNSAT samples fail complete width-3 saturation.
All twelve derive the empty clause at width 4, certifying exact minimum resolution width
four for those formulas. At \(n=64\), all 40 samples at densities 6 and 8 still refute
at width three.

The transition moves upward with \(n\). For five samples per cell, the fractions admitting
width-3 refutations were
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
n & \alpha=5.25 & \alpha=6 & \alpha=8\\
\hline
80&1/5&5/5&5/5\\
96&0/5&1/5&5/5\\
112&0/5&0/5&2/5\\
128&0/5&0/5&0/5.
\end{array}
\]
Representative exact minimum-width-four certificates were obtained at
\((n,\alpha)=(80,5.25),(96,6),(112,8)\). At \((128,8)\), width three is ruled out
exactly, but width-four saturation exceeded a five-million-clause cap, so no stronger
minimum-width claim is made.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Unlike DPLL node count or min-fill width, failure of complete width-3 saturation is a
proof-system certificate. The observed migration of the width-3 failure boundary toward
higher constant densities as \(n\) grows is qualitatively aligned with known asymptotic
random-CNF resolution lower bounds. However, resolution and \(\mathrm{Res}(k)\) remain
restricted proof systems. No theorem here transfers this exponential hardness to arbitrary
SAT algorithms, unrestricted Boolean circuits, or P versus NP.

\paragraph{Next target.}
Increase the exact-width panel using a more efficient width-four engine or a resolution-width
game implementation, and compare the resulting width growth with expansion of the
clause-variable incidence graph. A useful bridge would be a model-matched theorem that
identifies an invariant shared by resolution, elimination, and a stronger non-resolution
representation.
