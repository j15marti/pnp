\section*{SOLUTION-GEOMETRY1: discrete distortion of SAT solution spaces}

\textbf{STATUS: COMPUTATIONAL finite experiment plus KNOWN-THEOREM literature mapping. P versus NP remains OPEN.}

\paragraph{Literature.}
The single-flip solution graph is an established object, not merely an analogy. Gopalan,
Kolaitis, Maneva, and Papadimitriou prove structural and computational dichotomies for
Boolean solution graphs: on the hard side, connectivity can be PSPACE-complete and component
diameter exponential; on the tractable side, component diameter is linear. Random-CSP work
of Achlioptas, Coja-Oghlan, and Ricci-Tersenghi establishes clustering and freezing phenomena.
Gamarnik's overlap-gap framework converts geometric separation into rigorous lower bounds
against stable/local algorithm classes, and Bresler--Huang use a many-way OGP to obtain
lower bounds for low-degree polynomial algorithms on random $k$-SAT. These are restricted
algorithmic barriers, not unrestricted SAT lower bounds.

A 2026 preprint by Zhan proposes a discrete Boolean-hypercube geometric account of random
3-SAT clustering, freezing, and easy--hard--easy behavior. It is directly relevant as a
contemporary analogy, but it does not supply the arbitrary-algorithm bridge required here.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
For satisfying assignments $x,y$ in the same single-flip component, define
\[
\delta_F(x,y)=\frac{d_F(x,y)}{d_H(x,y)},
\]
where $d_F$ is shortest satisfying-flip distance and $d_H$ is Hamming distance. The experiment
also records component count, largest-component mass, local flip flexibility, frozen
coordinates, disconnected-pair fraction, intercomponent separation, and missing Hamming
distance bands.

\paragraph{Finite experiment.}
All $2^n$ assignments were enumerated exactly for $n=20,22,24$. Random simple genuine
3-SAT was sampled at densities $2.5,3,3.5,4,4.25,4.5$ using deterministic seed base
20261003. Repetition counts were 20, 10, and 5 respectively. The hostile controls are
planted 2-SAT, satisfiable Horn 3-CNF, a planted constant-treewidth local chain, and a planted
3-XOR system converted exactly to 3-CNF.

At $n=24$, random 3-SAT changes from a large weakly fragmented solution region at density
2.5 to much smaller, more frozen components by density 3.5. Median disconnected-pair
fractions are approximately
\[
0.071,\ 0.568,\ 0.708
\]
at densities $2.5,3,3.5$, while median frozen coordinates in the largest component are
\[
1,\ 6,\ 16.
\]
Local flexibility falls from about $0.307$ to $0.145$ over the same range.

By contrast, the intrinsic/Hamming stretch does not monotonically increase. Median maximum
sampled stretch is about $4.33$ at density $2.5$, $2.6$ at density $3$, and $1$ by density
$3.5$, because the surviving clusters become small and often geodesically convex. The
simple finite pair-distance-gap diagnostic is zero on the tested random-3SAT medians and
should not be confused with asymptotic or many-way OGP.

\paragraph{Hostile controls.}
The geometry is not a universal hardness invariant. At $n=24$, the planted bounded-treewidth
chain has median disconnected-pair fraction $0.826$ and 18 frozen variables in the largest
component. The planted affine/XOR control has 16 solutions in four components, 22 frozen
variables in the largest component, disconnected-pair fraction $0.8$, minimum intercomponent
Hamming distance 14, and an 11-step missing distance band. Yet these families admit
polynomial-time dynamic programming and Gaussian elimination respectively.

Conversely, planted 2-SAT can be extremely sparse while remaining a single connected and
easy solution component. Thus neither sparsity, fragmentation, freezing, nor raw overlap
gaps can serve as an unrestricted SAT hardness measure.

\paragraph{Correlation control.}
Across satisfiable random-3SAT instances pooled over the tested sizes, instance-wise Spearman
correlations between $\log_2(\mathrm{DPLL\ nodes}+1)$ and the geometry statistics are weak:
solution count $-0.151$, component count $-0.132$, frozen count $+0.118$, local flexibility
$-0.120$, disconnected-pair fraction $-0.064$, maximum stretch $-0.153$, and minimum
intercomponent distance $+0.009$. This is only a finite control against one DPLL
implementation, not an algorithm-independent statement.

\paragraph{Surviving candidate: atlas-normalized distortion.}
The useful repair is to quotient efficient structure before measuring geometry. Let
$\mathcal A$ be a declared tractable atlas containing, for example, affine elimination,
Horn/2-SAT closure, bounded-treewidth separator decompositions, and certified wrapper
quotients. A candidate resource is schematically
\[
\mathrm{Dist}^{\star}(F)
=
\min_{R\in\mathcal A(F)}
\mathrm{ResidualGeometry}(F/R),
\]
with acquisition of $R$ fully charged. The quotient should remove geometry already explained
by a polynomial-time coordinate system, while preserving unexplained continuation
incompatibility.

This is only a CANDIDATE architecture. No theorem currently implies that large
atlas-normalized distortion forces large arbitrary SAT runtime.

\paragraph{Next experiment.}
Normalize the XOR control explicitly into Gaussian coordinates and the chain control into
separator states, then recompute component/continuation geometry in the quotient. The
load-bearing question is whether random 3-SAT retains a growing primitive geometric
obstruction after the easy controls have collapsed.
