\section*{D7M8-REDUCE1: reducible-subcube circuit experiment}

\textbf{STATUS: COMPUTATIONAL. P versus NP remains OPEN.}

The exact eight-clause UNSAT family on seven ambient variables contains 132,685 exact codimension-three subcube partitions. An independent recursive-splitting enumerator recognizes 114,485 of them.

The support decomposition of the exact family is 35, 9,380, 63,630, 54,600, 5,040 for supports three through seven. The recursive recognizer captures 35, 7,980, 53,550, 47,880, 5,040 respectively. Hence its residual consists of exactly 18,200 support-four through support-six formulas and it misses no full-support seven-variable UNSAT formula.

For every codimension-two subcube, the circuit forms the OR of the five ways to split it into two codimension-three children. This costs five binary AND gates and four binary OR gates. There are 84 such subcubes, costing 756 gates. For the fourteen codimension-one subcubes, the analogous six-way split costs eleven gates each, or 154 total. The root has seven coordinate splits and costs thirteen gates. Thus the recursive partition recognizer has size 923.

Under the exact-weight-eight promise this recognizer is sound: a true root signal explicitly supplies eight selected, pairwise disjoint codimension-three leaves that partition the cube. Exhaustive enumeration gives completeness on the full-support subclass. Consequently the declared clause-presence encoding has the constructive bound G_support7(7,8) <= 923.

This does not replace the full-family bound. The circuit misses 18,200 lower-support UNSAT inputs, so the previously verified full-family upper bound of 2,285 gates remains current.

Filmus et al. prove that an irreducible homogeneous codimension-three subcube partition exists only in dimension four and is unique up to permutation and coordinate flips. The computational decomposition above is a separate finite enumeration, not a theorem transfer establishing the 923 count analytically for arbitrary parameters.

\paragraph{Hostile control.} The attempted strengthening that all D(7,8) UNSAT formulas are recursively splittable is falsified by 18,200 explicit residual inputs. The valid statement is restricted to the support-seven subclass in this experiment.
