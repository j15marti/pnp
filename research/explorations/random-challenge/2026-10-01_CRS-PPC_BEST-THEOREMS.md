Timestamp: 2026-10-01 10:34 PT

This document separates the exact results currently proved in the CRS-PPC / HEC program from the bridge theorems that would be needed for an unrestricted static-SAT lower bound.

## Definitions

### Definition 1 — Exogenous causal source

Let

$$
G=(R_1,R_2,\ldots),\qquad
R_t\stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2).
$$

At source rate $\lambda>0$, only

$$
R_1,\ldots,R_{\lfloor \lambda t\rfloor}
$$

have been released by global time $t$. Future bits are unavailable and cannot be queried early.

Let $\mathcal F_t$ be the sigma-field generated by the base input, all released source bits, and the controller/machine history through time $t$.

A random time $\tau$ is a stopping time when $\{\tau\le t\}\in\mathcal F_t$ for all $t$.

### Definition 2 — CRS epoch

At epoch start time $\tau_i$, capture the next $b_i$ fresh source bits

$$
B_i=(R_{\tau_i+1},\ldots,R_{\tau_i+b_i}).
$$

A deterministic controller constructs

$$
\Phi_i=C_i(x,\mathcal T_{<i},B_i).
$$

The solver returns the exact SAT decision $a_i$ and, when applicable, witness $w_i$. If the charged solve time is $c_i$, define

$$
\tau_{i+1}=\tau_i+b_i+c_i.
$$

The transcript is

$$
\mathcal T_A(x,G)=
((\tau_i,B_i,\Phi_i,a_i,w_i,c_i))_{i=1}^z.
$$

The controller should use $B_i$ for a partial restriction, audit location, pointer edge, or residual formula. A full assignment of every variable is treated separately below.

# Part I — PROVED/EXACT theorems

## Theorem 1 — Fresh Snapshot Lemma

Let $\tau$ be an almost surely finite stopping time for the filtration of the IID Bernoulli source. For every integer $b\ge1$,

$$
(R_{\tau+1},\ldots,R_{\tau+b})\mid\mathcal F_\tau
\sim U(\{0,1\}^b).
$$

Equivalently, for every $y\in\{0,1\}^b$,

$$
\Pr[(R_{\tau+1},\ldots,R_{\tau+b})=y\mid\mathcal F_\tau]
=2^{-b}
$$

almost surely.

### Proof

For each integer $t$, the event $\{\tau=t\}$ is determined by the source prefix and causal machine history through time $t$. Conditional on any event in $\mathcal F_t$ contained in $\{\tau=t\}$, IID independence makes the next $b$ source bits uniform and independent of that event. Partition over the disjoint events $\{\tau=t\}$ and apply the definition of conditional expectation with respect to $\mathcal F_\tau$.

Status: PROVED/EXACT.

## Theorem 2 — Constant-Rate Entropy Budget

By time $T$, a rate-$\lambda$ source has released exactly $\lfloor\lambda T\rfloor$ independent fair bits. Therefore

$$
H(G_{\le T})=\lfloor\lambda T\rfloor.
$$

If capturing one source bit consumes one clock tick, then every completed transcript satisfies

$$
\sum_i b_i\le T.
$$

### Consequence

True randomness prevents short-seed prediction, but random-bit counting alone cannot yield more than $O(T)$ independent information burden in $T$ elapsed steps.

Status: PROVED/EXACT.

---

## Theorem 3 — List-Precommitment Bound

Let

$$
B\sim U(\{0,1\}^b)
$$

be a fresh source block revealed after a commitment point.

Let $L$ be any random candidate set determined before $B$ is released, with

$$
|L|\le 2^w
$$

almost surely. Then

$$
\boxed{
\Pr[B\in L]\le 2^{w-b}.
}
$$

### Proof

Condition on the complete precommitment information. The set $L$ is then fixed, while $B$ remains independent uniform. Hence

$$
\Pr[B\in L\mid L]
=
\frac{|L|}{2^b}
\le 2^{w-b}.
$$

Average over the precommitment information.

### Corollary 3.1 — One-guess gap

One precommitted $b$-bit guess succeeds with probability

$$
2^{-b}.
$$

By contrast,

$$
\Pr_B[\exists w\in\{0,1\}^b:w=B]=1.
$$

Thus existential choice after randomness and commitment before randomness are not interchangeable.

Status: PROVED/EXACT.

## Theorem 4 — Side-Information State Lower Bound

Let $X\in\{0,1\}^K$. Let a free side channel

$$
Z=Z(X)
$$

take at most $2^r$ possible values.

After the payload is unavailable for rereading, a deterministic machine retains an $s$-bit state $S(X)$.

Assume that for every payload $x$ and every coordinate $j\in[K]$ a decoder satisfies

$$
D(S(x),Z(x),j)=x_j.
$$

Then

$$
s\ge \log_2\max_z |Z^{-1}(z)|.
$$

Since the $2^K$ payloads are partitioned among at most $2^r$ side-information fibers,

$$
\boxed{s\ge K-r}
$$

for the largest fiber.

### Proof

Fix a side-information value $z$. If two distinct payloads $x,x'\in Z^{-1}(z)$ produced the same retained state, choose a coordinate on which they differ. The decoder would then receive the same state, same side information, and same coordinate but would have to return two different answers. Contradiction. Therefore the state map is injective on every fiber. Pigeonhole gives a fiber of size at least $2^{K-r}$.

Status: PROVED/EXACT in the declared no-reread model.

### Corollary 4.1 — Free SAT answer

If the only free semantic information is one SAT decision bit,

$$
\boxed{s\ge K-1}.
$$

### Corollary 4.2 — Explicit witness side channel

If an explicitly transmitted witness/result channel has length at most $w$ bits,

$$
s\ge K-w
$$

for the largest side-information fiber, provided the legal future coordinate challenges still distinguish payloads within that fiber.

Witness existence without transmission does not reduce this state bound.

## Theorem 5 — Late-Bijection Invariance

Suppose a post-cut challenge specifies a bijection $\pi:[K]\to[K]$ and query position $j$, and the required answer is

$$
X_{\pi(j)}.
$$

If the legal challenge family can reach every payload coordinate, then Theorem 4 still yields

$$
\boxed{s\ge K-r}.
$$

### Proof

For each coordinate $q$, choose a legal challenge satisfying $\pi(j)=q$. This recovers arbitrary coordinate access and reduces directly to Theorem 4.

Status: PROVED/EXACT.

---

## Theorem 6 — Static Random-Access Reread Collapse

If the original payload $X$ remains available after the challenge through unit-cost random access, a late permutation does not force payload storage.

After receiving $(\pi,j)$, compute

$$
q=\pi(j)
$$

and issue one payload read.

Thus

$$
\boxed{
S_{\mathrm{payload}}=0,\qquad Q_{\mathrm{post}}=1,
}
$$

apart from the cost of evaluating $\pi(j)$.

Status: PROVED/EXACT upper bound.

Interpretation: late shifting/permutation is a valid delayed-address mechanism, but not by itself a static-SAT storage lower bound.

## Theorem 7 — Group-History Compression

Let chronological address transformations belong to a finite group $G_K$ whose elements have an $\ell_G(K)$-bit representation closed under efficient composition.

For

$$
\pi_1,\ldots,\pi_E\in G_K,
$$

the complete final action is summarized by

$$
\Pi=\pi_E\circ\cdots\circ\pi_1.
$$

Therefore the transformation history itself can be represented in at most

$$
\ell_G(K)
$$

bits independent of the number of epochs $E$.

### Corollary 7.1 — Cyclic shifts

Repeated shifts collapse to

$$
\Sigma=\sum_e\sigma_e\pmod K,
$$

requiring only $\lceil\log_2K\rceil$ bits.

### Corollary 7.2 — Affine transformations

Over a finite field, repeated maps

$$
x\mapsto a_ex+b_e
$$

collapse to one affine map

$$
x\mapsto Ax+B.
$$

Status: PROVED/EXACT.

Consequence: repeated cyclic or affine bit shifting is FALSIFIED/RETIRED as an accumulating-hardness mechanism.

## Theorem 8 — Literal-Payload Normalization Guard

If a construction literally supplies

$$
K=n^d
$$

independent payload bits, then the actual explicit input/transcript size $N$ satisfies

$$
N\ge K.
$$

Consequently an information-state lower bound

$$
\Omega(K)
$$

is at most linear in the actual explicit size $N$.

Status: PROVED/EXACT.

Consequence: varying the constant exponent $d$ is useful only if a compact representation of smaller exponent can be proved to force an $n^d$-scale computational obligation.

## Theorem 9 — Timing-Sensitivity Lemma

Let an IID fair-bit source remain unobserved during a solve interval.

Suppose two executions share the same causal prefix, but their next length-$b$ snapshot windows begin at two distinct source indices. Assume the offset difference is determined by the common prefix and not by the unseen source tail.

Then

$$
\boxed{
\Pr[B^{(1)}=B^{(2)}\mid\text{common prefix}]
=
2^{-b}.
}
$$

### Proof

Let the windows begin $d>0$ positions apart.

If $d\ge b$, they are disjoint independent uniform $b$-bit strings, so equality has probability $2^{-b}$.

If $0<d<b$, equality forces the length-$(b+d)$ source segment to be periodic with period $d$. There are exactly $2^d$ such strings out of $2^{b+d}$ possibilities, giving

$$
2^d/2^{b+d}=2^{-b}.
$$

Status: PROVED/EXACT under the stated blind-source hypotheses.

## Theorem 10 — Transcript Determinacy

For fixed deterministic solver $A$, deterministic controller $C$, base input $x$, and fixed source realization $G$, the CRS recurrence defines a unique transcript

$$
\mathcal T_A(x,G)
$$

provided all solve segments halt.

### Proof

Induct on the epoch index. The prior transcript fixes $\tau_i$. The fixed source gives $B_i$. The deterministic controller gives $\Phi_i$. The deterministic solver fixes its answer and charged runtime. Hence $\tau_{i+1}$ is fixed.

Status: PROVED/EXACT.

---

## Proposition 11 — Machine-Relative Transcript Obstruction

If

$$
\tau_{i+1}=\tau_i+b_i+c_A(\Phi_i)
$$

contains the actual runtime of the chosen solver, then in general

$$
\mathcal T_A(x,G)\ne\mathcal T_{A'}(x,G).
$$

Therefore exact runtime-sensitive transcript reproduction is not automatically a solver-independent language property.

There are three immediate variants:

1. Solver-relative game: each solver is judged on its own transcript.
2. Reference-solver transcript: every solver must reproduce one fixed solver's transcript.
3. External schedule: epoch boundaries are chosen independently of solver runtime.

Status: PROVED/EXACT model obstruction.

---

## Proposition 12 — Reference-Trace Simulation Upper Bound

If a reference solver $A$ and controller $C$ are fixed and their descriptions are available, a universal machine can reproduce

$$
\mathcal T_A(x,G)
$$

by simulating $A$ and applying the same controller/snapshot interface, with ordinary universal-simulation overhead.

Status: PROVED/EXACT.

Consequence: forcing another machine to reproduce the exact timed transcript of $A$ is a trace-simulation problem, not automatically a SAT lower bound.

## Theorem 13 — Causal Stagewise Witness Strategy

Let

$$
z(n)=n^{O(1)}.
$$

Suppose every currently revealed formula $\Phi_i$:

- has polynomial size,
- is promised satisfiable,
- has a polynomial-size satisfying assignment,
- and the assignment is polynomially verifiable.

Then a causal NDTM need not predict any future $G$-block.

At epoch $i$ it waits for $B_i$ and $\Phi_i$, guesses a satisfying assignment $w_i$, verifies it, and proceeds.

The total nondeterministic work remains polynomial in the total explicit transcript size.

Status: PROVED/EXACT control.

### Corollary 13.1 — Polynomial epochs do not imply NEXP

Polynomially many explicit satisfiable 3-SAT epochs do not become nondeterministic exponential time merely because the semantic witness tree contains exponentially many combinations.

---

## Proposition 14 — Full-Assignment Degeneracy

If a random source block fixes every variable of a 3-CNF, no SAT search remains. The assigned formula is evaluated directly in time linear in the number of clauses up to representation costs.

Status: PROVED/EXACT.

# Part II — Strongest surviving auxiliary model

## Definition 3 — CRS-PPC Audit Game

A fixed SAT-derived object and dependency DAG $D_n$ are established.

At each epoch:

1. a fresh private source block is revealed to the controller;
2. the controller selects a partial restriction, audit location, or outgoing pointer choice;
3. the solver responds;
4. the resource accounting charges retained data, pointer state, post-challenge rereads, recomputation, and sequential dependency traversal.

The candidate resource is

$$
\mathcal R=
S_{\mathrm{data}}
+
S_{\mathrm{pointer}}
+
wQ_{\mathrm{reread}}
+
C_{\mathrm{recompute}}
+
C_{\mathrm{sequential}}.
$$

This is currently an auxiliary online model, not a fixed static SAT language.

Pebbling is the closest exact STORE-versus-RECOMPUTE abstraction. Online Merlin-Arthur and annotated streaming are close formal analogues for an untrusted semantic helper interacting with bounded online state. Generic delay-function work is a close analogue for advice/preprocessing versus unavoidable sequential evaluation.

These transfers remain restricted to their native models.

## Target B — Causal Witness Lower Bound

Construct a SAT-derived response relation such that, even after the current random challenge is revealed, producing a valid response necessarily incurs the STORE/TRACK/REREAD/RECOMPUTE obligation.

Ordinary stagewise SAT-witness guessing must not bypass the bound.

Status: OPEN and immediate.

## Target C — Schedule Externalization / Staticization

Transform the solver-relative causal game into a fixed solver-independent instance or externally scheduled problem while preserving:

- challenge unpredictability,
- the PPC resource lower bound,
- resistance to preprocessing,
- resistance to arbitrary random-access rereading.

Status: OPEN and immediate.

## Target D — Fixed-Transcript Resource Preservation

Starting from

$$
\Pr_G[\operatorname{Cost}(A,x,G)\ge L]>0,
$$

fix a finite hard transcript $g^\star$ and prove that exposing $g^\star$ statically does not open a preprocessing/random-access shortcut that destroys the lower bound.

Existence of some $g^\star$ is immediate from positive probability; preservation of hardness after fixing is the missing theorem.

Status: OPEN.

## Target E — Static-to-CRS-PPC Compiler

For every deterministic exact SAT decider

$$
A\in\mathrm{TIME}(N^k),
$$

construct a model-preserving SAME_REALIZATION transformation to a CRS-PPC strategy with quantitatively controlled:

- preprocessing,
- cut state,
- rereads,
- recomputation,
- pointer tracking,
- sequential work.

A P-versus-NP-strength result would then require an independent lower bound exceeding the resulting upper resource bound on a polynomial-size SAT-derived instance.

Schematic target:

$$
A\in\mathrm{TIME}(N^k)
\Longrightarrow
\mathcal R\le N^{f(k)+o(1)}
$$

while

$$
\mathcal R>N^{f(k)+o(1)}.
$$

Status: OPEN and load-bearing.

Without Target E or an equivalent universal transfer theorem, the proved CRS-PPC results do not imply a lower bound for ordinary static SAT.
