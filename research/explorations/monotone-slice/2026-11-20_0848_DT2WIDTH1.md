## 1. Falsification of the TWOSATDEC protocol as a test of SAT-LOCAL-ERROR1

The protocol samples exact-layer 3-CNFs with `t = floor(3.5 q)` distinct clauses, i.e. clause density 3.5. KNOWN THEOREM (Kaporis–Kirousis–Lalas 2006; Hajiaghayi–Sorkin 2003): a random 3-CNF of density below 3.52 is satisfiable with probability tending to 1. The uniform distinct-clause model with `m = Theta(n)` is contiguous with the standard model for this property. So `Pr[UNSAT]` at density 3.5 tends to 0 as `q` grows, and TWOSATDEC57-96 (40 consecutive `0/16` UNSAT draws, q=62..101) are what the theorem predicts. They carry no information about decision depth. The rare UNSAT formulas below threshold are dominated by small dense unsatisfiable sub-formulas, which explains the shallow depth-5 survivors at q=30..42 without saying anything about the typical hard case.

## 2. Lemma DT2-WIDTH (PROVED/EXACT, standard argument, not independently reviewed)

Model. `F` is a CNF of width at most 3. A *free-2-SAT decision tree* of depth `r` queries variables. Each leaf `rho` (a partial assignment, `|rho| <= r`) must be *certified*: unit propagation on `F|rho` reaches an empty clause, or the clauses of width at most 2 that remain after propagation form an unsatisfiable 2-CNF. Surviving 3-clauses are ignored. This is exactly the TWOSATDEC certificate, and also the strong 2-SAT-backdoor notion with unit propagation (Williams–Gomes–Selman 2003): a strong backdoor `B` gives such a tree of depth `|B|`.

Claim. If `F` has a free-2-SAT decision tree of depth `r`, then `F` has a resolution refutation of width at most `r + 3`. Equivalently `r >= w(F |- bottom) - 3`.

Proof. Fix a leaf `rho`. Each unit-propagation step resolves one clause of `F|rho` (width at most 3) against at most two unit clauses. Every intermediate clause has width at most 3, and the remaining 2-clauses come from the same steps. Resolving clauses of width at most 2 gives a resolvent of width at most 2, and resolution is complete for an unsatisfiable 2-CNF. So `F|rho` has a refutation of width at most 3. By the standard restriction-lifting lemma (Ben-Sasson–Wigderson 2001), putting the literals falsified by `rho` back in turns this into a derivation from `F`, of width at most `3 + |rho|`, of a sub-clause of `C_rho`, the clause falsified exactly by `rho`. Now combine bottom-up. At an internal node querying `x`, the two children give sub-clauses of `C_rho or x` and `C_rho or not-x`. If either one lacks the literal on `x`, it is already a sub-clause of `C_rho`. Otherwise, resolving on `x` gives a sub-clause of `C_rho` of width at most `|rho| <= r`. At the root this is the empty clause, and every clause used has width at most `r + 3`. ∎

## 3. Corollary (CONDITIONAL only on the cited KNOWN THEOREMS; scope = random 3-CNF above the proved UNSAT bound)

KNOWN THEOREM (Chvátal–Szemerédi 1988 expansion; Ben-Sasson–Wigderson 2001, random k-CNF width bound): for every constant density `Delta`, w.h.p. a random 3-CNF on `n` variables with `Delta n` clauses has refutation width `>= epsilon(Delta) n` whenever it is unsatisfiable. KNOWN THEOREM (Díaz–Kirousis–Mitsche–Pérez-Giménez 2009): density `> 4.4898` is unsatisfiable w.h.p.

So for density `Delta > 4.4898`, w.h.p. every free-2-SAT decision tree, and every strong UP+2-SAT backdoor, has depth `>= epsilon(Delta) n - 3 = Omega(n)`. This answers the depth part of SAT-LOCAL-ERROR1 in the negative on the distribution where UNSAT is typical: decision depth with free exact 2-SAT is not bounded, and in fact grows linearly.

Scope limits. This bounds a restricted algorithm or proof class: queried variables plus free 2-SAT leaves, which is weaker than resolution. It is not a circuit lower bound. Max depth `Omega(n)` does not by itself bound the leaf count: an `exp(Omega(n))` leaf bound needs a separate argument (for example, a restriction-robust expansion bound for every path of length `<= delta n`), and that remains OPEN here. Achlioptas–Beame–Molloy 2001 already give exponential bounds for specific DPLL heuristics, and the corollary is likely folklore.
