\section{Exact uniform target}

Let \(\mathcal C_q\) be the proper signed 3-clause universe and
\[
M=|\mathcal C_q|.
\]
Let \(\mathcal O\) be any canonical orbit of unordered triples of distinct
clauses under variable permutations and independent variable complementation.

\begin{theorem}[FIXED-CLAUSE-TARGET]
Fix any clause \(C_0\in\mathcal C_q\).  The orbit distribution of a uniformly
random unordered 3-subset of \(\mathcal C_q\) is identical to the orbit
distribution of
\[
\{C_0,D,E\},
\]
where \(\{D,E\}\) is a uniformly random unordered pair from
\(\mathcal C_q\setminus\{C_0\}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The signed-variable permutation group acts transitively on
\(\mathcal C_q\).  For an orbit-invariant statistic of an unordered clause
triple, conditioning a uniformly random distinguished clause to equal
\(C_0\) therefore does not change the orbit distribution.  After fixing the
distinguished clause, the remaining two clauses are a uniformly random
unordered pair from the other \(M-1\) coordinates.
\end{proof}

Hence the exact uniform target can be obtained by enumerating only
\[
\binom{M-1}{2}
\]
pairs around one fixed clause.

For \(q=16\),
\[
M=4480,
\qquad
\binom{M-1}{2}=10{,}028{,}481.
\]
For \(q=18\),
\[
M=6528,
\qquad
\binom{M-1}{2}=21{,}297{,}601.
\]

Both targets contain 84 canonical order-three orbits.
