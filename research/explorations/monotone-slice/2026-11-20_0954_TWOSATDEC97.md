## Candidate and falsifiers

The candidate is that the exact layer `t=floor(3.5q)` keeps a non-vanishing UNSAT sample as `q` grows, so that the depth-r residual for SAT-LOCAL-ERROR1 stays measurable. It is falsified if (F1) the per-formula UNSAT probability decays so that `16p << 1` for the `q` already swept, (F2) a known theorem forces `P(UNSAT) -> 0` at density 3.5, or (F3) at a density where UNSAT has constant probability, the depth-r residual is not even comparable to the exact-layer numbers.

## Known theorem (F2)

Random 3-SAT with `m = c n` clauses is satisfiable with high probability for `c < 3.52`. This is shown in Kaporis, Kirousis and Lalas, "The probabilistic analysis of a greedy satisfiability algorithm," Random Structures & Algorithms 28 (2006), and independently in Hajiaghayi and Sorkin, IBM RC22942 (2003). The TWOSATDEC layer has `t/q = 3.455..3.500 < 3.52` at every index. It draws a uniform `t`-subset of distinct proper clauses, which is the standard `F(n,m)` model without replacement and is asymptotically equivalent for this statement. So `P(UNSAT on exact layer) -> 0` is a KNOWN THEOREM. An empty draw at large `q` is the predicted outcome and carries no information about SAT-LOCAL-ERROR1. Two caveats: the cited bound is a with-high-probability statement with no explicit finite-`n` rate, and the exact-layer convention of the earlier notes was not re-derived from TASK-008, which is absent.

## Stage B (F3 pilot), COMPUTATIONAL

The density is changed to `t=round(4.26q)`, near the empirical 3-SAT threshold, where UNSAT has constant probability. This uses seed `20261121` with the same generator and the same certificate rule as TWOSATDEC21: every assignment of the r-set, after unit propagation, gives an empty clause or an unsatisfiable width-at-most-2 residual, and a surviving width-3 clause fails the set. The table shows the minimum certifying depth `r` per UNSAT formula, searched up to `r <= 4`.

| q | t | UNSAT/N | r=2 | r=3 | r=4 | >4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | 60 | 21/80 | 2 | 19 | 0 | 0 |
| 18 | 77 | 20/80 | 1 | 14 | 5 | 0 |
| 22 | 94 | 20/60 | 0 | 10 | 9 | 1 |
| 26 | 111 | 21/60 | 0 | 2 | 16 | 3 |

The stage ran in about 41s; `q=26` alone took about 32s. A rerun reproduced every count, and only the timings changed. A `q=30, r<=4` stage was started and stopped before it finished, so no `q=30` data exists. At threshold density the minimum depth rises by about one per 4-8 added variables. The depth-at-most-4 residual reaches `3/21` at `q=26`. This is a usable sample on which SAT-LOCAL-ERROR1, a bounded-depth error, can actually be tested, and a growing minimum depth would count as evidence against it. Finite tests are not an asymptotic statement. The trend is consistent with the known exponential tree-like resolution lower bound for random 3-CNF at constant density (Chvátal and Szemerédi 1988; Beame, Karp, Pitassi and Saks 2002). That bound would already force `r -> infinity` for any decision-plus-closure certificate that a tree-like resolution refutation of size `2^r poly(n)` can simulate. Whether the free-2-SAT closure is so simulated is the open point below.

The next lemma (NAMED): a depth-r free-2-SAT closure certificate converts into a tree-like resolution refutation of size `2^r * poly(q)`. Each branch is a 2-SAT refutation, which has polynomial-size resolution. If this holds, Chvátal–Szemerédi and BKPS give `r = Omega(q)` with high probability at constant density above threshold. That would close SAT-LOCAL-ERROR1 negatively for this certificate class as a KNOWN-THEOREM consequence. It would still be a proof-system restriction, not a circuit bound.
