\section{Target}
Let \(\mathcal C_q\) be all proper signed 3-clauses on \(q\) variables and
\[
M_q=|\mathcal C_q|=8\binom q3.
\]
For \(A\subseteq\mathcal C_q\), let \(\mathrm{UNSAT}_{3,q}(A)=1\) iff \(A\) is unsatisfiable. Define
\[
S_{q,t}(A)=
\begin{cases}
0,&|A|<t,\\
\mathrm{UNSAT}_{3,q}(A),&|A|=t,\\
1,&|A|>t.
\end{cases}
\]
The load-bearing target remains \(C_{\rm mon}(S_{q,t})=M_q^{\omega(1)}\). This report closes only the ordinary computational-hardness-of-the-layer obligation.

\section{Literature audit}
\begin{itemize}
\item[] \textbf{KNOWN THEOREM: Valiant.} For an \(M\)-input slice function, monotone circuit complexity is at most a constant-factor enlargement of unrestricted circuit complexity plus \(O(M\log^2 M)\) \cite{Valiant1986}.
\item[] \textbf{KNOWN THEOREM/BARRIER: Wegener.} Monotone slice circuits admit a set-circuit viewpoint; standard monotone lower-bound methods fail on slices in their original form, and canonical slices such as CLIQUE can be linear-size \cite{Wegener1985}.
\item[] \textbf{KNOWN PRECEDENT: Dunne.} Central slices of several monotone NP-complete functions remain NP-complete \cite{Dunne1986}. Thus central-slice NP-hardness is not itself a new general phenomenon.
\item[] \textbf{KNOWN THEOREM/OPEN ENDPOINT: Hrube\v{s}--Pudl\'ak.} Their clause-presence \(\mathrm{UNSAT}_{k,n}\) theorem is superpolynomial in the coordinate count only when \(k\to\infty\). They explicitly note that restricting soundness to one Hamming layer invokes general-circuit power, and list constant \(k\), especially \(k=3\), as open \cite{HrubesPudlak2017}.
\item[] \textbf{KNOWN THEOREM: Tovey.} Proper 3-SAT remains NP-complete when each variable appears in at most four clauses \cite{Tovey1984}.
\item[] \textbf{CURRENT CHECK: Beimel--Nir.} ECCC TR26-213 studies multislices in monotone computation and formula models, but does not supply the general slice-circuit lower bound needed here \cite{BeimelNir2026}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Control-clause padding}
Let the source use \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\). Add controls \(d_1,\ldots,d_n\), so \(q=2n\). Let \(\mathcal P_n\subseteq\mathcal C_{2n}\) contain every proper 3-clause with at least one positive control literal. Setting all controls to one satisfies every clause of \(\mathcal P_n\).

Let \(R_n\) count target clauses containing no positive control. Then
\[
R_n=\sum_{j=0}^3\binom nj\binom n{3-j}2^{3-j}
=9\binom n3+6n\binom n2.
\]
Since \(M_{2n}=8\binom{2n}{3}\),
\[
|\mathcal P_n|=M_{2n}-R_n
\]
and
\[
M_{2n}-2R_n=\frac{n(n-1)(5n+2)}3>0.
\]
Hence \(|\mathcal P_n|>M_{2n}/2\) for \(n>1\).

\section{Middle slice}
\begin{theorem}[MID-SLICE-3SAT]
For even \(q=2n\ge6\), proper 3-SAT restricted to exactly
\[
t_{\rm mid}=M_q/2
\]
distinct clauses is NP-complete. Exact-layer 3-UNSAT is coNP-complete.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Given a proper 3-CNF \(F\) on \(n\) variables with \(m\) clauses, regard it as a formula on the first \(n\) of the \(2n\) target variables. Choose
\[
D\subseteq\mathcal P_n,\qquad |D|=t_{\rm mid}-m,
\]
and output \(F'=F\cup D\). The filler pool has more than \(t_{\rm mid}\) clauses, and source clauses contain no controls, so the choice is possible and disjoint. The output size is \(O(n^3)\).

If \(F\) is satisfiable, extend a satisfying assignment by setting every control to one; all fillers are then true. Conversely, any satisfying assignment of \(F'\) satisfies its subformula \(F\). Thus \(F\) and \(F'\) are equisatisfiable and \(|F'|=t_{\rm mid}\). Membership is immediate.
\end{proof}

\section{Sparse hard layer}
\begin{theorem}[SPARSE-SLICE-3SAT]
For \(q=2n\), proper 3-SAT restricted to exactly
\[
t_{\rm sp}=6q=12n
\]
clauses is NP-complete, using Tovey's bounded-occurrence source. Exact-layer UNSAT is coNP-complete.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If each source variable occurs at most four times, \(3m\le4n\), hence \(m\le4n/3<12n\). For \(n\ge3\), \(|\mathcal P_n|>M_{2n}/2\ge12n\). Add exactly \(12n-m\) fillers from \(\mathcal P_n\). The same control assignment argument proves equisatisfiability.
\end{proof}

For uniformly random \(A\in\binom{\mathcal C_q}{6q}\), a fixed assignment satisfies exactly \(7M_q/8\) available clauses. Hence
\[
\Pr[A\text{ SAT}]
\le2^q\left(\frac78\right)^{6q}
=\left(2\left(\frac78\right)^6\right)^q,
\]
where
\[
2(7/8)^6=0.89759063720703125<1.
\]
Thus the exact sparse layer is overwhelmingly UNSAT.

\section{Hitting-set and Hamming-scheme form}
For each assignment \(\alpha\in\{0,1\}^q\), define
\[
B_\alpha=\{C\in\mathcal C_q:\alpha\text{ falsifies }C\}.
\]
Then
\[
|B_\alpha|=\binom q3=M_q/8
\]
and
\[
A\text{ UNSAT}\iff(\forall\alpha)\;A\cap B_\alpha\ne\varnothing.
\]
Therefore the positive exact layer is
\[
\mathcal U_{q,t}
=
\left\{
A\in\binom{\mathcal C_q}{t}:
(\forall\alpha)\;A\cap B_\alpha\ne\varnothing
\right\}.
\]
If \(d=d_H(\alpha,\beta)\), then a clause is falsified by both assignments iff all three variables lie among the \(q-d\) coordinates on which they agree, so
\[
|B_\alpha\cap B_\beta|=\binom{q-d}{3}.
\]
Every positive weight-\(t\) input is a minimal positive input of \(S_{q,t}\), because every strict subset lies below the slice and is forced to zero.

\section{Hostile audit: direct monotone transfer is impossible}
\begin{proposition}[No nonconstant monotone constant-weight embedding]
If
\[
\Phi:\{0,1\}^r\to\{0,1\}^M
\]
is coordinatewise monotone and \(|\Phi(x)|=t\) for every \(x\), then \(\Phi\) is constant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If \(x\le y\), then \(\Phi(x)\le\Phi(y)\). Equal Hamming weights force \(\Phi(x)=\Phi(y)\). Since \(0^r\le x\le1^r\) for every \(x\), all images are equal.
\end{proof}

Therefore the padding maps above are ordinary polynomial many-one reductions, not monotone projections. The number of filler clauses depends on source Hamming weight. The known full-cube monotone lower bound for \(\mathrm{UNSAT}_{3,q}\) cannot be transferred through this padding by monotone projection. That direct route is \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}.

\section{Why Hrube\v{s}--Pudl\'ak does not close the target}
Their approximator must reject every satisfiable clause set globally. The slice extension \(S_{q,t}\) is forced to output one on all weights above \(t\), including satisfiable formulas, so their theorem does not apply. Their paper explicitly identifies the exact-layer soundness variant as reaching general-circuit power through slice functions \cite{HrubesPudlak2017}. For fixed \(k=3\), their theorem does not yield a superpolynomial bound in \(M_q\).

\section{Barrier audit}
The padding lemmas do not address relativization or algebrization. Any eventual constructive general-circuit property requires a Natural Proofs audit. The immediate barrier is model transfer: ordinary NP-hardness does not preserve monotone circuit lower bounds. Easy fixed-weight CLIQUE, Horn, 2-SAT, affine/Tseitin, bounded-treewidth, padding, and independent-product controls remain mandatory.
