SLICEWIDTH1 left widths $2$ and $3$ as the only place where the stated TR26-220 budget was still open. A width-2 term is a pair moment, but a width-2 DNF is a union.

\begin{proposition}[WIDTH2-REMAINDER]
Let $\mu$ and $\nu$ be laws on the same exact slice that agree on every one- and two-coordinate moment. For a width-2 monotone DNF, the Bonferroni truncation of the union through single terms is identical under $\mu$ and $\nu$. The first unmatched inclusion is the intersection of two terms. Two intersecting terms are a three-coordinate moment; two disjoint terms are a four-coordinate moment.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A term is an indicator of two coordinates, so its expectation is a pair moment. The first Bonferroni sum is therefore the same under $\mu$ and $\nu$. The intersection of two terms uses three coordinates if the terms share one, and four if they are disjoint. Neither is a pair moment.
\end{proof}

At $q=20$, $t=70$, a random width-2 DNF with $10{,}000$ terms has pair acceptance $5.81\times 10^{-5}$ and an intersecting term-pair rate $0.0002$. The third-order Bonferroni scale, using a six-coordinate upper bound on a triple of terms, is $0.027$. The MOMENTBRIDGE1 mean width-3 gap of $0.010$ sits inside that scale, so it is not evidence against an order-three design.

Beimel--Nir, ECCC TR26-213, prove that an explicit monotone function needs formula size $2^{\Omega(n/((w+1)\log^2 n))}$ over width-$w$ multislice gates, and that a width-$w$ multislice has a slice-gate formula of size at most $2n^{w+1}$. That is not a SAT theorem. It does say the width-$2$--$3$ window is not a free pass for formulas over multislice gates.

The remainder is generic to every fixed-weight slice, including the GE-easy XOR slice. $P\ne NP$ remains OPEN.
