## A. Density of the series (KNOWN THEOREM, applied)

Every raw file uses `t=floor(3.5q)`. The script checks that the density is at most `3.5` for `q=9..102`. Kaporis–Kirousis–Lalas (Random Struct. Algorithms 28 (2006) 444–480), and independently Hajiaghayi–Sorkin (arXiv math/0310193), show that random 3-CNF with density below `3.52` is satisfiable w.h.p. Their model draws clauses with replacement. The exact layer draws `t=O(q)` distinct clauses from `M=8·C(q,3)` proper clauses. A repeat occurs with probability at most `t²/(2M)=O(1/q)`, so the two models have total variation `o(1)`. Hence `Pr[UNSAT]→0` on the series, and empty draws such as TWOSATDEC92–97 are predicted. They carry no information about the residual. Status: the continuation is RETIRED as evidence.

## B. Depth bounds width (PROVED/EXACT here; elementary; not independently reviewed)

Lemma. Let `F` be a 3-CNF. Suppose a decision tree of depth `r` (adaptive, or a fixed variable set `R` with `|R|=r`) has the following property at every leaf `ρ`: unit propagation on `F|ρ` gives an empty clause, or the clauses of width at most 2 that remain form an unsatisfiable 2-CNF. Then `F` has a resolution refutation of width at most `r+3`.

Proof. Fix a leaf `ρ`. Unit propagation consists of unit-resolution steps on clauses of `F|ρ`, which have width at most 3, so every derived clause has width at most 3. An unsatisfiable 2-CNF has a resolution refutation in which every clause has width at most 2, because the resolvent of two 2-clauses has width at most 2. So `F|ρ` has a refutation of width at most 3. Now put the restricted literals back (BSW 2001; Lauria, Lecture 3, Exercise 5). This turns the leaf refutation into a derivation from `F` of some `C' ⊆ C_ρ` of width at most `3+|dom ρ| ≤ r+3`, where `C_ρ` is the clause falsified by `ρ`. Resolve these leaf clauses up the tree. Each intermediate clause is a subclause of a path clause of width at most `r`. ∎

Width lower bound (KNOWN THEOREM). Fix `0<ε<1` and `Δ≥1`. W.h.p., every resolution refutation of a random 3-CNF with `Δn` clauses has width `Ω(n·Δ^{-2/(1-ε)})`. Sources: Ben-Sasson–Wigderson, J. ACM 48(2) (2001); Beame–Karp–Pitassi–Saks, SIAM J. Comput. 31 (2002), Lemma 11, which gives the boundary expansion; Lauria, lecture notes 2015, Theorem 15, which I read for this note. The expansion and matchability events hold w.h.p. unconditionally. For a constant `Δ>4.4898` (Díaz–Kirousis–Mitsche–Pérez-Giménez upper bound on the threshold), `Pr[UNSAT]→1`, so the bound also holds w.h.p. conditioned on UNSAT. The same model-transfer argument as in A covers the exact layer.

Corollary (PROVED from the cited theorems). Fix a constant `Δ>4.4898` and fix `r`. On the exact layer at density `Δ`, the probability that any depth-`r` free-2-SAT decision certifies goes to 0. More precisely, w.h.p. every certificate has `r ≥ c_ε·q·Δ^{-2/(1-ε)} − 3`. For `Δ≤3.5`, A shows the UNSAT event itself vanishes. So no fixed density and fixed depth `r` keeps the residual bounded away from 1 as `q→∞`. The bounded-depth reading of SAT-LOCAL-ERROR1 is FALSIFIED asymptotically. Between those densities, the conditioning argument needs `Pr[UNSAT]` to stay bounded below. That case is OPEN only as a technicality and is not load-bearing.

This explains the earlier data: depth-5 survivors at q=30, 32, 38 and 42 were the start of linear growth, not noise.
