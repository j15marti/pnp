\section{What the literature actually supplies}

ECCC TR26-220, Theorem 1.1, is a robust sunflower lemma for a width-$w$ monotone DNF under a $(k,\varepsilon)$-pseudo-independent distribution, with local order $k=O(w\log(1/\delta))$ and an $\varepsilon$ of order $1/w$. The application in that paper is an arithmetic radical separation, not a slice SAT lower bound.

PSEUDOSLICE1 already proved that the exact $t$-slice forces
\[
\varepsilon^2\ge (k-1)\frac{M-t}{t(M-1)}.
\]
Filmus--Mossel (arXiv:1507.02713) show that a harmonic polynomial of degree $o(\sqrt M)$ has approximately the same distribution on the slice and on the cube. An order-three dual is degree 3 on $M=\Theta(q^3)$ coordinates, so it is not a circuit obstruction. DUAL-COLUMN3 already defeats it as a separator: 20/20 local exact-weight searches found a violating UNSAT column, and the margin fell from $4.93\times 10^{-6}$ to $2.94\times 10^{-6}$.

Scott--Sorkin (arXiv:1008.1260) leave the interval between the pure-literal threshold and the satisfiability threshold open. Density $3.5$ sits in that interval. Small-excess MUF structure is not the theorem they proved.

\section{The combination}

\begin{proposition}[SLICE-WIDTH-WINDOW]
Let $d=(M-t)/(t(M-1))$. Suppose a sunflower application requires some local order $k\ge c\, w\log(1/\delta)$ and an $\varepsilon$ budget $\varepsilon\le \gamma/w$. Then the exact-slice hypothesis is unavailable whenever
\[
(k-1)d > \gamma^2/w^2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
SLICE-PSEUDO-LOWER says every $(k,\varepsilon)$-pseudo-independent representation of the exact slice needs $\varepsilon^2\ge (k-1)d$. If that lower bound exceeds the application budget, the hypothesis of the sunflower lemma is not met.
\end{proof}

The constants $\gamma$ and $c$ are the application budget, not a constant copied out of TR26-220. With $\gamma=1$, $c=1$, $\delta=0.1$, the numerical check is:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
q&\text{open widths}&\text{first closed width}\\
\hline
16&2,3&4\ (0.398>0.25)\\
20&2,3&4\ (0.357>0.25)\\
24&2,3&4\\
64&2,3,4&5\ (0.222>0.20)
\end{array}
\]

So on the density-$3.5$ slice, the stated 2026 hypothesis is already closed for every width at least $4$, before any UNSAT distribution is chosen. A later positive-distribution $\varepsilon$ can only be worse.

\section{Status}
SLICE-WIDTH-WINDOW: \textbf{PROVED/EXACT} under the stated budget. The numerical table is the same inequality evaluated. It does not rule out a different approximation scheme, and it does not prove a monotone lower bound. $P\ne NP$ remains OPEN.
