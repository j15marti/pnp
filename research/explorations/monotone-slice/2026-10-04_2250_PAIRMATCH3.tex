\section{Purpose}

PAIR-CAMOUFLAGE2 showed that rare UNSAT exact-layer formulas at
\[
t=3.5q
\]
have a strong finite pair-orbit signal, especially an excess of one-variable
opposite-sign overlaps. A rectangular pair window reduced but did not remove
this leakage.

The present experiment instead matches each held-out UNSAT formula to a
held-out SAT formula with nearly the same standardized dominant pair-orbit
vector, and then searches for third-order signed-overlap structure on fresh
matched pairs.

No asymptotic conclusion is inferred from the finite data.

\section{Exact canonical form for three-clause orbits}

Take an unordered triple of proper signed 3-clauses
\[
(C_1,C_2,C_3).
\]
For a fixed ordering of the clauses, associate to every variable in their
union its signed incidence vector
\[
v_x\in\{-1,0,+1\}^3.
\]
Independent complementation of variable \(x\) identifies \(v_x\) with
\(-v_x\). Normalize by
\[
\widehat v_x=\min_{\rm lex}\{v_x,-v_x\}.
\]
Variable relabeling forgets variable names and preserves only the multiset of
normalized vectors. Finally minimize lexicographically over the six clause
permutations.

\begin{proposition}[TRIPLE-ORBIT-CANON]
Two unordered triples of proper signed 3-clauses lie in the same orbit under
variable permutations, independent variable complementations, and clause
permutations if and only if they have the same canonical normalized incidence
multiset.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For fixed clause order the signed incidence vectors completely record clause
membership and signs. Independent variable complementation acts independently
as \(v_x\mapsto-v_x\), and variable permutation changes only the variable
names. Thus the normalized multiset is complete at fixed clause order.
Minimizing over all six clause permutations quotients the remaining action.
\end{proof}

\section{Experiment A: nearest pair matching}

For each
\[
q\in\{20,24,28,32\},\qquad t=3.5q,
\]
6,000 exact distinct-clause formulas were sampled with deterministic seeds.
The first 3,000 were used only to estimate SAT means and standard deviations
for the six dominant pair coordinates
\[
(0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(2,0),(2,1),(2,2).
\]
Every held-out UNSAT formula was greedily matched, without SAT replacement, to
the nearest held-out SAT formula in standardized Euclidean distance.

The exact SAT solver was cross-checked against brute force on 250 small
formulas in each run.

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c|c|c}
q&U_{hold}&\bar d&d_{max}&
\mathrm{pair\ BA}&\mathrm{cubic\ BA}&\mathrm{triple\ BA}\\
\hline
20&78&0.639&1.422&0.551&0.564&0.474\\
24&42&0.662&1.558&0.548&0.333&0.333\\
28&23&0.805&1.884&0.500&0.458&0.542\\
32&26&0.635&1.134&0.577&0.462&0.500
\end{array}
\]

Pair BA is held-out balanced accuracy of a logistic classifier using only the
six matched pair coordinates. Cubic BA uses the predeclared local cubic feature
vector
\[
\left(
\sum_i\left[\binom{d_i^+}{2}d_i^-+d_i^+\binom{d_i^-}{2}\right],
\sum_i\left[\binom{d_i^+}{3}+\binom{d_i^-}{3}\right],
\sum_i\binom{d_i}{3},
\sum_i d_i^2,
\sum_i(d_i^+d_i^-)^2
\right).
\]
Triple BA uses a discovery-selected linear score from the eight strongest
sampled canonical three-clause orbits, evaluated on disjoint matched pairs.
Each matched formula contributed 400 uniformly sampled clause triples.

There is no stable held-out cubic or full triple-orbit advantage across all
four tested values of \(q\).

\section{Experiment B: tighter pair caliper}

The experiment was repeated with independent seeds and matching caliper
\[
d_{pair}\le0.75.
\]
Only matched pairs satisfying the caliper were retained. Canonical triple
sampling increased to 600 triples per retained formula.

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c}
q&U_{hold}&U_{keep}&\mathrm{keep\ frac}&
\bar d&\mathrm{pair\ BA}&\mathrm{cubic\ BA}&\mathrm{triple\ BA}\\
\hline
20&63&41&0.651&0.536&0.595&0.595&0.548\\
24&53&32&0.604&0.543&0.500&0.563&0.500\\
28&38&29&0.763&0.539&0.567&0.433&0.700\\
32&21&13&0.619&0.549&0.500&0.643&0.571
\end{array}
\]

After the caliper, mean absolute residual differences in the six pair
coordinates are approximately \(0.09\)--\(0.24\) SAT standard deviations.

The isolated \(q=28\) triple-orbit balanced accuracy of \(0.70\) is not
treated as asymptotic evidence: neighboring sizes give \(0.548,0.500,0.571\),
and the evaluation positive counts are only on the order of tens.

\section{Interpretation}

The finite experiments support only the following guarded statement:
\[
\boxed{
\text{pair matching largely removes the previously dominant pair leakage}
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\text{no reproducible order-three distinguisher was found in this run.}
}
\]

This is weaker than proving order-three camouflage. Rare UNSAT sample sizes
are small and the triple-orbit census was sampled, not exhaustive.

The pair-only classifiers being near chance after matching is an important
negative control: the fitted order-three score is not merely rediscovering the
original large pair separation.
