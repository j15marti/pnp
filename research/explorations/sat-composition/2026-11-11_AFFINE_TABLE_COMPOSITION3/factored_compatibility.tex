controller around the exact Horn/affine and 2-CNF/affine engines from
AFFINE-TABLE-COMPOSITION2. It discovers separators of size at most two,
validates a tree of constraint bags, passes exact boundary relations, and
reconstructs checked global witnesses. The earlier connected family with

The relevant parameter becomes maximum local affine rank after normalization,

\section{Input and the local exact engines}
The input is
\[
 H(x)\land Ax=b,
\]
where \(H\) is 2-CNF, or a general Horn CNF in the separate Horn mode.
The binary matrix and all clauses are explicit. Let \(N\) include the dense
variable universe, coefficient bits, signs, and source records. An external
DIMACS frontend densely relabels occurring identifiers before allocating
bit masks. It uses the earlier exact selector reduction for general CNF.

The frozen local engines use the least-Horn-model closure. On Horn models,
each monomial \(x_S\) equals the monomial indexed by \(\cl_H(S)\), or zero
if that closure is inconsistent. Distinct consistent closed supports form an
independent Boolean-function basis: the inclusion evaluation matrix is
triangular with diagonal one. The product
\[
 P(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+b_j+a_jx)
\]
over \(\F\) is the parity-satisfaction indicator. A zero canonical
dictionary proves UNSAT; a minimum-cardinality surviving key supplies a model.
Every key after \(j\) rows is generated by at most \(j\) variables, even
when its closure is large. With
\[
 D(n,d)=\sum_{i=0}^{\min(d,n)}\binom ni,
\]
the exact bound is \(\poly(N)D(n,d)\). Streaming seeds of size at most
\(d\) gives the same bound with polynomial workspace. A general 2-CNF is
translated around a satisfying base assignment into a zero-satisfied Horn
formula. These fixed-rank regimes have established prior art; the previous
report gives the complete constructive proof and source audit.

\subsection*{Normalization and a preserved Gaussian terminal}
For 2-CNF mode, every affine row on at most two variables is compiled exactly
into at most two clauses. A zero row with right side one is an empty clause;
a unary row is a unit; an equality or disequality is its ordinary binary
clause pair. In general Horn mode, unary rows and binary equalities are
compiled, but binary disequalities remain affine because their positive
clause need not be Horn. The local ranks in the automatic controller refer
to the rows retained after this normalization.

A recognized query containing only unit clauses, tautologies and affine
rows is solved directly by Gaussian elimination, independently of its rank.
Recognition work, row operations, and the final witness checks are recorded.
This terminal applies before global low-arity normalization when the original
input already has that form. This ordering repairs a tested regression:
turning a binary equality into nonunit clauses must not hide a pure affine
input from the Gaussian terminal. A three-variable control with rows
\(x_1\oplus x_2=0\), \(x_1\oplus x_2\oplus x_3=0\) now resolves with
both search budgets zero. The same terminal is checked again at each local
query. General unit/affine inputs therefore require no bounded-rank hypothesis.

\section{A verified decomposition is the composition contract}
A bag directory is a forest \(T\) with explicitly listed variable sets
\(B_t\). Each normalized source constraint has exactly one owner bag
containing its entire scope. For every variable, the bags containing it
must form a connected subtree. This is the running-intersection condition.
Every edge has separator \(B_t\cap B_u\) of size at most two in 2-CNF
mode, or at most one in general Horn mode.

The validator rejects cycles, repeated edges, invalid identifiers, uncovered
constraints, oversized separators, and disconnected occurrences of a variable.
For a variable occurring in \(c\) bags of an already verified forest, it
checks that exactly \(c-1\) forest edges contain that variable. This is
equivalent to connectedness of its induced occurrence forest. Empty clauses
and zero-scope affine contradictions are retained and handled explicitly.

\begin{lemma}[Separation of subtree variables]
After rooting a valid bag tree, the variables occurring both inside a child
subtree and outside it are exactly its child-parent separator.
Consequently, a child's exact extendibility relation on that separator
contains all information needed by its parent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If a variable appears on both sides, its connected set of occurrences crosses
the cut edge, so it belongs to both endpoint bags. Complete source ownership
prevents a constraint from referring to a private child variable while being
owned outside that subtree. Distinct child completions therefore agree
where required exactly when their separator assignments agree.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact small boundary encoding]
Every Boolean relation on at most two variables has an exact 2-CNF encoding
with at most four clauses. Every unary Boolean relation is Horn-definable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each excluded boundary assignment, add the clause falsified exactly by
that assignment. Its length is the number of boundary variables. On an empty
boundary the false relation is the empty clause and the true relation has
no clauses. This construction handles empty, full, unary and binary tables.
\end{proof}

\section{Automatic discovery with charged fallback}
Build a primal scope graph: each source factor makes its variables a clique.
These graph edges record co-occurrence and are not additional logical clauses.
The implementation uses an iterative low-link traversal to find articulation
vertices, then deterministic degree-ordered pair enumeration when needed.
Every candidate is checked by a component scan, and unsuccessful scans are
charged. Disconnected pieces are also separated.

For a chosen separator \(S\), create a bag \(S\). For every component
\(C\) of \(G-S\), recurse on \(C\cup S\), adding graph-only fill edges
to make \(S\) a clique. Attach the child's resulting tree at a bag containing
all of \(S\). The implementation uses an explicit work stack, so depth does
not depend on Python's recursion limit. A candidate or split budget exit
keeps the complete current residual graph as one bag.

\begin{theorem}[Exact polynomial discovery]
With the default cap of at most \(v\) splits, where \(v\) is the number
of occurring variables, the compiler constructs a valid decomposition with
at most \(1+v^2\) bags in polynomial time. All intersections have the selected
size bound. Budget fallback preserves exact source coverage but may leave
arbitrarily large local affine rank.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every original factor scope is a clique. After removing \(S\), a clique's
vertices outside \(S\) lie in at most one component. Its entire scope is
therefore contained in \(S\), or in one child \(C\cup S\).

Inductively, the child construction covers every clique of its filled graph
in a bag. This is true for a one-bag leaf. At a split, every clique is
contained in the separator or a single component together with the separator,
so the property passes down and back up. In particular, the filled clique
\(S\) occurs together in some child bag. Attaching there preserves running
intersection. A variable outside \(S\) occurs in only one child; a variable
in \(S\) connects all relevant child occurrence subtrees through the new
separator bag. A cap fallback is trivially valid.

Each split has at most \(v\) children and consumes one of at most \(v\)
split allowances, including disconnected splits. There are at most
\(1+v^2\) residual calls. Each call tests at most \(O(v^2)\) vertex pairs,
and one component test takes \(O(v^2)\) elementary graph operations.
Thus \(O(v^6)\) is a conservative graph-operation upper bound, with polynomial
bit, sorting, input, fill, copying and ownership overhead. The candidate cap
can reduce this work. A custom split cap is an additional charged parameter.
The implementation does not claim the linear bound of Hopcroft--Tarjan's
different triconnected decomposition algorithm \cite{hopcroft1973}.
\end{proof}

This theorem guarantees a valid decomposition. It does not guarantee that
the decomposition is useful for the local solver, optimal among decompositions,
or stable under rerunning the greedy search after deleting constraints.
ARC235 already identified the distinction between fixed-directory hereditary
properties and recognition by a new greedy run.

\section{Exact messages, witnesses, and unresolved states}
Root each component. For bag \(t\), let \(S_t\) be its intersection with
its parent, and let the root separator be empty. For every assignment
\(\alpha\) to \(S_t\), conjoin the bag's owned source constraints, the
exact encodings of its child messages, and units pinning \(\alpha\).
Invoke the local exact solver once. There are at most four calls per bag
in 2-CNF mode, at most two in general Horn mode, and one at each root.
The algorithm never enumerates the union of all child boundary variables.

\begin{theorem}[Exact factored compatibility]
For a validated decomposition, uncapped local engines decide the complete
source exactly. A SAT result includes a global assignment checked against
every original clause and affine row. If all local ranks are bounded by a
fixed constant, the complete computation is polynomial in the source and
decomposition lengths, including discovery under the preceding compiler.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct from the leaves. Given exact child extendibility tables, the separation
lemma makes the local query equivalent to extendibility of its parent-boundary
assignment to all sources in the subtree. The small-boundary lemma keeps
incoming messages inside the local clause language. A SAT local witness can
be joined with the recorded child witnesses it selects; the separator values
agree by construction. Exhaustion of all extensions is exact UNSAT for that
boundary assignment. Root feasibility is whole-component feasibility, and
disjoint components are conjoined. The final reconstruction rechecks shared
values and every source constraint. The time bound is proved below.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{A cap must not manufacture an UNSAT message}
Each boundary state is SAT, UNSAT or UNRESOLVED. A parent excludes only
proved UNSAT child states, so its local query is a relaxation of the exact
subtree problem. If the local solver proves this relaxation UNSAT, the parent
state is UNSAT. If it returns a model, that model certifies SAT only when
every selected child state is already SAT. Selecting even one unresolved
child downgrades the state to UNRESOLVED. A local budget exit also returns
UNRESOLVED. This preserves both sound answers without assuming a completion
for an unknown table entry.

An independent control exercises the critical downgrade: a Gaussian root
finds a local satisfying candidate, but the selected child with a genuine
nonunit constraint exhausted its expansion or seed budget. The controller
returns UNRESOLVED and no global witness. Conversely, a proved UNSAT component
can refute the entire forest even if another component remains unresolved.

The term and seed caps apply per local query to the named backend resource.
They do not bound total process time, discovery, input scans, Gaussian work,
or every live Python object. The separate discovery caps cause exact bag
fallback. Neither type of cap supplies a universal favorable-execution theorem.

\section{Time and space accounting}
Let \(n_t=|B_t|\), \(d_t\) be the coefficient rank of the normalized affine
rows owned by \(t\), and \(L_t\) include its owned source plus incoming
message clauses and pins. Incoming messages and pins add clauses only, so
they do not increase \(d_t\). A safe total bound is
\[
\begin{split}
 T \le {}& T_{\rm discovery}+T_{\rm validation}+T_{\rm ownership}
       +T_{\rm reconstruction}\\
 &+\sum_t 2^{|S_t|}\poly(L_t+n_t)D(n_t,d_t).
\end{split}
\]
For each recognized unit-affine query the corresponding summand can instead
use a polynomial Gaussian bound. One common polynomial can absorb recognition
and both routines' polynomial factors. Equivalently, its combinatorial factor
is the minimum of \(D(n_t,d_t)\) and one for recognized affine queries,
or \(D(n_t,d_t)\) for the other queries.

This is polynomial for fixed \(\max_t d_t\), with polynomially bounded
directory and discovery costs, even when global affine rank and individual
bag sizes grow. It is an XP bound in local rank; logarithmic local rank
does not automatically yield polynomial time. Every candidate product,
including canceled or rejected products, remains charged by the local engine.

\begin{lemma}[Exact occurrence identity]
For a valid forest with \(k\) bags, \(c\) components and \(v\) distinct
occurring variables,
\[
 \sum_t |B_t|=v+\sum_{tu\in E(T)}|B_t\cap B_u|
 \le v+2(k-c).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each variable, its connected occurrence subtree has one fewer edge than
vertices. Summing that identity over variables gives the equality. The
separator-size bound gives the inequality.
\end{proof}

At most four cached local assignment masks per bag need at most
\(4\sum_t|B_t|\) bits in an ideal packed representation. This does not count
Python object overhead, counters, source buffers, graph copies, directory
validation, or the active solver. With the streaming seed backend, all these
objects and the active local computation have polynomial workspace even
when \(d_t\) grows. Running time can still be exponential.

\section{Why the first expansion family now factors}
Take \(m\) selector triples \((p_i,q_i,r_i)\), at most one true selector
per triple, one odd-parity equation per triple, and implications from every
selector to one common anchor \(a\). The flat Horn polynomial has exactly
\(3^m\) terms. Removing \(a\) separates the triples. The compiler discovers
that cut, and every nontrivial bag has four variables and affine rank one.
Its unary message says that the anchor must be true. No global product of
all triple choices is constructed.

With two common anchors \(a,b\), there is no articulation vertex, but
removing the pair separates the triples. The compiler discovers this pair,
fills its graph edge, and obtains five-variable bags with rank one and
two-variable messages. The fill edge is not an extra logical constraint.
An exact binary table permits composition even when it contains positive
clauses. The same mechanism applies to less symmetric validated inputs.

The complete automatic 200-triple runs are recorded by the integration
verifier. Their global affine rank is 200 while every local rank is at most
one. The one-anchor and two-anchor runs each use 201 bags; their local query
counts are 401 and 801 respectively. These are verified structured controls,
not a general performance benchmark.

\section{A three-anchor obstruction to this certificate class}
Replace the common anchor by three anchors \(a_1,a_2,a_3\). Every selector
implies each anchor. Keep the three pairwise exclusions inside every triple.
Add one parity equation on each triple, with right sides specified below.
For \(m\ge2\), the scope graph is connected after removing any two vertices:
at least one anchor and one selector remain, and the surviving anchors and
selectors connect through their complete bipartite incidence. Removing all
three anchors separates the selector triples. Its vertex connectivity is
therefore exactly three.

\begin{theorem}[A full bag is unavoidable]
Every tree decomposition of this original scope graph with edge intersections
of size at most two has a bag containing all original vertices. Every other
bag then has at most two vertices. Consequently, if each original ternary
parity row is assigned whole to an owner bag, all \(m\) independent rows
belong to the full bag, and its local affine rank is \(m\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any tree edge, if both sides have exclusive graph vertices, their
intersection separates those vertices in the graph. An intersection of size
at most two cannot do this in a three-connected graph. Hence one side
contains all graph vertices. Orient the edge toward such a side. A finite
oriented tree has a sink. Every graph vertex belongs to its sink bag:
otherwise the connected occurrence subtree of a missing vertex lies beyond
one incident edge, contradicting that edge's orientation into the sink.

Let \(B_*\) be a full bag. If another bag contains a variable, that variable
also occurs throughout its path to \(B_*\). Thus the entire other bag lies
in its first edge intersection, and has size at most two. Each ternary row
requires three variables together, so it is owned by \(B_*\). The rows
have disjoint triple supports and hence coefficient rank \(m\).
\end{proof}

This statement quantifies over every decomposition in the declared
source-scope-preserving class, not just the greedy search order. It does not
cover arbitrary variable extensions, semantic rewrites, larger typed
interfaces, or other SAT algorithms. The instance family below has a directly
constructible satisfying assignment.

\subsection*{A balanced family also defeats the four flat local routines}
Let \(p\in\{0,1\}^m\) be the vector of triple parities in the deterministic
2-SAT base chosen by the frozen engine for the binary formula. Put
\(q=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), and choose a target vector \(b\) of weight
\(q\) maximizing \(h=\operatorname{dist}(p,b)\). This is a polynomial
construction: prefer target ones where \(p\) is zero. It gives \(h\ge q\ge1\).
For example, if \(p\) has \(z\) zero positions, the maximum distance is
\(m-z+q\) for \(q\le z\), and \(m+z-q\) otherwise; both are at least
\(q\) because \(q\le m/2\).

\begin{proposition}[Exact flat expansion sizes]
For the specified polynomial routines, the final Horn dictionary contains
\(3^q4^{m-q}\) keys and the translated 2-CNF dictionary contains
\(3^h4^{m-h}\) keys. Both grow exponentially with \(m\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For Horn least-model coordinates, an odd-target row contributes three
selector monomials; an even-target row contributes a constant and three
selector monomials. Closing a nonempty selector choice adds the common
anchors. At least one odd-target factor is present, so every product contains
a selector and therefore the same anchors. Distinct choices are distinguished
by their restrictions to each triple. All are consistent, all coefficients
are one, and no cancellation occurs.

For the translated 2-CNF base, a triple is initially empty or has exactly one
selected variable. If it is empty, the three generated local closures are
the three singleton selector choices, together with any required common
anchor flips. If its selected variable is \(p_i\), the three local supports
are \(\{y_{p_i}\}\), \(\{y_{p_i},y_{q_i}\}\), and
\(\{y_{p_i},y_{r_i}\}\). A parity mismatch contributes those three keys;
a matching parity contributes them plus the constant. If any base selector
is true, all base anchors are true and these generated supports introduce
no anchor flips, so blocks remain independent. If all base selectors are
false, common upward anchor flips do not merge distinct selector choices.
Since \(h\ge1\), every complete product includes a nonconstant choice.
The same distinctness and consistency argument gives the second count.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Mandatory seed prefixes]
Every successful generating seed for the original Horn closure has size at
least \(q\). Every successful seed in the base-translated 2-CNF coordinates
has size at least \(h\). The cardinality-ordered routines therefore exhaust
respectively
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\binom{3m+3}{i}
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 \sum_{i=0}^{h-1}\binom{3m+3}{i}
\]
unsuccessful seeds before finding a model.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every odd-target triple needs one true selector, which Horn closure cannot
generate from an anchor or from another triple. This gives \(q\).
Every satisfying model also sets all three anchors to one, since \(q>0\).
In translated coordinates, an anchor flip from one to zero is consequently
forbidden in any successful generating seed or its closure. Such a flip is
the only implication mechanism that can propagate selector changes between
different triples. Within a parity-mismatched triple, changing the parity
requires its own selector generator. This is either turning on a selector
of a base-empty triple or turning off the selected variable of a base-singleton
triple. Distinct mismatched triples therefore require distinct generators.
\end{proof}

The family remains easy SAT: set all anchors to one, select one variable in
each odd-target triple, and select none in even-target triples. A suitable
Gaussian particular solution followed by Horn closure also solves it.
The preceding propositions are scoped to the implemented expansion and
cardinality-ordered seed routines; they do not lower-bound arbitrary SAT
decision or an enlarged portfolio.

At \(m=8\), the recorded natural-base run has \(q=h=4\), 27 variables,
and 20,736 final terms in both polynomial modes. The two seed modes use
3,926 and 19,655 attempts; each must first examine the 3,304 seeds of size
below four. At \(m=20\), the proved formulas give 61,917,364,224 final terms
and 28,169,466,944 mandatory smaller-seed attempts for the recorded base
pattern. Those larger executions were not performed.

\section{The exact boundary at three shared variables}
The restriction on message language has semantic content. Equality and
disequality on two variables have identical full unary projections, while
their conjunction is empty. Unary summaries can therefore lose necessary
compatibility. Even a binary message can be non-Horn: private variables
\(p\oplus q=1\), with Horn implications \(p\Rightarrow u\) and
\(q\Rightarrow v\), project to \(u\lor v\).

At three boundary variables, private selectors with odd parity, pairwise
at-most-one clauses, and implications to \(u,v,w\) project to
\(u\lor v\lor w\). This relation has full affine hull and is not
bijunctive: its three unit vectors have majority zero, which it excludes.
It therefore has no boundary-only representation as 2-CNF plus affine
equations. Existential auxiliary variables in 2-CNF alone do not help,
because majority closure survives projection.

\begin{proposition}[Known rank-one adhesion-three encoding]
The class of supplied module trees with separators of size at most three,
local binary Horn clauses and local affine rank at most one already contains
an exact encoding of arbitrary 3-SAT.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put all original variables in a root bag. For each original clause
\((\ell_1\lor\ell_2\lor\ell_3)\), create a leaf containing its boundary
variables and private selectors \(p,q,r\). Give it the one affine equation
\(p\oplus q\oplus r=1\) and the three Horn binary implications
\(p\Rightarrow\ell_1\), \(q\Rightarrow\ell_2\),
\(r\Rightarrow\ell_3\). A true selector requires a true literal, and
odd parity guarantees at least one selector. Conversely, a true original
literal permits exactly its selector to be chosen. The projected leaf
relation is precisely the original clause. The root owns no affine row.
The star directory has running intersection and every leaf has rank one.
Conjoining its messages recovers the original 3-CNF.
\end{proof}

This is the previously recorded ARC230/ARC236 reduction, restated here to
prevent an unsupported generalization of the two-variable theorem. A
three-variable message can still be easy when its actual relation is
bijunctive. ARC236's recognized good messages and charged hitting sets for
the remaining ternary scopes are relevant to that next extension.

\section{Verification, provenance and remaining obligation}
The independent message audit checks exact subtree relations against brute
force, rather than checking only final answers. Its final-source record has
580 small/capped cases, 4,060 subtree-state comparisons and 11 deliberately
invalid decompositions. Automatic discovery is independently checked on all
1,100 labeled graphs with zero through five vertices: 2,200 decompositions
with separator bounds one and two, 1,100 low-link versus deletion comparisons,
and 28 cap/fallback controls. Every clique of size at least three was also
supplied as an affine factor to test whole-scope coverage.

The Gaussian terminal has eight 1,024-variable, rank-1,024 controls covering
both modes, strategies and answer classes with both backend budgets zero.
The integration verifier passes 696 API and semantic cases and nine CLI
cases. It separately checks normalization, automatic mixed
solving, CNF selectors, sparse identifiers, the repaired affine-terminal
ordering, the 200-triple examples and the prior 33-variable UNSAT graph.
All finite counts and source hashes are recorded in the package summary.

The balanced obstruction's polynomial counts are tested through \(m=9\),
and its seed bounds through \(m=8\). All 94 valid bases on the two smallest
instances are checked, together with 2,686 independent smaller-seed tests
using semantic model intersection as a closure oracle. Graph controls check
all 1,796 removals of zero, one or two vertices across \(m=2,\ldots,9\).
These records supplement the stated proofs; finite agreement does not
establish an unrestricted asymptotic theorem.

The root \texttt{main.tex} remains with its authorized owners. This report,
the executable package, and an additive coordination handoff update both
proof trackers. Existing research roles and schedules remain intact.
ARC235 and ARC236 originals are retained with their Git blob identifiers;
the report makes no claim to have read every historical project artifact.

The remaining target is a uniformly charged controller for semantic
compatibility that can exploit larger \emph{tractable} interfaces, residual
solvers, and other exact representations while bounding every failed search
and intermediate object. The current three-anchor obstruction says that
source-preserving two-variable interfaces with uniformly bounded local rank
cannot be the universal certificate. The adhesion-three reduction says that
merely enlarging the separator cap does not provide a universal polynomial
algorithm. P1-01, P1-03, P1-04 and the unrestricted P2 bridges remain OPEN.
