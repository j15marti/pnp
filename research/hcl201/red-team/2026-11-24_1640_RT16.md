## A. Exact common-coordinate recognition (PROVED)
Input: an explicitly encoded affine chart G with independent free generators t in F2^n, and a finite registry of rank-two rejection events E_i=[a_i(t)=s_i,b_i(t)=t_i] with independent linear forms. Use distinct notation t_i for RHS bit, not a generator. Every event is exactly one forbidden pair. All exterior and consumer observations to be retained must be registered in a linear subspace W of the dual generator space. First apply certified Gaussian consistency, remove impossible events, reject an event covering the chart, complement rank-one rejections, and merge same-plane exclusions when useful; two distinct exclusions leave an affine line and three leave a point. Restart after every certified deduction. Conditional chart restrictions stay in their guards. This is closure under the stated inexpensive rules, not full semantic closure. The theorem is on the resulting rank-two registry (rank-one equations absorbed). If the input is already in a free chart, no extra hidden affine constraints may be appended without recharting.

For distinct observation planes U_i=span(a_i,b_i), decide whether a single basis B of the dual makes every U_i a coordinate plane (span of exactly two basis elements) AND W a coordinate subspace. Repeated planes use the same chosen basis, but keep all original RHS/owners for the decision and lift.

For each plane list its three nonzero directions. Mark as mandatory every direction shared with another distinct plane and, if dim(U_i intersect W)=1, the unique nonzero intersection direction. If a plane has more than two mandatory directions, decline. Otherwise choose any two distinct directions including all mandatory ones. Let I be the union of the chosen directions lying in W and O the union outside W. Accept exactly if
  rank(I)=|I|, and rank(W+span O)=dim W+|O|.
On acceptance extend I to a basis of W, append O and extend to a basis of the full dual by Gaussian elimination.

Necessity: coordinate subspaces intersect in the span of common basis elements. Every shared line and every one-dimensional intersection with W must therefore be a basis direction. Distinct planes cannot share an unmarked candidate; such a line would have been marked. Thus the counts of distinct chosen directions inside and outside W do not depend on the choices of nonmandatory directions: all overlaps are already fixed, planes wholly inside W contribute two inside, planes meeting W in one line contribute exactly one inside, and disjoint planes contribute none. Moreover span(I) is invariant: it is the sum of all planes wholly inside W and all one-dimensional intersections with W. The span of W together with O is invariant, equal to W+sum U_i. Hence both rank tests are independent of all remaining choices. If any common basis exists its picks pass both tests, so the algorithm passes. Sufficiency: the resulting independent coordinate basis contains two chosen directions from every plane and a basis of W, by construction. Every rejection is now exactly a forbidden word on two coordinate bits. Convert its RHS using the actual chosen row combinations. A clause requiring at least one coordinate to differ is ordinary 2-CNF. This is an exact pointwise invertible change of coordinates, not a relaxation. Solve using implication SCC and reconstruct the original word by Gaussian elimination, verify all original owners. A negative recognition result means UNKNOWN for this terminal, not UNSAT.

The case W=0 is a complete recognition theorem for direct simultaneous coordinate-plane representations on this chart. It is not recognition of every semantically equivalent 2-CNF or arbitrary extended formulation. Example of a structural decline: three planes span(a,b), span(a,c), span(a,b+c) alone actually share only a and may fail by global rank; an explicit >2 mandatory case is U=span(a,b) with three other planes span(a,c), span(b,d), span(a+b,e) for independent a,b,c,d,e. All three lines of U are required. Another decline is three pairwise disjoint planes U1=span(a,b), U2=span(c,d), U3=span(a+c,b+d) on four independent directions: six distinct picks but rank four. Neither decline determines feasibility. The latter all-punctured example is always SAT by the earlier single-cycle saturation argument.

Exact exterior projection: when W is coordinate, the feasible exterior relation is itself 2-CNF on a basis of W. Eliminate private coordinates by resolving positive/negative occurrences of each variable, retaining resolvents of length <=2, dropping tautologies and duplicates. Davis-Putnam existential elimination is exact: conflicting demands on the removed bit occur precisely when a resolvent fails. There are O(n^2) distinct non-tautological width<=2 clauses; each step and all acquisition are polynomial. Equivalently, after global SCC consistency, add (not p OR q) for every implication path p->q with both literals exterior. These clauses suffice because any chain created by a resolution is a path, and unsatisfiable original relations yield a constant false projection. Original lift under a requested exterior word uses the same chart, unit closure and SCC with actual pins. No enumeration of 2^dim W. Projection is existential, not count preservation. This provides a reusable exact regional interface when all consumers accept the same coordinates; arbitrary conjunctions of independently changed bases still require a shared-chart recognition or charged gluing.

SCC has O(n+m) graph vertices/edges after conversion; projection can use O(n(n+m)) reachability plus O(n^2) stored clauses, or bounded-width resolution. Each deduction/conditioning retains chart and restarts applicable closure; no branching needed in accepted terminal. Repeated requested lifts charged individually. ## B. Infinite same-deficiency, same-rank, high-control SAT/UNSAT neighbors (PROVED)
For k>=2 use variables x,a_1,...,a_k,b_1,...,b_k. Implication paths
 x -> a_1 -> ... -> a_k -> not x,
 not x -> b_1 -> ... -> b_k -> x.
Each arrow is one 2-CNF clause, equivalently one rank-two affine rejection event. The two paths make the root UNSAT. Delete any arrow on one path. Choose x so the broken path starts true and ends false; its interior literals true before the break and false after. All interior literals on the other path false (it starts false and ends true). This is a witness satisfying all but the removed arrow and violating exactly that arrow. Changing that arrow's forbidden two-bit word to ANY of its other three values also makes this SAME witness legal. Hence every root has 3(2k+2) signed SAT neighbors with the same owner count and identical observation planes, and 2k+2 deletion SAT neighbors.

n=2k+1, m=2k+2, total observation rank n, deficiency d=1. Graph is two cycles joined at x. Every proper set of edges has at most as many edges as incident vertices: a proper subset breaks at least one cycle and all remaining components are trees or unicyclic. Its rank is exactly the number of incident vertices, because each plane is a coordinate pair. Thus D(J)<=0 for every proper J, D(full)=1; canonical smallest maximum-deficiency core is all owners, on root AND every signed neighbor. For every deletion neighbor D(J)<=0 throughout, so core empty. This independently reproduces the scope of MS16/BA10/DY10: the canonical core is minimal for the independent-complement certificate, not semantic minimization or decision. Review: their supermodularity and rank-increment proof are correct; actual independent rows modulo core give compatible complements for all core/exterior words. Their final failed BFS rank argument is sound as stated. We independently enumerate all subsets on small families, not execute the peer augmenting code.

All rank-two planes distinct for k>=2. Each individual rejection complement has full affine hull on its plane, so the stated event-local rank/unit closure and same-plane merging are stagnant at root. No claim about stronger cross-owner closure. Pick floor(k/2) disjoint internal edges on each path. Their 2 floor(k/2) planes form a direct sum. Any intrinsic RT15 control space hitting every plane must contain one independent direction per selected plane, hence h>=2 floor(k/2). This holds under any invertible affine mixing, as do all ranks and the core. Thus deficiency one does not imply small retained rank OR small intrinsic control rank, even for same-geometry SAT/UNSAT neighbors. Enumerating all core words or all control fibers is not a universal polynomial strategy. Yet the new terminal solves all these families in polynomial time after any invertible mixing. They are EASY controls, not an unrestricted lower bound; their graph separators also give alternative escapes. No claim that DO10's large-width critical family has this coordinate structure.

## C. Guard compatibility barrier (PROVED)
An arbitrary protected affine observation may NOT be treated as a coordinate pin. Given positive 1-in-3 SAT with triples (x,y,z), create a globally ordinary 2-CNF containing (not x OR not y),(not x OR not z),(not y OR not z) per triple. It is always SAT (all zero). Register the exterior observation x+y+z mod 2 per triple, and request all ones. Each triple must have at most one true variable and odd parity, so exactly one. Fixed-fiber feasibility is NP-complete by the standard positive 1-in-3 SAT theorem, even though the unguarded coordinate terminal is trivial. This is a reduction for arbitrary exterior pinning, not hardness of every W that fails recognition. It is valid even if the external all-one target is inconsistent (then both sides UNSAT); the ambient consistency check must be paid. No claim that the reduction survives every stronger closure or physical compiler. It explains why W must be included in the common-coordinate recognition or the chart must be recomputed under each actual guard. Full guard-sensitive closure is not automatic.
