## Strict cooperative gain: nonlinear phase-Tseitin family (PROVED)
On a connected simple cubic graph G, each edge carries a private-to-that-edge triple of Boolean coordinates; its phase is majority of the triple. An owner at each vertex permits precisely those nine-bit rows whose three incident edge phases XOR to a charge b_v. Each local table has 256 rows. Each phase fiber has four words and full affine hull in F2^3 (000,001,010,100 or its complement); each owner has full coordinate affine hull because it contains a Cartesian product of three such fibers. Any consistent pair of owner rows fixes at most two of a third owner's three incident edge triples; the remaining triple realizes either phase. Therefore ordinary row arc and pair path consistency stagnate. A single pivot fixes at most one incident triple of any other owner; the neighbor's restricted support still spans every other coordinate (product of full-hull phase fibers). Thus MS24 single-pivot coordinate hull also stagnates on this raw chart, including covered fusion. No claim against I24 higher-degree moments or BA18 squares.
For adjacent owners, pair components are indexed by the common three-bit edge word (each such word has extensions on both sides); the component equations can be summed for majority-one words, identifying the phase selector sums at both endpoints. At vertex v, the XOR of three such selector sums equals b_v times the odd-total equation, since every actual allowed row has that charge. Sum over all vertices: every edge phase cancels twice, leaving 0=sum b_v. Odd charge yields certified contradiction by lifted Gaussian without a branch. Even charge is SAT: solve ordinary incidence parity (spanning tree), choose any triple realizing each phase. Exact count 2^(|E|-|V|+1)*4^|E| for even charge, zero for odd charge. The recognized family is easy; generic discovery of a phase decomposition is not established. Connected prisms of size 2k for k>=3 give infinite same-geometry SAT/UNSAT neighbors by flipping one charge, with local hull and pair closure identical in the sense of no deletions, not identical row registries. This is a restricted explicit-table family (arity nine), not a proved strict E4/B4 native compiler.

## Counterexample to universal selector progress (PROVED)
Use a pair-registry chart with one three-valued color selector owner per vertex of an odd rim wheel, all spokes and cycle edges requiring distinct colors; nonedges unrestricted. Each allowed pair bipartite graph is connected (K_3,3 or K_3,3 minus its diagonal). Thus the component equations are just sums of the two odd-total equations, and the global system is the independent odd-total equation per vertex. All live rows and pairs survive, even full owner-row pins on one owner (apart from the pinned choice itself and ordinary compatibility filtering if subsequently performed). Raw arc and path consistency also stall: any two colors forbid at most two colors at a third vertex. UNSAT because hub consumes one color and an odd cycle cannot alternate the other two. Delete any rim edge: alternating path and hub give SAT. Delete any spoke: set its rim vertex to hub color, alternate the even path on remaining rim vertices, give SAT. All 2k single-edge deletions are SAT. These are infinite opposite-label neighbors; the exact wheel terminal is a cheap escape, not an unrestricted lower bound or simultaneous obstruction to every channel. Three colors can be represented as three two-bit rows; explicit pair masks are the source constraints in this chart. Native strict compiler not asserted.
