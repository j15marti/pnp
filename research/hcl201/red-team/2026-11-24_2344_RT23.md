## PROVED: exact interface language; lower-order marginals insufficient
For any nonconstant cut orbit U|V of k boundary variables, create a shared bit p. Impose p in the set of colors occurring in U and similarly V, using a chain R(p;u1,q1), R(q1;u2,q2), ..., R(q;u_{l-1},u_l). A singleton group identifies p with its one variable; if both are singleton, use equality. Induction proves each chain permits exactly the colors occurring in its group. Their intersection is empty iff the two groups are monochromatic opposite. This excludes exactly one complement orbit, and for k>=3 uses k-2 owners. Give each excluded orbit private auxiliary bits. Thus every constant-containing complement-invariant boundary relation is exactly R-definable with <=(k-2)(2^(k-1)-1) owners for k>=3. But expanding those owners at each recursive step is NOT covered by the one-table credit; keep the table or prove a separate bound.

For every k>=3, the relation missing just one complement orbit has full projection onto every proper subset of coordinates, just like the unrestricted relation. Given a partial word missing at least one coordinate, it has at least two full extensions and two extensions differing in one bit cannot both be complementary. Hence no conjunction of lower-arity constraints on only the boundary coordinates can express the excluded orbit. No lower-order marginal-only rule is a universal exact separator adapter. The R chain uses private bits; no lower bound against such compact representations or general SAT.
