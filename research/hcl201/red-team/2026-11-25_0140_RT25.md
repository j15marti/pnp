## PROVED: double-pinned full-support fractional and signed-integer obstruction
Let H be a 3-uniform hypergraph of Berge girth at least 8, with arbitrary signs on NAE triples. Owners accept six signed NAE words; pair masks are precisely equality of common actual bits (at most one shared bit), not arbitrary imported vetoes. For every compatible choice of zero, one or two actual owner rows, run coordinate forced-bit closure to a fixed point. It never rejects. On the remaining row and pair domains there exist (i) a rational local marginal solution positive on every live row and every live compatible pair, and (ii) a signed integer marginal solution supported on those same live rows and pairs. This covers the support refinement of the basic LP followed by AIP, even after rebuilding domains under two pins. Hence a closure whose root deletions require rejection of such a pinned relaxation cannot delete any root row or compatible pair on this raw class. It does not cover arbitrary recursively nested probes, higher-order tables, arbitrary affine guards, PSD, additional certified cuts or unrestricted algorithms.

Proof. A pinned owner fixes all three bits. With one owner or two intersecting owners, the fixed vertices lie in a connected incidence tree with at most two owner nodes. Any other triple meeting two of these fixed vertices creates a Berge cycle of length at most three. With two disjoint pinned owners, any other triple meeting at least two fixed vertices must bridge the two components (one from each); two distinct bridges create a Berge cycle of length at most four, using the unique paths in the two pinned owners. Thus there is at most one such bridge. If its two signed fixed bits agree, it forces the third bit to the opposite value; include this bridge in the fixed incidence tree (now at most three owners). If they differ, its remaining bit is unrestricted. No subsequent owner can touch two fixed vertices of the connected tree without a cycle of length at most four. Every constrained triple with two fixed bits either has the third forced or has both remaining choices; no all-fixed violated triple. This proves the claimed closure and its termination; identical pins are treated as one.

Construct a graph on the remaining free source variables, putting an edge for every owner with exactly one fixed bit, joining its two free variables. Label this edge by the fixed incidence component it meets (one or two components, each with at most three owners). Two distinct edges incident at the same free vertex with the same label would close a Berge cycle along the fixed tree with at most five owners; forbidden. Thus degree is at most two. A simple three-edge path repeats a label on its first and third edges (adjacent repeats already excluded). The free path and the path inside that fixed component create a Berge cycle of length at most six, also forbidden. This excludes cycles as well (a triangle has an adjacent repeated label, including the cyclic adjacency); hence graph components are paths of length at most two.

Give every free bit a marginal 1/2+epsilon*d_v. On each path choose root d=0 and recursively choose the next signed bias with magnitude abs(previous)+1 in the direction making the sum of the two signed free marginals strictly greater than 1 when the fixed signed bit is 0, strictly less than 1 when it is 1. Signs only change coefficients by ±1. Then abs(d_v)<=2. Choose epsilon=1/600. Every one-fixed NAE triangle has strictly positive barycentric weights on its three surviving words. For no-fixed owners, uniform six-row weights have mean (1/2,1/2,1/2) and are strictly positive; perturb along the unimodular simplex of signed rows 100,010,001,110, whose integer determinant has absolute value one. The perturbation by at most 2/600 in each coordinate keeps all six weights positive (explicit changes in checker). Two-fixed free owner has both surviving words and gets positive Bernoulli weights; all-fixed owners have their one accepted row. This is a single globally consistent family of rational row marginals. On disjoint owner pairs use product coupling; on shared bit b use product coupling within the b class divided by its positive class mass. Every compatible live pair has positive weight, exactly correct row marginals. Thus the LP's relative-interior support is all live rows/pairs, not a possibly misleading arbitrary boundary LP point.

For the signed integer lift choose any source bit word respecting the fixed bits, without requiring satisfaction elsewhere. On each remaining owner, any integer coordinate moments with those fixed values lift to signed integer masses summing to one: use the unimodular four-row simplex for zero fixed, the unimodular triangle for one fixed, full binary simplex for a free bit, or singleton for all fixed. Matching common-bit moments ensure component balances. BA19's spanning-tree signed transport realizes integer pair masses, including disjoint pairs, with these marginals. Their support is contained in the positive LP support. No nonnegative integer solution inferred. This reproduces the essential BA19 local lattice deduction and strengthens MS26's raw singleton obstruction to double-pinned support-refined LP+AIP.
