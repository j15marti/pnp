## 2. Exact intersection acquisition (PROVED)
Let each vertex have a two-bit color x_v, independently uniform at root, and write E_e={x_u XOR x_v=s_e}. A legal coloring avoids every E_e. For any selected edge set F, impose the equality constraints on the graph (V,F). A BFS from each component root assigns potentials and tests all edge equations. They are consistent iff the XOR of shifts along every cycle of (V,F) is zero. If consistent, exactly 4^kappa(F) original color words satisfy the selected bad events (one freely chosen root color per component, including isolates); otherwise zero. Hence Pr(intersection over e in F of E_e)=4^(-rank(F)) or zero, with rank(F)=|V|-kappa(F).
This is an exact deterministic query in O(n+|F|) graph operations, plus integer/output bit costs O(poly(N)). Arbitrary affine guards may also be included by Gaussian elimination on 2n bits; then the result is zero or 2^(dimension of the joint affine solution space). No assumption of independent events in a cyclic graph. This is a general exact acquisition routine for this explicitly encoded subclass.
Yet deciding whether the union covers the whole chart is coNP-complete: complement is signed four-color feasibility, in NP; zero shifts give ordinary 4-colorability, NP-complete by adding one universal vertex to an arbitrary 3-coloring instance. The reduction uses only bad affine events of codimension two and a rank-two punctured factor per graph edge, with polynomial encodings. Counting joint events cheaply does not supply a polynomial number of queries or a cheap aggregation theorem. No oracle lower bound against algorithms using full graph data.

## 3. High-girth critical neighboring family (PROVED)
For every integer g>=3 there is a finite simple graph with girth >=g and chromatic number at least five. This is the classical Erdős high-girth/high-chromatic theorem (source below). For completeness: choose a fixed 0<a<1/g, p=n^(a-1). The expected number of cycles of length <=g is O(n^(ag))=o(n). The probability of an independent set of size ceil(n/10) is at most 2^n exp(-p*t*(t-1)/2)=o(1). Thus for large n there exists a graph with fewer than n/2 short cycles and independence number <ceil(n/10); remove a vertex from each short cycle. The residual has >n/2 vertices and cannot be 4-colored (even four independent sets each <ceil(n/10) cover at most 4n/10). This proves existence without an imported construction.
Take an inclusion-minimal non-4-colorable subgraph G, deleting isolated vertices. It is connected, has chromatic number five (a 4-coloring of G-e can be extended to five colors by recoloring an endpoint), and every edge deletion is 4-colorable. Minimum degree >=4: otherwise color G-v with four colors, extend using an unused color. Every nontrivial cut has >=4 edges: both induced sides are 4-colorable by criticality, and one side can be translated by a common two-bit color. Each cross edge forbids at most one translation, so <=3 edges permit a shift and contradict non-4-colorability. This also reproves the I12 small-cut premise.
Use all-zero shifts for UNSAT instance U. For any edge e, change just its forbidden shift from 0 to ANY nonzero s (same vertices, edges, factor count, rank and row cardinalities). G-e has a proper 4-coloring; its endpoints on e must coincide, otherwise U would be SAT. The same word satisfies the modified factor. Conversely every legal word of the modified problem must have equal endpoints on e, since a word with unequal endpoints would be a proper 4-coloring of G. Thus the three signed SAT neighbors all have exactly the G-e color words, including a globally forced two-bit equality invisible to root affine row closure. Every original edge has three sign-only SAT neighbors.
As g tends to infinity, these are infinitely many graphs: finite graphs of minimum degree >=4 contain cycles and have girth at most n. Existence is not a claimed polynomial-time generation algorithm. A computable enumeration of all finite graphs with exhaustive label/criticality checks suffices for selecting examples, not for a normalization solver. Tests below do not claim to instantiate arbitrary g.

## 4. Full low-order law indistinguishability and sharp separating order (PROVED)
Every F with |F|<g is a forest. Its edge-difference coordinates are independent uniform two-bit values, since each assignment of these differences extends to exactly 4^kappa(F) vertex color words. Hence for ALL shifts and all F with |F|<g:
  Pr(all selected bad events)=4^(-|F|).
By inclusion-exclusion the whole joint 0/1 event law on F is the same independent Bernoulli(1/4) law, not just the all-bad intersection. This holds simultaneously for all such subsets; adaptive selection of subsets of bounded size does not help if only these laws are used. U and each one-edge sign neighbor have opposite feasibility but identical every labeled event marginal through order g-1. Consequently no universal decision or positive/negative coverage certificate based solely on these statistics and common graph geometry can distinguish the pair. The shifts themselves are in the encoded input; algorithms may use them, and this is not a lower bound against those algorithms.
Guard extension: fix a set P of relative edge differences to specified values (common legal rows on both compared problems). If |P|+|F|<g and F is disjoint from P, the union is a forest, so conditioned event law on F is still the same product law. In particular under at most g-2 relative edge pins, every remaining edge row extends the ambient affine consistency system, because the pinned edges plus that edge form a forest. The other unpinned factors have full affine hull. Thus complement-affine row closure yields no new deletion in this prescribed regime, even after closure is rerun after each relative pin. This is not a claim for arbitrary affine guard probes, which may explicitly test the changed shift, or for all possible semantic forced-information deductions. No guard-specific fact is exported unconditionally.
At root I12 unary/pair/triple saturation cannot progress: the cycle quotient Q has kernel equal to the cut space, and a dependence of <=3 scalar columns would be a nonempty cut of size <=3. All such column subsets are independent. Each individual edge plane is in the span of the others by a vertex-star cut containing that edge. Consequently every candidate row passes complement-affine closure, no full factor appears, and every <=3 factor subset has direct-sum planes with no repair dimension. These arguments apply to both signs.
Sharpness: in the one-edge twist pair, an arbitrary event subset has different all-bad counts iff it contains a cycle THROUGH the changed edge (all other shifts zero). Thus minimum distinguishing order is the length of the shortest cycle through that edge, obtained by BFS after deleting the edge. The first distinction is cheaply discoverable given the input, not a computational lower bound. A general signed graph may be handled by the equality query above. These results separate width of a correlation statistic from cost of identifying a useful subset.

## 5. Exact forest credit fails on every SAT neighbor (PROVED)
On the uniform vertex chart, for simple graphs any two distinct edges form a forest; individual event mass 1/4 and exact pair mass 1/16. For m bad events, any forest has at most m-1 edges, so even the maximum expected exact Hunter forest lower certificate on legal probability is
  C_exact <= 1-m/4+(m-1)/16 = (15-3m)/16.
It is nonpositive for m>=5, strictly negative on the critical family. M12 and MS12 valid pointwise/expected pair lower bounds are no larger than exact pair masses, so no forest of this form can certify the SAT neighbors at the root. This falsifies completeness, not soundness or the possibility of guarded repair, chart changes or more powerful joint certificates. Indeed the known deletion-color witness is a direct alternative. Root M12 or MS12 certificate can return UNKNOWN; neither may infer UNSAT from negative credit. A global semantic affine closure could find the forced equality on the SAT neighbor, but acquisition is not assumed free.

## 6. Constructive exact count/lift via cycle-space Fourier (PROVED)
Let kappa be the number of components, beta=m-n+kappa. Construct fundamental-cycle matrix Q (beta by m) using a spanning forest. A tuple d of two-bit relative edge colors is realizable by vertex colors iff Qd=0 (each bit separately). For any explicit allowed sets S_e subset F2^2, including singleton row guards or empty sets, let f_e=1[S_e], and unnormalized Walsh transform hat f_e(a)=sum_{d in S_e}(-1)^(a dot d).
Orthogonality yields
  Z_abs = 4^kappa / 4^beta * sum_{lambda in (F2^2)^beta} product_e hat f_e((Q^T lambda)_e).
Each compatible difference tuple has exactly 4^kappa absolute-color lifts. In the punctured case S_e=F2^2 minus {s_e}, hat f_e(0)=3, and for a!=0 it equals -(-1)^(a dot s_e). Formula remains valid for arbitrary explicit row sets; not a claim for missing protected observations. It is an exact signed sum: cancellation and integer division are handled at full precision; a vanishing single term or partial sum gives no semantic conclusion.
Discover spanning forest, basis and candidate row guards by graph/Gaussian operations. Before count, exhaust sound affine complement row deletion, consistency and empty-set checks; repeat after a row restriction or deletion. Reconstruct if Z_abs>0: for each edge not yet fixed, evaluate actual guarded counts with its allowed rows fixed in turn, select a positive guard and repeat closure. Preserve all prior relative row pins. At most 4m queries, then lift via tree traversal (one arbitrary root color per component), verify all original restrictions. This is an exact both-label solver; an I12 existential cut deletion is NOT count-preserving and cannot be used for this counting formula unless full multiplicities are included. Absolute affine guards may be expressed as additional authenticated relative constraints to an anchor only if the full observation is preserved; general absolute guard/count transfer remains separate.
With encoded input N and explicit rows, naive basis construction and every enumeration term use poly(N) bit work. At most 4^beta terms per query, intermediate signed count magnitude at most 4^beta 4^m, O(m+beta) bits; stream one term and accumulator. T<=4^beta poly(N), S<=poly(N) on same realization, including up to 4m witness queries, closure scans, failed guards, parsing, index/address costs, reconstruction, coefficient bits and source verification. CM<= (T+1)S, retained input included. Archive can be a polynomial list of edge pins, row deletions and actual positive-count decisions, no exponential table or speculative identity sharing. No numerical CM instrumentation claimed. Polynomial for beta=O(log N), but critical family beta>=n+1 (min degree 4), so not a universal escape. The signed flow expansion is standard Fourier orthogonality, no originality claim. Importantly cycle controls of arbitrary length have beta=1 even when marginal distinguishing order grows linearly.
