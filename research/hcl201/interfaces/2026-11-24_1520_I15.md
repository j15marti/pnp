## B. Branch tree and common-fiber join theorem (PROVED)
Supply a rooted binary tree on regions. At a node let A,B,C denote the column spaces of the left child, right child, and entire outside, respectively (A here is a space, not the observation matrix above). Store each partial table on its boundary I_J=U_J intersect U_out. DS08's exact join is h(z)=sum_{x+y=z} f(x)g(y), z in C. No independent marginal gluing; x,y are actual partial syndromes and all other correlations are represented in the authenticated input. Internal children have already enforced their local monitors. The root outside is 0 and h(0) is the exact count.

Let
  R={(x,y,z) in A×B×C : x+y+z=0},
  t=dim R=dim A+dim B+dim C-dim(A+B+C),
  D=A intersect B intersect C, d=dim D.
Let w_A=dim(A intersect (B+C)), with w_B,w_C analogous. Every pair of independent D shifts on x,y induces their sum shift on z. The resulting 2d-dimensional subspace of R is precisely the kernel of simultaneous quotienting all three entries modulo D. The quotient therefore has dimension t-2d.

Choose a linear splitting of the ambient quotient by Gaussian elimination (one common canonical remainder, not three unrelated coset representatives). For each quotient triple (xbar,ybar,zbar) in R/D^2, zbar=xbar+ybar. The associated contribution is the length-2^d XOR convolution
  h_bar(v) += sum_{u in D} f_bar(u) g_bar(v+u).
Compute this exactly by Walsh-Hadamard transform, pointwise multiplication, inverse transform and division by 2^d. Orthogonality of binary characters proves the convolution identity even for signed integers; exact division holds. Transform each child coset once, enumerate quotient triples from a kernel basis (not all pairs of cosets), accumulate output and discard zero entries only after exact arithmetic. Every original compatible pair belongs to exactly one quotient triple. A zero unsigned final count is an exact contradiction; a zero signed moment alone is not.

Input/output table cost is at most 2^(t-d) poly(N) because w_A=t-dim(B intersect C)<=t-d and similarly for w_B,w_C. Quotient enumeration and convolutions take 2^(t-2d) times 2^d poly(N). Thus join time <= 2^(t-d) poly(N), peak table+scratch space <= 2^max(w_A,w_B,w_C) poly(N). All rank calculations are polynomial and the output table includes all actual support, not just a marginal.

### Improved exponent in terms of separator width
Let i_AB=dim(A intersect B), etc. The three pairwise transfer spaces inside R, with tuples (u,u,0), (v,0,v), (0,s,s), together span a space of dimension i_AB+i_AC+i_BC-d: the only kernel of their sum is u=v=s in D. Therefore t>=i_AB+i_AC+i_BC-d. Projection onto A gives w_A=t-i_BC, and cyclically. Summing gives w_A+w_B+w_C>=2t-d, hence
  t-d <= (w_A+w_B+w_C-d)/2 <= 3w/2
where w=max(w_A,w_B,w_C).
In particular if A=B=C has dimension w, t=2w,d=w, and a 4^w pair join becomes a 2^w poly(N) exact convolution. If the pairwise spaces have independent sectors but no triple overlap, the 3w/2 bound can be attained (three pair sectors each rank w/2; each node boundary is two sectors). This is a worst-case bound for this join procedure, not a lower bound or optimality claim for all computation.

With a supplied tree and within-leaf orders, define u=max u_i and j=max(t-d) over nodes. Total exact count time <= poly(N) [sum_i K_i 2^u_i + sum_nodes 2^(t-d)] and space <= poly(N) [sum_i K_i 2^u_i + sum_nodes 2^w_node]. Thus for polynomial K_i and u,w=O(log N), deterministic polynomial. The nonlinear automata are evaluated separately, not multiplied across leaf groups.
