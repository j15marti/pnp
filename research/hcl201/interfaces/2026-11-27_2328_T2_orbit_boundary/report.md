## Exact theorem

Let F(z) be the chart under a=0, S=(a OR F(z)) AND (!a OR F(!z)) the checked projected root. A signed boundary guard g excludes a. Let -g mean flip every literal, and C_g the clause negating g. UP(F,g) conflicts iff UP(S,[-a,...g]) conflicts; similarly UP(F,-g) iff UP(S,[a,...g]) conflicts, because one copy reduces exactly to F and the other is satisfied. In consistent cases the forced assignments on z coincide after the appropriate complement. For a genuine root absolute guard g, feasibility is equivalent to SAT(F AND g) OR SAT(F AND -g). Thus one conflicting orbit orientation is insufficient evidence of root exclusion; both suffice. If the two UP conflicts have been obtained, add a OR C_g and !a OR C_g by RUP and then add C_g by RUP, since its negation produces a and !a. Exact source complement symmetry and complete incidence projection are prerequisites. This proof works equally after checked seeds are transported to both halves; no arbitrary CNF complement assumption. If UP(S,g) conflicts already, both anchored tests conflict by UP monotonicity. The converse is false in general and checked here. Cost of guard discharge is three explicit RUP rows per boundary exclusion, max width |g|+1; discovery of both orientations and source projection paid separately.

Rank-neutral operational admission: let q_C be the square-free Boolean vanishing polynomial for clause C, over F2, with anchor not in C. The indicators of a OR C and !a OR C are (1+a)q_C and aq_C. Their sum is q_C exactly. Once both guarded parent clauses are admitted, adding C does not increase the linear span rank of clause indicators. This does NOT imply propagation redundancy: take the two clauses a OR x OR y and !a OR x OR y, plus !y OR z and !y OR !z. Under x=0 no unit is present before the unguarded x OR y; afterward y=1 and z,!z conflict. This is an elementary proof, not an executed new canonical benchmark. Consequently a credit that counts only rank changes can miss productive operational admissions. This is not a lower bound for a complete portfolio; fixed-width admission dictionary separately pays each distinct clause once.
