\section{Two-stage common-mask acquisition is already a universal search problem (I23)}\label{sec:i23}
DR17 (Lemma~\ref{lem:dr17-mask}) replaces local plateau jumps by a common mask followed by a one-sided defect flip. We sharpen DS16 (Theorem~\ref{thm:ds16-one}): even restricting the requested successful move to this two-stage form and to one blocker per side does not supply an easy universal acquisition parameter.

\begin{lemma}[Private occurrence wire with two spare colors]\label{lem:i23-wire}
Every genuine positive linear four-perfect-partition NAE instance on $n$ variables can be compiled into a four-partial-partition registry on $81n$ variables with initially satisfied permanent wiring and one activatable owner per original owner. Every old word lifts to the permanent wiring; activated owners impose exactly the corresponding old constraints. Each new owner vertex has, before activation, exactly one wire owner of a different color and, after activation, two spare colors.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the affine plane gadget $G$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:ds16-slack}, deleting the color-zero lines $(0,3,6),(1,4,7)$. Each model of $G$ makes both removed lines monochromatic. They must have opposite values: if six vertices share one bit, the complete graph on nine vertices has at least $\binom62+\binom32=18$ monochromatic pairs, whereas exactly two bad owners would give $12+2(2)=16$. Attach external vertices $a,b$ in color zero by $(a,0,3),(b,1,4)$ respectively. Both attachments use vacant slots, the pairs were deleted, and the result enforces $a\ne b$. Both orientations lift using the explicit $G$ word and its complement. Relabel colors cyclically to put the attachments in any designated color $p$. 

For each original occurrence of vertex $v$ in color $p$, introduce private vertices $j,u$; use one such inequality gadget from $v$ to $j$ in color $p$, and another from $j$ to $u$ in color $p+1\bmod4$. Thus $u=v$. Replace that occurrence in its old owner by $u$, retaining color $p$ for the new owner. Original vertices have one attachment per color; $j$ has two distinct attachment colors; $u$ has one attachment and one activatable owner of a different color. Distinct copies are private and each external vertex meets fresh pairs, so linearity and partial partitions hold. There are $20$ fresh vertices per occurrence, hence $n+20(4n)=81n$, divisible by three. The constant-size gadget words give an explicit lift for every old word, and conversely all wire models satisfy the original equalities. Construction and verification are polynomial in encoded source length.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional equivalence for two-stage repair]\label{thm:i23-mask}
Suppose a fixed deterministic polynomially capped procedure, given a genuine four-perfect-partition instance, a pairing, two feasible S21 side words differing only at $d$, and a designated side $A$, acquires a verified common mask fixing $d$ followed by a flip of $d$ on $A$ whenever such a two-stage repair exists. On negative instances it may decline. Even when restricted to unguarded inputs with exactly one minority blocker at $d$ on each side, this guarantee implies $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$. Conversely $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$ supplies the guaranteed search on this domain by verification and NP witness search.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from the NP-complete genuine class (Darmann--D\"ocker--Dorn, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03395}{arXiv:2412.03395}). Compile by Lemma~\ref{lem:i23-wire}, maintain a word satisfying the activated prefix and permanent wires, and activate the old owners one at a time. If a new owner is already legal, continue. Otherwise it is monochromatic in its color $p$. Complete the partial instance by Lemma~\ref{lem:ds16-complete}, assigning all fillers by their fixed opposite-pair words. For each of the three actual private occurrence vertices $d$ of the new owner, set $q=p+2\bmod4$, designate $A=(p,q)$ and $B$ the other two colors. Let $y$ be the lifted old word, $x=y+e_d$. Side $A$ is legal after the flip, since the sole other $A$ owner at $d$ is a transparent filler; side $B$ is legal on $y$. At $d$ there is exactly one blocker on $A$ (the newly repaired owner) and one on $B$ (the permanent equality-wire attachment to two equal private terminals). Both fillers at $d$ have opposite auxiliary bits.

If the activated prefix is satisfiable, choose any satisfying old word. At least two vertices of its new owner are majority vertices. For either such candidate $d$, complement the old solution if necessary so its bit at the corresponding original vertex agrees with $y_d$. Lift through the permanent wires and the fixed completion to a common solution $z$. Both $z$ and $z+e_d$ are legal on $A$, since the new owner has $d$ as a majority and its other two bits differ, and the other $A$ owner is a filler. Then the single common mask $y+z$, zero at $d$, sends $(x,y)$ to $(z+e_d,z)$; the designated final flip yields $(z,z)$. At least two of the three designated queries therefore have a witness, even though neither their orientations nor their masks are supplied to the procedure. Any accepted mask is verified on the actual owners, guard, and paired circulation endpoints, then the common word is projected to the old variables. If all three decline, time out or return invalid outputs, the guarantee implies the current prefix is UNSAT, hence so is the original source. Conversely every accepted final common word is genuine, so no negative source is accepted. The old witness is updated only on verified success. At most $3m$ calls occur, and there is no hidden branching over alternative old models. NP witness search establishes the converse because the two-stage condition has a polynomial verifier. This is an adaptive Turing/search implication, not a many-one hardness assertion for a fixed paired registry or for the double-blocker subdomain.
\end{proof}
