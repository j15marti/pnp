## 2. Graphic gluing theorem
For every explicitly encoded independent I04 factor family, under a fixed available guard in each factor, local attainable tags are exactly a_i + D_i 1_{J_i}, with J_i an arbitrary subset of its own edge coefficients with sum w_e <= K_i. All w_e, K_i are nonnegative binary integers. D_i has two ones in distinct bit rows per edge. Each hub j enforces XOR of all its two-bit incident contributions equal to c_j. A degree-two equality hub with no additional masks is the zero-target XOR case. Higher degree equality or extra arbitrary masks are NOT included. No missing shared occupancy or tag constraints permitted. Physical occupancy can be encoded only where the I01 exact splitting/graphic support promises apply. No strict-wheel compiler asserted.

Identify the row vertices representing the same hub bit across factors. Give every edge a distinct ID and retain its owner i and cost. Concatenate columns. Each column is still incidence weight two, or zero if endpoints identify. With t = c + xor_i a_i, the global problem is exactly:
  choose J subset E with D 1_J = t and for every i sum_{e in J, owner(e)=i} w_e <= K_i.
Proof: the linear identification map implements exactly the hub XOR equations; owners retain all local costs and independent choices. Both directions preserve the coefficient tuple with no enumeration of hub colors. Parallel edges are distinct coefficients. Loops have zero boundary and may be omitted for decision/witness under nonnegative upper budgets. They may not be omitted for full support, counts or protected feature identities. ## 3. Free-edge contraction
First remove edges individually heavier than their owner's available budget, a certified forced-zero deduction. Let F consist of zero-cost edges and all eligible edges of any owner whose total eligible cost is <= its budget. Every subset of F is safe: an owner either has only zero-cost free edges, or is wholly nonbinding. Contract connected components of (V,F) and let q be the map that sums parity bits within each component. The image of D_F is exactly ker q (forest leaf elimination proves both inclusion and surjection). For paid edges E-F, D' = q D is the graph incidence of the contracted multigraph, with some loops. Therefore
  exists J_F: D_F 1_{J_F}+D_paid 1_{J_paid}=t
  iff D' 1_{J_paid}=q t.
Every paid solution lifts by forest leaf elimination on F for the residual target. All original costs are valid, because the free budget condition is independent of the paid choices. Drop quotient loops only for decision, since choosing none weakly improves its owner cost. Keep original edge IDs and endpoints for reconstruction. This is true quotient absorption, not an invertible gauge; contraction may combine physically remote hubs, and the original factor incidence feedback count need not decrease under a physical rewrite. It can be bypassed in the exact decision model.

## 4. Cycle-space block product and simultaneous budget minimum
For an undirected multigraph G after contraction, let B range over equivalence classes of edges generated by membership in a common simple cycle (parallel pairs are 2-cycles; bridges singleton). Compute a spanning forest, and union edge IDs along each fundamental cycle. This yields a sufficient edge partition with every binary cycle vector split into vectors in the block kernels: the fundamental cycles span ker D, and each is wholly inside a single computed block. Conversely every block-kernel vector extends by zero. No oracle or optimal decomposition required. A straightforward implementation takes O(mn) path work; isolated vertices included.

If any graph component has odd target parity, global UNSAT by the component-sum equation. Otherwise forest leaf elimination gives an explicit baseline join J0. For every feasible join J, J xor J0 is in ker D. Therefore
  D_B 1_{J intersect B} = D_B 1_{J0 intersect B} = t_B
and conversely any independent choices of t_B-joins in the blocks combine to a global t-join. Note that articulation vertices may appear in several blocks but the local target parity at each such vertex is FIXED by J0, not a guessed color. It is independent of baseline choice. This is an exact product of coefficient relations before applying the owner budgets.

Suppose every block is monochromatic by budget owner, not by hub color. Write i(B) for that owner. Independently compute minimum cost m_B of a t_B-join in each block. Then:
  SAT iff for every i, sum_{B:i(B)=i} m_B <= K_i.
Necessity by restricting any witness. Sufficiency by joining block minima; no tradeoff between budgets. The same owner may appear in arbitrarily many blocks; we sum its costs, never allocate budget independently per block. Use deterministic nonnegative shortest paths plus minimum-weight perfect matching on odd terminals. A join decomposes into terminal paths and cycles, so its cost is at least the minimum terminal pairing distance. Conversely XOR of shortest paths of an optimum pairing is a join of no greater cost; equality follows by the first inequality. This is the standard Edmonds-Johnson route. No exponential factor in local syndrome rank, local graph cycle rank, number of owners or original incidence feedback count.

Pure blocks can be optimized and removed even in the presence of mixed blocks: their parity is fixed and replacing them by minima cannot harm any upper budget. This is decision-preserving, not an all-witness reduction; a chosen minimum must not be promoted as a logical forced assignment. A singleton bridge, by contrast, has a genuinely forced coefficient given its cut target parity. Infeasible budget after summing minima is a certified contradiction (a checkable min matching optimum certificate or a recomputation with charged costs), guarded by the original guard tuple if conditional.

## 5. Paid adaptive mixed-cycle terminal
Let p = sum_B (|B| - max_i |B intersect E_i|). For a mixed block choose a non-majority-owner edge e. Branch on its coefficient 0/1, delete it, subtract cost and toggle its two endpoints if selected. Before a further split, rerun negative-budget/overweight/component parity closure, eliminate forced bridges, optimize and remove pure blocks, recompute the residual partition. All deductions preserve scope; conditional deductions remain guarded, and optimized representatives are not forced logical values.

Under edge deletion each new cycle block lies within an old one, because every new cycle was already a cycle. For the old block choose its original majority i. For all its child blocks the sum of minority counts is at most the number of surviving non-i edges, whether or not i remains their majority. Deleting e strictly decreases this bound by one. All other blocks do not increase their contribution. Thus every actual binary branching reduces p at least one. Other edge removals only decrease or preserve p. Contract free edges once before parameter measurement; do not silently re-contract later and assume p monotonic. Hence at most 2^p leaves, polynomial closure per path. Deterministic T <= 2^p poly(N), polynomial peak space via DFS, with an explicit polynomial discovery procedure. ## 6. Sharp boundary: two budgets already encode PARTITION
Take positive binary integers a_1,...,a_h with sum 2B. At each of h disjoint pairs of vertices put two parallel edges, one per owner, both weight a_j. Every vertex target is 1. Each pair must select exactly one of its two edges; each owner has budget B. The sum of both costs is 2B, so feasibility is exactly a partition of the weights into equal halves. Each owner's columns may be distinct within that factor. Encode each vertex pair as the two bits of one four-color hub, two factors incident at every hub, offsets zero, fixed guard, target 11. This is a polynomial many-one reduction and NP membership by coefficient certificate, so the explicit binary-weight two-budget graphic XOR problem is NP-complete. The reduction is from PARTITION; do not infer strong hardness of the full two-budget problem or unit-cost hardness. It has rank-one cycle blocks, yet all are mixed and p=h. Mere bounded block rank does not solve coupled budgets. Each independently minimized owner cost is zero (choose other owner everywhere); even a total budget test passes. Thus arbitrary budget pooling or independent per-owner minimization cannot replace compatibility.
