## PROVED: independent port-gauge obstruction
Over C, a planar arity-four matchgate signature has a fixed support parity. Its even identity is D=f0000*f1111-f1100*f0011+f1010*f0101-f1001*f0110=0 (Cai/Fu arXiv:1305.1409, Lemma 2.4); the odd identity is its bit-flipped form. Nonzero diagonal row scalings multiply D by a common nonzero factor. Individual bit flips preserve the class, directly by adding a pendant external vertex and making the old port internal (or by the parity identities). Thus they preserve violation as well.

B4 is the tensor with entries 1 at weight two, 0 elsewhere. Suppose g=(T1 tensor ... tensor T4)B4 is parity-pure, Ti invertible. Let Z=diag(1,-1), Pi=Ti^{-1} Z Ti. Then (tensor Pi) B4=±B4. For every two-versus-two flattening the image on the chosen pair is precisely Sym^2(C^2), as seen from the three independent row types 00,01+10,11. Thus Pi tensor Pj preserves Sym^2 for every i,j. Applying to v tensor v shows Pi v and Pj v proportional for every v, whence Pj=±Pi (each has eigenvalues ±1). Write P=Pi up to these signs. Consequently P^{tensor 4}B4=±B4. In the symmetric power polynomial representation B4 is a nonzero scalar times x^2 y^2. Its only two projective roots are the coordinate axes; P must permute them. A traceless involution is therefore either diagonal ±Z or antidiagonal [[0,t],[1/t,0]], t nonzero.

In the diagonal case every Ti is monomial and reduces to the original B4 after output flips and rescaling. D(B4)=-1. In the antidiagonal case every Ti reduces, by the same allowed output operations, to the common eigenbasis [[1,t],[1,-t]]; its action yields even signature t^2[6,0,-2,0,6] (listed by Hamming weight). D=32t^4, nonzero. All port orders are covered by symmetry of B4. Hence no four independent invertible complex bases turn B4 into an exact planar matchgate. A signed B4 is just an input bit flip absorbed into Ti.

For X, after any independent bases it remains A(a,c)B(b,d) with both 2x2 factors nonsingular (originally I,I). If total parity is fixed, each factor must have fixed parity since its nonzero entries form an independent Cartesian product with those of the other factor. Therefore each factor is diagonal or antidiagonal. Output flips make both diagonal; four nonzero entries then have a common nonzero complementary product p. In actual cyclic port order a,b,c,d the even identity is D=2p, never zero. This excludes arbitrary invertible product gauges of BW24's exact crossed equality, even with signed/complex matchgate weights. A direct signed repair exists: set X(1111)=-1 to obtain a matchgate F, then X=F+2 delta1111; the delta is also a matchgate. The fermionic sign is an actual correction, not a free gauge. Arbitrary reweighting of support, nonlocal grouping, enlarged bond dimensions, different representations and decision-only algorithms are NOT excluded. In particular B4-only decision can be easy even with this obstruction.

## PROVED: exact Fourier-pinned count and optimal pin count in this scheme
For a closed simple planar native graph with b B4 vertices, W wires, choose the same unnormalized H=[[1,1],[1,-1]] on every port. H^T H=2I; a signed transport X maps to Z, so the original count is 2^{-W} times the transformed signed contraction. E4 becomes [2,0,2,0,2] (matchgate); B4 becomes [6,0,-2,0,6] (not). Choose a set P of wires incident to every B4 and sum over their Fourier values. Each B4 loses at least one free port and the remaining tensor is parity-pure arity <=3, hence a matchgate. Partially evaluated E4 remains a matchgate by the same parity/identity characterization. The unpinned network is planar; unary sign weights preserve this. For each pin word evaluate its exact planar matchgate contraction by a constant-size rational gadget per tensor and a Pfaffian; sum and divide by 2^W. This is exact for native counts (not necessarily original-source counts if adapter lifts are nonunique), and detects zero. These are Fourier pins, not forced original bits; no source witness follows from one summand.

The minimum number of such wires is k=b-nu, where nu is maximum matching size of the induced B4 subgraph. Construct by maximum matching then one incident wire per uncovered B4. For any cover, assign each B4 to one selected incident wire; wires assigned two B4 form a matching, and counting assignments gives |P|>=b-nu. This is the exact minimum for the local-cover scheme, not arbitrary Pfaffian algorithms. Discovery polynomial; count time 2^k poly(N) bit operations, polynomial serial peak space and CM <=(T+1)S. Pin words generated serially; coefficients of constant size, intermediate exact numerator bit length O(V+W), standard exact Pfaffian arithmetic polynomial. k>=ceil(b/2), so this scheme cannot guarantee a universal polynomial cap by native planarization.
