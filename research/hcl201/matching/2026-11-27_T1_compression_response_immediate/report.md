## Exact theorem
For a Boolean factor registry F and variable v, let B be the complete set of live incident factors. Under the exact current guard, replace B by R(b)=exists v conjunction(B), where b is the union scope minus v. This is exact because factors outside B do not involve v. A witness choice v for each admitted boundary tuple is sufficient for SAT reconstruction. If a protected observation depends on v, retain it as an explicit factor/interface, or do not eliminate v. No count or weighted objective preservation is claimed for this Boolean support implementation. A dictionary key is the entire ordered normalized local incidence pattern and exact allowed-word masks, with eliminated coordinate distinguished. A checked bijection taking one complete bucket to another preserves R and witness choices; validate complete key bytes and all occurrence maps, never a digest alone. For K occurrences, D distinct keys, max total scope w, explicit truth-table response work is O((D*2^w+K) poly(N)) with charged acquisition of each exact key, verification once per entry and per-use map, and all failed scans. It is not a uniform bound on w, D, or global solver cost. Repeated dictionary entries can be shared across all verified instances of a region, but guards, source owners and reverse maps remain separate. General f-factor/blossom/weighted terminal sparsifier was not implemented.

Counterexample to loss of joint matching response: graphs A={(0,1),(2,3)}, B={(0,2),(1,3)} on four terminals have equal individual deletion marginals, equal attainable deletion-cardinality profiles (0,2,4), equal counts at each cardinality. Yet deleting {0,1} admits a perfect matching in A and none in B. Executed exact matching recursion gives support masks A [0,3,12,15], B [0,5,10,15]. Thus these summaries cannot replace the exact occupancy interface. This is a scoped obstruction, not a general sparsifier lower bound or P != NP.
