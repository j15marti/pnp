## Lemma 1 (exact unilateral distance-one neighborhood)
Let A,B be two pairs of perfect partitions of a positive linear triple registry, x feasible on A, y feasible on B, x XOR y=e_d. Both satisfy the declared coordinate pins. Let K_A be the A owners incident to d in which x_d is the unique minority; K_B analogously for y. A nonidentity unilateral A move remaining at Hamming distance at most one from fixed y must flip d alone or flip d and one j != d. This follows by writing x'=y or x'=y XOR e_j. Thus it is impossible when |K_A|=2: both blockers would become monochromatic after d is flipped, and their other two coordinates are disjoint by linearity. B is symmetric. If both have two blockers, the distance-one state is isolated under ALL one-sided nonincreasing moves, even with exact min-cost circulation and arbitrary optimal tie selection. The two original words each fail both opposite partitions, so simply reusing them as feasible words for another pairing is not valid. New words or nonlocal rewrites remain possible. This is a finite counterexample to universal unilateral progress, not a complexity lower bound.
