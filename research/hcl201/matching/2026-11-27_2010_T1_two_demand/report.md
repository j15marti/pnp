## Scoped theorem: bounded-width two-demand Hall simulation

Let F contain literal-disjoint demand clauses A union P and B union Q, with nonempty A,B and each cross pair a in A,b in B either opposite literals or explicitly forbidden by the binary clause (not a or not b). Then C=P union Q is necessary. If C is non-tautological and |C|<=2, fixed-chart saturated width-two RUP subsumes this cut, or has already derived the empty clause.

Proof: disjoint signed-literal demands imply |P|+|Q|<=2. WLOG |Q|<=1. For each a in A, learn D_a=(not a) union Q: assuming a and every literal of Q false, every b in B is false by the direct conflict clauses (or Boolean complement), contradicting B union Q by unit propagation. D_a has width <=2. If D_a is tautological (a in Q), no addition is required; a is already false under not Q. (Under literal-disjoint demands this particular overlap cannot occur.) Under not C, each D_a forces not a, and A union P is contradicted. Therefore C is RUP after these at most |A| intermediate additions, and all additions are from the fixed width-two candidate universe. Every UP test and clause admission is charged; with fixed maximum original row width there are O(1) additions per proposed pair. No new affine credit for a cut already present. This is an implication between inference systems, not proof of universal acquisition. Exact response compression: the cross-conflict rectangle suffices for the *single necessary clause*, not the full joint support, matching cost or witness lifting. For >=3 demand rows, general Hall deficiency is not covered by this simulation and is the next receiving-track target.
