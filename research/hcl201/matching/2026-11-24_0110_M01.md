## 2. Complete deterministic affine closure from Lovasz
KNOWN THEOREM: El Maalouly, Steiner, Wulf, Exact Matching: Correct Parity and FPT Parameterized by Independence Number, ISAAC 2023, arXiv:2207.09797v2, Section 4, Theorems 19 and 20, Corollary 22. Theorem 20 cites Lovasz's polynomial algorithm for the linear span over any fixed finite field of perfect-matching incidence vectors. Theorem 19 provides deterministic polynomial decision AND search for one prescribed parity of red edges in arbitrary simple graphs. Verified actual theorem and proof pages 47:14–16, not just abstract. This is a known algorithm, not a claimed new solution to general exact matching.

Let S={sum_{e in M} g_e : M perfect}. First ordinary matching checks S nonempty. To compute its affine hull, add a disjoint forced edge f with tag (0,1), augment all old labels to (g_e,0), and apply Theorem 20 over GF(2) to the augmented graph. Map the returned incidence basis through the label matrix. Its span U is exactly span{(s,1):s in S}. Therefore the slice {(s,1) in U} is exactly aff(S), not merely an upper bound. Pick ordinary perfect matching M0, a=syndrome(M0), and solve for a basis of V={v:(v,0) in U}. Then aff(S)=a+V. Gaussian elimination outputs every affine consequence w.s=w.a for w in a basis of V^perp, and c outside this hull is certified impossible by a separating parity. No need for randomized Pfaffian filtering or exponential target queries for this affine step. Computing this hull is polynomial for arbitrary tag rank r. A second proof is GD-01's witness-backed hull extraction with CPM as the exact single-direction query: select w annihilating current span, color edges by w.g_e and ask for the opposite parity; any returned witness increases span, at most r successful rounds, <=r(r+1) CPM calls. Witness lifting is paid. Lovasz basis need not consist of actual matchings, so use CPM search for real representatives when needed. The latter can be implemented by repeated edge deletion using parity decision (Section 4 of the cited paper). If the surviving syndrome affine dimension is zero or one, S=aff(S) (there are only one or two points, both witnessed by definition), so this is a deterministic exact terminal solver including witness recovery. General rank>=2 does not imply exact support.

For GD-01: with fixed occupancy at a boundary, delete vertices stipulated unmatched locally; require perfect matching on all remaining vertices including occupied ports and internal vertices. The label projection can encode any required linear form of edge-incidence contributions. Ordinary matching gives exact feasibility for occupancy-only predicates, and the present method is exact for tag constraints when the effective syndrome rank is <=1 or the peeling below closes. An arbitrary boundary predicate with several interacting syndrome bits still needs an exact support algorithm; the affine hull is a necessary-only message otherwise. To preserve exposed observations across a projection, include them in the retained label map and only eliminate directions whose images vanish on those protected observations, or perform the test separately for fixed observations. Do not quotient away a boundary direction just because it is globally satisfiable. This is the matching-side analogue of GD-01's protected Rado span.

## 3. Factor-component affine peeling theorem
Compute allowed edges: e=uv is allowed iff G-{u,v} has a perfect matching. At most |E| ordinary matching tests; remove all other edges. Connected components C_i of this allowed-edge graph partition the vertex set (assuming a PM exists). Every PM uses only allowed edges and is a disjoint union of PMs in C_i; conversely any such union is a PM. Each component has even order. This is a paid exact decomposition even for a physically connected original graph. Let S_i be its syndrome support, a_i a syndrome of a chosen ordinary PM, V_i=span(S_i+a_i), obtained deterministically as above. Global support is sum_i S_i. Initially W=0, a=0, R=all components. Invariant:
S = a + W + sum_{i in R} S_i,
with removed components realizing EXACTLY a+W, and with a constructive representation using their actual matching choices.

If dim((V_i+W)/W) <= 1, then in the quotient F/W the image of S_i is its entire affine hull: a singleton or two points. Thus
W+S_i = a_i + W+V_i.
Remove i; set a<-a+a_i and W<-W+V_i. This identity is exact independent of overlaps between the other components. In particular, a component of initially large syndrome rank can become rank one after previous absorptions. Repeat to fixed point, renewing unconditional affine closure after every deduction; the removed count decreases, so at most h eliminations and O(h^2) component rank tests. Scan residual components again whenever W grows; do not branch on their joint assignments. For rank-zero elimination retain a baseline matching. For rank-one elimination choose an actual second matching with quotient difference d_i outside W, using a single CPM query with a separating linear direction on the quotient. The pair {a_i,a_i+d_i} plus prior W generates every coset in the newly enlarged W; their two actual matching witnesses are enough. Induction: the sum of selected alternatives of removed components equals a+W and they are mutually vertex-disjoint. The chosen d_i are independent in insertion order. If all components peel, solve c+a=sum_i b_i d_i by Gaussian elimination, recover one matching per component, unite and verify on original graph. If c outside the full affine hull, reject at any stage. Otherwise an unpeeled core is OPEN for this method, not SAT. For a residual exact solver, project remaining tags and target c+a modulo W, solve there, and lift its returned syndrome to c using stored matching pairs. This is a deterministic reduction with no uncharged discovery or global assignment enumeration. It preserves decision/witness, not counts.

Define eta=dim(span of projected remaining V_i). If the residual target lies outside its affine span, reject. Otherwise shift by baseline residual syndrome and restrict labels to that span using an extension of a basis (coordinates outside it sum to a constant on PMs, so can be removed after verification of the hull). HCL-196's Fourier-Pfaffian method now uses at most 2^eta polynomial work, with its original one-sided error; eta can be much smaller than raw dual defect nu. In particular complete peeling is deterministic polynomial even with unbounded global syndrome rank. Additional deterministic option: if each remaining component has an efficiently constructed Pfaffian orientation, Fourier character sums of signed-weight Pfaffians with unit magnitude recover exact syndrome counts, since the original oriented Pfaffian terms have the same sign and the label character multiplies each term by its syndrome sign. No random evaluation needed in that class. For each local target the integer sum divided by 2^r yields signed count; compare to zero and reconstruct via edge deletion, recomputing or retaining the valid orientation on PM-preserving subgraphs. Bit sizes polynomial in N plus rank (counts <= |E|^{|V|/2}); exact rational arithmetic via polynomial-bit elimination. This option was not implemented this run. No claim quotient gadgets preserve planarity or that an arbitrary Pfaffian orientation is efficiently discoverable. For general remaining components, eta<=1 is deterministic CPM, eta=O(log N) currently only the stated randomized route unless a further derandomization applies.

## 4. Deterministic bipartite realization without Lovasz implementation
For bipartite G with ordinary PM M0, index matched pairs (u_i,v_i). Contract each pair to vertex i and encode edge u_i v_j by directed arc i->j labeled g_ij+g_ii. Relabel if needed to make M0 diagonal. A perfect matching corresponds to a permutation of these pairs; its nontrivial permutation cycles are vertex-disjoint directed cycles. The matching difference in syndrome is exactly their arc-label XOR. Every directed simple cycle is individually flippable. Thus the span of directed simple-cycle labels equals V, and SCCs are exactly the allowed-edge factors in contracted coordinates; cross-SCC arcs cannot belong to a permutation cycle.

In each SCC choose root, directed paths root->i and i->root for all i. Generate closed walks root->i->j->root for every arc, and root->i->root for every i. If p_i is the label of root->i, the difference of first walk for arc i->j and second walk for j is p_i+g'_ij+p_j. These reduced arc labels sum to the original labels on every closed cycle by telescoping, so generated walks span all cycle labels. Decompose each generated closed walk into simple directed cycles using a stack, keeping the actual cycle witnesses and their labels. Their span is exact (each is a real cycle). At most polynomially many walks/cycles of polynomial length, BFS and GF(2) elimination; no enumeration of all cycles. In projected rank one, choose any stored simple cycle with nonzero quotient label, flip it against M0 for the second matching. This is the algorithm executed below. It works for a supplied base PM; finding one on an arbitrary bipartite input is ordinary deterministic matching with polynomial cost. Empty/no-PM input handled by that initial check; the random generator supplies a base PM.
