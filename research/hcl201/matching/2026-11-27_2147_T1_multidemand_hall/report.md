## Exact claim
Let demand clauses D_i be pairwise disjoint as sets of signed literals. Partition their union into bins such that every pair of distinct literals within a bin is either complementary or has an explicit checked negative-pair clause. Build bipartite incidence demand->bin. Any satisfying assignment selects one true literal per demand; these selections must be distinct and occupy distinct bins. A Hall-deficient demand subset S, with |Gamma(S)|<|S|, certifies UNSAT. Under a common exact guard the same certificate rejects that guard; lift only via a checked guard discharge. This is a sound outer matching relaxation, not an exact general SAT reduction. A matching witness need not lift. Discovery after a partition is standard augmenting paths; greedy partition/candidate acquisition is incomplete. At most polynomial explicit bit operations for fixed algorithm (naive loops, comparisons and graph searches; no semantic oracle), with all parsing/checking included. No universal coverage claim.

For functional PHP(n+1,n), n>=4, width<=2 RUP is saturated initially. A consistent assignment of at most two assumed literals either violates an explicit binary exclusion (already a clause) or unit propagation sets only pair-exclusion neighbors of positive assumptions false. Every unassigned pigeon retains >=n-2 choices: each positive assumption excludes at most one hole of that pigeon; each negative assumption excludes at most one candidate; a pigeon with a positive assignment has its positive demand satisfied. Hence no other unit follows, and no conflict. Opposite assumptions are tautologies. This proves no new non-taut width<=2 RUP clause beyond the original binary exclusions, at any number of iterations. The n+1 disjoint demands and n hole cliques give an O(n^3) literal-pair certificate, independently checkable by integer count. For a guard literal g fresh and added to every demand, the same argument shows the unit g is outside width-two RUP closure (n>=4); assuming not g gives the original PHP, and the Hall certificate exports g. This is an analytic variant, not a newly registered or executed instance. No general lower bound on unrestricted resolution or SAT deciders.
