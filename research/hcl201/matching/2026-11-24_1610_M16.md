## A. Certified tight shores (PROVED)
Input: loopless planar multigraph G with edge IDs, perfect matching target, integer quota edge weights (fixed number k, bounded numeric magnitudes after M14/M15 when applicable), binary tags in F2^q, and supplied pairwise disjoint nonempty connected odd shores S_i. Check graph/embedding, IDs, odd cardinality, connectivity, disjointness, ordinary perfect matching feasibility. A shore is tight iff every perfect matching crosses its cut exactly once. Since every matching crosses an odd shore an odd positive number of times, maximize the sum of cut-edge indicators by ordinary weighted perfect matching. The optimum is 1 iff tight. A primal witness plus an exact blossom-dual bound of 1 certifies it. Otherwise decline this normalization, not UNSAT. Charge one matching optimization per shore. Force/exclude edges by deleting competing edges, keeping original vertex and shore registry; original certified tightness persists in any nonempty restricted matching domain. Run ordinary matching coordinate and M13 affine parity closure before tagged processing; if a pin is learned, repeat closure, preserve the guard. Keep original embedding as topological witness even if edge weights become zero. No pin under a target or additional observation is exported to a relaxed graph.

Contract each connected shore to one vertex, delete its internal edges from the skeleton, retain crossing-edge identities. The skeleton is planar by connected contraction; parallel edges allowed. Every original perfect matching restricts to a skeleton matching with exactly one cut edge e at each S_i; its local matching is a perfect matching of G[S_i] minus the endpoint of e. Conversely any skeleton matching and independently chosen such local matchings glue to an original matching. This is a bijection of graph matchings with these indexed tuples. In particular local counts must depend on the actual selected cut edge (or its actual endpoint if no extra guard distinguishes IDs), not merely its existence. The cut edge's weight/tag is included ONCE, in the skeleton. Regions can be adjacent: an edge between two contracted vertices selects both local endpoints. Retain both tables and multiply them, never duplicate that edge contribution.

## B. Private-syndrome quotient (PROVED)
Let U_0 span the skeleton edge tags and U_i span internal edge tags of S_i, in the original F2^q registry. Define
 W = U_0 + sum_i [ U_i intersect (U_0 + sum_{j != i, j>=1} U_j) ].
All spaces computed by Gaussian elimination, polynomial in encoded graph/tag size. Let V_i be a complement to U_i intersect W in U_i. Then the images of V_i in the quotient by W form a direct sum. Proof: if sum_i u_i in W, write that sum as w_0+sum_i w_i, with w_0 in U_0 and w_i in the displayed intersection for i. Then each u_i+w_i lies in U_i and the span of all other U_j plus U_0, hence in W. Thus each u_i in W. Consequently W plus the direct sum of the V_i spans the complete tag space U_0+sum U_i. Choose and retain actual complementary basis vectors, not just dimensions.
If target t outside total span, certified UNSAT. Otherwise uniquely write t=w+sum v_i. Every original matching must have private component v_i in S_i; no other region or skeleton can repair a different component. This is an unconditional necessary identity relative to the declared target. Conversely imposing each local private target and shared sum w is sufficient. No enumeration of all private target tuples. For fixed endpoint e compute local transfer T_i,e(a,h) = number of local graph matchings with private projection v_i, shared W tag a, integer quota contribution h. These are actual simultaneous coefficients. A zero table deletes that selector edge in the contracted instance (under this target only). Reclose after every such deduction, with zero weights or equivalent edge deletions; retain local constraints and source IDs. Optional tighter choice of affine difference spaces requires separate offset accounting; raw linear spans above suffice.

## C. Weighted skeleton exactification (PROVED)
Work in R=Z[F2^r][z_1^+-1,...,z_k^+-1], r=dim W, with Laurent monomials for exact integer quotas. For each skeleton edge e, let
 P_e = z^{h_e} [g_e] product_{i incident contracted shore} (sum_{a,h} T_i,e(a,h) z^h [a]).
Ordinary uncontracted endpoints contribute 1. By A/B,
 [w,z^target] sum_{M_s perfect} product_{e in M_s} P_e
 equals the exact number of original GRAPH matchings satisfying tags and quotas. Nonnegative coefficients; zero iff impossible. No source-count claim through M15 nonparsimonious gadgets. This combines S15's mixed group coefficients with a matching selector skeleton, rather than explicit Cartesian joining of all local tables.
For planar graph, choose sign-coherent Pfaffian orientation and reference perfect matching to determine common sign. Delete unmatched endpoint locally; inherited planar embedding suffices to acquire local orientations separately. For local tables compute exact tag/quota coefficients via Walsh characters on U_i, radix encoding with safe nonnegative shifts; use M15 exact algorithm or an independently verified equivalent. Filter private projection and translate to W. For skeleton, evaluate P_e at each Walsh character on W and at integer radix base for quota coordinates, then compute the signed Pfaffian with these scalar weights. All character terms correspond to same weighted graph partition function; normalize common sign, inverse Walsh BEFORE digit extraction. For negative weights/quotas use a common exponent shift per edge chosen, since every skeleton matching has same edge count. For local P_e, weight exponent bounds include all internal contributions represented by that edge; pad every skeleton-edge polynomial by the same offset. Mixed radix exponents encode each coordinate without alias when chosen larger than complete shifted range. Choose base > total number of original matchings (2^m bound for m original edges); intermediate signed evaluations may be negative, but final coefficients nonnegative. Exact arithmetic and bit length charged. Empty skeleton or no shores handled by identity or M15 as appropriate.
If graph size/coefficients are N-polynomial, k constant, max local tag rank c=O(log N), and r=O(log N), the full tagged/quotad decision is deterministic polynomial, even if q and sum of private ranks are linear. More explicitly T <= (sum_i |delta(S_i)| 2^{dim U_i} + 2^r) poly(N) for one acquisition/decision, including dense quota table range product and polynomial radix/Pfaffian arithmetic; polynomial factor includes processing all leaf table terms and skeleton edge polynomials. Peak space <= (2^c+2^r) poly(N) on sequential workspace reuse, including all retained local tables, maps and certificates. CM <= (T+1)S on same bit execution. This does not promise near-linear polynomial; it removes the global 2^q Walsh factor. ## E. Acquisition beyond supplied shores (partial, no general discovery claim)
Classical tight-cut decomposition is not itself new. To find one nontrivial tight cut of a connected matching-covered graph, deterministically acquire a rational affine basis of matching incidence vectors. Start with one matching, maintain span of differences, optimize min/max along a rational basis of its annihilator; whenever objective varies, add an actual new matching, restart. At most m increases; polynomial weighted blossom queries and bit lengths (rational linear algebra on 0/1 rows). Average the resulting affine spanning matchings. Every odd-cut inequality is >=1, and equality at this average iff equality on every matching (nonnegative slack plus affine spanning). The average is a genuine relative-interior certificate for these inequalities. Merely averaging one witness per allowed edge need not detect all non-tight cuts, so do not substitute it.
Minimum T-odd cut separation alone does not give a nontrivial tight cut, since singleton cuts already have weight 1. Constraining both shores to have at least three vertices requires a separate algorithm; none is imported here. Thus the relative-interior certificate is proved, but automatic nontrivial-cut discovery is not established by this argument. Classical tight-cut algorithms may supply it separately. Automatic acquisition of a disjoint family with small shared rank remains open regardless.
Alternative simple exact discovery under constant shore size: enumerate all odd subsets of size <=fixed b, check connectivity and weighted tightness, greedily retain disjoint successful candidates. Polynomial for fixed b; no guarantee of finding optimal family or terminal caps. This suffices to acquire the size-five control shores in principle.
