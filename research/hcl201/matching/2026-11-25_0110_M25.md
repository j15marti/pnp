\begin{lemma}[Certified Hall projection]\label{lem:m25-hall}
Let the live selector rows be partitioned into buckets, each containing pairwise incompatible rows, where different rows of the same owner count as incompatible. Form a bipartite graph from owners to buckets incident to their rows. Every legal transversal induces a matching saturating the owners. A Hall deficiency certifies UNSAT; a deficiency after restricting an owner to a single incident bucket certifies deletion of every row of that owner in that bucket. The converse need not hold. A supplied or deterministically greedily acquired partition is polynomially verifiable, including all pair conflicts and the Hall neighbor set. No maximum clique oracle is used.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A transversal uses one row per owner and cannot use two rows in a bucket. Thus it maps injectively into buckets. A deficient set $X$ with $|N(X)|<|X|$ precludes any such map. Restricting an owner to a bucket covers every candidate row of that owner in the bucket and yields the stated guarded certificate; a contradiction under that guard entails the corresponding unconditional deletion in the current chart. Failed augmenting-path search supplies the reachable owners and buckets as a Hall witness. Positive matching solutions are only relaxation witnesses; actual row pair checks and source lift remain mandatory. This is the classical all-different matching filter of Regin (AAAI 1994) applied to authenticated conflict buckets rather than a predeclared value domain.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[All conflict-clique partitions stall on raw linear NAE]\label{lem:m25-barrier}
For every unpinned signed linear 3-uniform NAE registry with three distinct variables per owner and no additional pair exclusions, assign weight $1/6$ to each of its six rows. Every conflict clique has total weight at most one. Consequently every partition into conflict cliques has an owner-saturating fractional matching with strictly positive flow on every incident owner-bucket edge. Bipartite matching integrality implies every such edge extends to a saturating matching. No root Hall contradiction or single-row Hall deletion is possible in any of these partitions, on SAT or UNSAT instances, even if a partition is acquired by an unlimited search. More generally the complete system of clique capacity inequalities and owner one-hot equalities admits this strictly positive vector.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If a clique meets only one owner it has at most six rows. If it meets $k\ge2$ owners, every pair of such owners must intersect, and all clique rows from one owner must disagree on the shared coordinate with all rows from the other. Thus each such coordinate is constant within an owner's clique rows. Three of these owners cannot share one coordinate because three binary values cannot be pairwise different. Linearity implies that an owner meets its $k-1$ other clique owners in distinct coordinates; hence $k\le4$. For $k=2$, at most three NAE rows per owner have a specified coordinate value. For $k=3$, at most two rows per owner have two specified bits; for $k=4$ at most one row per owner has three specified bits. The clique size is therefore at most six in every case (respectively $6,6,4$). Signs do not change these bounds. Send the weight of each row to its bucket; every owner has sum one and every bucket sum at most one. The integral bipartite matching polytope decomposes this fractional vector into saturating matchings, so every positive edge appears in at least one. This proves the obstruction for arbitrary partitions and all clique inequalities, not for arbitrary guarded probes, additional pair exclusions, nonlinear constraints or changed representations. In particular it is not a complexity lower bound on unrestricted SAT.
\end{proof}
