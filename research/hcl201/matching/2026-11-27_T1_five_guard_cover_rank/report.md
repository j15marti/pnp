## Affine cover theorem
Let A be a nonempty d-dimensional affine space over F2 and G_i (i=1..k) affine subspaces of A. If they cover A irredundantly, every G_i has codimension <= k-1 in A. Proof: choose x_i in G_i outside every other guard. For each j != i choose a defining equation of G_j violated by x_i and reverse its RHS. The resulting affine flat H contains x_i, avoids every other G_j and has codimension <= k-1. Cover implies H subset G_i. Tight for prefix guards x1=0, x1=1 and x2=0, ..., all first k-1 bits=1. Empty guards redundant. Every five-guard cover therefore has an irredundant subcover whose individual ranks are at most four. If the union does not cover A, choose x outside all guards and reverse one violated equation per nonempty guard: the uncovered region contains an affine flat of codimension <=k. For fixed k, acquire exclusive/uncovered point by inclusion-exclusion counts and coordinate self-reduction (O(d) count queries, each <=2^k GE intersections), exact count bits <=d+1. Greedy deletion tests obtain irredundant subcover. This is post-acquisition polynomial, not universal guard discovery: even rank-one affine normals number 2^d-1. An uncovered relaxation point is not a source witness. Redundant high-rank guards do not satisfy bound, nonaffine guards need not satisfy theorem. Review requested.
