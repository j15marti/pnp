## D. Strict gain and adverse migration family (PROVED)
Adding vertex potentials a_v to every incident edge changes every full matching's tag by constant xor_v a_v, but may inflate raw label ranks linearly. A connected odd shore formed by a port r, k disjoint K4 blocks joined to r by bridges, and a single selected external edge at r, leaves k K4 matchings after endpoint removal. For block one, tag three matchings with 00,01,10 (assign tags to edges at one vertex); for others tags are gauge only. Use four distinct independent gauge coordinates at each K4, shared across two such shores if desired. Each block has raw gauge rank 3; local raw tag rank 3k+2 when first block tag rows independent of gauges (as tested), but actual selector variation rank 2, count 3^k, missing fourth tag. Connect the two ports by one crossing edge. This is a planar tight-shore graph; bridges are forbidden after the selector chosen and can be deleted without losing original embedding. Alternative decomposition can solve this family too; not a lower bound.
Counterexample to mandatory MOVE: k disjoint skeleton 4-cycles, each vertex replaced by a triangle with two distinct incident external ports. Every odd triangle cut has degree 2 so is tight. Tag one internal edge of one triangle in each cycle by a private independent bit; choose ports so one selector induces that edge and the other does not. Every selector-local matching is unique; all crossing tags zero. Raw and KEEP shared rank zero, independent local owner bits, but MOVE transfers these independent bits to the skeleton, whose variation rank is k. Both exact methods preserve support F2^k; moving all offsets is NOT universally rank-decreasing. KEEP is a safe rank-nonincreasing fallback; test both, do not claim universal discovery.
