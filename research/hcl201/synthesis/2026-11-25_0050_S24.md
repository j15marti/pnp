## FALSIFIED: pure fusion universal fixed polynomial cap
For n>=3 take one owner for each edge of K_n, each domain all four assignments to its endpoint bits. Pair compatibility agrees on shared endpoints, unconstrained otherwise. All global assignments are legal; initial AC has no deletion, no bit is forced. A pure sequence of pair fusions forms a binary merge tree on L=binom(n,2) original owners. Some internal block contains between L/3 and 2L/3 edges (descend while a child has >2L/3 leaves; take larger child at first stopping node). Let C_b be vertices touching both that block and its complement. Vertices exclusively in the block and exclusively outside cannot both exist: their connecting edge would violate one exclusivity. Hence one side has all edges within C_b, so binom(|C_b|,2)>=L/3 and |C_b|=Omega(n). After any fusion history, all assignments of bits appearing in >=2 current blocks remain feasible: extend new shared-bit assignments to previous ones; each new shared bit is observed by some unchanged exterior owner, and exact exterior projection preserves its value. AC cannot delete a feasible transversal. Thus at the balanced block every assignment of C_b is attained and every row determines its boundary bits by its actual lift. At least 2^|C_b| rows are required, even with maximal-neighborhood dominance and arbitrary merge order. Input explicit pair graph is polynomial in n. This falsifies universal completion by explicit exterior-preserving pair fusion alone under any fixed polynomial row cap. It is a deliberately easy SAT control; autarky or unconstrained-bit elimination bypasses it. No lower bound on unrestricted algorithms, factored representations, general source decisions or strict native tetravalent geometry.

Thus every pair fusion has >12 rows, so the raw nonincreasing-row gate always declines. Scope-changing growth is needed or another terminal; not a statement about learned charts.
