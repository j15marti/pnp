## Lemma 1: exact bijunctive response and non-resetting refinement
For F=A(x,y) AND B(y,z) with B a 2CNF, eliminate private z via complete Davis-Putnam buckets: remove every clause containing v or -v, add every non-tautological resolvent between the two groups. Any surviving model extends by choosing v; impossibility of both choices would falsify a retained resolvent. Thus each elimination preserves exact projected support and admits a constructive reverse lift. Resolvents never exceed width 2. With b boundary variables, at most 2b^2 distinct nonempty clauses (or empty clause); intermediate dictionaries likewise polynomial in total coordinates. Duplicate removal, lookup, failed pair enumeration and verification polynomial. Equivalently check 2SAT by SCC. If region B is satisfiable, an inconsistent boundary assignment yields a contradiction from at most two assumptions: implication closure of unit assumptions and base units is union of their separate reachability closures. A complementary-literal collision must come from at most two sources; if no collision, the consistent closed assignment extends by the 2SAT SCC construction. Thus a narrow cut can be acquired via at most O(b^2) local UP checks, with initial SCC check for B already UNSAT (the implementation used local UP and capped exact search; this theoretical initial SCC option was not used in the lazy experiment).
Progressive consumer: initially A alone (and any certified root pins). Ask its solver for a model or verified contradiction. If model, query B at its boundary. On SAT combine witnesses and check full F; on UNSAT insert one newly verified narrow clause excluding that boundary word. Reclose immediately. A new cut is necessary because the current consumer model satisfies every previous cut. At most 2b^2 productive insertions, and at most one extra terminal call. If consumer calls have uniform polynomial bit cost, this yields full polynomial decision/reconstruction on that class. Otherwise the bound does NOT pay the consumer's DPLL tree. ## Lemma 2: simultaneous fixed-boundary export refusal
For n>=3, define f(x0,...,x_(n-1)) = XOR_i x_i XOR product_(i=1)^(n-1)(1+x_i) over F2. This swaps the labels of exactly two opposite-parity words 0 and 1 (x0 least significant). Let L=f^-1(0), R=f^-1(1). Both nonempty, disjoint, size 2^(n-1), cover the cube. Each contains all but one point of its original parity hyperplane plus one point outside it. Since 2^(n-1)-1>2^(n-2), retained points span the original hyperplane; hence both affine hulls full. For any fixed assignment to <=n-2 coordinates, the resulting subcube has >=4 points, with equal original parity counts; each label can lose at most one point. Hence each label remains present, and every <=n-2 projection of each side is full. No nontrivial clause of width <=n-2 is a consequence of either boundary relation. A GF2 polynomial that is 0 on L and 1 on R must equal f on the entire Boolean cube; unique square-free ANF has degree n-1. Thus even a polynomial-size regional description and full affine hulls do NOT guarantee a fixed-degree separating polynomial or narrow-clause export. Both sides have O(n)-gate circuits and linear-size gate CNF extensions with private auxiliaries. Adding the shared computed observation f gives an immediate exact one-bit separator. The family is trivial jointly (complementary predicates), not an unrestricted hardness example; it isolates the need for adaptive shared observations and paid acquisition.
