## Exact selector-masked hull theorem
Let F(x) be a signed NAE-3 registry (rank-two forbidden affine packets E_i), x in F2^n, and optionally actual consistent guard G. Introduce k+1 fresh bits z, k>=2, and p(z)=sum z_j mod 2. Replace each forbidden packet E_i by E_i AND p=1 (rank three on raw distinct triples). Keep G exactly. The new solution set is
S=(G x ker p) union ((Sol(F) intersect G) x {z:p=1}).
Given a Gaussian basepoint of G, p=0 gives a model without solving F. A basis of the affine space H=G x ker p consists entirely of models and is polynomially acquired. If F under G is UNSAT, affine hull S=H; otherwise one odd-selector model lies outside H and affine hull S=G x F2^(k+1), because H has relative codimension one. Thus there is precisely one independent extra affine equation in the former case, zero in the latter. Computing this hull, deciding whether it is proper relative to the ambient guard, or deciding whether the displayed p=0 is necessary is coNP-hard even with a supplied witness and a spanning basis of H. More precisely proper-hull recognition on the promise is coNP-complete: nonproper hull has a polynomial certificate of full-dimensional affinely independent models (or one outside H with the supplied basis). For raw G, every assignment on <=k actual original/selector coordinates extends by choosing one remaining selector bit to make parity even. Both labels therefore have identical full k-coordinate projections, no literal backbone and all necessary equations, when present, have support k+1. Raw rank-one packet closure has no deduction. This strengthens ordinary literal-backbone hardness; it is not a claim that any SAT solver must compute the hull. Indeed the wrapped SAT decision is trivial.

The reduction is uniform for fixed k or k polynomial in source length. A dense parity row is explicitly encoded, not an oracle. For general 3-CNF use rank-four masked packets, or signed NAE-3 (NP-complete) directly. There is also a polynomial *projected* signed NAE-3 version: compute parity and relative literals with XOR gates encoded by four 3-CNF clauses per binary XOR; encode each conditional NAE as two gated 4-literal ORs, splitting each 4-OR by an auxiliary t as (a OR b OR t) AND (not t OR c OR d). Convert each 3-OR to NAE4 with common free reference r in relative coordinates, then split NAE4(a,b,c,r) as exists t: NAE3(a,b,t) AND NAE3(not t,c,r). If a=b then t is forced to its complement; if c=r then t=c; these demands conflict exactly when all four inputs agree. All encodings are exact on projection and lift by gate evaluation or local choice; the all-complement gauge is removed by taking relative observations. Thus the projected affine hull of the zero-energy NAE words remains hard despite a known zero optimum and supplied projected basis. No hardness assertion about full auxiliary hull or stricter wheel-only geometry; strict E4/B4 transfer needs exact I21 adapter and projection replay.
