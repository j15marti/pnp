## A. Arbitrary-rank simultaneous coordinate recognition (PROVED)
Let V be a finite dimensional dual free-generator space over GF(2), with explicit basis rows for U_1,...,U_m and any protected spaces W_j added to the same list. Decide whether some basis B of V makes every U_i (and W_j) the span of a subset of B. This concerns the supplied representation only, not all equivalent formulas.

At a stage with ambient V', set I=V'. Scan each restricted registered subspace X once in deterministic order. Replace I by I intersect X if nonzero; otherwise mark X skipped. Pick any nonzero v in final I. Every skipped X has I intersect X=0 (later intersections only shrink). Let S be the sum of skipped X. If v in S, decline. Otherwise Gaussian elimination finds a functional f on V' with f(v)=1 and f(S)=0. Set H=ker f in V'. Record v, replace each X by X intersect H, and recurse on H. Stop at dimension zero. Output collected independent vectors, verify their rank and for each original X that the vectors lying in X span it. All tests are ordinary Gaussian operations, no enumeration of membership patterns or intersection lattice.

Soundness: for each X accepted in the scan, v lies in X and X=span(v) direct sum (X intersect H). For each skipped X, X lies in H. Thus if recursion succeeds, all X are coordinate subspaces of the returned basis. Necessity: suppose a compatible basis exists at this stage. Every nonzero scanned intersection I is coordinate in it. Every basis vector in final I is in every accepted X and no skipped X. Replace one such basis vector having nonzero coefficient in v by v; this remains a compatible basis because all basis vectors involved share exactly the same membership pattern. The sum S of skipped spaces is spanned by basis elements outside I, so v not in S. For the actual chosen f, replace every other basis vector b by b+f(b)v. If b belongs to a skipped space, f(b)=0; otherwise every space containing b contains v (all skipped spaces exclude b). This shear therefore preserves every relevant space, and gives a compatible basis of H for their intersections. Induct. Decline is a rigorous no-common-basis result, not an UNSAT result. The argument works over any field with the appropriate signed shear, here GF(2). An obstruction transcript contains the greedy intersections, the skipped spaces and a linear combination proving v in S at the failure; the induction proves correctness of the earlier choices.

At most n stages and O(n(m+number of protected spaces)) intersection/sum/membership operations on n-dimensional rows, each poly(N); straightforward dense elimination gives conservative O((m+w+1)n^4) bit operations excluding input/output overhead, polynomial space. The dimension drops unconditionally at each recognized coordinate step. No need to enumerate 2^m intersection patterns. Include protected W as an ordinary registered subspace; full fixed W word becomes coordinate pins, but arbitrary new partial dense parity guard must be recharted and recognition rerun, not assumed coordinate.

## B. Exact conversion and known deficiency theorem (PROVED conditional transfer)
After affine guard consistency and certified unconditional closure (drop inconsistent rejection, reject covering chart, complement rank-one rejection and restart), let owner i reject on A_i x=b_i with consistent RHS and rowspace U_i. If A succeeds, define y_j=B_j x. For each basis row in U_i obtain its RHS by Gaussian combination of actual owner equations. The event is exactly a forbidden word on its coordinate subset. Complement gives an ordinary CNF clause. This is invertible pointwise on the free chart; source reconstruction solves B x=y then lifts through original chart. All owners and RHS provenance retained. Rank zero and contradictions handled before conversion. No physical count equality after prior existential peels.

For every subset J, dim(sum U_i)=number of distinct CNF variables in those clauses, since the same basis works for all. Hence d=max_J (|J|-dim(sum U_i)) is exactly ordinary maximum clause-variable deficiency (with multiplicities retained; duplicate clauses can alternatively be removed, which never increases maximum deficiency). For a fixed full protected W word, use the quotient by W (after consistency and deleting/shortening clauses) and recompute d on the actual remaining registry. Ordinary CNF matching obtains d in polynomial time. Apply Szeider's known deterministic O(2^d poly(N)) algorithm to the resulting CNF. This handles arbitrary local ranks, not just RT16 rank two, and all coordinate-compatible logarithmic-deficiency cores even if core rank or cut rank grows linearly. The known SAT subroutine was not implemented here. Szeider's result does not automatically extend to noncoordinate affine owners. If d is large, RT16 rank-two SCC remains an alternate cheap terminal. ## C. Counterexample and exact gap (PROVED restricted)
Let V=span(a,b,c), with U1=span(a,b), U2=span(a,c), U3=span(a,b+c). Three distinct planes share line a. A common coordinate basis would contain a and only two possible distinct coordinate planes containing a, contradiction. All events may be rank two and remain after single-owner closure; with three owners and rank three, maximum deficiency zero, so DY10 solves by transversal despite recognition decline. This shows common basis is not a necessary condition for tractability and no recognition result should be used as a decision. Arbitrarily many coordinate-compatible width-three clauses already include arbitrary 3-SAT, so universal polynomial maximum-deficiency bound is not established. Low d is insufficient for automatic coordinate recognition. No proof that all remaining noncoordinate cores admit another polynomial terminal; no general lower bound either.
