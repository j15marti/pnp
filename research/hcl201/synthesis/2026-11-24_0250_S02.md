## 2. Exact hereditary cancellation theorem
Let E be a common finite coordinate set of size n, D:GF(2)^E -> GF(2)^d a binary linear map with columns d_e. For each factor i let I_i be a nonempty hereditary family of subsets of E (downward closed, including empty), with exact support
    S_i = a_i + {D 1_J : J in I_i}.
All local choices must be independent except the explicitly represented global sum target; for graph regions shared vertices require I01 incidence splitting and full occupancy target or an equally exact product proof. All source observations needed for gluing must be included. Let a=sum a_i. Define U={disjoint unions J_1 union ... union J_h: J_i in I_i}.
Then the ENTIRE support satisfies
    S_1+...+S_h = a + D{1_J : J in U}.
Proof: choose local subsets. For each coordinate e whose multiplicity is odd, retain exactly one occurrence; for each even multiplicity retain none. Each new local set is a subset of the old one, hence legal. The sets are disjoint and preserve the XOR before applying D, including if D is noninjective. Reverse inclusion immediate. This is exact qualitative cancellation, not an affine-hull substitution. Example if heredity is dropped: two forced singleton sets {e} yield XOR zero but cannot form a disjoint legal union. Offsets are kept and not removed from the target arbitrarily.

If every I_i is a matroid independence family, U is the independence family of their matroid union (Edmonds). Consequently one common-dictionary group can be replaced by a single matroid-support factor with precisely the same attainable syndrome relation. This holds even with large primal, dual or MS02 binary-fiber variation ranks. It is not necessary to enumerate all independent sets of the union or to choose an optimal binary split. Retain original factors as a flat list for oracle and lift computation rather than recursively nesting expensive oracles. The equality holds for arbitrary hereditary families; the efficient decision theorem uses the extra matroid property.

## 3. Exact deterministic terminal and qualitative rank contradiction
Given target c, first perform certified unconditional affine check: span of all columns nonloop in at least one M_i equals the direction of the exact global affine hull (every available singleton and empty attainable; all other states in their span). Reject c+a outside. Enumerate solutions z of D z=c+a using Gaussian elimination, dimension q=n-rank(D); at most 2^q, including zero columns. For a lift z let T=supp(z). Membership in U is exactly existence of disjoint local sets partitioning T. Matroid union gives equivalently
    for all X subset T: |X| <= sum_i rank_i(X).
One deficient X is a qualitative contradiction: the required coordinates cannot be distributed among the regions even after arbitrary reassignment. Rank_i computed by greedy using its independence oracle. No need to enumerate all X. Construct copies (i,e) for e in T. On copies use direct sum matroid of M_i restricted to T, and partition matroid allowing at most one copy per e. Run ordinary unweighted matroid intersection, checking if maximum common independent cardinality is |T|. Its min-max theorem also yields a deficient X on failure: a minimizing copy subset A satisfies r_direct(A)+r_partition(E_copies minus A)<|T|. Let X be coordinates whose every copy is in A. Then r_partition(E_copies minus A)=|T|-|X|, and r_direct(A)>=sum_i rank_i(X). Thus strict deficiency. Standard shortest exchange path algorithm supplies a minimizing cut at maximality; no general optimization or counting oracle. For q=0 there is at most one lift and one such obstruction suffices. For q>0 every lift must be tested or otherwise excluded; no one Hall violation excludes the whole affine fiber in general.

## 4. RT02 integration and scaling
