## A. Exact structural acquisition barrier (PROVED)
Input: explicitly indexed subspaces U_i of a finite-dimensional binary vector space V, and integer k. Decide whether deleting at most k indexed owners leaves an arrangement with a common coordinate basis (a basis of V with every remaining U_i spanned by a subset). This is in NP by a deletion set and basis certificate, independently checked by rank and membership. Define kappa as the minimum such deletion count; do not equate it to a minimum SAT backdoor, semantic minimization, or decision cost.

Reduce Vertex Cover on a simple graph G=(V_G,E_G) of maximum degree three. For each edge e=uv allocate a separate two-dimensional block with independent a_e,b_e. Fix a deterministic endpoint order. Make one vertex owner per vertex, with U_u containing its endpoint line in each incident block (a_e for first endpoint, b_e for second). Make a third owner T_e=span(a_e+b_e) per edge. All listed owner spaces are direct sums of their block lines. Optionally append a private independent line p_i to every owner; it is absent from all other owners. Thus ranks are <=3 without padding, <=4 with padding, and encoding size polynomial in the graph.

For any surviving arrangement, if the three owners of an edge survive, write A,B,C for the two endpoint spaces and the edge space. The coordinate distributive identity A intersect (B+C) = (A intersect B)+(A intersect C) would hold if all were coordinate. On the e block the left contains the first line, the right is zero. Because the spaces decompose over the independent blocks, contributions in other blocks and private lines cannot repair this strict inequality. Hence all three cannot survive. Conversely if at least one of the three owners is removed for every edge, at most two distinct lines remain in each two-dimensional block. They extend to a basis in that block. Concatenate these bases and the private unit directions. It is a common basis for all surviving owners, including their direct sums. This proves the exact equivalence for every deletion subset, not merely a one-way reduction.

Any vertex cover gives a valid deletion set of equal size. From any valid deletion set take the deleted vertices; for every edge with neither endpoint yet selected, its edge owner must have been deleted, and add one endpoint. The resulting vertex cover size is no greater than the deletion set size. Thus kappa(U)=tau(G) exactly. By the classical degree-three Node Cover NP-completeness (Garey, Johnson, Stockmeyer, Some simplified NP-complete graph problems, 1976, DOI 10.1016/0304-3975(76)90059-1), the coordinate-deletion decision problem is NP-complete even rank <=3 (or <=4 with private padding). This is a structural acquisition result, not a lower bound for SAT, a fixed-parameter hardness result, or a proof P!=NP.

Closure firewall: use the padded construction on graphs without isolated vertices; every owner is a single forbidden affine event with all basis RHS zero, rank >=2. Setting all private observations to one makes every owner accept for any shared word. Each private bit can also be flipped individually while choosing a shared word that makes at least one of its owner's nonprivate equations false. Taking differences of these actual solutions shows that the full solution affine hull is V* (all shared coordinate directions and every private direction). Therefore no nontrivial global affine equation is forced; even complete affine-hull closure does not remove the structural recognition obstruction by pins. Yet the entire SAT instance is trivial, with independent private escape rows and maximum deficiency zero. BA12/MS16 can bypass it. This explicitly falsifies an inference from hard optimal representation discovery to hard decision.

In the returned basis compute the union C of coordinate supports of the W basis rows; every exception depends only on C. Its cardinality b is charged, not just dim W. Improved sufficient recognition: run S17 on R together with W as a single additional registered space. On success W is coordinate and b=dim W; its individual exception subspaces need not themselves be coordinate. On failure one may still use the first basis and its actual support size. No optimal basis/support search is assumed.

Convert R into ordinary CNF by S17; let d be its maximum deficiency (max over all owner subsets of number of clauses minus number of occurring coordinates, including empty subset). It is computed by maximum bipartite matching. For each of 2^b complete words on C, condition on that actual guard, run certified local closure at entry, after every deduction and at stagnation (archive consequences); if inconsistent or an exception rejects, discard the word. Remaining exceptions are constant true. Restrict the ordinary CNF to that word, deleting satisfied clauses and falsified literals. The restricted formula has maximum deficiency <=d+b: for any surviving subset of original clauses, at most b original variables have disappeared; deletion of other clauses cannot raise its maximum over subsets. Empty clauses are rejected. The archived necessary consequences do not have to be reinserted as dense parity clauses, because every surviving model of the exact restricted formula satisfies the exceptions and hence the archived rows on that guard. No conditional pin is exported to another word.

Use Szeider's deterministic bounded-maximum-deficiency SAT algorithm (ECCC TR03-002 revision 1, 2003; journal DOI 10.1016/j.jcss.2004.04.009). The ECCC abstract explicitly guarantees O(2^d n^3) and a satisfying assignment or regular resolution refutation, so no unbounded-deficiency self-reduction is needed to get witnesses. Lift actual assignment by solving Bx=y in the original chart and check all original events/guards. UNSAT iff every guard rejects or its CNF is UNSAT. This is exact decision, not counting, full protected support, or physical-flow reconstruction unless independently authenticated.

Same execution: T <= 2^(d+2b) poly(N) for a supplied set, including two recognition attempts, basis/support verification, Gaussian closure (<=ambient dimension successful pins per guard, repeated scans charged), all branches, failed guards, CNF construction, matching and Szeider witness/refutation, lift and final verification. Generated nodes/jobs bounded by actual work, charge all retained proofs and any cross-module adapter. For fixed d+b=O(log N) the terminal is polynomial. This bound is not a universal guarantee and not a claim that minimizing b is easy. If the modulator is not supplied, trying all <=k deletions costs sum_{j<=k} binom(m,j) polynomial recognition jobs; this is polynomial for fixed k, not a polynomial guarantee for k=O(log N). No free discovery or optimum representation.
