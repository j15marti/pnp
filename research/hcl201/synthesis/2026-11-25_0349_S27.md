## Theorem (PROVED scoped, independent review pending)
For all sufficiently large n=2^k-1, there are opposite-label signed projective Steiner triple registries differing by one literal sign (up to whole-owner complement) whose pair-energy matrices J have rank_Q J[B,A] > floor(n/100) for EVERY disjoint A,B with |A|,|B|>=n/4. The input is explicit O(n^2 log n) bits. No claim of efficient discovery of these witnesses or native four-perfect-partition geometry.

Proof. Use all triples {a,b,a+b} of distinct nonzero vectors of F2^k, each pair exactly once, m=n(n-1)/6. Per triple choose one of four forbidden complementary bit pairs, independently. A uniformly random choice makes each fixed global word legal with probability 3/4, independently, so probability SAT <=2^n(3/4)^m. Independently choose a planted registry, each owner uniform among the three classes accepting the all-zero word. Replace original choices by planted choices one owner at a time. Each of the m+1 stages has independent per-owner choices, each either uniform among four or uniform among the planted three classes. A specified crossing-edge sign has probability <=2/3, and a specified pair of crossing signs from one owner has probability <=1/3 <=(2/3)^2. The latter follows because any two pair products determine the class. At most two edges per triple cross disjoint A,B. Thus probability of a particular a-by-b cross matrix <=(2/3)^(ab). For rank j, choose j basis rows and j pivot columns: those rows and the entries of all other rows in the pivot columns determine the matrix. Number <= binom(a,j)binom(b,j)2^(j(a+b-j)); summing j<=r=floor(n/100) yields <=(r+1)2^(n+rn). Union bound over <=3^n disjoint pairs and m+1 stages gives <=(m+1)3^n(r+1)2^(n+rn)(2/3)^(n^2/16)=o(1) since log2(3/2)/16>1/100. Together with first-moment UNSAT endpoint, positive probability all stages have the rank property, initial UNSAT, final planted SAT. Some adjacent stages cross the labels; different complementary classes on three bits differ by exactly one literal flip modulo complement. This is an existence proof; finite samples are not its justification.
