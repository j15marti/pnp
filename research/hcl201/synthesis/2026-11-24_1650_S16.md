## A. Mixed affine lattice cut envelope (PROVED)
Input N includes encoded independent nonempty finite local supports S_i in A=Z^a x F2^r, target t, tree, certified local source decoders, all consumer identities and guard context. Explicit support or paid exact local acquisition allowed, weights nonnegative integers with charged bit lengths; indexed alternatives retained for lifting. Empty leaf rejects. For each leaf choose baseline s_i and subgroup L_i generated by all s-s_i, s in S_i (or a certified containing subgroup). For tree side J define a_J=sum s_i, L_J=sum L_i. All actual subtotals belong to a_J+L_J. Also compute integer coordinate intervals from sum of local minima/maxima (or safe certified bounds).
At cut J|K every globally feasible subtotal lies in
 E_J=(a_J+L_J) intersect (t-a_K+L_K) intersect (integer box_J intersect (t_Z-box_K)).
If coset intersection is empty or a coordinate interval is empty, UNSAT. Otherwise coset intersection is c+L, L=L_J intersect L_K. This is a necessary envelope, not an exact support. Both the integer and binary channels are kept in the same subgroup.

Acquisition: represent A as Z^(a+r)/D where D=0^a x 2Z^r. Lift each subgroup to its preimage lattice generated by lifts of its supplied generators and D. The preimage of a sum or intersection equals the corresponding sum or intersection of these lattices. Solve [B_J,-B_K](u,v)=t-a_K-a_J in integer coordinates, with B matrices containing D; an integer solution gives an intersection point, homogeneous kernel images give intersection lattice. Smith form with multipliers gives solvability, particular solution, integer kernel, and Hermite form gives canonical bases. This works for rank-deficient lattices; no enumeration of integer magnitudes. Kannan-Bachem polynomial bit algorithms for integer matrix normal forms supply the algebraic primitive. Source generators/bounds, particular solutions and basis bit lengths are charged; can derive each cut from original generators to avoid uncontrolled repeated coefficient growth. A coset-only or interval-only test is weaker.

## B. Stride-sensitive cardinality cap (PROVED)
Let P=projection of L onto Z^a, k=rank_Z P, and H=kernel(L -> P), a binary subspace of dimension h. Obtain P from projected generators; obtain H by taking integer kernel of projected generator matrix and reducing binary coordinates mod 2. For any integer subtotal in the projected coset, its binary fiber has exactly 2^h points, because lifts differ by H. This preserves cross-channel coupling: h may be zero even if tag marginal rank is high.
Select k original integer coordinate rows I making projection injective on span_Q P (Gaussian row selection). The projected lattice P_I is full rank in Z^k. Compute a column HNF triangular basis with positive diagonal d_1,...,d_k. For each selected row let width W_i=upper_i-lower_i >=0 of the intersected box. Then
 K_J = 2^h product_i ( floor(W_i/d_i)+1 )
bounds the number of possible mixed subtotal keys. Empty product is 1. Proof: in upper-triangular HNF solve coordinates from last to first. At each stage already chosen coordinates fix an offset; values in an interval are spaced by d_i, at most floor(W_i/d_i)+1 choices. Multiply bounds, then multiply by the exact torsion fiber size. Each projected coordinate tuple identifies at most one original integer subtotal by injectivity. Remaining coordinate bounds may further reduce support. Negative or nonzero coset offsets change no bound. If k=0 only the torsion fiber remains. This is a cardinality *upper* bound, not an assertion that every tuple exists. All products/widths can be computed and compared to polynomial caps in binary without enumerating the box. No optimal basis search required, and arbitrary coordinate choices may be loose. Exact membership by lattice solve and range check is polynomial bit work per actual key. A small stride-normalized width can replace a huge raw radix product; even high ambient integer dimension may collapse to k=1. Unlike S15, no modular wrap is possible.
