## A. Cut-safe pin deficiency theorem (PROVED)
Fix a free binary chart x in GF(2)^n after parameterizing the current affine guard (including any external value fixed for lifting). Each bad event e_i is a deterministic explicit ordered block monitor of A_i x+o_i, with block size <=k, polynomial state count, paid transition encoding. Let V_i=row(A_i), and L_ij,R_ij the row spans at each local block cut j, so V_i=L_ij+R_ij. Put w_i=max_j dim(L_ij intersect R_ij). All rows are actual private chart forms; no common interleaving is required.
A nonzero form b is CUT-SAFE for event i iff b is in V_i and at every local cut b lies in L_ij OR R_ij. Every actual nonzero observation row of that event is safe. This is a polynomial set of Gaussian membership tests. The explicit candidate pool C is the union of all nonzero actual observation rows (additional paid candidate forms inside V=sum_i V_i may be included).
Let B be ANY basis of V selected from C. Define B_i={b in B: safe for i}, and deficiency d_i=dim V_i-|B_i| >=0. Then for ANY subset of B pinned to consistent values, and ANY further affine restrictions of relative rank p, all restricted local widths obey
    w'_i <= w_i + d_i + p.
The rank bound is independent of the number of selected safe pins or how they are interleaved.

Proof. Write U0=span of selected B rows, S_i=span B_i, H_i=U0 intersect V_i, Q_i=U0 intersect S_i. Because B is independent, Q_i is exactly the span of selected safe basis rows. At a local cut, these rows each lie in L or R, so Q_i=(Q_i intersect L)+(Q_i intersect R). For any subspace H of V_i, quotient width equals
    w(H)=w(0)+dim H-dim(H intersect L)-dim(H intersect R).
This follows by subtracting dimensions of quotient images of L,R,V_i. Hence w(Q_i)<=w(0). The projection H_i -> V_i/S_i has kernel Q_i, so dim H_i-dim Q_i <= d_i. Passing from Q_i to H_i can increase width by at most this difference (same rank law on quotient images). Restricting by U0 on the global chart is exactly quotienting local row spaces by H_i, even when individual global pins outside V_i combine to imply a local form. Additional guard rank p increases dim((U0+extra) intersect V_i) by at most p and therefore increases width by at most p. RHS values affect offsets but not the ranks. This proves the inequality for every cut. It does not say arbitrary nonbasis pins are harmless.

## B. Polynomial basis discovery (PROVED)
Assign candidate b the integer weight a(b)=number of events for which b is safe. Sort descending a and greedily retain independent rows until they span V. This is maximum weight basis of the represented linear matroid: for each prefix of weight levels greedy has taken a maximum independent subset of that prefix; any basis has at most the same prefix count, so summation by differences of adjacent weights proves optimality. Since
    sum_i d_i = sum_i rank A_i - sum_(b in B) a(b),
the procedure exactly minimizes TOTAL cut-safe deficiency over bases in the specified candidate pool. In particular it finds a zero-deficiency basis whenever one exists there. It does not optimize the maximum individual deficiency, optimize the monitor orders, or discover all possible forms outside C. A verified basis can also be supplied or different candidate pools tried under charged caps. d_i=0 for each i suffices to preserve its original width under every basis-subset pin.

## D. Strict separation from common-order premise (PROVED)
Take n=6h Boolean coordinates, actual rows e_1..e_n in one event and reversed in the other. Each is a deterministic 8-state XOR monitor tracking three parities; legal iff the 3-bit final state has weight <=1. For j indexed from zero let first monitor update on bit x_j by unit label 1<<(j mod 3). Second monitor (with reverse order) uses the same label for j mod 6 >=3, and the XOR of that unit and unit (j+2 mod 3) otherwise. The combined six parity forms have rank six: subtract first triple from second, leaving a permutation of the three first-half residue-class parities; first triple then recovers other three. All bits are essential in each event (the four legal states have no nontrivial translation stabilizer). Each local observation space is the whole n-space with independent rows, so w_i=0 and the coordinate basis is cut-safe for both: d_i=0. Every allowed common interleaving of the two occurrence orders has a cut after n occurrences with exactly one occurrence of each coordinate on either side (a forward prefix and reverse prefix partition the coordinates), hence W_*=n, irrespective of the interleaving. This is a strict gap for the supplied fixed monitor orders; both monitors could be reordered or solved by direct parity reasoning, so not an inherent complexity barrier.
Each bad probability is 1/2 and legal sets in the two independent triples each occupy four of eight states. Union-bound credit is zero; exact full Fourier supports of both monitors lie in union of the two 3-dimensional parity row spaces, <=15 features. Taking their projection sum h=e1+e2 exactly makes PSD credit equal legal indicator, with mean 1/4. Full legal affine hull (each legal triple contains zero and all unit vectors, and the combined map is surjective). Thus exact positive lift succeeds with no common order and no affine-hull forcing needed at root. Negative neighbor replaces second monitor by complement of first, preserving orders and safe basis: no legal assignment, each separate legal hull still full, credit zero. Solver reports UNKNOWN, not UNSAT from zero credit; mathematical negative label is independently established. Hence d_i=0 is a cost condition, not a success theorem.
Counterexample to arbitrary guard stability: independent rows a,b have width zero; pin a+b, and quotient images coincide, width one. Guard rank is not free. A basis of all event rows cannot always have zero deficiency (three event spaces span(a),span(b),span(a+b)); bound is sufficient, not necessary for actual width preservation. No universal small-deficiency discovery claim outside candidate pool.
