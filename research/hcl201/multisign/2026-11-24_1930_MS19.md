## 4. Strict coupled examples and firewall (PROVED)

(A) Two binary choices u,v cost columns (1,1),(1,-1), target (1,0). Both coordinate ranges and individual gcd=1 permit target, and rational mixture u=v=1/2 realizes target. But u+v=1,u-v=0 implies 2u=1, integer impossible. Joint lattice rejects before quota enumeration. This demonstrates strictly stronger coupled obstruction than coordinate gcd and real convex feasibility (not necessarily stronger than all GF2 channels).
(B) Three choices u,v,w cost columns (1,1),(1,-1),(0,4), target (1,1). Integer equations give u+v=1, v=2w. Direct mod2 equations only give u+v=1. Integer slice gives v=0 mod2, u=1; renewed closure forces actual binary v=0, then w=0. Unique core actual witness (1,0,0). This is beyond separate row gcd and direct mod2: each cost row is primitive. (C) Lattice feasibility alone is not sufficient. Costs (2,3), target 1 has signed integer solution (-1,1) but no binary solution. Implemented closure rejects after additional rows. Thus the guarded lattice slice still projects mod2 onto the full odd parity hyperplane; no further affine forcing. Target is within guarded extrema and correct gcd coset. This refutes universal lattice-closure completeness even combined with interval/gcd tests. M19 exact residual DP or other cheap subset-sum reasoning can resolve it. Not a runtime LB.
