## 3. Lattice intersection separator theorem (PROVED; full parameter computation symbolic)
Work with equality target K in Z^d and q-bit XOR target t after slack conversion, fixed order of B blocks. At cut j, choose reference rows on each side with cost sums a_j,b_j. Let A_j and B_j be integer column matrices of all row cost differences in prefix and suffix. Write P_j=im_Z A_j, Q_j=im_Z B_j. Every extendible partial cost p belongs to
    C_j=(a_j+P_j) intersect (K-b_j-Q_j).
Find exact integral solution of A_j u+B_j v=K-a_j-b_j. If none, UNSAT. Otherwise set p0=a_j+A_j u0 and compute a lattice basis H_j of P_j intersect Q_j by taking A_j-images of the integer kernel of [A_j B_j], followed by HNF. Every integer solution of the matrix equation is its particular solution plus this integer kernel; therefore C_j=p0+H_j Z^rho, rho=rank H_j. This includes nonprimitive lattices and arbitrary index; rational nullspace alone is insufficient. Use polynomial-bit SNF/HNF including multiplier matrices [1]. Choose independent pivot rows of H_j and invert their square submatrix to get rational linear map L_j with L_j H_j=I. For any p in C_j, z=L_j(p-p0) is integral and injectively determines p.

For each row linear functional l of L_j, sum exact local minima/maxima on prefix to bound l(p-p0), and separately sum suffix minima/maxima to bound l(K-suffix-p0). Intersect these rational intervals, round inward to integers, and call the resulting integer range [lo,hi]. Exact local row extrema suffice; no global optimization assumed. An empty interval is UNSAT. Define
    V_j=prod_k max(0,hi_jk-lo_jk+1).
Rank zero uses empty product 1, i.e. a uniquely determined cut cost (if coset exists). Set V=max(1,max_j V_j).
