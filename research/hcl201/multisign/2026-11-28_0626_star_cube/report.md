A rank-two pair forbids one point of a two-dimensional dual observation plane. A rank-one pair is tautological, contradictory or imposes a unary affine equation. **Common-coordinate criterion.** For each distinct remaining two-dimensional dual plane L, list its three nonzero vectors. Let F_L be the vectors appearing in at least two distinct planes. There exists one linear basis of their total span making every plane a coordinate plane iff (i) every |F_L| <= 2 and (ii) t = |union F_L| + sum_L (2-|F_L|) equals the rank r of the total span. Proof: coordinate planes that intersect in a line share a basis vector, so every shared line is mandatory; any distinct nonshared vector can belong to only one plane. Conversely choose all forced lines and fill each plane to two independent vectors (any two distinct nonzero vectors in GF(2)^2). Their union has exactly t vectors and spans all planes. If t=r they form a basis. The necessity and sufficiency concern this common coordinate-plane representation, not every possible 2-SAT reduction. With admission, each plane mask becomes a binary clause on independent coordinates. Solve by SCC, lift arbitrary chart assignment by Gaussian elimination and then check native ports. An admitted common basis is found deterministically without matroid search or guessing. For fixed explicit input sizes, all pair/line grouping, GE, SCC, masks and maps have polynomial bit cost in original N.

**Private-dimension peel.** After grouping and affine closure, every nonaffine factor allows three of four points. If L intersects the sum of all other dual planes in dimension <=1, every assignment satisfying the others extends to a valid assignment on L: the shared observation has surjective support, and remaining coordinates can be chosen freely. Delete the factor, retain its owner tags and lift backwards by choosing one allowed point consistent with the already imposed observations. Multiple such factors can be removed in any order since the other span only shrinks. For acquisition form a matrix with two columns per plane, compute a basis of its nullspace with tagged Gaussian elimination, and project the nullspace onto the two columns of each plane; the dimension of that projection equals dim(L intersect sum others). This is a polynomial source-computable test, not a semantic oracle. The rule is an exact contraction on the reduced factor representation. On a fixed chart each peel removes at least one factor, and each independent affine addition raises rank; a fixed polynomial bound for this module follows.
