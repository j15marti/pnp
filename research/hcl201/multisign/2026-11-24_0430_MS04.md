## 2. Common-isotropic direction space
After affine closure, parameterize residual free variables by E=GF(2)^n. Equations p_i(x)=0 and phase f(x) have degree <=2. Define the polar alternating bilinear form B_i(u,v)=p_i(u+v)+p_i(u)+p_i(v)+p_i(0); in characteristic two its matrix is symmetric with zero diagonal. For a direction subspace U, all equations are affine on EVERY coset a+U iff B_i|U x U=0 for all i. This follows by the second difference identity, or the squarefree quadratic expansion in a basis of U. It does NOT require the phase f to be affine. Define k=min codim_E U among common-isotropic subspaces. This is a linear-coordinate strong backdoor, not merely a set of original variable indices. Rank q_quad of independent quadratic equations and k are different parameters.

## 3. Deterministic polynomial factor-two approximation (PROVED)
Initialize X=E. While some equation form has nonzero restriction B on X, compute R=rad(B|X) by Gaussian elimination, replace X by R. Each nonzero restriction has even rank 2s>=2; record s and basis of X,R. Terminate at U=X, which is common-isotropic. All restriction operations, rank, radical and complement construction use polynomially many bit operations on polynomial-size matrices; at most floor(n/2) iterations. Let b=codim U=sum 2s.
Fix any optimal common-isotropic U*. At a step write k_X=codim_X(U* intersect X). The quotient X/R carries a nondegenerate alternating form of dimension 2s. The image of U* intersect X is isotropic there, so its dimension d<=s: indeed its inclusion in its symplectic orthogonal implies 2d<=2s. The kernel of that image map is U* intersect R. Consequently
  k_R=dim R - dim(U* intersect R)
      = k_X - 2s + d <= k_X - s.
Sum across steps; k_final>=0 and k_initial=k imply sum s<=k. Thus
  b/2 <= k <= b <= 2k.
The lower certificate b/2 is valid ONLY for this common-isotropic parameter, not SAT work. Every recorded step is verifiable by Gaussian elimination. Discovery is polynomial and the approximation bound independent of q_quad or choice of nonzero restricted form. A single nondegenerate form can attain b=2k. For coordinate pair forms associated with a graph, k is exactly minimum vertex cover: a coordinate projection isomorphism of any d-dimensional isotropic subspace uses d pivot indices, which cannot include both ends of any graph edge (their joint projection would be surjective and violate isotropy). Hence d<=independence number; an independent-set coordinate span realizes equality. Exact optimal k is NP-hard, even if all equations x_i*x_j=0 and all-zero assignment satisfies. The approximation bypasses optimal discovery. No claim of novelty for common-isotropic spaces or exact optimization hardness.
