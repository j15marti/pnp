## 3. Outer normalized Fourier theorem
Define Z=sum_lambda sum_{x in D_lambda} (-1)^{f_lambda(x)}. By interchanging finite sums, each selector meeting unconditional requirements contributes the sum over V(x)^perp. If theta(v) is outside V, pair characters with a separating character and cancel; if inside V, all signs +1. Thus
    Z = sum_{compatible x} 2^{r-dim V(x)}.
Z is a nonnegative integer, positive exactly for SAT. Not an original matching count, nor generally a selector count. This is the MS02 normalization with arbitrary two-bit nonlinear offsets and guard survival retained exactly.

## 4. Inner quadratic rank closure and Fourier theorem
Given all degree<=2 equations p_j=0, store squarefree coefficients (1, x_i, x_i*x_j). Gaussian row reduction on the quadratic coefficient columns must carry the affine coefficients and constants. A zero quadratic part gives an affine consequence. A nonzero constant with no variables is contradiction. Otherwise solve each nontrivial affine equation, substitute into every equation and phase, reduce again; degree remains <=2. A new nonconstant affine deduction reduces the free variable count, so at most n such substitutions, polynomial cost. This is done unconditionally on lambda=0 before enumeration, and inside each character under its guard. When stable, the remaining equations can be represented by q_lambda polynomials with independent quadratic parts. Equivalence follows from elementary row operations and affine parameterization, not from dropping equations with duplicate quadratic parts. On contradiction the character contributes zero. Example xy=0, xy+1=0 must reject; deleting the second equation merely because quadratic parts match is unsound.

On the remaining m free bits, let f be the transformed phase and p_1,...,p_q the remaining basis. For y in F2^m the product of indicators [p_j(y)=0] equals 2^(-q) sum_{eta in F2^q} (-1)^{sum eta_j p_j(y)}. Therefore
    Z_lambda=2^(-q_lambda) sum_eta G(f+sum eta_j p_j),
    G(g)=sum_y (-1)^{g(y)}.
No expansion of assignments or guard combinations. Each inner numerator divisible by 2^q; individual Z_lambda may be negative. Outer Z >=0 is the decision quantity.

Quadratic Gauss sum algorithm, complete elementary proof: take one free bit z, write g=g0+z*l, where l is affine in other bits. Summing z gives 2*(-1)^g0 on l=0 and zero otherwise. If l=1, G=0; if l=0, drop z and multiply by 2; otherwise solve l=0 for one other variable, substitute in g0 and multiply by 2. Repeat until constant, when the remaining sign is exact. Each stage decreases the number of variables; substitution preserves degree; polynomial arithmetic and O(n)-bit integers suffice. This is deterministic classical computation, not a quantum algorithm. All zero/negative sums retained.

Define q=max q_lambda over noncontradictory characters (zero if none), measured after certified closure with an explicit deterministic elimination order. Time for explicit fibers <= sum_lambda 2^q_lambda poly(N) <= 2^(r+q) poly(N); polynomial peak space with sequential eta,lambda generation, coefficient matrices and integer sums of O(n+r+q) bits. Thus a new deterministic terminal for r+q=O(log N). Nonlinear offset curvature can remain outside W as quadratic quotient equations; or enlarge W and remeasure r,q. This is a tradeoff, not an optimization guarantee. For a fixed affine phase with all survivor domains affine and affine offsets q=0 and MS02 recovered. Quadratic phase alone also costs polynomial if q=0. Independent nonaffine restrictions need not be small rank, so no blanket four-color solver.
