## D. Basis-independent common-polar obstruction
For a raw unpinned signed NAE3 chart with distinct actual variables in each triple and 2-vertex-connected primal graph, every common simultaneous orthogonal decomposition of all owner polar forms has one block of dimension at least n-1, regardless of invertible affine basis. The rank-two polar of a triple (a,b,c) has row plane span(e_a+e_b,e_a+e_c); sign changes alter only lower-degree terms. If all forms are block diagonal, even rank additivity places each rank-two plane entirely in one block dual. Every primal edge difference belongs to a plane; repeated edge differences must belong to the same block. For two adjacent edges at v, 2-connectivity gives a path between their other endpoints in G-v, hence a simple cycle containing them. If its edges belong to different blocks, summing their differences gives zero, and directness would force a nonempty proper subset of that simple cycle to sum to zero, impossible (the only even subgraphs of a simple cycle are empty and full). Thus all adjacent edges, hence all edges, belong to one block. The differences span dimension n-1 by a spanning tree. This leaves at most the common global-complement radical. Fixing one actual reference coordinate yields the same bound n-1 on the reduced space since restriction on the even-weight difference span is injective. This excludes a universal decomposition of the raw source into small common polar blocks even with arbitrary basis, not a lower bound against term-specific decompositions, nonlocal rewriting, new variables or unrestricted algorithms. BW17 and Fano satisfy the hypothesis. The authenticated parity gadget family can instead first eliminate its private bits (A), so the combination has a genuine escape on that family.
