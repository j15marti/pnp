## PROVED: separation from sparse Fourier and from raw phase-key cancellation
Let Q=sum_{i=1}^k u_i v_i. The nonnegative Boolean function f=(1+(-1)^Q)/2 has only two affine-quadratic atoms. Its Walsh transform is nonzero at every one of 2^(2k) modes: the phase transform is the product over pairs, each sum_{u,v}(-1)^(uv+au+bv)=2(-1)^(ab); at zero the constant term adds rather than cancels. This exact compatibility function has a polynomial-size ordinary 3-CNF source with uniquely extendible AND and XOR circuit auxiliaries; after eliminating all these private gates its conditional count is f. Hence an explicit nonzero Fourier list can be exponential even when an exact nonnegative phase representation is tiny. This does not establish cheap acquisition of the two atoms from an arbitrary circuit, or a strict native NAE adapter. Conversely raw phase-only expansion on a linear NAE registry before projection has exactly 2^s distinct nonzero keys after s owners in any order: each owner has its own unique pair monomial in its quadratic violation polynomial, so all subset sums have distinct quadratic parts even modulo affine polynomials. Sign choices affect only lower terms. This restricted no-projection obstruction is bypassable by flat expansions, projection, phase fusion, nonlocal inference or other representations. Neither statement establishes P!=NP.
