## 1. Signed normalized mass: no additional quadratic constraint rank

With MS03 notation, D_lambda is the domain of the lambda character, f_lambda its quadratic phase. Let q_lambda be the residual quadratic equation rank of that character after its certified affine closure; q=max q_lambda. Base exact mass Z=sum_{x in S} m(x). For ANY degree<=2 Boolean polynomial p, compute
  Z_p=sum_lambda sum_{x in D_lambda} (-1)^(f_lambda(x)+p(x)).
Interchange sums: the factor (-1)^p is independent of lambda and local choice. The same annihilator character cancellation used in MS03 gives exactly
  Z_p=sum_{x in S} m(x)(-1)^p(x).
Hence the weight of violating selectors p=1 is (Z-Z_p)/2; weight of p=0 is (Z+Z_p)/2. These are nonnegative integers. For nonempty S, p=0 is an unconditional identity iff Z_p=Z; p=1 iff Z_p=-Z. If Z=0 handle UNSAT separately. This is not the original model count.
The key is phase insertion, NOT appending p=0 or p=1 as an extra quadratic constraint. The existing character domains, equations, r and q are unchanged. Substitute the stored affine closure into p, then use the same inner Fourier expansion and quadratic Gauss sums. Degree remains <=2. Thus each query costs at most 2^(r+q) poly(N,n), polynomial arithmetic space when characters are streamed. ## 3. Exact qualitative implication projection on quadratic observations
For any two degree<=2 predicates p,t and bits a,b, the mass of p=a,t=b is
  Z_ab=(Z+(-1)^a Z_p+(-1)^b Z_t+(-1)^(a+b) Z_(p+t))/4.
This is exact by the two-bit Fourier indicator expansion, with positive weights on all feasible selectors. Thus a signed implication (p=a) -> (t=b) holds on S iff Z_(a,1-b)=0. Enumerating all t-subsets of a large dictionary is not necessarily polynomial. This gives exact pairwise projection, not a complete 2-CNF representation of S unless independently proved bijunctive.
