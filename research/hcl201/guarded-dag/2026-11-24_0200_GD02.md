## 1. Exact witness-feature recurrence
Let F be a conjunction of m explicit factors on z in GF(2)^d. Factor i observes a subspace W_i of the dual, has rank <=q, and has an exact predicate on the linear valuations of W_i, available in charged time C(N). Set U_i=sum_{j<=i}W_j, V_i=sum_{j>i}W_j, B_i=U_i intersect V_i, w=max dim B_i. N includes source encoding, exact factor representation and supplied order. Extend an ordered basis of U_(i-1) to one of U_i by adjoining l_i<=q independent rows from W_i. At end, if needed append unconstrained independent observations completing a basis of the ambient dual; this cannot increase existing cut width because the added span is independent modulo U_m, and the new cuts have width zero. A valuation on U_i is encoded by its basis-value vector y.

For a fixed boundary state b on B_i let S_i(b) be all feasible prefix valuations with that boundary. For fixed integer k>=1, define phi_k(y)=(product of y_j for j in J) indexed by squarefree J of size <=k, including empty product 1. Define M_k(r)=sum_{j=0}^{min(k,r)} binom(r,j). Keep a subset R_i(b) of actual feasible prefix valuations whose lifted vectors form a basis for span phi_k(S_i(b)), at most M_k(dim U_i) points. Empty state is absent. Initially the unique empty valuation has feature 1.

For each previous state and each assignment a to the l_i fresh basis bits, append a to each retained valuation. Every possible previous history in this state has identical factor-i value: every old component of a row in W_i after removing its fresh coordinates is in U_(i-1) intersect W_i, hence B_(i-1). It also has identical next boundary value: for any row in B_i, its old component after removing the fresh rows is in U_(i-1) intersect V_(i-1), because the row and the fresh rows belong to V_(i-1). Thus test the factor and compute next state using one actual representative. A failed transition excludes every history of that state for that a; an accepted transition admits all of them. No synthetic affine-hull point is tested.

For fixed a, phi_k(y,a)=L_a phi_k(y) for an explicit binary linear map: each monomial is either zero or its old-variable monomial after substituting fixed a. Linear maps commute with span, and span of a union is sum of spans. Consequently the extended retained points span exactly the lifted feature vectors of every possible history of the transition; merge incoming points in their new state by Gaussian elimination, retaining original points for independent lifted columns. Induction proves the invariant at every cut. At the last cut boundary is zero; completed basis gives an invertible linear change from z to y. Substitution of a linear (or affine) map into a squarefree degree<=k polynomial, reducing z_j^2=z_j, preserves the degree bound. Lifted row span in original coordinates follows by converting actual witnesses and recomputing features. Thus in ONE interface pass recover a set of at most M_k(d) actual full satisfying assignments spanning the lifted feature vectors of all solutions. If no final state, UNSAT. Full source verification/lift still required for any separate compiler.

For k=1, the feature span has homogeneous coordinates (1,z), yielding the exact affine hull, with <=d+1 witnesses, without GD01's O(d^2) separate contradiction queries or their extra bit of width. For general fixed k, orthogonal complement of final feature span gives a basis for ALL GF(2) polynomial equations of total degree <=k satisfied by every solution, in Boolean quotient ring. This includes quadratic forbidden relationships and cubic guarded contradictions. Example: OR(x,y,z) has full affine hull and no nonzero quadratic invariant, yet (1+x)(1+y)(1+z)=0 is a cubic invariant. Extract basis by Gaussian elimination, not by enumerating all coefficient vectors. Invariant generation is complete only within selected degree. It is NOT a representation of exact support in general; use witnesses or exact predicates for decisions.

## 2. Resource ledger
Enumerate at most m*2^w*2^q candidates (including polynomially many completion factors). For each accepted candidate, <=M=M_k(d) original prefix representatives extend; compute their features in O(M*k*poly(N)) elementary bit work and insert into a Gaussian basis in O(M^2) bit work each (conservative dense cost). Basis conversion, cut intersections, row coordinates, source parsing and deterministic reconstruction are poly(N); final nullspace O(M^3) bit work. T <= D(N)+2^(w+q) poly(N)(C(N)+M^3) plus paid closure and verification; S <= S_D(N)+S_C(N)+2^w poly(N)(M^2+dM) plus closure storage. Dictionary keys w bits, two layers, actual representative storage counted, output at most M^2 bits plus witnesses. CM <= T*S for the same sequential execution, including discovery, failed transitions, cache, conversion and output. D,S_D denote supplied-order acquisition/verification or actual charged discovery, not zero-cost promise. For fixed k, M polynomial, hence T=2^(w+q)poly(N) if C polynomial and supplied order. Log width and q are a sufficient terminal condition only if discovery is polynomial. No claim of improved asymptotic exponent versus GD01's polynomial factor; removes repeated calls and extra width and generalizes output. Fixed k is essential: M=2^d at k=d. OR on k+1 independent bits is full feature rank through degree k: any nonzero function vanishing everywhere except all-zero is the unique Boolean polynomial product (1+x_j), of degree k+1. Thus bounded-degree closure is incomplete as exact support, independently of width; this is no decision lower bound.
