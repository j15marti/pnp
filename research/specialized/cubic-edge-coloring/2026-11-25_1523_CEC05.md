## 1. Balanced boundary lemma (constructive, known Kempe mechanism)
Take actual ordered six-edge cut delta(X), materialize each half-edge as edge to a private leaf. A proper coloring of pole has each color occurring even number of times on ports by 3|X|=2m+6 and incidence counting. Canonical states are A=(6,0,0) one orbit, (4,2,0) 15 orbits, (2,2,2) 15 orbits, 31 total. Call latter balanced; each is an unordered perfect pairing of six port IDs.
Given ANY valid local word, choose two colors a,b with a occurring >=4 on ports and b absent. Their subgraph has degree two at internal vertices and degree one at ports bearing a or b. Each path ends at two ports, necessarily both a. Switch a/b on one such path. Starting from 6,0,0 yields 4,2,0; then from 4,2,0 choose abundant color and absent third to get 2,2,2. At most two traversals and recolorings, no target-state assumption. More generally even k-port counts can be balanced to differ by <=2: if counts a-b>=4, endpoint imbalance ensures at least one aa path; interchange reduces sum of squares by 4(a-b)-8>=8. At most O(k^2) operations, each polynomial. Only unguarded local states; a path can change port/internal pins. The six-port existence mechanism appears in Fiol/Vilaltella's Lemma 4.1 (2015), not a priority claim.

## 3. Sharp obstruction to weakening saturation and to global balancing
B^- is K4 with every edge subdivided, stubs at the six subdivision vertices in order 01,02,03,12,13,23 of K4 edges. Bipartite 4/6, all ports on subdivision side; same incidence count forces balance. Its exact table has 14 balanced types, missing 012210 (three opposite-edge pairs). Symbolic obstruction: if opposite K4 edges bear the same missing color, each original vertex sees three different missing colors. A legal permutation of its actual incident internal edge colors must be one of the two fixed-point-free permutations of the missing colors. At a subdivision vertex the two internal colors must differ, requiring the two original endpoints to choose opposite cyclic orientations. This would bipartition K4, impossible. Other 14 witnessed by executed exact finite table acquisition (raw near.states).
For any pairing P build E(P) from three disjoint copies of K4 minus edge 01, with the two stubs at endpoints 0,1 assigned to the corresponding pair of port IDs. Each component forces its two port colors equal by parity and admits all common colors by permutation; explicit extension comes from K4 coloring. Hence E(P) has exactly one balanced type P, plus A and the three two-color coarsenings. Joining B^- to E(012210) gives connected simple cubic NO on 22 vertices, while B to same side gives YES on 24. Thus 14/15 balanced coverage is NOT universally sufficient, even with no other state ambiguity. This does not rule out other safe cap criteria.
A second connected simple source on 24 vertices joins E(001122) to E(011220). The union of their port pairings is a single 6-cycle, so all boundary edges forced equal. Both sides are colorable and locally normalizable, but NO full source coloring has balanced boundary. Full graph is YES by all-equal gluing, verified directly. Thus cannot universally balance each side independently or demand balanced boundary at every cut. Each side here is disconnected but the original graph is connected; no assertion for connected sides. First audit used another pairing with a disconnected source; final connected variant strengthens the counterexample and preserves both records.
