## Lemma 1: recover the circuit compiler
Anchor variables a_0..a_4 with five clauses C(a_i,a_(i+1),a_(i+2)) cyclic. Every pair occurs, so colors are all distinct. Allowed triples are complements of consecutive pairs on the 5-cycle; preserving those triples forces the ten dihedral frames, a_i=s+di mod5, d=1 or -1. Work in canonical frame; all constructions equivariant under these ten automorphisms of C (not necessarily of binary R).

For each Boolean wire B impose C(a_j,B,U_j) for j=2,3,4 and distinct fresh helpers; exactly the values a_0,a_1 survive, each with two helper extensions. NOT gate input P output X: C(a_2,P,X). OR gate inputs P,Q output X: C(a_1,a_2,Y), C(a_0,a_1,Z), C(a_4,P,Y), C(a_3,Q,Z), C(X,Y,Z), fresh Y,Z. Canonical Y=3 or 0 for P=0 or 1; Z=4 or 2 for Q=0 or 1. Last clause forces X=P OR Q. Final output pin C(a_0,a_2,O) forces O=a_1. Fresh outputs, even repeated OR input is allowed because no physical clause repeats a name. Truth tables checked exhaustively. Each Boolean input satisfying the circuit lifts by forward evaluation and first legal constant-size helper values; each C witness decodes by anchor indices. With b base wires and g_or,g_not gates, n=5+4b+2g_or, m=6+3b+5g_or+g_not. CircuitSAT NP-complete (e.g. compile 3-CNF by OR/NOT/De Morgan gates); verification is polynomial, so strict C decision NP-complete. No counting consequence used.

## Lemma 2: exact single-puncture compiler (both labels)
Given any Boolean circuit H(x), add a new input g and gates computing O=g AND H(x), using two input NOTs, OR, final NOT. Apply Lemma 1. Let d be the final pin clause. Evaluate at x=0,g=0 and lift using the canonical frame. Every clause except d is satisfied, and d is false because O=a_0. This is a uniform near-model for BOTH SAT and UNSAT sources, obtained without a source decision. With d restored, satisfiability iff g=1 and H(x)=1. A full solution projects to a source witness. Deletion d alone admits a known model in polynomial time and does not imply any original SAT witness. This is an ordinary feasibility decision problem with supplied designated clause and near-word, not an all-deletions critical promise. Invalid promise encodings are rejected in polynomial time.

## Lemma 3: exact linearization and occurrence-degree bound
For any colors x,y, exists h C(x,y,h) iff x!=y (every unordered pair belongs to at least one allowed consecutive triple; equal values never belong). Thus simulate a graph edge by a C-clause with its own fresh head. To enforce x=y, create four distinct private vertices forming a clique, connect each to both x and y, omit xy, and simulate each of the 14 edges with its own head. Any model colors the clique with four distinct colors, leaving precisely the fifth for both terminals. If the terminals agree, assign the four other colors to the clique in order and choose each head by scanning 0..4. This exact equality gadget uses 18 private names, 14 clauses; endpoints each occur four times, clique vertices five times, heads once. Its hypergraph is linear because the graph is simple and heads are unique.

Split each original variable into a separate copy at every occurrence, keeping all original clauses as disjoint triples, and enforce equality between consecutive copies with the gadget above. Distinct gadgets share at most one copy, and any gadget and any old clause share at most one name: each old clause had distinct original names. Hence every pair of names occurs together at most once. Every copy occurs in one old clause and at most two equality gadgets: degree <=9. All private names degree <=5. For original L=3m occurrences and a active variables, t=L-a equality links, n'=L+18t, m'=m+14t. Unused declared names may be restored arbitrarily on decoding. The original d becomes one disjoint copied clause d'. Lift the known near-model through equality gadgets using its original occurrence values, so it violates exactly d'. Conversely every full witness has consistent copies and satisfies every original clause. Thus the language of linear degree<=9 strict-C formulas with a designated d' and supplied word satisfying exactly F-d' and violating d' is NP-complete under uniform many-one reductions. The near-word is part of the encoded instance, not advice. Independent source labels preserved without knowing either label.

## Positive terminal and limit for topology tracks
A strict-C instance with one private variable per clause projects exactly to a simple graph 5-colorability problem on the other variables (choose the private variable per clause, then discard it; deduplicate graph edges). If that graph is 4-degenerate, success is uniform polynomial: repeatedly remove a vertex of degree <=4; restore in reverse order choosing an unused color, then lift all private heads. If peel fails, UNKNOWN not UNSAT. Conversely this entire private-head subclass is already NP-complete: join an arbitrary simple graph 3-colorability input with K2, obtaining a 5-colorability instance, then apply edge projection. No pair-overlap cycles required. This is a C-specific proof, related to the earlier Q private-head reduction but not imported. Consequently local deletion, private-head elimination and bounded incidence alone cannot be assumed to give a universal tractable domain. The new near-model linearization shows even a successful puncture and linear sparse incidence can coexist with arbitrary source decision difficulty.

Unconditional forced-information closure: full domains at root admit all five values at each occurrence by translations, so generalized arc consistency cannot eliminate any; sound closure may use stronger correlations. Near-word values are NOT unconditional pins. When testing a supplied complete word the singleton domains are guarded by that word; closure after pin deductions and at stagnation scans all clauses including d unless under the explicit deletion guard. Equality contractions are unconditional consequences of intact gadgets; if a track deletes a gadget clause its equality consequence must not be reused without reproof. In the constructed designated-deletion track all equality gadgets intact; contract and replay original clauses exactly, then forward circuit evaluation gives the relaxed witness. Restoring d is still arbitrary CircuitSAT. No claim that stronger unconditional deduction or a different track is impossible.
