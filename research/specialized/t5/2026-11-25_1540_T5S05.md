## Theorem 2 (overlapping saturation and confluence)
Pure saturation only: fix the original column multiset and each sign domain {1,4}; remove zero columns harmlessly. If J1,J2 saturate U1,U2, respectively, even when J1 intersects J2, the union of their columns saturates U1+U2. Proof: modulo U1, contributions from J1 and J1 intersect J2 vanish. Therefore the surviving J2 columns realize every point of the image of U2, since projection of its full attainable set is surjective. Choose a surviving J2 word for the projected target; residual lies in U1 and is attained by an independent J1 word. No sign variable used twice. Same argument applies to any already-eliminated subspace W and any group saturated in a prior quotient V/W0 where W0 subset W.

Consequently for every fixed d, all maximal sequences of rank<=d saturated quotients have identical eliminated subspace W in original V (not necessarily identical witness choices). Let W' be the final subspace of another maximal sequence. Induct on first sequence; previous W0 subset W'. The next group's image in V/W' is saturated, has rank <=d, and all columns removed by the second sequence project to zero. If its image is nonzero, there is a candidate in the terminal second quotient, contradiction. Thus W0 plus the next group lies in W'. Reverse the roles to conclude equality. The fully eliminated space is itself saturated by reverse reconstruction. This is a precise order-independent normalization theorem for the pure operation, not for arbitrary heterogeneous solvers or arbitrary sign-domain deductions. Forced pins alter the active column set; recompute under their actual guard, do not infer confluence across different guards. It eliminates the need to branch over competing saturation-order tracks. Cross-track knowledge must be actual surjectivity, not affine span or independent feasibility.
