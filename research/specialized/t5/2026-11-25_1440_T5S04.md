## Theorem 1: a known-complete geometric-middle chart can still have NP-complete residual decision
Define M(l,m,r) iff l=m+t and r=m-t in F5 for some t in {1,-1}. Each allowed C tuple has exactly one geometric middle (the middle of the three consecutive pentagon colors). This is NOT the ordered tournament head rule. A chart supplies one chosen middle name per C clause; endpoints may be ordered arbitrarily. Source semantics remain C; charts restrict witnesses. Consider the language of pairs (F,chart) where F is satisfiable in the chart. It is in NP. In addition, the following explicit reduction produces plain C sources F for which SAT(F) iff SAT(F,chart), without needing to decide source SAT.

Let A(u,v) be ordinary undirected pentagon adjacency: v-u=+/-1. Implement it by three C owners with fresh a,b,h:
C(a,u,v), C(u,v,b), C(a,h,b).
For u=v no extension. For distinct nonadjacent u,v, there is only one possible third color in a C triple; then a=b and the final owner fails. For adjacent u,v with v=u+t, t=+/-1, the two possible third colors are u-t and v+t. They are distinct and at distance two. Assign a=u-t,b=v+t,h=u+3t. All owners are legal, with geometric middles u,v,h respectively. Thus the gadget projects to A both without any chart and with the fixed chart specifying these three middles. Every A assignment lifts in constant time; every C witness decodes A regardless of chosen middles. Audit found 20 total legal gadget words and 10 fixed-chart words over 5^5 possible words.

A length-three path of A edges projects exactly to inequality on its endpoints: sum of three +/-1 steps in F5 belongs to {1,2,3,4}, and every such residue occurs. Therefore compile graph 5-colorability into plain C by replacing each graph edge with a length-three path with two private intermediate names, and replacing each of its three edges with the above gadget. Use the fixed geometric-middle labels throughout. n'=n+11m, m'=9m, distinct names within every owner. Every source 5-coloring lifts by choosing path steps then gadget assignments; every arbitrary C witness decodes the original coloring. Graph 5-colorability is NP-complete via the join of a graph 3-colorability instance with K2 (two new mutually adjacent universal vertices consume two colors); source graph is simple. Polynomial-time membership and verification. This establishes NP-completeness on a polynomially generated reduction image with a supplied complete chart; no promise oracle required by compiler. It does not assert a polynomial recognizer for all semantic complete charts, nor that the earlier degree-nine or supplied-near-word restrictions hold simultaneously with this chart promise. The construction has shared pairs and is not linear. No weakening of strict C or new base language: the only guarded relation is M in the algorithm, and all compiled source constraints are strict C. A uniform polynomial chart solver even on this image would imply P=NP; failure of one algorithm is not P!=NP.

## Theorem 2: exact homogeneous F5 syndrome representation of a fixed chart
For each chart owner i=(l,m,r), introduce one sign t_i in {1,4} subset F5. Add two directed formal arcs m->l labeled t_i and m->r labeled -t_i; reverse arc traversal negates its label. The graph can have parallel arcs. Choose a spanning forest by ordinary BFS, one root per connected component. Let L_v in F5^m be the coefficient vector of the signed path from root to v. Set L_root=0. All chart equations are equivalent to the system B t=0 over F5 whose row for arc (u,v) labeled s*t_i is L_v-L_u-s e_i. Zero and duplicate rows may be removed; ordinary Gaussian elimination gives row basis. Necessity by telescoping. Sufficiency: x_v=L_v*t plus arbitrary component translation then realizes every arc and M relation. No extra orientation choices, no unknown chart acquisition. For fixed color pins within a component, additional rows from differences of L vectors with corresponding pinned-value differences can be included, and root translation recovered; this extension is symbolic only (not tested). Arbitrary lists are not automatically handled. This theorem and Theorem 1 imply homogeneous modular signed-syndrome feasibility over F5 is NP-complete, even on the derived middle-chart matrices. Full linear relaxation has the zero solution always and is insufficient. In particular sign constraints, not orientation choice, are a genuine remaining nonlinear obligation.

## Theorem 3: exact quotient of a saturated column line
Generalize to B t=b, t_i in {1,-1}. Row-reduce and check inconsistency. If zero column, remove it and store any sign. For any one-dimensional column subspace U=span(v), v!=0, take a group J of all columns in U, B_i=lambda_i*v, lambda_i nonzero. Define S_J={sum_{i in J} lambda_i*t_i : t_i in {1,-1}} subset F5. This finite set and one actual sign word per residue are acquired by a five-state dynamic program, not by enumerating 2^|J| sign words. If |J|>=4 then S_J=F5: starting from a singleton, adding a two-element set of distinct shifts strictly increases cardinality unless already full, since a proper nonempty subset of the cyclic prime group cannot be invariant under a nonzero translation. At most four additions reach all five. Smaller groups may also saturate; test exactly.

If S_J=F5, quotient the equation by U:
  (B t=b feasible) iff (pi(B_{not J}) t_{not J}=pi(b) feasible),
where pi is the canonical quotient map chosen via a nonzero pivot coordinate of v. Forward immediate. Conversely the residual difference lies in U; the stored five-state witnesses recover signs of J giving exactly that difference. Explicitly for pivot coordinate p, replace row r != p by row r - (v_r/v_p)*row p, delete row p and columns J, retain old pivot row coefficients, RHS and the five words for reconstruction. This map preserves existential projection onto remaining sign variables, not merely an affine hull of actual feasible words. Repeated quotients remove at least one row and one column each. Recompute row basis and groups every time. A line may saturate only after another contraction. Can apply to inhomogeneous right hand side; no assertion all subspaces are saturated from rank or multiplicity alone.

Paid closure: run GAC on original C and chart M at entry (translations make full-list singleton-color elimination vacuous); after every substitution or quotient run row-reduction and exact row-wise sign support tests. For each coefficient in a row, use five-state sum DP on the other nonzero columns to see which of its two signs can reach the target. Empty support proves guarded NO, singleton support is a necessary pin, substitute and restart. After strict reductions stop only at fixed point and re-run the same closure before bounded terminal. This is a specified sound closure, not an oracle for all global forced literals. It may miss forced correlations. Quotient projections are exact and guard-dependent; no deletion or chart guard discharged without original C verification. Reconstruct in reverse and replay matrix and all original owners.

After saturation let r be the rank of the residual matrix, q the number of remaining sign variables. Enumerate syndrome states in F5^r by DP, not individual sign words. Initialize syndrome 0. For each column c add +c or -c to every reachable state; retain one actual partial word per state. Accept iff b reachable. Reverse quotient and substitution records, then lift to strict C. Uniform deterministic time 5^r poly(N), and peak space 5^r poly(N), including explicit state words, not assuming hash-map constant access (sort or sequential lookup; for exact 5^r time use lexicographic sorting of at most 2*5^r candidate states in each round, costing O(r) comparisons per candidate up to polynomial bit factors). All initial matrices have polynomial bit length and fixed-field entries. For fixed log cap r<=c log_5 N, the terminal is polynomial. Outside cap return UNKNOWN; row/quotient contradictions are exact only for the chart. Component factoring can be added by splitting the row-column bipartite support graph after row basis and saturation (independent existential systems, sum of 5^r_j costs); this variant not implemented.

Connected strict chart family showing raw rank is not a necessary cost: take k pentagon graph cycles meeting at one vertex, each edge u->v encoded by C(v,u,w) with private leaf w and geometric middle u. Fundamental cycle equations use disjoint groups of five +/-1 signs; rank k, yet every group saturates a line and the quotient finishes at rank zero. All signs +1 per ring give direct lift, but solver discovers elimination without planted words. Replace cycles by triangles to obtain chart-UNSAT because three +/-1 never sum to zero mod5. These triangles can be ordinary C-SAT when geometric-middle guards removed, so this is not a general source rejection. The construction is infinite and symbolic; only listed sizes executed.
