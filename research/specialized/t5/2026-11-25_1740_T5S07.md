## Theorem 1: exact quotient for a prospective saturated sign group
Fix connected loopless G (multigraph also allowed), m edges, n vertices, B a fundamental cycle matrix with one column per edge. For J subset E let q=c(G-J), counting isolates. Contract each component of G-J to one vertex and keep exactly J with its original orientation, obtaining connected quotient multigraph H, including loops.
Then ker(B restricted to J) consists exactly of signed gradients of vertex potentials on H: extend a vector on J by zeros elsewhere. It lies in ker B iff it is a gradient on G (spanning-tree integration). Zero differences on E-J force constancy on each component; conversely any potential on H lifts by repeating its value on the associated component. The gradient dimension on connected H is q-1 even with loops. Thus
    r(J)=rank(B_J)=|J|-q+1.
For arbitrary b in im B_J, solve B_J h=b by elimination. The equation B_J t=b with t_i=+/-1 is equivalent to t-h being a gradient on H. Therefore it has a witness iff H admits potentials with every edge difference in {-h_i-1,-h_i+1}; on loops test that zero is permitted. Conversely every h defines a target b. The group J is saturated iff EVERY such assignment of edge offsets on H admits potentials. This is an exact structural characterization with polynomial translation in both directions, not an efficient general saturation recognition oracle. It permits using S06's constructive difference solver on H for any target, including a target passed from another wheel; no independent treatment of a single tied sign is allowed. Loops may reject a target. Arbitrary tied-sign charts have a different kernel; only apply this lemma to an independently verified graph-derived matrix.

## Theorem 2: no saturated nonzero column group in a four-edge-connected graph
For q>=2, every component of G-J has original cut size >=4. Every crossing edge is in J and counts at most twice, so 4q<=2|J|. Hence r(J)=|J|-q+1>=|J|/2+1. If q=1, r(J)=|J|. Consequently every nonempty J has r>0 and |J|<=2r. Its attainable signed sum set has at most 2^|J|<=4^r<5^r elements. It cannot fill its span. This excludes ALL dimensions, not just fixed-dimensional discovery. Pure saturation cannot even take its first nonzero step. An elementary cardinality test rejects each candidate in polynomial time by rank, without generating 5^r states. The theorem is not a lower bound on decision; a solver may use any alternative representation.

## Theorem 3: this raw saturation obstruction occurs on an NP-complete strict-C reduction image
A graph 5-colorability instance arises by joining a 3-color instance to K2. Replace each edge by an independent three-edge path of undirected pentagon adjacency A. Three +/-1 steps produce exactly the four nonzero differences in F5, so graph 5-colorability is equivalent to C5 homomorphism of the subdivided graph K, connected in this construction. Then replace every vertex by four independent twins, and every edge by a complete K4,4 between its endpoint twin classes. Call result G. A homomorphism K->C5 extends to G by constant values on twin classes; restricting any G homomorphism to one fixed representative per class gives a K homomorphism. Every nontrivial cut of connected G crosses at least one K4,4 edge. A nontrivial cut of that K4,4 crosses at least four edges (for a,b selected endpoints from its two classes, crossing count 4(a+b)-2ab >=4 except (0,0),(4,4)). Hence G is simple and four-edge-connected.
Convert each G edge uv to strict C with private a,b,h: C(a,u,v), C(u,v,b), C(a,h,b). If u=v impossible; nonadjacent distinct endpoints have only one possible third value so a=b and third owner fails. For v=u+t, t=+/-1, set a=u-t,b=v+t,h=u+3t; geometric middles u,v,h. Both unrestricted and supplied fixed-chart projections equal A. Each gadget lifts in constant time. The fixed graph projection preserves signs as single A-edge variables; S06 cographic matrix is computed from G, not from unprojected tied arcs. Thus this polynomially generated strict-C source image, with chart and optional verifiable compiler metadata, remains NP-complete even though its raw projected matrix has no saturation of any positive dimension. The promise can be represented as explicit graph + deterministic gadget compiler, checked by regenerating IDs; no semantic chart oracle. All blowup factors constant; complete source-to-G-to-C and witness lift polynomial in encoded input length. Reverse strict-C witness restricts to G, representative K, then original 5-coloring and remaining three colors after excluding the join's K2. This does NOT establish NP-hardness after arbitrary forced-information closure or canonicalization.

## Theorem 4: exact affine closure still cannot create saturation on an infinite SAT family
For any connected bipartite G, choose potential 0 on one part and independently +/-1 on the other. Every such assignment is an A-homomorphism. Flipping one vertex changes the edge-difference vector by a nonzero scalar times that vertex's incidence-gradient column. Doing this on either side gives every vertex-gradient direction. The affine hull of actual sign words is therefore the whole gradient space ker B, since those columns span ker B and all actual words lie in it. In particular no extra affine linear equations beyond B t=0 can be forced even by an oracle giving the complete affine hull. Each individual sign takes both values; for distinct edges in a simple bipartite graph choose an endpoint incident to just one of them and flip its potential with the other part fixed at 0, so neither sign equality nor polarity is universally forced. This excludes rescue by pure affine closure or pair sign identification, not by nonlinear deductions or recognition of bipartiteness.
Use even square tori C_k square C_k, k even >=6, n=k^2,m=2n,r=n+1. Four-edge connectivity: if two rows are partially selected, each supplies >=2 crossing horizontal edges. If exactly one row partial, some column partial (other rows full or empty), adding >=2 vertical crossings. If no rows partial and cut nontrivial, each column partial. Thus Theorem 2 applies. These tori have no false twins: two different vertices having identical neighborhoods would share four neighbors, but for k>=6 distinct vertices share at most two (check offsets of two unit coordinate steps). Their unbounded rank remains after affine closure; the bipartite terminal immediately solves them, so this is a deliberate easy-family control, not evidence of hard runtime.
