## Theorem 1: exact normalized double cover
For each clause choose one true literal as representative. Label = variable. A label can represent 0,1,2 clauses; if two, both must be of the same sign, and are exactly its two occurrences of that sign. Conversely any such representative assignment lifts to SAT by setting used labels to the representative sign, unused arbitrarily.

Every SAT source admits such a representative assignment using ALL n labels. Moreover given any SAT word, normalize in polynomial time (may change its truth assignment). Proof: start with arbitrary true representatives. For unused label u, search the full unsigned incidence graph from u to adjacent clauses. When a clause is currently represented by a single-use label v, continue from v to all its incidences. Stop if a reached clause is represented by a double-use label. If none is reached, let L be all reached labels; all adjacent clauses are represented by distinct single-use labels in L excluding u. Thus |Gamma(L)| <= |L|-1. But every label has four incidences and each clause three, so |Gamma(L)| >= 4|L|/3. Contradiction. Along a shortest alternating path reassign its terminal clause from the double-use label to the preceding label, then work backward, moving each formerly single-use label to the next clause and using u at the start. Double-use terminal label retains its other clause, each moved single-use label still represents only one clause, u now represents one. Thus the same-sign restriction is preserved; at least one more label is used. At most n augmentations, each BFS linear in explicit incidence size; reconstruct and verify all source clauses. All flips may cross the old SAT word, not necessarily preserve arbitrary external pins. This is normalization from a supplied witness, not a solver that acquires it.

Consequently EXACTLY d=m-n=n/3 labels represent two clauses and the remaining 2n/3 labels represent one. The two-clause representatives are disjoint signed pairs, no repeated variable label. Equivalent graph view: choose a rainbow matching D of size n/3 in disjoint union of positive and negative cubic clause multigraphs, where both sign edges of a variable share its label. Remove its paired clauses and its used labels. There are exactly 2n/3 remaining clauses and labels. Find a perfect matching in their bipartite incidence graph. Every matching plus D lifts to a satisfying assignment by setting each label to the sign of its representative(s). Conversely normalization supplies at least one such D on every SAT source. For each fixed D, parsing, pair check, matching, decision and witness lift polynomial (augmenting paths suffice). This is a specialized NP-complete image with polynomial fixed-track terminal, not polynomial acquisition of a successful D. Reject invalid encodings; empty case handled trivially. All source constraints checked. A fixed D failure excludes that representative track, not necessarily even its associated truth-value guards.

## Theorem 2: exact source-level Hall inequalities with polynomial separation
For each signed pair p, endpoints V(p) (two clauses), label l(p); let y_p in {0,1} indicate selection. Require sum y=n/3, at most one per clause and at most one per label. For every clause subset S, write Gamma(S) for its neighbor labels in FULL original incidence. Then D completes iff all inequalities
  sum_p (|V(p) intersect S| - 1[l(p) in Gamma(S)]) y_p >= |S|-|Gamma(S)|
hold. Necessity: restrict matching to unpaired clauses of S, count available labels in Gamma(S). Sufficiency: for S consisting only of unpaired clauses the inequality is exactly Hall on residual graph. For any integral valid D, failed matching yields a violated inequality by reachable alternating-tree set S from an unmatched clause (all reached neighbors matched to reached clauses; one extra root). This is a verified globally necessary cut on representative selections, not a deduction on unguarded truth bits. It excludes every D violating it, independent of which algorithm generated D. A family of one-pair-deleted or zero-deletion tracks can exchange such cuts; no bound on the number of cuts or integer optimization follows. For fractional separation also use a flow/min-cut with capacities 1-sum incident y at clauses and 1-sum label y at labels, infinite-capacity original incidence arcs; if total demand not routed, min-cut yields a violated S. This extension follows from nonnegative capacities under local constraints and equal total supply/demand; not implemented.

## Theorem 3: two cheap relaxations provably cannot decide
Any valid nonempty source has a rainbow pair packing of size n/3, even if UNSAT. Its conflict graph on 2n signed pairs has max degree <=5 (at most four other edges sharing endpoints, plus the opposite sign of same label). Greedy maximal independent set has >=2n/6=n/3, trim to exact size. So merely obtaining the correct number of disjoint pairs is vacuous as SAT test.

Even adding ALL Hall inequalities and relaxing y to real [0,1] never rejects a valid source. Set y_p=1/6 for each pair. Local constraints hold (clause sum 1/2, label sum 1/3), total n/3. Left side for S is |S|/2-|Gamma(S)|/3, which is >= |S|-|Gamma(S)| by 3|S| <=4|Gamma(S)|. Equivalently assign singleton incidence flow z=1/6 on every original incidence: each clause has three pair terms and three singleton terms summing to one; each label two pair and four singleton terms summing to one. This fractional point exists for U198 too. No claim of integrality-gap runtime lower bound. In particular polynomial separation plus LP does not close the integer-track coverage theorem.
