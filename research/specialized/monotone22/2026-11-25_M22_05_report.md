## Theorem A: exact joint chart of two actual relaxed matching tracks

Their symmetric difference decomposes into pairwise vertex-disjoint even alternating cycles C_1,...,C_q, including length-two parallel-edge cycles. The family of all perfect matchings using only edges of M union L is EXACTLY M XOR union of C_j with switch z_j=1. Proof: common edges force their endpoints; every remaining vertex has degree two in the union, and each cycle has precisely its two alternating perfect matchings. Thus all independent switch patterns are realized. Let a_j be the GF(2) projection of the characteristic vector of C_j onto label parities. Then the exact attainable syndrome set in this chart is sigma(M)+span{a_j}; unlike the full matching hull in M22-04, this equals the image, not merely an outer bound. For an initially bad label a binary row prefers unequal phases, and costs one when equal. Branch on one cycle variable for each such bad binary row (deduplicate; at most h variables). ## Theorem C: target-relative coordinated descent
If L is a known exact target, for each label currently correct, either no changed edges or two changed edges: if on distinct cycles, connect those cycles with an equality link. Initially defective labels have exactly one changed edge, thus attach a defect marker to its cycle. Correct labels whose two changed edges lie on the same cycle cancel. Each connected component of the cycle-link graph can be flipped as a unit: no currently correct label becomes defective, and every defect marker in the component is corrected. At least one component strictly improves when h>0. If a subset X of cycles is flipped, defect count is exactly h - number of markers in X + number of equality links crossing X (parallel links counted). This proves existence of coordinated descent relative to a target and explains single-cycle stagnation. It is NOT an acquisition algorithm, since the target was supplied; no inference that small components suffice or a plateau is polynomially navigable.
