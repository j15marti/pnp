## Theorem 1: exact affine hull acquisition without raw-pattern enumeration
For a nonempty relaxed perfect-matching family let M0 be an actual anchor (ordinary matching), and define the syndrome sigma(M) in GF(2)^d by sigma_v(M)=1 iff precisely one of the two pair edges of v is chosen. Equivalently sigma is a linear map of the edge characteristic vector: the coordinate is the sum modulo two. Let S={sigma(M): M perfect matching}; target t=all ones. Exact D feasibility iff t belongs to S; this is not a counting claim.

Let L be the binary linear span of ALL characteristic vectors of perfect matchings of the simple expanded graph. Lovasz's matching lattice theorem, as stated in El Maalouly, Steiner and Wulf, ISAAC 2023 Theorem 20, supplies a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm to compute a basis of L over any finite field. Their Theorem 19 supplies deterministic correct-parity matching (one red-edge parity), with witness recovery by actual edge deletion. Use the basis theorem, not an assumed simultaneous exact matching oracle. All edge weights for a coordinate are binary; expansion preserves identities and size. If a is any vertex incidence row of the nonempty expanded graph, a dot chi_M=1 for every perfect matching. Therefore L intersect ker(a) is exactly the span of chi_M+chi_M0 (even linear combinations). Its linear projection W under sigma is exactly span{sigma(M)+sigma(M0)}. Consequently A=sigma(M0)+W is the exact affine hull of S, acquired by polynomial matching, Lovasz and Gaussian elimination. Alternatively augment an actual syndrome basis using single-parity queries against a basis of its annihilator; a violated linear form returns an actual matching outside the current span, increasing rank. At most d increases, O(d^2) single-parity queries. If every annihilator form is constant, the hull is exact. The latter gives a way to get actual basis witnesses even if the lattice algorithm outputs only abstract vectors. Each call paid, no multiple-parity oracle assumed.

If t outside A, reject D exactly. If t in A, this certifies all affine necessary equations, not existence of an actual matching. See counterexample below.

## Theorem 2: exact rank-basis terminal and puncture identity
Set r=dim W. Choose r coordinate indices P such that restriction to P is injective on W (row/column Gaussian elimination); no arbitrary parity conditions needed. If t lies in A, for every actual perfect matching, sigma(M)|P=t|P iff sigma(M)=t. For each v in P, require exactly one of its two pair edges by branching which is forced and banning the other. At most 2^r assignments; forced edges must have disjoint endpoints, remove them and test residual ordinary perfect matching. Any resulting matching has sigma=t and lifts. If none, D-NO. The theorem works for any certified affine *outer* hull containing S, using its rank and anchor; exact hull minimizes this rank among affine outer hulls. In a recursive version recompute hull after each guarded branch and use a remaining mismatched coordinate; its rank drops by at least one when restricted to the target bit, or the branch is rejected. This version can apply paid guarded UP/failed-literal closure before each recursion and after any accepted new force; branch choices never become global source facts. The 2^r static leaf bound already suffices.

For every relaxed perfect matching, exactly d pair edges are selected, hence sum_v sigma_v = d mod 2. Therefore r<=d-1 for d>0. More strongly: removing the exact-one constraint of any SINGLE double label makes no change whatsoever to feasible matchings when every other exact-one constraint remains. Indeed d-1 pair edges are consumed by the other labels, leaving exactly one for the omitted label. If TWO label obligations are omitted, their joint occupancy is exactly two; either both one (valid) or one zero, the other two (defect pair). Thus the minimal nontrivial label puncture removes TWO obligations, not one. This applies to relaxed label constraints, not to deleting a graph edge (which restricts the matching family) or deleting a source clause (which changes the compiler). This gives a precise source-specific limit on importing NAE owner-puncture or CEC source-edge-puncture results. No claim that two-puncture repair is always polynomial.

## Theorem 3: projected component syndrome acquisition and heterogeneous exact join
Delete all edges not in any perfect matching using forced-edge matching tests. The surviving graph splits into connected components K; their independent perfect matching choices combine exactly (parallel IDs retained). Let S_K be the local syndrome image in the same d coordinates; each local coordinate is a parity of at most two local pair edges. Global S is exactly the XOR sum of all S_K (not their union or independent coordinate product). Compute actual affine hull A_K=z_K+W_K by Theorem 1 or a certified outer hull, with r_K=dim W_K. No full internal edge-indicator patterns need be enumerated. Choose r_K coordinate pivots injective on W_K. Enumerate at most 4^r_K status configurations of the at-most-two local edges for each pivot label, forcing or banning each ID. Every local matching is covered, and all matchings agreeing on pivots have identical syndrome. A successful ordinary matching query supplies its whole syndrome and actual preimage; deduplicate. This enumerates S_K exactly, with <=2^r_K stored syndromes, even if exponentially many internal indicator patterns share one syndrome. Note the stronger statement than M22-03: no exact-one constraints are silently enforced inside components before projecting; the XOR join enforces all of them simultaneously.

If the actual image S_K has size 2^dim(span(S_K+z_K)), it is its own affine hull, because it is a subset of that hull and has equal cardinality. For rank zero or one, every nonempty S_K is automatically affine (one can recover actual matching for each syndrome using a pivot bit and single parity matching). For any acquired affine image, write its syndrome as z_K+B_K u_K with unrestricted binary u_K. After fixing choices on all nonaffine components, solve a single Gaussian system for the remaining affine components so the XOR sum equals t. If successful, recover actual local preimages, union their matchings, check t and source witness. Otherwise try the next nonaffine tuple. Let b=sum actual ranks over nonaffine components, rmax=max local upper/actual rank; time poly(N)*(1+sum_K 4^r_K+2^b), peak storage poly(N)*(1+sum_K 2^r_K) with sequential tuple search. This is polynomial for uniform rmax,b=O(log N); many affine components can have arbitrarily large total rank. If all local images are affine and their exactness is certified independently, the final join is polynomial without bounding their total rank. Do not assume arbitrary affine hull equals image. No claim that local rank or b is universally logarithmic, or that all admissible D can be found.

## Exact counterexample: even all affine matching equations insufficient
Reuse strict SAT X12 (one-based variables; clauses in this order):
[2,6,11],[3,8,9],[3,7,9],[4,5,10],[1,5,10],[1,2,7],[4,8,12],[6,11,12],
[-1,-10,-12],[-2,-6,-11],[-6,-7,-9],[-4,-5,-10],[-1,-3,-4],[-8,-11,-12],[-5,-7,-9],[-2,-3,-8].
D={1,2,3,7}; source SAT word has true variables {2,3,4,5,6}. Every exact matching on D would have to select 3 positive: if 3 negative, its pairs consume clauses 13,16, forcing 1,2 both positive, conflicting on clause 6. Then 7 negative because its positive pair intersects 3 positive at clause 3. But 3 positive and 7 negative consume all four clauses incident to singleton 9 (2,3,11,15). Thus no exact matching on D.

Nevertheless three actual relaxed matching witnesses have syndromes 3,5,9 (integer bit encoding in D order 1,2,3,7). Explicit clause representatives (one entry per clause 1..16, sign inherited from clause):
M3 = [6,9,7,4,5,7,12,11,1,2,7,10,1,8,7,2].
M5 = [2,3,3,4,5,2,8,12,1,2,6,10,1,11,9,2].
M9 = [2,8,9,4,5,2,12,6,1,2,7,10,1,11,7,2].
Every singleton occurs exactly once and every selected double sign occurs at both its clauses. These are genuine relaxed perfect matchings, not source words (one double may occur four times). 3 XOR 5 XOR 9 = 15, so t lies in affine hull, since the combination has odd cardinality. Therefore the hull test alone fails on an authenticated strict source, irrespective of how efficiently or exactly acquired.
