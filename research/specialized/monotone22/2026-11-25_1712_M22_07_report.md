## Theorem A: exact signed occupancy at any source-incidence cut
Let R be any region of variable and clause nodes. Orient each incidence from variable to clause. Let v,c be node counts in R, d,u the numbers of variables in R selecting two/zero occurrences. Let O,I be selected boundary edges directed out/in. Count internal selected incidences at both sides:
    v+d-u-c = O-I.
Hence d-u = c-v+O-I. In normalized mode u=0. The boundary word determines the total local double count, with no independent guessed cardinality or separate count state. At root d-u=m-n=n/3, in normalized mode d=n/3. This holds even if a variable's signed pair crosses the cut: its four incident selection bits are coupled by its actual variable table, not freely assigned. Compatible child tables add the occupancy equations, cancel each internal crossing once, and preserve the conservation law. No claim that count conservation alone implies local feasibility.

A second exact source-geometry restriction: let c_+,c_- be counts of positive and negative clause nodes in R; let delta_+,delta_- be OUT minus IN counts of all crossing edges by sign, selected or not. Then 2v-3c_+=delta_+, 2v-3c_-=delta_-, so delta_+ congruent delta_- mod 3. A single-edge cut is impossible (one signed difference +/-1, other 0). A two-edge cut either has two equal-sign edges in opposite directions (the other signed difference is zero, so same-sign delta=0), or opposite-sign edges in the same direction (both signed differences +/-1 and congruence). Every connected strict source is bridgeless. These restrictions do not assert tractability of large cut atoms. Verified no bridges and all two-cuts for Y6/X12 and 3-copy Y6 ring by edge deletions and BFS: zero, zero and three 2-cuts respectively, all three same-sign opposite-direction.

## Theorem C: infinite SAT family falsifies universal logarithmic raw cut width even for optimal binary merge trees
Take k-by-k toroidal copies of Y6, k>=3. In each copy replace occurrence +2 in first clause by +2 of its east neighbor and occurrence -3 in sixth clause by -3 of its south neighbor (zero-based clause IDs 0 and 5). All other occurrences unchanged. Each replaced sign occurrence is permuted bijectively. Distinct variables per clause, unique signed triples (base clause projections distinct; unshifted variables identify copy), exact22 preserved. Any Y6 satisfying word repeated periodically satisfies all source clauses. Base 14-node incidence graph after removing the two shifted occurrences is connected: executed BFS order [0,6,7,10,11,2,3,4,1,5,9,12,8,13] with variable vertices 0..5 and clauses 6..13. Hence full connected graph has k^2 connected cells with one cross edge per toroidal neighbor bond.

For ANY binary merge tree on 14k^2 leaves, some cluster R has size between one-third and two-thirds of all leaves: descend through a child larger than two-thirds until none, then at least one child has size above one-third. Let P be number of partially included cells. Each partial cell contributes an internal crossing by connectivity. If P>=k/12, cut already has >=k/12 edges. Otherwise, at least k^2/3-P>=k^2/4 full cells and at least k^2/4 empty cells. In the k-by-k toroidal grid the set of full cells has at least k/2 boundary bonds: if >=k/4 rows are mixed, each has >=2 horizontal boundary bonds; otherwise there must be both a full row and an empty row from the two size bounds, giving >=2k vertical bonds. At most 4P<k/3 of these bonds touch partial cells. Thus at least k/6 full-to-empty source occurrence edges cross R. Therefore every merge tree has maximum raw boundary >=k/12 = Omega(sqrt(n)), even on easy periodic SAT sources. This bound concerns RAW explicit edge interfaces, not semantic compressed relations, alternate graph representations or any unrestricted time lower bound. It falsifies a universal O(log N) raw-width theorem; a compact alternative approach may solve these sources immediately.
