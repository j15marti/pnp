## Theorem A: exact affine-direction backdoor equivalence
Every fiber of the map z -> (w_j z)_j, for a basis of W, makes every original owner affine (as a Boolean polynomial of degree at most one) iff W meets each P_i nontrivially. The tangent H=ann(W) has restriction map V -> H* with kernel W. On a fiber the quadratic part of f*g is the wedge of the restricted homogeneous rows, which is zero exactly when they are linearly dependent; equivalently P_i has a nonzero vector in W. The Boolean identity t^2=t accounts for dependent rows even with affine shifts. Thus each of the 2^dim(W) fibers is a Gaussian system after substitution, including all retained guard rows. Solve, reconstruct, verify source; all fibers cover the chart, hence both labels exact. This is the strong affine *linear-observation* backdoor property, not ordinary coordinate backdoor detection, not necessary for SAT. A SAT-only partial portfolio may stop on a verified witness but cannot report NO without full coverage or independent exact certificate.

## Theorem B: polynomial discovery of a factor-two backdoor and lower certificate
Initialize W=0. While an uncovered plane P_i has P_i intersect W={0}, add BOTH its generators to W. Test coverage by membership of a_i,b_i,a_i+b_i using elimination. The planes selected along the way form a direct sum (each intersects the span of all earlier planes trivially). If t planes selected, every admissible W* must contain a nonzero vector from each selected plane; those t vectors are linearly independent by the direct sum. Therefore t <= k <= dim(W)=2t <= 2k. The final W and the actual selected owner list certify the upper and lower bound using only rank checks. At most d/2 iterations; polynomial bit work. Consequently there is a uniform deterministic solver with O(4^k poly(N)) time, polynomial peak storage with serial fibers, no supplied basis or optimal order needed. A prescribed logarithmic cap admits all k <= c log N by using the exact search below or doubling the greedy cap; cap failure is UNKNOWN. This advances S26's greedy maximal private-space observation to a guaranteed approximation of the *minimum codimension* for rank-two owners.

## Theorem C: exact minimum discovery in O(3^k poly(N))
For a desired size limit K, start W=0. At an uncovered P_i, branch on adding one of its three nonzero vectors a_i,b_i,a_i+b_i. Each increases dimension by one. Stop with success when all planes hit, or fail at K with an uncovered plane. Any feasible extension W* containing the current W must contain at least one of these three vectors; following that child keeps W subset W*. Induction proves complete bounded discovery. Search depth K; at most (3^(K+1)-1)/2 nodes. Iterate K from greedy lower bound t up to greedy upper 2t (or original fixed logarithmic cap), stop at first success; sum geometric O(3^k) if run to optimum. Evaluate ONLY the resulting minimum W's 2^k fibers, not all discovery branches. Time O((3^k+2^k)poly(N)) = O(3^k poly(N)); polynomial space in depth-first execution. In particular logarithmic k yields P with full acquisition cost. Linear restriction by any extra consistent affine guard maps W to a space of dimension <=k meeting every still-live restricted plane (if its original chosen direction maps to zero the restricted plane is no longer live). Coordinate or arbitrary affine target tests therefore preserve admission. No assumption that an arbitrary SAT input has logarithmic k.

## Obstructions and complementary families
1. On a fixed chart, deleting j live owners gives k_R <= k_F <= k_R+j: a cover for the retained planes can be extended by one nonzero direction per omitted plane; conversely any original cover covers retained planes. Thus fixed-count deletion alone cannot uniformly reduce this parameter. For a consistent affine restriction, k cannot increase, by the image argument above. Do not use fixed-chart inequality across unrelated chart reparameterizations without proving containment.
2. Exact minimization of k is NP-hard even for always-SAT Horn products z_i z_j=0 for graph edges. Let H be the tangent of an admissible W. In a basis of H, coordinate restrictions are row labels l_i in H*. For adjacent i,j they must be equal or one zero. On connected components of the subgraph induced by nonzero labels, all labels equal. The labels span H*, so dim H <= number of these components <= alpha(G), since one vertex per component is independent. Conversely coordinate tangent supported on a maximum independent set is admissible. Hence k=d-alpha(G)=vertex-cover number. All-zero plus unit vectors are models, so unconditional affine normalization does not shrink this family. Complete graph gives k=d-1 and easy SAT, disproves universal logarithmic k but not other tracks. No unrestricted lower bound.
3. Dense shared factor h=z1+...+zd, products h*z_i=0 for i=1..d-1. Quadratic rank d-1 because kernel of wedge(h,.) is span(h), disjoint from span(e1,...,e_{d-1}). Actual solution set is h=0 plus singleton z=e_d, whose affine hull is all of GF(2)^d. No sound unconditional affine deduction can shrink it. k=1; fixing h makes both fibers linear. A strong coordinate-only affine backdoor needs d-1 pins: if >=2 variables remain free, at least one is among i<=d-1, and its live product retains a cross-term on the free variables. Thus linear observation discovery is genuinely stronger than coordinate search on this chart. The private-copy source encoding is exact; no assertion that every alternate source coordinate basis has high coordinate complexity (indeed an invertible change using h solves it). UNSAT neighbor adds actual h=1 and z_d=0; normalization rejects. The previous first draft with products for all d variables was caught by closure because their XOR is h, forcing h=0; not used as the separation control.
