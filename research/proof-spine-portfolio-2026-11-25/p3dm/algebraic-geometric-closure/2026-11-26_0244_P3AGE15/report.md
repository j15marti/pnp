## 1. Optimal local gauge theorem (PROVED)
For each live tuple vertex use tensor E=|000>+|111>, and owner of degree d use W_d=sum_i |e_i> with exactly one 1. Remove all fixed incidences and covered owners after sound closure; the residual retains native degree-three tuples. Empty uncovered rows reject; degree-one owners can be pinned. In this section d>=1, not necessarily closed. A local gauge independently selects any invertible 2x2 complex matrix G_e per incidence, applies G_e to the tuple end and G_e^{-T} to owner end (bilinear contraction exactly invariant). A vertex is good if transformed tensor is a planar matchgate signature, in particular supported on one parity; bad otherwise. No grouping, auxiliary source extensions, or nonlocal basis transformations allowed in this parameter. Characteristic zero; no restriction to orthogonal, real, symmetric or uniform gauges. For necessity define Z=diag(1,-1) and Q_e=G_e^{-1} Z G_e, so tuple parity means tensor product Q_e has E as a +/- eigenvector. Owner parity is the analogous equation with Q_e^T. Every Q_e is invertible, traceless, an involution.

At any pair of legs of E, the flattening image is span{|00>,|11>}. Product Q_i tensor Q_j must preserve it, since the third factor is invertible and E is an eigenvector. The only product lines in this plane are |00> and |11> (determinant of diag(a,b) vanishes iff ab=0). Consequently the two factors must both be diagonal or both antidiagonal. Applying all pairs shows all three have the same type. In diagonal case traceless involutions have entries (s,-s), s=+/-1, so the eigenvalues on |000> and |111> are opposite. This contradicts parity eigenvector. Therefore every leg of a good E has antidiagonal Q_e.

For d>=3, the two-leg flattening image of W_d is span{|00>,|01>+|10>}. Its general 2x2 coefficient matrix has determinant -b^2, so its only product line is |00>. If W_d is a parity eigenvector, Q_i^T tensor Q_j^T preserves the image and this product line. Thus Q_i^T preserves |0> for each leg (upper triangular), and cannot be antidiagonal. For d=1, a parity eigenvector condition preserves |1>, again excludes antidiagonal. Degree-two W_2 is exceptional: it permits compatible antidiagonal gauges. It follows that an incidence connecting a good tuple and a good owner of degree other than two is impossible.

Define conflict graph B on original tuple vertices and non-degree-two owner vertices, with their actual incidences. Every gauge defect set is a vertex cover of B (discard degree-two defects if any); hence >=tau(B). Conversely choose any vertex cover C, put G_e=H=[[1,1],[1,-1]] if the tuple endpoint is outside C OR the owner has degree two, and G_e=I otherwise. Every tuple outside C uses three H transforms and becomes an even ternary signature with values2. Every non-degree-two owner outside C receives I on all legs and retains W_d. Every degree-two owner receives H^{-T} on both legs, becoming (1/2,0,0,-1/2). These are planar matchgates: fixed-parity ternary/binary signatures satisfy the matchgate identities; W_d is an exact-one signature, realized by a planar star with each arm subdivided once and the outer leaf as external vertex under removal convention. When exactly one external vertex is removed, the center is matched to its intermediate neighbor and all other arms match internally; any other removal pattern fails. Constants/rational signs are legitimate. Thus every vertex outside C is good, and optimum defect count is exactly tau(B), obtained by deterministic bipartite matching and Konig's theorem. This is a parity obstruction sufficient to attain the exact MATCHGATE optimum, not merely a necessary lower bound for parity.

After native unit closure every remaining owner has degree >=2, so let H be the set of high-degree owners (degree >=3). For any S subset H, at least 3|S| incidences land in its neighboring tuple set, each tuple has at most three. Hall implies a matching saturates H. Therefore tau(B)=|H| exactly. No optimization over continuous gauges can improve this parameter; simply choose all high-degree owners as exceptions. This is scoped to the ungrouped native representation after certified pins; NOT a bound for fused regions, signed quotients with redundant rows removed, arbitrary holographic gadgets, decision algorithms, or P versus NP.
