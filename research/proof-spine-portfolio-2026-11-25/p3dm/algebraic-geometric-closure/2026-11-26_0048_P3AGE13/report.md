## 2. Named lemma: uniformly acquire a hierarchy when minimum raw width is logarithmic
Let w(A) be minimum maximum f(S) over branch decompositions of the column ground set (unshifted convention, standard matroid branchwidth = w+1). Screenshot p13 verified. This is a known theorem; the following implementation is an unshifted refinement specialized to represented binary matroids, plus exact source seam integration. Do not conflate this with later FPT algorithms hiding a possibly superpolynomial factor at k=log N.

For integer k>=1, cap K=3k+1. Build a fundamental bipartite graph from an arbitrary Gaussian column basis. In standard form [I|D], a nonbasis vertex connects to basis columns in its binary representation. For every cut S, rank of the crossing adjacency equals f(S): split basis/nonbasis coordinates on both sides; rank(A_S)=|basis intersect S|+rank D[basis outside S,nonbasis inside S], and the complementary formula gives the sum of the two disjoint crossing blocks. f is symmetric submodular, f(singleton)<=1.

Start with all E in one leaf. At any leaf S with more than one element and f(S)<K, split off any singleton; symmetry/submodularity gives both new cuts <=K. If f(S)=K, choose K independent rows X outside S of the fundamental cut matrix with columns in S. For every bipartition P,Q of X, acquire a minimum-f cut Z separating P from Q. This is a polynomial matroid-intersection operation, not SAT. On ground E minus (P union Q), intersect matroids obtained by contracting P or Q and deleting the other. Edmonds min-max says maximum common independent size equals min_U [r(A_(P union U))-r(A_P)+r(A_(Q union (E' minus U)))-r(A_Q)]. The resulting f-minimizer is Z=P union U. Implement ordinary shortest augmenting-path exchange graph on linear matroid circuits, then use reachable-set complement for U; check the min-max rank equality on every executed query. Gaussian elimination suffices for contractions and circuits.

If every tested split satisfies f(Z)>=min(|P|,|Q|), X is well-linked: every cut separating it has f>=min side size. Any subcubic leaf tree with |X|=3k+1 has a cut with both sides containing >=k+1 X leaves: take weighted centroid, largest of up to 3 incident branches has >=ceil(|X|/3), <=half. Thus w(A)>k. This is a structural certificate only, never UNSAT. Otherwise take minimizing Z with f(Z)<min side count. Independence of rows X implies f(Z minus S)>=|P| and f((E minus Z) minus S)>=|Q|, since the crossing adjacency contains independent rows restricted to S. In particular both S intersect Z and S minus Z are nonempty (otherwise f(Z) would be >= one of these bounds). By submodularity,
  f(S intersect Z) <= f(S)+f(Z)-f(S union Z) < f(S);
and the complementary inequality gives f(S minus Z)<f(S). Refine the leaf by those two children. At most m-1 splits; each split costs at most 2^K polynomial rank/matroid-intersection work. The resulting hierarchy has width <=3k+1, or a certified w(A)>k. Algorithm never requires a supplied partition or matching witness.
