## 2. Acquired sufficient anchor and exact laminar recognition (PROVED)
Anchor criterion: choose side Y (or Z). For each anchor vertex u acquire the union of domains of all its incident edges. Retain candidate pairs at u whose domain equals that union. If the restricted projection has a PM, it dominates ALL PMs by the slot bijection matching at u. Two ordinary matching queries (plus initial PM if needed) suffice, arbitrary set intersection patterns. Equal-histogram criterion: for each distinct domain D, compute min and max count of D-type edges over all PMs by mincost matching. If equal for each type, any PM dominates all PMs by matching equal domains. This admits arbitrary domains with constant group quotas.

General exact criterion: let P be distinct nonempty edge domains ordered by inclusion. For any downset I of P write h_M(I)=#edges in M whose domain lies in I. Then M* dominates M iff h_M*(I)<=h_M(I) for every downset. Necessity: a reference slot in I must receive an original slot whose domain is a subset, hence also in I. Sufficiency by Hall, viewing reference slots as left vertices and original slots as right. For any subset Q of reference slots take I the downward closure of their domain classes. Its original-slot count is exactly the number of neighbors of Q, >= h_M*(I)>=|Q|. Thus a perfect inclusion matching exists. This is an exact criterion for slot domination, not merely X footprint containment.

If P is laminar (any two intersecting domains nested), each principal downset consists of a subtree of its unique inclusion forest. Any downset is the disjoint union of principal downsets of its maximal elements: no nonempty domain belongs to two disjoint maximal domains. Therefore it suffices to test the g principal downsets I_D={A:A subset D}. Compute m_D=min_M h_M(I_D) via unit edge cost mincost PM. Compute one M* minimizing the sum of these g counts, using edge weight equal to the number of tested principal downsets containing its domain. If the total equals sum_D m_D then every minimum attained and M* dominates every PM. Conversely if ANY universally dominant PM exists, its count on each I_D is necessarily m_D (itself feasible, and <=all other counts); thus the sum minimum must attain that bound. Exact acquisition with g+1 mincost PM calls, not a search over PMs. Failure rules out this particular dominance representation only. More generally, for inclusion poset of fixed width w, enumerate downsets by their at most w maximal elements, O(g^w) candidates, deduplicate and use the same simultaneous minima test. Fixed-width admission can be checked by enumerating (w+1)-element antichains for fixed w. This generalization is theorem-level unexecuted; arbitrary width and nonlaminar posets do not have a proved polynomial complete dominance recognizer. The anchor and quota sufficient tests still apply.
