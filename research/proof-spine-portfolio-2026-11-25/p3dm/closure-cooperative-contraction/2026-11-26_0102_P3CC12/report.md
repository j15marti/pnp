## 1. NM: near-period conditional mass (PROVED)

Let F(D) count indexed PMs in C avoiding all classes in D. For a potential pair type i, let D_i consist of all classes realizing that type; E_i is the event of avoiding D_i. Acquire a spanning forest of potential label graph of minimum sum F(D_i), using Kruskal. Its types form a basis of W. A good skeleton hits every basis type, therefore H_Q=W; not every spanning skeleton need be good. If F(empty)=0 reject source. For any forest J on basis-event indices define
G(D)=F(D)-sum_i F(D union D_i)+sum_{ij in J} F(D union D_i union D_j).
Pointwise a matching with bad set B contributes 1-|B|+|edges(J[B])|. For B empty this is one; otherwise at most zero since J[B] is a forest. Consequently G(D) is a lower bound on actual good indexed matching mass. Choose maximum-weight spanning forest on event indices using intersection masses for strongest bound within fixed basis. This extends simple union bound, does not require a robust common period, and is exact rational/integer computation. Nonpositive G is OPEN, not NO.

## 3. B4C: balanced two-color four-incidence bond contraction (PROVED scoped)

Enumerate pairs of edges of same color and group by signature XOR. Equal nonzero XOR across two different colors yields a candidate four-edge cut. Every four-edge bond consisting of two edges of each of two colors is found: a zero pair XOR would give a proper nonempty cut subset, contradict bond minimality. Verify actual deletion yields exactly two connected shores, all four edges cross, each shore has equal numbers of owners in all three colors, and each involved color has one outgoing tuple incidence and one incoming owner incidence on each shore.
