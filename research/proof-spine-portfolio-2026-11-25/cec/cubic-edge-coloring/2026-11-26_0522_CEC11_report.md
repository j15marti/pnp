## 1. Exact simultaneous cut certificate
Let x:V->{0,1} be any vertex partition, D(x) its set of monochromatic edges. For any perfect matching M, define E(x,M)=|D(x)\M|. G is colorable iff there exist x,M with E=0. Indeed E=0 means complement G-M is a spanning 2-factor crossing the cut, hence even; alternate two colors on each cycle and assign third to M. Conversely any proper edge coloring supplies M as a color class; bipartition every even complementary cycle, choosing any phase. This works without bounded defect count, no fixed direction supplied, no original target coloring.

Given x, test E=0 polynomially: D must be a matching. Delete endpoints of D; remaining induced graph is bipartite, since all original monochromatic edges were in D. Acquire maximum bipartite matching by augmenting paths, accept iff perfect, add D to it, verify actual source coloring. If no perfect matching, alternating search from unmatched vertices on deficient side supplies Hall set S with |N(S)|<|S| (or use larger unmatched side if all left matched). This is exact NO FOR x, never source NO. If D overlaps at a vertex, two distinct incident monochrome edges cannot belong to M. Necessary balance: with d_L,d_R counts of internal cut edges, 3(|L|-|R|)=2(d_L-d_R). After deleting D endpoints free-side difference is -(4/3)(d_L-d_R); hence compatibility implies equal d_L,d_R and |L|=|R|. All compatible cuts are bisections, not conversely.

For fixed x and no promise D matching, min_M E=|D|-max_M|D intersect M|, ordinary maximum-weight perfect matching (integer 0/1 weights); a maximum-cardinality priority can be implemented by adding m+1 to every edge weight. For fixed M, min_x E equals number of odd cycles in G-M: each odd cycle necessarily contributes >=1, one is achievable independently, even cycles zero. Hence min_{x,M} E equals classical oddness for graphs with a perfect matching. Each exact best response is polynomial; joint optimization/strict universal progress NOT inferred. The cut terminal is simpler than multi-mark suppression and covers arbitrary many same-color defects if partition acquired. Apply split components, bridges NO by flow conservation, bipartite terminal, and exact triangle retraction before costly chart attempts. On bridgeless cubic graphs x_e=1/3 belongs to matching polytope, so edge-by-edge ordinary matching support is universal; it cannot decide the simultaneous condition. ## 2. Main scoped theorem: triangle expansion creates a linear acquisition obstruction
For simple connected bridgeless cubic H on h vertices, T(H) replaces each vertex by a triangle with one port for each original incident edge. External edges are a perfect matching of T(H), with original source IDs; new graph simple connected bridgeless. Let b(G)=min_x |D(x)| (bipartite index), and c(G)=min over compatible x |D(x)|, infinity if none.

Every triangle requires at least one monochrome edge, so b(T(H))>=h. Choose any perfect matching of H, orient each complementary cycle cyclically and orient its matching edges arbitrarily. Every original vertex has both an incoming and outgoing incident edge. Assign the triangle port bit according to that orientation. All external edges cross and every triangle contributes exactly one D edge; b(T(H))=h. All optimal cuts are balanced because all external edges form a perfect matching and cross. Thus this also optimizes over bisections.

Suppose x' on T(H) compatible with M'. Each triangle has exactly one monochrome internal edge (three monochrome would violate D matching); M' contains that edge. Its two endpoints (majority bits) are matched internally, remaining minority vertex necessarily matched externally. Project those selected external edges to a perfect matching M of H. Let x(v) be the majority bit of triangle v. An original edge in M has minority endpoints on BOTH triangles; one outside M has majority endpoints on BOTH. Therefore every external edge is monochromatic iff corresponding original edge is in D(x), and D(x) subset M. Conversely given compatible x,M on H, assign each majority pair bit x(v), minority port opposite, where minority corresponds to M. Select the majority internal pair and external M edges. This lifts x,M to compatible x',M', and |D(x')|=h+|D(x)|. Both directions prove
c(T(H))=h+c(H)
including infinity, and actual witness projection/lift.

Consequently if H nonbipartite, NO maximum cut of T(H) is compatible, even when H is colorable; if H bipartite, some maximum cut is compatible (not necessarily all). ALL maximum bisections fail in nonbipartite case. For G_k=T^k(K4), n=4*3^k, k>=1, c(K4)=2, so b(G_k)=n/3 and c(G_k)=n/2. Every compatible cut has D matching and hence exactly n/2 internal edges. The additive gap is n/6. Furthermore any compatible cut differs from every maximum cut by at least n/6 vertex flips (even after global complementation): at least n/6 new D edges must appear; each has a flipped endpoint and new D is a matching, so distinct flipped endpoints are needed. This excludes even logarithmically bounded Hamming-ball repair of an optimal cut for this family. It is a barrier ONLY to this representation policy, not an unrestricted time lower bound.

Matched-order SAT/UNSAT families T^k(pentagonal prism) and T^k(Petersen), both n=10*3^k. Base exact matching enumeration: prism b=c=2, Petersen b=3,c=infinity. Thus SAT family c=n/2-3,b=n/3, UNSAT has same b=n/3 with c=infinity. They cannot be distinguished by optimal cut size. Every maximum cut of both families fails the matching certificate. This is not claimed to survive triangle closure: both admit an exact productive alternative.
