## 1. Duality of abstract and actual contexts
For fixed k, Omega is the parity-admissible set of ordered boundary words modulo global color permutation. For k=6, |Omega|=31. K(D) is the greatest Kempe-consistent subset of D, computed by monotone deletion: each surviving state, each two-color pair must admit a pairing of relevant boundary ends whose entire subset-switch orbit is in D. Actual unguarded multipole supports are consistent because alternating paths pair their ends and switch independently. Consistent sets are union-closed, including empty; K(D) is their union contained in D and monotone. Define D_K(S)=K(Omega minus S). For consistent S,T, T disjoint S iff T subset D_K(S). Therefore D_K is order reversing, S subset D_K(D_K(S)), and D_K^3(S)=D_K(S) (apply order reversal to extensivity and extensivity to D_K(S)). Thus double dual is a closure on consistent sets. Two consistent sets have identical compatibility against ALL abstract consistent contexts iff their D_K values coincide: sufficiency by subset test, necessity by taking maximal disjoint consistent context itself. This is an upper approximation to real contextual equivalence, not a realization theorem.

Define D_real(S) = union of actual multipole supports disjoint from S (same fixed k). For every actual exterior B, B incompatible with S iff S(B) subset D_real(S), since the union remains disjoint from S. Therefore D_real equality is necessary and sufficient for all actual contextual decisions, even if its union is not itself an actual support. D_real subset D_K. No color counts or guarded transfer.
