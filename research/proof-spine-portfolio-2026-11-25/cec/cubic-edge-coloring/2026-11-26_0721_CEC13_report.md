## 1. Guard all optimal matchings exactly
For fixed vertex bits x let D={uv:x_u=x_v}, and Phi(x,P)=|D\P| for PM P. Compute k=min_P Phi(x,P) by ordinary maximum-weight PM with 0/1 weights. If no PM, global NO; if k<2 then the returned complement has <=k odd cycles because every odd cycle has at least one noncrossing edge, and its number of odd cycles is even since the cubic graph order is even. Hence verified coloring. If k>2, this terminal declines. For k=2, decide whether ANY optimum PM has even complement, not whether a particular tie-break succeeds.
Enumerate all unordered T subset D of size2. Exact guard D\P=T is equivalent to requiring F=D\T subset P, T disjoint P. If F not a matching reject. Delete endpoints of F and both excluded T edges (when still present) to form H. Every edge in H crosses x, so H is bipartite. Every PM of H joined with F yields an original PM with exact cost2, and conversely. This is exact both labels and actual original edge IDs; no marginal feasibility inference. All feasible T collectively cover the optimal face.

## 2. Exact allowed-edge components from one PM and SCC
Find one PM P0 of H (or reject guard). Orient non-P0 edges left to right, P0 right to left. Every non-P0 edge allowed in some PM iff its endpoints lie in same directed SCC: PM symmetric difference consists of alternating cycles, and a directed simple cycle is exactly an alternating cycle that can be flipped. P0 edges always allowed. Thus acquire all allowed edges A in one SCC pass after matching; matched edges not on alternating cycles mandatory. Connected components S_i of undirected allowed graph A are an exact product decomposition of all residual PMs: every PM consists of allowed edges and stays inside components; conversely independent local perfect matchings on A[S_i] join to a PM. Unlike arbitrary weighted general matching support, here all local combinations satisfy the original cost by the fixed T guard. Add each edge of F as a forced two-vertex component. All crossing component edges not in T are necessarily complement edges. Local constraints remain exact, not arbitrary color or phase marginals.

## 3. Local port responses and four-port theorem
Cut the two excluded T edges out of the complement, replacing each incidence by a distinct terminal end. For every local component S, make a port for each crossing complement edge incidence and each T incidence (multiple incidences on one original vertex get distinct IDs). Any local PM induces degree-two paths and cycles inside S with these ports as degree-one leaves. All internal cycles even since F selected and T removed, all remaining edges cross x. Boundary paths pair the ports; retain the actual local PM per attained pairing. Matching choices independent across S. Crossing edges and four terminal wires form one fixed wire matching W on the ports/terminal ends. Components with 0 or 2 ports have one possible pairing, acquire from P0, even if enormous or internally complicated. An allowed component that is a cycle has just two local PMs; acquire both even when arbitrarily long. A general component of size <= fixed a*log(N) permits explicit enumeration in polynomial time (max degree3, branching factor <=3 per matched vertex pair). Other components may use independently proved complete relation acquisition or return UNKNOWN. Fixed test cap eight vertices used in executable, then twenty for three diagnostics only.
For any actual selection of local pairings, global complement has even cycles iff the two T edges lie on one cycle, since cycle parity equals parity of count of noncrossing edges on it. Equivalently after cutting T, the four ends pair crosswise between the two distinct original T edges. This also handles incident T edges; terminal IDs distinguish incidences.
Saturate two exact rewiring rules: if a transition pair appears in every local pairing, join its two external wire ends and erase those ports. If a fixed wire has both ends in one local component, absorb it in each local pairing by pairing their partners or deleting the closed loop. Retain one actual local PM for each new distinct pairing. Each rule preserves terminal connectivity for every word; closed loops contain no T incidence and hence are even. Each step removes two ports, at most O(n) steps. Reapply at stagnation until no rule. Fixed/forced deductions remain under exact T and x.
For large effective components (>4 ports), branch over one attained pairing each. For all remaining components (<=4 effective ports), allow every transition edge occurring in at least one attainable pairing. This is exact for finding a SINGLE alternating path: any vertex-disjoint subset of transition edges on <=4 ports is contained in one actual pairing. For one edge this follows by membership; for two disjoint edges they are complementary and every pairing containing the first contains the second. The argument permits arbitrary multivalued relations, not merely two phases.
Build J with fixed W and all these transitions plus fixed transitions of chosen large words. For each of the four pairs (u,v), u an end of first T and v an end of second T, query ordinary PM Q on J minus {u,v}. If exists, W symmetric-difference Q contains an alternating u-v path. Its non-W edges are disjoint allowed transitions; by preceding observation choose an actual pairing per small component containing all those path transitions. On large components use already fixed word. The chosen path is retained; remaining terminal ends necessarily pair crosswise. Union recorded actual local PMs yields original even complement, verify. Conversely an actual cross path yields a PM after deleting its terminal endpoints by taking path transition edges and W outside that path. Thus at most four matching queries per branch, exact both labels. Coincident W/transition edges cannot be required as a non-W path edge; such coincidence only gives a closed two-edge abstract loop. No simultaneous incompatible paths need be selected: with exactly four terminals, one cross path forces the other. This is the new generalization of CEC06's two-phase matching terminal to a target-free acquired optimal matching face, including multivalued components.
Let R_i be actual reduced pairing tables of large components. Per fixed T work is (product_i |R_i|) poly(N) including local table acquisition when admitted. A fixed polynomial cap on that product gives an exact polynomial terminal (or explicit UNKNOWN if exceeded). If every large component has two words, the bound is 2^L poly(N); logarithmic L is sufficient. No universal bound claimed. Across all T O(m^2) guards, charge all failed/UNKNOWN attempts. Return NO only when all T definitively fail; NO is guard-level, not global source NO. A supplied old matching with exactly two odd cycles and q complementary cycles has O(n^2 2^q) optimal cuts (one defect location on each odd and one phase per cycle). Thus with q=O(log N) and hereditary admitted guards the complete one-neutral-step existence test is polynomial from that matching, without a supplied cut. For arbitrary q, acquisition of the right cut is still open. No global search over old matchings asserted.

## 4. Exact guard-preserving two-edge-cut bypass
Let a connected even side S of a two-edge cut have distinct inside endpoints a,b and outside endpoints u,v. Require all edges incident to S cross x and simple capped graphs (parallel cases may be handled separately; current code skips). Replace S by new crossing edge uv. The internal bipartition places a,b opposite: cap graph S+ab is bipartite cubic, equivalently this follows by equal-degree sum minus its two seam incidences; implementation directly checks opposite. Both cap-edge matching statuses possible, acquired by two ordinary PM tests (delete cap edge for out, delete endpoints for in). Every original PM crosses this cut either zero or twice because |S| even; project zero to uv absent and twice to uv selected. Conversely actual inside witnesses lift either status. D unchanged, Phi identical. For complement, if no crossing factor edges all internal cycles even. Otherwise the two seam edges lie on one inside-to-outside path from u to v, of odd length because they have opposite x labels; replaces odd length1 edge, preserving parity of every outside cycle; internal cycles even. Thus all feasible optimum costs and oddness, including neutral decision, preserved exactly in both directions. This is stronger than ordinary unguarded feasibility contraction for the present objective. It is discovered by enumerating removed edge and bridges of remaining graph, verifying labels, matching statuses and indices. At most n contractions, no need for annotated gadget history; store actual lifts, strictly decrease n. No general weighted semantic reduction for arbitrary flow objective: FP19's shared-zero objective has nonadditive paired seam interactions; do not import this cut-cost proof there without separate adapter.

## 5. Neutral face connectivity is not acquisition
If two PMs optimize the same fixed additive matching objective, each component of their symmetric difference is an alternating cycle with zero weight change; any strictly positive change would improve one PM. Therefore flip those cycles one at a time in <=floor(n/4) flips for a simple graph (cycles length>=4). All intermediate PMs optimal. Every such complement has oddness <=k; if k=2, a route to an acquired even PM never increases oddness above2. This elementary known-style reconfiguration fact requires both endpoints or separate acquisition; it does not select a useful destination. The new component terminal supplies a destination in its exact admitted class. General zero-cost face may be enormous; do not assert polynomial enumeration or shortest reconfiguration.
