## 3. Ternary kernel stability (PROVED)

General lemma: for any fixed 3-uniform 4-regular base hypergraph with distinct variables per owner, the independent incidence-permutation p-lift has, with probability 1-o(1), exactly the pullback of the base GF(3) incidence kernel.

Proof: Fix a ternary word x on lifted variables. In the fiber of each base variable v, let M_v = p!/(a_v! b_v! c_v!) be its multinomial count. For a base clause e={u,v,w}, expose the permutations at the other two incidences, leaving the fiber with maximum M_v last. The ternary sum-zero equation at every copy prescribes one exact ordered word at this last incidence. Its probability is <=1/max_{v in e} M_v. Events for distinct base clauses use disjoint independent incidence permutations, even when the fixed word has the same variable fiber in both clauses. Hence the probability x belongs to the lift kernel is <= product_e max_{v in e} M_v^(-1). For a fixed list of fiber types the number of words is product_v M_v. Since max_{v in e} M_v >= (product_{v in e} M_v)^(1/3), and every variable is in exactly four clauses, the union bound for this type list is <= product_v M_v^(-1/3).

For one fiber, sum M^(-1/3) over all nonconstant types is o(1). Indeed let k=p-largest count, 1<=k<=2p/3. At most 3(k+1) types have that k and M>=binom(p,k). For fixed k the summand tends to zero; for 1<=k<=sqrt(p) use binom(p,k)>=(p/k)^k>=p^(k/2), so sum <= sum_{k>=1} 3(k+1)p^(-k/6)=o(1). For k>sqrt(p), q=min(k,p-k)>=sqrt(p) for sufficiently large p, and binom(p,k)=binom(p,q)>=2^q. Polynomial type count times 2^(-sqrt(p)/3) tends to zero. The three constant types each have M=1. With fixed base variable count b, summing the bound over all type lists with at least one nonconstant fiber gives (3+o(1))^b-3^b=o(1). All fiber-constant kernel words are exactly pullbacks of base kernel words, by the incidence equations. QED.

For the explicit base in Section 2, the lifted kernel is just constants with probability 1-o(1). Then no strong three-coloring exists (a rainbow triple sums to zero in GF(3), and every ternary sum-zero triple is either constant or rainbow). N4FN02 chart must decline even with unbounded kernel enumeration. Nullity 1 implies connectedness, because any component indicator is a kernel word. SAT nevertheless follows from the repeated Boolean witness. Do not infer a NO answer from this chart failure.

## 4. Simultaneous absence of small affine regions (PROVED)

For fixed base b variables, a=4b/3 owners, choose fixed sufficiently small delta>0. In the same lift probability space prove:

P1: every owner set S with 2<=s=|S|<=delta p has at least s+2 distinct variables.
P2: every nonempty variable set I with i=|I|<=delta p has a vertex incident to at least two clauses having exactly one variable in I.

P1: choose the s lifted owners in binom(ap,s) ways and a candidate set of s+1 variables in binom(bp,s+1) ways, enlarging any smaller support. For each base incidence, the selected owner positions are sent into a specified fiber subset of size at most s+1. Sampling without replacement bounds the probability by ((s+1)/p) to the number of selected positions; independent incidence permutations and 3s positions give ((s+1)/p)^(3s). The union bound is <= binom(ap,s) binom(bp,s+1) ((s+1)/p)^(3s). Using binom(q,k)<=(eq/k)^k and (1+1/s)^(2s)<=e^2 bounds this by C^s (s/p)^(s-1) for a constant C depending only on the base. Sum s<=sqrt(p) by geometric ratio C/sqrt(p), and the remaining s<=delta p with C delta<1/2. Total o(1).

P2: fix I. Expose, at each base clause, the lifted owner positions hit by its selected variable copies, using inverse incidence permutations; 4i draws total. Within one incidence there is no replacement. A collision means that an exposed position is already occupied by another incident fiber at that clause. Conditional probability <=i/(p-i)<=2i/p. If all I vertices have at most one singly-I clause, at least 3i incidences belong to owners with >=2 I vertices. Each such owner contributes at least half its I incidence count to collisions, so >=ceil(3i/2) collisions. Union over exposure times and choices of I gives <=binom(bp,i) 2^(4i) (2i/p)^(3i/2) <= (16 e b 2^(3/2) sqrt(i/p))^i. Split sum at sqrt(p), choose delta small enough that upper-range ratio <1/2; total o(1). This is a probabilistic existence argument, not randomized algorithm or efficient all-input source acquisition.

For any nonempty region R of at most delta p owners, every nonempty subset S has binary observation rank >=|S|+1: each connected piece has >=owner count+2 variables (including singleton), and the rank of raw difference rows on a connected support is number of variables minus one. For any nonzero linear form h, quotient by its span loses at most one rank. Rado's theorem (one independent representative per labeled owner iff rank of every subunion >= number of owners) gives independent representatives in the quotient, so impose their opposite-forbidden values plus h=e for either e and solve by Gaussian elimination. Thus actual solutions have full affine hull; any coordinate projection also has full affine hull. This is N4ES07's proven slack argument. No identification of full hull with full support.

Let I be private variables whose four incident clauses are in R, B=V(R)\I. Then 4|I|<=3|R|. If I is empty, set an owner's three boundary ports all 0, a missing word. Otherwise P2 gives v in I with two singly-private clauses. All four other variables are in B and distinct by linearity; pin one pair 00, the other 11. No value of v extends this boundary. Actual projected support is therefore nonempty, proper and has full affine hull. It cannot be affine. This includes neutral full-response deletion. No claim after guard changes or nonlinear rewrites.

Intersect the three probability-1-o(1) events (kernel stability, P1, P2). Every sufficiently large p has an authentic connected SAT example with nullity one and NO nonempty exact affine raw boundary region of size <=delta p = (delta/16)m. Its maximum raw deficiency is n/3+1=4p+1 by N4FN02. Hence neither small affine normalization nor ANY strong-coloring chart suffices universally, even jointly. This is the first load-bearing coverage arrow, not an unrestricted solver barrier.

## 5. Exact nonaffine interface attempt and open step

If a region has no clause containing two private variables, its exact boundary formula has polynomial size without enumerating boundary words. Keep its all-boundary NAE clauses. For private v, each incident pair (a,b) of ports forces v=1 iff a=b=0 and v=0 iff a=b=1. For every ordered pair of these incident clauses (v,a,b),(v,c,d), add the boundary clause (a OR b OR NOT c OR NOT d), forbidding a=b=0,c=d=1. These conditions are necessary and sufficient: for each private variable, independently choose its demanded value, or 0 if neither demanded. Thus at most d(v)^2 ordinary clauses per private variable, exact witness lift; no affine or marginal substitution. This is a bounded-occurrence instance of resolution elimination; it does NOT yield a closed NAE4 or strict-deficiency-one residual, or guarantee additive progress under repeated elimination. If two private variables share a clause, the simple independent lift cannot be used. Charge generated occurrences, original ID map, guard interactions; guard involving private variables must be included in exact elimination or decline. This route shows why nonaffine group summaries remain viable, but the first OPEN arrow is arbitrary NAE4 -> polynomially acquired, exact nonaffine or other productive normalization with controlled repeated interface cost. No general coverage/recurrence.
