## B. Any feasible mask has linearly many uniform owners (PROVED)
On any zero-voltage subfamily, if a pair's owner is mixed, its endpoints must have different ordered colors (d,a) in F2^2. If d differs they already differ; if d agrees, the unique equal-d pair has a_u != a_v. Therefore if all owners are mixed, (d,a) is a proper strong four-coloring, and conversely a proper strong four-coloring on zero-voltage owners implies both physical NAE clauses (a and a+d may individually fail on a mixed owner? In a triple of distinct (d,a) colors, d is necessarily mixed, its equal-d pair has distinct a, so both hold). More generally for arbitrary offsets check the actual signed pair, not an ordinary coloring.

In each of the 3n untouched AG9-minus-two-parallel-row modules of C(F), vertices {0,3,6,7,8} (local IDs) form a K5 in the primal graph: the only missing edges lie within the first two removed rows, and this set takes one vertex from each and all three from the retained row. Every one of its ten pairs lies in an internal zero-voltage owner. If all these owners were mixed, five vertices would require five distinct colors from a four-color alphabet, impossible. Thus every attainable d requires at least one uniform owner per module, on disjoint owner sets. At least 3n of the 72n quotient owners (1/24) are uniform, even when allowed arbitrary nonuniform masks. FN07 separately gives at least n coordinates of each mask bit in all models, because each ES11 A eleven-line core forbids all-zero mask and B/C opposite-mask phase connectors force b=c and b!=c. These are simultaneous bounds on the SAME connected NP-hard compiler. The uniform-owner bound is a raw structural lower bound; it does not guarantee that those owners survive additional semantic contractions, nor that their joined system is intrinsically hard. This K5 is a primal graph clique, not the historical strict-d1 paired-edge K5/Fano matroid obstruction. No automatic identification.
