## 4. Same-family asymptotic rank theorem (PROVED)
Take N4ES07 model: three groups A,B,C each of size p; four independently uniform perfect tripartite matchings, one per supplied partition (equivalently use independent random permutations of each group then align positions; relative matchings uniform). Group labels are NOT supplied to the solver. For fixed ternary vector x let M_A,M_B,M_C be multinomial counts of distinct permutations of its symbols in each group; let M=max M_i. In one partition, condition on the other two groups' permutations and expose the group attaining M. To satisfy all ternary equations, its ordered word is uniquely determined. Probability <=1/M, including impossible assignments. The four partitions are independent, hence probability x lies in kernel <=M^-4.

Group vectors by their three frequency triples. There are M_A M_B M_C <=M^3 vectors of each type. Union bound for one nonconstant type therefore <=1/M. For type triples not constant on all groups let k be maximum number of minority entries (p minus largest symbol count) in any group. Then 1<=k<=2p/3, M>=binom(p,k), and at most 27(k+1)^6 type triples have that k (choose a majority symbol in each group, then its two minority counts at most k; ties only overcount). Sum bounds by
 sum_{k=1}^{floor(2p/3)} 27(k+1)^6 / binom(p,k) = o(1).
Proof: k=1..15 contribute O(1/p). For 16<=k<=sqrt(p), binom(p,k)>=(p/k)^k>=p^(k/2); use crude total p choices times O(p^6) numerator to bound by O(1/p). For k>sqrt(p), q=min(k,p-k)>=sqrt(p) for sufficiently large p, q<=p/2 and binom(p,k)=binom(p,q)>=2^q; polynomial factor is negligible. This proof counts actual possible words, not marginal rank expectations.

With probability 1-o(1) the kernel therefore consists precisely of vectors constant in each group, with the three group constants summing to zero. This is a 2-dimensional F3 space. Nonconstant vectors assign distinct group colors and every owner is rainbow. The deterministic Gaussian test discovers a full witness from owner lists alone, without the planted groups.
