## A. Large-prime deck-group lemma (PROVED)
Use the incidence graph H of the actual source, with five vertex colors (variables and each of four supplied partitions). H is simple, max degree 4. Treat connected components independently. Let G be its color-preserving automorphism group, acting faithfully on M=n+4n/3 vertices. For a prime p>4, every p-subgroup S of G acts semiregularly: if an element of S fixes a vertex, its induced permutation on at most four neighbors has p-power order, so is identity. Induction on distance in the connected graph fixes every vertex. Thus every point stabilizer in S is trivial and every variable orbit has size |S|. In particular |S| divides n. This argument does not require a spectral gap, girth, or genericity.

Suppose the source is a connected regular cover of a SAT base on b variables with deck group D of order p^a, p>4, a>=1, gcd(p,b)=1. Here regular means the specified color-preserving deck action is free and transitive on each fiber, and each lifted incidence corresponds locally bijectively to its base. n=b p^a. D<=G and is a p-subgroup. For a Sylow p-subgroup S of G, p^a <= |S| <= p^a because its order divides n. Hence D itself is Sylow. By Sylow conjugacy S=gDg^{-1} for some actual color-preserving automorphism g. Its orbit quotient is isomorphic as a constraint multiset to the base quotient (all original clause IDs retained). A base Boolean witness lifts along the fibers, and conjugating by g gives an S-orbit-constant original witness. Therefore any acquired Sylow p-subgroup produces a SAT chart. No need to identify the deck group itself, original fiber labels, g, a target word, base or annihilating polynomial. In particular all connected prime cyclic lifts of a fixed SAT base with p>max(4,b) are covered, regardless of voltage magnitudes. Also covers noncyclic p-groups and logarithmic b when gcd(p,b)=1.

For each clause, the first two shifted 1-sets are disjoint and cover all sheets except t=k; third covers that missing point. Thus all triples contain at least one 1 and at least one 0.
