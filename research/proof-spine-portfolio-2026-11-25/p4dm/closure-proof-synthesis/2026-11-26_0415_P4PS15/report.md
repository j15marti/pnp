## 1. Adaptive signed local table theorem (PROVED)
Input loopless plane indexed edge-CSP, Boolean bit x_e shared by two distinct factor endpoints. Each vertex has arity k<=4 and nonempty explicitly listed constant-parity relation R. Cyclic port order fixed by an acquired embedding (parallel edges subdivided for embedding). An orientation sign word s in {+1,-1}^k is admissible at R iff (i) sum_i s_i x_i is constant for x in R, and (ii) the integer corner height relation obtained by integrating the signed bits around the disk is closed under coordinatewise min and max. Precisely, for each x retain the normalized offsets o_0=0, o_j=sum_{i<j}s_i x_i, j=1..k-1; use all translates o+t*1, t integer. The corner cycle's last step can subtract the common divergence. A common reference-bit vector with that divergence subtracts a fixed offset from these modes, leaving min/max closure invariant. For k=0 treat singleton empty word directly. Sign reversal negates the mode relation and swaps min/max, so preserves admissibility.

Claim: the admissible sign words are either empty or the solution set of pairwise signed equalities s_i*s_j=+/-1. Proof:
A. If no two masks differ on four positions (in particular k<=3), any two distinct same-parity masks differ on two positions. Constant divergence therefore imposes one pairwise signed equality per differing pair. Under these equations, the signed differences around the disk have one +1 and one -1, so the difference of their normalized corner offsets has range<=1. Any two integer translates are comparable coordinatewise; min/max returns one input. Thus no further condition.
B. If k=4 and x,y are complementary, twist x to0000 and y to1111 by complementing coordinates where x=1 and changing the corresponding sign. This subtracts a fixed offset, preserving both tests. Constant divergence requires two + and two -. If these are the only masks, exactly the two alternating sign words work. Alternating increments have range1, grouped increments range2; choosing a translate giving opposite strict extrema in the grouped case creates a distinct mixed word, not in the two-word table. Thus one antipodal sign pair.
C. If there is an additional mask z, after twist it has weight2. Conservation forces opposite signs at its two positions and at the other two. This leaves at most four sign words, two antipodal sign pairs. The lattice test can only remove whole sign pairs. Any singleton antipodal pair is a signed equality system tying all signs; a union {s,-s,t,-t} of two distinct pairs is a signed equality system with two classes, obtained by splitting coordinates according to s_i*t_i=+/-1 and tying signs within each class. Empty is explicit decline. This proves the claim without relying on experiments. A constant local enumeration of at most16 sign words and all pairs of masks and relative shifts in [-2k,2k] obtains the exact admissible set and its equality equations. Outside that shift range the two modes are comparable. Acquire equations by testing which pair products are constant. All allowed sign words satisfy them and the classification proves the converse.

Global sign selection: choose a reference endpoint per indexed edge. One sign per edge is its outward sign at that endpoint; other endpoint uses negative. Substitute into each local equation. BFS with signed offsets (or parity DSU) solves the entire orientation system, linear size. Conflict gives a certificate that this particular local lattice-orientation representation cannot be used in the acquired rotation, NOT a source UNSAT certificate. Re-embedding may change admission. The theorem is exact for the stated local condition on unrestricted translations, not a claim that every globally feasible height subset must satisfy this local condition.
