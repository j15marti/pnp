## 2. Full-domain lattice classification (PROVED)
For two local height words A,B, differences D=A-B change by at most 1 between cyclically consecutive corners, since every indexed edge bit is binary. For <=3 corners, D cannot contain both positive and negative entries, hence words comparable and arbitrary union of such mode chains is a lattice. For 4 corners, noncomparability forces D to be +1 and -1 at opposite corners, 0 at the other two. All four edge bits differ. Hence pinning ANY actual port makes the whole bounded integer relation closed under min and max; this is stronger than unit-cell bijunctivity (also median-closed, so threshold-bijunctive).
For a 4-factor there are exactly six signed-conservation masks, three complementary pairs. In cyclic sign ++--, the resolving pair is {0110,1001}; in +-+-, it is {0000,1111}. Rotations, reversal and overall sign inversion cover all six balanced sign sequences. The resolving pair's height words are always comparable across translates; for each of the other two pairs, an incomparable choice yields exactly the resolving pair after coordinatewise min and max. This follows by integrating the four signed edge differences around the cyclic corners: the difference has the alternating-extrema pattern above; min and max replace the plus and minus extreme, giving the displayed pair. Thus the unrestricted translation support is lattice-closed iff either no nonspecial complementary pair is complete, or the resolving pair is complete. Its least lattice completion adds at most the two missing resolving masks. On unrestricted translation domains median closure also implies min/max closure by a third translate chosen above/below both. Bounds and repeated-corner equalities restrict lattice relations by intersection; the unbounded distinct-corner iff criterion is not asserted for these restrictions. Implementation tests the actual normalized offsets.
