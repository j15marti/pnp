## 1. Universal cut-color conservation (PROVED)
Work on raw incidence graph retaining pinned tuple vertices (do not erase physical incidences and then reuse E3 degree arithmetic). For any vertex shore S, t=number of tuple vertices in S, r_c=number of color-c row vertices in S. Write o_c and i_c for cut incidences directed for counting purposes from tuple in S to row outside, or tuple outside to row in S respectively. Degree counting gives
 t-3r_c=o_c-i_c.
For any actual cover with a selected tuples in S, row sums give
 a-r_c=sum(out_c x)-sum(in_c x).
These identities hold regardless of embedding or guards. If cut size<=3, one color d is untouched, so t=3r_d, a=r_d. Thus each signed cut imbalance is divisible by3 and actual selected-bit flux is (o_c-i_c)/3. A 1-edge cut is impossible. For a 2-edge cut, both incidences must share color with opposite orientation; their bits are equal (previous PS13 seam context). For a 3-edge cut the odd total excludes all balanced color imbalances, so all three incidences are one color, all same direction. Choose S the tuple-endpoint shore. Then all three port tuples are distinct (one incidence of that color per tuple), and exactly ONE of the three is selected. The row-side endpoints need not be distinct; the formula still holds and row occupancy handles repetition. This is a universal necessary boundary law, not sufficiency without acquiring actual attainable states.
For a connected graph, every cut of size3 with two connected shores is a bond. General polynomial discovery: enumerate O(m^3) incidence triples, BFS and verify exactly the two shores and physical cut. No 1-edge cuts by above. Retain actual row/tuple IDs, signs, physical darts and source guards. Color conservation on raw graph remains valid when a guard makes the instance NO.
