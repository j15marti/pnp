\section{Source and semantic contract}\label{sec:source}
Let $N$ be the total bit length of an explicitly encoded 3-CNF. Rename occurring variables by a deterministic dictionary, retaining the reconstruction map. Over $\mathbb F_2$, a non-tautological clause forbids one assignment to its occurring coordinates, hence one affine packet $\{x:A_ix=b_i\}$. Remove tautologies, canonicalize repetitions, and detect the empty clause. This is a polynomial-size, uniform, exact source compiler into the affine-packet language. It is not a strict physical tetravalent compiler; any terminal requiring the latter must prove and charge its own adapter.
A residual chart records actual variables, necessary affine equations, conditional guards, and the authenticated remaining constraints. Each accepted transformation has a proved decision identity and a polynomial-cost witness lift (or a separately charged lift). Conditional deductions are valid only under the recorded guard. Run certified unconditional forced-information closure initially, after each strict deduction, and at stagnation before branching. Row reduction, unit clauses, and proved local rules are permitted; complete semantic closure is not an oracle.
\section{Sufficient aggregate progress theorem}\label{sec:aggregate}
\begin{theorem}[Conditional exact polynomial decision]\label{thm:aggregate}
Fix a deterministic implementation, independent of the input, and fixed constants $a,b,c$ with $c\ge1$. Suppose every reachable normalized residual state $s$ of an input of length $N$ has a computable integer potential $\mu(s)\in[1,(N+2)^a]$, and a uniform procedure costing at most $(N+2)^b$ bit operations either correctly decides it (including source verification and necessary reconstruction) or produces an exact Boolean composition of child occurrences $s_i$ satisfying
\[
\sum_i \mu(s_i)^c\le \mu(s)^c-1 .
\]
Assume all states, guards, Boolean composition descriptions, lifts and temporary objects have polynomial bit size in the original $N$, and all remaining per-occurrence overhead, including discovery, failed attempts, closure, verification, canonicalization, data access, composition and lift, is uniformly polynomial in $N$. Then the input is decidable in deterministic polynomial time.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Unfold the computation into an occurrence tree, charging duplicate occurrences independently. Assign credit $\mu(s)^c$ to an occurrence. Each expansion consumes at least one unit and transfers no more than its remaining credit. Each terminal occurrence consumes at least one unit since $\mu\ge1$. Hence the number of expanded and terminal occurrences together is at most $(N+2)^{ac}$, by telescoping, with every edge and composition charged. Exact composition and reconstruction give correctness inductively. Serial depth-first evaluation, storing polynomial-size descriptions and the charged outputs needed for reconstruction, uses polynomial peak space $S$ and polynomial time $T$ on the same execution; cumulative live storage is at most $(T+1)S$ when sampled at step boundaries. Any memoization is optional, and its identity checks, storage and consumer accesses are paid. The theorem does not presume that the potential or its successful procedure exists.
\end{proof}
\begin{obligation}[U-PROGRESS: universal normalization and aggregate bound]\label{obl:progress}
Construct the single uniform procedure and fixed constants in Theorem~\ref{thm:aggregate}, or prove a different unconditional uniform polynomial bound. In particular, a polynomially capped terminal that can decline, an existential decomposition, or a decreasing potential on a pre-certified successful plan is not sufficient. The procedure must cover every reachable residual under its actual scheduling, not just a favorable representation. This is the load-bearing unresolved step.
\end{obligation}
\section{Diagonal quantifiers}\label{sec:diagonal}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:diagonal}
For a fixed total exact deterministic portfolio $A$, if its runtime has no uniform polynomial upper bound, there exists a sequence $F_j$ with strictly increasing encoded lengths $N_j$ and $T_A(F_j)>(N_j+2)^j$. If a particular certified terminal is guaranteed to be discovered and executed (or simulated) by $A$ within a fixed polynomial on a domain $D$, then all sufficiently late $F_j$ lie outside $D$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At step $j$, choose exponent at least $j$ large enough to dominate the finitely many runtimes on inputs no longer than the previous length. The absence of a polynomial bound produces a violating input beyond that length. For the second assertion, any fixed polynomial is eventually smaller than $(N_j+2)^j$. For a finite family of such domains take the maximum of their thresholds. For a countable family the conclusion holds eventually for each fixed finite subfamily, not uniformly for all domains.
\end{proof}
No assertion that this sequence is any particular critical hypergraph follows. A terminal whose discovery is not guaranteed in a fixed polynomial does not qualify. Failure of a fixed portfolio is not a lower bound on unrestricted SAT.
\section{Obstruction to raw local progress: disjunctive packet products (DO15)}\label{sec:do15}
Let $F(x)$ be a conjunction of avoidance constraints $x\notin P_i$ for $m$ nonempty affine packets $P_i$ on $n$ Boolean coordinates. For a fixed integer $r\ge2$ take $r$ disjoint copies $x^{(j)}$, and form
\[
D_r(F)=\bigvee_{j=1}^r F(x^{(j)})
=\bigwedge_{(i_1,\ldots,i_r)\in[m]^r}
\neg\left(\bigwedge_{j=1}^r[x^{(j)}\in P_{i_j}]\right).
\]
The second equality is elementary distributivity. It is an explicit avoidance registry of $m^r$ product packets of rank at most $r$ times the maximum source rank. For fixed $r$ its construction, verification and witness recovery cost polynomially in the original encoded length. If the source is a 3-CNF this also gives a bounded-width CNF of width at most $3r$ without auxiliary variables.
\begin{lemma}[Robust product and criticality]\label{lem:robust}
For fixed $r$, $D_r(F)$ is satisfiable exactly when $F$ is. If $F$ is satisfiable, every consistent affine guard of rank less than $r$ on the product coordinates admits a satisfying extension. If $F$ is inclusion-minimal UNSAT, $D_r(F)$ is also inclusion-minimal UNSAT.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion follows from the disjunction. For a consistent guard $Gx=h$ of rank $k<r$, choose $k$ independent columns of $G$. At least one whole copy contains none of these columns. Fix that copy to any satisfying source word, and solve the guard using the independent columns outside it; the remaining nonpivot coordinates are arbitrary. This proves robustness and in particular full affine hull. For minimal UNSAT, removing any product packet indexed by $(i_1,\ldots,i_r)$ permits a source assignment in each block that violates precisely packet $i_j$ in that block (minimality). The combined word violates only the removed product packet.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Direction and small-batch obstructions]\label{lem:directions}
Every product direction which is not invisible to all packets changes the observations of at least $m^{r-1}$ product packets. For minimal UNSAT $F$, every subregistry of fewer than $m$ product packets has the rank-$<r$ robustness property of Lemma~\ref{lem:robust}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A direction visible to any block observation hits some source packet $i$ in that block. Every tuple with that fixed index, and arbitrary remaining indices, has a nonzero block row evaluation. For the batch result, in each copy the set of source indices occurring in the batch omits an index; the corresponding proper source subregistry is satisfiable. For a guard of rank $k<r$, choose the copy avoiding its independent columns, fix a model of that copy's proper subregistry, and solve the guard outside it. Every product packet in the batch is then avoided.
\end{proof}
These lemmas jointly refute a universal rule requiring a visible direction hitting only a fixed number of raw owners, or an affine deduction/contradiction from a single batch of fewer than $m$ raw product packets (even with a rank-$<r$ guard). On the SAT promise of this product, all rank-one affine probes are feasible. Therefore robust SAT versus UNSAT remains NP-hard on this explicit bounded-rank packet language by the polynomial map from 3-CNF. This is a reduction, not a separation: no claim that the product evades a factorization terminal, matching, general nonaffine inference or arbitrary representation changes is made. Indeed the displayed disjunction exposes a cheap structural shortcut whenever the original instance is easy. A single product deletion need not retain full affine hull; replacing every source copy by a SAT neighbor does. The strict physical compiler is not covered by this argument.

\section{A paid repair for the exposed product obstruction}\label{sec:product-repair}
\begin{lemma}[Discoverable Cartesian factor contraction]\label{lem:cartesian}
For an explicit, deduplicated registry of nonempty positive-rank forbidden affine packets on a common coordinate chart, construct a graph on coordinates by joining the support of each canonical equation row within every packet. Let its connected components be $B_1,\ldots,B_s$. Each packet is the Cartesian product of its projected packets on these blocks. Let $L_j$ be the distinct canonical projected packets on $B_j$, and $L$ the distinct tuples of projections of the original packets. If
\[
|L|=\prod_j |L_j|,
\]
then the original avoidance problem is exactly the disjunction of the $s$ block avoidance problems, each avoiding the packets in $L_j$.
If $s\ge2$, every $|L_j|\ge2$, and all local packets have positive rank, the potential $\mu=1+M$, with $M$ the sum of packet ranks, satisfies the aggregate inequality with $c=1$ on this step.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Canonical Gaussian elimination, support-graph construction, tuple projection and exact dictionary deduplication are polynomial in the explicit registry length. Each tuple belongs to the Cartesian product; equality of cardinalities certifies equality of the lists without enumerating a larger product. The product cardinality has polynomial bit length. Distributivity gives the exact disjunction. Any satisfying child word extends by arbitrary words on other blocks; conversely a satisfying parent must satisfy at least one child. Write $m_j=|L_j|$ and $M_j$ for each child's rank mass. Disjoint block rows give
\[
M=\sum_j M_j\prod_{h\ne j}m_h\ge2\sum_j M_j.
\]
Since $\sum_j M_j\ge s$, the total child credit $s+\sum_j M_j\le M=\mu-1$. Verification and reconstruction are paid. Closure is performed before the test and in each child, with any conditional chart retained; this lemma applies when all remaining constraints are the stated packets in that chart. Whole-space local packets give an immediately UNSAT child and may be handled separately; no contraction claim is needed outside the displayed hypotheses.
\end{proof}
For a Cartesian product of copies of a connected monotone NAE incidence registry, canonical rows within a triple connect its three coordinates, so this graph recovers the disjoint copies. Thus the product obstruction of Section~\ref{sec:do15} does not evade this terminal. The Fano double product has 49 rank-four packets, rank mass 196 and child credit 30; the double product of the six-edge SAT neighbor has rank mass 144 and child credit 26. Removing one product packet gives 48 of 49 tuples and correctly fails the Cartesian equality check. This does not imply that the latter instance is difficult. A general grouping of components, dense change of chart, non-Cartesian registry or further synchronized constraints need not pass this test. No universal factorization theorem is claimed.

\section{Near-Cartesian guarded decomposition (GD21)}\label{sec:near-cartesian}
The exact Cartesian check of Lemma~\ref{lem:cartesian} need not fail merely because a few tuple constraints are missing. The following extension preserves actual violation correlations, even when the projected local packets overlap. It does not require deciding whether any local packet is uniquely violated.

\begin{lemma}[Discoverable sparse-hole decomposition and paid contraction]\label{lem:holes}
Use the same block chart and local lists $L_j$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:cartesian}; assume all remaining constraints are the tuple packets in $L\subseteq\prod_j L_j$ and all local packets have positive rank. Write $H=(\prod_j L_j)\setminus L$ and $h=|H|$. For each $D\subseteq L_j$, let $F_j(D)$ be the local avoidance formula omitting exactly the packets indexed by $D$. Let $\mathcal B$ be the distinct nonempty boxes $\prod_j D_j$ contained in $H$. Then
\[
\operatorname{SAT}(F)=\bigvee_j\operatorname{SAT}(F_j(\varnothing))
 \ \lor\ \bigvee_{B\in\mathcal B}\bigwedge_j\operatorname{SAT}(F_j(D_j)).
\]
For fixed $C$, if $h\le C\log_2(N+2)$, the boxes and Boolean composition can be discovered uniformly in polynomial bit time. Distinct child formulas may be listed once, with all consumer edges charged. With $M=\sum_{P\in L}\operatorname{rank}(P)$ and $M_j(D)$ the retained local rank mass, the step satisfies Theorem~\ref{thm:aggregate} with $\mu=1+M,c=1$ whenever the computed sum of child credits $\sum_{(j,D)}(1+M_j(D))\le M$. Otherwise the rule declines; the identity alone is not a polynomial SAT algorithm.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For an actual word on block $j$, let $V_j$ be the set of all local packet indices it violates. The parent accepts exactly when $(\prod_j V_j)\cap L=\varnothing$. If a $V_j$ is empty, its full local avoidance child accepts. Otherwise the product is a nonempty box contained in $H$. Conversely, a model of $F_j(D_j)$ has $V_j\subseteq D_j$, so models of all children of any such box glue on disjoint actual blocks to an accepted parent. If a full avoidance child accepts, arbitrary assignments on other blocks suffice. This establishes both directions and a witness lift; no inference of count preservation is made.

Compute the missing cardinality by exact integer arithmetic before enumeration, and decline if the cap fails. If it passes, the ambient product has $|L|+h$ tuples, hence the hole list is acquired by polynomial dictionary lookup. For each nonempty subset of $H$, take its coordinate projections, reject unless their Cartesian product is contained in $H$, and deduplicate. Product cardinalities greater than $h$ are rejected before materialization. Every nonempty box is recovered by choosing that box as the subset. At most $2^h-1$ candidates are generated. Thus the complete Boolean composition, its child descriptions, and all consumer edges have polynomial bit size under the cap. Canonicalize child packets and charge repeated access to shared child results; even without result caching, distinct children can be evaluated once as inputs of the explicit parent Boolean circuit. Child closure may be performed independently with its own guard provenance, and the credit check can conservatively use the pre-closure mass. If closure enlarges a representation, the implementation must pay and recertify its credit; no cost-free semantic closure is assumed. The stated inequality is a direct check before dispatch.
\end{proof}
For a single hole $H=\{(i_1,\ldots,i_s)\}$, the only additional term is the conjunction of the single-deletion children. Put $m_j=|L_j|$, $M_j=M_j(\varnothing)$, and $R=\sum_j\operatorname{rank}(P_{j,i_j})$. Before closure the total credit of the $2s$ children is $2s+2\sum_j M_j-R$, whereas the parent mass is $\sum_j M_j\prod_{k\ne j}m_k-R$. Hence contraction holds whenever
\[
\sum_j M_j\left(\prod_{k\ne j}m_k-2\right)\ge2s.
\]
In particular this holds for two blocks with at least three positive-rank packets each, or at least three blocks with at least two each. A Fano double product with one tuple deleted has parent mass $192$ and total child credit $56$, whereas the previous exact Cartesian test declined. A two-by-two registry of rank-two point packets with one hole has mass $12$ and credit $16$, demonstrating that even this exact rule is not always credit-decreasing. Local singleton affine closure is stagnant on rank-two avoidance packets (their complements have full affine hull); no semantic closure cost is hidden.

This addresses a concrete failure of raw Cartesian acquisition, not the universal U-PROGRESS obligation. A connected chart with no such block decomposition, a dense missing-tuple registry, or a failed credit test is not covered. In particular, the diagonal argument cannot assume that an arbitrary critical source has a sparse-hole presentation; proving such a presentation or another paid contraction remains necessary. See the GD21 checker and report in the guarded-DAG namespace for finite controls.

\section{Low-rank guards cannot guarantee raw rank-mass progress (DA15)}\label{sec:da15}
This section attacks Obligation~\ref{obl:progress} on connected linear signed NAE registries rather than products. It concerns a specified closure and representation, not every possible inference rule. On a linear 3-uniform hypergraph $H$, an edge $e=\{u,v,w\}$ forbids one observation word on the two-dimensional plane $U_e=\langle e_u+e_v,e_u+e_w\rangle$ over $\mathbb F_2$ (signs change only the right-hand sides). Distinct planes have zero intersection because distinct edges share at most one vertex. Use a consistent affine chart $Gx=b$ of rowspace $W$; the residual packet observation rank is $\dim(U_e/(U_e\cap W))$. Here closure means Gaussian substitution, deletion of inactive packets, rejection of active rank-zero packets, and forcing the complement of an active rank-one packet, iterated after each strict deduction. This is paid singleton affine closure; arbitrary semantic or nonlocal rules are not included.

\begin{lemma}[All one-bit branches fail the raw rank-mass credit test asymptotically]\label{lem:da15-one}
For any nonzero linear query $a\cdot x$ on a linear signed NAE registry with $m\ge1$ distinct edges, both guard children survive the stated closure without a local contradiction. Each retains either all $m$ rank-two packets or exactly $m-1$ rank-two packets. The retained projected observation planes are pairwise distinct. Even allowing exact affine-hull closure of every pair of retained packets gives no further row. Thus for every fixed $c\ge1$, with $M$ the residual packet rank mass and $\mu=1+M$, neither choice of a dense query nor an invertible change of coordinates makes the two-child inequality in Theorem~\ref{thm:aggregate} hold for all sufficiently large $m$ under these operations.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $a$ lies outside every $U_e$, each packet remains active of rank two, and no singleton deduction occurs. If $a\in U_e$ for some edge, it is unique. One branch makes that packet inactive; the other gives one rank-one avoidance packet, forcing an independent row of $U_e$. The chart rowspace is then $U_e$ and that packet is inactive. Every other packet remains rank two since its plane intersects $U_e$ trivially. In either case there is no rank-zero active packet. For two remaining planes $U_f,U_g$, equality of their rank-two images modulo the chart would imply $\dim((U_f+U_g)\cap W)\ge2$. This is impossible for $\dim W=1$. If $W=U_e$, equality would require $U_e\subseteq U_f+U_g$, impossible since the support of $U_e$ contains all three vertices of $e$ while $f\cup g$ contains at most two of them. No duplicate or strict inclusion between rank-two packets is possible. The complement of one rank-two packet has full affine hull. If two rank-two forbidden packets together exclude an entire affine hyperplane of the chart, their sizes (one quarter each) force them to be disjoint halves of that hyperplane. Two affine hyperplanes within the same affine hyperplane can be disjoint only if parallel; hence their original observation planes coincide, already excluded. This proves stagnation of pair affine closure. Child credits are at least $(2m-1)^c$ each, versus parent $(2m+1)^c$. Their ratio to the parent tends to $2$, violating the required sum. These statements depend only on linear observation relations, so a simultaneous invertible reparameterization cannot evade them.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Guard-seed amplification bound]\label{lem:da15-seed}
Suppose the Berge girth of the linear hypergraph is greater than $2k$ and the initial consistent affine guard has rank $k\ge1$, with arbitrary dense rows. Before the stated singleton closure terminates (possibly with a local contradiction), it acquires at most $2k-1$ independent necessary rows. Its final chart rank is at most $3k-1$. If closure does not reject and maximum vertex degree is $D$, then at least $m-4D(3k-1)$ original packets retain rank two and are not identical or contained in another surviving packet. Bounds with a negative right-hand side are vacuous.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For any intermediate chart rowspace $W$, consider the equivalence graph on source vertices with pair rows $e_u+e_v\in W$. Maintain a spanning forest of its components, using such pair rows as independent vectors. Initially add at most $k$ forest edges. When an active rank-one packet forces a new row, choose an original pair row of its plane independent of $W$, with the necessary right-hand side. Since rank has fallen to one, some nonzero pair row of the same plane already belongs to $W$. The new pair row thus joins two components and introduces at most one previously isolated vertex. Include it in the forest and then add enough other newly implied pair rows to span all equivalence components. These latter edges may introduce two vertices each. With $q$ explicit forced rows the forest rank is at most $k+q$, hence there have been at most $k$ other edges in total, including initial ones. All $q$ distinct forcing triples are fully contained in the nonisolated set of at most $q+2k$ vertices; once a packet forces a row it is inactive forever. If $q$ first reaches $2k$, those $2k$ triples occupy at most $4k$ vertices, whereas an incidence forest of $2k$ three-edges requires at least $4k+1$ vertices. A Berge cycle of length at most $2k$ follows, contrary to girth. Thus $q\le2k-1$. This argument accounts for extra pair equivalences generated by dense initial guards, rather than presuming they are coordinate pins.

Let $r=\dim W$ after closure and $B$ be the union of the supports of all row vectors in $W$ of Hamming weight at most four. A basis selected from these vectors spans their span, so $|B|\le4r$. Every original triple avoiding $B$ has rank two on the chart. Its projected plane has zero intersection with any other projected plane: otherwise some nonzero pair vector from each distinct original plane has their sum in $W$ of weight at most four, with at least one coordinate from the triple avoiding $B$ (linearity of $H$), a contradiction. The same support argument rules out packet containment with any remaining rank-two packet. At most $D|B|$ triples meet $B$. This proves the bound. For $k=0$, no singleton row is forced at all.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Raw interface persistence]\label{lem:da15-width}
Let $\omega$ be the minimum, over binary factor trees on all original observation planes, of the maximum cut intersection dimension of the two rowspace sums. Delete $d$ owners and quotient by a chart rowspace $W$ of dimension $r$. For at least two retained owners, every factor tree on the retained projected planes has a cut of intersection dimension at least $\omega-2d-r$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Extend a retained tree to all owners by attaching the deleted leaves in a subtree at an existing edge. Cuts within the new subtree have intersection dimension at most $2d$. Any original cut's dimension rises by at most $2d$ from adding the deleted observation rows to either side, using the dimension formula. Thus a retained tree before quotient has maximum width at least $\omega-2d$. For any two rowspace sums $X,Y$, the exact quotient identity is
\[
 \dim(\pi_W X\cap\pi_W Y)=\dim(X\cap Y)+\dim(W\cap(X+Y))-\dim(W\cap X)-\dim(W\cap Y).
\]
The right side is at least $\dim(X\cap Y)-r$ (the first added term is at least each subtracted term). Apply this to the same cut. No assumption that quotient width is monotone is used.
\end{proof}
In particular, for the bounded-degree logarithmic-girth, linear raw observation-width families claimed and proved in the DO09 report, any surviving chart from an arbitrary initial guard of rank $k$ with $2k$ smaller than the girth retains linear raw width when $k=O(\log n)$: take $d\le4D(3k-1)$ and $r\le3k-1$. This is conditional on the DO09 structural family theorem and concerns only singleton closure, duplicate deletion and the original observation planes. It is not a lower bound for algebraic matching, nonlinear normalization, arbitrary pruning, or SAT algorithms. The one-bit credit failure also holds on simple satisfiable linear families; it cannot establish a simultaneous hard sequence. On the Fano root and all 21 sign neighbors, exhaustive finite controls confirm the one-bit statement (all 127 nonzero masks, both values, 5588 charts). Additional rank-two charts on loose cycles of lengths 5--7 verify the seed bound on small instances. See DA15 report and checker.

\paragraph{Constructive repair for guards across factor blocks (conditional).}
Lemmas~\ref{lem:cartesian} and~\ref{lem:holes} require independent block coordinates after any chart parameterization; a remaining cross-block affine guard must not be dropped when gluing witnesses. Given an explicit Boolean OR of block-product terms from those lemmas, and a global affine guard $\sum_jG_jx_j=b$ of rank $r$, acquire for each term and block the actual attainable local syndrome set $S_j=\{G_jx_j:x_j\text{ satisfies that block term}\}$ using at most $2^r$ exact local queries with the syndrome equations retained. Unconstrained blocks use Gaussian elimination. A term accepts exactly when $b\in S_1+\cdots+S_s$; Boolean XOR-convolution by a table of at most $2^r$ states and $O(s4^r)$ operations per term, with actual witness backpointers, gives an exact composition. All calls, including failures, and all consumers of stored local results are charged; the local queries are child occurrences unless an already paid terminal decides them. At $r=O(\log N)$ this bounds acquisition and composition, not the total cost of deciding the local queries; the aggregate credit gate must still cover every child. This repairs cross-block gluing without asserting universal availability. It complements the persistence obstruction above, rather than assuming all connected critical charts factor.

\noindent\textbf{U-PROGRESS remains open.} Any full closure must either supply a different polynomial potential or prove a uniformly discoverable nonlocal representation change on the remaining connected critical registries, or another paid complete terminal. A one-bit split with raw rank mass cannot fill this gap.


\section{Red-team audit of semantic reuse and paid closure (DR15)}\label{sec:dr15}
A possible way to evade the raw-credit obstruction of Lemma~\ref{lem:da15-one} is to merge semantically equivalent guarded DAG states rather than expand both. The following reductions identify the cost of a \emph{complete} merge or closure oracle. They do not prohibit sound incomplete syntactic merges or constructive structural certificates.

\begin{lemma}[Semantic merge and backbone barriers]\label{lem:dr15-merge}
On explicitly represented affine-packet avoidance charts of maximum packet rank five, testing equality of accepted Boolean assignments is coNP-complete, even when both charts have an explicitly supplied satisfying assignment and full affine hull, and paid singleton affine closure at their roots is stagnant. A complete polynomial-time canonical key for semantic equality on this domain would imply $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$. On maximum rank-four charts with a supplied satisfying assignment and stagnant singleton closure, testing whether a specified coordinate is forced is likewise coNP-complete.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $F(x)$ be arbitrary 3-CNF with nonempty nontautological clauses. For fresh bits $s,t$ define $B=\neg(s\land t)$ and $G=B\lor F(x)$. Explicitly, for each forbidden source packet $P_i$, the gated forbidden packet is $P_i\times\{(1,1)\}$. This adds exactly two independent rows, hence rank at most five; $B$ itself forbids one rank-two packet. Both $B$ and $G$ contain $(x,s,t)$ for every $x$ and selector word $00,01,10$. They have the common all-zero witness and full affine hull (the zero word and all unit vectors are present), and no rank-zero or rank-one packet. Therefore the specified singleton rule makes no deduction. Yet $B=G$ exactly when $F$ is UNSAT: a satisfying source word distinguishes them at $s=t=1$. This is a uniform polynomial reduction; a differing assignment witnesses inequivalence in polynomial time. Comparing complete canonical keys would decide this equality, and hence 3-SAT. For the forcing assertion use $H=\neg t\lor F(x)$, with one extra row per packet. It has a known witness and no singleton rank below two; $t=0$ is necessary exactly when $F$ is UNSAT. A model with $t=1$ certifies nonnecessity. In particular complete affine-hull acquisition is at least as hard as this test even on always-SAT charts; the paid BD14 support queries cannot be omitted.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Projected semantic equality has an additional quantifier]\label{lem:dr15-project}
Given an explicit bounded-rank affine-packet chart $R(u,v)$ with designated exterior variables $u$ and private variables $v$, testing whether its exact existential projection is the full exterior cube is $\Pi_2^{\rm P}$-complete. Hardness still holds at maximum packet rank five, with an explicitly supplied source witness, full affine hull of both the source support and the exterior projection, and stagnant singleton closure at the source root. Thus complete equivalence testing of two implicitly projected relations is $\Pi_2^{\rm P}$-complete under these restrictions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Start with a standard $\Pi_2^{\rm P}$-complete quantified 3-CNF instance $\forall u\,\exists v\,F(u,v)$. Add the two fresh exterior selectors above and use $G(u,v,s,t)=\neg(s\land t)\lor F(u,v)$. Its projection is
\[
 Q(u,s,t)=\neg(s\land t)\lor\exists v\,F(u,v).
\]
It equals the full exterior cube exactly when the quantified instance is true; all the witness, hull and local-closure properties follow as before. The target full cube may itself be presented by an empty packet registry. Membership follows from the displayed universal-existential definition. For general equality of two such projections, each containment has the form $\forall u,v\,\exists w\,[\neg R_1(u,v)\lor R_2(u,w)]$; conjunction of the two containments stays in $\Pi_2^{\rm P}$. The private variable sets can be renamed apart. All reductions use explicit polynomial-size registries, not a truth table of projected words. The promise of a full affine hull does not provide an exact compatibility summary.
\end{proof}

The two lemmas apply to proposed universal semantic cache keys, not just to flat rank bounds. They do not establish $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$, and do not establish that the hard inputs of Lemma~\ref{lem:diagonal} must encounter these tests. In fact $G$ is trivially SAT as a decision input; it can be solved without the expensive semantic equality question. If a proposed universal shortcut relies on a polynomial-time verifier and polynomial-size certificates for \emph{every} true projected equivalence, the last lemma implies $\Pi_2^{\rm P}\subseteq\mathrm{NP}$ (and hence a collapse of the polynomial hierarchy); merely proving soundness of selected structural certificates does not imply that completeness claim.

\paragraph{Safe constructive alternative and aggregate accounting.}
Use only exact, paid syntactic identity of canonical authenticated packet/guard charts with the same exterior/private coordinate identities and semantics (or a checked explicit isomorphism with a witness lift). Store the full canonical representation or compare full bytes after hashing; hash collisions alone never certify equality. Keep source reconstruction maps and consumer guard provenance separately. If a residual is a projected relation rather than a raw chart, syntactic equality of its authenticated defining charts is sufficient, not necessary; never identify it just by its affine hull, marginal projections, or a decision bit. Each cache hit, key construction, failed lookup, retained result and consumer edge is charged. For the conditional theorem, one can evaluate a finite explicitly emitted Boolean composition once per syntactically identical child, treating its unique input gates as child occurrences and charging all edges in the parent overhead; this is the precise interpretation of Lemma~\ref{lem:holes}. It is not a bound on the number of globally distinct reachable keys. A universal polynomial bound on the actual acquired states and edges of such a sound incomplete merge policy, or an independent terminal covering the remaining charts, is still required by Obligation~\ref{obl:progress}.

Finite controls in the DR15 checker include the Fano root, all 21 sign and 14 budget neighbors, two quantified controls and 80 seeded random 3-CNF inputs. They check both gated identities, actual affine hulls, and explicit projected supports by exhaustive enumeration; they are not a polynomial projection algorithm. No full portfolio or formal kernel verification was performed.


\section{Pure-exclusion degree feasibility and a source-compiler obstruction (BW15)}\label{sec:bw15}
This section addresses the physical-adapter part of Section~\ref{sec:source} and Obligation~\ref{obl:progress}, not arbitrary equality/balance networks. Use a loopless explicit multigraph of maximum degree four counting every parallel edge. Each edge $uv$ has an actual sign $s_e\in\mathbb F_2^2$ and excludes $x_u+x_v=s_e$. All colors are two bits. Full-color pins may be given. Other explicit unary lists are allowed only if, after fixed-color deletion and singleton closure, each remaining list has size at least its remaining degree. Empty lists reject; a failed capacity test declines this terminal.
\begin{lemma}[Degree-feasible pure-exclusion terminal]\label{lem:bw15-dp}
The preceding domain admits uniform deterministic polynomial decision and actual witness reconstruction. In particular arbitrary full-color pins of a degree-four pure-exclusion network with otherwise unrestricted colors are eligible.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Deleting a fixed vertex after checking its fixed neighbors removes at most one color per incident physical edge from each remaining neighbor's actual list, and removes that edge from its degree. Thus the list-size lower bound is preserved. Propagate singleton colors after each deduction and test capacity at stagnation; no semantic closure oracle is used. Empty lists reject. A remaining component is a degree-feasible correspondence-coloring (DP-coloring) instance: the forbidden cross-color pairs for each edge form a matching restricted to the two actual lists. We invoke the established theorem of Bernshteyn, Kostochka and Pron (2017), Theorem 9, \href{https://doi.org/10.1134/S0037446617010049}{On DP-coloring of graphs and multigraphs}, stating that a connected multigraph fails to be DP-degree-colorable exactly when every block is a uniformly multiplied clique or cycle. Hence a component with at least one other block is satisfiable for these lists. In the exceptional all-brick case, use the actual block-cut tree, with messages consisting of the supported colors of the shared articulation vertex. A clique has at most five vertices under the physical degree cap, so its message is acquired by testing at most $4^5$ words. A cycle message follows by path dynamic programming for each fixed first color, with a closing-edge check. At vertices intersect the child masks and the actual local list. All edge multiplicities and signs are checked, so the exact block messages glue on actual colors, not independent bit marginals. Isolated vertices have a nonempty list. This gives both-label decision, without treating existence as a witness oracle.

Recover a witness by at most four full-color trials per vertex, retaining a successful trial and its actual pin. Each trial leaves the remaining nonfixed lists degree-feasible after deletion, so the same exact decision method applies; these are witness choices, not unconditional necessary pins. Reclose after each deduction and before dispatch, and verify the original source word. There are at most $4n+1$ decision calls. Block recognition, explicit cover/list construction, the constant-size clique checks, cycle tables, propagation, failed trials, composition, and verification all have polynomial cost in the explicit encoding length. A serial dense implementation has polynomial peak storage and cumulative live storage at most $(T+1)S$. No complete affine semantic closure, model count, or arbitrary cross-hub guard is assumed.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Unpinned native-star compiler obstruction]\label{lem:bw15-star}
Every BW13/BW14 unpinned pure-exclusion network consisting only of private star centers, each joined to three distinct terminal neighbors by edge multiplicities $(2,1,1)$, and with all physical degrees at most four, is satisfiable. This includes overlapping endpoint cycles, with arbitrary fixed signs. No exact both-label compiler of arbitrary SAT into this subclass exists, regardless of its output size.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A connected component with a vertex of degree below four can be colored in reverse spanning-tree order rooted at that vertex. Each other vertex has at least one still-uncolored incident edge when processed, and the root has fewer than four incident edges, so a color always remains. Otherwise the component is four-regular and bipartite between centers and terminals. Suppose every block is a uniformly multiplied clique or cycle. Choose a leaf block of the block-cut tree (or the sole block). In a bipartite graph a clique has at most two vertices; there are at most two parallel edges between any center and terminal, so a noncut vertex in such a block cannot have degree four. A cycle block has even length at least four, and contains at least two centers, at least one of which is not a cut vertex of the component. Such a center has exactly two distinct neighbors in the entire component, contrary to the three distinct neighbors of a native star. Thus some block is not a brick. Lemma~\ref{lem:bw15-dp} gives satisfiability and polynomial witness acquisition. Every image in the proposed unpinned compiler range would therefore be SAT, whereas the source language has UNSAT inputs. This contradiction concerns this exact range, not all four-valent networks.
\end{proof}
The pure-exclusion terminal tolerates arbitrary full-color pins; one such pin in an otherwise unpinned native-star component is always extendible by global two-bit translation. A low-only pin on a degree-four terminal instead has list size two, and cannot be presumed degree-feasible. Additional higher-arity equality/balance predicates, arbitrary affine couplings, occupancy and tags are not edges of this cover. A uniform exact polynomial compiler to the larger eligible pure-exclusion domain would itself suffice to solve SAT in polynomial time, but none is proved. In particular BW13's exact doubled Fano star registry exceeds degree four; the degree-four single-star version is satisfiable even though the Fano source is UNSAT. The finite BW15 checker executes block recognition, block DP and witness trials (319 controls, 240 SAT/78 UNSAT/1 decline; 836 assertions) and checks the Fano neighbors separately. No generic E4/B4 compiler or complete portfolio execution is claimed. U-PROGRESS remains open; this rules out one specific universal compiler target rather than another raw-width heuristic.


\section{Matching transfer and convex--lattice stagnation (M21)}\label{sec:m21}
The following attacks the actual aggregate gap in Obligation~\ref{obl:progress}: can the M19/M20 weighted matching normalization, combined with the MS19 signed-integer lattice filter, supply universal progress when the raw affine guard fallback of Lemma~\ref{lem:da15-one} fails? The answer is negative for the explicit representation below, but there is an exact alternative transfer to the M18 syndrome terminal.

\begin{lemma}[Exact wheel transfer and full-support relaxation]\label{lem:m21-wheel}
Let $H$ be any linear 3-uniform signed NAE registry with $m$ owners and $n$ occurring variables. For each owner let $A_i=\{0,1\}^3\setminus\{f_i,f_i+\mathbf1\}$ be its six allowed words. A disjoint union of planar seven-rim wheels, each with one of its seven spokes deleted, encodes the registry exactly with either $q=3m-n$ integer equality quotas or $q$ binary XOR target bits. The construction and witness lift are polynomial. In the integer-quota representation, the M20 convex-face and local signed-integer lattice filters retain every option at the root. Moreover, after either value of any one source variable is fixed, these filters retain every option consistent with that pin. This includes UNSAT instances.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The hub must choose exactly one of six spokes, and deleting its rim endpoint leaves an even path with a unique perfect matching. Assign each spoke one actual local word. For each variable of degree $d$, take a star on its $d$ occurrences; on each of the $d-1$ star edges impose equality of the two endpoint bits, using integer difference zero or XOR zero. Each matching now chooses one local word per clause. The equalities are necessary and sufficient for an actual global word. They are not omitted in any decision or lift. There are $O(m)$ graph edges and $q$ rows, with coefficients in $\{-1,0,1\}$ for integer quotas.

For the root, put probability $1/6$ on each option: all occurrence means are $1/2$ by complement symmetry. This is a strictly positive feasible point of the product-of-simplices relaxation sliced by the quota equalities. It therefore certifies that every option survives M20's convex-face pruning. The product of the local distributions also witnesses positivity of every reachable coarse derivation transition; it does not assert that any actual derivation has the target quota.

For a source pin $x_v=b$, use means $1/2$ on all other variables initially. In each owner containing $v$, the other two bits exclude exactly one pair $p$. Set their means to $1/2+\epsilon$ if the corresponding bit of $p$ is zero, or $1/2-\epsilon$ otherwise, with $\epsilon=1/32$. These prescriptions are consistent because linearity makes the other variables of distinct owners through $v$ disjoint. The three allowed pairs, expressed as deviations from $p$, have distribution $(1/2-\epsilon,1/2-\epsilon,2\epsilon)$ on $01,10,11$. For every owner not containing $v$, start with the uniform distribution on its six options. For each of its three coordinates there is a pair of allowed words differing only in that coordinate; transfer the desired signed mean change along that pair. The total magnitude of transfers is at most $3\epsilon<1/6$, so every weight stays strictly positive. This gives a joint rational witness of full permitted support in both guarded children.

For the signed-integer affine relaxation, the difference lattice of the six local words is $\mathbb Z^3$: for each coordinate there is an allowed pair differing only there. The three permitted words after fixing one coordinate likewise generate the entire affine integer lattice in their two free coordinates. To certify any one permitted option, fix it in its owner, choose arbitrary integer (even Boolean) values for all other source variables consistent with the current pin, and represent the resulting local occurrence word in every other owner by integer coefficients on allowed words summing to one. Negative coefficients are permitted in this relaxation. All quota equalities hold. This is a certificate of lattice membership for each guarded option, not an actual matching. It also certifies survival under any necessary outside-lattice envelope computed from these option sets. Since neither filter removes anything else, alternating them does not produce a strict reduction. All these witnesses have polynomial encoding length; the rational construction uses denominator 96 and the signed-integer construction needs only a base word and at most three unit-difference transfers per owner.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[No extremal-basis rescue of the flat defect product]\label{lem:m21-defect}
For the unpinned representation of Lemma~\ref{lem:m21-wheel}, let $C$ be its $q$-row integer quota matrix on local words and let $K$ be the convex hull of attainable quota vectors before imposing target zero. Then $K$ has dimension $q$ and zero is an interior point. For any injective integer quota basis transformation $R$ (with $q$ independent columns), M19's coordinatewise defect product, even after valid coordinate gcd normalization, is at least $2^q$. In either one-source-pin child the same claim holds with $q'=q-(\deg(v)-1)$ after removing the fixed equality rows.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The local six-word polytope has full affine dimension three by the unit differences. The star difference rows for each variable are independent on the distinct occurrence coordinates. Thus the image of the product of the local polytopes has full dimension $q$. The strictly positive distribution in Lemma~\ref{lem:m21-wheel} is in the relative interior of that product; its image is interior in $K$. Equivalently every nonzero linear functional on the quota space attains values strictly on both sides of zero. After a pin, the incident local polytope has full dimension two on free bits, and deleting the fixed variable's equality rows gives the same proof. Every independent nonzero integer transformed row therefore has an integer defect at least one. Any valid gcd scaling still leaves a strictly positive integer defect because the target has a signed-integer affine certificate, so the target minus the attainable minimum belongs to the corresponding difference lattice. There are at least $q$ independent rows, giving the product bound. For a connected incidence graph, $3m\ge m+n-1$, hence $q\ge m-1$. This is a lower bound on a particular \emph{state-budget formula}, not on the number of actually visited states or on running time of arbitrary algorithms.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact grouped joint cut rank]\label{lem:m21-cut}
In the XOR version of Lemma~\ref{lem:m21-wheel}, assign each edge the joint column consisting of binary endpoint occupancy and its syndrome label, as in M18. For every partition into whole owner wheels, the dimension of the intersection of the two column spans equals the number of source variables having occurrences on both sides.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a variable of degree $d$, the $d$ occurrence syndrome vectors are the columns of the binary incidence matrix of the occurrence star, with rank $d-1$. Their only nonzero dependence is the sum of all columns. Thus a nontrivial bipartition of the occurrences has intersection dimension one; an unsplit variable contributes zero. Distinct variables have disjoint row coordinates. Since owner wheels have disjoint occupancy coordinates, any common joint column-span vector has zero occupancy. Conversely each occurrence syndrome vector lies in the zero-occupancy subspace of its owner: take the XOR of the incidence vectors of the two perfect matchings for allowed words differing only in that coordinate. Their endpoint occupancy cancels and their syndrome difference is exactly that vector. No other syndrome directions occur in the owner's edges. Summing over owners proves the formula.
\end{proof}
This transfer provides an exact alternative to integer-defect enumeration: retain the equality constraints as actual XOR targets and use the M18 occupancy-disjoint join and exact affine payload on a supplied decomposition. A grouped tree with logarithmic maximum variable boundary gives polynomial evaluation, closure and source lift; the local eight-vertex wheel refinements add at most a constant to the cut bound (splitting one owner adds at most its three occurrence directions and eight occupancy directions). The tree acquisition cost remains separate. The raw observation overlap in Lemma~\ref{lem:da15-width} is bounded above by the corresponding variable boundary, since observation rows have support on their owners' variables. Thus the DO09 raw-width premise also obstructs a uniformly small \emph{grouped} matching width. It does not exclude a differently interleaved tree, exact parity matching, S20's applicable one-hot terminal, or another nonlocal representation.

The unpinned Fano plane is a concrete UNSAT control with a full-support rational and signed-integer relaxation. The 21 single-owner sign changes and four loose-cycle families are SAT controls. An exact checker also tested both coordinate-pin children, 406 charts total (391 SAT and 15 UNSAT), 12,936 local signed-integer pin certificates, and 2,884 grouped cut-rank equalities using actual wheel edges. No discrepancies. This finite evidence does not prove universality. The physical strict four-valent adapter is not supplied (the wheel hub has six spokes), complete semantic closure is not free, and neither the product-state bound nor the grouped-width bound excludes efficient alternative solvers. A bounded-rank parity or nonaffine guard must be carried and reanalyzed; the pinned relaxation claim is only for one source coordinate. U-PROGRESS and unconditional COST remain open. A useful next attack is a paid rule which exploits actual equality compatibility without enumerating either the global integer defect product or the grouped boundary states, and a numerical aggregate credit covering its failures.

\section{Uniform strict signed E4/B4 source compilation and reverse normalization (I21)}\label{sec:i21}
This closes the physical source adapter in Section~\ref{sec:source} for explicit signed equality/balance networks, but not for the narrower pure-exclusion star class of Lemma~\ref{lem:bw15-star}, nor for any more restrictive geometric wheel-only class. It attacks Obligation~\ref{obl:progress} by testing whether the actual compiler removes the nonlinear compatibility in the DA15/M21 critical cores.

\begin{lemma}[Strict simple tetravalent source compiler]\label{lem:i21-source}
There is a uniform deterministic polynomial-time both-label reduction from explicit 3-CNF to simple loopless signed four-regular Boolean port networks with only E4 (all port bits equal) and B4 (exactly two port bits one) vertices. All wires enforce XOR equal to their explicit sign. A valid network word yields an actual original source witness in polynomial time; given a source witness a network word is constructible in polynomial time.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
After source normalization, pad clauses of length one or two by repeating literals, and handle the empty clause by a fixed contradictory signed NAE instance such as $\operatorname{NAE}(x,x,x)$; an empty conjunction needs no constraints (or a fixed SAT control). Use a fresh shared reference bit $r$ and one fresh bit $y_i$ per clause. Replace each clause $(a\lor b\lor c)$ by
\[
 \operatorname{NAE}(a,b,y_i)\ \land\
 \operatorname{NAE}(\neg y_i,c,r).
\]
For a fixed word on $a,b,c,r$, a choice of $y_i$ exists exactly when these four bits are not all equal: the first constraint restricts $y_i$ only if $a=b$, the second only if $c=r$, and the two requirements conflict precisely when $a=b=c=r$. Here $a,b,c$ are literal values of the chart bits. Decode each original variable as $x_v+r$. When $r=0$ the four-bit NAE is precisely the original clause; when $r=1$ complement every chart bit, which complements every literal and preserves all NAE predicates. Conversely fix $r=0$ and choose each $y_i$ by checking its two values. This is a linear-size NAE registry, allowing repeated positions.
For each signed NAE triple with literal values $a,b,c$, introduce a private bit $d$ and a B4 vertex enforcing $a+b+c+d=2$ over the integers. Such $d$ exists uniquely exactly when the triple is NAE. Duplicate the entire list of B4 vertices, synchronizing \emph{all} underlying bits, including the private $d$ of each triple, between copies. Every underlying variable now has an even positive occurrence count $2t$ (unused coordinates can be omitted and reconstructed arbitrarily). For $t=1$, directly connect the two occurrence ports with wire sign equal to the XOR of their literal signs. For $t\ge2$, form a path of $t-1$ E4 vertices, with equality wires internally, and attach the $2t$ occurrences to its available ports with their respective literal signs. There are exactly $4(t-1)-2(t-2)=2t$ ports. Each occurrence bit is therefore its common variable bit XOR its literal sign. The path enforces no other condition. All ports are used, every vertex has degree four, and the two copies are distinct vertices, so the direct two-occurrence wire has distinct endpoints. Parallel wires are possible but can be eliminated uniformly: replace each wire $uv$ of sign $s$ by a private copy of $K_5$ with one edge $pq$ removed, all five vertices E4, with zero internal signs and external edges $up,qv$ of signs $s,0$. The internal graph is connected, so its common bit enforces the original signed transport and is uniquely determined. All its degrees become four. Distinct wire gadgets have disjoint vertices, making the entire graph simple and loopless.
These transformations preserve both decision labels, and decoding follows the signed equality components and reference gauge. For a given assignment satisfying the NAE registry the strict network has exactly one port-word lift (the private $d$ bits and E4 gadget bits are determined). No constant multiplicity is claimed for the initial 3-CNF-to-NAE stage. The number of graph objects is linear in literal occurrences; binary identifiers and reconstruction dictionaries cost polynomial bits. Construction, degree and sign verification, witness lift, and source verification are deterministic polynomial bit work.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Reverse affine normalization and exact survival of the NAE core]\label{lem:i21-reverse}
Every explicit signed E4/B4 network admits a uniform polynomial-time affine chart and a rank-at-most-two affine avoidance registry exactly equivalent to its port assignments. For the compiler of Lemma~\ref{lem:i21-source} applied to a signed NAE registry, the chart can be chosen to recover that registry exactly (up to duplicate deletion and the usual constant/rank reductions). Thus the physical degree cap alone does not discharge universal normalization.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Form the signed transport and E4 equality equations and add even parity at every B4 vertex. First perform certified Gaussian closure, rejecting inconsistent affine equations and substituting their actual affine solution chart. On the even-parity four-bit subspace, B4 fails exactly at the all-zero and all-one words. These are exactly the affine event $p_1+p_2=0,\ p_1+p_3=0$ (all sums here in $\mathbb F_2$), since parity forces the fourth bit equal when the first three agree. Substituting affine chart expressions gives one forbidden affine packet of rank at most two for each B4. Remove inconsistent forbidden events, reject whole-chart events, and turn every rank-one forbidden event into its complementary necessary affine equation. Reclose and repeat after every such deduction, and at stagnation. Each strict equation lowers chart dimension, and duplicate removal is exact. All of these operations are polynomial in the original encoding; no semantic oracle is assumed. The retained chart and port dictionary reconstruct every accepted word. Conditional equations, if imposed later, remain under their actual guards.
For the constructed network, signed equality components are exactly the shared underlying NAE variables and private $d$ bits. B4 parity solves $d=a+b+c$ over $\mathbb F_2$; the duplicated parity rows coincide. The resulting free coordinates are exactly the original NAE variables, and each duplicated B4 gives precisely the original signed NAE forbidden packet. In particular, if each triple has three distinct variables, its packet has rank two and its affine hull of legal local assignments is full. Root singleton affine closure and individual local affine-hull acquisition make no deduction. On the positive Fano registry, every pair of the seven variables occurs in exactly one triple: a nonmonochromatic triple contributes exactly one monochromatic pair, whereas any two-color partition of seven vertices has at least $\binom32+\binom42=9$ monochromatic pairs. Hence the root is UNSAT despite this stagnation. Sign neighbors can be SAT on the same geometry. This explains why the exact strict adapter does not convert M21's compatibility problem into independent physical choices, and why the pure binary exclusion terminal cannot be substituted for it. It does not rule out other global inference.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Consequences and aggregate gate.}
The I19 matching adapter applies to the explicit strict graph: B4 is represented by four ports and two private dummies joined to every port; E4 by two disjoint internal port-pair edges carrying the same unique binary tag; an unsigned wire by a direct edge and a negative wire by a subdivided edge with a private midpoint. Exact perfect matching requires the external occupancy bits to satisfy the signed transport and all B4 balances. For each E4 the two tagged internal indicators must agree, forcing all four external bits equal. Each B4 has exactly two internal lifts and all other choices are determined, hence the tagged perfect-matching multiplicity is $2^b$ per native word, where $b$ counts B4 vertices. This is an explicit polynomial-size physical adapter, not ordinary untagged matching. Dropping tags or assuming their effective rank universally bounded is not licensed. A uniform polynomial decision algorithm for this strict signed language would now suffice for arbitrary 3-CNF; the same is true for the rank-two affine avoidance language. The two adapters and reverse closure have fixed polynomial time and peak storage bounds on the same serial execution, with cumulative live storage at most $(T+1)S$. They produce no recursive children and no hidden oracle queries. They do not establish a credit-decreasing step for arbitrary connected nonlinear residuals of Theorem~\ref{thm:aggregate}. In fact the reverse map recovers the critical registry, so compiler-plus-local-closure alone cannot be the universal progress rule. The precise unresolved requirement is an exact paid global compatibility step on this rank-two chart (or an alternative algorithm), with an unconditional aggregate bound. SRC is proved for the signed E4/B4 language; U-PROGRESS, universal SEM/CLOSE implementation, and unconditional COST remain open.

The I21 checker constructs actual simple four-regular signed graphs (including private wire gadgets), contracts the signed equality equations before any enumeration, and compares complete small native supports and decoded 3-CNF projections against independent source enumeration. 94 controls (86 SAT, 8 UNSAT), including the Fano root and 21 single-occurrence sign neighbors, 390828 equality-component words and 126670 assertions, no disagreements. This is computational evidence for the adapter, not a polynomial SAT solver. The full matching backend, reverse rank-one closure implementation, and manuscript formal kernel verification were not executed.


\section{Exact coordinate-basis discovery versus signed support rank (BD15)}\label{sec:bd15}
This section attacks Obligation~\ref{obl:progress} directly: can one always use a polynomial number of signed linear-transfer states after affine closure, or convert the rank-two residual of Lemma~\ref{lem:i21-reverse} to a polynomial implication system? The former fails even on easy SAT inputs; the latter admits an exact recognition criterion but fails on the unchanged Fano core. Neither failure is an unrestricted runtime lower bound.

\begin{lemma}[Polynomial discovery of a common rank-two coordinate basis]\label{lem:bd15-basis}
After paid affine guard substitution and certified rank-zero/one and same-plane closure, let the remaining explicit forbidden packets have rank two on a consistent free-coordinate chart. Deduplicate their observation planes (not their forbidden right-hand sides). Let $\mathcal U$ be the distinct two-dimensional row spaces, $D$ the nonzero forms belonging to at least two distinct planes, and $R=\dim(\sum_{U\in\mathcal U}U)$. A common independent family $B$ containing a basis for every $U$ exists if and only if
\[
 |D\cap U|\le2\quad(U\in\mathcal U),\qquad
 |D|+\sum_{U\in\mathcal U}(2-|D\cap U|)=R.
\]
The condition, an actual family $B$, and the exact decision and source witness on this domain are uniformly polynomial-time computable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each plane has three nonzero forms. If two different planes intersect nontrivially, their intersection is a line. In a common independent coordinate family, intersections of subspaces spanned by subsets of the family are spanned by the intersections of those subsets. Hence every form in $D$ is mandatory, and a plane can contain exactly two members of the family. Conversely, take all of $D$, and in each plane add arbitrary nonshared forms until it contains two. A form outside $D$ belongs to exactly one plane, so these additions neither duplicate nor alter another plane's count. The resulting family spans the whole observed row space, and has exactly the displayed cardinality; equality with its rank is precisely independence. Necessity follows from the same count: restrict any common family to forms in the observed planes. This proves a constructive equivalence, not merely an existential change of basis.
For each retained packet rewrite its two equations in the selected basis of its plane, including the actual right-hand sides. The packet excludes exactly one pair of Boolean values, i.e.\ one ordinary two-literal clause on the coordinates $y_a=a x$, $a\in B$. Independence means every Boolean word on these coordinates lifts to the current chart by Gaussian elimination; no tag or correlation is dropped. Solve the implication graph by the strongly connected component criterion, and lift a satisfying word by the same linear system, verifying the original source. One may instead use paid reachability and coordinate self-reduction. Canonicalization, forming $D$ from three forms per plane, rank, reconstruction and verification are polynomial bit work in the explicit original encoding provided the chart size is polynomial. Rank-zero/one and same-plane closure uses exact four-word local supports, adds a necessary row or detects contradiction and restarts; each strict row reduces the free dimension. All deductions remain under any external conditional guard. At stagnation the recognition rule either decides or declines, not an uncharged branch. There are no recursive children for a successful dispatch.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Signed linear support-transfer obstruction on easy normalized charts]\label{lem:bd15-rank}
For an even $L$, take the $L\times L$ square grid $G$ on $n=L^2$ variables and an actual shared reference bit $r$. Impose $\operatorname{NAE}(x_u,x_v,r)$ for every grid edge. The rank-two chart is SAT and has full affine hull, and Lemma~\ref{lem:bd15-basis} solves it in polynomial time. Nevertheless, every ordered read-once linear transfer over a field whose scalar amplitude is nonzero exactly on the satisfying assignments has a cut of dimension $2^{\Omega(L)}$, regardless of the variable order and signed cancellation. This also holds for a binary variable-leaf tensor tree having such a linear factorization at every cut.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $y_v=x_v+r$; the edge predicates become $y_u\lor y_v$. All-one $y$ and each single-zero perturbation satisfy, so their affine hull is the whole $y$ cube. The reference bit is independent, giving full affine hull in $(x,r)$ as well. The forms $x_v+r$ are independent and span each observation plane in pairs. There is no missing unconditional affine equation to acquire.
Fix the reference to zero for the lower bound. In any order of the grid variables take the balanced cut; in a binary variable-leaf tree one can instead select an edge separating at least $n/3$ and at most $2n/3$ grid leaves (ignore the reference leaf). Such a cut has at least $L/4$ crossing edges: if at least $L/4$ rows contain both sides, each gives a horizontal crossing; otherwise both a completely processed and a completely unprocessed row must exist by the balance condition, giving a vertical crossing in every column. In a graph of maximum degree four, greedily select a crossing edge and remove crossing edges having an endpoint equal or adjacent to one of its endpoints. At most $2(4+1)4=40$ edges are removed per selection, giving an induced matching of size $k\ge L/160$.
Fix every grid variable outside its $2k$ endpoints to one. With $r=0$, exactly the matching's $k$ disjunctions survive. Index left endpoint assignments by the subset $A$ of one bits and right endpoint assignments by the subset $B$ of zero bits. The resulting amplitude submatrix has nonzero entry exactly when $B\subseteq A$. In a common order of subsets by increasing size it is lower triangular with nonzero diagonal, regardless of the actual nonzero amplitudes. Its rank is $2^k$ over the field. At an ordered linear transfer cut of width $w$, all amplitudes factor as a left row of length $w$ times a right column of length $w$, including after fixing bits; hence every submatrix has rank at most $w$. The same factorization applies to the specified tensor-tree cut. Thus $w\ge2^k$. This is a support-rank argument, not just an exact-count or characteristic-function lower bound; arbitrary signs and cancellations cannot fix this particular linear interface model. It does not constrain arbitrary nonlinear keys, multi-pass algorithms, decision-preserving rewrites or representations that need not encode the full original support.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Consequences for universal progress.}
For a linear NAE hypergraph with distinct variables per triple and $m$ owners, distinct planes have zero pairwise intersections, so $D=\varnothing$ and the candidate basis requires $2m$ independent forms. In the connected DO01 critical registry the total observation rank is $n-1$ (its 2-section graph is connected); every vertex has degree at least two, hence $3m\ge2n$ and $2m>n-1$. The exact common-basis criterion therefore fails on that raw registry and its same-registry sign neighbors, including Fano ($14$ required, rank $6$). The I21 strict compiler and reverse closure do not change this fact. This is a counterexample to the proposed universal coordinate-basis normalization, not to an arbitrary nonlocal decision-equivalent transformation. It is also essential not to mistake failure of a full-support representation for hard SAT: the grid family is decided by the implication terminal even while every ordered linear support transfer has superpolynomial width in the explicit input length. Conversely, polynomial circuit description of the support is trivial and does not give a polynomial linear-transfer state bound. The portfolio should dispatch the paid basis recognizer before any full-support expansion, and then use actual decision certificates rather than demand universal preservation of all support amplitudes. Remaining non-coordinateizable connected residuals require a new globally discoverable progress rule or another uniform decision algorithm; U-PROGRESS and unconditional COST remain OPEN.

\section{Rank-Hall core extraction and the remaining signed-basis problem (DS15)}\label{sec:ds15}
We attack Obligation~\ref{obl:progress} after the exact I21 source adapter. The following polynomial preprocessing is not restricted to coordinate-private directions, Cartesian charts, bounded-width cuts, or low-rank global guards. First absorb the actual affine guard by Gaussian parameterization (or reject inconsistency); retain its lift. Perform certified singleton closure before, after every deduction and at stagnation. Work on the resulting explicit positive-rank nonempty forbidden packets, each described by an independent row basis of $U_i$ and the forbidden right-hand-side functional $f_i:U_i\to\mathbb F_2$. A packet is avoided as soon as one row $a\in U_i$ has $a x\ne f_i(a)$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact rank-Hall core and polynomial discovery]\label{lem:ds15-core}
For owner set $E$, put $R(J)=\dim\sum_{i\in J}U_i$, $d(J)=|J|-R(J)$ and $\delta=\max_{J\subseteq E}d(J)$. The intersection $C$ of all maximizers of $d$ is itself a maximizer, and is uniformly polynomial-time discoverable. Every word satisfying the packets in $C$ has a polynomial-time constructible extension preserving all its $C$ observations and satisfying every packet in $E$. Thus the input is equisatisfiable with the restriction to $C$, with an exact observation-preserving lift. If $C=\varnothing$ this is a paid SAT terminal. If $C\subsetneq E$ is nonempty, the unary reduction strictly decreases $\mu=1+\sum_i\dim U_i$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Rank of a sum of subspaces is submodular by the dimension formula, so $d$ is supermodular. Its maximizers are closed under intersection and union. On a given owner set $J$, use the disjoint labeled copies of each owner's \emph{basis rows} as a ground set of a partition matroid (one row per owner) and a binary linear matroid. The maximum common independent set has cardinality $|J|-\max_{K\subseteq J}d(K)$ by the Rado partial-transversal theorem (equivalently Edmonds' matroid intersection min--max theorem). The ground set has only the total explicit row count; no enumeration of every element of a rowspace is required. Standard shortest augmenting path matroid intersection with Gaussian independence and owner tests is deterministic polynomial bit work. Compute $\delta$ once and for every single-owner deletion $E\setminus\{i\}$. The owner $i$ belongs to $C$ exactly when that deletion strictly lowers the maximum deficiency. This uses at most $|E|+1$ intersection computations, including failed deletion tests; it does not assume that a greedy single deletion of the maximizing set would find the smallest maximizer.

Let $W=\sum_{i\in C}U_i$. For any $J\subseteq E\setminus C$, maximality gives
\[
 R(C\cup J)-R(C)\ge |J|.
\]
These are precisely the Rado conditions for an independent transversal of the projected basis rows in the quotient by $W$. Acquire it by one more intersection computation, then let $a_i$ be its actual original row for each outside owner. Given a model of $C$, keep the value of every row of a basis of $W$ and impose $a_i x=f_i(a_i)+1$ for all outside owners. Independence modulo $W$ makes these equations consistent for every prescribed $W$ word; Gaussian elimination lifts the model and verifies the full parent. The converse is restriction. Conditional guards were absorbed in the chart, so the lift preserves them. Rank mass decreases by at least one for a proper nonempty core because all deleted packets have positive rank; no branching occurs. The retained rowspace can be represented in its own basis with a polynomial lift to the original chart. Reapply closure after contraction. The number of rows, ground elements, matrix bits, augmentation calls and lift records is polynomial in the explicit input length; a serial implementation has polynomial time and peak storage, hence cumulative live storage bounded by $(T+1)S$. This is a polynomial preprocessing on every explicit chart, not a decision of every nonempty core.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The irreducible rank-Hall core remains NP-complete]\label{lem:ds15-residual}
Every inclusion-minimal UNSAT positive-rank packet registry has $C=E$, and for every proper subset $J\subsetneq E$,
\[
 |E|-R(E)>|J|-R(J).
\]
In particular the deficiency is positive. Deciding rank-two avoidance charts satisfying this strict deficiency condition, with singleton and same-plane closure stagnant, is NP-complete. On such a nonempty core of rank $r$, the signed compatibility problem is exactly the existence of a maximum independent owner-row transversal whose imposed opposite right-hand sides yield a word satisfying \emph{all} owners, including those not represented.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A proper maximizing subset of a minimal UNSAT registry would be SAT and would lift to a parent model by the preceding lemma. For a smallest maximizer $C$, no proper subset of $C$ attains $d(C)$; on the restricted registry this is the displayed strict condition. For NP-hardness apply the uniform I21 rank-two compiler, Gaussian singleton and BD15 same-plane closure, and deduplication, then core extraction. Closure eliminates rank-zero and rank-one packets or detects contradiction; a same-plane family forbidding at least two distinct words either rejects or forces an affine equation. Surviving distinct rank-two planes remain distinct and rank two after restriction and rowspace coordinate compression. For fixed NO and YES strict cores use the positive Fano registry and its neighbor changing the forbidden word of the triple $(0,1,2)$ from $00$ to $10$ in the basis $(e_0+e_1,e_0+e_2)$, respectively. Use Fano lines $012,034,056,135,146,236,245$. Every proper nonempty subset of $j$ lines is connected on its union and has union size at least $j+1$: for $j=1,2,3,4,5,6$ this follows from line size three, pair intersection one and the fact that exactly four lines omit any fixed vertex. Its rank is at least $j$. The full rank is six, so the full deficiency is one and is strictly maximal. The NO label is the Fano pair-count contradiction; assigning ones on $012$ and zeros elsewhere gives a model of the sign neighbor. Both registries have distinct planes and stagnant singleton/same-plane closure. Map an empty core to this fixed YES chart and a closure contradiction to the fixed NO chart. This is a uniform deterministic many-one reduction from 3-SAT, not a promised free source family. Membership in NP follows from polynomial recognition of the strict condition by the extraction procedure and verification of a model.

For a nonempty strict core, $\delta=|C|-r$, so a maximum independent owner-row transversal has size $r$. Its opposite-row equations determine the values of every observed row. If $x$ satisfies all packets, the good rows of each owner, those with $a x+f_i(a)=1$, span $U_i$: they are a nonempty coset of the kernel of a nonzero linear functional, and their pairwise sums generate the kernel. Rado's formula on these good row sets yields a transversal of size $r$, consistent with $x$. For rank two the complete candidate row set has just three nonzero rows per owner. Conversely any such transversal whose induced word passes all original packets supplies a model. Finding the appropriate transversal is not solved by ordinary maximum matroid intersection: for forbidden points $00,01,10$ on two bits, choose $x=1$ as the opposite row of the first point and $y=0$ of the second. They form a maximum independent transversal but force the third forbidden point. The same core has model $11$ and deficiency one (this elementary example is removed by same-plane closure). The failure persists on the same-plane-closed Fano sign neighbor above: take ones on $034$, zeros elsewhere. All original triples except $034$ are nonmonochromatic and the $012$ observation word is $11$, not the newly forbidden $10$. The six good-row sets of the other owners span their respective planes, and the proper-subset rank condition just proved supplies an independent good-row transversal of size six. It forces exactly the observed word of this assignment, hence still violates $034$. This is a maximum transversal despite the existence of a different satisfying word. Thus arbitrary maximum-basis selection fails even for a closed SAT core with the smallest positive deficiency. No assertion that all low-deficiency cores are hard is made.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Cooperative integration and diagonal consequence.}
Unlike the BS14 private-pair rule, Lemma~\ref{lem:ds15-core} can delete a whole exterior with no direction touching only one or two owners. Attach to a Fano core four rank-two observation maps on private two-bit words $a,b$, namely $a,b,a+b,a+\omega b$ over $\mathbb F_4$ with $\omega\notin\{0,1\}$; add the same observed core linear form to one bit of each output. The four quotient planes satisfy the Rado conditions (any two distinct maps span four rows, and each singleton has rank two). Every nonzero private direction has at most one zero map and thus hits at least three owners. Any direction visible in Fano hits at least three Fano owners (a nonconstant cut separates at least six variable pairs; each crossing triple accounts for two pairs). Consequently the complete registry has no one-/two-owner visible direction, but the attached four owners are jointly removable for every core word. One may attach arbitrarily many independent modules, including on SAT sign neighbors. This is exact nonlocal normalization, not a rank or basis heuristic. It preserves I21 source semantics, complements DA15's failed affine split and M21's convex/lattice stagnation, and uses no DR15 semantic-equivalence oracle.

Prepend this reduction to the fixed total cooperative portfolio. A polynomial-time exact solver for the strict cores would give a polynomial solver for every input, so the first remaining universal problem may be restricted to these cores. If the resulting portfolio has no polynomial bound, its hard computation must survive this preprocessing; the reduced core encodings have polynomial size and their solve times cannot be uniformly polynomial in their own lengths (else composition would be polynomial). The diagonal lemma applies to the fixed total core solver, not to all imaginable solvers simultaneously. This does not establish that the surviving sequence has a particular hypergraph geometry or label. The NP-completeness result shows that this reduction alone cannot discharge U-PROGRESS; the new precise constructive target is uniform signed-basis compatibility or another paid contracting rule on strict cores. Complete affine closure is still not an oracle, and all failed branches, key accesses and witness lifts remain subject to Theorem~\ref{thm:aggregate}. See Rado (1942), \href{https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/os-13.1.83}{A theorem on independence relations}, and standard deterministic matroid intersection. This is an algebraic extension of the matching-autarky/deficiency idea, not a claim of novelty for the underlying matroid theorem.

The DS15 checker executed binary linear/partition matroid intersection and core discovery, with 91 exhaustive-reference rank-two controls (89 SAT, 2 UNSAT), plus six scaling controls up to 55 coordinates. It verified 3209 lifts and 215391 assertions; 739 matroid calls, 9824 augmentations, 77115 recorded elimination reductions, 194 stagnant singleton scans and 21232 reference words. The one-module Fano pair had minimum visible support three and reduced mass $22$ to $14$; the 12-module pair reduced $110$ to $14$. Scaling decisions enumerate only the seven-variable core, not the large source. The general affine guard absorption, actual strict E4/B4 input adapter, and full portfolio were not executed in this checker. Physical cumulative memory and kernel verification were not measured. U-PROGRESS and unconditional COST remain OPEN.


\section{Quadratic polar elimination and a persistent strict-core obstruction (BA15)}\label{sec:ba15}
To attack the signed-basis gap of Lemma~\ref{lem:ds15-residual}, we allow a global simultaneous algebraic cancellation across \emph{all} owners, rather than just singletons, pairs, or private directions. This is distinct from complete semantic closure and does not invoke it. The result below identifies which linear combinations of the original degree-two violation equations can give unconditional information on the actual guarded chart.

\begin{lemma}[Paid quadratic-polar normalization]\label{lem:ba15-polar}
For an explicit rank-at-most-two affine-packet chart, absorb the actual affine guard by Gaussian parameterization. A packet with two independent equations $a_i x=b_i,\ c_i x=d_i$ has Boolean violation polynomial
\[
 q_i=(1+a_i x+b_i)(1+c_i x+d_i)
\]
in the Boolean ring over $\mathbb F_2$, with $x_j^2=x_j$. Acceptance is exactly $q_i=0$ for every $i$; rank-zero/one packets have the corresponding constant/linear polynomials. There is a uniform polynomial-time exact preprocessing which, after mandatory singleton closure, finds any nontrivial $\mathbb F_2$-linear combination of these polynomials whose degree-two part vanishes. Such a combination is either a necessary affine equation, a contradiction, or an identically zero polynomial certifying that one original equation is redundant. Repeated substitution and deletion terminates with either a decision (if contradicted or all packets removed) or a chart whose retained quadratic parts are independent. It preserves the entire original accepted support through the guard lift.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the unique squarefree degree-at-most-two coefficient vector, obtainable by evaluating at zero, each coordinate vector and each pair of coordinate vectors in the free chart. There are $O(d^2)$ entries for $d$ free variables. Gaussian elimination of the quadratic coordinates, carrying the lower-degree coefficients and optionally the original owner IDs as payload, finds a dependence. If the residual polynomial is the constant one, reject. If it is a nonconstant affine polynomial, impose its zero equation, which lowers the free dimension, and restart singleton closure and elimination. If it is zero, the owner with the new dependent row is a linear combination of earlier owners' equations, so delete only that owner; the others imply its equation. Repeat at stagnation. Same-plane cancellation of different forbidden words is included. Each restart decreases $1+d+m$, where $m$ is the number of live packets; no packet is duplicated and no new nonlinear constraint is emitted. A lift is given by the actual stored affine parameterization, verified against the original chart. The number of coefficient evaluations, Gaussian operations, substitution records, closure restarts and accesses is polynomial in the explicit encoding (and the original $N$ whenever its current chart length is polynomial in $N$); all finite-field operations use polynomial-bit representations. On a serial implementation the same execution has polynomial $T,S$ and $CM\le(T+1)S$. This is a paid unary normalization, not a common global potential for all portfolio rules. It concerns only constant-coefficient linear combinations of the $q_i$, not arbitrary polynomial-calculus multipliers or the complete semantic hull.
\end{proof}
For instance, three distinct rank-two packets with violation polynomials $ab,ac,a(b+c+1)$ have stagnant singleton and same-plane closure yet their sum forces $a=0$. Changing the last constant to zero gives a genuine redundant packet instead. On a separate pair of private bits $d,e$, adding $(1+a)d,(1+a)e,(1+a)(d+e+1)$ forces $a=1$ and produces a contradiction. These are actual necessary deductions, not choices of witnesses.

\begin{lemma}[Linear NAE strict cores defeat root quadratic-polar closure]\label{lem:ba15-core}
For every linear signed 3-uniform NAE registry on distinct triples, the unguarded quadratic parts of the violation polynomials are independent. Moreover, deciding the nonempty strict rank-Hall cores of Lemma~\ref{lem:ds15-residual} is NP-complete even on signed linear 3-uniform NAE registries of maximum variable degree four, with singleton, same-plane, and the entire root quadratic-polar preprocessing stagnant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a triple $u,v,w$, the quadratic part is $x_u x_v+x_u x_w+x_v x_w$, independent of its forbidden signs. Every pair of vertices appears in at most one triple by linearity; thus each polynomial contains its own unique quadratic coordinates. An invertible affine parameterization preserves independence of the quadratic parts because polar bilinear forms transform by invertible congruence. Apply the established NP-completeness of positive linear NAE-3SAT with every variable occurring exactly four times (Darmann, D\"ocker and Dorn, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03395}{arXiv:2412.03395}, also citing the earlier degree-four result). Use the polynomial rank-Hall core extraction of Lemma~\ref{lem:ds15-core} on this source. The retained subset remains linear with maximum degree at most four, and is strictly deficient if nonempty; it can be returned in its original variable names after removing unused variables. It is equisatisfiable by the proved lift. If the extracted core is empty, return the fixed signed Fano YES core of Lemma~\ref{lem:ds15-residual}; the monotone Fano root is a fixed NO core. Both are linear with maximum degree three. No singleton or same-plane deduction occurs at these roots, and the unique-pair argument excludes any nontrivial quadratic-polar combination. The resulting restricted decision problem is in NP: check linearity and degrees, rank-Hall strictness via core recognition, then verify a model. Hence the restriction is NP-complete. This is a reduction to a more precise remaining universal problem, not a proof that another polynomial decision method is impossible.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Low-rank guards restrict global polar cancellations on high-girth cores]\label{lem:ba15-guard}
Let a signed linear NAE hypergraph have maximum variable degree $D$ and Berge girth $g$ (number of hyperedges in the shortest incidence cycle). Let $W$ be any consistent affine guard rowspace of rank $k$. If a nonempty set $J$ of original owners has a sum of violation polynomials that becomes affine on the guard chart, then
\[
 |J|\le 4D^2 k/3.
\]
If additionally $g>4D^2 k/3$, then $|J|\le k$. In particular, for a raw linear NAE source with $g>8D^2/3$, arbitrary one-bit affine branching followed by the singleton and quadratic-polar preprocessing above still has the rank-mass credit failure of Lemma~\ref{lem:da15-one}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $B_J$ be the symmetric zero-diagonal binary polar matrix of $\sum_{i\in J}q_i$. Its off-diagonal entries form the adjacency matrix of the 2-section of the selected triples; linearity makes its number of nonzero entries exactly $6|J|$. Its row weight is at most $\Delta=2D$. If its rank is $\rho$, the union of supports of $\rho$ basis rows covers every nonzero column, so at most $\rho\Delta$ columns are active; symmetry gives the same bound for rows. Consequently $6|J|\le \rho\Delta^2$. If the sum becomes affine under the guard, its polar form restricted to the translation subspace $\ker W$ vanishes. In a basis with that subspace last, the matrix has a zero bottom-right block and only $k$ other rows/columns, so $\rho\le2k$. This proves the first bound without any restriction on guard density or signs.
If $g>4D^2 k/3$, the selected incidence subgraph is a forest. In any component root at a variable node, choose a deepest triple node, and delete it together with its two private leaf variables. The polar matrix block on the two leaves is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$; its only coupling to the other vertices is through their shared parent variable. Its Schur complement contribution is zero in characteristic two. Hence deleting the triple lowers rank by exactly two. Induction yields $\rho=2|J|$ and thus $|J|\le k$.
For the branching corollary, after singleton closure on either one-bit child, Lemma~\ref{lem:da15-one} gives guard rank at most two, surviving rank-two packets and pairwise distinct projected observation planes. A single such packet has nonzero polar form; two such forms can cancel only if their rank-two rowspaces coincide, since the polar matrix of $(a x)(c x)$ is $ac^\top+ca^\top$ and its image is $\langle a,c\rangle$. The preceding bound would require a dependency on at most two owners, which is impossible. Therefore no additional quadratic-polar deduction, deletion or contradiction occurs in either child; their two-child fixed-power rank-mass obstruction is unchanged. This does not constrain higher-degree algebraic inference, nonlinear rewrites, or unrestricted SAT algorithms.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Implication for the actual missing universal step.}
The exact I21 compiler and DS15 preprocessing can be followed by this paid global closure, but the latter cannot universally discharge Obligation~\ref{obl:progress}: an NP-complete signed linear strict-core class survives even at maximum degree four. Assuming a fixed exact core solver lacks a uniform polynomial bound, Lemma~\ref{lem:diagonal} supplies a hard sequence for that solver on this restricted problem if its restriction is itself nonpolynomial; the NP-completeness statement alone neither implies that hypothesis nor assigns any particular geometry or girth to that sequence. A genuinely different normalization, e.g.\ signed-basis search with actual consistency or a higher-degree inference whose aggregate cost is proved, is still required. A rank decrease on a successful preprocessing plan is not a proof of universal coverage. No assertion of relativizing or nonrelativizing separation is made; no unrestricted lower bound follows. U-PROGRESS and unconditional COST remain OPEN.

The BA15 executable finite checker (379 rank-two controls, 123 SAT and 256 UNSAT by reference, plus 14 rank-three single-budget Fano neighbors counted only by reference) checked complete accepted assignment sets before and after closure, including 254 one-bit guarded Fano root charts and 100 seeded affine-packet controls. It recorded 39473 assertions, 747 closure scans, 145 forced rows, 223 deletions, 40368 reference words, 267 nonempty polar subsets and 3204 guarded subset tests, with no discrepancy. Fano root and all 21 sign neighbors have no root deduction; among the 254 guarded Fano root charts, 14 reject by the stronger closure, so the high-girth hypothesis is essential to the branching corollary. The three synthetic controls respectively force one row with four models, delete a redundant packet with five models, and reject. The general degree-four NP-hardness reduction, rank-Hall implementation, high-girth infinite families, strict native compiler, full portfolio and formal kernel verification were not executed here; the general claims above are symbolic proofs with stated premises. Physical cumulative memory was not instrumented.


\section{Joint Fourier--PSD, lattice and rank-Hall stagnation under genuine guards (MS21)}\label{sec:ms21}
We test the actual missing progress in Obligation~\ref{obl:progress} on the rank-two charts of Lemmas~\ref{lem:i21-reverse} and~\ref{lem:ds15-residual}. The usual signed NAE triangle moment identity is $4e_i=1+\sum_{a<b}\eta_{ia}\eta_{ib}s_as_b$, where $s_v=(-1)^{x_v}$ and the $\eta$ are occurrence signs. An exact rational PSD infeasibility certificate for zero expected rejection is sound; for the positive Fano chart, summing the seven triangle equations gives $\mathbf 1^\top G\mathbf 1=7-14=-7$ for any proposed PSD moment matrix $G$ with unit diagonal. This resolves that control, unlike the M21 convex and lattice filters alone. We ask whether adding even the global source-spin triangle SDP to those filters repairs the universal branch-progress gap. The following gives a simultaneous obstruction, including nontrivial parity guards, not merely pins of the global complement symmetry.

\begin{lemma}[An exact positive-definite source-spin moment certificate]\label{lem:ms21-gram}
For any signed NAE registry on a $(3,8)$-biregular incidence graph of girth greater than $50$, there is an explicit rational positive-definite matrix $G$, with diagonal $1$, signed pair moment $-1/3$ on every clause pair, and $|G_{uv}|\le1/3$ on distinct variables, strictly smaller for nonadjacent variables. It has zero entries when primal distance exceeds eight. All triangle inequalities hold. The same conclusions except regularity apply on any subregistry by restricting the matrix.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use primal distance (one step between variables in a common clause), and let $\xi(u,w)$ be the product of occurrence signs along the unique shortest incidence path for distances at most four. Define the integer matrix $Z_{uw}=(-1)^h4^{4-h}\xi(u,w)$ for $h=\operatorname{dist}(u,w)\le4$ and zero otherwise. For overlapping supports of two rows at distance $h\le8$, their union and its connecting paths lie in the radius-$24$ incidence ball at the first variable; girth greater than $50$ makes this ball a tree. In particular all signs in their inner product have the common path sign. The signed-free diagonal squared norm is $4^8[1+16\sum_{t=1}^4 14^{t-1}16^{-t}]=282496$. With $r=-1/4$, the signed-free off-diagonal dot product divided by $4^8$ is the sum of the following two expressions:
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{h}\ \sum_{\substack{t\ge0\\j+t,h-j+t\le4}} a_{jt}r^{h+2t},
\qquad
\sum_{j=1}^{h}\ \sum_{\substack{t\ge0\\j+t,h-j+1+t\le4}}14^t r^{h+1+2t},
\]
where $a_{j0}=1$ and $a_{jt}=2(8-d_j)14^{t-1}$ for $t>0$, with $d_j=1$ at the two endpoints and $2$ otherwise. The first sum counts branches at path variables, the second at path clauses (one extra variable per clause). Multiplying by $4^8$ gives for $h=1,\ldots,8$ respectively $-94920,28432,-7132,1656,-290,52,-7,1$. Therefore
\[
G=(ZZ^\top+2264 I)/284760
\]
is strictly positive definite, has the stated diagonal and signed moments, and satisfies every triangle inequality $1+\epsilon G_{uv}+\delta G_{uw}+\epsilon\delta G_{vw}\ge0$ for $\epsilon,\delta\in\{-1,1\}$ by the absolute bound. All entries have constant-size rational numerators and denominators. Construction by bounded-radius searches, exact arithmetic and matrix products, and verification of all equations and PSD, are polynomial bit work. This is an exact finite-radius specialization of the signed Gaussian-wave approach of Deshpande et al., SODA 2019, \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.05230}{arXiv:1804.05230}, Sec.~5, not an assertion of novelty for the underlying SDP gap.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Simultaneous guard-stable obstruction on strict cores]\label{lem:ms21-guards}
There are arbitrarily large inclusion-minimal UNSAT linear signed NAE registries and same-geometry SAT sign neighbors satisfying Lemma~\ref{lem:ds15-residual}. On each UNSAT registry, one can select $p=\Omega(n_c)$ disjoint relative-parity observations, where $n_c$ is the number of occurring core variables, such that every assignment of any subset of those observations leaves the following specified filters stagnant: singleton/same-plane and two-owner affine-hull closure, rank-Hall peeling, M21 local-option full-support convex/lattice filtering, and the source-spin PSD/triangle relaxation coupled to the same local first and pair moments. The entire registry remains UNSAT. In particular neither child of these genuine one-bit guards is certified by these filters or has reduced rank mass $1+2m$. This is a restricted dispatch obstruction, not a lower bound for arbitrary guard choices or normalization.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every fixed $g>50$ choose a finite $(3,8)$-biregular incidence graph of girth greater than $g$. Existence follows from the random-lift cycle theorem quoted as Theorem~4.3 in Deshpande et al. (the base graph is $K_{8,3}$). With $n$ variables and $8n/3$ clauses, independent uniform occurrence signs give expected number of satisfying words $2^n(3/4)^{8n/3}<1$ because $8\cdot3^8<4^8$. Thus an UNSAT signing exists. Choose an inclusion-minimal UNSAT subregistry, existentially only; there is no claim of a polynomial algorithm to discover it. It is connected, is linear, and has more than $g/2$ clauses: an incidence forest admits a model by assigning outward from a root variable, choosing the two new variables at each clause to satisfy it. A cycle in any subregistry uses at most twice its clause count. Since the original degree is bounded, $n_c$ is unbounded with $g$. Lemma~\ref{lem:ds15-residual} gives the strict Hall condition. Deleting one owner admits a model that violates just that owner; flipping one of its occurrence signs gives a SAT neighbor on the identical geometry, hence with identical rank-Hall conditions. Lemma~\ref{lem:ms21-gram} applies to both signings before restriction. I21 compiles either to strict E4/B4, but this moment certificate is asserted only on the reverse source chart, not on all native edge variables.

Select a set of core variables with pairwise primal distance greater than $24$ measured in the original graph. Greedy radius-$24$ packing yields at least $n_c/B$ variables for an absolute constant $B$ (the primal degree is at most $16$). Pair them arbitrarily, discarding at most one.
