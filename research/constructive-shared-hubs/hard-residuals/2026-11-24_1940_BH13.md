## B. Interval-factor compiler (PROVED; classical matching backend)
If every residual variable occurs in at most two owners, create a multigraph whose vertices are owners with degree intervals [L_i,U_i]. A variable with two occurrences of the same sign is one edge between its owners; selected iff their common literal is true. Opposite signs use a private midpoint with degree exactly one, with one edge to each owner; exactly one of the two literals is true. A one-occurrence variable has an edge to a private dummy of degree interval [0,1]. Fixed pins are already subtracted. Variables absent from live owners may be assigned freely subject to the recorded pins. This is a bijection between actual residual Boolean assignments (on occurring variables) and edge subsets satisfying degree intervals. Parallel graph edges retain identity.

Explicit perfect-matching reduction, avoiding a free interval-factor oracle: for each graph vertex v create U_v slots, L_v mandatory and U_v-L_v optional. Each graph edge e=uv has two distinct incidence vertices e_u,e_v, joined together; each incidence vertex also joins all slots at its own endpoint. Make all optional slots a clique; if sum U_v is odd, add one private dummy to this clique. Mandatory slots have no clique edges. In a perfect matching the two incidence vertices are either matched together (edge absent), or both matched to local slots (edge selected). The latter covers between L_v and U_v slots at each vertex. Conversely, given a valid factor, match selected incidence vertices to distinct local slots, covering all mandatory slots, and match absent edge pairs together. Remaining optional slots, including the dummy when present, number sum U_v - 2|selected edges| + dummy, which is even; pair arbitrarily in the clique. Thus both SAT directions hold, no multiplicity transport asserted. O(N) vertices, O(N^2) edges for explicit occurrence length N after clamping. General deterministic maximum-cardinality matching decides existence of a perfect matching and gives an actual factor; Edmonds 1965, https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1965-045-4. The executed JS checker implements augmenting-path blossom contraction; no randomized algebra or planar premise.

## D. Raw DO01 barrier and bounded branching (PROVED)
For full signed NAE3 with distinct variables occurring at most twice locally, every region is SAT. For any subset J of owners, the 3|J| incidences meet at least 3|J|/2 variables; hence Hall saturates all owners. Match each owner to a pivot variable. Form arcs from each pivot to its two other variables, labeled by the required literal disagreement (XOR 1 XOR signs). Every pivot reaches an unmatched variable: if a nonempty set of pivots reachable from one pivot contains no unmatched vertex, it is closed under these arcs and its |S| owners have all 3|S| incidences among |S| variables of degree <=2, impossible. Choose for each pivot one arc decreasing distance to a free variable. These chosen arcs form a forest directed toward free variables. Assign free variables arbitrarily, propagate required XORs backwards, satisfying each owner. This specializes BH10/BH11's matched-cycle approach without needing even directed circuit detection. Complement symmetry gives both values at any individual hub port. On a raw full NAE forest as in C, every subtree therefore permits both parent values, and the whole source is SAT. Thus every critical DO01 root, including arbitrary high-girth roots, MUST fail at least one raw local-degree or forest test. This does not say it fails after nonlocal elimination, and does not prove a runtime lower bound. Single-budget neighbors need not have both bit values; the general interval messages handle them exactly.

For fixed cap k, after closure at a noneligible residual choose a maximum-occurrence variable and branch on its two bits (exhaustive disjoint). Reclose each child and use C if eligible. SAT from either witness; UNSAT only if both children reject; budget-exhausted leaf is UNKNOWN. At most 2^(k+1)-1 calls. Reserve for a subtree with remaining depth d is 2^(d+1)-1; both child reserves sum to parent minus one. This is polynomial when the cap is fixed or logarithmic, but there is no theorem every input terminates within such a cap. No unrestricted coverage claim. ## E. Single-incidence boundary exchange firewall (PROVED; symbolic)
For an interval-factor region where every private variable occurs at most twice and each distinct Boolean boundary variable occurs exactly once, consider two feasible boundary words a,b and choose actual lifts as edge subsets X,Y from B. In the factor graph, color X minus Y red, Y minus X blue. At every graph vertex pair opposite-colored incident half-edges as far as possible. This decomposes the symmetric difference into alternating trails and circuits; unpaired endpoints at any vertex all have the color of its net degree excess. Starting at the private dummy for any boundary variable on which a,b differ, follow its unpaired half-edge along the resulting alternating trail. Flipping this trail in X preserves degrees at interior visits and moves each endpoint degree one unit toward its degree in Y (if two endpoints coincide, two units, still within the interval). Hence the flipped factor remains feasible. It changes the chosen boundary bit and at most one other boundary bit, and any such second changed bit is one where a,b differed. The boundary dummy cannot be an interior vertex because its degree in the graph is one. Signs merely invert the boundary coordinates. In particular the exact three-port equality relation {000,111} is impossible in this single-incidence interval-factor language: the exchange between 000 and 111 would require another word differing in one or two bits. This refutes a natural fanout shortcut for BW13; repeated external incidences, a shared high-degree hub, general nonlinear owners, and different physical encodings are not excluded. No universal compiler impossibility claim. This is a direct alternating-trail proof of the relevant exchange property, not an appeal to a possibly inapplicable matching-signature theorem.
