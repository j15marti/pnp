## 1. Exact Cartesian fiber factorization (different from DO15 tuple products)
Let vertices V=product_j V_j, with each |V_j|>=2; every R owner is a lift of a factor owner on V_j into each fixed tuple of the other coordinates, and there are no other owners. Normalize duplicates. Each factor has m_j>=1 owners. The only non-R condition is a designated pair inequality or a nonconstant triple D. Let F_j be the factor with demand on projections of D (all identical -> impossible; two distinct -> inequality). Then F is SAT iff at least one F_j SAT. Proof: in a satisfying global word pick a pair of D with different bits. Traverse between them changing one coordinate at a time. At a changed-bit step, restriction to that complete fiber gives a legal factor word separating the pair's projected coordinates. Conversely pull a factor word back by projection; constraints along that coordinate are satisfied and every other coordinate's owner receives a constant legal R word. It satisfies the full exceptional demand. A single oriented pair guard s=0,t=1 follows by complement. This does NOT cover arbitrary further pins, affine guards, or nonconstant-valid relations. Guarded common-mask demand sets with several XORs are not transported by this OR theorem.

Discovery: build primal graph with each owner triple a triangle. For connected primal graph apply the polynomial Cartesian graph prime factorization theorem (Imrich-Peterin, Discrete Math 307:472-483, DOI 10.1016/j.disc.2005.09.038). A triangle varies in only one prime coordinate: along two different directions its endpoints differ in two coordinates and cannot be adjacent. Normalize the ordered minority/ unordered majority tuple in each fiber of each prime direction. Join prime coordinates j,k whenever the complete direction-j owner signature changes when only coordinate k of the fiber is changed. This dependency test compares explicit signatures, including empty ones. Every valid grouping of these coordinates into independent replicated factors must put j,k together; conversely after grouping connected dependency components the signatures are invariant in each external coordinate, hence in all external coordinates by coordinate paths. Verify the full reconstructed owner registry against the input (set equality, real coordinate IDs); product of factor vertex counts is exactly explicit n. Thus find the finest grouping of these coordinates in polynomial time. With supplied channel labels an easier verifier obtains each coordinate by contracting all other channels and verifies full grid and every fiber signature. Code executes this labeled verifier only; no unlabeled prime-factor algorithm was executed. Graph theorem is an imported known theorem, not a formal kernel proof.

Credit mu=1+m for repair states with m distinct R owners; parent m=sum_j m_j product_{k != j}|V_k| >= 2 sum_j m_j for >=2 nontrivial factors. Sum_j(1+m_j)<=2 sum_j m_j<=m=mu(parent)-1. Any directed arc v->v+s e_i, s in {1,3}, avoiding the forbidden vertex (0,0), admits an axis-only return path. Required axis advance D=L-s. A shortest unpinned sequence of steps has floor(D/3) threes then D mod 3 ones. If it hits the forbidden point internally, swap adjacent unequal steps; if adjacent are both 3 replace 3,3 by 1,3,1,1; if both 1 (only possible at the end) replace final 3,1,1 by 1,1,3. Each substitution avoids the unique forbidden point and adds at most two steps. Thus axis-only return length <= floor(D/3)+4. A return involving another axis must advance by a positive multiple of L there, costing >=ceil(L/3) extra steps on top of ceil(D/3) in the first axis. Since L>=16, every shortest return is axis-only, regardless of tie-breaking. The cycle lies in one row/column. Horn closure stays in that line: an orthogonal owner cannot have both majorities in the line; a parallel owner only produces a minority there. But every selected vertex then fails its orthogonal R owner. So EVERY one-shortest-return-cycle Horn closure fails, although source SAT, even under one actual zero pin.
