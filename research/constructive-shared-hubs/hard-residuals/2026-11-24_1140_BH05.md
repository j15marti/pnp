## 2. Exact all-boundary projection (PROVED)
Input is an explicit conjunction of signed NAE clauses, each a finite literal list with optional fixed constants. NAE means at least one 0 and one 1. Collapse duplicates, drop tautologies containing complementary literals, reject normalized clauses of length <=1. Clause-wide complementation preserves NAE. Constants are a protected zero bit and its complement. Fix pins as constants in their guard. Do not eliminate a protected bit or a variable in any outside constraint not represented here. For an arbitrary external affine guard, retain all its support or separately authenticate its projection; no general guard erasure.

Select unprotected variable x. Normalize each of its d active clauses by complementing all literals if needed so its x occurrence is positive. Write C_i=NAE(x,A_i), A_i nonempty. All other clauses are R. Then for EVERY assignment y to the remaining variables:
    exists x [R AND AND_i NAE(x,A_i)]
    iff R AND AND_{i<j} NAE(A_i, complement(A_j)).
Proof: each A_i that is mixed imposes no requirement on x. Each monochromatic A_i of value b requires x=1-b. A conflicting pair exists iff A_i is all b and A_j all 1-b, which is precisely failure of NAE(A_i,complement A_j). If all pair constraints pass, the forced values agree or no value is forced; choose accordingly. This is exact joint support, not affine hull, not marginal support and not model count preservation. d=0,1 impose no residual constraint. Use actual signed literals and constants.

A two-clause complementary sibling identity is also pointwise:
    NAE(A,x) AND NAE(A,not x) iff NAE(A).
This holds without requiring x private and may replace both even if x appears elsewhere. Binary NAE(l1,l2) gives a parity equation, with exact substitution and archived lift for an unprotected endpoint. A binary constraint between protected endpoints remains in the projected system. All three deductions preserve all other constraints. Canonical complement-orbit key uses full sorted signed literal IDs, not ranks or statistics.

Interpretation: each incident clause contributes a two-valued conditional repair request to a shared Boolean hub; pairwise disagreement is exactly a new nonaffine rejected fiber. Pair constraints can be passed to multisign DAG/affine-rejection modules without pretending their joint support is affine. A two-bit tetrahedral hub can be projected by two successive Boolean eliminations only if the actual Boolean source and all its coupling constraints are authenticated and included; guard pins or outside support remain protected. For DO01 paired unit owner bounds 1<=sum literal values<=2, use NAE; a single upper or lower relaxation is exactly NAE with the corresponding fixed constant (0 permits all ones; 1 permits all zeros). Owner identities and original clause mapping retained. No transfer to unrepresented integer owners or a signed-counting backend.

## 3. Paid capped normalization and unary potential (PROVED)
For d incidences, at most d(d-1)/2 candidate pair clauses replace d old clauses. Thus d<=3 gives no clause growth before simplification, d=3 can produce exactly three. For arbitrary d, generate actual canonical resolvents, allow cancellation/duplicate merging, and check fixed cap B>=initial source clause count. Reject a trial if distinct nontrivial residual clauses exceed B; it is permissible to decline even if further simplification could recover the cap. Use fixed deterministic variable ordering (minimum occurrence then ID), try all unprotected active candidates until a feasible one is found. Apply canonicalization, parity substitution and sibling closure at entry, after each strict deduction and at stagnation. No complete semantic closure asserted or charged as free. If no candidate fits, return UNKNOWN with exact residual and archive, not UNSAT. If no residual constraints remain, reconstruct and verify. If only protected variables remain, return their exact residual, not universal compatibility.

Let v count uneliminated variables and m current active canonical constraints. Potential mu=(B+1)v+m. Any committed existential or binary elimination lowers v by at least 1 while m increases by at most B, so mu decreases >=1. A sibling deduction decreases m. No exponential branch trees hidden: a unary path only. The initial potential is polynomial for B=N^C fixed. This is a charged sufficient progress lemma, not guaranteed universal progress. No branch aggregate theorem follows when stagnation forces splitting.

Resource proof: all canonical clauses have at most n+1 distinct literal coordinates and polynomial-size identifiers. At most n committed eliminations (binary plus existential), and at most O((n+1)B) sibling reductions because each lowers m by 1 and at most n eliminations replenish at most B. Each closure scan computes at most O(B n) deletion keys of length O(n log N), using exact comparisons (a deterministic balanced tree/sorted array suffices; JS Map is only the test backend). A candidate has at most B^2 pairs, each literal normalization polynomial, early stop allowed. At most n candidates per variable-elimination round. Charge all failed trials, source authentication and compaction, pair and closure key work, dedup, full key comparisons, literal visits, archive and final verification. With n<=N and B polynomial, total work and peak space polynomial in original N. For example coarse bit bounds O((n+1)^4 (B+1)^2 polylog(N+B)) time with elementary sorting/comparison and O((n+1)^2 (B+1) polylog(N+B)) space suffice; alternatively use the explicitly described product of operation counts and per-operation polynomial cost. An archive of at most n eliminated hubs stores at most B parent groups of O(n) literals each; closure sibling steps need no witness choices. A replay proof can store each accepted pair-generation/closure transcript with polynomial extra space; not executed as an independent proof checker. Reconstruct in reverse order from actual exterior assignment, recover each forced hub or arbitrary 0, and verify every original signed clause and pins. Deterministic serialized run has CM <= (T+1)S, including the archive. Do not claim measured peak bytes or cumulative memory from operation counters. Cache/duplicate merging within one source/guard is exact signed literal equality. Cross-guard sharing not implemented; retain full protected guard and source identity. No arbitrary signed count aggregation, model multiplicity or physical replay claim.

## 4. Optimal local projection barrier (PROVED)
Take d independent private two-bit boundary groups A_i={a_i,b_i}, all protected, each with one positive NAE(x,a_i,b_i). The projection excludes exactly those exterior assignments having at least one group 00 and one group 11. Every pair clause NAE(a_i,b_i,not a_j,not b_j) is necessary within the pair list. Stronger: ANY conjunction of signed NAE clauses over just the 2d boundary bits, without auxiliary variables (fixed constants allowed), needs at least binom(d,2) nontrivial clauses. For each unordered pair choose a witness assignment with exactly those two groups monochromatic and opposite; all other groups mixed. Each nonempty rejection subcube of an entailed NAE clause must force one full opposite monochromatic pair. Otherwise extend its partial assignment by making every incompletely specified group mixed and observing that already fully specified monochromatic groups do not conflict. Such an extension would satisfy the projected relation, contradiction. Without constants the two rejecting subcubes are complements, hence force the same unordered pair; with constants at most one is nonempty. Consequently a single clause can reject at most one of the chosen pair witnesses. Lower bound follows and exact pair projection attains it. This is a tight direct-representation count, not a runtime lower bound; retaining the original shared hub is a compact existential representation. For d=4, cap 4 fails and cap 6 succeeds; all 256 boundary assignments executed.

## 5. High-girth strict-cap barrier (PROVED)
For any linear signed 3-uniform hypergraph with every vertex degree >=4 and distinct edges, initial closure above makes no deductions (no binary clauses, no equal supports for sibling identity). At any candidate x the incident other pairs A_i are pairwise variable-disjoint. All binom(d,2) pair resolvents have distinct four-variable supports and are nontautological. No retained triple can be a subset of any of these supports: it would share at least two variables with one original incident triple, violating linearity. No pair resolver subsumes another with different support or any retained triple, and no sibling pair shares support. Thus with cap B=m no first elimination fits; exact post-generation duplicate/subsumption/sibling simplification does not fix this first step. This proof works for ANY signs. It does not rule out increasing the polynomial cap, different representations, derived nonlocal deductions or the full portfolio.

For arbitrary fixed Berge girth g>=2, existence of monotone UNSAT high-girth min-degree-four inputs follows by a robust random alteration (symbolic, not executed). On n vertices sample 100n independent uniform distinct-vertex triples. Duplicates occur with probability O(1/n). Every Boolean coloring has monochromatic probability >=1/5 for large n (minimum near equal split); Chernoff bounds and union bound over 2^n colorings show with probability tending to one that all colorings have >=5n violated sampled triples. For each fixed j<=g, expected number of Berge j-cycles is O_g(1): choose j connector vertices, at most n choices of the third vertex per edge, and j distinct sample indices, each prescribed triple probability 1/binom(n,3). Hence w.h.p. at most n short cycles. Delete one edge per such cycle, then repeatedly remove vertices of degree <=3 and their incident edges (at most 3n edge deletions). Every coloring of the surviving graph extends to an original coloring, and fewer than 5n deleted edges cannot remove all violations. A remaining UNSAT component has min degree >=4 and girth >g. On this SAME structure choose one negated literal per clause to plant all-zero SAT, and change signs one literal at a time from monotone UNSAT to planted SAT; some consecutive pair has opposite labels, both still strict-cap stagnant. This is a single-sign neighbor, not necessarily DO01's single-budget critical neighbor. Connected criticality after extraction not guaranteed: removing edges to obtain a critical subformula can lower degrees. The theorem excludes a restricted strict cap, not all polynomial caps or all algorithms.

Independent known scope: Darmann, Döcker and Dorn, arXiv:2412.03395 (2024-12-04), Theorem 3 / abstract: linear positive 3-uniform k-regular NAE remains NP-complete for fixed k>=4 even with a partition decomposition; their nine-variable four-partition example is a no-instance. This corroborates the degree-four barrier but does not prove our high-girth critical claim. The actual AG(2,3) nine-vertex control is UNSAT even after EACH single-owner relaxation (brute force), so it is explicitly noncritical. Do not call those neighbors SAT.
