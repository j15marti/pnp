## Theorem 1 (PROVED restricted): signed pair-energy representation
Each explicitly given factor has at most three Boolean ports, finite nonnegative integer costs, and f(x)=f(1-x) (all bits complemented). A hard relation is represented by cost 0 on allowed rows and 1 on forbidden rows. This includes signed NAE and arbitrary complement-invariant binary or ternary relations, not a general signed OR. For a triple ordered x0,x1,x2, write y=x0 XOR x1, z=x0 XOR x2, a=f(0,0), b=f(1,0), d=f(0,1), e=f(1,1) in relative coordinates. Then pointwise
  2 f = 2a+(b+e-a-d)y+(d+e-a-b)z+(b+d-a-e)(y XOR z).
Verify on the four relative rows. For a binary factor 2f=2a+2(b-a)y; unary and empty factors are constant. Thus signed coefficients on a common edge sum exactly. Replace coefficient D_e on parity x_u XOR x_v by min(0,D_e)+|D_e| times disagreement with preference p_e=[D_e<0]. Keep constant offset and all original port identities. No division/rounding errors, no lost guard correlations, and no inference from individual affine hulls. Paired opposite BW01 clause wheels are NAE; their combined forbidden-row cost is complement-invariant. A lone OR is not. Four-port even parity violation has a nonzero degree-four Walsh coefficient and cannot be exactly expressed on the same ports by pairwise terms alone. This is a scoped expressiveness limit, not an impossibility with auxiliaries.

## Theorem 2 (PROVED restricted): exact nonlocal planar signed compatibility
Let the aggregate simple pair graph (including zero-weight fill edges) be planar, with a paid embedding. Minimize signed disagreement. For each connected plane component, let T contain dual faces with odd XOR of preferred parities on their boundary walks, counting multiplicity. An edge-disagreement set J corresponds to a primal assignment iff p XOR 1_J sums to zero around every face. Face boundaries generate the GF2 cycle space; integrate along a spanning tree, checking all edges, to obtain actual bits. In the planar dual this is exactly the T-join condition; bridge edges give dual loops, which can be omitted with nonnegative weights. The minimum nonnegative T-join equals minimum perfect matching on the metric closure of T. Proof: decompose any T-join into terminal-pair paths plus cycles, giving a matching of no greater length; conversely XOR shortest paths for any matching gives a T-join of no greater weight. Thus deterministic polynomial shortest paths and general minimum-weight perfect matching give optimum and witness (known polynomial matching algorithm). Recover all original costs by signed offset and divide by 2. Sum across components. All cycle constraints, not merely local face consistency as a relaxation, are enforced.
For a boundary B of <=3 vertices whose clique is in the planar graph, compute the optimum conditional on every relative boundary pattern. Add a penalty M=1+sum_e |D_e| in the scaled energy for each violated anchor-to-other boundary parity. Every pattern is consistent on the boundary, and all original costs are finite; a satisfying assignment to the pattern exists before optimization. M dominates the range of the remaining energy, so a minimum has the requested pattern. These edges already exist, hence preserve planarity. At most four T-join calls per patch, independent of interior rank, treewidth, number of local assignments, and wheel duplication. Absolute orientation is recovered by global complement within the connected message subtree, not dropped when matching parent bits.
