## Common syndrome coordinate theorem
For remaining U_i of dimension <=2, deduplicate nonzero spans. D contains every generator of a one-dimensional span and every nonzero vector occurring in two distinct two-dimensional spans. A simultaneous independent coordinate basis for all U_i exists iff each two-plane contains at most two D vectors and
|D| + sum_{distinct planes P}(2-|D intersect P|) = rank(sum_i U_i).
Necessity: coordinate intersections are coordinate subspaces, so all D vectors are mandatory. Sufficiency: complete the mandatory vectors inside each plane to a basis with private vectors. No private vector belongs to another plane or prescribed line, the resulting union spans, and equality of its cardinality and rank makes it independent. Enumerate just three nonzero vectors per plane, Gaussian rank and deterministic completion. Test target membership in the total span and express each translated row and target in that basis. Each remaining table touches at most two independent syndrome checks even when its original coordinate footprint is large. I28 matching gadget now applies with exact representative lift. No exhaustive search of bases, no matching of marginal syndrome bits independently. The code implements the parity-pad, component clique (one private vertex if target parity odd), direct wire and parity-neutral reservoir, then an augmenting-path Edmonds blossom implementation. Source replay and row verification separately charged. The proof may rely on the established polynomial general matching theorem, not finite implementation evidence.

## Barrier to universal applicability
Arbitrary signed NAE-3-SAT compiles to the syndrome problem above with three allowed difference words (x_u+x_v,x_u+x_w) per constraint, a rank-at-most-two local affine hull. If desired duplicate each observation owner before quotienting by the stacked map's image. Every nonzero vector confined to a single duplicated owner's two positions lies outside the image, so each local list remains exactly three distinct words of rank two and is nonaffine. Hull closure stagnates: for any chosen local word there exists an original variable assignment realizing it, and all other *affine hulls* allow all four local words; projecting gives the required extension in hulls. This establishes NP-completeness of the general stagnant rank-two syndrome family (membership in NP immediate), not hardness of every instance nor a universal solver lower bound. Duplicate source owners may be removed by other rules. Uniform availability of a common syndrome basis after this pipeline is not proved, and actual opposite-label S27 cases decline. This does not establish P!=NP.
