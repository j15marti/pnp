\section{What this formulation establishes}

The strongest defensible formulation is an exact constructive framework with a specific unproved universal acquisition and work bound. Its logical order is
\[
\text{explicit source}
\longrightarrow \text{exact guarded representation}
\longrightarrow \text{paid composition or reduction}
\longrightarrow \text{verified decision and witness}.
\]
Every arrow needs both a semantic proof and a resource bound. A small formula, graph, signed circuit, or DAG does not by itself establish that its accepting support can be found in polynomial time. Conversely, an exponential upper bound for one representation is not an unconditional lower bound for its source problem.

This report follows the user-directed constructive route. It incorporates the recovered CC02--CC08 artifacts, the published CC01 result, the original DO01/DP01/S06 dependencies, and recent M07, MS08, BD13, DY13, DP13, and MS19 reports. It does not claim to have audited every earlier chat or every branch of the repository. In particular, recovered historical results are distinguished from tests executed for CC09.

\section{Source domain, encoding, and proof obligations}

\subsection{A source class sufficient for the constructive objective}

Let $V=\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$, $n=3m$. The input supplies four partitions $P_1,P_2,P_3,P_4$ of $V$ into triples. The whole triple family is linear: distinct triples meet in at most one variable. A word $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ satisfies a triple $\{u,v,w\}$ when
\[
1\le x_u+x_v+x_w\le2.
\]
The resulting monotone NAE problem with four supplied partitions is NP-complete by Darmann, D\"ocker, and Dorn, Theorem 3.6, arXiv:2412.03395v3, revised 11 November 2026 \cite{ddd}. The supplied partitions are part of the input, so their discovery is not being hidden. A uniform polynomial-time decider for this whole class would therefore imply $P=NP$.

The imported theorem is only the source completeness result. The circulation, full-hull, exchange, and accounting proofs below are given explicitly. The ordinary NP-completeness reduction need not preserve solution counts, so no consequence about counting is inferred from decision completeness.

Let $N$ be the total encoded input length. It includes variable and clause IDs, partitions, signs if an extended signed domain is used, all extra guards, and the data describing a claimed source adapter. A residual can cease to have four complete partitions after conditioning or deleting clauses. The scope of each residual terminal is checked anew.

\subsection{Ports and actual local relations}

A tetrahedral or multisign hub with one, two, three, or four available ports is described by its actual relation on those ordered ports, together with any private variables and a reconstruction map. Its type is more than its degree. Projecting private variables gives an existential boundary relation; counting requires multiplicities as well. A pairwise projection can be full while the joint relation remains restrictive. The six-word NAE relation is a basic example.

Distributed wheels, duplicate hubs, and parallel tracks can provide exact adapters. They must preserve shared source identities and any high-channel guards that survive projection. A theorem proved for ordinary cubic coloring or the monotone NAE class does not automatically become a theorem for every signed tetravalent physical network. That compiler is a separate obligation wherever it is invoked.

\subsection{Closure and correctness conventions}

Before using a terminal, after each strict deduction, and at stagnation before logical branching, apply the available certified unconditional rules to a fixed point. In this audit the executable source prepass is CNF unit closure with restart after each new pin. NAE triples compile to the two CNF clauses forbidding $000$ and $111$. The circulation routine additionally acquires exact affine necessities of its own feasible set.

A statement proved under a pin or a parity equation remains conditional on that guard. Dense affine equations are recorded rather than silently reinterpreted as original coordinate bounds. Complete semantic closure is not assumed to be free. The root UNSAT example in Section~\ref{sec:counter} has no unit clauses; its absence of a unit deduction is a property of this rule set, not evidence that every sound inference procedure must stall.

An exact terminal may return SAT with a source-verifiable witness, UNSAT with the justification of its exact decision procedure, or UNKNOWN because its certified scope or budget fails. UNKNOWN is never treated as UNSAT.

\section{Exact two-partition circulations}

\subsection{Compilation and both directions}

For $P=P_1$ and $Q=P_2$, make one left vertex for each triple of $P$ and one right vertex for each triple of $Q$. Variable $e$ becomes a distinct edge from its left triple to its right triple. This is a cubic bipartite multigraph; the linear source assumption makes the relevant pairs simple, but the following compilation also permits indexed parallel edges.

Introduce one hub $h$ and directed arcs
\[
h\longrightarrow L_i\quad[1,2],\qquad
L(e)\longrightarrow R(e)\quad[0,1],\qquad
R_j\longrightarrow h\quad[1,2].
\]
Every interval has width one. For a coordinate pin $x_e=b$, replace its variable interval by $[b,b]$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact circulation compiler]\label{thm:flow}
The integral feasible circulations in this network are in bijection with the Boolean words satisfying both partitions and the specified coordinate pins. The compiler, feasibility test, inverse map, and source verification are deterministic polynomial-time procedures.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
From a source word, put flow $x_e$ on each variable arc. At each clause vertex, its selected degree is either one or two, so put this degree on its hub arc. Conservation holds at every clause vertex. Total left degree equals total right degree because both sum the same variable bits; conservation also holds at $h$.

Conversely, an integral flow on a variable arc is a bit. Conservation says that the sum of its incident bits is the adjacent hub-arc value, which is one or two. Thus every triple is NAE-satisfied. Pins are enforced by fixed bounds. Each hub value is uniquely determined by its variable bits, proving bijectivity.

For feasibility, subtract lower bounds, compute the resulting vertex imbalances, attach a supersource and supersink, and run an integral augmenting-path maximum-flow algorithm. Full saturation is equivalent to a feasible circulation. Capacities and all graph sizes have polynomial encoding length. The inverse simply reads the original variable arcs and checks the original clauses.
\end{proof}

Applying this construction to $(P_1,P_2)$ and $(P_3,P_4)$ yields two exact feasible sets $X,Y\subseteq\F^n$. The original question is exactly
\[
X\cap Y\ne\varnothing.
\]
Each side can be solved with arbitrary original-coordinate pins in polynomial time. This does not prove that their intersection can be solved in polynomial time.

\subsection{Every unpinned pair has full affine hull}

\begin{theorem}[Full-hull theorem]\label{thm:fullhull}
For every pair of partitions of $n$ variables into triples, let $X$ be the set of words NAE-satisfying the two partitions, with no external pins or guards. Then
\[
\aff_{\F}(X)=\F^n.
\]
Two disjoint perfect matchings give a polynomial-size constructive certificate of this statement.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The incidence graph is cubic and bipartite. Hall's condition holds: for a set $S$ on one side, its $3|S|$ incident edges enter at most three edges per neighbor, so $|\Gamma(S)|\ge|S|$. Find a perfect matching $A$. Its complement is a union of even cycles, including length-two cycles if there are parallel edges. Alternating along each cycle gives a second perfect matching $B$ disjoint from $A$.

Every perfect matching is in $X$, since each clause has selected degree one. Fix a source edge $e$. If $e\notin A$, take $M=A$; otherwise take $M=B$. Thus $e\notin M$. Both $M$ and $M\cup\{e\}$ are in $X$: adding $e$ changes its two endpoint selected degrees from one to two and changes no other degree. Their difference over $\F$ is the unit vector $\mathbf e_e$.

The direction space of an affine hull contains every difference of two members. It therefore contains all $n$ unit vectors and equals $\F^n$. The matchings and the $n$ one-edge extensions can be constructed and verified in polynomial time.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unconditional affine synchronization is vacuous]
For every input in the four-partition source class,
\[
\aff(X)=\aff(Y)=\F^n.
\]
Consequently, a test using only these two affine hulls cannot distinguish SAT from UNSAT on this class. Choosing a different pairing of the four partitions does not change this conclusion.
\end{corollary}

This is a statement about the information retained by the proposed summary. It is not a lower bound on computation. After pins or nonaffine reductions, a half-system hull can become smaller and useful; the next theorem acquires it exactly.

\section{Residual exchanges and exact affine acquisition}

\subsection{Width-one difference decomposition}

Consider an explicitly encoded directed network with integral bounds $\ell_a\le f_a\le u_a$ and $u_a-\ell_a\le1$. Assume source observations are designated Boolean arcs. Fix one feasible integral circulation $f$. Every nonfixed arc is at one endpoint of its interval, so it has exactly one residual direction, of capacity one. Original arc IDs remain distinct.

\begin{lemma}[Feasible differences are disjoint cycle collections]\label{lem:cycles}
For every other feasible circulation $g$, the nonzero signed differences $g-f$ form a directed Eulerian subgraph of the residual graph of $f$, using each residual arc at most once. They decompose into pairwise arc-disjoint directed simple cycles. Conversely, adding any pairwise arc-disjoint collection of residual directed cycles to $f$ gives another feasible integral circulation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each nonzero difference has magnitude one and uses the available residual direction. Conservation for $g$ and $f$ implies zero net imbalance in their difference. A nonempty finite Eulerian directed subgraph contains a directed cycle; remove a simple one and repeat. This partitions its arcs into cycles. Conversely each cycle preserves conservation, and arc-disjointness ensures that no unit residual capacity is exceeded. Fixed arcs never appear.
\end{proof}

The cycles may share vertices, including the hub. Vertex-disjointness is unnecessary. Sharing a residual \emph{arc} is the forbidden operation in this representation.

\subsection{One parity preference can be decided constructively}

Given $w\in\F^n$, label a residual arc by $w_e$ if it comes from source variable $e$, and by zero otherwise. Orientation does not affect a modulo-two label. If the baseline source word $a$ already has the requested parity, it is a witness.

\begin{lemma}[Odd-cycle oracle]\label{lem:odd}
A feasible circulation with source parity different from $w\cdot a$ exists if and only if the residual graph contains a directed simple cycle of odd total label. Such a cycle, or its absence, can be determined in polynomial time.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If a desired flow exists, decompose its difference from $f$ using Lemma~\ref{lem:cycles}. The XOR of the cycle labels is one, so some cycle is odd. Conversely augmenting one unit along an odd residual simple cycle is feasible and changes the source parity.

Replace every residual vertex $v$ by $(v,0),(v,1)$, and every arc $u\to v$ of label $b$ by $(u,p)\to(v,p+b)$ for both $p$. For each $v$, test reachability from $(v,0)$ to $(v,1)$. A successful path projects to an odd directed closed walk. Decompose that walk into directed simple cycles; at least one has odd label. If no such reachability exists, no odd directed cycle exists. Breadth-first searches and walk decomposition are polynomial and produce an actual exchange.
\end{proof}

This is the one-bit mechanism already identified in M07 \cite{m07}. It applies here directly to original variable arcs, with no hidden category-selection or physical-source adapter.

\subsection{At most n queries for the whole affine hull}

\begin{theorem}[Witnessed affine-hull acquisition]\label{thm:hull}
For a feasible pinned width-one circulation system with $n$ Boolean source observations, its exact affine hull can be acquired using at most $n$ calls to the odd-cycle oracle, plus polynomial linear algebra and witness reconstruction. Empty support is detected by ordinary flow feasibility.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $a$ be a feasible source anchor. Maintain two spaces: $D$, spanned by differences of actual feasible witnesses from $a$, and $I$, spanned by certified parity identities on all feasible differences. Initially both can be zero. Always $D\subseteq I^\perp$.

If $\dim D+\dim I<n$, choose $w\in D^\perp\setminus I$ by Gaussian elimination. Ask whether a feasible source word $x$ has $w\cdot(x+a)=1$. A successful odd-cycle query gives an actual witness whose difference is outside $D$, increasing $\dim D$. A failed query proves the new identity $w\cdot(x+a)=0$ for all feasible $x$, increasing $\dim I$. Prior identities remain valid, so the invariant is preserved.

Each query increases $\dim D+\dim I$ by one. At equality $n$, the annihilator relation is tight. The full feasible difference span lies between $D$ and $I^\perp$, which are then equal. Thus the exact affine hull is $a+D$, its identities are $I$, and its stored generators have actual feasible witnesses.
\end{proof}

For a network with $v$ vertices and $a$ arcs, a simple implementation spends $O(v(v+a))$ graph operations per parity query. The source compiler has $v,a=O(n)$. Gaussian elimination, word labels, bounds, identifiers, and counters use polynomially many bits. A conservative polynomial bit bound, rather than a sharp exponent, suffices here. The same serial implementation stores polynomially many graph records, parity rows, and witnessed cycles. Thus $T,S=\poly(N)$ and $\CM\le(T+1)S$.

The theorem obtains all \emph{linear} consequences of this exact domain. It does not acquire its nonlinear support relation. In particular, it does not supply a polynomial oracle for a flow conditioned on an arbitrary system of simultaneous parity equations. Appending such equations changes the domain and requires an exact, separately paid backend.

\section{An explicit failure of affine closure}\label{sec:counter}

Use nine variables, with four partitions given by the lines of the affine plane of order three:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
P_1&012&345&678\\
P_2&036&147&258\\
P_3&048&156&237\\
P_4&057&138&246
\end{array}
\]
Here $012$ abbreviates the triple $\{0,1,2\}$, not a numerical value. This is a linear four-partition source.

\begin{proposition}[The source is UNSAT]
Every two-coloring of these nine points has a monochromatic line.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose a set $A$ of five points contains no line. Every pair of points of $A$ has a unique third point on its line, and that third point lies outside $A$. There are $\binom52=10$ such pairs. Fix a point $p$ outside $A$. The lines through $p$ partition the other eight points into four disjoint pairs, so at most $\lfloor5/2\rfloor=2$ pairs of $A$ have third point $p$. There are only four points outside $A$, allowing at most eight pairs, a contradiction. Thus every five-point set contains a line. One color class in a two-coloring of nine points has at least five points.
\end{proof}

Each half $(P_1,P_2)$ and $(P_3,P_4)$ nevertheless has 102 models, as verified by complete enumeration of its 512 words. Both affine hulls are $\F^9$, both by Theorem~\ref{thm:fullhull} and by the independent acquired bases. Every projection of either half onto two coordinates is full, also checked exhaustively. The two supports are disjoint. Their full affine descriptions therefore agree while their exact joint decision is negative.

The counterexample does not imply that the instance is hard: the five-point argument is a short refutation. Its role is to falsify the claim that separately complete affine information suffices for arbitrary gluing.

\subsection{Adversarial neighboring controls}

Deleting any one of the twelve NAE constraints leaves an UNSAT formula in the finite audit. Flipping any one of its 36 literal occurrences also leaves an UNSAT formula. These unsuccessful attempts are recorded rather than presented as SAT neighbors.

Deleting any two NAE constraints gives a SAT formula: the checker finds 12 models for the twelve pairs in a common partition and two models for the other 54 pairs. More sharply, first remove the NAE constraint $012$, producing an UNSAT residual with 22 CNF clauses. Delete one of those 22 CNF clauses. Every resulting formula is SAT: four have six models and eighteen have one model. Each is a genuine one-CNF-clause neighbor of the same UNSAT residual. All of these formulas have empty root unit closure.

These are residual CNF controls, not complete four-partition inputs after deletion. Every reported SAT witness is checked directly against its original retained clauses. The uniqueness in eighteen neighbors is not used to infer that their forced bits can be discovered for free.

\section{The one-third phase representation and its quantifiers}

\begin{theorem}[One-third affine chart through a supplied model]\label{thm:chart}
For two linear partitions of $n=3m$ variables into triples and any supplied satisfying word $x$, a deterministic polynomial-time procedure constructs an affine chart of dimension $m=n/3$ that contains $x$ and satisfies both partitions identically.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose one bichromatic pair from each triple under $x$. The pairs chosen from a single partition form a matching. Their union has degree at most two; every cycle alternates between the two matchings and has even length. Linearity ensures that the $2m$ selected edges are distinct.

If a cycle contains the selected pair $uv$ of a triple $\{u,v,w\}$, the third vertex $w$ has degree at most one, because that partition used $u,v$ rather than $w$. It lies outside the cycle. Exactly one of $uw,vw$ is bichromatic under $x$. Replace $uv$ by that pair. Removing $uv$ opens its cycle into a path, and joining to $w$ attaches it to a different path or isolated vertex. No cycle is created, the degree bound remains two, and the number of cycles drops. Repeat until a forest remains.

The forest has $n$ vertices and $2m$ edges, hence $n-2m=m$ components. Impose $z_u+z_v=1$ on every selected edge. The word $x$ satisfies these equations. One bit per forest component determines all its values, so the affine solution space has dimension $m$. Every original triple contains its selected unequal pair and is NAE-satisfied throughout this chart. Cycle discovery, replacement, component labeling, and the inverse phase map are polynomial operations.
\end{proof}

The quantifiers are
\[
\forall x\in X\quad \exists\text{ a certified dimension-}n/3\text{ chart through }x.
\]
They are not the assertion that one chart contains all models, that a polynomial list covers all models, or that a chart can be selected while preserving an unknown completion in $Y$. The two-partition flow solver can supply some $x\in X$ in polynomial time, but a chart through that $x$ may omit all of $X\cap Y$ even if the latter is nonempty.

\subsection{A counting obstruction to listing all such charts}

Take the explicit twelve-variable four-partition SAT instance in the executable record. Its variables form three groups of four, and each clause contains one variable from each group. Choose one group freely and set the other two groups to opposite constants. There are six such choices of free group and orientation, each giving 16 models. The six mixed constant group patterns are each counted twice, while all other models in these families are counted once. This exhibits 90 distinct models; exhaustive enumeration confirms that the whole instance has exactly 90.

For $q$ independent copies, there are $90^q$ models and $n=12q$ variables. An affine chart of dimension at most $n/3=4q$ contains at most $16^q$ words. Therefore any explicit family of such charts covering every model needs at least
\[
K_q\ge(90/16)^q.
\]
This is an exponential lower bound on that specific all-model listing. The source family itself factorizes into independent constant-size components and is easy to decide. The bound does not prohibit a compact shared representation, a decision-only witness search, or a polynomial solver with another invariant.

Similarly, a decrease from $n$ bits to $n/3$ per chart does not alone imply polynomial time. If $b(n)$ charts must be explored at each level, the recursion has aggregate multiplier
\[
\prod_{j<\log_3 n} b(n/3^j).
\]
Constant $b$ is compatible with a polynomial bound. Polynomial $b(n)$ at every level can instead give $\exp(\Theta((\log n)^2))$. A proof must bound the aggregate, not only the phase dimension of one child.

\section{Exact guarded cycle composition}

\subsection{The correct multisign representation}

Fix a feasible baseline $f$ and an explicit catalogue $\mathcal C$ of residual simple cycles. Introduce a switch $z_C\in\{0,1\}$ for each cycle. If $C,D$ share any residual arc, impose
\[
\neg z_C\lor\neg z_D.
\]
This includes shared hub arcs, not only variable arcs. Under these guards every chosen cycle collection is arc-disjoint, and its source word is exactly
\[
x_e=f_e+\sum_{C\ni e}z_C\pmod2.
\]
The formula is affine, but its allowed switch domain retains the capacity guards. Omitting those guards admits invalid flows.

For two catalogues with source maps $x=a+Uz$ and $x=b+Vt$, synchronization is
\[
Uz+Vt=a+b,
\]
conjoined with both sets of arc-conflict clauses. These constraints are exact relative to the acquired catalogue coverage. If each catalogue contains every residual simple cycle, Lemma~\ref{lem:cycles} proves coverage of every feasible source word. Smaller catalogues need a separate completeness theorem for the decision target.

A chart obtained from one arc-disjoint decomposition needs no internal conflict guards: every subset of its cycles is feasible. Two such supplied affine charts can be synchronized by Gaussian elimination, with source reconstruction. Gaussian failure proves those charts have no common word; it does not reject other charts of the source.

Different cycle decompositions may represent the same flow. This does not affect existential decision or a verified witness, but a sum of switch assignments need not equal the number of source models. Counting requires disjointness or an explicit multiplicity correction.

\subsection{There can be exponentially many indispensable explicit cycles}

\begin{proposition}[Catalogue-size obstruction]\label{prop:catalogue}
For arbitrarily large cubic bipartite two-partition systems, a complete fixed-baseline catalogue of residual simple cycles must contain exponentially many members, even though ordinary source feasibility is polynomial.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $k\ge3$, use left and right vertices $L_i,R_i$, with edges $L_iR_i$, $L_iR_{i+1}$, and $L_iR_{i+2}$ modulo $k$. The source has $n=3k$ variables. Take the matching $L_iR_i$ as baseline. Contract its reverse residual matching arcs; nonmatching residual moves give directed steps $i\to i+1$ and $i\to i+2$.

Every increasing path from $0$ to $k-1$ with steps one or two, followed by $k-1\to0$, gives a distinct residual simple alternating cycle. The number of such paths is the Fibonacci number $F_k$, so it is $\Omega(\varphi^k)$, where $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$.

The target obtained by flipping just that cycle has exactly that simple cycle as its nonzero residual difference support; its hub flows do not change. A disjoint-cycle decomposition of that support can only be the entire cycle. Therefore a catalogue capable of representing every target by disjoint residual cycles from this fixed baseline must contain each of these $F_k$ cycles. The graph has size linear in $k$.
\end{proof}

The bound concerns explicit simple-cycle listing. The graph itself is a compact implicit description, and Theorem~\ref{thm:hull} does not enumerate its cycles. Symbolic cancellation or another evaluator might avoid the listing. Such an evaluator still needs an exact coverage and work proof.

\subsection{Binary exclusions plus affine equations are not automatically easy}

\begin{proposition}[Generic guarded-affine feasibility is NP-complete]\label{prop:guardhard}
Satisfiability of negative binary clauses together with affine equations over $\F$ is NP-complete, even when the affine equations have disjoint supports of size three.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Given a 3-CNF formula, introduce a selector for each literal occurrence. For the three selectors of each clause impose odd parity and forbid every pair from both being selected. Odd parity allows one or three selected selectors; the exclusions remove three. Thus exactly one occurrence is selected per clause.

Forbid a selected occurrence of $x$ and a selected occurrence of $\neg x$ anywhere in the formula. These are also negative binary clauses. A satisfying source assignment supplies one true occurrence per clause, so the selector instance is satisfiable. Conversely a feasible selector choice contains no contradictory literals. Assign selected literals their requested truth values and extend all other variables arbitrarily. Every clause has its selected true literal. The transformation has polynomial size, and a selector assignment can be verified in polynomial time.
\end{proof}

This theorem concerns the general mixed constraint language. It does not prove that every such instance is a residual-cycle conflict graph of the special flow source. That additional realization would need its own proof. Nevertheless, it rules out the inference that a formulation is polynomial merely because its nonaffine guards are binary.

DY13 gives a useful narrower result: with fixed total syndrome rank $k$, a 2-CNF support can be searched through unions of at most $k$ principal implication closures. The number of candidates is $\sum_{i\le k}\binom gi$, polynomial for fixed $k$ but not automatically for $k=O(\log N)$ \cite{dy13}. Thus a logarithmic \emph{exponential kernel} and a logarithmic parameter in an arbitrary algorithm are different promises.

\section{A precise constructive composition theorem}

The strongest current positive synthesis is not arbitrary hull intersection. It preserves the exact information required by a specified consumer. The following formulation restates the BD13 witnessed-span construction with its scope made explicit \cite{bd13}.

\subsection{Authenticated affine observation tree}

Let $z\in\F^d$ parameterize one consistent affine chart. We are given an explicit binary tree. Each leaf $i$ is a vector function $f_i(z)\in\mathbb Q^{D_i}$. Its coordinates are explicit signed sums of affine-subspace indicators. Internal node $S$ with children $A,B$ computes an explicitly represented bilinear map
\[
f_S(z)=B_S(f_A(z),f_B(z)).
\]
The root has a linear readout $r^T f_{\mathrm{root}}(z)$. A source adapter must establish that a source witness exists if and only if some chart word makes that readout nonzero, with a polynomial inverse map. CNF provides a direct example: a clause is $1-\mathbf1_{\text{all literals false}}$ and the root is the product of the clause factors.

Let $V_S\subseteq(\F^d)^*$ be the span of all observation rows used below $S$, and let $V_{\bar S}$ be the span of rows outside it. The exact cut space is
\[
W_S=V_S\cap V_{\bar S},\qquad w_S=\dim W_S.
\]
A key is an actual linear functional on $W_S$, with its current source/chart/guard provenance. It is not a collection of independent marginal signs.

\begin{lemma}[Exact child-key gluing]
For sibling subtrees $A,B$, the common space $V_A\cap V_B$ lies in both child cut spaces. Compatible child words glue uniquely as a linear functional on $V_A+V_B$. Their keys determine the parent key.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each sibling belongs to the other's exterior, so their common observation rows belong to both cuts. Linear functionals on two spaces extend consistently to their sum exactly when they agree on the intersection, and that extension is unique on the sum. If $v\in W_S$, write $v=a+b$ with $a\in V_A,b\in V_B$ and $v\in V_{\bar S}$. Then $a=v+b\in W_A$ and $b=v+a\in W_B$. Hence the value on $v$ is determined by the child keys. A functional on any row subspace extends to the full chart, so these are actual possible observation words.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Store witnessed spans, not fictitious witnesses}

For each key $\lambda$ on $W_S$, define
\[
X_S(\lambda)=\{f_S(z):z\text{ is an actual chart word with key }\lambda\}.
\]
Store a basis of $\spanof_{\mathbb Q}X_S(\lambda)$ selected from actual members of $X_S(\lambda)$, with an actual chart observation word for each basis vector. A formal signed linear combination is never itself declared to be a source witness.

\begin{theorem}[Exact witnessed-span contraction]\label{thm:span}
For an authenticated tree as above, compatible pairs of child basis vectors generate exactly the parent output span. At the root, a nonzero readout exists if and only if it is nonzero on one retained actual basis vector. The corresponding stored words reconstruct a genuine source witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix compatible child keys. Every actual child output is a linear combination of its retained basis. Bilinearity expresses any actual parent output as a linear combination of the images of basis pairs. Conversely each retained child vector has an actual word, and compatible keys guarantee that each basis pair's words glue. Its bilinear image is therefore an actual parent value. Taking an independent subset of these actual images preserves exactly the parent span and actual witnesses. Induct over the tree.

At the root, the readout is linear. If it vanishes on the retained basis it vanishes on every actual value. If it is nonzero on a retained vector, that vector has a stored genuine word. Apply the authenticated inverse map and verify the original source.
\end{proof}

The consumer restriction is essential. Equal spans of $\{1\}$ and $\{0,1\}$ do not determine whether zero occurs. Separately compressing two occurrences of the same hidden variable before a nonlinear multiplication can lose their correlation. For example, $x$ and $1-x$ each take a nonzero value, but their product never does. The cut key must keep the shared $x$, or a different exact joint adapter must be paid for. An arbitrary DAG cannot be unfolded exponentially and then charged as if the tree were its original size.

\subsection{Polynomial acquisition of one leaf span}

On an affine fiber $C$ of a leaf key, form the exact Gram matrix
\[
Q_C=\sum_{z\in C} f_i(z)f_i(z)^T.
\]
Expanding the explicit signed packet list reduces each entry to counts of intersections of affine spaces. Gaussian elimination gives each count as zero or a power of two. If a leaf has $L_i$ signed terms in total, this takes $O(L_i^2)$ such intersection jobs, with polynomial overhead for vector entries and rational arithmetic.

The matrix is positive semidefinite over the reals. Its kernel is exactly the space orthogonal to all actual values on $C$, so its column space is the desired output span. Given a retained actual span $H$, the rank test
\[
\rank[H\ Q_C]>\dim H
\]
holds exactly when a new actual value exists outside $H$. Choose such a word by fixing chart coordinates one at a time. At each step, test the zero half; retain it if the rank test succeeds, otherwise retain the one half. The existence invariant is preserved. After at most $d$ probes an actual new vector is found. Repeat at most $D_i$ times.

These coordinate choices are guarded witness-search steps, not unconditional deductions. The method uses an explicit local packet description. It does not supply a free global signed expansion of a product of many source factors.

\subsection{The actual work bound}

Define the reserved job count
\begin{align}
J={}&1+\sum_{i\text{ leaf}}2^{w_i}(D_i+1)(d+1)\max(1,L_i)^2\\
&+\sum_{S=A\cup B\text{ internal}}2^{w_A+w_B}D_A D_B.\label{eq:jobs}
\end{align}
Let $A(N)$ include parsing, source compilation, authentication, discovery of the tree and its cuts, and failed candidate attempts. Under polynomial encoding bounds for all represented objects,
\[
T\le A(N)+\poly(N)J,
\qquad
S\le\poly(N)\left(1+\sum_S2^{w_S}D_S\right).
\]
Exact values and rank computations have polynomial bit length for an explicit polynomial-size arithmetic tree with polynomial-bit coefficients. A bilinear node adds child bit bounds plus the coefficient and summation overhead. This argument does not cover an uncharged exponential unfolding of shared repeated squaring.

Thus fixed logarithmic cuts, polynomial tree size, polynomial vector dimensions and packet sizes, and polynomial discovery suffice for a uniform polynomial terminal. Large local affine dimension $d$ alone is not fatal. The unproved condition is that every relevant residual can be transformed into such a paid representation, or another exact terminal, with a polynomial total reserve.

\section{How the earlier wheel, kernel, and spectral results fit}

The following components support the construction when their hypotheses are verified. They do not jointly imply universal coverage simply because each is polynomial on its own promised domain.

\subsection{Cubic colorings, small cuts, and small exponential kernels}

In the zero-overlap matching domain, colors are the three nonzero elements of $\F^2$. At a cubic vertex their XOR is zero exactly when the three colors are distinct. Summing vertex equations across a region gives zero XOR on its boundary. A bridge therefore rules out coloring; a two-edge cut has three equal-color boundary words, and a three-edge cut has six distinct-color words.

CC02 gives exact tree gluing after these cuts, retaining original edge IDs and charging each original cost once. If $d_i$ is a torso's binary cycle rank and $w_i$ is the crossing-edge width of an acquired order, its two exact backends give
\[
T\le\poly(N)\left(1+\sum_i\min\{2^{d_i},3^{w_i}\}\right).
\]
The second binary flow coordinate is eliminated as one phase per active cycle after fixing the first, subject to the local interface-message hypothesis. This is an exact upper bound with a paid representation, not a claim that every torso has small $d_i$ or $w_i$ \cite{cc02}.

An exponential kernel is permitted in a polynomial algorithm. The sufficient quantitative statement is $2^{c k}N^d$ with fixed constants and $k\le C\log_2 N$, giving $N^{d+cC}$. By contrast, $N^{O(k)}$ or $2^{k^2}$ at $k=O(\log N)$ need not be polynomial. All kernel discovery and lifting costs also belong in the bound.

The earlier twisted and untwisted flower families show why parallel techniques are useful: cycle rank grows linearly while a suitable acquired frontier order stays bounded. CC01 diamond-chain elimination similarly handles arbitrarily large structured repairs by a constant-state interface rather than enumerating every internal change \cite{cc01,cc02}. These are infinite-family constructions with explicit adapters. An arbitrary core can survive the contraction, so the examples do not prove universal simplification.

\subsection{Fewer ports can yield a real terminal}

\begin{proposition}[Restricted bipartite port completion]\label{prop:ports}
Let a bipartite multipole have total degree three at every internal vertex, counting dangling ports. Suppose an ordered port-color word is prescribed and some color $a$ occurs on all but at most two ports. Feasibility can be decided and a completion constructed in polynomial time. In particular, all feasible boundary words on at most five ports fall within this test.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write the bipartition as $L,R$. For each color $c$, a necessary boundary balance is
\[
p_L(c)-p_R(c)=|L|-|R|,
\]
obtained by counting color-$c$ incidences on both sides. Reject repeated prescribed colors at a vertex and any failed balance. Vertices already incident to an $a$-colored port need no internal $a$ edge. On all other vertices, a completion must select a perfect matching of the induced remaining graph; find one by bipartite matching or reject.

After coloring that matching by $a$, every vertex has exactly two remaining incidences, so the remaining components are even internal cycles and paths between non-$a$ ports. There are at most two such ports. One port cannot pass the balance equations. With two ports of the same color, those equations put their endpoint vertices on opposite sides, so the path has the parity permitting equal endpoint colors. With two different colors, they put the vertices on the same side, giving the parity permitting different colors. Alternate the remaining two colors around each cycle or along the path. This constructs a completion for any found matching.

For at most five ports, the usual color parity identity follows from $|V|=2e_c+p(c)$ for each color. All three port-color counts have the same parity. For an even number at most four, the largest count leaves at most two other ports; for an odd number at most five, the only feasible positive patterns are $(1,1,1)$ and $(3,1,1)$ up to permutation. Thus the dominant-color condition covers every feasible boundary word.
\end{proof}

This is the CC06 terminal. It needs bipartiteness, actual port colors, and the bounded exceptional-port condition; it is not a theorem about all hubs merely because they have small degree. The recovered checker reports 7,220 exhaustive boundary comparisons on 100 graphs. Those are historical results, not newly rerun measurements \cite{cc06}.

\subsection{Spectral acquisition is exact when the packet is explicit}

CC08 studies an explicitly supplied signed affine packet
\[
f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^M c_j\mathbf1_{A_j}(x).
\]
If $A_j=a_j+L_j$, its unnormalized Fourier transform is
\[
\widehat{\mathbf1_{A_j}}(w)=|A_j|(-1)^{w\cdot a_j}\mathbf1_{L_j^\perp}(w).
\]
For any affine frequency constraint $Q$, the squared energy
\[
E_Q=\sum_{w\in Q}\widehat f(w)^2
\]
can be computed from $M^2$ pairwise character sums on affine intersections. Gaussian counting evaluates those sums exactly, including signed cancellation. Since $E_Q\ge0$, it is positive precisely when actual Fourier support meets $Q$.

Repeatedly query outside the span of discovered frequencies and use guarded coordinate self-reduction to find a new one. After at most $d$ independent additions the exact support span is known. Its orthogonal complement is precisely the translation-period space of $f$. This gives a constructive minimal linear observation space for the explicit packet \cite{cc08}.

The hypothesis is not harmless: a polynomial-size \emph{global Boolean} signed affine packet for arbitrary SAT would already allow polynomial decision by summing its values through affine counts. The missing global compilation cannot be assumed in order to prove the algorithm. Local spectral simplification is useful when packet growth, consumer scope, and every subsequent query are paid.

\subsection{What factorization and extra tracks can actually contribute}

CC04's matching-rim work uses exact bounded-port transfer states to discover repairs whose size grows linearly in the source. CC05's triangle expansion preserves arbitrary cubic coloring while producing constant-size local rims. Together they separate two issues: large repairs can be polynomially represented on a suitable quotient, but small local pieces do not guarantee an easy global quotient \cite{cc04,cc05}.

DP13 avoids multiplying independent feedback costs across articulation blocks. It acquires probe-stable endpoint function spans, removes certified rank-one couplings, and sums the paid slice jobs of biconnected blocks. The unweighted rank of one contraction is insufficient: a later pin can increase that rank \cite{dp13}.

MS19 adds joint integer-lattice necessities to the affine channels. Integer-lattice nonmembership can refute a target; membership can still contain holes and is not a genuine feasible matching. New rows may increase interface width, which must be remeasured \cite{ms19}. S06 combines flow with exchangeable exceptional counts, while DP01 supplies exact conserved count-box credit \cite{s06,dp01}. These results demonstrate cooperation when their messages refer to the same guarded source.

Changing orientation, switching tracks, or using a double cover, M\"obius-style drawing, or another topology is useful only through an exact algebraic or combinatorial adapter. A duplicated graph does not create independent source choices. The projection to the original assignment, all synchronization equations, boundary orientations, and any reconstruction multiplicity remain part of the representation. No universal benefit follows from the name of the topology alone.

\section{The conditional global proof and its resource ledger}

\subsection{A sufficient completion theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Paid universal normalization implies $P=NP$]\label{thm:conditional}
Suppose one uniform deterministic procedure, on every valid input $F$ of the four-partition NP-complete source class, does the following with constants independent of $F$.
\begin{enumerate}[label={},leftmargin=0pt,itemsep=3pt]
\item[] It acquires and verifies source-equivalent guarded states of encoding length polynomial in $N=|F|$, retaining explicit source reconstruction.
\item[] Each state is solved by an exact paid terminal, or replaced by an exact finite decision-cover of guarded children. The parent is SAT exactly when at least one child is SAT; all child-to-source lifts are correct.
\item[] All discovery, failed attempts, closure, terminal operations, branch aggregation, dictionary operations, and reconstruction fit a polynomial aggregate job reserve, with polynomial bit cost per job.
\end{enumerate}
Then the procedure decides that class in deterministic polynomial time and $P=NP$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An induction over the finite reduction computation proves decision correctness from exact terminals and exact decision-covers. A successful terminal witness lifts through the recorded maps and is verified against $F$. Exhausting all covered children certifies UNSAT by the same induction. Polynomially many jobs of polynomial bit cost give polynomial total deterministic time. The source completeness theorem transfers decision to arbitrary NP languages via polynomial reductions. SAT witness reconstruction, when needed, is already charged or can be obtained by polynomially many calls to a uniform decision procedure. This does not imply exact counting.
\end{proof}

This theorem isolates the missing premise rather than proving it. No construction in the current record establishes its polynomial aggregate reserve on every source input.

\subsection{Potential sufficient conditions and necessary precautions}

A concrete tree reserve can be a positive integer $B(s)$ satisfying $B(\text{root})\le N^C$ and
\[
\sum_{u\text{ child of }s}B(u)\le B(s)-1.
\]
Every processed state consumes at least one unit. This bounds the total processed states by the root reserve. Terminal work must either be polynomial per state or explicitly allocated its own reserve. If each state has a polynomial-size nonnegative integer potential $\mu(s)\ge1$ with
\[
\sum_u\mu(u)^c\le\mu(s)^c-1
\]
for one fixed positive integer $c$, then $B=\mu^c$ supplies this condition. It is the magnitude, not the bit length, of $B$ that must be polynomial. Zero-credit children and uncharged fan-out cannot be used to hide work.

For a shared DAG, an alternative proof may bound the number of distinct fully specified reachable keys and all attempted edges. Each key must include the original source, residual constraints, pins, affine right-hand sides, quotas, actual port order, retained observers, and reconstruction data. A semantic-equivalence test is not free canonicalization. A dictionary keyed by exact serialized data is legitimate when its volume and comparison cost are bounded. Reusing a node saves its recomputation; every incoming edge and read still costs work.

The reserve $J$ defined in Section 8.4, the CC02 sum of local kernel costs, and DP01's count-box potential are valid examples within their certified domains. None is presently proved polynomial on every residual. Large values also do not establish hardness; an alternative terminal may be cheap.

\subsection{A finite exact portfolio and the diagonal quantifiers}

Let $A$ be a fixed exact deterministic portfolio with a terminating fallback and a fair schedule of finitely many certified channels. Suppose its scheduling overhead is polynomial in input length and the fastest channel's fully charged time. If $A$ has no uniform polynomial worst-case bound, then for every exponent and every length floor there is a longer input violating that cap; otherwise finitely many short inputs could be absorbed into a constant.

Choose an increasing sequence with sufficiently enlarged exponents to absorb scheduling constants and polynomial overhead. Along it every fixed channel eventually fails to finish within each fixed polynomial cap. This is a simultaneous hard sequence for that \emph{fixed portfolio}. It does not say that one finite input defeats every algorithm, that both SAT and UNSAT occur infinitely often, or that a large parameter upper bound proves an actual running-time lower bound.

A genuine universal structural theorem could contradict such a sequence by forcing a paid polynomial terminal or aggregate contraction. DO01 proves obstructions only to specified raw terminal parameters; its high-girth construction is not an exclusion of all algorithms \cite{do01}. The present full-hull result similarly excludes one summary rule, leaving the universal problem open.

\section{The remaining theorem to prove}

\begin{conjecture}[Universal compatible-repair acquisition]\label{conj:gap}
There is a uniform deterministic procedure that, for every four-partition monotone linear NAE source and each residual it creates, discovers a source-equivalent guarded composition or exact reduction whose total acquisition and evaluation reserve is bounded by $N^C$ for one fixed constant $C$. All shared variable correlations and original witness maps are retained, and the procedure terminates with either a verified witness or an exact refutation.
\end{conjecture}

The strongest concrete version uses the actual-interface tree of Section 8 and proves $A(N)+\poly(N)J\le N^C$, or reaches another exact terminal under the same global reserve. One need not prove that every individual rank is logarithmic if a direct algorithm, factorization, or an additive sum of local jobs avoids paying that exponential. One must nevertheless bound the work of discovering and combining those alternatives.

The current results narrow what can satisfy this obligation. Two-half affine closure at the unpinned root cannot do so, by Theorem~\ref{thm:fullhull}. A polynomial list of all one-third-dimensional charts cannot be assumed, by the explicit cover bound. Listing every residual simple cycle cannot be assumed polynomial, by Proposition~\ref{prop:catalogue}. Retaining only binary conflicts and affine equations does not by itself give an easy constraint language, by Proposition~\ref{prop:guardhard}. These exclusions preserve the possibility of a different exact constructive route.
