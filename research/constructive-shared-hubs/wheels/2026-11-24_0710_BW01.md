## 2. Palette and literal carriers (PROVED)
Let p0,p1,p2,p3 form a K4. Colors are named by their palette vertex; this is a relative frame, not externally precolored input. A vertex with list L is joined to all palette vertices outside L. All lists in this proof are implemented with actual graph edges. For each Boolean variable x, create x and its complement carrier, each adjacent to p0,p1 and to one another. They have colors 2+x and 3-x. Thus true literal carriers have color 3, false carriers color 2. Distinct literal occurrences may attach to the same carrier. No sharing of private auxiliaries.

## 3. Exact odd-wheel factor (PROVED)
Take an odd rim length L>=3, with private rim vertices v0,...,v(L-1), edges around the cycle and all spokes to a center h of color 3. For each of at most three literal sites, join its rim vertex to the appropriate literal carrier; join each unused rim site to p2. If every literal is false, all rim vertices must use colors 0,1, impossible on an odd cycle. If any literal is true, put color 2 at that site and alternate 0,1 on the remaining path. All spokes and extra edges hold; this is an explicit O(L) lift. Therefore the exact projected support is the OR of the literals, including repeated literals or short clauses, not an affine hull or independently selectable guards. An empty clause has no extension. For a clause of three distinct literals, the relation is full affine hull and not majority-closed (100,010,001 -> 000), so exact purely affine or bijunctive projection cannot replace this factor. NAE triple is two such wheels with opposite literal signs.

## 4. Shared hub equality and sharding (PROVED)
In ordinary 4-coloring, add three fresh private vertices forming a triangle and make each adjacent to both tips a,b. The triangle uses three distinct colors, hence both tips have the fourth color. Conversely if tips have the same color, assign the other three colors to the triangle. Exact equality, six private extensions. For an arbitrary hub and any partition of its incident edges, create a fresh clone for each part and link it to the original hub by its own equality triangle; route each old edge to the appropriate clone. Both directions follow pointwise, and independent private triangles lift simultaneously. O(number of clones) extra size; source edge multiplicity and all protected tip colors must be retained. This applies to splitting spokes among shared copies, not to a claim that replacing arbitrary rims by disjoint odd cycles preserves them without additional constraints.

For clause wheels choose private centers h_i equal to p3 by this gadget. Their exact projection is the same as identifying all centers with p3. Entire wheels may also be duplicated, with identical signed literal carriers, guard and hub frame. Conditional extension is unchanged because private witness choices can be made independently. Counts of full colorings generally change; decision and protected boundary relations are exact. In a signed multisign DAG, identical clause reject guards can be syntactically merged after source equality authentication. A reject guard is ALL its literals false. It is not an unconditional contradiction merely because the center or wheel type agrees.

## 5. Actual free tetrahedral two-bit hub (PROVED)
Pack a pair of Boolean carriers b0,b1 into one vertex H with arbitrary four-color domain and require color(H)=2*b0+b1. For each bit b, add a private shadow s with list {0,1}; b carrier is c with list {2,3}. An auxiliary vertex with list {a,d} adjacent to two endpoints whose lists are disjoint forbids the unique endpoint pair (a,d) when a belongs only to the first and d only to the second. For a free-color H as first endpoint, it still forbids precisely (H=a, endpoint=d) if the second endpoint cannot take a: the swapped pair is impossible. Use two auxiliaries with lists {2,1} and {3,0}, adjacent to c,s, to enforce s=b. For each color a of H choose c when a<2, s otherwise; the chosen endpoint has a domain not containing a. Let d be its color for the WRONG desired bit of a in the binary expansion. Add auxiliary with list {a,d} adjacent to H and chosen endpoint. This excludes exactly the wrong bit at a. Four such tests per bit; all auxiliary vertices are private, so all permitted endpoint assignments extend independently by selecting an available list color. Thus exact packed hub projection and O(1) reconstruction. Uses one H, two shadows, twelve auxiliaries, 52 edges beyond existing carriers/palette. H is a free four-state boundary observation, distinct from the palette-fixed clause-wheel center. Pairing bits does not reduce independent entropy.

## 6. Strict source compilation and global gluing (PROVED)

## 7. Critical consequence: gluing remains NP-complete (PROVED)
Even the class with a single shared fixed-color wheel center after clone contraction, constant length private rims, one palette and Boolean literal carriers, contains a polynomial pointwise image of arbitrary 3SAT. Thus global wheel compatibility is NP-complete (membership by explicit coloring verification). Equivalently the OR of all local reject guards covers every carrier assignment iff the input is UNSAT; universal coverage is coNP-complete. This is a concrete geometric realization of the previously abstract GD coverage barrier, not a separation or new general NP-completeness theorem for coloring. A claim that one hub class, bounded local wheel size, or polynomial guarded DAG size alone guarantees a P terminal would imply P=NP. It does not rule out a new global polynomial solver. In particular DO01 NAE scopes transfer pointwise via opposite-sign wheel pairs; Fano neighbors remain SAT. S06/DP01 cost/count grouping requires its own exact compatibility hypotheses and cannot be inferred from wheel shape. DA01 binary-interface case may remain useful; REL01 exact overlap deletions may be applied on projected clauses. Nonempty closure is UNKNOWN.
