## 2. Domain
Let K=F2^2. G is an explicit connected loopless multigraph of maximum degree four, counted with edge multiplicity. Each edge uv forbids x_u+x_v=s_e, with s_e in K. Every vertex has the full four-color domain. No hidden consumers, pins, quotas, native equality or balance factors. For a fixed actual common exterior chart word z, allow signs s_e(z); then all claims apply pointwise. The chart is not a physical free wiring mechanism. A terminal is any designated vertex of degree <=3, with at least one spare physical exclusion port. T can be any subset of such vertices. All other slack vertices are also relevant to extension. The presence of a terminal guarantees a coloring by elementary greedy construction. This is NOT the full E4/B4 model.
For distinct terminals a,b let R_ab={x_a+x_b : x proper on G}. Global translation of every color by the same element of K preserves every edge, so R_ab completely specifies the exact two-terminal projection. Write delta_ab=4-|R_ab|.

## 3. BW12-A: pair-support decomposition and constructive lifts (PROVED)
Remove a,b, keeping all actual edge incidences as boundary records. For each resulting connected component C, classify:
(i) slack: some v in C has original degree <=3. For ANY prescribed colors of a,b, color C in reverse spanning-tree order rooted at v. Every nonroot has an uncolored tree parent and hence at most three already active exclusions; root has at most three total. This remains true with parallel edges. All boundary restrictions respected.
(ii) one-sided: C meets at most one of a,b. For any prescribed boundary color, use the restriction of an original coloring translated to agree with that boundary. Such an original coloring exists by reverse spanning-tree greedy rooted at a. No interaction with the other boundary.
(iii) tight two-sided: every vertex in C has original degree 4, and C meets both a,b. Only these components and direct a-b edges can restrict R_ab. Each component is independent given a,b, so feasibility is the conjunction of its exact tests with all direct exclusions.
Define A_ab as the set of edge IDs incident to a that are either direct a-b edges or enter a tight two-sided component for this pair. Define A_ba analogously. Freeze b and the tight interiors to their colors in one original model. Vary a. At most |A_ab| colors of a are excluded by these actual incidences. All other components extend for each choice by (i)/(ii). Consequently delta_ab <= |A_ab|, and symmetrically delta_ab <= |A_ba|. This proof does not depend on a coloring oracle.

Exact acquisition: for each pair test the four normalized pins (a=0,b=d), d in K. Greedy/translation for (i)/(ii); for (iii) use BW11/DA11 authenticated boundary-list projection and fixed-word witness reconstruction, with the actual boundary occurrences and affine signs retained. The local projection theorem applies because total exclusions including boundary occurrences are <=4; no extra consumer is discarded. If a chart z is variable, each d yields an exact factored AND of the acquired regional escape unions and direct tests, rather than enumerating all z. This is a support relation, not a signed Boolean packet count. For fixed signs, any returned pair word has a global lift assembled over the disjoint private components. Every original model supplies the converse.

## 4. BW12-B: port-deficit conservation (PROVED, elementary)
For a fixed terminal a the sets A_ab, as b ranges over other terminals, are pairwise disjoint. Suppose e=a-v lies in A_ab. If e is direct to b then the neighbor b is slack and cannot belong to an active two-sided component for (a,c), c different from a,b. Otherwise v is in a tight component C of G-{a,b} with a path from v to b avoiding a and all other terminals (they are slack and so cannot be in C). In G-{a,c}, v lies in the component containing b, which is slack. Therefore e cannot be in A_ac. Distinct direct edges and multiplicities are counted by actual IDs. It follows that

    sum_{b in T minus {a}} (4-|R_ab|) <= sum_b |A_ab| <= deg_G(a) <= 3.

The bound holds for any topology, cycles, duplicated wheels, parallel channels, fixed signs or pointwise affine signs. It does not require the DP-degree-colorability theorem. More generally the first inequality is valid for any four-color translation-invariant singleton-exclusion graph meeting the degree/slack premises. It is a conservation law for exact pairwise support deficits, not a time lower bound.

### No-fanout corollaries
Full color equality between two terminals (possibly translated) has |R|=1 and consumes three incidences at each endpoint. A pair forced to share one nonzero linear bit (possibly with offset) has |R|<=2 and consumes at least two. Three distinct spare terminals sharing the same bit would give deficit >=4 at EACH terminal, impossible. This rules out even single-bit fanout, not merely two-bit equality copying.
Stronger: let each terminal have its own nonconstant Boolean decoder f_a:K->F2, possibly nonlinear or dependent on the fixed exterior z. If every coloring enforces f_a(x_a)=f_b(x_b), global translation gives f_a(u)=f_b(u+d) for all u and every d in R_ab. For two such d,d', f_b is invariant under d+d'. Its period subgroup is proper because f_b is nonconstant, hence size <=2. Thus |R_ab|<=2. The same conservation law rules out three-way agreement of arbitrary nonconstant single-vertex decoders. No assumption of linear decoders is needed. It does NOT exclude logical encodings distributed across several vertices per terminal, native equality hyperedges, non-translation-invariant lists, or integer balances.
Sharp controls: triple parallel edges with signs 1,2,3 force equality, degrees 3,3. Double edges with signs 2,3 force equality of the high bit, degrees 2,2. Add a single exclusion from one endpoint to a third vertex: pair deficits 2+1=3 attain the cap, but no third shared bit. Chain two double edges: middle vertex has degree four, cannot be used as a spare terminal. An arbitrarily long chain can transmit one bit to its two ends, not expose all intermediate vertices. This clarifies why the earlier 3-color diamond quotient with high-degree hub clones is not a strict four-valent physical splitter.

## 5. Constructive pairwise compression and its joint-support limit
At fixed signs, replace each terminal pair by one parallel signed exclusion for each forbidden difference in K minus R_ab. The conservation law certifies total degree at each terminal <= its original degree. This realizes the exact pairwise relaxation on terminals using O(|T|) edges (at most 3|T|/2). Every original projection satisfies it, hence its own pair marginals equal the original pair marginals (the new pair constraints give the reverse inclusion). For exactly two terminals this is an exact existential replacement with paid original lift. For three or more, it is only a necessary relaxation unless complete joint equivalence is separately certified. At variable signs, do not interpret the fixed-word replacement as a single unguarded graph; retain actual guarded pair support.
Hostile exact three-port gadget: private center u has two edges to a with signs 0,1 and one each to b,c with sign 0. Relative to x_a=0, reject exactly when (x_b,x_c)=(2,3) or (3,2). Each terminal-pair support is FULL, yet the joint support is proper. This is a degree-four center and spare endpoints, so the obstruction is within the very same language. Pairwise deficit zero cannot certify deletion. The example also shows that even a locally tractable physical graph may express a nonaffine joint relation when ports are fixed by a larger context.
For fixed-sign exact 2-terminal substitution, all old components must have no outside observations except these two ports. Store an original source map and lift procedure rather than merely a support word. No model-count preservation promised. For variable signs, retain chart and all conditional events. No implicit conversion to count packets, occupancy tags, or S06 row-product semantics.
