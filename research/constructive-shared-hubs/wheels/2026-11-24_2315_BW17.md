## Exact theorem: minority cover and common batch girth
Let four perfect triple partitions be positive and linear (no repeated unordered pair); A and B are two paired sides, x and y their respective legal words, x XOR y=e_d. Keep actual common pins. For every owner record its unique minority coordinate i in the legal word of its side; remaining coordinates j,k are majority. A common simultaneous flip S, not including d or pinned coordinates, preserves both side feasibilities iff for every actual owner S intersect {i,j,k} is neither {i} nor {j,k}. Proof: in the original legal triple the minority differs from both majority bits; the only two ways to make it constant are flipping just the minority or both majority positions. This is an exact signed-local criterion too, with minority in literal values. Every nondefect coordinate is blocked as a common singleton iff it is minority in at least one owner (pins independently block). If d is minority in both incident owners of each side, M23's proof excludes every nonidentity unilateral replacement keeping disagreement <=1.

Moreover if each unpinned nondefect coordinate is minority in an actual owner, any nonempty feasible common batch S has size at least the Berge girth of the combined registry. For each i in S choose an owner in which it is minority. By the exact criterion at least one majority coordinate j belongs to S. Direct i to one such j. The finite digraph has a directed cycle of distinct coordinates; the owners on this cycle are distinct because each has exactly one recorded minority. This gives a Berge cycle of that length. In particular linearity rules out a two-cycle. This conditional lower bound does not assert existence of unbounded-girth frozen distance-one SAT starts, and does not exclude nonmonotone moves, multiple simultaneous unilateral changes, target acquisition, or different representations.
