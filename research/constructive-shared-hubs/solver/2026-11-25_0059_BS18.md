## Target and theorem (proved, restricted)
Work with an explicit binary row-selector CSP, all actual guards absorbed or represented, one live row per owner and all pair compatibilities. For a fixed owner block H of size h and a compatible tuple a on H, let N(a) be the live exterior rows compatible with every member of a. For distinct compatible alternatives b1,...,bt on H, put B_i=N(a) minus N(b_i). If there is no selection of one blocker from each B_i that is mutually compatible (the same exterior row may be reused, different rows of the same owner are incompatible), delete internal tuple a while retaining the alternatives. Indeed any exterior transversal supporting a but none of the alternatives gives one selected blocker for each. The certificate therefore preserves exact exterior projection; conversely all surviving transversals already satisfy the prior chart. For fixed h,t discovery/replay costs polynomial in explicit length (enumerate O(R^h) tuples, O(R^(ht)) alternative choices, O(R^t) blocker choices; pair tests and all AC/access/lifts charged). For h=1 row deletion and h=2 pair deletion fit the fixed-span DR18/BD18 reserve; for h>2, an explicitly charged higher-arity representative registry and consumer semantics are additional obligations, not free pair masks. Never export necessity from the representative chart to the original without transport. An existing explicit tuple registry can also be shrunk by this rule, with its own bounded reserve.
For h=1,t=2, let B,C be blockers; the test is B∩C empty and no compatible cross pair (equivalently C disjoint from B union union_{z in B} N(z)). Single dominance is t=1. This extends the class of deletion certificates beyond single dominance and complements DP19 conditional pivot replacement, not a claim of strict superiority to DP19. Strict fixed-point example: center A={a,b,c}, binary X,Y,Z; a accepts either X but only Y=0; b accepts X=0 and either Y; c accepts X=1 and either Y; Y=Z, other pairs unrestricted. All rows have incomparable same-owner supports; deleting a preserves all four exterior words, models 6->4 (executed separate exhaustive Python check). Primary checker strict-two simpler two-owner example demonstrates a specific deletion not available by one alternative.
Raw linear signed NAE with min coordinate degree >=2 defeats the h=1,t<=2 certificate: choose one differing coordinate per alternative. If same coordinate, one exterior owner row agreeing with a blocks both. If different, choose respective exterior owners (distinct by linearity), each agrees with a on its one shared coordinate; their possible intersection is at most one additional coordinate, choose its value jointly. Full two-coordinate projections of signed NAE extend both. Thus compatible blockers exist. Applies to DO01 critical UNSAT and corresponding raw signed NAE SAT neighbors where degree condition holds; no claim about all relaxed budget neighbors or transformed charts. More generally all-shared blocks (each internal coordinate also observed outside block) uniquely fixed by exterior: no actual feasible tuple can be replaced while keeping exterior fixed.
## Universal recognition barrier
Exact removability preserving all exterior words of one universal row is coNP-complete even on a binary star with independent Boolean leaves and one center. Given 3-CNF F, create universal row a and one alternative per non-tautological clause, compatible precisely with assignments falsifying that clause (independent restrictions to leaves, unconstrained absent coordinates). Removing a preserves exterior iff every word falsifies some clause iff F UNSAT. A missing exterior word is a polynomial certificate, so coNP-complete. Star itself always SAT, no decision lower bound. Arbitrary complete coverage cannot be assumed polynomial; fixed alternatives and sound incomplete recognizers avoid circularity. Falsifiers: loss of exterior witness, uncharged blocker enumeration, missing actual guards, counting/necessity confusion.
