\section{Planar algebraic contraction and a crossover obstruction (BA16)}\label{sec:ba16}
The S21/DS16 one-mismatch acquisition problem remains the load-bearing instance of U-PROGRESS. BW14's locally planar wheel replacements and M21's disjoint wheels do not control planarity of their synchronized global source. We investigate whether a joint matching/algebra terminal can use planar topology plus a low-rank exceptional syndrome, and whether local crossover replacement could supply that topology universally. The classical unguarded planar NAE decision result is Moret (1988), \href{https://doi.org/10.1145/49097.49099}{DOI}. The extension below uses the signed perfect-matching polynomial and its cofacial identities (Cai--Gorenstein, \href{https://doi.org/10.4086/toc.2014.v010a007}{Matchgates Revisited}, Theorem 2.1, equations 2.1--2.6).

\begin{lemma}[Planar signed energy with logarithmic exceptional syndrome]\label{lem:ba16-planar}
Let an explicit signed ternary NAE registry have $n$ variables and $m$ owners. Replace each owner by its three labelled pair interactions, counting multiplicity. Suppose a set of exceptional interactions $E_1$ is supplied or acquired within a fixed polynomial cap, whose removal leaves a planar multigraph $E_0$. Let $W$ be the binary span of the incidence parity rows $e_u+e_v$ of $E_1$ and the rows of a recorded actual affine guard $Gx=b$. If $h=\dim W\le C\log_2(N+2)$ for a fixed $C$, exact guarded decision, counting, witness acquisition and individual arbitrary affine parity probes are uniformly polynomial-bit procedures. The exception set and embedding are verified, not assumed discoverable universally. The unguarded result does not require any bound on vertex degree.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
With actual signed literal bits $x_u+\eta_{iu}$, let $d_e(x)$ indicate equality of the two literals at an owner pair. The energy $L(x)=\sum_e d_e(x)$ equals $m+2B(x)$ where $B$ is the number of violated owners. Thus the coefficient of $t^m$ in $Z(t)=\sum_{Gx=b}t^{L(x)}$ is the exact guarded model count. Every pair factor is $t^{d_e(x)}=A+B_e\chi_{e_u+e_v}(x)$ with $A=(1+t)/2$, $B_e=\pm(t-1)/2$, and $\chi_a(x)=(-1)^{a^\top x}$. The sign is determined by actual literal signs. Loops give constants. At any positive integer evaluation point $t$, expand the product of exceptional factors and guard indicators $(1+(-1)^{b_j}\chi_{G_j})/2$ as $\sum_{a\in W}c_a\chi_a$. Gaussian coordinates in $W$ give an array of $2^h$ coefficients updated by two-term convolutions in $O((|E_1|+\operatorname{rows}(G))2^h)$ arithmetic operations, including zero/duplicate rows. Then
\[
 Z(t)=\sum_{a\in W}c_a Z_{0,a}(t),\qquad
 Z_{0,a}(t)=\sum_x \chi_a(x)\prod_{e\in E_0}t^{d_e(x)}.
\]
Expansion of planar factors and spin summation give
\[
 Z_{0,a}(t)=2^n A^{|E_0|}\!
 \sum_{\substack{S\subseteq E_0\\ \partial S=a}}
 \prod_{e\in S}(B_e/A).
\]
Here $\partial S$ is the actual mod-two odd-incidence vector. It is not an enumeration of $S$: replace each planar vertex by a disk gadget with one port per edge and signature equal to one iff the selected ports have parity $a_v$. A ternary even-parity gadget is a planar $K_4$ with outer port triangle (outer edge weights $1/3$, central spokes $1$); the odd-parity gadget is the port triangle with edge weights $1$. Their signatures count perfect matchings after deleting selected ports. Binary even parity is a single unit edge; binary odd is a length-two unit path; unary even is one unit edge with one port, unary odd is one isolated port; nullary parity is constant. Larger arity splits into a planar chain of ternary parity gates in cyclic port order, with a unique internal parity word for each allowed external word. Join matching ports across each original edge by a new edge of weight $B_e/A$ (internal chain connectors have weight one). A perfect matching uses precisely the selected connectors, and the internal weighted sums equal the parity signatures. The resulting planar graph has polynomial size and rational bit weights. Its exact signed matching sum is computable via a planar Pfaffian orientation; determine the overall sign using positive weights on the same nonzero edge support (or a supplied matching), not an absolute value of the signed Pfaffian. Disconnected and isolated vertices are handled componentwise. This construction charges all graph assembly and matching computation. With at most $3m+1$ distinct positive integer $t$ evaluations, rational interpolation recovers the coefficient; all denominators, powers, coefficient arrays and determinant/Pfaffian arithmetic have polynomial bit length by the polynomial graph size and bounded evaluation points (exact fraction-free or rational minor-based arithmetic). One extra parity probe adds at most one to $h$.

For reconstruction do not accumulate absolute pins. Choose a nonloop planar edge, test its two relative parity values by substitution $x_u=x_v+\epsilon$ and contraction of that edge. This is a planar multigraph minor; transform exceptional and guard rows by the same affine substitution, whose linear image cannot increase their joint rank. Retain all objective constants, signs, parallel labelled edges and the original target coefficient. The counting test selects a positive fiber. Repeat at most $n$ times. If no planar edges remain, the whole remaining objective and guard depend only on the at most $h$ observations (plus constants); enumerate their words, solve the affine system and choose a valid one, then lift through the recorded contractions and check the original source. This also handles disconnected components and absolute pins represented by guard rows. No affine basis change is allowed to silently destroy the certified embedding. With BD14's witnessed affine-hull method, an anchor plus at most $n$ independent necessary-row or independent witnessed-difference parity queries yields complete affine hull on the same eligible input; newly certified necessary rows can remain recorded consequences rather than enlarging the guard used in subsequent equivalent count queries. Run ordinary certified closure first, after each accepted deduction and before any dispatch/decline; use closure consequences only with actual provenance. A conservative total bound is $2^{h+1}\operatorname{poly}(N)$, including all witness and closure probes, verification, reconstruction, discovery within the supplied cap and every consumer. Peak space is polynomial on the log-rank domain, and on the same serial run $CM\le(T+1)S$. This is a paid terminal, not a universal proof that one can acquire $E_1$ with the required rank.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[No cofacial exact crossover, even support-only]\label{lem:ba16-cross}
Consider any unpinned signed ternary NAE network with planar incidence graph embedded in a disk and four distinct actual boundary terminals in cyclic order $1,2,3,4$. Let $f(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)$ count private extensions and put $a_j=f(0,\operatorname{bin}_3(j))$, $0\le j<8$. Then
\[
 a_0a_5+a_3a_6=a_1a_4+a_2a_7.
\]
In particular no such network, of any size or auxiliary degree, has support exactly $x_1=x_3,\ x_2=x_4$ (nor its fixed signed translate).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Global complement gives $f(x)=f(x+\mathbf1)$. The local signed NAE Fourier factor is $(3-\sum_{u<v}(-1)^{\eta_u+\eta_v}\chi_{e_u+e_v})/4$. Ignore the common $1/4$ per owner. In incidence coordinates its signature has value 3 at zero, signed unit values at pair selections and zero at odd selections. Realize it by a planar $K_4$: the central spoke to port $i$ has weight equal to the pair value for the other two ports; each opposite triangle edge has reciprocal weight, so the undeleted graph has matching sum 3 and the two-deleted cases give the required signed values. Variable parity gadgets are those of Lemma~\ref{lem:ba16-planar}, with an extra external port for each boundary character. All incidence connectors have weight one. The unnormalized Walsh transform $H_y=\sum_x f(x)(-1)^{y^\top x}$ is a common scalar multiple of the resulting planar matchgate signature, by summing each original spin. The four external ports retain their cyclic order. Theorem 2.1 of Cai--Gorenstein applied to complementary odd strings $1000,0111$ gives
\[
H_{0000}H_{1111}-H_{1100}H_{0011}
+H_{1010}H_{0101}-H_{1001}H_{0110}=0.
\]
For even $y$, $H_y=2\sum_{j=0}^7 a_j(-1)^{(y_2,y_3,y_4)\cdot\operatorname{bin}_3(j)}$. Substitution gives $32(a_0a_5+a_3a_6-a_1a_4-a_2a_7)=0$. An exact crossover has only $a_0,a_5>0$, regardless of multiplicities of extensions, impossible. Fixed literal gauge changes on terminals preserve planarity and recover this same obstruction for signed crossings. No claim is made about an additional nonplanar affine guard, unary field, arbitrary B4/E4 native predicates, or nonlocal decoding.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exponential flat planar cover and raw exception-rank obstruction]\label{lem:ba16-cover}
For $k$ disjoint alternating four-terminal crossing blocks on one disk boundary, an exact union of projected unpinned signed planar NAE supports, each contained in the independent crossing relation, requires at least $2^k$ nonempty pieces, even with arbitrary private variables and differing planar graphs. Separately, for every genuine positive linear four-perfect-partition source on $n\ge3$ vertices, any removal of pair interactions making the unchanged primal graph planar has exceptional parity rank at least $(n+6)/8$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Marginalize all other boundary coordinates of any nonempty piece. The four chosen ports remain in cyclic order, the projected counts are nonnegative and complement-symmetric, and the matchgate identity holds. Since every projected word obeys crossing, either $a_0=0$ or $a_5=0$. Thus each piece fixes the relative bit of the two independent wires at every block, and contains at most $2^k$ of the $4^k$ target words. Counting gives the lower bound. This is a flat support-cover bound, not a decision or factored-sharing bound; a factorized equality registry is trivial to solve. Arbitrary nonlocal guards can encode crossings themselves; their low-rank cover complexity remains an open question.

For the second assertion, every vertex occurs four times and every pair occurs at most once, so the primal graph is simple 8-regular with $4n$ edges. A planar simple subgraph has at most $3n-6$ edges. Thus $|E_1|\ge n+6$. The binary incidence rank of $E_1$ is $\sum_C(|C|-1)$ over nonisolated connected components. Each has at most $4|C|\le8(|C|-1)$ edges, giving $|E_1|\le8\operatorname{rank}(E_1)$. An absolute seed pin retained as a guard does not change the raw edge graph or this bound. Consequently no raw exception-rank choice can dispatch Lemma~\ref{lem:ba16-planar} with a fixed logarithmic cap on the entire growing S21 class. The argument does not apply after a genuinely nonlocal source rewrite; in particular the known GD22 seam bypass solves BS15. It is not an unrestricted runtime lower bound.
\end{proof}
