## Model, theorem and proof
For every explicitly encoded 3-CNF, compile the integer count of violated clauses into sparse Walsh form E (at most eight masks per clause before merging, denominator at most eight), with 0 <= E <= m. The NAE replay uses 4E instead. On an authenticated guard, Gaussian parameterization preserves the range. Each exact existential minimum preserves integrality and this bound. For an actual nonzero direction q, choose a linear section of the quotient by q and write E(y,z)=A(y)+(-1)^z B(y). Then min_z E=A-|B|, with witness z=1 iff B>0 (either if zero). A coordinate section is sufficient; arbitrary directions also admit a polynomial linear basis construction.

For a fixed K, we can recognize whether |2B| has at most K distinct values without enumerating the 2^d assignments, regardless of affine rank or coefficient dominance. Put X=(2B)^2 and let m_j=uniform mean X^j, obtained exactly from the constant Walsh coefficient. Sparse XOR convolution computes the powers through X^K; m_{i+j}=<X^i,X^j> by character orthogonality. For r=1,...,K, solve H_r c=-(m_r,...,m_{2r-1}), H_r=(m_{i+j})_{i,j<r}. For r no greater than the number of actual distinct X values, H_r is positive definite (a nonzero polynomial of degree <r cannot vanish at all of those distinct points). The monic P(X)=X^r+sum c_i X^i vanishes everywhere exactly when the rational nonnegative quantity E_uniform[P(X)^2] is zero. This is an exact identity check, not probabilistic testing or semantic equivalence search. Stop at the first such r; if none, decline. All values of |2B| are integers in [0,M], with M the original polynomial violation bound. Enumerate j=0,...,M and retain j^2 that are roots of P; exactly r distinct roots are obtained. Lagrange interpolation of sqrt on those roots yields degree <=r-1 polynomial Q satisfying Q(X)=|2B| pointwise. The next energy is A-Q(X)/2. The integer energy range invariant and bounded rational coefficient size follow because each normalized Fourier coefficient of an integer-valued function on d Boolean coordinates is an integer multiple of 2^-d with absolute value <=M. For B numerator representation with denominator D, use X=(2B_num)^2, roots (jD)^2, interpolation values jD, then divide by 2D. The supplied checker uses this scaled form.

## Strict separation from rank/fanout rules
Take k address bits a, s=2^k independent data bits d_i, and B=s(-1)^{d_a}. Its Walsh expansion is sum_{i,u in {0,1}^k} (-1)^{i dot u} chi_{u(a)+d_i}; all s^2 coefficients have magnitude one. In E=s+chi_z B the z-direction toggles s^2 characters, no dominant coefficient for s>=2, and the affine difference rank of the masks is s-1+k. Nevertheless X is constant, and min_z E=0. Add one to obtain a positive-energy opposite-label control. This is an explicit sparse-energy family, not a claim that a strict physical E4/B4 adapter preserving this particular sparse representation exists. Address functions have established quadratic sparsity/dimension separation (Sanyal 2019); here it is used to separate exact min-plus rules.

## Falsifiers and obstruction
Falsifiers sought: false moment identity, missing root, false lift, over-cap acquisition, and an exponentially large exact quotient. For even d, set B=sum_{i=1}^d chi_i on independent characters, E=d+chi_z B. B has d/2+1 absolute magnitudes, so fixed K is insufficient in this direction. Moreover |B| has exactly 2^(d-1) Walsh terms: odd coefficients vanish by global complement; constant is positive. For any even nonempty S of size l, use |v|=(2/pi) integral_0^infinity (1-cos(tv))/t^2 dt, and independence of the spins. Its coefficient is -(2/pi)(-1)^(l/2) integral sin(t)^l cos(t)^(d-l)/t^2 dt, which is finite, strictly positive in absolute value since d,l even. Thus a flat explicit Walsh quotient in this chosen direction grows exponentially even though the input has d+1 terms. This instance is easy by the zero-offset preferred parity test, so no universal hardness, no all-order obstruction, no claim P!=NP. Factored expressions and other directions remain legal. The exact open gap is efficient nonlocal normalization or factored high-magnitude elimination with bounded total consumer cost and a universal discovery theorem.
