## C. Direct polynomial discovery, without scanning subsets (PROVED and implemented)
Start with any independent partial transversal of projected rows, at most one row per owner. In a round, form an echelon basis of selected rows, carrying owner coefficient masks. BFS starts at every unmatched owner. From reached owner i inspect all its actual projected equation rows. If a row is outside the span of selected rows, terminate at an augmenting endpoint. Otherwise add every owner having a nonzero coefficient in its unique expansion. Record the actual row that led to each newly reached owner.
For a shortest owner path i0,...,il to a free row, choose rows v0,...,v_l, where v_j has nonzero coefficient on old selected row i_(j+1), and no coefficient on any later path owner (otherwise a shorter BFS path exists); v_l is outside the old span. Replacing the selected rows of i1,...,il with the path rows and adding i0 preserves independence: the coefficient matrix of v0,...,v_(l-1) on the removed basis rows is triangular with diagonal ones, and v_l adds an independent direction. At most m augmentations. In the final no-augment round the reached owners C' have every row in the span of selected rows belonging to C'. All unmatched owners belong to C'. Thus r(C') equals the selected count in C', and D(C') equals the total unmatched count. For any J, D(J) <= number unmatched in J <= total unmatched, because selected rows of J are independent. Hence C' maximizes D and the transversal is maximum. For every maximizing J, equality forces all unmatched owners into J and all its rows to be in the span of selected rows of J. By BFS induction J includes C'. Thus C'=C independent of choices. Moreover the final selected rows outside C are independent modulo the core span directly, so no second matroid intersection is required.
