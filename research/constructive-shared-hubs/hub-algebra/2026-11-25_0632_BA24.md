## A. PROVED short-circuit classification and basis rigidity
For a linear three-uniform signed NAE hypergraph, introduce t_i=sum_{v in e_i} s_iv x_v on real source spins x_v, with all source and auxiliary coordinates required to be signs. The coordinate row registry in Q^n consists of source unit vectors and signed triple rows. Every subset of at most three distinct rows is independent: for one triple and <=2 units, some triple coordinate is uncovered; for two triples and <=1 unit, each triple has >=2 private coordinates; for three triples each has >=1 private coordinate. Units alone are independent. A four-row circuit is either one triple and its three source units or four triple rows forming a Pasch configuration (six variables each used exactly twice). Indeed for two triples each has >=2 private coordinates, so two units cannot cover all; for three triples each has >=1 private coordinate, so one unit cannot cover all. For four triples, each variable must be repeated, and linearity permits at most one shared variable per pair, so each triple meets all three others in distinct vertices. In a dependence, each shared coordinate forces opposite signed coefficients of equal magnitude; propagate around K4 and test consistency. When consistent all four coefficients are ±1 up to overall scale. In the monotone case coefficients would have to alternate on K4, impossible. This is over Q, not F2.
For any actual-coordinate basis, each nonbasis row has support at least three. Support exactly three gives a four-circuit with coefficients ±1, hence its sign feasibility on the basis is a signed NAE constraint, never an affine sign relation. If there are no signed-dependent Pasches and every source vertex has degree >=2, the source basis is the unique actual-coordinate basis in which every nonbasis row has support <=3. Proof: each omitted row is assigned its fundamental four-circuit, an original owner; distinct omitted rows cannot have the same circuit because all other circuit rows must lie in the basis. There are m omissions and m owners, so each owner is assigned once and has exactly one omitted coordinate. Any omitted source coordinate touches at least two owners, which would both have to be assigned to that coordinate, contradiction. In general if k sources are omitted and h owners meet them, >=h-k nonbasis auxiliary rows cannot use their original circuit; in the absence of signed-dependent Pasches those rows have support >=4. For min degree 4 this gives >=ceil(k/3). This covers DO01 high-girth cores (critical min degree >=2) when unguarded, but is no barrier to arbitrary latent basis, guarded quotients, other nonlinear consumers or general SAT. Positive linear degree-four NAE is NP-complete (Darmann et al. arXiv:2412.03395), and the all-sparse actual basis is rigid on that raw domain. No unrestricted lower bound.
