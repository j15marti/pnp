## Construction and exact decision equivalence

Use symmetric C on Z5: all permutations of 012,123,234,340,401, with three distinct variable names per clause and all initial domains full. Ordered R accepts when its first color beats one other and loses to the other in the tournament i beating i+1,i+3 modulo five. R has 40 ordered tuples, C has 30. Every emitted constraint here is C.

Declare k Boolean inputs x_0 through x_(k−1). The following gate order is part of the encoding. Each gate has a fresh output, even a gate whose result is unused. Number wires starting with the inputs in increasing order.

1. Make T=x_0 OR (NOT x_0), emitting NOT first.
2. For each bit d of A, least significant first, emit l=NOT T, r=NOT l. Emit s=NOT l, h=NOT r if d=1 and s=NOT r, h=NOT l if d=0. Keep s as the selector. Both l and r have the same number of uses for either setting.
3. For each i emit a single wire U_i=s_i OR x_i, in increasing index order.
4. Generate the B selectors by the same four-gate rule.
5. For each i form V_i=U_i AND B_i by two NOTs on the operands, their OR, and a final NOT. OR-fold the V_i from left to right with k−1 gates. Output the negation of this fold.

Apply the accepting arm of the balanced compiler [3]. For reproducibility, its complete clause rules are these. Append q0=NOT out, q1=NOT q0, q2=NOT q1. Number the five anchors first, then the circuit wires in gate order, then fresh helpers in order of generation. Emit the five consecutive cyclic C triples on the anchors. For every wire X in order and j=2,3,4 emit C(a_j,X,h), C(a_(j+2),a_(j+3),h), with fresh h and anchor indices modulo five. Then for every NOT P with output X emit C(a2,P,X). For each OR(P,Q) with output X allocate fresh Y,Z and emit C(a1,a2,Y), C(a0,a1,Z), C(a4,P,Y), C(a3,Q,Z), C(X,Y,Z). Pin with C(a0,a2,q1).

Replace every clause slot by a distinct occurrence copy in scan order and emit all those core triples first. In increasing unsplit variable order, link consecutive copies u,v of that variable in occurrence order. Allocate four private names per link. With (r0,...,r4)=(u,f1,f2,f3,f4), emit its five consecutive cyclic triples followed by C(r4,v,r1). Allocate private IDs consecutively after all copies. Encode the resulting ordered list using the exact header and IDs below. The executable exposes image(k,A,B) [4].

The selectors compute their encoded bits. Thus each coordinate requires (A_i OR x_i) AND B_i to vanish. If A and B intersect, that condition fails at an intersection for every x. Conversely, for disjoint A,B it holds precisely when x_i=0 at every set bit of B, giving exactly 2^(k−|B|) assignments. The all-zero x supplies one witness in that case. Composition uses the proved fibers of [3]. In a chosen dihedral anchor frame the domain and gate helpers extend each Boolean evaluation uniquely. There are ten frames. A private equality link has two extensions for equal ports and none for unequal ports, and its private names occur nowhere else. Hence every satisfying split assignment projects to a satisfying x and each such x has exactly 10·2^E extensions. These statements prove both directions and give the count. All names occur. ## Compulsory transport on the same execution

Fix one uniform deterministic total machine M with Q≥2 control states and a fixed finite alphabet. One physical read/write head starts at the left origin, moves at most one cell per transition and carries across a physical boundary only its finite control. Input, work, and final canonical binary output with terminator occupy this tape. No free clock, side channel or independent input head. M is correct on every valid encoded strict-C instance, including all hybrids. The following proof works separately for decision and for counting. In counting the single zero digit differs from the leading one of every positive canonical count. For decision use the fixed left-origin answer bit.

Let B be uniform on k-bit masks of weight floor(k/2) and A be its complement. Every such input is SAT, has the same target count, and has 2^ceil(k/2) original Boolean models. Put P=binom(k,floor(k/2))≥2^k/(k+1). For distinct B,B' of this weight, B' has a bit outside B. Thus I(complement B,B') is UNSAT. This supplies a fooling set for the real decision problem even on the valid compiler image; positive source-model growth comes on the very distribution used in the lower bound.

Fix one selected boundary. Record the complete sequence of states just after head crossings, through termination. Equal sequences for two distinct diagonal inputs synchronize every excursion. The head starts on the left, and the alternating direction is determined by index. On the hybrid take left excursions from the first run and right excursions from the second. Induction over excursions gives exactly those local tape histories because only a state crosses, even when the machine has rewritten the entire region. Equal sequence lengths make the final side agree. After the last crossing, the local halting continuation on that side is the corresponding original one. The hybrid has the first run's final left-hand digit, hence says positive, in conflict with its correct zero or UNSAT answer. Consequently the P transcripts are distinct. This is the one-tape splicing method of Hennie's Theorem 1 [5], with the answer and layout proved here.

For d=floor(log_Q(P)/2), there are at most Q^d state strings of length less than d. Thus at each cut the mean crossing length on this common distribution is at least d(1−P^(-1/2)). This avoids any prefix-code assumption. For fixed Q it is Ω_M(k). One transition crosses at most one of the L distinct boundaries. Summing expectations on the *same runs* yields
    E_B time_M(I(complement B,B)) ≥ 72kw · d(1−P^(-1/2)) = Ω_M(N²/log N).
For every fixed M and sufficiently large k, at least one such SAT input attains the mean bound. Neither a common hard input for all solvers nor repeated post-relocation service at one cut is needed. Constants depend on M. The input distribution is uniform on fixed-weight complements only for measuring cost; the correctness domain contains off-diagonal inputs as well.
