\begin{abstract}
We derive an explicit $q^2$-seed $\varepsilon$-biased distribution from the field trace $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_q/\mathbb F_2}(az^i)$, use it to deterministically decide every one-sided-error polynomial-time verifier with a known polynomial bound on the complete random-tape acceptance function's Fourier spectral norm, and prove the hereditary version that constructs an accepting tape. A polynomial-time, width-two quadratic-phase verifier has exponentially large spectral norm, decisively blocking extrapolation to arbitrary $RP$. The primitives originate in the established small-bias literature; the contribution here is a fully charged project-specific transfer audit and falsifier, not an unconditional new complexity-class equality. Both $P=RP$ and $P\ne RP$ remain open, as does $P$ versus $NP$.
\end{abstract}

\section{A fully explicit restricted derandomizer}
Fix a uniform ordinary $RP$ verifier $R(x,r)$ with $m(n)\le\operatorname{poly}(n)$ independent fair coin bits, evaluation time $T_R(n)$ and workspace $S_R(n)$. On NO inputs $R(x,r)=0$ for all $r$; on YES inputs $\mathbb E_r R(x,r)\ge1/2$. Let
\[
\widehat f_x(S)=2^{-m}\sum_{r\in\{0,1\}^m}f_x(r)(-1)^{\oplus_{i\in S}r_i},\qquad
\|f_x\|_A=\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}|\widehat f_x(S)|.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Restricted TRACE-FOURIER4]
Suppose there is a fixed, explicitly computable polynomial $B(n)\ge1$ satisfying $\|f_x\|_A\le B(n)$ for every input $x$. Then the language of $R$ belongs to deterministic $P$ by a uniform, all-input, both-labels-correct algorithm. It uses no advice, oracle or cryptographic assumption.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $q=2^k\ge8mB$ be the least power of two with this property, and construct $\mathbb F_q$ by searching for an irreducible binary degree-$k$ polynomial (at most $q$ candidates and $\operatorname{poly}(k)$ bit operations per candidate). For uniform $a,z\in\mathbb F_q$, form a length-$m$ tape
\[
H(a,z)_i=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb F_q/\mathbb F_2}(az^i),\qquad 0\le i<m.
\]
For nonempty $S\subseteq[m]$, $P_S(Z)=\sum_{i\in S}Z^i$ is nonzero of degree at most $m-1$. Since $a\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(aP_S(z))$ is balanced when $P_S(z)\ne0$, the parity character bias is exactly
\[
\mathbb E_{a,z}(-1)^{\oplus_{i\in S}H(a,z)_i}=\Pr_z[P_S(z)=0]\le(m-1)/q.
\]
Consequently, for any $f$,
\[
|\mathbb E_{a,z} f(H(a,z))-\mathbb E_{r}f(r)|
\le\frac{m-1}{q}\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}|\widehat f(S)|\le\frac18.
\]
Enumerate all $q^2$ pairs and accept iff at least one $R(x,H(a,z))$ accepts. Every NO input rejects everywhere; on each YES input at least $3/8$ of pairs accept, so one exists. As $q=O(mB)$, construction and enumeration are polynomial. More explicitly, total time is bounded by
$O(q\operatorname{poly}(k)+q^2[m\operatorname{poly}(k)+T_R(n)+n])$,
and workspace by $O(m+S_R(n)+n+\operatorname{poly}(k))$. The $m=0$ case simply evaluates $R$.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Hereditary Fourier mass and constructive accepting tape]
For every partial assignment $u$ of any subset of the coin coordinates,
$\|f\upharpoonright_u\|_A\le\|f\|_A$. A deterministic accepting tape can be extracted on YES inputs in polynomial time under the same polynomial bound $B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
After fixing coordinate $i$ to $b$, coefficients $\widehat f(T)$ and $(-1)^b\widehat f(T\cup\{i\})$ merge; the triangle inequality bounds their absolute sum by the previous norm. Iterate. At each of $m$ levels, use the trace distribution of length $\ell$ with $q\ge8m^2B$ to estimate both suffix conditional means to additive error at most $\delta=1/(8m)$. Choose the prefix child with larger estimate. Its true mean exceeds the parent's mean minus $2\delta$. The final mean is at least $1/2-2m\delta=1/4>0$, forcing the final Boolean value to be one. At most $2mq^2$ charged executions of $R$ are needed.
\end{proof}
\section{A decisive failed unrestricted implication}
\begin{proposition}[Quadratic-phase spectral explosion]
For even $m=2h$, let $Q_m(r)=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{h-1}r_{2j}r_{2j+1}$ and $f_m(r)=1-Q_m(r)$ as a Boolean value. Then
\[
\Pr[f_m=1]=\tfrac12+2^{-h-1},\qquad
\|f_m\|_A=\tfrac12+2^{h-1}.
\]
The verifier $R(x,r)=[\operatorname{parity}(x)=1]f_m(r)$ has two-sided input labels with valid one-sided RP error, executes in linear time with width two at pair boundaries and width at most four on individual bit reads, and recognizes a deterministic linear-time language.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each factor $(-1)^{uv}$ has four Walsh coefficients of magnitude $1/2$, and expectation $1/2$. Their tensor product has all $2^m$ coefficients of magnitude $2^{-h}$ and mean $2^{-h}$. Since $f_m=(1+(-1)^{Q_m})/2$, its nonempty Fourier coefficients have magnitude $2^{-h-1}$ while its constant coefficient is $1/2+2^{-h-1}$. Summing yields $1/2+2^{h-1}$. The verifier maintains only the running XOR of successive pairwise ANDs. Input parity supplies a direct deterministic bypass.
\end{proof}
A complementary strict-extension example is $OR_m$, whose $\mathbb F_2$ algebraic degree is $m$ while its Fourier $L_1$ norm is $2-2^{1-m}<2$. Thus the Fourier criterion covers some examples excluded by the previously proven fixed-degree criterion. Conversely, the quadratic-phase function is covered by degree two and excluded by any fixed polynomial spectral bound.
