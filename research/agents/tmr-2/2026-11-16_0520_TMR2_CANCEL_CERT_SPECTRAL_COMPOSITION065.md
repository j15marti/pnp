## Theorem 16: composed source-to-count algorithm (PROVED/EXACT restricted)

Process authentic gates in discovered topological order. Every E leaf maps to its a-by-b GF(2) unit matrix; every early X gate maps to the XOR of its two true predecessor matrices, preserving repeated-child cancellation. Discover a Gaussian basis B_1,...,B_d of the span of all actual early gate matrices and explicit coordinate masks c_t satisfying A_t=sum_j c_(t,j) B_j. The basis rows are physically constructed, not oracle access.

Introduce *formal* coordinates w_1,...,w_d, treated as degree zero in the original z variables but **not** as independent original inputs. Represent each genuine source gate by a parity bitmap over one constant, original-input atoms I(z_i), formal W(w_j) atoms, and interned AND-of-two-parity-bitmap atoms. For early gate t, its vector is XOR_(j:c_tj=1) W_j. For late P and N use bitmap XOR and constant toggle; for A apply interned AND; for O use U XOR V XOR AND(U,V). Apply only exact Boolean identities 0 AND U=0, 1 AND U=U, U AND U=U and U AND(1 XOR U)=0. Ordered operand bitmap keys are compared as explicit finite strings; no semantic-equivalence oracle or exponential expansion is used.

Give I atoms raw-degree one, W atoms raw-degree zero, and each genuine nontrivial AND atom the capped upper bound min(3,degree(U)+degree(V)). Degree of a bitmap is maximum of degrees of its present atom identities (constant degree zero). Test only the *actual normalized root* after XOR cancellation. If its bound exceeds two, return OUTSIDE. Otherwise mark its exact transitive atom dependencies; each surviving atom has bound <=2 by nonnegativity of construction bounds. For every formal word u in GF(2)^d, substitute W_j=u_j, evaluate this surviving atom DAG with packed square-free coefficients in the Boolean quotient ring R_n=GF(2)[z]/(z_i^2-z_i), and obtain a quadratic p_u(z). Source induction over arbitrary z and formal u proves the pointwise identity F(z)=p_(w(z))(z), where w_j(z)=z_left^T B_j z_right. Formal independent-coordinate reasoning is safe because it proves an identity for *every* u; one then specializes to the actual correlated u=w(z).

For lambda in GF(2)^d set Q_(u,lambda)(z)=p_u(z)+sum_j lambda_j w_j(z), an actual quadratic polynomial of n original bits, and H(Q)=sum_z(-1)^Q(z) as an exact integer. Quadratic character elimination is source-independent: if Q=z_i z_j+z_i A(t)+z_j B(t)+R(t), direct summation over the pair equals 2(-1)^(R(t)+A(t)B(t)). This reduces to a residual affine character in <=floor(n/2) iterations. Indicator orthogonality, valid on every original assignment regardless of unattainable u, yields:

    #ONE = [2^(n+d) - SUM_(u,lambda) (-1)^(lambda.u) H(Q_(u,lambda))] / 2^(d+1).

The integer division is exact. Proof: 1[w(z)=u]=2^(-d) SUM_lambda (-1)^(lambda.(u+w(z))). Substitute this into sum_z(-1)^F(z), exchange finite sums, then use #ONE=(2^n-signed_gap)/2. Every original z is counted exactly once. There are no free wire extensions and no unproven reduction to arbitrary SAT.

This is a strictly richer authenticated domain than /064's early-free case, and admits high-degree canceled internal shared gates disallowed by /063's per-source-gate relative-degree certificate. It **does not** recognize every true low-semantic-degree output; /064's coNP-completeness barrier explains why no unconditional polynomial-time complete recognizer is being assumed.
