## The two-arc certificate

Fix \(\Sigma=\mathbb Z_5\) and \(R(a,b)\Longleftrightarrow b-a\bmod 5\in\{1,3\}\). For any four-ary polymorphism \(f:T_5^4\to T_5\), each componentwise power arc \(u\to v\) must imply \(R(f(u),f(v))\).

A four-ary Siggers operation obeys
\[
 f(x,y,x,z)=f(y,x,z,y)\qquad(\forall x,y,z\in\mathbb Z_5).
\]
Consider the following **three original 4-tuples**:
\[
 s=(0,1,0,2),\qquad p=(3,2,1,0),\qquad t=(1,0,2,1).
\]
The two arcs \(s\to p\) and \(p\to t\) really belong to \(T_5^4\), since their four componentwise differences are respectively
\[
 p-s\bmod 5=(3,1,1,3),\qquad
 t-p\bmod 5=(3,3,1,1),
\]
and every entry is 1 or 3. By the Siggers identity with \(x=0,y=1,z=2\), we have \(f(s)=f(t)\). Preserving both original power arcs would force
\[
 R(f(s),f(p))\land R(f(p),f(t))
 =R(f(s),f(p))\land R(f(p),f(s)).
\]
This is impossible: \(T_5\) is an irreflexive tournament with no two-cycle. Therefore **no Siggers polymorphism of arity four exists**, including non-idempotent operations. There are no continuity, symmetry, computational model or large-input assumptions.

## Minimality of the proof certificate

One genuine product arc cannot directly join a Siggers-matched pair \((x,y,x,z)\) and \((y,x,z,y)\) in either direction. Such an arc would require both \(R(x,y)\) and \(R(y,x)\) from its first two coordinates; when \(x=y\), these components would instead require loops. Thus **two is the minimum possible number of power arcs** in an obstruction that consists of a directed path between a single Siggers-identified pair. The exhibited path uses three distinct product vertices, two product arcs and one Siggers-identity substitution. This is a restricted minimality theorem for this certificate form, not a claim that every possible algebraic proof needs three vertices.

The quotient of the fourth power by the Siggers identities contains a directed digon: the two ends of the exhibited path are identified. This provides a concrete, source-checkable explanation for the absence of a homomorphism from the quotient to \(T_5\). Unlike static adjacency-matrix rank, it is a homomorphism-preservation contradiction tied to **two real source transitions**.
