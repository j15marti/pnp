\section*{Exact certificate-encoding lemma}
For a CNF with $m$ original clauses on $n$ variables, every binary Resolution refutation DAG with $q$ paid derived nodes has a source-authenticated proof certificate consisting solely of $q$ triples $(a_i,b_i,p_i)$: indices of previously available antecedents, and a nonzero signed pivot. It need not write out the derived clauses. Each triple costs at most $2\lceil\log_2(m+q+1)\rceil+\lceil\log_2(2n+1)\rceil+O(1)$ bits. A deterministic checker reconstructs each resolvent, confirms complementary pivot occurrences and previous antecedents, and accepts only if the final reconstructed clause is empty. Thus the complete output-omitting proof uses $O(q\log(m+n+q))$ bits plus the original source. Reconstructing and retaining all lines takes polynomial work and space in $N+q$; this does NOT imply that the checker runs in $O(q)$ native steps or that $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ admits the same compact encoding for every non-Resolution rule.
