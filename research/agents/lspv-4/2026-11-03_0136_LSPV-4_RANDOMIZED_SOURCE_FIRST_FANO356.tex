\section*{Randomized delayed-index model and theorem}
A randomized machine reads the canonical source for $F_X$ in a single pass, then permanently loses source access. Only afterward it receives $I\in[L]$ and returns a guess for $X_I$. Here $X$ is uniform on $\{0,1\}^L$ and $I$ is uniform independent; public randomness $R$ is independent. $M$ is the entire retained post-source configuration with at most $S$ bits, including writable memory, head/index state and control except public coins. The joint average error is at most $\epsilon\le 1/2$. A worst-case error guarantee implies this average condition. No source rereads are allowed.
\begin{theorem}
For every $L\ge1$, $S\ge L(1-H_2(\epsilon))$, where $H_2(e)=-e\log_2e-(1-e)\log_2(1-e)$. In particular, for error at most $1/3$, $S\ge0.0817041659L=\Omega(N/\log N)$. If $c$ finite-control and $h$ other nonwritable post-source configurations are separately free, then $S_{\rm writable}\ge L(1-H_2(\epsilon))-\log_2(ch)$. This is a lower bound on exact/approximate delayed source authentication, NOT on general SAT decision.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each $i$, the optimal estimate of $X_i$ given $(M,R)$ has error no worse than the machine's randomized decoder $e_i$, so binary Fano gives $H(X_i\mid M,R)\le H_2(e_i)$. Conditional entropy subadditivity and binary entropy concavity give $H(X\mid M,R)\le\sum_i H_2(e_i)\le L H_2(\epsilon)$. Uniform independence implies $I(X;M\mid R)=L-H(X\mid M,R)\ge L(1-H_2(\epsilon))$. But $M$ has at most $2^S$ values for each public seed, hence $I(X;M\mid R)\le S$. Fresh private decoder randomness cannot beat Bayes optimal. QED.
\end{proof}
