\begin{theorem}[Strictly stream-only reverse-query cache-recharge]
Let an opaque $M$-bit child be regenerated by a deterministic sequential
emitter whose every pass begins at output index zero.
The consumer requests distinct child positions $M-1,M-2,\ldots,0$.
The only way to learn a previously unknown child bit is to advance this
stream; the simulator retains at most $b$ unconsumed literal bits,
$0\le b<M$, and has no direct arbitrary-bit emitter, compression oracle
or other readable child materialization. Each emitted symbol costs a step.
Then the cumulative emitted-symbol count $R$ satisfies
\[
R\ge
\left\lfloor M/2\right\rfloor
\left\lceil\frac{\lfloor M/2\rfloor}{b+1}\right\rceil
=\Omega(M^2/(b+1))\quad\text{if }b=o(M).
\]
The grouping scheme with $h=\lceil M/(b+1)\rceil$ emits exactly
\[
R_{\rm group}
=hM-\frac{(b+1)h(h-1)}2,
\]
matching the asymptotic order for $b=o(M)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Restrict attention to the upper half of requested indices.
A pass first acquiring any such bit must emit at least
$\lfloor M/2\rfloor$ symbols. Because the consumer requests
indices in descending order, one newly acquired upper-half bit
can be answered directly on the pass. At most $b$ earlier
lower-index bits can be kept for later descending answers.
Thus at most $b+1$ upper-half answers are assigned to one
acquisition pass, so there must be at least
$\lceil\lfloor M/2\rfloor/(b+1)\rceil$ such passes.
Multiplication gives the lower bound. For the upper bound, group
$b+1$ consecutive reverse queries, scan from the start to the
largest needed index, cache at most $b$ earlier bits and answer
the group in descending order. Sum the resulting prefix lengths.
The theorem applies only to the declared opaque stream interface.
\end{proof}
