\section{Exact source-probe transcript lower bound}
Let $\pi\in S_m$ be given as $m$ read-only source words $\pi(1),\ldots,\pi(m)$. One indexed word probe reveals one complete value in $[m]$. A fixed deterministic preprocessor may inspect the source arbitrarily but retain at most $s$ source-dependent bits, including stored input-head positions and other metadata. Then a deterministic online stage receives the fixed query sequence $\pi^{-1}(1),\ldots,\pi^{-1}(t)$, $1\le t\le m$, must emit every correct answer for every $\pi$, and performs at most $Q$ adaptive source-word probes. Its other computation may be arbitrary but no uncharged source-dependent oracle is available.
\begin{theorem}[Restricted information-versus-probe bound]
Every exact such uniform interface obeys
\[
\boxed{2^s m^Q\ge(m)_t=\frac{m!}{(m-t)!}},\qquad
\boxed{s+Q\log_2m\ge\log_2\frac{m!}{(m-t)!}}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are exactly $(m)_t$ different inverse-answer tuples. Fix one of the at most $2^s$ retained preprocessor states. The query stage is a deterministic source-observation decision tree with branching degree at most $m$ and depth at most $Q$. Padding early leaves by a fixed dummy symbol gives at most $m^Q$ observation transcripts and therefore as many different correct output tuples. Summing over all initial states gives at most $2^sm^Q$ outcomes. For exact correctness they must cover all $(m)_t$ possibilities. Adaptive probe positions and arbitrary non-source computation do not change this information count.
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Restricted corollaries.} With $t=m,Q=0$, full inverse lookup after preprocessing requires $s\ge\lceil\log_2(m!)\rceil=\Theta(m\log m)$ bits. An explicit inverse array occupies $m\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil$ bits and takes an $m$-word pass. For a fixed-width original CNF source $N=\Theta(m\log m)$, either full inverse storage costs $\Theta(N)$ counted bits, or a controller that literally rescans \emph{all $N$ source bits for each} of $q=N^{3/5+o(1)}$ children expends $\Omega(N^{8/5-o(1)})$ time. These are counterexamples to those implementation policies, \emph{not} all-algorithm lower bounds. A selective-source-query or structural arithmetic accessor may evade both extremes. If per-child discovery costs $N^{\theta+o(1)}$, the $q$-fold stage has exponent $3/5+\theta$. The originally hypothesized ARC additive stage $11/10$ permits $\theta\le1/2$, while the isolated strict FLMV time slice $c=241/200$ permits only $\theta<121/200$, assuming other time charges are negligible.
