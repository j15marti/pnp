\section{Exact full-state nonreconvergence}
\begin{theorem}[Exact assignment contexts cannot reconverge]\label{th:full}
In a rooted binary directed acyclic proof whose internal node \(v\) is labeled by the \emph{complete} original-variable assignment \(\alpha_v\), suppose each branch picks a variable \(x\notin\operatorname{dom}\alpha_v\), the two outgoing edges extend by \(x=0\) and \(x=1\), and no downstream transition ever removes or changes an assignment. Then no two divergent root-to-node paths can end at the same exact-context node. Every reachable node has at most one reachable parent, so this proof representation offers no nontrivial shared DAG reconstruction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At the first divergence from a shared node, the paths assign the \emph{same} variable \(x\) to opposite values. By the retained-context hypothesis all descendants on each path retain their respective incompatible literal \(x=0\) or \(x=1\). Their complete assignment labels cannot agree. Thus reconvergence contradicts an exact-context label. This proof is independent of branch-selection adaptivity after the divergence and of satisfiability of the source. Context \emph{forgetting}, compressed parameterized state, conditional proof reuse or semantically justified quotienting are outside its hypotheses.
\end{proof}

\section{An explicit proper 3CNF with identical residuals and different origins}
Fix \(k\ge1\). Use selector names \(x_i=i\), and distinct payload names \(a_i=k+i\), \(b_i=2k+i\). In physical canonical DIMACS order write
\[
F_k=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k}\bigl[(x_i\vee a_i\vee b_i)\wedge(\neg x_i\vee a_i\vee b_i)\bigr].\tag{1}
\]
Every source clause has exactly three distinct identifiers, and no two clauses are identical. The family is \emph{ordinary proper signed 3CNF, not exact-$(2,2)$}. For an independent UNSAT control add eight proper source clauses on three fresh names \(y,z,w\), one for every sign triple, denoting the result \(F_k^{\bot}\). All source identifiers have canonical decimal encodings, every source line ends in \texttt{0} and a newline, and the header declares the actual \(n,m\). The physical length satisfies \(N_k=\Theta(k\log(k+2))\) bits for both families.

\begin{theorem}[Residual identity does not bind physical source origins]\label{th:provenance}
For every selector assignment \(\sigma\in\{0,1\}^k\), the fully simplified remaining payload clauses of \(F_k\) are exactly
\[R_k=\bigwedge_{i=1}^k(a_i\vee b_i),\tag{2}\]
independently of \(\sigma\). Nevertheless, if \(p_i(\sigma)\) denotes the \emph{physical source-byte offset} of the unique \(i\)-th original clause not satisfied by \(\sigma\), the map
\[\sigma\longmapsto(p_1(\sigma),\ldots,p_k(\sigma))\tag{3}\]
is injective and has exactly \(2^k\) images. The same claims hold for \(F_k^{\bot}\) after appending its common eight-clause residual. Each authenticated offset for \(\sigma\) points to a clause \emph{satisfied} under the opposite choice at that coordinate.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If \(\sigma_i=0\), \(x_i\vee a_i\vee b_i\) reduces to \(a_i\vee b_i\), while its negated-selector partner is satisfied. If \(\sigma_i=1\), the opposite clause survives, with the same pair of payload literals. Therefore the normalized source formula after the restriction is (2), but the authentically surviving original source clause is different for each selector bit. Two distinct selector vectors disagree at some \(i\), hence their \(i\)-th source byte offsets differ, proving injectivity. The eight independent clauses on \(y,z,w\) are never touched by selector restrictions and are inconsistent by exhaustion of all three-bit assignments, supplying an actual UNSAT control without altering the provenance map.
\end{proof}

\section{Separated replay needs memory, but interleaving avoids it}
Define the fixed-source relation \(\mathcal R_k(\sigma,p)=1\) iff \(p\) equals the physically correct \(k\)-offset vector (3). The \emph{separated forward-only proof-stream interface} presents all \(k\) bits of \(\sigma\) first, then all \(k\) offsets of \(p\), forbidding rewind, a preloaded independently indexed proof tape, or external advice.
\begin{theorem}[Separated provenance one-way state barrier]\label{th:replay}
Every nondeterministic or deterministic exact one-pass recognizer of \(\mathcal R_k\) must have at least \(2^k\) distinct midpoint configurations after the selector prelude. Under the standard bounded-time finite-alphabet fixed-tape model with read-only ordinary SAT source and all writable/index bits counted, this entails \(\Omega(k)\) live bits up to harmless logarithmic head-position accounting.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For every \(\sigma\), choose one accepting computation of the valid pair \((\sigma,p(\sigma))\), and write \(q_\sigma\) for its complete configuration immediately after the \(k\)-bit prelude. If two distinct selector prefixes \(\sigma\ne\tau\) had \(q_\sigma=q_\tau\), splice the accepting continuation that reads \(p(\sigma)\) into the legal prefix run for \(\tau\). The machine would accept \((\tau,p(\sigma))\), which is invalid by injectivity in Theorem~\ref{th:provenance}. This contradiction holds also for nondeterministic choices because the accepting path itself is spliced. There are therefore at least \(2^k\) distinct stored midpoint configurations. This is a \emph{proof-input communication/state argument}, not a SAT decision lower bound. If arbitrary machine time/head positions are treated as free state or the proof stream can rewind, the statement requires extra accounting and is not asserted in those models.
\end{proof}
For \(k=\Theta(N/\log N)\), literal retention of \(k\) bits exceeds the space exponent \(\beta(241/200)=11642479/23232400\) at the selected FLMV point. The exact exponent gap for the \(N^{1-o(1)}\) storage policy is
\[1-\beta(241/200)=\frac{11589921}{23232400}>0.\tag{4}\]
This is \emph{not} an instance of FLMV because the separated verifier is not a uniform exact all-input deterministic SAT decider.

\begin{theorem}[Positive countercontrol: interleaved source-order verification]\label{th:interleave}
The same restricted relation can be checked, with a source-order physical scan, using \(O(\log N)\) combined live writable/index bits and \(N\operatorname{polylog}N\) bit time if proof records are presented as interleaved pairs \((\sigma_i,p_i)\) and the source retains the canonical paired-clause order of (1). This is a different interface than Theorem~\ref{th:replay}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Maintain the current byte pointer, the current ordinal \(i\), the length of the two adjacent canonical source lines, and one selector bit. Check both expected source literals against their actual physical source words, compute the offset of the clause unsatisfied by this bit, compare to the immediately following offset proof record, and discard the bit/offset. Every counter, literal identifier and physical offset fits \(O(\log N)\) bits. A single forward pass suffices; constant many source line parses per record plus the ordinary source syntax scan give the stated cost. The eight optional UNSAT control clauses are parsed as an additional fixed suffix. No need exists to remember all \(k\) selector bits at once.
\end{proof}
