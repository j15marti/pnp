\section{Target, falsifiers, and resource model}
The target is a uniform all-input exact SAT time-space successor in the deterministic read-only indexed-input random-access Turing model of Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas (FLMV). An achievable pair $(t,s)$ requires \emph{one fixed machine} that is correct on every ordinarily encoded input and simultaneously uses $N^{t+o(1)}$ time and $N^{s+o(1)}$ counted work/index space. The revised existential cofinal finite-route target is weaker than a fixed compiler for every implementation, but remains OPEN. We specifically falsify the inference that exact counts of many source-linked small partial projections suffice for an all-input favorable transition.

Three prespecified mechanisms were tested.
\begin{description}
\item[(M1, FALSIFIED/RETIRED for the declared oracle.)] Adaptive exact model counts of every proper primary-variable pin projection identify the parent label.
\item[(M2, PROVED/EXACT restricted.)] If those projection counts convey no orientation information, a total exact deterministic controller still must acquire every independently label-sensitive source orientation group.
\item[(M3, FALSIFIED/RETIRED as ARC amplification.)] This acquired-bit requirement forces post-acquisition superlinear work or an ordinary child whose size is a strict power of the parent length.
\end{description}
The oracle discussed here is deliberately stronger than one-bit SAT-status access, but weaker than a witness-returning or full-pin oracle. Neither it nor its replies are free primitives of an ordinary SAT machine.

\section{Uniform fixed-width proper-3CNF construction}
For every integer $q\ge1$ and $e=(e_1,\ldots,e_q)\in\{0,1\}^q$, introduce original primary variables $x_1,\ldots,x_q$, functional parity-prefix variables $z_1,\ldots,z_q$, and $2(q+1)$ free pin-gadget variables $(u_j,v_j)$, $0\le j\le q$. All variable identifiers are distinct and fixed independently of $e$. Put
\[
 t_q=1\oplus(q\bmod 2).
\]
The base formula $B_{q,e}$ explicitly imposes
\[
 z_1=x_1\oplus e_1,\qquad
 z_i=z_{i-1}\oplus x_i\oplus e_i\quad(2\le i\le q),
 \qquad z_q=t_q.
\]
The first equation uses exactly two proper binary clauses, choosing the equality pair when $e_1=0$ and the disequality pair when $e_1=1$. Each later XOR3 equation is encoded by four proper ternary clauses, namely one clause falsified by each of its four forbidden truth rows. For distinct $y,u,v$ define
\[
\operatorname{Pin}(y=b;u,v)=
(\ell_b\vee u\vee v)\wedge(\ell_b\vee u\vee\neg v)
\wedge(\ell_b\vee\neg u\vee v)\wedge(\ell_b\vee\neg u\vee\neg v),
\quad
\ell_b=\begin{cases}y&b=1,\\\neg y&b=0.\end{cases}
\]
It forces $y=b$ while leaving $u,v$ arbitrary. The base includes $\operatorname{Pin}(z_q=t_q;u_0,v_0)$. Define the full parent
\[
 P_{q,e}=B_{q,e}\wedge
 \bigwedge_{i=1}^q\operatorname{Pin}(x_i=1;u_i,v_i).
\]
There are exactly $n=4q+2$ declared variables, $m=8q+2$ clauses (two binary and $8q$ proper ternaries), $q$ functional and $2(q+1)$ free auxiliaries. Distinct clause occurrences are retained. Encode the two header fields $(n,m)$ and each clause as four fixed-width records $(\mathrm{arity},\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)$, padding a binary clause's third literal record with zero. With signed identifier codes and
\[
 w(q)=\left\lceil\log_2[64(12q+5)]\right\rceil,
 \qquad N(q)=(32q+10)w(q)=\Theta(q\log(q+2)),
\]
every $e$ gives the same encoded length and identifiers. Flipping $e_i$ changes signed-literal fields only in its own disjoint XOR-record group. Fixed-width group addressing and index-tape work are charged. A machine with words wider than the declared width requires an explicitly adjusted packing ledger.

\section{The exact full-count and marginal-count identities}
For $S\subseteq[q]$, $a\in\{0,1\}^{S}$, permit the ordinary child
\[
 C_{q,e}(S,a)=B_{q,e}\wedge\bigwedge_{i\in S}
 \operatorname{Pin}(x_i=a_i;u_i,v_i).
\]
Unused $u_i,v_i$ remain declared, free variables, not deleted from the universe. For all-positive $a$, these are literal pin-block deletions from $P_{q,e}$; allowing arbitrary $a$ strengthens the projection oracle.

\begin{theorem}[Exact count profile; PROVED/EXACT]\label{thm:counts}
For every $q\ge1$, every $e$, every $S$, and every $a$,
\[
\# C_{q,e}(S,a)=
\begin{cases}
4^{q+1}2^{q-|S|-1},& |S|<q,\\[2pt]
4^{q+1}\mathbf1\left[
 \bigoplus_{i=1}^q a_i =
 1\oplus(q\bmod2)\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^q e_i
 \right],& S=[q].
\end{cases}
\]
In particular $\SAT(P_{q,e})$ holds exactly when $\bigoplus_i e_i=1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The XOR equations determine a unique sequence $z_1,\ldots,z_q$ for every $x$, and their last equality is equivalent to
$\bigoplus_i x_i=1\oplus(q\bmod2)\oplus\bigoplus_i e_i$.
Every such primary solution admits exactly $4^{q+1}$ completions of the pin helpers, because a pin gadget never restricts its own two helpers even when its primary is fixed; unused helpers are equally free. If at least one primary variable remains unpinned, exactly half of the $2^{q-|S|}$ free-primary choices meet the parity equation. If none remains, the single pinned word meets the equation or fails it. On the full parent $a_i=1$ for every $i$, so $\oplus_i a_i=q\bmod2$, giving precisely $\oplus_i e_i=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Unlimited adaptive marginal-count no-go; PROVED/EXACT restricted]\label{thm:oracle}
Consider any total deterministic controller promised a canonical $P_{q,e}$ and allowed arbitrary adaptive queries $\#C_{q,e}(S,a)$ with $S\subsetneq[q]$, optionally interspersed with paid reads of signed source fields. Its oracle-answer transcript alone is independent of $e$. On $e=0^q$, an exact decision controller must read at least one signed-literal bit in each of the $q$ disjoint orientation groups. Thus the source-field lower bound is $q=\Omega(N/\log N)$, with no nontrivial lower bound after these groups are acquired.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:counts} makes every allowed oracle response a function only of $q$ and $|S|$, even when $S$ and $a$ are selected from the entire prior transcript. Suppose a deterministic controller never reads orientation group $i$ on $e=0^q$. Flipping $e_i$ changes no previously read bit and changes no available model-count answer, so the complete adaptive execution and its output are identical. Yet the parent label flips from UNSAT to SAT. Contradiction. Apply the argument independently to each $i$.
\end{proof}
This is an information-acquisition theorem for a specially restricted interface, not a general post-acquisition complexity theorem. If $S=[q]$ is allowed, Theorem~\ref{thm:counts} makes the parent label directly accessible. Even for $|S|=q-1$, a satisfying \emph{witness}, unlike its count, leaks the remaining source parity bit. These are essential oracle-type countercontrols.
