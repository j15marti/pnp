\section*{Theorem and proof}
Consider the indexed random-access multitape machine in FLMV Section 2.1 [1]. An indexed jump erases its index tape contents, but the stated semantics do not force the index-tape head to reset to cell zero. Fix a correct deterministic total SAT machine $A$, integers $h,q\ge1$, and an original source of length below $2^h$.

Construct a label-preserving wrapper $A_{h,q}$ as follows. Move the input-index head to index cell $h$, write a 1, make an indexed input jump to address $2^h$, and read the blank. On each of the next $q-1$ repetitions, the index head remains at cell $h$, so write a 1 in that same cell, jump, and read a blank again. Restore the input head to position zero by an indexed jump with the empty address, then move the input-index head left by $h$ ordinary steps to restore its initial head position, before running $A$ unchanged.

The wrapper adds $\Theta(h+q)$ native steps and $\Theta(h)$ counted index-tape positions, and preserves SAT and UNSAT because all preliminary reads are ignored. A simulator specified to scan all $h+1$ index cells before every guest indexed jump incurs $\Omega(hq)$ additional operations. This is a restricted lower bound on that simulator \emph{only}. A family-specific cached-high-bit simulator avoids the scan penalty. Erasure of index \emph{contents} does not establish constant amortized cost for rescanning the \emph{index-address span}.

For $h=N^\epsilon$, $q=N^\kappa$, the extra guest work exponent is $\max(\epsilon,\kappa)$ but naive rescanning has exponent $\epsilon+\kappa$. The copy, delay and recomputation wrappers remain required adversarial controls. General ordinary guest head movements, dynamic child accessors and all-input contraction are not solved here.
