\]
Arrange $p=r^2$ distinct cells in an $r\times r$ square grid. Use four positive occurrence ports per cell: N is the $a_i$ occurrence in positive clause 1; E is the $a_i$ occurrence in positive clause 2; S is the $b_i$ occurrence in positive clause 3; W is the $c_i$ occurrence in positive clause 4. Along each horizontal grid edge exchange the two occurrence identifiers at E and W; along each vertical edge exchange the S and N identifiers. \begin{theorem}[Strict M22, grid minor, trivial certificate]
For every $r\ge1$ the resulting $F_r\in\CS$ has $k=6r^2$, $m=8r^2$, $m_+=m_-=4r^2$, no individual complementary-clause pairs, and a connected primal graph of maximum degree at most eight. \end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each exchange is between two TRUE-positive occurrences, preserving each variable's two positive uses and not altering its two negative uses or literal truth value under the displayed assignment. Each touched clause receives one variable from a distinct cell, leaving two distinct local names. No duplicates occur between cell-distinct clauses, and all original local clauses are unique. Negative clauses remain entirely local, and original $Y$ has no complementary pairs. Crossed positive clauses cannot share the exact support of a local negative clause. Hence strict grammar, nonpairing and the displayed satisfying assignment hold.
The four untouched negative clauses in each cell have supports $abd,acf,bce,def$ and connect all six local variable vertices in the primal graph. Each exchanged port creates a primal edge between its grid-neighboring cells. Contract the six-variable connected subgraph of each cell: the $r\times r$ square grid remains as a minor. Treewidth is minor-monotone and square-grid treewidth is $r$ for $r\ge2$. Finally each original variable occurs in four ternary clauses, so it has at most eight distinct primal neighbors.
\end{proof}
