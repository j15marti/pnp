\section*{ITERATE: Theorem}
Every UNSAT source-authenticated 2-CNF $F$ with nonempty clauses on $n\ge1$ appearing variables admits an ordinary strict binary Resolution contradiction DAG with at most $4n-3$ paid inference nodes. Therefore $W_{R_{\mathrm{mix341}}}(F)\le 4n-3$ in the unchanged mixed calculus. A source containing an empty clause, if allowed by a grammar, requires zero paid steps.
\begin{proof}
The implication graph has $2n$ signed literals. Each binary source clause $(a\lor b)$ gives $\neg a\to b$ and $\neg b\to a$, and each unit $(a)$ gives $\neg a\to a$. Unsatisfiability implies some $x$ and $\neg x$ are mutually reachable. Choose simple paths $P:x\to\neg x$ and $Q:\neg x\to x$, each of length at most $2n-1$. A path from $s$ to $\neg s$ of length $k$ derives unit $\neg s$ in at most $k-1$ Resolution inferences: combine the source implication clauses along the path. If an internal edge is a unit clause, the current resolvent becomes $\neg s$ and the construction stops early. Resolve the two resulting complementary units to contradiction. The node count is at most $(|P|-1)+(|Q|-1)+1\le4n-3$. Every premise is a source or earlier paid inference, and all pivots are strict and checked.
\end{proof}
\section*{Countercontrol: not a minimum}
Enumerate all 256 subsets of the eight proper unit/binary clauses on two variables. Exactly 95 are SAT and 161 UNSAT. In 41 UNSAT sources, a first-variable, two-BFS-path proof uses more nodes than an independent exhaustive minimum-DAG Resolution search. Example $F=\{\neg x,y,\neg y,x\lor y\}$ has a one-node refutation on $y,\neg y$, whereas this path controller spends two nodes. This falsifies the assumption that a generated upper bound is an exact minimum.
