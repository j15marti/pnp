\section{Claim card, three falsifiable mechanisms, and model}
Fix the language of proper, unique 3-CNF records in which every source variable appears exactly twice positively and twice negatively. The grammar admits both monotone and non-monotone clauses; invalid encodings are rejected. It is NP-complete because it contains D\"ocker's strictly monotone exact-(2,2) NP-complete sublanguage \cite{docker}. In a valid input with $n$ variables and $m$ clauses, $3m=4n$.
We work with two header words and $m$ four-word clause records, containing arity plus three signed literal identifiers. With
\[
w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil,\qquad N=(4m+2)w,
\]
one addressed literal-word read or write costs one word operation; sign bits can alternatively be separately queried. Input validation, acquisition, failed tests, preprocessing, output construction, witness replay, and peak space are charged. Formula evaluation gates are not SAT-decider lower bounds.

\noindent\textbf{M1 (sharp local repair, PROVED/EXACT).}
Does an independently certified UNSAT exact-(2,2) source admit a SAT member differing in the signs of \emph{only two occurrences}, with every unsigned clause support unchanged? Yes, using two occurrences of variable 3; an explicit witness is checked.

\noindent\textbf{M2 (swap-path boundary, PROVED/EXACT).}
Do all legal signings of a fixed four-regular support lie in one graph under a swap of one positive and one negative occurrence of the same variable? Yes; diameter at most $2n$. Whenever both SAT and UNSAT signings exist, a two-sign opposite-label boundary exists. This is an existential structural statement, not a fast algorithm for finding the boundary.

\noindent\textbf{M3 (amplification, FALSIFIED/RETIRED for the proposed direct product).}
Do the same-support signed-sensitive pairs force superlinear SAT work after replication? No: the $p$-block family has a sharp $p=\Theta(N/\log N)$ literal-word lower bound, but an $O(p)$-word recognizer decides it.

\section{Exact two-sign repair on the published 198/264 source}
Let $U$ be the fixed signed formula with 198 variables and 264 original clauses in
\[
\texttt{research/agents/lspv-2/2026-10-24\_0011\_DOECKER61\_original\_source.txt}.
\]
LSPV-2 DOECKER-CORE-CERT61 independently checked a source-coherent finite UNSAT certificate for $U$. That prior certificate is an explicit dependency: its full proof tree was not rerun in this run. Its original clause indices are zero-based. In particular:
\[
U[3]=(\neg x_2\vee\neg x_3\vee\neg x_{194}),\qquad
U[13]=(x_3\vee x_9\vee x_{10}).
\]
Form $V$ by replacing these two clauses, and no others, with
\[
V[3]=(\neg x_2\vee x_3\vee\neg x_{194}),\qquad
V[13]=(\neg x_3\vee x_9\vee x_{10}).
\]
\begin{theorem}[Two-sign same-support opposite-label pair, PROVED/EXACT with imported UNSAT certificate]
$U$ is UNSAT and $V$ is SAT. Both are proper, unique, signed exact-(2,2) 3-CNFs with exactly $n=198$ variables, $m=k=264$ genuine ternaries, and
\[
w=15,\qquad N=15{,}870\ \mathrm{bits}.
\]
They have identical \emph{ordered unsigned clause supports}, identifiers, arities, degrees, Hall profiles, and encoded metadata. Their signed-literal Hamming distance is exactly two, achieved at positions $(3,1)$ and $(13,0)$ (zero-based).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The two changed literal occurrences belong to the same original variable $x_3$ and initially have opposite signs. Exchanging the signs preserves its two positive and two negative occurrences, with all other variables unchanged. Neither absolute identifier nor clause support changes. The original supports are unique and contain distinct variable identifiers, so the new clauses remain proper and distinct. D\"ocker's original $U$ is UNSAT as checked by the supplied finite-source certificate. An explicit satisfying assignment of $V$, indexed by $x_1,\ldots,x_{198}$ in that order, is the 198-bit binary expansion (padded on the left) of the hexadecimal integer
\[
\texttt{39a2b53c16ab15bc16ab15ca4b9715c4faa7143d31b0f26598}.
\]
Independent replay verifies all 264 clauses of $V$. On $U$ the same assignment falsifies \emph{only} zero-based clause 3. It sets $x_3=x_9=1$, so the new positive $x_3$ repairs clause 3 while $x_9$ keeps clause 13 true after $x_3$ is negated. Every other clause is unchanged.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Two occurrences are optimal, PROVED/EXACT]
Among \emph{distinct} legal exact-(2,2) signings of one ordered support, at least two literal signs differ. The constructed pair attains this minimum.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Flipping one sign changes the positive and negative count of its variable from $(2,2)$ to $(3,1)$ or $(1,3)$, invalidating the grammar. The pair above changes exactly two.
\end{proof}

\section{Uniform swap-path theorem}
Let $H$ be a fixed ordered 3-uniform support on $n$ variables of degree four, with four distinct occurrence slots per variable. Every exact-(2,2) signing selects a two-element positive-slot subset in each variable.
\begin{theorem}[Signed Johnson-product connectivity, PROVED/EXACT]
The graph of legal signings, with an edge for exchanging one positive and one negative occurrence of a \emph{single} variable, is the Cartesian product $J(4,2)^n$. Its diameter is $2n$, attained by signings with complementary positive-slot pairs at every variable. The shortest swap distance between $P$ and $Q$ is exactly half their sign-bit Hamming distance. If a support admits both a SAT and an UNSAT legal signing, it admits an opposite-label pair at distance one swap, equivalently two sign bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each variable the two positive positions specify one of the $\binom42=6$ vertices of $J(4,2)$; adjacent vertices exchange one selected and one unselected slot. Two selected 2-sets $A,B$ require $2-|A\cap B|$ exchanges, in $\{0,1,2\}$. Variables are independent in the sign grammar, so distances add and the maximum is $2n$. Take any path from a SAT signing to an UNSAT signing. As labels differ at the endpoints, some adjacent path vertices have opposite labels. Existence does not provide an efficient \emph{uniform} algorithm for finding a boundary without a SAT decision procedure.
\end{proof}
This differs from a statement that \emph{every} UNSAT signing has a SAT neighbor, which is not proved. The concrete $U$ happens to have one.

\section{Exact scalable both-label family and lower-bound ceiling}
For $p\ge1$ create $p$ disjoint copies with fresh source identifiers in block order. Use $V$ for a one bit, $U$ for a zero bit; call the resulting input $B_{p,b}$, $b\in\{0,1\}^p$. Then
\[
B_{p,b}\in SAT\iff b=1^p,\quad n_p=198p,\quad m_p=k_p=264p,
\]
\[
w_p=\left\lceil\log_2(64(462p+1))\right\rceil,\quad
N_p=(1056p+2)w_p.
\]
No auxiliary variables are introduced. A SAT witness is the concatenation of $p$ independent copies of the checked 198-bit source assignment, with variable offsets charged; any UNSAT block certifies the conjunction UNSAT.

\begin{theorem}[All-decider word-probe bound, PROVED/EXACT in the declared addressed-word model]
For every deterministic total exact decider $A$ of the full NP-complete signed exact-(2,2) language and every $p\ge1$, the run of $A$ on $B_{p,1^p}$ reads at least one of the \emph{two} orientation-sensitive literal words in each block. Hence $T_A(B_{p,1^p})\ge p=\Omega(N_p/\log N_p)$ addressed input-word probes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose the execution reads neither of the two changed words in block $i$. Replacing that block's $V$ by $U$ leaves the entire observed transcript, encoded length and all previously read words unchanged, but changes the correct answer from SAT to UNSAT. Determinism yields a contradiction. The two-word sensitivity sets are pairwise disjoint between blocks.
\end{proof}
The assertion does not give a $p$-probe bound on arbitrary UNSAT product inputs, nor an encoding-independent bit-time bound. A sequential parser compares each 264-clause block against the two fixed constant templates and returns AND of block labels; this uses $O(p)$ word reads, $O(1)$ auxiliary words beyond the input and fixed templates, and one output bit. For malformed/unrecognized inputs, a total exact decider falls back to a generic solver. Thus this family has a matching $O(p)$ source-aware fast path.

The baseline $F_b$ of the user-specified 2-SAT chain has $n=q+3$, $m=q+4$, $N=(4q+18)w$, is UNSAT iff $b=1^q$, and requires $q$ orientation-field probes on $1^q$ in that interface. Mapping its fields to copies of $U,V$ by an explicit reduction writes $1056q+2$ target words, greater than the original $q$-query bound. Paid lower-bound transport leaves a negative remainder. Direct inclusion gives only the new input-order lower bound.
