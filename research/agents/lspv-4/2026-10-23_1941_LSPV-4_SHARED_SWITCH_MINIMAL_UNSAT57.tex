\section{The original transparent 2-SAT baseline}
For $q\ge1$ and $b\in\{0,1\}^q$, take variables $z_0,\ldots,z_q,u,v$ and four endpoint clauses
$(z_0\vee u),(z_0\vee\neg u),(\neg z_q\vee v),(\neg z_q\vee\neg v)$
and $q$ orientation clauses $C_i=(\neg z_{i-1}\vee z_i)$ when $b_i=1$, or $(\neg z_{i-1}\vee\neg z_i)$ when $b_i=0$.
Then $F_b$ is UNSAT iff $b=1^q$: the endpoints force $z_0=1$ and $z_q=0$, and a first zero orientation permits a transition from true to false. Here $n=q+3$, $m=q+4$. On the all-one input, every exact deterministic sign-field algorithm must inspect all $q$ source fields. With fixed four-word clause records, two header words and $w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil$, $N=(4q+18)w=\Theta(q\log q)$. This is only a $\Omega(N/\log N)$ addressed-word input-observation bound, matched by a source scan.

\section{Exact all-ternary construction}
For $q\ge3$ use named Boolean source variables $x_0,\ldots,x_{q-1},s,t,y_1,y_2$ and $\sigma\in\{0,1\}^q$; cycle indices are modulo $q$. Each parity edge $x_i\oplus x_{i+1}=\sigma_i$ is represented by two proper ternary clauses with shared literal $s$. For $\sigma_i=1$ use
\[
(s\vee x_i\vee x_{i+1})\wedge(s\vee\neg x_i\vee\neg x_{i+1});
\]
for $\sigma_i=0$ use
\[
(s\vee\neg x_i\vee x_{i+1})\wedge(s\vee x_i\vee\neg x_{i+1}).
\]
Append
\[
Q=(\neg s\vee t\vee y_1)(\neg s\vee t\vee\neg y_1)
(\neg s\vee\neg t\vee y_2)(\neg s\vee\neg t\vee\neg y_2).
\]
Juxtaposition denotes conjunction. Call the resulting formula $H_{q,\sigma}$. It has exactly $n=q+4$ named source variables, $k=m=2q+4$ proper width-three clauses, and zero functional auxiliary variables. The padding variables $y_1,y_2$ are free on accepted assignments. Every original variable occurs positively and negatively; there are no initial units, pure literals, tautologies or repeated variables within clauses.

\begin{theorem}[Labels, witness counts and clause minimality; PROVED/EXACT]
\[
\#\mathrm{SAT}(H_{q,\sigma})=
\begin{cases}16,&\bigoplus_i\sigma_i=0,\\0,&\bigoplus_i\sigma_i=1.\end{cases}
\]
For odd parity, deleting \emph{any} original clause makes the formula SAT. Deleting a parity-edge clause yields exactly eight models, and deleting any of the four clauses in $Q$ yields exactly $2^{q+1}$ models. For even parity, the respective deletion counts are $16$ and $16+2^{q+1}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first pair of $Q$ clauses gives $(\neg s\vee t)$ and the second pair gives $(\neg s\vee\neg t)$, so $s=0$. The parity clauses become an XOR cycle, consistent iff the orientation XOR is zero. There are two $x$ assignments, and $t,y_1,y_2$ are independent, giving 16 models. Upon removal of one parity clause, the other $q-1$ parity edges form a path with two possible full $x$ assignments. For even total parity both satisfy the last edge regardless of the deletion; for odd parity exactly one satisfies its surviving half-clause. Multiply by the eight free choices of $t,y_1,y_2$. If one clause of $Q$ is deleted, the $s=0$ branch is unchanged. On $s=1$, all parity clauses are automatically satisfied, while the three surviving $Q$ clauses fix $t$ and one $y$ but leave the other $y$ and every $x_i$ free, giving $2^{q+1}$ additional assignments. \end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Minimum strong 2-SAT backdoor and ordinary resolution; PROVED/EXACT]
The minimum syntactic strong 2-CNF backdoor is the single variable set $S=\{s\}$, regardless of $q$. Both SAT and UNSAT are decidable after paid source recognition in $O(q)$ word operations and $O(q)$ workspace. Every odd-parity member admits an explicitly constructed ordinary-resolution refutation with exactly $4q+2$ derived inference nodes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every clause contains either $s$ or $\neg s$, so setting $s$ reduces all clauses to width at most two. The empty set cannot be a backdoor because all clauses are width three. If $s=0$ the remaining instance is an XOR-2SAT cycle; if $s=1$ the remaining clauses from $Q$ are contradictory 2-CNF. For resolution, resolve the two pairs of $Q$ clauses on $y_1,y_2$ and then on $t$ to derive $\neg s$ in three inferences; resolve that reused unit with each of $2q$ parity clauses to recover their binary versions. The two forward implication chains starting from $x_0=0,1$ take $q-1$ resolution inferences each to derive contradictory units on $x_0$ because total parity is odd. One final inference derives the empty clause. The inference count is $3+2q+2(q-1)+1=4q+2$. \end{proof}

\section{All-decider source-word lower bound and encoding limits}
Use two header words and four fixed-width words per clause (arity plus three signed-literal slots), each of width $w=\lceil\log_2(64(3q+9))\rceil$. The encoded input bit length is
\[
N=(8q+18)w=\Theta(q\log q).
\]
Flipping one orientation $\sigma_i$ changes the SAT label and exactly two literal-sign source words, in disjoint record positions for different $i$, leaving headers, length and variable names unchanged. Every deterministic exact decider of the full canonical proper-three-CNF language, whether polynomial or not, must read at least one changed word position for each edge on each family member. Otherwise the read transcript on that member and its one-flip opposite-label neighbor is identical. Hence $T_A(H_{q,\sigma})\ge q=\Omega(N/\log N)$ addressed input-word observations for the stated representation. A checked template recognizer and parity scan match $O(q)$ time. Packed-bit or other encodings require separate counting. This does not imply superlinear decision time.

\section{Charged ladder and failed amplification}
For general $F=B\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1}^kD_i$ with 2-CNF $B$ and $k$ genuine ternaries, selecting one variable per $D_i$ yields a strong 2-CNF backdoor $S$ of size at most $k$. Exact decision is bounded by $2^{|S|}\mathrm{poly}(N)$. Let $h$ be the minimum variable hitting-set size of the ternary supports. A uniform recursive 3-Hitting-Set search, branching on the three variables in an uncovered clause, discovers a size-$h$ hitting set in $O(3^h\mathrm{poly}(N))$ charged time, and $2^h$ 2-SAT branches decide. This known backdoor method gives $O((3^h+2^h)\mathrm{poly}(N))$ time inclusive of discovery and failed branches; it is not a lower bound. Our family has $k=2q+4$ yet $h=1$, with every clause essential on UNSAT. Allowing unrestricted $k$ includes arbitrary 3-SAT, a known NP-complete endpoint; that fact proves no $2^k$ lower bound. The source-aware backward normalization recognizes shared $s$ and generates the original XOR-2SAT cycle in $O(q)$ charged operations, whereas creating $H$ from the 2SAT source also costs $O(q)$ source reads and writes. No positive-remainder lower-bound transport beyond input order is demonstrated.

\paragraph{Resource ledger.} Entire input reads and grammar checks $O(q)$ words; literal occurrences $3m=6q+12$; common-backdoor discovery at most three candidate scans in $O(q)$; two SCC calls or a single parity continuation $O(q)$; witness reconstruction and full original-clause replay $O(q)$; UN\-SAT proof and verification $4q+2$ derived nodes with two parent references each, requiring $O(q\log q)$ output bits; reused unit $\neg s$ is one DAG node read by $2q$ derived clauses; peak live space $O(q)$ words.
