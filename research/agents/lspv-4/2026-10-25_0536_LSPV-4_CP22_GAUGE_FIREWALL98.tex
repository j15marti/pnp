\section{Incidence gauge criterion}
Put $b(C,x)=1$ if $x$ is negative in clause $C$ and $0$ otherwise. A variable-wide sign gauge chooses $g_x\in\{0,1\}$; after gauging, every clause is monotone exactly when there are clause bits $h_C$ such that
\begin{equation}
b(C,x)\oplus g_x=h_C\quad\text{for every incidence }(C,x). \label{eq:gauge}
\end{equation}
\begin{lemma}[Signed incidence cycle test]
The equations \eqref{eq:gauge} have a solution if and only if the XOR of incidence signs around every cycle in the bipartite variable--clause graph is zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
XOR equations along a cycle: every $g_x$ and $h_C$ appears twice and cancels. Conversely propagate assignments from a root over a spanning forest. Every non-tree edge is consistent precisely when its fundamental cycle has zero XOR. This is the elementary signed-graph balance criterion, related to Harary's switching theorem \cite{Harary}.
\end{proof}
Variable renamings, record reorderings and whole-clause polarity toggles cannot change any cycle XOR. Whole-clause toggles may themselves violate exact signed quotas, but the invariant remains valid. Noninjective source identifications or arbitrary gadget replacement are outside the model.

\section{One immutable bad four-cycle per port gadget}
The original compiler has $8m$ duplicate-selector clauses, $R=12m$ padded clone-implication clauses and $R/3=4m$ fresh copies of a fixed nineteen-clause gadget $Q=W_1\setminus\{(1,7,13)\}$. In the original executable two $Q$ clauses are
\begin{equation}
C=(-x_1\lor x_3\lor -x_{14}),\qquad
D=(-x_1\lor x_{13}\lor x_{14}). \label{eq:bad}
\end{equation}
These share $x_1,x_{14}$ with parity
\begin{equation}
\chi=b(C,1)\oplus b(D,1)\oplus b(C,14)\oplus b(D,14)
=1\oplus1\oplus1\oplus0=1. \label{eq:chi}
\end{equation}
Their four incidences form a signed odd four-cycle. Every one of the $4m$ $Q$ copies uses fresh names $R+15j+1,\ldots,R+15j+15$ and disjoint records.

\begin{theorem}[Universal gauge obstruction for this compiler; PROVED/EXACT]
For every nonempty proper source $F$, the actual $\mathcal T(F)$ cannot be made clausewise monotone by any source-variable-wide sign gauge, injective variable relabelling, clause permutation or whole-clause sign complement. After any such gauges at least $4m$ target clauses remain mixed, one per $Q$ copy.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each $Q$ copy contains a distinct bad four-cycle with $\chi=1$; the incidence criterion forbids making both of its two clauses monochrome. There are $4m$ disjoint copies. The permitted relabellings and switching operations preserve each parity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Necessary literal-occurrence repair budget; PROVED/EXACT restricted]
Any representation repair consisting of the above gauges/reorderings and edits to individual signed literal \emph{incidences}, with all original clause records retained, needs at least $4m$ occurrence-sign changes before the output can be clausewise monotone. If original clauses may instead be deleted or replaced, at least one original clause in each of the $4m$ bad $Q$ pairs must be touched. This says nothing about preservation of SAT under such repairs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each parity-one cycle must lose an edge incidence or have at least one signed incidence bit flipped. The cycles share no incidence and no clause record, so one edit cannot resolve two of them. Additional exact-$(2,2)$ grammar and semantic requirements were not assumed and may increase cost.
\end{proof}
A virtual decider need not produce this representation at all; these are \emph{representation-edit bounds}, not lower bounds on every decision algorithm.

\begin{equation}
\#\mathrm{mixed}(\mathcal T(F))\ge 68m+12m+4m=84m=\tfrac78(96m). \label{eq:mixed}
