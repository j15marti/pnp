\section{Restricted exact lemma, borrowed from OBDD theory}
For a prescribed fixed order of $q$ exact Boolean child-status bits, consider a deterministic label-only irreversible one-pass decoder $D:\{0,1\}^q\to\{0,1\}$. It cannot reread the source, replay or reorder children. At a cut $k$, a prefix $u\in\{0,1\}^k$ induces the residual function $D_u(v)=D(uv)$. Write
\[w_D(k)=|\{D_u:u\in\{0,1\}^k\}|.\]
\begin{theorem}[Known deterministic residual-function state bound; exact]
Every exact one-pass decoder must have at least $w_D(k)$ distinct internal states after cut $k$, or at least $\lceil\log_2 w_D(k)\rceil$ mutable state bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}Suppose two prefixes $u,u'$ having distinct suffix functions reach the same internal state. Some common suffix $v$ has $D(uv)\ne D(u'v)$, but determinism from the same state and subsequent suffix would give one common output. Contradiction.\end{proof}
For $q=2h$ and blocked equality $D(u,v)=[u=v]$, all $2^h$ first-half prefixes induce different residual functions; the midpoint therefore requires at least $h=q/2$ bits. For the \emph{paired} arrival order $u_1,v_1,u_2,v_2,\ldots$, a mismatch flag and pending first bit use at most two state bits, excluding the fixed finite controller/program phase. AND/OR/XOR accumulation needs one bit. These are interface-specific distinctions: choosing a good order and decoding its meaning can itself consume time/space. No lower bound on arbitrary SAT algorithms follows.
