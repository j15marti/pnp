\section{PROPOSE: falsifiable mechanisms fixed before targeted source search}
The missing accounting lemma is whether an adaptive SAT algorithm can invoke a fresh $h$-update entropy bound on \emph{each} of $p$ successive phases while omitting information already discarded, yet still answer correctly after all phases without reading its source again. Four precise possibilities were identified before the source-specific search and implementation.
\begin{description}
\item[]\textbf{H1, horizon restart.} A phase-local $h$-update sketch can be recycled over $p$ phases with the same information bound even when no original source is reread. \textbf{FALSIFIED}: a four-bit, two-phase counterexample; general information lower bound depends on total $H=\min(m,ph)$.
\item[]\textbf{H2, child-only compression.} A single physical child can be $N^\lambda$ bits, preserve the exact decision under arbitrary $H=m^\gamma$ future original-variable overwrites, and prevent parent rereads, for all fixed $\gamma\ge\lambda$. \textbf{FALSIFIED for this dynamic interface grammar}: exact distinguishability forces a longer child.
\item[]\textbf{H3, small-horizon positive implementation.} If $\gamma<\lambda$ and $H=m^\gamma$, a source-authenticated sparse-zero summary with update transcript and a fixed uniform updater fits inside $N^\lambda$ bits with polynomial construction and update cost. \textbf{RESTRICTED PROVED}; this is an easy AND-chain class.
\item[]\textbf{H4, model transfer.} A read-only-source sketch lower bound also constrains a model with editable source tape and free revisits, or yields an unrestricted SAT time-space lower bound. \textbf{FALSIFIED as a transfer}: explicit source-repair or reread is a different charged model.
\end{description}

\section{BUILD I: canonical physical source and total-horizon equivalence}
Use the exact source encoding $\Phi_m(b)$ from LSPV-4 FINITE-HORIZON-UPDATE-ENTROPY213. Its source bits $b=(b_1,\dots,b_m)\in\{0,1\}^m$ appear only through four sign-control literals per coordinate, in a complete, physically authenticated ordinary proper-3CNF of $6m+5$ variables and $9m+8$ clauses. The formula is satisfiable iff $\bigwedge_i b_i=1$. Every source has the same unpadded binary bit length $N_m=\Theta(m\log(m+2))$. Replacing a source control bit means overwriting its underlying four signs consistently, not changing the number of clauses or variables. The full source generator and parser are embedded in the executable audit source.

An initial deterministic preprocessor produces a retained configuration $z$ (including any child, auxiliary memory, head positions and readable output). The original $b$ and its CNF representation then become permanently inaccessible. The machine subsequently receives an adversarial stream of indexed point overwrites $b_i\leftarrow c$ and must answer SAT correctly after each, using one fixed program and no advice about the answer side. Phase boundaries are merely control events: they neither reveal original bits nor reset past changes. Let there be $p$ phases, at most $h$ updates per phase, hence $H=\min\{m,ph\}$ relevant repairs.

\begin{theorem}[No free horizon restart]\label{thm:stage}
The exact number of distinguishable initial source behaviours under all common future streams of at most $ph$ overwrites, divided into $p$ phases of at most $h$ updates, is
\[
\boxed{E(m,H)=\begin{cases}
1+\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{H}\binom mj,&H<m,\\
2^m,&H=m,
\end{cases}\qquad H=\min\{m,ph\}.}
\]
Thus every full retained initial configuration has at least $\lceil\log_2 E(m,H)\rceil$ bits of distinguishing information, \emph{not} merely $\log_2 E(m,h)$ bits. If $ph\ge m$, the information requirement is $m$ bits even when $h=1$ in every phase.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $S\subseteq[m]$ for the initially zero coordinates. If $|S|>H$, no stream of at most $H$ updates can turn every coordinate to one; these sources have the same always-UNSAT answers. If $|S|\le H$, setting every position in $S$ to one makes the answer SAT. Two distinct zero sets of size at most $H$ are distinguished by repairing one of the sets which is not a superset of the other (interchanging the two if necessary), while a small set is separated from the overflow class by repairing itself. Every such length-at-most-$H$ stream can be divided into $p$ consecutive phases with at most $h$ operations each, adding empty phases. Therefore the ability to observe phase boundaries does not change equivalence. Distinct initial response behaviours cannot yield one and the same deterministic retained configuration. This proves the exact count and bound. The argument uses only overwrites setting bits to one, so allowing writes of zero does not weaken it.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Concrete two-phase failure of local restart]\label{prop:two}
Take $m=4$, $h=1$, $p=2$, and initially zero sets $S=\{0,1\}$ and $T=\{0,2\}$. Both belong to the same one-step overflow class: each has at least two zeros, so neither can become SAT in one update. Perform the common first update $b_0\gets1$. Both outcomes remain UNSAT. Perform the second update $b_1\gets1$. The source $S$ becomes SAT and the source $T$ remains UNSAT. Hence any attempt to recycle the one-step overflow flag for the next step without reconstructing lost information is incorrect.
\end{proposition}

\section{BUILD II: an exact child-only information barrier}
A stronger interface assertion requires the preprocessor to output exactly \emph{one physical binary child string} $C(b)$ of length at most $L_m$, with no parent source, cache, side advice, or uncharged oracle after output. A fixed updater must answer correctly for any subsequent stream of at most $H$ overwrites of the original $b$ using $C(b)$ and the command stream. This is a \emph{dynamic-maintenance-preserving} child, which is strictly stronger than a static SAT/UNSAT-preserving child.

\begin{theorem}[Dynamic child bit-length lower bound]\label{thm:child}
Every such uniform deterministic child-only interface satisfies
\[
\boxed{2^{L_m+1}-1\ge E(m,H),\qquad L_m\ge\lceil\log_2(E(m,H)+1)\rceil-1.}
\]
If all children use an identical fixed length, the stronger bound $L_m\ge\lceil\log_2E(m,H)\rceil$ holds. In particular, $H=m$ forces $L_m\ge m$ even if variable-length child codes are allowed. For $H=m^\gamma$ rounded to an integer, $0<\gamma<1$, any $0<\lambda<1$ with $\gamma\ge\lambda$ excludes $L_m\le N_m^\lambda$ for all sufficiently large $m$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Two distinguishable source strings cannot produce identical children, since a deterministic updater starting with the same child and given the same future overwrite stream must emit the same answers. There are $2^{L_m+1}-1$ binary strings of length at most $L_m$, proving the first inequality. When $H=m$, $E=2^m$ and the inequality forces $L_m\ge m$. For $H=\lfloor m^\gamma\rfloor$ with $0<\gamma<1$ and $m$ large, $H\le m/2$ and
\[
\log_2E(m,H)\ge\log_2\binom mH
\ge H\log_2(m/H)=\Omega(m^\gamma\log m).
\]
The middle bound follows termwise from $(m-i)/(H-i)\ge m/H$ for $0\le i<H$. Since $N_m=\Theta(m\log m)$, the ratio $m^\gamma\log m/N_m^\lambda$ diverges for $\gamma>\lambda$ by a polynomial margin and for $\gamma=\lambda$ by a $(\log m)^{1-\lambda}$ factor. The no-parent-read and dynamic-update assumptions are indispensable.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Matching upper region]\label{prop:upper}
For $0<\gamma<\lambda<1$ and $H=\lfloor m^\gamma\rfloor$, there is one uniform, correct, physical dynamic-state encoding of $O(H\log(m+2)+\log(m+2))=o(N_m^\lambda)$ bits and a deterministic updater handling every SAT and UNSAT outcome along all streams of length at most $H$. It is not a general SAT shrinker.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Authenticate the entire original canonical CNF and count its zero coordinates. If more than $H$ are zero, emit an overflow tag: even after $H$ repairs the answer is always UNSAT. Otherwise emit the sorted list of at most $H$ zero addresses plus an exact size/horizon header. As updates arrive, maintain the changed zero set (at most $2H$ positions during a total of $H$ overwrites), or retain the initial list and a length-$H$ overwrite log. SAT holds exactly when the resulting zero set is empty. The total code and update bookkeeping require $O(H\log m+\log m)$ bits. A direct scan and list-update implementation has polynomial total time. Since $\gamma<\lambda$, this length is $o((m\log m)^\lambda)$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Polarity and limitation.} The lower bound counts source strings on \emph{both} initial SAT and UNSAT sides under one common uniform updater. It is a dynamic source-information obstruction: it does not imply that a static SAT decision requires $m$ bits, because this restricted scalar family is immediately decidable and a static decision-only shrinker can output a constant YES or NO base formula. Likewise a writable-input algorithm that keeps the original physical signs, or a program that rereads the source or queries an oracle, escapes the premise but must charge those resources.

The one-tape simulation arrow is an \emph{upper-bound construction}, not a reverse necessary implication. A decreasing rank must have polynomial \emph{value} and the largest intermediate length must remain polynomially bounded to imply polynomial total runtime.
