\section*{Model and theorem}
A single fixture has $V$ cubic vertices, four signed SOURCE leaves per vertex, a left-linear local chain of three paid AND3 deductions per vertex, and a left-associated chain of $V-1$ paid binary XOR deductions consuming the completed vertex parity factors. Every source occurrence and nonsink paid result has one consumer in these exact proofs. A paid-result register stores one derived factor/affine object as an \emph{atomic value}, regardless of its byte width. SOURCE leaves are read-only and occupy no such register. Every paid inference is executed once. Both paid antecedents must be live immediately before a binary XOR. After reading both inputs, the output may destructively overwrite a dying paid parent; no additional output slot is counted. Deletion of last-use parents costs no paid deductions. Inference steps, not Node instruction counts, are the time checkpoints.

Write $L_t$ for the number of retained paid results just \emph{after} paid step $t$. Define
\[
A_{\mathrm{slot}}=\sum_{t=1}^{4V-1}L_t,\qquad
P=\max_t\{\text{simultaneously allocated paid-result registers during step }t\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Restricted native-/341 destructive-overwrite retention frontier]
For the fixed left-associated original /341 proof template with $V\ge4$, among all no-recomputation topological execution orders whose transient paid-result peak satisfies $P\le 2$, the minimum post-step retention is
\[
\boxed{\min A_{\mathrm{slot}}=7V-4,\quad \min P=2.}
\]
The original all-vertex-first producer order instead has
\[
\boxed{P_{\rm producer}=V,\qquad A_{\rm producer}=2V^2+V.}
\]
Both use $W=3V+(V-1)=4V-1$ original unit-paid deductions and $R=2(4V-1)$ binary-parent applications. The longest paid dependency span is $V+2$. Reordering entire original JSON proof record objects leaves the one-newline compact JSON byte count unchanged.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The paid subexpression of each vertex is a chain of three AND3 results, each consuming the preceding result and one fresh SOURCE factor (the first consumes two SOURCE factors). It can run in one register by destructive last-use overwrite. Every XOR takes two \emph{distinct paid} factors/affine outputs. It requires at least two simultaneously live paid registers, so $P\ge2$.

For the upper bound, compute one vertex's three AND3 results using a single slot. Compute the second vertex chain while retaining the first completed factor (two slots), then XOR the two factors in place, leaving one accumulator. For each of the remaining $V-2$ vertices, compute its three-AND3 chain with the accumulator retained (two slots) and XOR its parity factor into the accumulator, leaving one slot. The post-step area is
\[
3(1)+3(V-1)(2)+(V-1)(1)=7V-4.
\]
The schedule is a legal topological permutation of the original record identities and parents. The archived checker examines earlier-parent existence, not numeric monotonicity of labels; the same DFS permutation was already accepted against the original checker in /523.

For the area lower bound, every intermediate paid result is a required ancestor of the unique terminal, and no recomputation is allowed, so at least one paid result remains live after every step. The only paid steps capable of leaving exactly one live register are (i) local AND3 operations within the \emph{first} uninterrupted vertex factor, before a separate required value exists, and (ii) XOR joins consuming two live values. Under the two-register cap, each local AND3 of any later vertex must coexist with an already retained result from an earlier vertex or accumulator, since that earlier value cannot be deleted before its unique XOR consumer; interleaving local chains only increases or maintains the number of two-live checkpoints. At most three of the $3V$ local AND3 checkpoints can therefore have a single retained value. Each of the $V-1$ XOR joins leaves one retained value in an optimal two-slot schedule. Thus at least $3V-3$ local steps have two retained slots, at least three local steps have one, and all joins at least one:
\[
A_{\mathrm{slot}}\ge3+2(3V-3)+(V-1)=7V-4.
\]
For the emitted producer chronology, the first vertex's three steps retain one value, and the $j$th vertex's three steps retain $j$ values, giving $3\sum_{j=1}^V j=3V(V+1)/2$ before XOR. The $V-1$ XOR reductions reduce live count from $V$ to $1$, contributing $\sum_{k=1}^{V-1}k=V(V-1)/2$. Hence $A_{\rm producer}=2V^2+V$ and peak $V$. Finally, paid record objects are only permuted within one JSON array; their fields, counts and comma separators are unchanged, so compact packet length is invariant. \qedhere
\end{proof}
