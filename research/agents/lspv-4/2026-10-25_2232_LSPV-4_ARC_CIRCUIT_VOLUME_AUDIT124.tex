\section{Exact same-realization child-volume lemma}
\begin{theorem}[Fully charged adaptive aggregate volume]
Fix \emph{one} uniform total deterministic exact all-input ordinary-SAT
random-access TM $A$ with simultaneous bounds
$T_A(m)\le m^{d+o(1)}$ and
$S_A(m)\le m^{s+o(1)}$, for constants $d\ge1,s\ge0$.
Fix one total uniform compiler/simulator $G[A]$ which on \emph{each}
ordinary source of full encoded length $N$ sequentially invokes $A$ on
finitely many \emph{genuine} ordinary encodings
$x_1,\ldots,x_k$, where
\[
 V=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|x_i|\le N^{b+o(1)},
 \qquad M=\max_i|x_i|\le N^{\lambda+o(1)}.
\]
Calls and child selection may be adaptively determined by the prior
complete computation transcript, but the resulting total path is charged
in the worst case for both SAT and UNSAT sources. Assume the local
source parsing, adaptive policy discovery, rejected candidates,
actual child generation, header/body/variable-index arithmetic,
gluing and validation, unchanged \emph{and} changed writes,
output/certificate production, all failed routes and termination cost
$N^{r+o(1)}$ steps in aggregate, except the guest simulation
and the guest's virtual input probes. Assume \emph{every actual guest
input probe}, including its source/workspace reads, addressing, and
regeneration, costs at most $N^{u+o(1)}$ steps.
If the compiler's combination of all guest answers yields the correct
ordinary-SAT label on \emph{every} input, then a fixed same-model machine
$B[A,G]$ obtains the simultaneous \emph{time upper certificate}
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 t_B\le \max\{r,\ u+b+\lambda(d-1)\}.\quad} \tag{1}
\]
For its \emph{peak writable/index space}, let $\sigma$ denote the
producer/selector live state, $\eta$ the guest input-service workspace,
$\kappa$ all concurrently retained ancestors, previous answers,
caches and partially emitted certificates, and $\omega$ any additional
live verification/gluing workspace, all as $N$-exponents.
If a guest child is \emph{eagerly materialized}, put $e=\lambda$;
if virtual and unmaterialized, put $e=0$ and include its complete
generator state in $\eta$. Then this same machine simultaneously obeys
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 s_B\le\max\{\sigma,\eta,\kappa,\omega,e,\lambda s\}.
 \quad} \tag{2}
\]
A bound on output \emph{write-only} size by itself does not add space,
but each output bit and header is charged in time; reading that output
later requires a new counted representation or recomputation.
Both displays are only conditional on the fully verified premises,
not a free all-input contractor.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each child $m_i=|x_i|\le M$, use
$m_i^d=m_i m_i^{d-1}\le m_i M^{d-1}$.
Summing gives
\[
 \sum_i m_i^d\le VM^{d-1}
 \le N^{b+\lambda(d-1)+o(1)}. \tag{3}
\]
At most one input probe occurs per guest step. Charging each probe
an additional factor $N^{u+o(1)}$ and adding all local work gives (1).
The $m^{o(1)}$ upper-bound factors can be enlarged to a common
$N^{o(1)}$ across polynomially many calls; fixed short inputs have
uniform finite costs, charged through $V$. For (2), no guest call is
parallel: the largest guest workspace and every simultaneously live
producer, service, retained-answer, ancestor, materialized-child,
and verification buffer coexist as appropriate. Their sum is bounded
by the largest exponent; counted input indices and unchanged writes
are included. All code for fixed $A,G$ is fixed, never $N$-dependent
free advice. SAT and UNSAT correctness is a separate explicit premise,
not a consequence of (3).
\end{proof}

\section{Adversarial resource false positive and both-label checks}

\section{Independently checked primary theorem/model import ledger}
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{FLMV, indexed random access [1].}
Read-only random-indexed input, counted writable and index work,
input-index jump semantics, output write-only direction, and bit-level
time must be respected. The decreasing curve
$\beta(c)=\tfrac12((c+2)/c^2-c)$ yields the sufficient
strict forbidden simultaneous rectangle
\[
 c<\frac{241}{200},\quad
 s<\frac{11642479}{23232400}
 =\beta\!\left(\frac{241}{200}\right).
 \tag{4}
\]
For the conditional ideal $(6/5,1/2)$ cell, exact strict margins are
$1/200$ for time and $26279/23232400$ for space.
No boundary equality nor a drifting $o(1)$ term without fixed slack
establishes membership in a forbidden region.

\item[] \textbf{Williams corrected product and cosine [2,3].}
Williams [2, Theorem 4.11] proves, for polynomially bounded
time $T$ and space $S$, $TS=\Omega(N^{\varphi-\varepsilon})$
infinitely often for every $\varepsilon>0$,
$\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$. Hence a fixed same-machine pair
$t+s<\varphi$ is forbidden, with its exact cited model.
The more recent $2\cos(\pi/7)$ SAT time exponent instead
requires $N^{o(1)}$ \emph{space}. The old claim that
$t+s<2\cos(\pi/7)$ universally follows is FALSIFIED/RETIRED.

\item[] \textbf{Van Melkebeek survey [4, Theorems 1.3 and 6.3].}
Theorem 1.3 is a \emph{two-algorithm} alternative:
if $(c-1)d<1$ or $cd(d-1)-2d+1<0$, for some
$\varepsilon>0$ the SAT coNTIME $N^c$ algorithm and
the SAT deterministic random-access
DTISP$(N^d,N^\varepsilon)$ algorithm cannot both exist.
For the \emph{EXTRA} $d=901/500=1.802$ small-space SAT
assumption, the second strict inequality requires
\[
 c<\frac{2d-1}{d(d-1)}
 =\frac{651000}{361301}
 =1.801821749732\ldots. \tag{5}
\]
The deficit from $d$ is the positive exact
$32201/180650500$; setting the coNTIME algorithm equal
to the same $N^{1.802}$ deterministic seed supplies
no contradiction. $P=NP$ alone grants neither this $d$
nor a sufficiently small $\varepsilon$.
Theorem 6.3 instead juxtaposes \emph{all-input nonuniform
SAT decision circuits} of size $N^c$ against
\emph{$\Sigma_2$SAT} deterministic $N^d$-time
$N^\varepsilon$-space computation, under
$(c-1)d<c$ or $cd(d-2)-2d+2<0$.
It is not a theorem about one SAT instance or a
formula representation. A proposed numerical circuit
threshold $1.444997427915\ldots$ has not been traced to
an exact verified composition and is \textbf{PENDING}, not
imported as a contradiction.

\item[] \textbf{Bathie--Williams 2024 [5, Theorem 1.4,
Definition 1.5].}
A lower bound for \emph{strongly uniform NAND formulas}
of depth $(c-o(1))\log N$, $c<4\cos(\pi/7)$,
with its explicit strong gate-indexing uniformity;
a formula is not an arbitrary reusable-fanout circuit.
Neither arbitrary circuits nor SAT algorithms inherit this depth
bound without an explicit uniform formula conversion and blowup proof.

\item[] \textbf{Pippenger--Fischer 1979 [6].}
The classical $O(T\log T)$ gate circuitization applies
to the stated multitape TM and bounded-fan-in gates.
A random-indexed FLMV input is not silently a standard
multitape input with the \emph{same} $T$ and $S$.
Circuit construction uniformity, number of input wires,
gates, outgoing wires, fanout, and a gate's switch
operation are separately charged. Neither this theorem nor
simple unrolling gives a small-gate SAT \emph{lower bound}.

\item[] \textbf{Allender--Kouck\'y [7], Borodin [8].}
The $n^{1+\epsilon}$ \emph{wire} context for
NC$^1$/TC$^0$ self-reducibility is not a general
unrestricted SAT gate lower bound. Borodin's
space-to-depth simulation invokes a separate read-only
input/work-tape model and has depth overhead of order $S^2$:
it cannot be mixed with gate count for an unrelated indexed
RTM realization without a proved and charged translation.

\item[] \textbf{Shalunov 2025 [9, Theorem 1.2].}
Circuit-value evaluation of an explicit binary-basis
$G$-gate circuit admits space
$O(\sqrt{G\log G})$ in the stated model.
Producing or reading the explicit encoded circuit, its
assignment and output, adapting indices, and evaluating
its value take charged time; this upper bound on
space \emph{alone} does not transfer to a simultaneous
$N$-bit ordinary SAT decider.
\end{description}

\section{Certificates, nonuniformity, and precise non-implications}
Suppose one input-specific representation has $N^{1.29}$ gates.
Even an exact circuit $C_F$ that outputs the correct label
for one fixed instance $F$ can have a single constant gate,
using \emph{the already known answer}. The nonuniform
SAT decision-circuit hypothesis in [4] is instead
\[
 \forall N\ \exists C_N\ \forall x\in\{0,1\}^N:
 \quad C_N(x)=\mathrm{SAT}(x),\quad |C_N|\le N^{q+o(1)}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Neither the existence of arbitrary $\{C_F\}$ nor the
count of the instance-specific \emph{representation} produces
(6). If a uniform algorithm \emph{constructs} $C_F$,
its source reads, certificate discovery, gate and wire
writes, verification, and construction time must be charged.

Nondeterministically guessing a certificate is legal
\emph{only within the correct nondeterministic class and
with each guessed bit and verifier step counted}.
A SAT satisfying assignment witnesses SAT in NTIME,
not an UNSAT certificate for the coNTIME(SAT) premise
in [4, Theorem 1.3]. A valid guessed UNSAT certificate
with an actual fast nondeterministic \emph{complement}
verifier would supply a coNTIME upper bound, but cannot
be presumed from the certificate's semantic existence.
If a deterministic compiler prints its witness or proof,
printing length is a lower bound on the compiler's
output time, although read-only preexisting input and
write-only output may have distinct space conventions.
