\section*{ITERATE: exact legal prefix and restricted sharp theorem}
Original variables are t,x_1,...,x_k,y_1,...,y_k. Take the SAT source
\[
F_k^+=(t)\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1}^k(t\vee x_i)\wedge(t\vee y_i),

\Gamma_k=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb F_2^{2k+1}:z=Q(x,y)\},\qquad Q(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^k x_i y_i\bmod2.

The target to test is an \emph{explicit list} of affine subspaces (equivalently, conjunctions of GF(2) rows) whose union equals \Gamma_k, with no shared implicit generator and no retained nonlinear auxiliary gate. This target is not declared the full original mixed calculus.

\begin{lemma}
Every affine subspace A contained in \Gamma_k has at most 2^k points.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Projection onto (x,y) is injective on A, giving an affine image a+U. The z coordinate restricted to a+U must be affine. Its quadratic second derivative therefore vanishes on every u=(p,q),v=(r,s) in U:
\[
B(u,v)=p\cdot s+r\cdot q=0.
\]
This B is a nondegenerate alternating bilinear form on a 2k-dimensional vector space: B((p,q),(r,0))=q\cdot r and B((p,q),(0,s))=p\cdot s. Thus U\subseteq U^\perp and dim U+dim U^\perp=2k, forcing dim U\le k. Hence |A|=2^{dim U}\le 2^k.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}
The minimum number of affine subspaces covering \Gamma_k is \boxed{2^k}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The graph has 2^{2k} points and each covering affine piece at most 2^k, so at least 2^k are required. For each a\in\mathbb F_2^k, the fixed-x slice x=a, z=a\cdot y is an affine k-plane; the 2^k such planes form a disjoint cover.
\end{proof}
