\section{New restricted SAT certificate}
Let $E_j$ be the event that source clause $C_j$ is all false under a uniformly random completion of partial assignment $\alpha$. Let $p_j$ and $p_{jk}$ be the exactly computed conditional probabilities of $E_j$ and $E_j\cap E_k$. They are zero or dyadic numbers determined by consistent original-source U targets.
\begin{theorem}
For any spanning tree $T$ on the $m$ source clause IDs,
\[
B_T(\alpha)=1-\sum_j p_j(\alpha)+\sum_{\{j,k\}\in E(T)}p_{jk}(\alpha)
\le\Pr(F\text{ SAT under a random completion}\mid\alpha).
\]
If $B_T(\alpha)>0$, a deterministic polynomial-time procedure builds and checks a full source-variable SAT witness by choosing a positive-bound branch for each unset variable while retaining the same tree. All scanning, conditional probes, maximum-spanning-tree selection, exact arithmetic and witness checks are charged.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Order the rooted tree parent-before-child. At each addition, the new union mass grows by at most $p_h-p_{h,\mathrm{parent}(h)}$. Sum over vertices and subtract the union bound from one. For any unset source variable, the parent bound is the arithmetic mean of the two child bounds. Therefore a positive bound gives a positive child, until a complete verified satisfying assignment is reached. This combines Hunter's known spanning-tree inequality with conditional expectations.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
For duplicate-free proper $k$-CNF with $m$ source clauses,
$B_T(\emptyset)\le 1-(m+1)/2^{k+1}$.
Thus no unconditioned root certificate can be positive when $k=3,m\ge15$. This is tight on the SAT star $G_m=\bigwedge_j(x\vee y\vee z_j)$, where $B_T=(15-m)/16$ and $x=1$ satisfies all clauses.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each false-cube mass is $2^{-k}$. Two distinct noncontradictory $k$-clause false cubes fix at least $k+1$ source variables, so intersect with mass at most $2^{-k-1}$. A spanning tree has $m-1$ edges, and the star achieves equality.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
For a proper width-$k$ CNF, $m<2^k$ implies SAT. At $m=2^k$ it is UNSAT exactly when every source clause pair shares an opposite-polarity variable. Hence every proper signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3CNF with $n=6,m=8$ is SAT.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each U cube has mass $2^{-k}$; an exact cover by $2^k$ such cubes requires pairwise disjointness. Eight clauses require 28 conflicting pairs, but six variables with two positive and two negative occurrences each provide only $6(2)(2)=24$ opposite-sign occurrence-pair witnesses.
\end{proof}
