\section{Exact weighted-free fractional cell lemma}
Let $f:D\subseteq\{0,1\}^N\to\{0,1\}$, $f(x^0)=0$, and $\mathcal E\ne\emptyset$ contain mutation supports $E$ with $x^E=x^0\oplus\mathbf1_E\in D$ and $f(x^E)=1$. Let $\mathcal P$ be a fixed partition of physical input bits into source cells. Its hyperedge for $E$ is $B(E)=\{P\in\mathcal P:P\cap E\ne\emptyset\}$. Define
\[
\tau^*=\min_{z\ge0}\{\sum_{P\in\mathcal P}z_P:\;\sum_{P\in B(E)}z_P\ge1\text{ for every }E\}.
\]
The dual maximizes $\sum_Ey_E$ with $\sum_{E:P\in B(E)}y_E\le1$ for every cell $P$, $y_E\ge0$. Integral $z\in\{0,1\}$ instead gives the prior deterministic hitting number $\tau$.

\begin{theorem}[Coupled-coin randomized authentication]\label{th:lp}
Fix one total worst-case-clocked randomized input-cell reader $A$ that is correct on the baseline with probability at least $1-\epsilon_0$, and on every mutant $E$ with probability at least $1-\epsilon_E$. Source preprocessing, failed strategies, virtual access, caching, metadata, proof authentication and final output are ordinary charged work. If $Q_\omega$ is the set of \emph{distinct} original input cells read on the baseline with random coins $\omega$, then
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E|Q_\omega|\ge \delta\tau^*},\qquad
\delta=\max\{0,1-\epsilon_0-\sup_E\epsilon_E\}.
\]
In particular, with uniform two-sided error $\epsilon<1/2$, $\delta\ge1-2\epsilon$; with zero error on the baseline, $\delta\ge1-\epsilon$ for error $\epsilon$ on every mutant. The theorem also applies to a completely emitted randomized SAT-label-preserving virtual child if correctness refers to the \emph{actual SAT label} of the output child on both sources.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the same random tape on $x^0$ and $x^E$. Unless the baseline run reads a cell in $B(E)$, every source response, address, cached result, child length, requested bit, stop condition and output remains identical on the mutant. However the two runs have opposite correct labels simultaneously with probability at least $1-\epsilon_0-\epsilon_E$. Thus
$\Pr[Q_\omega\cap B(E)\ne\emptyset]\ge\delta$.
Putting $p_P=\Pr[P\in Q_\omega]$ and using the union bound gives
$\sum_{P\in B(E)}p_P\ge\delta$ for every $E$. If $\delta>0$, the vector $p_P/\delta$ is LP-feasible; summing yields the result. The $\delta=0$ case is trivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Two charged virtual layers]\label{co:two}
Suppose every random execution uses at most $r_0$ separately charged direct original probes, $p_1$ first-layer initialization probes, $q_1$ further original probes per first-layer service request, $p_2$ such requests in second-layer initialization, $q_2$ first-layer calls per second-layer request, and a final guest makes at most $\bar Q$ second-layer requests. All headers, lengths, endmarkers and failures are charged service calls. If the complete generator preserves the SAT label on baseline and each mutant with two-sided error $\epsilon$, then
\[
(1-2\epsilon)\tau^*\le r_0+p_1+q_1(p_2+q_2\bar Q).
\]
If only expected guest requests are bounded, the corresponding expectation may replace $\bar Q$ provided the per-request caps hold for all coins. No guest input-scan count or hereditary strictly shorter ordinary-SAT child follows without a separate proof.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For each random tape, unroll all services and count distinct original source cells by the displayed upper bound. Average, and use Theorem~\ref{th:lp}. A per-stage error bound $\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2$ suffices for a composed error cap via the union bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Sharp controls and connected source}
For the partial promise $0^r$ versus its $r$ singleton-one mutants, $\tau=\tau^*=r$. The two-sided $\epsilon$ lower bound $(1-2\epsilon)r$ is exact: with probability $\epsilon$ announce the mutant label without reading; otherwise independently inspect each cell with probability $a=(1-2\epsilon)/(1-\epsilon)$, reporting a mutant iff a changed cell is found. Baseline error is exactly $\epsilon$, mutant success exactly $1-\epsilon$, and baseline expected inspected cells $(1-2\epsilon)r$. With baseline zero error the exact cost is $(1-\epsilon)r$.

For all perturbations of size $M/2$ on an even $M$-bit zero baseline, $\tau=M/2+1$ but $\tau^*=2$ (uniform primal weights $2/M$ and equal normalized dual edge weights). Two independent uniform probes with replacement detect every mutant with probability $3/4$ and never misclassify the baseline. Thus an integral deterministic bound is \emph{not} a bounded-error randomized bound, even up to constant factors.

For the connected cubic prism $C_h\square K_2$ with $h\ge3$, fix an odd total charge in the parity equations on its $2h$ vertices and $3h$ edge variables. Each vertex contributes four proper ternary clauses forbidding the opposite parity assignments, giving $8h$ clauses, $24h$ literal-sign fields and exact four positive plus four negative occurrences per edge name. Since the global charge XOR is odd, the source is UNSAT. Deleting one vertex leaves the graph connected, so pinning its three incident edges to a forbidden local word and solving all other parity equations yields an assignment violating only that one clause. Flipping any of the clause's three signs produces SAT. This independently checks the earlier ARC191 $24h$ one-bit SAT-sensitive family, which is ordinary proper 3CNF but not within the signed exact-$(4,4)$ promise after one sign change.

To stay inside signed exact-$(4,4)$, pair each of the two positive appearances at one endpoint of every edge with one of the two negative appearances at its other endpoint, and vice versa. There are $12h$ disjoint pairs. Deleting two adjacent prism vertices leaves the remaining graph connected. Pin at one vertex the forbidden local word and at the other the complement of its paired forbidden word; the shared edge agrees by the opposite signs. Remaining equations are consistent. The first sign flip repairs the unique bad clause, the other still leaves at least two true literals. Each paired mutation therefore yields SAT while preserving the signed quota. The source construction and pair lemma were independently first established by LSPV-1; this run regenerates their witnesses.

Use a 16-bit header tag plus Elias-gamma lengths for counts $3h$ and $8h$, followed by records of length $3(w+1)+1$ for $w=\lceil\log_2(3h+1)\rceil$. Then
