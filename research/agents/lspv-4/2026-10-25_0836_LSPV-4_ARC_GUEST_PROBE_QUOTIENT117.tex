\section{Two-level guest-read-sensitive conditional upper ledger}
For an ordinary source $F$ of encoded length $N$, suppose a uniform
semantically exact parent policy creates legal ordinary first children
of length $L_1=N^{\theta+o(1)}$ and ordinary second children of length
$L_2=L_1^{\mu+o(1)}=N^{H+o(1)}$ where $H=\theta\mu<1$.
At most $N^{a+o(1)}$ sequential \emph{final} invocations of the
\emph{same} total exact ordinary SAT guest $A$ occur, including all
failed attempts. On each actual eligible grandchild, in addition to
the global $L_2^{t+o(1)}$ work and $L_2^{s+o(1)}$ peak, suppose $A$
makes at most $L_2^{q+o(1)}$ indexed guest-input read events,
including repeats. This image-specific behavior is a \emph{separate
premise}; membership $(t,s)\in E$ only supplies $q\le t$ automatically.

One second-child input-bit generator performs at most
$L_1^{u+o(1)}$ local work and at most $L_1^{p+o(1)}$ virtual
first-child reads. Each first-child bit read costs at most
$N^{g+o(1)}$ fully charged input/access work. Their simultaneous
generator writable/index memory is at most $N^{\eta_1+o(1)}$
and $L_1^{\eta_2+o(1)}$. Non-guest work including parent source
validation, discovery, switching, rejected routes, compatibility
and any output work uses $N^{r+o(1)}$ bit work and
$N^{h+o(1)}$ live space, with retained ancestor/cache peak
$N^{\kappa+o(1)}$. Separate policy-selector guests,
materialized children, or parent-scale fallback are \emph{not} free.

\begin{theorem}[Fully lazy two-level guest-input-probe upper envelope]
If the stated all-input ordinary-SAT policy actually exists, and
both levels are eligible and SAT/UNSAT correct, then its literal
fully lazy charged simulation has \emph{joint} exponents
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\gamma_2&=\max\{\theta u,\theta p+g\},\\
t'&\le\max\{r,\ a+Ht,\ a+Hq+\gamma_2\},\\
s'&\le\max\{h,\kappa,\eta_1,\theta\eta_2,Hs\}.
\end{aligned}}\tag{1}
\]
Each additional $N^b$ guest selector calls on size $N^\rho$ adds
$b+\rho t$ base time plus its own fully charged access service
and at least $\rho s$ guest live space. Materialized selector or
child buffers add their actual writable length. One cannot
substitute different programs for the two coordinates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A guest performs at most $L_2^{t+o(1)}$ counted elementary
work operations, including its own index writes, erasures and
unchanged writes. Servicing each of its at most
$L_2^{q+o(1)}$ input reads invokes a generator with local
$L_1^{u+o(1)}$ work and at most $L_1^{p+o(1)}$ first-child
bit requests costing $N^{g+o(1)}$ each. Hence one
input-bit service costs $N^{\gamma_2+o(1)}$, and all final
guests together cost at most
$N^{a+Ht+o(1)}+N^{a+Hq+\gamma_2+o(1)}$.
Add all $N^{r+o(1)}$ parent work. Depth-first execution
retains a real guest, both generator states and all declared
live parent/ancestor/cache workspace \emph{simultaneously};
their finite sum has the asserted maximum space exponent.
Input read operations are already included in guest base work,
so charging their virtual replacement service again gives a
conservative upper bound. This theorem neither proves the
policy exists nor lower-bounds all SAT algorithms.
\end{proof}

\section{Two same-$E$ guests falsify automatic sparsity}
\begin{theorem}[Conditional padded-guest behavioral separation]
Assume $P=NP$; choose a \emph{fixed} total uniform
ordinary-SAT decider $B$, exact on all strings including malformed
encodings, with time exponent $K$ and space exponent $S$.
For any fixed integer $D>\max\{K,S,2\}$, two globally correct
fixed ordinary-SAT programs $A_{\mathrm{quiet}}$ and
$A_{\mathrm{read}}$ have the same allowable joint upper
pair $(D,S')\in E$, where $S'=\max\{S,0\}$ at exponent
precision. Both perform $n^{D+o(1)}$ real counted operations
on every input $x$ of length $n$, but
\[
\mathrm{InputReads}(A_{\mathrm{quiet}},x)
\le n^{K+o(1)},\qquad
\mathrm{InputReads}(A_{\mathrm{read}},x)
\ge n^{D-o(1)}. \tag{2}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both fixed programs perform $\lceil n^D\rceil$ iterations
writing the same zero to the same dummy work cell on
\emph{every} iteration, including unchanged writes, then
execute and return $B(x)$ for both SAT and UNSAT.
The quiet loop does not consult the input tape; the reading
loop also writes and erases a legal indexed source address,
reads one indexed source bit per iteration, and ignores it.
Counters and guest indices use $O(\log n)$ live bits and
polylogarithmic charged instruction overhead per iteration.
The shared peak is $S_B(n)+O(\log n)$, and the shared
actual work exponent is $D$ up to $o(1)$.
Only $B$ reads input in the quiet variant, at most its
$n^{K+o(1)}$ work; the reading loop makes at least $n^D$
reads. Both return the correct globally defined SAT bit.
\end{proof}
An aware compiler could eliminate either useless loop
or directly execute a discovered $B$. Therefore this is
\emph{not} a SAT lower bound or an impossibility theorem
against all adaptive selectors. It establishes that the
image-specific $q$ assumption in (1) must be \emph{proved},
not read off the achievable pair $(t,s)$.
