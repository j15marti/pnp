\section*{Exact representation-dependence theorem}
Write $g(p)=p\oplus(p\gg1)$ for reflected Gray encoding. The FLMV machine has a finite control, a read-only indexed input tape and writable/index tapes; an ordinary move of an index-tape head by one cell is a native transition, and an indexed jump erases the index tape's \emph{contents}, not its head position [1]. In the following declared audit the head position is represented \emph{offline}, in a fixed $(h+1)$-bit coordinate field. Its bits are not charged as physically written tape bits merely because the diagnostic representation changes.

\begin{theorem}[Index-head binary carry amplification]
For each $h\ge2$, let $p=2^h-1$ and consider any even $R\ge2$. An FLMV-legal deterministic control loop can move a blank index-tape head successively between physical positions $p$ and $p+1$, performing exactly $R$ native head moves, no writable/index tape cell writes, no indexed jumps, and no source modification. With an alternating single control bit, the cumulative adjacent complete \emph{encoded} configuration XOR along the oscillation is
\[
D_{\rm binary}=R(h+2),\qquad D_{\rm Gray}=2R,
\quad D_{\rm physical\ tape\ content}=0,
\quad D_{\rm endpoint}=0.
\]
In particular, even with the same physical execution, coordinate-only XOR differs by a factor $h+1$, and no universal $D_{\rm encoded}\le cT$ with fixed $c$ follows from a fixed number of writable tape write ports. For a whole initially blank trace, an initial one-cell-at-a-time head movement from $0$ to $p$ costs $p$ additional native steps and contributes
\[
D_{\rm prep,binary}=2^{h+1}-h-2,\qquad
D_{\rm prep,Gray}=p.
\]
These changes are still \emph{head-coordinate diagnostics}, not physical write counts.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The binary strings of $2^h-1$ and $2^h$ are $0\underbrace{11\cdots1}_{h}$ and $1\underbrace{00\cdots0}_{h}$ in $(h+1)$ aligned cells, so every bit flips. Adjacent nonnegative integers differ in exactly one reflected Gray bit. The finite control toggles once for every direction reversal. For even $R$, both control state and head position return to their initial values. Every index tape cell stays blank. During the preparation phase, binary bit $j$ flips exactly $\lfloor p/2^j\rfloor$ times, summed over $j=0,\ldots,h-1$; the resulting geometric sum is $2^{h+1}-h-2$. Gray code changes one bit per adjacent position. This proves each formula directly without a SAT decision or a source oracle.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uniform correctness-preserving wrapper with logarithmic head extent]
For every fixed deterministic total exact decider $A$ of the \emph{whole} canonical language (including malformed rejection), one can prepend a deterministic redundant head-motion phase, restore the input and index-head starting positions and all temporary contents, and then execute $A$. On inputs of length $N$, choose $h=\lfloor\log_2\log_2 N\rfloor$ for sufficiently large $N$, so $p=2^h-1=\Theta(\log N)$, and oscillate $R=2N$ times. The resulting uniform wrapper preserves both SAT and UNSAT outputs, has
\[
T_{\rm wrapped}(N)=T_A(N)+O(N\log N),\qquad
S_{\rm wrapped}(N)=O(S_A(N)+\log N),
\]
while its \emph{binary-encoded head-coordinate} XOR includes $\Omega(N\log\log N)$ from the redundant phase, versus $O(N)$ for the same head coordinates Gray-encoded.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Scan the read-only source to obtain $N$ in a logarithmic-space binary counter. Compute $h$ and $p$ with logarithmic workspace, move the originally blank input-index head to $p$ in $p=O(\log N)$ steps, then scan the read-only input twice while alternately moving that index-tape head right and left. The source input head can return to position zero by an indexed jump whose all-zero address is already present; that jump preserves the index-head coordinate. The two scans give an even $R=2N$ moves; at the end the oscillating head is back at $p$. Move it left $p$ cells, clear auxiliary counter work and restore the remaining blank index tapes and input-head coordinate. This is charged $O(N\log N)$ time and $O(\log N)$ live tape extent, even under conservative index-head high-water accounting. Finally run $A$ untouched. The wrapper never decides the input by itself and works identically for well-formed SAT, well-formed UNSAT and malformed strings. The XOR diagnostic does not supply a lower bound on the required work of $A$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Model caveat.} FLMV defines configurations using the finite control, work/index \emph{contents}, and index-head positions [1, Section 2.1]. An arbitrary fixed binary or Gray coding of the latter is an \emph{offline tableau representation}, not a primitive index tape register. The physical-content recharge theorem $X_{\rm content}\le2qT+B_0$ remains valid exactly as stated in the prior research. This result refutes only an unjustified extension of that inequality to encoded head-coordinate or full-configuration Hamming distance. The space high-water mark and input/head movements remain fully charged, even when tape contents do not change.
