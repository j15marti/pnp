\section{Exact SAT/UNSAT source families and normalizer theorem}
For every $q\ge3$, with all private $a_i,d_i$ distinct and shared $x$, let
\[
P_{q,b}^{+}=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{q}(\ell_i\lor a_i\lor d_i),\qquad
\ell_i=\begin{cases}x&b_i=0\\\neg x&b_i=1.\end{cases}
\]
Let $U_8$ contain all eight sign patterns as three-distinct-variable clauses on three fresh variables, and $P_{q,b}^{-}=P_{q,b}^{+}\wedge U_8$. Every $P^+$ is SAT by setting all $a_i=1$, and every $P^-$ is UNSAT. A change to a single $b_i$ modifies one signed-literal orientation word, not the source length, clause headers, identifiers, or control label.

For source variable $v$ with $p_v$ positive and $n_v$ negative occurrences, the original PC22\_ALLFIELD1 balanced compiler defines
\[
r_v=\max(p_v,n_v,2),\qquad
g(F)=\sum_{v}(2r_v-p_v-n_v).
\]
For $m$ source clauses its exact proper signed exact-$(2,2)$ output $U(F)$ has
\[
V(U(F))=9(m+7g(F)),\qquad M(U(F))=12(m+7g(F)).
\]
The compiler is source-uniform, polynomial-time, and does not decide SAT before emitting its output. Assume the declared ordinary indexed output grammar contains a binary encoded variable-count header and that the source has separately addressed fixed-width orientation words.

\begin{theorem}[One output-header bit requires all orientations; restricted]
For either sign-labeled family $P^{+}$ or $P^{-}$, at the all-zero orientation $b=0^q$, every exact deterministic field-query evaluator for the second least significant bit of $V(U(P_{q,b}^{\pm}))$ must read all $q$ source orientation words. The lower bound also applies to a total uniform indexed output-bit evaluator that has no stored source-dependent precomputation when asked for that header bit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The $2q$ private variables each occur once and contribute three to $g$, hence $6q$. At $b=0^q$, the shared $x$ has occurrence counts $(q,0)$, contributing $q$. Each of the three $U_8$ variables has $(4,4)$ occurrences and contributes zero. Thus $g=7q$, and for SAT and UNSAT respectively,
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &P_{q,0^q}^{+}&P_{q,0^q}^{-}\\\hline
m&q&q+8\\
g&7q&7q\\
V&450q&450q+72\\
M&600q&600q+96
\end{array}
\]
Flipping any single orientation changes the shared occurrence counts to $(q-1,1)$. Since $q\ge3$, its contribution decreases by exactly two. Therefore $V$ decreases by $9\cdot7\cdot2=126\equiv2\pmod4$, toggling bit one. An exact deterministic transcript that omits field $i$ is unchanged under that opposite-bit neighbor, so cannot answer both. This holds independently for each of the $q$ fields.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{Model limits.} For four $w$-bit words per three-clause and two header words, with $w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil$, the source size is $N=(4m+2)w=\Theta(q\log q)$, so this is precisely an $\Omega(N/\log N)$ addressed-word acquisition bound. It is not a lower bound on SAT decision. A single paid $O(q)$ source scan obtains $g$ and stores $O(\log q)$ bits of metadata; subsequent header evaluation is constant-word-time. Full materialization emits $\Theta(q)$ ordinary records and $\Theta(q\log q)$ bits, with $O(q)$ word-time and $O(q)$ conservative peak workspace. There is no $\sqrt{N}$-length ARC child here.
