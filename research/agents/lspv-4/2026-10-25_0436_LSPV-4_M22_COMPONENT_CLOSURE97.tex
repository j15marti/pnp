\section{Exact deletion-closure theorem}
For $F\in\CM$, let $I(F)$ be the bipartite clause--variable incidence graph, and let $F_1,\ldots,F_c$ denote its connected clause-containing components. For a subset $J$ of the original clause indices, $F[J]$ retains exactly these signed clauses without pinning, rewriting, renaming or adding variables. We count the empty CNF as vacuously in $\CM$, fully paired and outside $\CS$.

\begin{theorem}[All valid deletion subsets; PROVED/EXACT restricted]
For every $F\in\CM$ and every $J\subseteq[m]$,
\[
F[J]\in\CM\iff J=\bigcup_{i\in S}\operatorname{clauses}(F_i)
\quad\text{for some }S\subseteq[c].
\]
Also $F[J]\in\CS$ precisely when this selected component union contains at least one nonpaired component. If exactly $d$ of the $c$ components are nonpaired, the numbers of legal source-indexed deletion subformulas in $\CM$ and $\CS$ are respectively
\[
\boxed{2^c\qquad\text{and}\qquad2^c-2^{c-d}.}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If a variable $x$ appears in any kept clause, its signed degrees in the kept formula must each be exactly two. They were each exactly two in the parent; therefore all four occurrences of $x$ and all their incident parent clauses must remain. Every variable met in the newly forced clauses must similarly retain its four occurrences. Induction along every clause--variable path forces the entire parent incidence component. Thus each component is wholly retained or omitted. Conversely, a union of complete components preserves duplicate-freeness, exact ternarity, clausewise monotonicity and all occurrence quotas. A complementary partner of a clause uses the same three names, so lies within the same component. Full pairing is therefore component-local. The count follows by all $2^c$ selections, deleting the $2^{c-d}$ subsets containing only paired components.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Connected both-label obstruction; PROVED/EXACT restricted]
If $F$ is connected and $F\in\CS$, the only $\CM$ deletion subformulas are $F$ and the empty CNF, and the only $\CS$ deletion subformula is $F$. A two-level deletion-only contractor cannot obtain a strict in-target recursive child from such an input. This is a syntactic statement and does not cover rewriting, pinning with charged type restoration, compact semantic methods or existential ARC successors.
\end{corollary}

\section{Positive exact restricted projection}
Let
\[
\Pi(F)=\mathop{\dot\bigcup}_{i:F_i\notin\mathcal P_{\rm pair}}F_i,\qquad F\in\CS.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Paired-component erasure; PROVED/EXACT restricted]
For every $F\in\CS$, $\Pi(F)\in\CS$, $\Pi^2(F)=\Pi(F)$, and
\[
\SAT(F)=\SAT(\Pi(F)),\qquad
\#\SAT(F)=\#\SAT(\Pi(F))
\prod_{i:F_i\in\mathcal P_{\rm pair}}\#\SAT(F_i).
\]
Each removed factor is positive. When $F$ is connected, $\Pi(F)=F$, so this source-preserving method has exactly source-scale output.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The union contains at least one nonpaired component and satisfies the grammar by the preceding theorem. Different components have disjoint variables, so model counts multiply and SAT is their conjunction. Every removed fully paired component is satisfiable by D\"ocker. No paired component remains after one projection. On a connected nonpaired source the operator is the identity.
\end{proof}
The theorem is a genuine exact restricted positive algorithmic component, but cannot by itself supply a favorable all-input ARC contraction. A straightforward charged parser, incidence traversal, complement check and reserializer takes polynomial elementary bit work and up to $O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)$ live writable bits, including buffers; using Reingold's undirected-connectivity theorem can reduce component-query space, but full repeated source seeks and output access must still be paid \cite{reingold}. A write-only output tape is not a free new read-only input.
