\section{FLMV machine and indexed guest input}
FLMV uses an indexed random-access multitape Turing machine. Its original input is read-only; work and index tapes have counted space; indexed access to an input position follows the binary address written on an associated index tape, whose contents are erased at the jump. The read-only input \emph{head position} is space exempt, so a guest may be very far beyond the last nonblank source cell. A virtual-child simulator cannot simply replace the guest's input by a writable tape at the same high address. It must return the exact input symbol (blank beyond the physical child length), and respect subsequent ordinary left/right moves and future indexed jumps. The physical child input has length $m$ and a nonblank final cell $y_{m-1}$. Address positions start at zero.
\begin{theorem}[Tight far-side distinguishability with bounded re-entry]
Fix an integer $r\ge1$ and a nonempty physical child $y$ whose last symbol is nonblank. The $r+1$ possible guest input-head positions
\[m,m+1,\ldots,m+r\]
are pairwise distinguishable by a sequence of at most $r$ ordinary \emph{left} moves followed by one read. Consequently any \emph{state-only} head abstraction that must answer all such continuations, without retrieving the discarded address from another source, needs at least
\[\boxed{\lceil\log_2(r+1)\rceil}\]
bits for those far-side states. A saturated abstract head coordinate, which preserves the exact position below $m+r+1$ and maps all coordinates at or above $m+r+1$ to one absorbing far sentinel, returns the same input symbol as the physical input for every continuation containing at most $r$ ordinary moves before the next indexed jump. Its state requires $O(\log(m+r+2))$ bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For two positions $p=m+u$ and $q=m+v$ with $u<v\le r$, exactly $u+1\le r$ left moves put the former at the nonblank last input cell $m-1$ but the latter at $m+(v-u-1)\ge m$, which is blank. Thus each pair demands distinct internal states if no address-reconstruction resource is available. For the upper construction, until the logical head crosses the saturation threshold its exact coordinate is represented. Once it reaches $m+r+1$, in at most $r$ remaining moves it cannot reach the final nonblank cell, so all reads are blank; a later indexed jump reconstructs the appropriate state from the new address. This establishes exact \emph{bounded-horizon} semantics and no unbounded guarantee. \end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Guest-accounted exact input-head register]
Let a guest indexed machine run for at most $T$ steps. Suppose that during the run every binary input-index address is represented using at most $s$ meaningful indexed-tape positions, including any high bit; $s$ is already charged as guest index-tape use. Then its logical input-head coordinate has magnitude at most $2^s+T+1$. Consequently its exact signed binary \emph{representation} needs at most
\[\boxed{s+\lceil\log_2(T+2)\rceil+O(1)}\]
bits. For a guest on a child of physical size $M$ with $T_A(M)\le M^{K+o(1)}$ and index space $S_A(M)\le M^{L+o(1)}$, the abstract extra head-register space is $O(S_A(M)+\log M)$, allowing a guest-dependent constant $K$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each indexed move replaces the input-head coordinate with a nonnegative binary number below $2^s$. Each ordinary move changes it by at most one and there are at most $T$ such moves in total. The initial head position is zero. Therefore every reached coordinate lies in the interval of magnitude at most $2^s+T+1$. A sign bit and binary digits give the stated representation. This is an upper bound on \emph{information stored}, not a proof of the time required to load binary indexed jumps, erase guest index tapes, perform repeated counter updates, guard blank cells, or simulate arbitrary guest work tapes. \end{proof}
\section{Why the time obligation remains open}
Suppose a hypothetical all-input hereditary stage produces $q=N^{3/5+o(1)}$ serial physical ordinary-SAT children of length $M=N^{1/2+o(1)}$ and invokes a fixed guest $A$. At the abstract representation level, reusing one exact head shadow contributes no new positive space exponent beyond the already charged guest index bound $N^{L/2+o(1)}$, plus logarithmic bookkeeping. Retaining $q$ complete head descriptors simultaneously instead can cost $q$ times as much and is \emph{not} justified. Neither fact establishes the required total-work recurrence.
A naive virtual simulator which \emph{rescans all $s$ binary address digits on every ordinary guest input-head move} spends $\Theta(Ts)$ selected events on an $s$-bit far-head alternating left/right path of length $T$. The original guest uses $O(s+T)$ such events after setting the far address once. With $s=T=M$, the explicit scanner can cost $\Theta(M^2)$ even for a guest taking $O(M)$ steps. This is a falsification of a specific \emph{naive scanner}, not an intrinsic lower bound against efficient incremental or redundant counters. Known real-time counter simulations supply a plausible direction, but their integration with guest arbitrary binary jumps, index erasure, other tapes and source access in FLMV remains an OPEN model-transfer obligation. If a guest-time multiplier $N^\delta$ is incurred, the target FLMV box requires the \emph{strict} $\delta<1/400$ (assuming other proposed coefficients). A surcharge of $N^{L/2}$ would typically violate it.
