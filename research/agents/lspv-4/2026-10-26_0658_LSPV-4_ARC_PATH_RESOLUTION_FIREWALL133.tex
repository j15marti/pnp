\section{Exact syntax and quantifiers}
Fix $N$ as physical bits of a canonical proper ordinary signed ternary CNF $F$. Consider an explicit depth-first certificate built from $\mathsf{BRANCH}(x,P_0,P_1)$, $\mathsf{EMPTY}(p)$, and $\mathsf{PATH}(x,\gamma_+,\gamma_-)$. Each branch variable is fresh on its root-to-leaf path. For ancestor assignment $\alpha$, EMPTY authenticates one original source clause falsified by $\alpha$. PATH authenticates two chains of implications $x\leadsto\neg x$ and $\neg x\leadsto x$ using \emph{original source clauses}, each of whose residual after $\alpha$ has width at most two; physical offsets, signed endpoints and continuity are checked. Surviving unrelated ternaries confer no extra semantic rule. Let $B$ count branch nodes, $L=B+1$ leaves, $E$ implication-edge records, $R=B+L+E$, $h$ depth, and $P$ proof bits. The compact grammar admits $P=O(R\log N)$. Affine/XOR, unrestricted algebraic proofs, and arbitrary semantic lifting are \emph{not} included.

Write $D_\alpha=\bigvee_{\alpha(v)=0}v\vee\bigvee_{\alpha(v)=1}\neg v$, which forbids precisely $\alpha$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact linear-step transport to ordinary Resolution]\label{th:transport}
For every canonical source $F$ and every accepted certificate $\pi$ of the BRANCH/EMPTY/PATH grammar, there is an ordinary Resolution refutation of $F$ with at most $O(R(\pi))$ inference lines. The result holds for all adaptive branching orders and both labels as required by verifier soundness, but asserts no converse efficient simulation of all Resolution proofs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct bottom-up to derive $D_\alpha$ at each proof node. At EMPTY, the authenticated original clause $C$ is falsified by $\alpha$, hence $C\subseteq D_\alpha$; weaken it to $D_\alpha$. At PATH, an authenticated implication $u\to v$ comes from an original clause $C$ whose residual after $\alpha$ is $(\neg u\vee v)$ or the unit $(v)$, so $C\subseteq D_\alpha\vee\neg u\vee v$ (for a unit take $u=\neg v$). Weaken each original axiom to the displayed implication clause, resolve consecutive edges to obtain $D_\alpha\vee\neg x$ from $x\leadsto\neg x$, and $D_\alpha\vee x$ from $\neg x\leadsto x$; resolve on $x$ to derive $D_\alpha$. Each edge uses $O(1)$ lines. At BRANCH on fresh $x$, the two children derive $D_\alpha\vee x$ and $D_\alpha\vee\neg x$, resolving to $D_\alpha$. The root derives the empty clause using $O(B+L+E)$ steps, initially allowing weakening.

Eliminate weakening by tracking a subclause for every line: keep each original axiom, ignore every weakening, and at each Resolution inference resolve the predecessor subclauses if both pivots survive; otherwise retain the pivot-free predecessor subclause. Every resulting clause is a subclause of its simulated line, and no extra Resolution inference is introduced. In particular the final empty clause survives.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[False universal polynomial certificate cap]\label{cor:hard}
There are infinitely many ordinary proper UNSAT 3CNF inputs $F_N$ such that every accepted unaugmented BRANCH/EMPTY/PATH certificate has
\[
 R(F_N)\ge 2^{\Omega(N/\log N)}.
\]
For every fixed finite $a$, the assertion $\forall F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}\cap\{0,1\}^N\;\exists\pi:\ R(\pi)\le N^{a+o(1)}$ is \textbf{FALSIFIED} for this grammar.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose odd-charge cubic-expander Tseitin graphs $G_n$ on $n$ vertices. Four proper width-three clauses encode each degree-three parity vertex, with one Boolean variable per graph edge. Theorem 4.4 and Corollary 4.5 of Ben-Sasson--Wigderson \cite{bsw} give every \emph{general} Resolution refutation size at least $2^{\Omega(n)}$. Standard canonical decimal-ID source encoding has $N=\Theta(n\log n)$ physical bits, so Theorem~\ref{th:transport} forces the stated $R$ bound. This is not a SAT algorithm lower bound. Each such graph-edge variable occurs four times positively and four times negatively, so these instances are \emph{not} signed exact-$(2,2)$; transporting the lower bound to that subclass requires a separate proof-system-preserving regularizer.
\end{proof}
