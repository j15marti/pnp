\section*{STATICNP1 theorem}
\textbf{Theorem.} Let \(W(x,h,y)\) be a continuation-winning predicate with responses bounded by a polynomial \(p(|x|)\). Suppose there is a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction \(f\) such that
\[
W(x,h,y)=1\iff f(x,h,y)\in\mathrm{SAT}.
\]
Suppose also that every reachable legal prefix \(h\) admits at least one \(y\), \(|y|\le p(|x|)\), with \(W(x,h,y)=1\). Then, under \(P=NP\), a continuation-winning response can be selected in deterministic polynomial time. Polynomially many causal stages can therefore be played in polynomial total time.

\paragraph{Proof.}
The reduction gives \(W\in NP\). For a candidate prefix \(u\) of the response define
\[
L=\{(x,h,u):\exists v,\ |uv|\le p(|x|),\ W(x,h,uv)=1\}.
\]
Because \(W\in NP\) and \(v\) is polynomially bounded, \(L\in NP\). Under \(P=NP\), \(L\in P\). Standard prefix self-reduction constructs a response one bit at a time while preserving membership in \(L\). Totality supplies an initial accepting extension. The construction uses polynomially many polynomial-time calls, hence is polynomial. Repeating over polynomially many stages remains polynomial. \(\square\)

\section*{Polynomial adaptive SAT-query variant}
The same closure holds when the proposed continuation procedure is an otherwise polynomial-time deterministic machine making polynomially many adaptive SAT queries of polynomial size. Under \(P=NP\), each SAT query is decidable in polynomial time, so the whole continuation procedure is polynomial time. If its output relation is total and polynomially balanced, RESPSEL1/GLOBALGAME1-style prefix selection again applies.

This is an upper-bound closure under the contradiction hypothesis, not a lower bound.

\section*{Compiler dilemma}
STATICNP1 gives the following hostile dichotomy:
\[
\boxed{\text{polynomial SAT staticization of continuation winning}
\Longrightarrow \text{NP-level closure under }P=NP.}
\]
If instead continuation winning genuinely retains polynomially many unresolved alternating future choices, it may have PSPACE-style structure, but then an ordinary polynomial SAT staticization of that exact predicate has not been established. Inserting a stronger game into the protocol is not a model-preserving transfer from static SAT.

Thus CRS-E or a Search-G compiler cannot simultaneously rely on genuine beyond-NP continuation hardness and on a polynomial ordinary-SAT encoding of that same continuation predicate without resolving the tension in favor of the NP closure.
