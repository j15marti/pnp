\section{Euler pairing theorem: a simultaneous SAT/NAE certificate}
Let \(m=|\mathcal C|\), let \(L=\sum_C|C|=rm=\sum_v d_v\), and assume every \(d_v\) even. The output is a signing on exactly the original \(L\) incidence locations. No original Boolean variable or clause is added or deleted. The prescribed \(a\) is data for constructing a \emph{new} signed formula, not an unknown witness inferred for a given signed formula.

\begin{theorem}[Complementary witness Euler signing, PROVED/EXACT]
For every such \(H\) and every \(a\in\{0,1\}^{V}\), a deterministic uniform algorithm constructs a signed formula \(G(H,a)\) in \(O(L+m+|V|)\) word time, with \(O(L+m+|V|)\) peak words, such that each \(v\) has exactly \(d_v/2\) literals of each sign, and for each clause \(C\)
\[
\left\lfloor\frac r2\right\rfloor
\le \#\{\text{true literals of \(C\) under }a\}
\le\left\lceil\frac r2\right\rceil.
\]
The same inequalities hold for \(\overline a\). Hence both distinct assignments satisfy the formula when \(r\ge2\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Pair the \(d_v\) distinct clause-occurrence sockets of each variable \(v\) into \(d_v/2\) arbitrary pairs. For every pair construct a temporary undirected graph edge joining its two clause vertices, and label the edge by \(v\) and the two exact source sockets. Because clauses are proper, there are no loops; parallel edges are allowed. Each clause vertex has degree exactly \(r\).

Every finite undirected multigraph has an orientation with the incoming and outgoing degrees differing by at most one. For completeness, pair odd-degree vertices by temporary dummy edges (the number of odd-degree vertices is even), obtaining all-even degrees; orient the edges along Euler tours of the resulting components; delete the dummy edges. The indegree of an original degree-\(r\) vertex is between \(\lfloor r/2\rfloor\) and \(\lceil r/2\rceil\). All steps take linear graph time.

For every \emph{original} graph edge, select its head occurrence as true under \(a\), and its tail as false. Set the corresponding literal sign positive precisely when its desired truth value equals \(a_v\). Each variable labels \(d_v/2\) edges, each selecting one head and one tail occurrence. Thus it has exactly \(d_v/2\) true and \(d_v/2\) false signed occurrences under \(a\), equivalently exactly \(d_v/2\) positives and negatives. At a clause vertex, the number of true literals is its indegree. Under \(\overline a\) the truth values of all its literals reverse, so the number true is \(r-\operatorname{indeg}(C)\), which satisfies the same inequalities. Source sockets and names have not been independently resampled.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact signed-$(2,2)$, proper 3-CNF]
For \(r=3\), \(d_v=4\), the source has \(n=3t,m=4t\) for some integer \(t\), and the output has \(n\) original variables, \(m\) original genuine ternary clauses, no new SAT auxiliaries, and exactly \(1\) or \(2\) true literals in every clause under either \(a\) or its complement. Each clause is mixed-sign when \(a=1^n\). The temporary clause graph has \(m\) cubic vertices, \(2n=3m/2\) original edges, and \(m/2\) dummy edges: exactly \(2m\) Euler edges.
\end{corollary}

\section{Sharp distance with the stronger two-witness contract}
Call a 3-clause \emph{monotone} when all its signs agree. A formula is monotone exact-$(2,2)$ if every clause is monotone, proper, and every variable has exactly two positive and two negative occurrences.

\begin{theorem}[Optimal monotone NAE repair, PROVED/EXACT]
For every monotone exact-$(2,2)$ proper 3-CNF \(F\) with \(m\) clauses, the minimum number of changed literal-sign fields among exact-$(2,2)$ signings of precisely the same unsigned occurrence records, subject to \emph{both} \(1^n\) and \(0^n\) satisfying the repaired formula, is exactly \(m\).
An optimum repair can be constructed by one bipartite perfect matching in \(O(m\sqrt m+n+m)\) word time.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The target property says every clause must contain both a positive and a negative literal, since it must be true under both complementary constant assignments. Every original clause is monochromatic, so at least one literal sign in \emph{each} of its \(m\) clauses must change: distance at least \(m\).

Each variable of \(F\) has two positive occurrences and two negative occurrences. Pair each of its two positive sockets with one distinct negative socket. Make a bipartite multigraph with one vertex for every positive clause and one for every negative clause; add one edge for each paired opposite-sign occurrence pair, retaining exact source socket IDs. Every clause vertex has degree three. In particular the two sides have the same size \(m/2\), and for any subset \(Q\) of positive clauses its \(3|Q|\) incident edges end at negative clauses, each of degree three, so \(|N(Q)|\ge|Q|\). Hall's theorem yields a perfect matching.

For each matched edge, flip the signs of its two endpoint occurrences, one positive-to-negative and one negative-to-positive, both belonging to the \emph{same original variable}. Every source variable remains exactly sign-balanced. Every clause has exactly one flipped occurrence, and thus has two literals of its original polarity and one of the opposite polarity. It is satisfied by both all-TRUE and all-FALSE. There are exactly \(m/2\) matched edges and two flips per edge, for exactly \(m\) flips. The lower bound is attained.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact bottom-up ladder and fully charged transport}
The prescribed 2-SAT baseline is
\[
F_b=(z_0\vee u)(z_0\vee\neg u)(\neg z_q\vee v)(\neg z_q\vee\neg v)
\bigwedge_{i=1}^q(\neg z_{i-1}\vee (z_i \text{ if }b_i=1;\ \neg z_i\text{ otherwise})).
\]
Its first two clauses force \(z_0=1\), its endpoint clauses force \(z_q=0\); a first zero in \(b\) permits the \(1\to0\) transition, whereas all ones imply a contradiction. Thus UNSAT iff \(b=1^q\). Here \(n=q+3,m=q+4\); all \(q\) orientation fields are necessary on the all-one input \emph{in the declared field-query model}, but a source scan or SCC algorithm decides in linear word time. With \(w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil\) and four-word clause records \(N=(4m+2)w\), the lower bound is \(\Omega(N/\log N)\) addressed-word probes, not \(\Omega(N)\) machine time across encodings.

For arbitrary 2-CNF plus \(k\) added genuine ternaries, select one original variable from each ternary and take their union \(S\), \(|S|\le k\). After assigning \(S\), every branch is 2-CNF, so \(T\le 2^{|S|}\operatorname{poly}(N)\); fixed \(k\) and \(O(\log N)\) are polynomial. The standard padding control
\((a\vee b)\equiv(a\vee b\vee y)\wedge(a\vee b\vee\neg y)\)
makes \(y\) \emph{free} on every satisfying source assignment. Horn, XOR, independent components, and source-aware affine normalization are mandatory bypass controls. With unrestricted \(k\), signed 3-SAT is NP-complete; the monotone exact-$(2,2)$ restricted endpoint is also KNOWN NP-complete by D\"ocker, which supplies both SAT and UNSAT labels. Neither endpoint fact proves a time lower bound.

\section{Falsification, direct recognizer, and barrier audit}
A total exact deterministic SAT decider may first parse the source, recover the unsigned support \(H\), construct the \emph{canonical} \(G(H,1^n)\) with prescribed deterministic pairings, dummy order and Euler tie breaks, and compare every original sign field. If equal, it can return SAT together with a direct all-TRUE witness (and all-FALSE witness if requested); otherwise it falls back to exhaustive deterministic SAT search. Such an algorithm is correct on the \emph{entire} signed language and runs \(O(n+m)\) on every canonical constructed image. The source and comparison are fully charged. This falsifies claims that the constructed regular source, mixed clause polarities, nontrivial clause graph, or two-witness bookkeeping must require expensive continuation. The fallback may take exponential time on other inputs; no \(P=NP\) conclusion follows.
