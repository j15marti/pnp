\section{CONSTRUCTION/PROOF ATTEMPT}
Write the source clauses as
\[F=\bigwedge_{i=1}^m(a_i\vee b_i\vee c_i).
\]
Introduce fresh variables $u_i,z_i$ for $1\le i\le m$ and $e_i$ for
$1\le i<m$.  Define
\begin{align*}
A_i&=\operatorname{NAE}(a_i,b_i,u_i),\\
B_i&=\operatorname{NAE}(\neg u_i,c_i,z_i),\\
E_i&=\operatorname{NAE}(z_i,\neg z_{i+1},e_i)\wedge
\operatorname{NAE}(z_i,\neg z_{i+1},\neg e_i),
\end{align*}
and $T(F)=\bigwedge_i(A_i\wedge B_i)\wedge\bigwedge_{i<m}E_i$.

\begin{lemma}[Proper two-clause equality]
$E(x,y;e)$ is satisfiable over $e$ exactly when $x=y$.  If $x=y$, both values
of $e$ work.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $x=y$, the first two literal values are $x,\neg x$ in both clauses, so both
clauses are true.  If $x\ne y$, then $x=\neg y$.  The first clause forces
$e=\neg x$, while the second forces $\neg e=\neg x$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pin-free gauge-chain compiler]
For every nonempty proper signed 3-CNF $F$,
\[\boxed{F\in\mathrm{SAT}\iff T(F)\in\mathrm{NAE\mbox{-}SAT}.}\]
Moreover every source model extends and every target model projects.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Given a source model, set every $z_i=0$.  The exact identity
\[
a\vee b\vee c\iff
\exists u\,[\operatorname{NAE}(a,b,u)\wedge
\operatorname{NAE}(\neg u,c,0)]
\]
chooses each $u_i$, and the equality lemma permits either $e_i$.

Conversely, a target model has $z_1=\cdots=z_m=z$ by the equality lemma.  If
$z=0$, project the source coordinates directly and apply the displayed
identity clausewise.  If $z=1$, complement every target variable.  Every
signed literal value is complemented and NAE is invariant under complementing
all three inputs, so the complemented assignment is still a target model and
all gauges are zero.  Project its source coordinates.  Thus the decoder is
\[D(x,z)=x\quad(z=0),\qquad D(x,z)=\bar x\quad(z=1).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact ledger]
For $m\ge1$, $T(F)$ has
\[
\boxed{N=n+3m-1\text{ variables},\qquad M=4m-2\text{ clauses}.}
\]
Each $u_i,e_i$ occurs twice; an endpoint $z_i$ occurs three times and an
internal $z_i$ five times.  Hence maximum auxiliary occurrence is five
(three when $m=2$, two when $m=1$).  Every source occurrence is preserved
exactly once.  All target clauses are proper.  Even repeated source clauses
produce distinct target clauses because their auxiliaries are fresh.
\end{corollary}

The encoder and both witness maps take $O((n+m)\log(n+m))$ bit time under the
explicit identifier encoding.  The ordered image is recognizable: from
$M\equiv2\pmod4$ recover $m=(M+2)/4$ and
$n=N-3m+1$, then check the two front clauses and equality pairs in order.
Off-image and malformed strings are rejected; generic signed-NAE formulas are
not silently decoded as source instances.

With a 32-bit magic, 32-bit $N$, 32-bit $M$, and
$w=\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil=\lceil\log_2(n+3m)\rceil$ identifier bits, the
unpadded target encoding length is exactly
\[
\boxed{96+(4m-2)\,3(w+1)\text{ bits},}
\]
rounded up to the next byte with zero padding.

\section{SEPARATOR AUDIT}
The chain does not preserve an arbitrary source separator exactly.  If a
clause partition is a prefix/suffix split in encoder order, only one equality
link crosses.  Assigning its two clauses to either side introduces exactly one
additional shared gauge, so width $w$ becomes $w+1$.

For noncontiguous partitions, every alternation can cut another chain link.
Exact minimization on crafted sources gave
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{cccc}
\toprule
source width & clause order & minimum target width & increase\\
\midrule
6 & grouped & 7 & 1\\
6 & alternating & 12 & 6\\
8 & grouped & 9 & 1\\
8 & alternating & 16 & 8\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thus ``six/eight are preserved'' is \RET\ for this chain.  The useful repair is
sharply delimited: an encoder may order clauses according to a declared
decomposition and pays one extra boundary gauge.  A decomposition-oblivious
constant-additive guarantee needs a different equality network and proof.
