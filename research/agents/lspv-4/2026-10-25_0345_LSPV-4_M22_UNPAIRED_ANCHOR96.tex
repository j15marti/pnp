\section{Target and three falsifiable mechanisms}
Define \(\CM\) as the set of CNFs whose clauses are unique, each has three distinct variables, each is entirely positive or entirely negative, and each active variable appears exactly twice with either sign. Thus \(k=n(F)\) original active variables, \(m=4k/3\) clauses, and \(m_+=m_-=2k/3\). Let \(\bar C\) reverse every literal sign while retaining OR. Set \(\mathcal P_{\rm pair}=\{F\in\CM:\forall C\in F,\bar C\in F\}\), \(\CS=\CM\setminus\mathcal P_{\rm pair}\), and \(L_\star=\{\operatorname{enc}(F):F\in\CS,\ F\in\SAT\}\), rejecting malformed encodings. Partial complementary pairs are permitted; no connectedness, minimal UNSAT or every-instance hardness is imposed.

M1 proposes that checking all complement partners is required to reduce \(\CM\) to \(\CS\). M2 proposes that \(\CS\) forces a positive fraction of unpaired clauses on SAT or UNSAT. M3 proposes that NP-completeness automatically transfers the published fine-grained ordinary-SAT FLMV forbidden box. The first two are falsified by exact constructions below; the third is unsupported without additional model-matched transport.

Define unsigned triple lists

and the six-variable CNFs \(H=P^+\cup P^-\), \(Y=P^+\cup Q^-\). Here each triple means an OR-clause with the indicated common polarity. \begin{theorem}[No-test unpaired anchor]\label{th:anchor}
For every valid \(F\in\CM\) on \(k\) variables, shift the six variables of \(Y\) to fresh indices and define
\[
\mathfrak A(F)=F\dot\cup Y.
\]
Without testing whether \(F\) is paired, \(\mathfrak A(F)\in\CS\), and
\[
\SAT(\mathfrak A(F))=\SAT(F),\qquad
\#\SAT(\mathfrak A(F))=20\,\#\SAT(F).
\]
A source witness extends with \(111000\); any target witness projects to a source witness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Disjoint supports preserve the unique clause grammar and every old occurrence count. The six fresh variables inherit \(Y\)'s exact quotas. Its eight unpaired clauses remain unpaired irrespective of the source's complementary pairs. Every source model combines with exactly 20 models of \(Y\), while an UNSAT source has no target model.
\end{proof}

\section{Matched vanishing-defect SAT and UNSAT family}
For each integer \(p\ge0\), using disjoint fresh variable labels and a fixed block order, set
\[
A_p=H^{\dot\cup(p+33)}\dot\cup Y,\qquad
B_p=U\dot\cup H^{\dot\cup p}\dot\cup Y.
\]
Let \(D(F)=|\{C\in F:\bar C\notin F\}|\), which counts unpaired \emph{clauses}, not pairs.
\begin{theorem}[Matched length, opposite labels and vanishing defect]\label{th:matched}
Both \(A_p,B_p\in\CS\), and they share
\[
k=6p+204,\quad m=8p+272,\quad m_+=m_-=4p+136
\]
and the same full encoded length for any fixed-width layout with these headers and clause counts. Their model counts and unpaired-clause numbers are
\[
\#\SAT(A_p)=20\cdot18^{p+33}>0,\quad
\#\SAT(B_p)=0,\quad D(A_p)=8,\quad D(B_p)=272.
\]
Consequently
\[
D(A_p)/m=1/(p+34)\to0,\qquad
D(B_p)/m=34/(p+34)\to0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The 33 copies of \(H\) have \(198\) variables and \(264\) clauses, matching \(U\). The paired \(H\) blocks contribute zero defects; \(Y\) contributes eight and the original \(U\) contributes 264 as verified from its records. Different blocks use disjoint variables, so no new cross-block complementary pair can appear. Model counts multiply, and D\"ocker's \(U\) makes \(B_p\) UNSAT.
\end{proof}
These are easy \emph{recognizable islands}: one uniform input-reading, block-template checker distinguishes their labels with \(O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)\) elementary work and \(O(\log N)\) writable/index bits, provided it falls back to a paid exact general solver outside the checked grammar. A nonzero defect count or a defect-density premise is not an all-decider hardness certificate. The construction does not say all low-defect inputs are easy.

\section{Exact dense encoding, malformed inputs and bit-local reduction}
Fix an ordered-clause explicit encoding with \(w=\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil\), self-delimiting prefix \(1^w0\), a \(w\)-bit \(k\) field, a \((w+1)\)-bit \(m\) field and three ascending signed literal records per clause, each with one sign bit and \(w\) ID bits. On a valid \(\CM\) formula,
\[
N=3w+2+3m(w+1).
\]
The anchored output has \(k'=k+6\), \(m'=m+8\), \(w'=\lceil\log_2(k+7)\rceil\). Because valid \(k\ge6\), \(w'-w\in\{0,1\}\) and
\[
N'-N=3(m+1)(w'-w)+24(w'+1)=O(k+\log k),\qquad
N'=N(1+O(1/\log(k+2)))=N^{1+o(1)}.
\]
No source variable is eliminated. The six new variables are independent of the source; on satisfying inputs they have 20 legal joint assignments, and are not functional auxiliaries.

On a \emph{valid-source promise}, a streaming encoder charges \(N^{1+o(1)}\) bit work and \(O(\log N)\) writable/index space. A virtual accessor calculates any output position using \(\operatorname{polylog}N\) bit work, \(O(\log N)\) live bits and at most one source literal-field bit, including reformatting when \(w'\ne w\). It does not silently store an \(N'\)-bit target input on a writable tape. A \emph{total} many-one language reduction first validates the entire input grammar (arity, literal polarity, exact quotas, uniqueness, header and no trailing fields), outputting the fixed malformed one-bit string \(0\) on failure. A deterministic nested-scan validator has \(O(N^2\operatorname{polylog}N)\) bit work, \(O(\log N)\) live space. Thus \(L_\star\) is NP-complete by reduction from D\"ocker's \(\CM\) language and direct NP verification. A faster, bit-locally addressable total validator has \emph{not} been established here.

If one fixed uniform total \(L_\star\) decider has simultaneous time \(N^{t+o(1)}\) and writable/index space \(N^{s+o(1)}\) under the matched random-access read-only-source model, a paid virtual-source simulation yields a \(\CM\) decider with the conservative \emph{joint} bounds
\[
T_{\CM}(N)\le N^{\max\{2,t\}+o(1)},\qquad
S_{\CM}(N)\le N^{s+o(1)}+\operatorname{polylog}N.
\]
Under the valid-source promise only, \(2\) may be replaced by \(1\). This is a \(\CM\)-to-\(\CS\) reduction, not an ordinary-SAT-to-\(\CS\) local reduction. D\"ocker's NP-completeness does not itself preserve the FLMV ordinary-SAT time-space endpoint. In particular, a \(\Theta(N')\)-bit writable child buffer would spoil a sublinear-space claim.

\section{State-adaptive ledger and two proof spines}
For canonical \(A_p,B_p\), the pre-step state contains an unvalidated indexed read-only input, current address and \(O(\log N)\)-bit counters. Acquisition parses headers, extracts \(p\), and validates each expected \(H,U,Y\) signed record, charging source reads, source seeks, address arithmetic, comparisons, policy selection and failed recognitions. Alternatives considered are SCC, Horn, affine elimination, decomposed solving and exact general fallback, not a mandatory finite menu for all algorithms. On successful recognition the new state contains verified block identity and a one-bit semantic label derived from a known exact theorem; it outputs that bit after \(O(1)\) further operations. Correctness is exact equality to the verified template. The restricted work bound is \(O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)\), peak simultaneous writable/index bits \(O(\log N)\), and cumulative bit-time at most \(O(N\operatorname{polylog}^2N)\). Actual writable-bit flips are a distinct activity metric, not a substitute for paid work; there is no free semantic output toggle, hidden source copy, cache, child buffer or ancestor.

At this checkpoint \(T_A(F)=C_A(F,R)+T_{\rm finish,A}(F,R)\), with \(C_A=O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)\) and \(T_{\rm finish,A}=O(1)\) for the \emph{restricted template policy}. After a complete decision, a declared solved-state convention can set a single residual \(r_1=0\), \(\Psi=1\), \(R_{\rm search}=k\), but this is not a mandatory-enumeration lower bound and \(R_{\rm raw}\) need not be defined. For genuine enumerated residual lists \(\Psi=\sum_j 2^{r_j}\), \(R=k-\log_2\Psi\), and \(T_{\rm model}=C+2^{k-R}\); for \(C>0\),
\[
\max\{\log_2 C,k-R\}\le\log_2T_{\rm model}\le\max\{\log_2 C,k-R\}+1.

Distinct child labels and witness compatibility cannot be assumed. Pinning a member of \(\CS\) generally breaks exact occurrence quotas; a target-only recursive solver requires a paid valid adapter.
