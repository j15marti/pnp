\section{Source occurrences and complement invariants}
An occurrence $x_{i,j}$ is variable $x_i$ at source clause index $j$, with sign $\sigma_{ij}\in\{\pm1\}$ and preferred value $b_{ij}=(1+\sigma_{ij})/2$; it is not a new Boolean variable. Its status under a partial assignment is true, false, or unset. For a full assignment $a$, let $N_h(a)$ count ternary source clauses with $h$ true literal occurrences, $E=N_0+N_3$, and $D=N_3-N_0$. In every proper strict signed exact-$(2,2)$ source with $n$ variables, $m=4n/3$ and exactly $2n=3m/2$ literal occurrences are true under every complete assignment. Therefore
\[
N_h(\bar a)=N_{3-h}(a),\quad N_2-N_1=-3D,\quad
N_0=\frac{E-D}{2},\ N_3=\frac{E+D}{2},
\]
\[
N_1=\frac{m-E+3D}{2},\ N_2=\frac{m-E-3D}{2},
\qquad |D|\le E,\quad E+3|D|\le m.
\]
In particular $\min(N_0(a),N_0(\bar a))=(E-|D|)/2$.
$E$ is the number of NAE-bad source clauses and is invariant under complementation; $D$ changes sign. They are diagnostics on tested assignments, not an answer-finding oracle.

\section{BUILD: exact source-faithful strict22 theorem}
\begin{theorem}\label{th:strict}
For each integer $r\ge3$, with indices modulo $r$, set
\[
F_r=\bigwedge_{i=0}^{r-1}
(a_i\vee u_i\vee v_i)\land(a_i\vee u_{i+1}\vee v_i)
\land(\neg a_i\vee\neg u_i\vee\neg v_i)
\land(\neg a_i\vee\neg u_{i+1}\vee\neg v_i).
\]
Then $F_r$ is connected, proper, strict exact-$(2,2)$, with $n=3r,m=4r$,
\[
\#\mathrm{SAT}(F_r)=5^r+1,\qquad
Q_j(F_r)=\frac{5^{r-1}-1}{2}\quad\text{for every source clause }j.
\]
The exactly $4n=12r$ opposite-sign source occurrence pairs generate $10r$ tautological and $2r$ distinct non-tautological width-four resolvents. Their additive unrestricted-assignment cube weights sum to $r/8$, but their exact union density is
\[
U_r=1-\frac{\lambda_+^r+\lambda_-^r+1}{4^r},
\qquad
\lambda_\pm=\frac{3\pm\sqrt{17}}2,\qquad
U_r\longrightarrow1.
\]
All $2r$ non-tautological resolvents are non-merge. Adding the entire first-generation set to the retained source does not alter the fixed point or conflict status of saturated UP at any partial assignment. The original source has an explicit satisfying witness $u_i=1,v_i=0$ with arbitrary $a_i$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every $a_i,v_i$ appears twice positively and twice negatively in its local block; each $u_i$ occurs in two neighboring blocks with both polarities, establishing the exact signed counts and connectedness. Fix all $u_i$. The choices $(a_i,v_i)=01,10$ always satisfy the four clauses. Choice $00$ additionally requires $u_i=u_{i+1}=1$; choice $11$ requires $u_i=u_{i+1}=0$. Hence the local count is $3$ for equal neighboring $u$ values and $2$ otherwise. The $2\times2$ cyclic transfer matrix $K=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3&2\\2&3\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has eigenvalues $5,1$, so $\operatorname{tr}(K^r)=5^r+1$. To falsify exactly one specified source clause and satisfy all others, its two adjacent $u$ bits must be opposite, while all other blocks are unrestricted subject to $F_r$; the exact count is the off-diagonal entry $(K^{r-1})_{0,1}=(5^{r-1}-1)/2$. Complement symmetry handles positive versus negative clauses.

Pivoting on $a_i$ or $v_i$ produces tautologies because the corresponding opposing parents retain $v_i,\neg v_i$ or $a_i,\neg a_i$. Pivot $u_i$ produces two tautologies and two distinct non-tautological four-literal clauses. These latter clauses exclude exactly the two directed adjacent block transitions $00\to11$ and $11\to00$ among the four states $(a_i,v_i)\in\{00,11,01,10\}$. Every such resolvent has no shared nonpivot parent literal, hence is non-merge. The four-state transition matrix $M$ has all entries $1$ except those two transitions, eigenvalues $\lambda_+,\lambda_-,1,0$. The exact number of $r$-cycles avoiding all non-tautological cubes is $\operatorname{tr}(M^r)=\lambda_+^r+\lambda_-^r+1$. The independent $u$ bits cancel upon normalization by $2^{3r}$, proving $U_r$. For fixed-point source UP, a false non-merge resolvent forces opposing pivot values in its parents and a unit non-merge resolvent's sole unset literal is already forced by its parents. Thus no new unit or conflict is available; applying this to all pairs preserves the whole UP closure. Finally $u_i=1,v_i=0$ satisfies every clause for either value of $a_i$.
\end{proof}
