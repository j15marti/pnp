\begin{theorem}[P-complete one-node validity, conditional grammar]
Fix the SAT source $P=(x)$. For a zero-external-input circuit $C$, deciding whether the proposed single inference $P\Rightarrow C$ is valid is P-complete under logspace reductions, although $N=O(1)$, charged node count $q=1$, and conclusion external support zero.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because $(x)$ is satisfiable, it implies $C$ precisely when the closed circuit outputs $1$. A CVP instance with fixed input assignment is mapped in logspace to a closed circuit by replacing its input gates with the assigned constants; all later gates and output are unchanged. Ladner (1975) established CVP P-complete. Conversely the candidate is checked in polynomial time by topological evaluation and grammar validation. Eight external truth-table rows do not make a row's circuit evaluation constant work.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Raw output length not bounded by node count]
For each $g$ there is an accepted one-node conditional conversion of source length $N=O(1)$ whose circuit output description contains $g$ gates. Thus no function of $N+q$ bounds the supplied raw proof record length in $R_{\rm long}$. At least $\Omega(g)$ bits are needed, and a fixed-width pointer encoding of a chain containing $\Theta(g)$ binary gates uses $\Theta(g\log g)$ bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Encode a constant-$1$ circuit followed by $g-O(1)$ AND-with-$1$ gates and designate its final gate. Its output remains true, hence is entailed by $(x)$. All gate records are present although the source and unit-node count remain fixed. This refutes a universal raw-presentation bound, not existence of a shorter equivalent output and not the restricted canonical theorem.
\end{proof}
