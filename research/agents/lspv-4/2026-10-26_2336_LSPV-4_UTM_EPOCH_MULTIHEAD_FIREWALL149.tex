\section{Four falsifiable model bridges registered before source consultation}
The model is one \emph{fixed} deterministic finite-state universal interpreter
$U$ with $q$ states, a single two-way \emph{bounded} sequential read-only
input head, and $k\ge1$ fixed ordinary sequential read/write work heads/tapes.
The input is a read-only program prefix of at most $b$ bits followed by
the ordinary SAT source; all work tapes are initially blank. The input head
starts at the left end and cannot leave the two bounding endmarkers. Each
program $p$ is \emph{globally sound}: for every well-formed ordinary source
$F$, $U(p,F)$ accepting implies $\SAT(F)$. The program may be
\emph{incomplete} on SAT. Count, on the same accepting run, actual transition
time $T$, union of visited work cells $S$, actual changed work-cell symbols
$D$, and reversals $R$ of the sole input head (ignoring stays).
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{H1:} A no-change epoch can carry arbitrarily many crossing
events while work heads range over $S$ blank cells. \textbf{FALSIFIED} for
halting runs, by Lemma~\ref{lem:epoch}.
\item[] \textbf{H2:} Counting all $S$ visited cells as nonblank is necessary
when only $D$ actual symbol changes occur. \textbf{FALSIFIED}: a sparse
support upper bound gives a sharper information alphabet,
Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse}; positional head information remains charged.
\item[] \textbf{H3:} The time-product inequality follows from the
time-averaged crossing bound with no additional traversal check.
\textbf{INCOMPLETE AS AN INFERENCE}; Theorem~\ref{th:portfolio} explicitly
supplies the $\Theta(N)$ Bob-shore traversal for every accepted
nonzero diagonal, controlling the additive $+1$ crossing count.
\item[] \textbf{H4:} The same write/space face applies when the input
interface supplies two independently movable read-only heads or indexed
addresses. \textbf{FALSIFIED} by Theorem~\ref{th:twohead} on the exact
separated-disjointness promise.
\end{description}

\section{Finite no-change epoch and sparse-label theorems}
\begin{lemma}[Finite-epoch cap]\label{lem:epoch}
For every \emph{halting} execution of the declared $U$, let
$I=N+b+2$ bound read-only input-head positions and
$h=2S+3$ bound possible positions of each visited work head.
Then, with constants fixed by $U$,
\[
 \boxed{T\le (D+1)qIh^k+D.} \tag{2}
\]
At a specified input gap cut, crossings during any one epoch
between actual work-symbol changes number at most
$V(S)=2qh^k$; across the complete run they number at most
$\min\{R+1,(D+1)V(S)\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Partition the transitions into at most $D+1$ maximal intervals
without actual changes to any work symbol. Within an interval
the \emph{complete} work contents remain fixed; the mutable
state is entirely $(\text{control state},\text{bounded input-head
position},k\text{ work-head positions})$. This has at most
$qIh^k$ possibilities. Repetition before the next change or
halt would force deterministic repetition forever and contradict
the existence of a later change/halt. Charging at most $D$
separating change-transitions gives (2). At a particular cut,
the input-head position is fixed to an adjacent cell, and a
direction bit suffices to distinguish orientations; hence at
most $2qh^k$ crossing configurations in an epoch. Consecutive
crossings of a fixed cut alternate directions, so at most
$R+1$ occur over the entire run.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Sparse crossing alphabet]\label{lem:sparse}
For work alphabet size $g\ge2$, a full configuration label at
a fixed cut can be encoded from an alphabet of size at most
\[
 \boxed{A(S,D)=2qh^k
 \sum_{j=0}^{\min(D,kh)}\binom{kh}{j}(g-1)^j},\qquad h=2S+3.
 \tag{3}
\]
For the fixed interpreter and fixed $k,g,q$,
\[
 \log_2A(S,D)
 =O_U\!\left(\min\{S+1,(D+1)\log(S+2)\}\right).\tag{4}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each nonblank work cell must first have undergone a symbol
change from its initially blank value. Consequently at most
$D$ of at most $kh$ possible positions can be nonblank.
Select their support and their $(g-1)$ nonblank symbols,
then the $k$ head positions, control state and orientation.
The unrestricted-alphabet bound yields $O(S+1)$ bits because
$k,g$ are fixed; the sparse-support bound gives
$O((D+1)\log(S+2))$ bits. This uses total
\emph{visited} work cells for head-position capacity, even
when $D=0$. Neither program code nor source is stored in
the initially blank work tapes.
\end{proof}

\section{Sound-portfolio packing and the additive time repair}
\begin{theorem}[Finite same-run portfolio bound]\label{th:portfolio}
Suppose at most $2^{b+1}$ programs, each globally sound on
\emph{every} ordinary NO instance, cover every member of
$\mathcal F_m$ by accepting within the \emph{same per-run}
ceilings $(T,S,D,R)$. Define
\[
 V=2qh^k,\quad
 L=\min\{R+1,(D+1)V\},\quad
 L_T=\min\{\lfloor T/G\rfloor,L\}.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{2^m\le2^{b+1}A^{L+1}},\qquad
 \boxed{2^m\le2^{b+1}G A^{L_T+1}}. \tag{5}
\]
If $b\le\eta m$ for fixed $\eta<1$, $N=\Theta(m\log m)$,
and $G=\Theta(N)$, with $M=\min\{S+1,(D+1)\log(S+2)\}$,
the necessary asymptotic inequalities are
\[
 \boxed{(R+1)M=\Omega(N/\log N)},\quad
 \boxed{(D+1)(S+1)^kM=\Omega(N/\log N)},\quad
 \boxed{TM=\Omega(N^2/\log N)}. \tag{6}
\]
For polynomial ceilings $(T,S,D+1,R+1)
=(N^{t+o(1)},N^{s+o(1)},N^{d+o(1)},N^{r+o(1)})$
and \emph{fixed} $k$,
\[
 \boxed{r+\min(s,d)\ge1,\quad
 d+ks+\min(s,d)\ge1,\quad
 t+\min(s,d)\ge2.} \tag{7}
\]
Equality is not excluded by a strict lower-bound exponent
condition.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $p$ and cut $c$ in the common gap, record each
crossing's \emph{entire} work contents, every work-head
coordinate, control state and crossing direction. If distinct
accepted diagonal inputs had identical crossing transcripts,
splice the left execution half of one with the right execution
half of the other. Equality of every complete crossing label
makes the spliced run a valid accepting run for a mixed
formula. One orientation of the mixed formula is UNSAT,
contradicting global soundness of that \emph{same} program.
The histories are therefore injective for each $p,c$.
The number of histories of length at most $L$ is
$\sum_{\ell=0}^L A^\ell\le A^{L+1}$.
For each accepted diagonal choose a gap cut with at most
$\lfloor T/G\rfloor$ crossings, by summing crossings over the
$G$ cuts. There are at most $G$ choices. Counting the programs
gives (5). A fixed-cut count yields the first inequality
without the factor $G$.

For the time inequality, explicitly discharge an otherwise
hidden condition: for every accepted diagonal with $a\ne0^m$
choose $i$ with $a_i=1$. On the SAT diagonal the corresponding
Bob control has $b_i=0$. Flipping only that Bob control to $1$
produces an UNSAT source with \emph{identical} initial program
and Alice/fixed-gap cells. If the accepting computation never
visits the Bob-dependent region it would accept this NO
source, impossible. Thus every such accepting run must
traverse the entire $\Theta(N)$ fixed gap. Since the portfolio
covers $2^m-1$ nonzero diagonals, its common time ceiling
satisfies $T\ge G$. Taking logarithms in the second (5),
\[
  m-b-1-\log_2 G
  \le(\lfloor T/G\rfloor+1)\log_2 A
  \le 2T\log_2 A/G.
\]
As $b\le\eta m$ and $\log G=o(m)$, the left side is
$\Omega(m)$. Apply (4) to conclude the time inequality (6).
The first two follow directly from the first (5) with
$L\le R+1$ and $L\le(D+1)V$.
The polynomial-exponent consequences follow since
$m=N^{1-o(1)}$ and $Gm=N^{2-o(1)}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Extremely small-change specialization.}
At $D=0$, (6) gives
$(S+1)^k\log(S+2)=\Omega(N/\log N)$.
For $k=1$ this forces $S=\Omega(N/\log^2 N)$,
up to harmless constants. A blank work-head's \emph{position}
is a genuine counter. This is why $D=0$ alone cannot be
mistaken for constant-space finite automata.

\section{Explicit two-head countermodel and machine ledger}
\begin{counterexample}[The one-head face is not two-head invariant]
\label{th:twohead}
Consider the separated input promise $a\#X^{4m}\#b$,
with $|a|=|b|=m$ and the task to accept precisely when
$a\wedge b=0^m$. A deterministic finite-control machine
with \emph{two independent} read-only heads on the
\emph{same bounded} input scans head two to the first
Bob bit while head one waits at the first Alice bit,
then advances both heads synchronously, rejecting on
a pair $(1,1)$. It uses \emph{no work tape},
$S=D=R=0$, and exactly $T=N$ transitions in the declared
control schedule. Its time exponent is $t=1$.
Hence a claimed extension of (7) to this model
would falsely assert $1+0\ge2$ and $0\ge1$.
\end{counterexample}
\begin{proof}
A finite state tracks whether the advancing second
head has encountered the first and second separators.
After the second separator, the two heads scan
paired bits synchronously; the separators also permit
checking the common suffix length. The first head never
reverses, the second head never reverses, and no tape
symbol changes. One entire repositioning plus $m$
paired steps costs precisely the source length
$N=2m+4m+2$. The earlier splicing proof is inapplicable
because while the \emph{first} head stays on Alice the
\emph{second} head can simultaneously read Bob.
\end{proof}

For a fixed sequential multitape program with time $T_k$ and
visited work cells $S_k$, the conservative \emph{one-tape
simulation upper bound}, not an invariant lower bound, is
\[
 T_1=O(T_k(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2))),\quad
 P_1=O(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2)).
\]
For polynomial ceilings this gives
$t_1\le t_k+\max(1,s_k)$ and
$p_1\le\max(1,s_k)$, without a converse.
Hennie--Stearns's $O(T_k\log T_k)$ result concerns
\emph{two} tapes, not one. \cite{hs}

The UTM fixed $q$ must not be silently replaced by
a different native interpreter on each source.
If the program starts on writable tape, its initially
nonblank cells invalidate Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse}
unless initialized and charged. If the read-only input
head is allowed unbounded unmarked excursions, the
finite $I=N+b+2$ input-position factor in (2) is
unavailable; a separate simulation argument is needed.
A merely guessed SAT witness is not a \emph{sound
acceptor}; source-only Alice witnesses for W1 cannot
instantiate (5) without a paid verifier.
