\section{Claim card, model and three falsifiers}
Fix a proper 3-uniform clause-support hypergraph with $n$ named variables, $m$ named clauses, and $3m$ original incidence sockets $(v,C)$. Every variable has degree four and must have exactly two positive and two negative occurrences. Fix a supplied complete Boolean assignment $a$. Some literal signs are frozen, and all other source occurrence signs may be selected. We ask if there is a completed signing for which \emph{both} $a$ and its complement $\bar a$ satisfy every clause. Source support identities and clause metadata are unchanged. This problem is separate from deciding SAT of the original signing.
\par\noindent \textbf{M1 (PROVED/EXACT):} individually feasible variable and clause quotas may be globally incompatible. We give a seven-pin counterexample.
\par\noindent \textbf{M2 (PROVED/EXACT):} global compatibility has an integral circulation and complete two-family cut characterization, so it is decidable in polynomial time.
\par\noindent \textbf{M3 (FALSIFIED/RETIRED):} exponentially many possible signed completions impose exponential computation for this completion problem or for every original-input exact SAT decider. The first implication is false by flow; the second lacks any mandatory-repair bridge.
\section{Exact flow and cut theorem}
For every unfrozen occurrence socket let $x_e=1$ mean that its literal is chosen true under fixed $a$. This determines the sign uniquely. For variable $v$, let $p_v$ be its number of pinned-true sockets and $f_v$ its free-socket count. For clause $C$, let $t_C$ be its pinned-true count and $f_C$ its free count. Define
\[
R_v=2-p_v,\qquad L_C=\max(0,1-t_C),\qquad U_C=\min(f_C,2-t_C).
\]
Reject if any $R_v\notin[0,f_v]$ or $L_C>U_C$. The exact remaining constraints are
\[
\sum_{e\ni v,\ {\rm free}}x_e=R_v,\qquad
L_C\le\sum_{e\in C,\ {\rm free}}x_e\le U_C,\qquad x_e\in\{0,1\}. \tag{1}
\]
Clause bounds enforce one or two true literals under $a$, and consequently one or two under $\bar a$. Write $E_f$ for the free socket relation, $R(X),L(Y),U(Y)$ for subset sums, and $e_f(X,Y)=|E_f\cap(X\times Y)|$.
\begin{theorem}[Exact frozen antipodal cut criterion, PROVED/EXACT]
Assuming the local interval tests pass, the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] A quota-balanced signing completion satisfies both $a$ and $\bar a$.
\item[(ii)] The network with arcs $s\to v$ of exact flow $R_v$, $v\to C$ of capacity one for each free socket, $C\to t$ of lower/upper flow $[L_C,U_C]$, and $t\to s$ of exact flow $R(V)$ admits an integral feasible circulation.
\item[(iii)] For every $X\subseteq V$ and $Y\subseteq\mathcal C$ both hold:
\begin{align}
R(X)&\le e_f(X,\mathcal C\setminus Y)+U(Y), \tag{2}\\
L(Y)&\le R(V\setminus X)+e_f(X,Y). \tag{3}
\end{align}
\end{enumerate}
An integral completion or a violated cut can be found in polynomial time, without enumeration of the subsets.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conservation at every variable and clause in the network gives precisely (1), and conversely any feasible assignment to (1) gives a circulation. Integer arc lower and upper bounds produce integer feasible flow by the lower-bound transformation and max-flow integrality. Apply Hoffman's circulation theorem: for every cut, lower incoming flow cannot exceed upper outgoing capacity. When the cut contains $s$ but not $t$ with variable part $X$ and clause part $Y$, the return arc has incoming lower flow $R(V)$; outgoing capacity is $R(V\setminus X)+e_f(X,\mathcal C\setminus Y)+U(Y)$. This is exactly (2). The remaining three possibilities for membership of $s,t$ yield (2) or (3), possibly on the complemented cut. These exhaust every cut. Conversely all Hoffman inequalities therefore hold, and feasibility follows.
\end{proof}
With integer per-socket penalties for changing a preferred sign, integral min-cost circulation also obtains a minimum-Hamming two-witness completion. The one-witness frozen-sign flow was already available in MAIN103. Here the \emph{simultaneous upper bound} of two true literals per ternary clause is added. The procedure never chooses $a$.
\section{Concrete seven-pin nonlocal obstruction}
Let $n=6,m=8$ and use the simple unsigned supports
\[
C_0=012,\ C_1=034,\ C_2=135,\ C_3=245,\qquad
C_4=345,\ C_5=125,\ C_6=024,\ C_7=013.
\]
Fix $a=1^6$. Pin $(2,C_0)$ true. Pin $(0,C_1)$ true, $(0,C_6)$ false, $(0,C_7)$ false. Pin $(1,C_2)$ true, $(1,C_5)$ false, $(1,C_7)$ false. Every variable and clause individually passes the local capacity checks. Variables $0,1$ each have three frozen positions, of which exactly one is true, so each must set its sole free socket in $C_0$ true. Together with the third frozen-true socket, $C_0$ becomes all true, falsifying $C_0$ under $\bar a$. In (2), take $X=\{0,1\}$ and $Y=\{C_0\}$:
\[
R(X)=2,\quad e_f(X,\mathcal C\setminus Y)=0,\quad U(Y)=1.
\]
The source-readable cut witnesses the failed inequality $2\le1$. This is an exact coupled feasibility obstruction, \emph{not} a SAT UNSAT certificate on the original formula.
