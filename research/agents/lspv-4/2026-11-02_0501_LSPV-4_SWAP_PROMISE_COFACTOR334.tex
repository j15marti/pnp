\section{General truth identity for a signed-quota-preserving exchange}
Let $H_1,H_2$ be disjoint original-variable sources and select same-sign literals $u$ and $v$ from two original clauses $(u\vee A)$ and $(v\vee B)$, where $A,B$ are the disjunctions of the other two original signed literals. Let $R_1,R_2$ be the source conjunctions with precisely those clauses deleted. The original disjoint conjunction and its single swapped occurrence version are
\[
 P=R_1R_2(u\vee A)(v\vee B),\qquad
 Q=R_1R_2(v\vee A)(u\vee B).
\]
All products are Boolean AND; $u,v$ are \emph{literal truth values}, so this includes exchanging two negative literals. Direct truth tables give the pointwise identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xor}
 \boxed{P\oplus Q=R_1R_2(u\oplus v)(A\oplus B).}
\end{equation}
Indeed $u=v$ makes the original and exchanged clause products identical. If $u\ne v$, the two products reduce to $A$ and $B$ in opposite order, hence their XOR is $A\oplus B$. The untouched signed occurrences and the equal-sign exchange preserve each variable's two-plus/two-minus quota and each clause's monotonicity, distinctness and width. For the explicit gadget below duplicate clauses remain absent, and the exchanged source is connected.

\begin{theorem}[CoNP-complete promised one-exchange equivalence, and counting-equality hardness]\label{thm:promise}
Let $G$ range over \emph{all valid} canonical D\"ocker original formulas. On a name-disjoint source, construct
\[
 P_G=G\land Z_6^{(1)}\land Z_6^{(2)},\qquad
 Q_G=G\land (Z_6^{(1)}\land Z_6^{(2)})^{\rm swap}
\]
where \(\rm swap\) exchanges precisely one selected positive occurrence from each $Z_6$ copy, or two negative occurrences, without altering original clause IDs. Both outputs are valid original formulas, with the same original names, exact literal quotas, widths, clause counts, and private evaluator gate totals. Moreover both have the \emph{same SAT decision bit} for every $G$, but
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \#P_G&=324\,s,& \#Q_G&=318\,s,& \#(P_G\land Q_G)&=270\,s,\\
 \#(P_G\land\neg Q_G)&=54\,s,&
 \#(Q_G\land\neg P_G)&=48\,s,&s&=\#SAT(G).
\end{aligned}
\]
Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gate}
 \boxed{P_G\equiv Q_G\iff \#P_G=\#Q_G\iff G\in\mathrm{UNSAT}.}
\end{equation}
Under the explicit promise that both inputs are canonical and differ by exactly one same-sign cross-source occurrence exchange, deciding exact \emph{pointwise} equivalence is coNP-complete. Equality of total model counts is coNP-hard even on this narrower certified image; no classification of arbitrary counting-equality instances is claimed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Disjoint union of valid originals preserves width, sign quotas, and clause uniqueness under disjoint renaming. The source swap preserves those properties exactly. Every complete assignment to $G$ combines independently with the twelve gadget variables, so the five gadget model counts multiply by $s$. Since the positive factors $324$ and $318$ are nonzero, both formulas are satisfiable exactly if $G$ is satisfiable. The $54$ and $48$ strict symmetric-difference factors are nonzero; hence \eqref{eq:gate}. D\"ocker established NP-completeness of SAT on the valid target grammar \cite{docker2019}. Thus its complement reduces in polynomial time, on properly formed inputs, to pointwise equivalence of the pair. Formula inequivalence has a polynomial original-variable assignment witness: check the two original clause lists on that same assignment, so the promised equivalence problem lies in coNP. The reduction's distinguished pair and two positions are explicitly source-authenticated, so construction/recognition requires no SAT oracle. The model-count-equality claim is only hardness via the same reduction.
\end{proof}
