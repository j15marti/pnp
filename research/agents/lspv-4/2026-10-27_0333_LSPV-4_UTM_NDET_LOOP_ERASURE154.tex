\section{Proof of nondeterministic loop normalization}
\begin{lemma}[Cycle deletion preserves the accepting resource budget]
Let $\rho$ be a finite accepting path of a nondeterministic ordinary TM, with a fixed source/proof input and initially fixed work maps. If two complete configurations along $\rho$ coincide, deleting every transition between their occurrences leaves a valid accepting path with the same initial and final configurations. Its $T,S,D,R$ do not increase. Repeating this deletion yields a path having no repeated complete configuration.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The suffix transition chosen after the later occurrence is also enabled at the earlier occurrence, since its finite control, all head coordinates, all scanned symbols and the entire stored work maps are identical. The other suffix transitions then remain legal in the same order. Removed transitions cannot increase the number of moves, visited cells, or actual changes. For reversals, the nonzero source-move sign sequence after deletion is a subsequence of the original sign sequence; its number of changes cannot increase. The argument does \emph{not} delete a cycle based merely on equality of state and source coordinate while the work contents differ. Each deletion reduces finite path length, so it terminates.
\end{proof}
\begin{counterexample}[An arbitrary accepting path need not be epoch-short]
Consider a nondeterministic read-only machine that, on an accepted source, can bounce left and right across a fixed cut $j$ times with \emph{zero} work edits and unchanged work-head coordinates before taking an exit-to-accept transition. The branch chooser may take any $j$, but this choice history is not stored in $U$. Crossing labels repeat, and $j$ can exceed the finite no-change-epoch bound based on state/work-head positions. A loop-erased accepting path exits immediately. In the executable control $0\mid...\mid1$, $D=0$, $S=2$, $V=126$, the raw branch has 521 crossings and 531 transitions, while the normalized branch has one crossing and 11 transitions. Hence the unqualified \emph{every-path} epoch claim is false, but the \emph{existential bounded-path} theorem is not.
\end{counterexample}

\begin{lemma}[Epoch cap for a normalized accepting path]
For any cut $c$ and a cycle-free accepting path, put
\[
h=2S+3,\qquad V=2qh^k,\qquad
L=\min\{R+1,(D+1)V+D\}.
\tag{3}
\]
The path crosses $c$ at most $L$ times.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Actual work-symbol changes partition the chosen accepting path into at most $D+1$ epochs with constant complete work contents. Within a fixed epoch, any repeated directed crossing label (control state, crossing direction, $k$ work-head positions) is a repeated \emph{complete configuration}, because the source-head coordinate at cut $c$ is fixed by crossing direction. It contradicts loop normalization. There are at most $V$ such labels per epoch. Up to $D$ crossings can coincide with actual changes. Independently, crossings at a fixed cut alternate direction and require reversals between successive crossings, hence $R+1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Main nondeterministic edit-chronicle theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Existential-path extension of EDIT152]
For one fixed nondeterministic machine satisfying (2), with common \emph{simultaneous} ceilings $S,D,R,T$ on one selected accepting path for every SAT diagonal, define
\[
h=2S+3,\quad V=2qh^k,\quad C=1+V+khg,\quad
L=\min\{R+1,(D+1)V+D\}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{2^m\le C^{L+D+1}},\qquad
\boxed{2^m\le G C^{\lfloor T/G\rfloor+D+1}},\qquad
\boxed{2^m\le C^{(D+1)V+2D+1}}.
\tag{4}
\]
For polynomial ceilings $S+1\le N^{s+o(1)}$, $D+1\le N^{d+o(1)}$, $R+1\le N^{r+o(1)}$, $T\le N^{t+o(1)}$ in this one-source-head model, the restricted necessary exponent conditions remain
\[
\boxed{d+ks\ge1,\qquad \max(r,d)\ge1,\qquad
\max(t-1,d)\ge1.}
\tag{5}
\]
Strict violation is excluded; equality is not.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First apply cycle deletion to the accepting path chosen for each $a$. The cap remains valid by the first lemma. At any fixed interior source cut, construct a single chronological event word recording \emph{every} actual work-cell edit by tape index, coordinate and new symbol, and \emph{every} input-head cut crossing by direction, post-transition state and all work-head coordinates. Simultaneous edits have fixed tape-index order, before the crossing label. Replay the edits from initially blank tapes; at every crossing this reconstructs the full work contents. Therefore equal event words imply equal \emph{complete} crossing sequences.

If two different SAT diagonals had identical complete sequences, splice the left source half and local nondeterministic transition choices from one accepting computation with the right half and local choices from the other. Every transition remains locally permitted because complete configurations agree at crossings. At least one mixed source is UNSAT by (1), but the splice gives it an \emph{accepting} branch, contradicting the NO-side quantifier in (2). Thus the event words are injective even though different branches are selected for different SAT inputs.

Each word has at most $D+L$ events over an alphabet of at most $C$ labels, so at most $C^{D+L+1}$ possibilities. For the time inequality, in each accepted run some one of the $G$ fixed source-gap cuts has at most $\lfloor T/G\rfloor$ crossings; identify this cut with $\log G$ charged bits. This proves (4) using no determinant assumption on the choice mechanism. Taking logarithms, $m=\Theta(N/\log N)$, $\log C=O(\log N)$ and $V=O((S+1)^k)$ yield (5).
\end{proof}
