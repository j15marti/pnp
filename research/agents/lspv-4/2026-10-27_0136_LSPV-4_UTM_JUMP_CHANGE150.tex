\section{Corrected local-jump crossing theorem}
Let $U$ be one fixed interpreter with $q$ finite control states,
fixed finite input alphabet $\Sigma$, $k\ge1$ fixed work tapes,
work alphabet $g\ge2$ containing blank, $S$ total visited
work cells, $D$ total \emph{actual} work-cell symbol changes,
$R$ reversals of nonzero input moves, and $T$ transitions,
all measured on the \emph{same} halting execution.
The input-head position is bounded by the input's two endmarkers.
There is no free programmable-input random access, second source head,
reusable output tape, or preloaded nonblank work tape. A jump changes
input-head position by at most $J\ge1$ and is charged one or more
transitions (including actual bit-offset formation).

Put
\[
 h=2S+3,\qquad
 Z=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\min(D,kh)}\binom{kh}{\ell}(g-1)^\ell,
 \quad
 A_J=2q^2|\Sigma|^2(J+1)^2h^{2k}Z^2,
 \quad V_J=2q(J+1)h^k.
\]
These intentionally overcount possible local crossing labels.
$Z$ includes every permissible full work-content pattern with
at most $D$ nonblank visited cells.
$A_J$ includes the transition's orientation, source and landing
offsets relative to a fixed cut, its input symbols and full
work configurations \emph{before and after} the crossing.
The extra input-symbol entries allow an implementation whose
jump instruction reads its target symbol immediately.
Writing to any work cell and crossing a cut in the same
transition remains allowed and charged.

\begin{lemma}[Finite bounded-jump epochs]\label{lem:epoch}
The number of crossings of one fixed gap cut on a halting
execution obeys
\[
 L_J=\min\{R+1,(D+1)V_J+D\}.
\]
The total time additionally satisfies
$T\le (D+1)qIh^k+D$, where
$I=N+b+2$ bounds physical input positions (program included).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Break the run at actual work-symbol changing transitions.
There are at most $D+1$ no-change intervals and $D$
changing transitions. In an interval, work contents are
constant. At the cut, each crossing has a post-configuration
consisting of control state, input landing coordinate
within $J$ of the cut on its side, and all $k$ work-head
coordinates. Including crossing orientation bounds this by
$V_J$. Repetition of the same post-configuration before
the next change or halt forces a deterministic loop and
contradicts termination. Each change transition can cross
the cut at most once, contributing the additional $D$.
Crossings alternate direction, bounding them by $R+1$.
The same argument over the $I$ whole-source positions
gives the time ceiling (up to the separating $D$ steps).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Source- and destination-authenticated jump packing]
\label{th:packing}
Let the verified W1 exact signed-$(2,2)$ separated family
$F_m(a,b)$ have $N=\Theta(m\log m)$ physical source bits,
a common $G=\Theta(N)$ gap, and
$\SAT(F_m(a,b))\iff a\wedge b=0^m$.
Assume at most $2^{b+1}$ different programs, all globally
sound SAT acceptors on \emph{all ordinary NO sources},
collectively accept all $2^m$ diagonals $F_m(a,\bar a)$.
Each accepted run uses at most common ceilings $T,S,D,R,J$ on
one and the same fixed $U$. Then
\[
 \boxed{2^m\le 2^{b+1}A_J^{L_J+1}},\qquad
 \boxed{2^m\le 2^{b+1}G
 A_J^{\,1+\min\{L_J,\lfloor TJ/G\rfloor\}}}.       \tag{1}
\]
With $b\le\eta m$ for fixed $\eta<1$ and $1\le J=O(N)$,
define
$M=\min\{S+1,(D+1)\log(S+2)\}$. Then necessarily
\[
 \boxed{(R+1)(M+\log(J+1))=\Omega(m)},                 \tag{2}
\]
\[
 \boxed{(D+1)(J+1)(S+1)^k(M+\log(J+1))=\Omega(m)},   \tag{3}
\]
\[
 \boxed{TJ(M+\log(J+1))=\Omega(Gm)}.                  \tag{4}
\]
For $T=N^{t+o(1)},S=N^{s+o(1)},D+1=N^{d+o(1)},
R+1=N^{r+o(1)},J=N^{j+o(1)}$, $k$ fixed, and
$\mu=\min(d,s)$, the necessary exponent faces are
\[
 \boxed{r+\mu\ge1,\qquad d+j+ks+\mu\ge1,\qquad
 t+j+\mu\ge2}.                                     \tag{5}
\]
No equality case is excluded by these asymptotic statements.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each fixed program $p$ and gap cut $c$, record the
ordered list of all jumps traversing $c$ with labels as in
$A_J$. Every jump moves at most $J$ and reads no source
cells between its endpoint positions. If two distinct accepted
diagonals for the same $p$ have identical complete event lists,
splice the left subruns of one with the right subruns of the
other. The matched full pre/post work states and both input
endpoint symbols make the \emph{crossing transitions themselves}
reproducible, including a jump that reads its target during
the transition. The result accepts the two crossed inputs.
At least one crossed input is UNSAT, by disjointness of the
diagonal fooling family, contradicting global soundness.
Thus transcripts are injective for a fixed program/cut.

The epoch lemma bounds list length by $L_J$, yielding
the first inequality by counting all programs.
Each jump traverses at most $J$ fixed-gap cuts, so
the sum of crossing events over those $G$ cuts is at
most $TJ$. For each accepted diagonal select a gap
cut with at most $\lfloor TJ/G\rfloor$ crossings;
count the at most $G$ cut choices for the second
inequality. Finite alphabet $A_J\ge2$ bounds
lists of length at most $\ell$ by $A_J^{\ell+1}$.

An accepted diagonal with $a_i=1$ at some coordinate
cannot avoid Bob-controlled source cells: changing
$b_i=0$ to $1$ would otherwise preserve its entire
computation and yield an accepted UNSAT source.
The input head starts left of Alice, so visiting Bob
requires physical source displacement at least $G$.
Therefore the uniform time ceiling is $TJ\ge G$.
Since the portfolio covers all $2^m$ diagonals,
it covers a nonzero one. For $b\le\eta m$,
$m-b-O(\log G)=\Omega(m)$.
Since $\log A_J=O_U(M+\log(J+1))$ and
$1+\lfloor TJ/G\rfloor=O(TJ/G)$, the three
inequalities follow. Finally,
$N=\Theta(m\log m)$ and the logarithmic
factors are $N^{o(1)}$, proving (5).
\end{proof}

\section{Endpoint counterexample and write-free saturation}
\begin{counterexample}[Naive jump splicing fails]
A one-bit equality promise uses Alice bit $x$ and Bob's
two-bit representation $10$ for $y=0$, $01$ for $y=1$.
One charged input jump to Bob landing offset $x$
and acceptance exactly when the landing bit is $1$
accepts $x=y$ and rejects $x\ne y$.
On both accepted inputs $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$
the post-jump state, orientation and blank work tapes
can be identical. A conventional crossing label
\emph{without the landing coordinate} therefore
collides, although both crossed inputs are rejected.
Adding the landing coordinate separates the events.
This falsifies an unqualified use of adjacent-cell
crossing transcripts in a jump model.
\end{counterexample}

\begin{lemma}[No-write unary-head control]
On the abstract promise source
$\texttt{\^}a\#X^{4m}\#b\texttt{\$}$,
a deterministic finite-control machine with one
bounded sequential source head and ONE initially
blank work tape whose head can detect its permanent
left endmarker decides DISJ exactly using
$D=0$, $S=m+1$, $T=O(mN)$, $R=O(m)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Interpret work-head distance from the fixed work left
endmarker as the current coordinate $i$.
At each iteration start the input head at the input left
endmarker. Move the input head right and work head
left in lockstep until the work left endmarker,
then read Alice symbol $a_i$ (or terminate on $\#$).
Reverse this synchronous motion to restore the work
coordinate $i$. In finite control keep the one Alice
symbol while scanning to Bob's second separator.
Move the two heads synchronously again to read $b_i$.
Reject on $(1,1)$. Reverse the synchronous motion,
return the input head to its left endmarker, and
advance the work head one cell. No work symbol is
ever changed, and no binary index is stored.
The total source travel per loop is $O(N)$,
there are at most $m$ loops, and only $m+1$
work cells are visited. This is a promise-model
control, not an all-input proper-3CNF SAT decider.
\end{proof}
