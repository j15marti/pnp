\section{Exact width-renormalization certificate}
The input to this theorem is an \emph{upper certification rule}, not
a lower bound on optimal width. Let $N$ be a root source length,
let $n_i=N^{2^{-i}+o(1)}$ at each \emph{fixed} level $i$, and assume
one fixed grouped serializer \emph{can certify}
\[
 w_{i+1}\le3w_i+B,\qquad B=210,\qquad
 w_0\le\kappa_0\log_2N+o(\log N).
 \tag{1}
\]
The $w_i$ are nonnegative live source-name widths of declared
correctly typed event orders.  The existence of such a serializer
for \emph{all} ARC-selected ordinary children is a separate
\OPEN{} premise.

\begin{theorem}[Sixfold normalized certificate tax]\label{th:amplify}
For every fixed depth $d$, rule (1) supplies the width bound
\[
 w_d\le3^dw_0+\frac B2(3^d-1)
 \le 3^d\kappa_0\log_2N+o(\log N)+\frac B2(3^d-1).
 \tag{2}
\]
Relative to the \emph{actual} depth-$d$ child length,
\[
 \frac{w_d}{\log_2n_d}
 \le6^d\kappa_0+o(1).
 \tag{3}
\]
Consequently, with a fixed strict target $\kappa_\star>0$,
this recurrence by itself certifies
$w_i<(\kappa_\star-o(1))\log_2n_i$ through every
fixed level $i\le d$ whenever
\[
 \boxed{\ \kappa_0<\kappa_\star/6^d\ }. \tag{4}
\]
The factor $6^d$ is sharp for the \emph{abstract recurrence}
when its inequalities are saturated; it is not shown necessary
for any real M22 compiler, which may exploit a better order.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct in (1) and sum the geometric series:
$B(1+3+\cdots+3^{d-1})=(B/2)(3^d-1)$.
For fixed $d$, $\log_2n_d=(2^{-d}+o(1))\log_2N$.
Division yields (3); strict slack in (4) absorbs the fixed
additive constant and all uniform $o(1)$ losses.
Since $6^i$ is increasing, the depth-$d$ inequality
suffices at the earlier levels.  The abstract sequence
$w_i=3^iw_0+(B/2)(3^i-1)$ saturates (1),
establishing certificate-level sharpness.
An independently found order with smaller actual width
does not need to follow this sequence.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Instantiating the published FLMV-facing margin.}
The M22 conditional deepening profile requires
$\kappa_\star=41/800$ because
$1+4\kappa_\star=241/200$.
The representative root $\kappa_0=1/20$ is
strictly admissible at level zero, but the recurrence gives
a \emph{certified upper ratio} $6\kappa_0=3/10$ at
level one and $36\kappa_0=9/5$ at level two.
These are bounds, not observed widths.  To \emph{deduce}
two-level eligibility solely from (1), one instead needs
\[
 \kappa_0<\frac{41}{28800}\approx0.00142361 .
\]
Thus the current tripling guarantee cannot be substituted
for hereditary $\kappa_\star$ without a stronger width/order
lemma or a quantitatively narrower root domain.

\begin{corollary}[Growing-depth gap in the certificate]\label{cor:depth}
The implication (4) supplies no \emph{fixed positive}
$\kappa_0$ that certifies arbitrarily many levels.
Moreover at depth $D=\Theta(\log\log N)$ the additive
term in (2) can be $3^D=(\log N)^{\Theta(1)}$,
potentially exceeding $\log n_D$ by a power,
even when $w_0=O(1)$.
This is failure of an \emph{upper-bound argument},
not an assertion that every actual generated child
has that width or that SAT requires those resources.
\end{corollary}

\section{Every bit of an eager boundary schedule: a conditional envelope}
Suppose, additionally and \emph{only} for a fixed-depth
declared boundary-enumeration architecture, that each
level-$i$ parent of length $n_i$ launches at most
\[
 q_i\le n_i^{1/2+o(1)}2^{2w_i}
\]
ordinary child calls of size at most $n_i^{1/2+o(1)}$,
the previous width certificate is the only quantitative
width information, and all requested children are
eagerly serialized sequentially.  For fixed $d$ the
worst-case accounting \emph{upper envelopes} are
\[
 \begin{split}
 \log_N q_i&\le 2^{-(i+1)}+2\kappa_0\,3^i+o(1),\\
 P_d:=\log_N(\text{number of depth-$d$ children})
 &\le 1-2^{-d}+\kappa_0(3^d-1)+o(1),\\
 W_d:=\log_N(\text{all depth-$d$ child bits written})
 &\le \boxed{1+\kappa_0(3^d-1)+o(1)} .
 \tag{5}
 \end{split}
\]
The same outer bound holds for the number of \emph{ordinary child
input-body bytes emitted} after fixed-factor encoding conventions.
The actual implementation additionally pays changed and unchanged
writes, all source and work/index reads, arithmetic, tests,
headers, named compatibility joins, aborted policies,
cache/replay misses, result retention, and guest execution.
If a single surviving child buffer is held, peak space
includes its $n_{i+1}$ bits simultaneously with producer
and guest index/work tapes, ancestor frames and selectors.
Retaining every depth-$d$ result instead requires an
additional $\log_N(\text{population})$ memory channel.

\begin{proof}
At a node at depth $i$, substitute (2) and
$n_i=N^{2^{-i}+o(1)}$ into $q_i$.
Multiplying child counts at all preceding levels adds
the query exponents; the geometric sums yield
\[
 \sum_{i<d}2^{-(i+1)}=1-2^{-d},\quad
 \sum_{i<d}2\kappa_0 3^i=\kappa_0(3^d-1).
\]
Writing at most $n_d=N^{2^{-d}+o(1)}$ ordinary
body bits per depth-$d$ child adds $2^{-d}$,
establishing (5).  Fixed-depth $B$ overhead costs only
a fixed multiplicative factor
$2^{B\{(3^d-1)/2-d\}}$, hidden in $N^{o(1)}$.
When $d$ grows with $N$, that factor may be
superpolynomial and cannot be suppressed as a
uniform $N^{o(1)}$ factor.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Numerical certificate audit.}
With $\kappa_0=1/20$, (5) gives eager body-writing
exponent $W_1=11/10$ at the first level but
$W_2=7/5$ at the second.
The latter exceeds $c_\star=241/200$.
\emph{It is not a lower bound on actual runtime:}
a loose worst-case upper bound of $N^{7/5}$ simply
fails to \emph{certify} a better actual execution.
A separate proof of pruning, a tighter order, fewer
selected children, streaming fusion, or shared generation
could establish a smaller upper bound.
Conversely, no strict-box implication may omit this
unresolved factor and silently reuse the first-level margin.

\paragraph{Space and distinct resource channels.}
Equation (5) is a count of \emph{required writes only for the
specified eager work schedule} under its actual child
population, not a universal lower bound on SAT.
One-child eager depth-first space is bounded by the
maximum of guest $n_d^{s+o(1)}$ work/index cells,
the largest extant ordinary child buffer, live ancestors,
producer state, authentication/validation and join state.
The worst-case upper bound on that peak is not the sum
of all emitted bits; ordinary bit-time, logical bit
operations, real bit flips, physical switches,
transistors, wires and electrical energy remain
separate metrics.  The JavaScript experiment below
measures logical records and output bits, not those
physical or FLMV machine-trace channels.

\section{A genuine two-level ordinary proper-3CNF ordering control}
For an integer $m\ge12$ let $w=4+\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor$.
The core $C_m^\pm$ has distinct spine names
$x_1,\dots,x_w$, private names $z_1,\dots,z_m$,
the $m$ proper clauses
\[
 (x_{1+(j\bmod w)}\lor
  x_{1+((j+1)\bmod w)}\lor z_{j+1})
 \quad (0\le j<m),
\]
and seven or eight sign-pattern clauses
on the \emph{same original} spine names $x_1,x_2,x_3$.
For the SAT core omit the all-negative clause from the
eight patterns; the all-true spine and all-true
private assignment satisfies it.  For the UNSAT core
include all eight patterns, which exclude every
assignment on the first three spine coordinates.
No clause repeats an unsigned variable.

Let $P_h$ contain $h$ independent positive
proper-ternary clauses on wholly fresh variables.
Define the \emph{actual parsed ordinary CNF} levels
\[
 D_0=C_m^\pm\ \dot\wedge\ P_{m^4},\qquad
 D_1=(C_m^\pm)^{\dot\wedge3}\ \dot\wedge\ P_{m^2},\qquad
 D_2=(C_m^\pm)^{\dot\wedge9}.
 \tag{6}
\]
All disjoint copies receive fresh, dense integer
names and every physical header is regenerated.
Each transition is a fixed deterministic source-family
parse/validate/remove-padding/clone algorithm
and \emph{preserves both SAT and UNSAT} because a
disjoint conjunction of a positive number of identical
cores has the original core's status.
Complete encoded bit lengths satisfy
\[
 |D_0|=\Theta(m^4\log m),\quad
 |D_1|=\Theta(m^2\log m),\quad
 |D_2|=\Theta(m\log m).
\]
The two ordinary child powers are therefore
$1/2+o(1)$ and $1/2+o(1)$.

\begin{theorem}[Chosen order inflation versus a paid alternative]
Open all spine names of all copies, then process one
proper clause at a time with its at most one fresh
private name, and close all spines at the end.
A valid exact scoped event order for the same
ordinary $D_i$ has peak live-name width
\[
 w_{\mathrm{sim}}(D_i)=3^iw+1\quad(i=0,1,2).
 \tag{7}
\]
If instead the disjoint source copies are streamed
\emph{sequentially}, closing each copy's spine names
before opening the next, the same $D_i$
has another valid exact event order with
\[
 w_{\mathrm{seq}}(D_i)=w+1 .
 \tag{8}
\]
Thus the factor-three inflation is an
\emph{order-specific genuine event resource},
not a lower bound on minimum scope width.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every core clause mentions only its own copy's
spine names and possibly one private name that
occurs in that clause alone.  The simultaneous
order keeps $3^iw$ distinct spines open and
temporarily opens exactly one $z_j$ for each
of the $m$ local clauses; the padding clauses,
processed afterward, open at most three names.
This gives (7).  Since the copies are disjoint,
processing all clauses of one copy before the next
does not change the ordinary CNF, the original
variable identities, any witness, or either SAT
label.  It keeps $w$ names plus one private name
open at a time.  The disjoint padding is processed
one clause at a time, giving (8).
\end{proof}

a $2^d$ shrinkage tax when width merely persists
and explicitly warned that descendant width might grow.
The present $6^d$ factor is \emph{new only for the
specific quantitative factor-three grouped

contradicts that prior $2^d$ lemma.
