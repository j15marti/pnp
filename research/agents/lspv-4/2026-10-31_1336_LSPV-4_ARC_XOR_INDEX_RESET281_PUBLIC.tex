\section{Exact operational theorem}
Write \(W_t\) for fixed-location binary ordinary work-cell vectors and \(I_t\) for all index tapes on the same fixed physical coordinate alignment. Each ordinary transition has at most \(q\) single-cell writes across all writable tapes and at most \(q_W\) single-cell writes on non-index work tapes alone. The indexed-move primitive may atomically erase \(e_t\) live index cells in addition to any ordinary local edit. For chronologically disjoint checkpoint intervals \([a_j,b_j]\), set
\[
D_{\rm all}=\sum_jd_H((W,I)_{a_j},(W,I)_{b_j}),\quad
D_W=\sum_jd_H(W_{a_j},W_{b_j}),\quad E=\sum_{t\in\cup_j[a_j,b_j)}e_t.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Index-erasure corrected Hamming accounting]
For \(T\) actual indexed-machine transitions in those intervals,
\[
\boxed{D_{\rm all}\le qT+E,\qquad
T\ge\max\{0,(D_{\rm all}-E)/q\},\qquad D_W\le q_WT.}
\]
The last bound is the valid ordinary work-buffer inequality. It does not charge virtual semantic clause states or binary \emph{encodings} of head positions as if they were tape cells.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the Hamming triangle inequality, one native transition changes at most \(q+e_t\) fixed-location cells; only \(q_W\) of these can be ordinary non-index work cells. Apply the triangle inequality along each interval, sum over disjoint intervals and rearrange.
\end{proof}
