\section{Three falsifiable mechanisms and scientific status}
\noindent\textbf{M1, PROVED/EXACT, pending independent red team.}
For \emph{every} proper exact signed-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF, maximum Hall deficiency equals $n/3=m/4$, independently of the support graph, sign placement and SAT label. Its unique maximizing clause subset is the entire source.
\par\noindent\textbf{M2, PROVED/EXACT, pending independent red team.}
Deleting any one original clause lowers maximum deficiency by exactly one. The canonical Horn-selector lift preserves the exact deficiency and also the uniqueness of the maximizer.
\par\noindent\textbf{M3, FALSIFIED/RETIRED.}
Within signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF, maximum Hall deficiency cannot discriminate SAT versus UNSAT, detect special signed interaction patterns, or supply mandatory per-instance continuation work. It is entirely fixed by the grammar counts. This does not disprove any universal \emph{worst-case} time lower bound.
\paragraph{Models and quantifiers.}
The fixed language is properly encoded exact signed-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF; every clause has three distinct variable identifiers, every original variable occurs twice positively and twice negatively, and malformed encodings are rejected. A deterministic decider must be uniform, total and exact for every input. Clauses use two header words and four fixed-width source words (arity and three signed IDs); for $n$ variables and $M$ clauses, $w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+M+1))\rceil$ and $N=(4M+2)w$ bits. RAM word probes cost one; on a bit tape reading one word is not free. All validation, parsing, source reads, failure paths, output writing, witnesses, reconstruction and peak workspace are charged. No decide-first compression or uncharged per-instance advice is allowed.

\section{Exact universal deficiency and uniqueness theorem}
Write $\nu(F)$ for the maximum clause-to-variable matching in the unsigned incidence graph, and
\[
\delta^*(F)=\max_{Q\subseteq F}\bigl(|Q|-|\operatorname{var}(Q)|\bigr)
=|F|-\nu(F).
\]
The second equality is Hall's deficiency theorem (\textbf{KNOWN THEOREM}).

\begin{theorem}[Regular deficiency saturation, PROVED/EXACT]
Let $F$ be any proper signed exactly-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF with $n$ variables and $m$ clauses. Then $n=3t$, $m=4t$ for an integer $t\ge1$, and
\[
\boxed{\nu(F)=n,\qquad \delta^*(F)=t=\frac n3=\frac m4.}
\]
The entire clause set is the unique subset attaining $\delta^*$.
For \emph{every} original clause $C$,
\[
\boxed{\delta^*(F\setminus\{C\})=t-1.}
\]
The statement holds on both SAT and UNSAT inputs and does not assume connectedness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every variable occurs four times and every clause has three distinct variables, so $3m=4n$.
For any subset $X$ of variables, its $4|X|$ incidence edges end at clauses each with at most three neighbors in $X$. Thus $|N(X)|\ge4|X|/3\ge|X|$, and Hall's theorem yields a matching saturating all $n$ variables. Since there are only $n$ variable endpoints, $\nu(F)=n$ and $\delta^*(F)=m-n=t$.

For any clause subset $Q$, write $V=\operatorname{var}(Q)$. Counting its literal occurrences gives
\[
3|Q|=\sum_{x\in V}\deg_Q(x)\le4|V|.
\]
Hence $|Q|-|V|\le |V|/3\le n/3=t$.
Equality requires $V$ to contain all source variables and $\deg_Q(x)=4$ for every $x$. As every source clause contains variables, this forces $Q=F$. Thus the maximizer is unique.
On deleting any single $C$, every variable retains at least three occurrences. The complete remaining set has $(m-1)-n=t-1$, so its maximum deficiency is at least $t-1$. Its subsets are all proper subsets of $F$ and, by uniqueness and integrality, have deficiency at most $t-1$. Equality follows.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{Implication for the earlier ladder.}
The equality $\delta^*=h/3$ for the connected exact-$(2,2)$ prism (where $h=n$ degree-four variables) is not a distinctive feature of its incidence geometry; the equality holds on the \emph{entire} exact-regular target class. The prism remains a valid easy-family hostile control. Previously attributing the saturation to its special structure would overstate what it shows.

\section{Exact incidence-boundary decomposition}
For an arbitrary proper 3-CNF whose variables have degree at most four, fix a clause subset $Q$ and $V=\operatorname{var}(Q)$. Define
\[
h_Q=|\{x\in V:\deg_F(x)=4\}|,\quad
s_Q=\sum_{\substack{x\in V\\\deg_F(x)\le3}}(3-\deg_F(x)),\quad
\beta(Q)=\sum_{x\in V}(\deg_F(x)-\deg_Q(x)).
\]
Here $\beta(Q)$ counts source incidences crossing from variables \emph{used by} $Q$ to clauses outside $Q$.
\begin{theorem}[Exact boundary penalty, PROVED/EXACT]
For every $Q\subseteq F$,
\[
\boxed{3\bigl(|Q|-|V|\bigr)=h_Q-s_Q-\beta(Q).}
\]
Consequently, if $\delta(Q)\ge a$ and $\beta(Q)\ge B$, then $h_Q\ge3a+B+s_Q$. The latter is an incidence-accounting condition, not a runtime lower bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since every source degree is at most four,
$\sum_{x\in V}\deg_F(x)=3|V|+h_Q-s_Q$.
Subtract the outside incidence count $\beta(Q)$ to obtain
$3|Q|=\sum_{x\in V}\deg_Q(x)=3|V|+h_Q-s_Q-\beta(Q)$.
\end{proof}
On an exact signed-$(2,2)$ instance all source degrees equal four, so $s_Q=0$ and
\[
3\delta(Q)=|V|-\beta(Q).
\]
Changing signs does not change any term. Incidence expansion bounds or source cut size may bound the deficiency of restricted subsets, but they cannot alone establish a hard one-bit SAT decision. This is an \textbf{EXACT OBJECT/THEOREM TRANSFER} from bipartite incidence counting. Turning the cut into a computation lower bound remains an \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}, defeated on affine, Horn and recognizable direct-decision controls.

\section{Horn-selector transport with uniqueness}
Let $\Phi$ be proper 3-CNF with $n_0$ original variables and $m$ clauses $D_j=(\ell_{j1}\vee\ell_{j2}\vee\ell_{j3})$. Introduce fresh selectors $s_{j1},s_{j2},s_{j3}$ and put
\[
H(\Phi)=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}
[(s_{j1}\vee s_{j2}\vee s_{j3})\wedge
\bigwedge_{a=1}^3(\neg s_{ja}\vee\ell_{ja})].
\]
The binary backbone is Horn; the $m$ positive genuine ternaries have disjoint variable supports. The source has $n_0$ original variables, the target has $3m$ existential selectors that are generally neither functional nor freely unconstrained, and there are no additional padding variables. For a fixed original assignment with $r_j$ true literals in clause $j$, the number of selector extensions is $\prod_j(2^{r_j}-1)$. Hence $H(\Phi)$ is SAT iff $\Phi$ is SAT and projection of a target witness recovers an original witness.

For any $\Phi$ the earlier independently documented identity is
\[
\delta^*(H(\Phi))=\delta^*(\Phi),\qquad
\nu(H(\Phi))=3m+\nu(\Phi).
\]
The prior proof takes an arbitrary target subset $Q$: only groups containing their positive ternary and all three implications can make a positive deficiency contribution; all incomplete groups contribute nonpositively. Therefore the target maximum is bounded by the maximum on a subset of complete source groups.
\begin{theorem}[Unique-maximizer transport, PROVED/EXACT]
If $\Phi$ is exact signed-$(2,2)$ then $H(\Phi)$ has unique maximal-deficiency clause subset $H(\Phi)$ itself, and
\[
\delta^*(H(\Phi))=\frac{n_0}{3}=\frac m4,\qquad
\nu(H(\Phi))=3m+n_0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The previous subset inequality says that any target subset of deficiency $n_0/3$ must contain all three implications and the positive ternary for every source clause in a source maximizing subset. The preceding theorem says that the only such source subset is $\Phi$ itself. Thus the only maximizing target subset is the full $H(\Phi)$.
\end{proof}
For exact signed-$(2,2)$ source, $m=4n_0/3$. Thus $H$ has $n_H=n_0+3m=5n_0$ variables, $m_2=3m$ Horn binaries, $k=m$ genuine ternaries, $M_H=4m=16n_0/3$ clauses, and $N_H=(16m+2)\lceil\log_2(64(n_H+M_H+1))\rceil$ bits. Canonical forward construction, image recognition, inverse source recovery, selector/witness reconstruction, and replay cost $O(n_0+m)$ word operations and $O(n_0+m)$ peak words. Explicit target construction writes $16m+2$ words. The inverse exists only for the checked Horn-image grammar, not for all formulas with a Horn backbone.

\section{SAT/UNSAT controls and reproducible audit}
The elementary signed-$(2,2)$ SAT control on $n=3,m=4$ is
\[
(x\vee y\vee z)\wedge(x\vee\neg y\vee\neg z)
\wedge(\neg x\vee y\vee\neg z)
\wedge(\neg x\vee\neg y\vee z).
\]
It has four satisfying assignments. Both the source and the selector target have the predicted Hall profile. The matched \emph{UNSAT} class is known to exist: Zhang--Peitl--Szeider (2024) establish a smallest unsatisfiable proper $(3,2,2)$ instance with 20 clauses and give an explicit matrix in their appendix.
