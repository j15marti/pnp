\section{Obstruction and theorem}
Only valid proper duplicate-free D\"ocker monotone 3-SAT-(2,2) originals are the target; malformed strings are rejected. Ordinary pinned residuals and Tseitin circuits are not canonical children. The obstacle is that a same-polarity clause-guard swap can preserve syntax yet erase source SAT semantics under existential projection. We quantify an unavoidable supply of such bad ports on \emph{every satisfiable canonical original}.

Let $F$ have $n$ variables, $m=4n/3$ monotone width-three clauses, and $s=\#SAT(F)$. For an original full assignment $a$, let $t_j(a)$ be the number of true literals of source clause $C_j$, and let $N_r(a)$ count clauses with $r$ true literals. For each of the $3m=4n$ source-occurrence ports $(j,k)$, write $C_j=(v_{jk}\vee B_{jk})$, with $v$ a signed original literal and $B$ the OR of the other two signed literals. Set
\[
W_{11}(j,k)=\#\{a:F(a)=1,\ v_{jk}(a)=1,\ B_{jk}(a)=1\}.
\]
This is identical to the earlier LSPV-4 336 $W_{11}$ defined through $H=F\setminus\{C_j\}$, since $v=B=1$ automatically satisfies the deleted clause.

\begin{theorem}[All-length canonical guard port-conservation]
Every complete assignment, even one that does not satisfy $F$, satisfies
$\sum_{j=1}^m t_j(a)=2n=3m/2$.
For a satisfying assignment $a$,
\[
N_2(a)+2N_3(a)=m/2,\qquad
2N_2(a)+3N_3(a)=m-N_3(a)\ge 3m/4.
\]
If $s>0$, then
\[
\boxed{\sum_{j=1}^m\sum_{k=1}^{3}W_{11}(j,k)
=\sum_{a:F(a)=1}(m-N_3(a))\ge ns.}
\]
At least $n$ of the $4n$ signed source-occurrence ports have $W_{11}>0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each variable contributes exactly two true original literal occurrences under either assigned value, giving $\sum_jt_j=2n$. A satisfying assignment has $N_0=0$ and $\sum_rN_r=m$, so $N_2+2N_3=m/2$ and $N_3\le m/4$. A clause with two true literals contributes exactly two distinguished-true-and-another-true ports; a clause with three contributes exactly three, and one with only one contributes zero. Thus a satisfying assignment contributes $2N_2+3N_3=m-N_3\ge3m/4=n$ port hits. Summing over models yields the identity. Since each $W_{11}\le s$, at least $n$ distinct ports have positive $W_{11}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Restricted single-swap consequence]
For a satisfiable canonical guard, at least one quarter of its ports are clause-erasing under the \emph{independent-guard, same-polarity, single-occurrence-swap architecture} of LSPV-4 336. If an independently certified satisfying witness is supplied, one source scan can identify at least $n$ of these ports; finding that witness is not assumed free.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The previous exact one-swap interface matrix is
\[
E=\begin{pmatrix}W_{11}&W_{01}+W_{11}\\
W_{10}+W_{11}&W_{00}+W_{01}+W_{10}+W_{11}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For every $W_{11}>0$ all four entries of $E$ are positive. Thus the original selected clause ceases to constrain the source assignment after existential projection of the independent guard. Each true distinguished literal with another true literal in its source clause is a witness to $W_{11}>0$. The preceding theorem guarantees at least $n$ such ports.
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Important quantifier limitation:} No universal theorem states that \emph{every} port of \emph{every} satisfiable canonical guard has $W_{11}>0$. Other connector architectures or guards may evade this restriction. An unrestricted SAT algorithm need not construct the interface.
