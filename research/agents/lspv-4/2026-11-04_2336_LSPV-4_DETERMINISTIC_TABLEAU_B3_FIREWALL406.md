## IDEA before SEARCH and falsifiable prediction

**Hypothesis:** For every *fixed-input rejecting* deterministic fan-in-two circuit `C`, a fully source-authenticated ordinary Resolution refutation of its **acceptance tableau** exists with at most `2q+1` inferences, where `q` is the number of gates. But that proof's sources are new transition/definition clauses, **not the original SAT formula F**. A source-retraction compiler that simply relabels the tableau's sources as clauses of F is unsound.

Predictions / falsifiers: (F1) true-output inputs also acquire a valid tableau contradiction; (F2) a wrong always-reject circuit on a SAT original still generates a short tableau contradiction; (F3) illegal parents, changed unit inputs, malformed sources and corrupted pivots evade the verifier; (F4) compressed/shared gate evaluation is confused with free physical computation or with an already legal native `R_mix341` proof of F. A genuine lifting must also prove, *in the same fixed calculus*, the machine-correctness/reflection assertion connecting original assignments to acceptance.

## ITERATE: exact theorem with different source language

**Theorem 406A, PROVED/EXACT (elementary, not new to the literature).** Fix any acyclic deterministic Boolean circuit C with m input bits, q distinctly named gates of fan-in at most 2, each gate given by a complete truth table, and output gate z. Fix any explicit m-bit input b. Define the **tableau CNF**

$$
T(C,b)=\left(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}\{u_i=b_i\}\right)
\land\left(\bigwedge_{g\in C}\mathrm{DefCNF}(g)\right)
\land(z),
$$

where each \(\mathrm{DefCNF}(g)\) consists of the at most four CNF clauses forbidding incorrect assignments to that gate and all source clauses have distinct authenticated source labels.

For **every C, b such that C(b)=0**, there is a source-labelled ordinary shared-DAG binary Resolution refutation \(\pi\) of \(T(C,b)\) with

$$
\boxed{|\pi|_{\mathrm{RES}}\le 2q+1.}
$$

**Proof.** The m input units are source axioms. Proceed through the gates in topological order. Assume the two predecessor values have been obtained as signed unit source/derived clauses. Select the gate-defining clause falsified by the actual two input values and the *incorrect* output value. This clause contains the correct signed output literal and at most two predecessor literals, each false under the corresponding known input unit. Resolve away those predecessor literals, using at most two genuine binary Resolution inferences. The remaining unit states the correct gate output. A gate of arity 0 contributes a unit source clause; arity 1 costs at most one inference. Sharing does not duplicate gates because each gate unit is derived at most once and reused. Finally, because the output is 0, resolve the derived \(\neg z\) against the asserted positive source clause \((z)\), deriving the empty clause with one inference. Every premise and pivot is explicit. Hence the upper bound. □

For fixed gates over \(m+q\) variable names, explicit bit-index encoding of the tableau and source-checked proof is \(O((m+q)\log(m+q))\) bits (up to constant factor per bounded-width gate), *relative to circuit size*. Proof discovery in this case is not needed: it is a deterministic forward evaluation. The construction is not a proof of **original F**.

**Source-type obstruction, exact:** The source leaves of \(\pi\) are `u_i=b_i`, `DefCNF(g)`, and `z`. They are not the signed original clauses of the SAT input F encoded by b. In particular the Boolean variables in C are input-description bits and gate variables, not assignment variables of F. Replacing the source leaves by references to F without an authenticated conversion is illegal. For a **fixed correct SAT decider A**, knowing metatheoretically that \(F\in\mathrm{SAT}\Rightarrow C_A(\mathrm{enc}(F))=1\) is not an R-derivation of that implication or of contradiction from F. On a rejecting input, the desired connection reduces to original UNSAT itself and cannot be inserted as a free reflection axiom.

**Proposition 406B, explicit falsifier to blind lifting.** Let F=(x), which is SAT. Let one fixed incorrect circuit output \(z=x_1\land\neg x_1\) on the input-encoding bit \(x_1\); it always rejects. Its tableau \(T(C_{bad},\mathrm{enc}(F))\) has a 3-inference ordinary Resolution refutation even though F is satisfiable. Thus the proposed rule `refutation of rejecting-program tableau => refutation of original F` is false **without a separate correctness/reflection premise**. The invalid circuit is used only to demonstrate failure of a proof-translation *rule*, not to pretend to be a correct all-input SAT decider.

This rejects a circular B3 route while preserving the legitimate existing fixed R_mix341 rules. The tableau proof belongs to R_mix341 because ordinary RES is a subsystem, but its **input is T(C,b), not F**.

## Finite experiment, two independent implementations and hostile controls

The producer is a deterministic no-import Python3 script and the independent referee a separately implemented no-import Node script. The single 19-gate evaluator C reads **eight** original-CNF clause-presence bits (the eight distinct non-tautological unit/binary clauses in variables x,y), computes four assignment-compatibility terms and ORs them. C is identical for **all 256 inputs**, not hardcoded separately for each SAT label. Exact brute-force ground truth: **95 SAT, 161 UNSAT**. All 161 rejecting tableaus received real, source-checked ordinary Resolution proofs, with **3,771 total inferences, maximum 27**, below the theorem's 39-node envelope. Each of 95 accepting tableaus had a verified satisfying gate assignment and no refutation.

The wrong always-reject circuit on SAT source `(x)` has 2 NOT/AND gates and a **3-node** tableau Resolution refutation, demonstrating source mismatch. The Python producer passed **780 assertion groups**, including six malformed source masks and five corrupted proof/source controls (wrong pivot, wrong conclusion, missing parent, changed input source, and modified defining facet). Independently authored Node verified **1,541 assertions**, reconstructed all 256 SAT labels and the two complete raw proof DAGs, and rejected three additional pivot/conclusion/reference mutations. The Python producer initially failed on an undefined local variable in the brute-force ground truth; that was corrected before final tests. ## Full resource accounting and asymptotic scope

Write \(m\) for description-input bits, \(q\) for circuit gates, \(M=O((m+q)\log(m+q))\) for explicit fixed-input tableau + proof bits. Circuit acquisition/unrolling from a fixed polynomial-time A, input parser and malformed-input handling, indexed reads and head operations, clause generation and facet-source authentication, gate-value computation, saved gate units, sharing, Resolution parent/pivot checking, proof construction/output, and final verification all have polynomial **bit-work** in \(m+q\) by direct bounded-width-array implementations. A deliberately conservative bound is \(O((m+q+2)^4)\) bit-time and \(O((m+q+2)^2)\) all-phase writable-space for a fully materialized generator, including temporary parse and output buffers. A verifier can use \(O(M^3)\) bit time with \(O(M)\) writable-space by buffering; rescanning the immutable Resolution certificate permits \(O(\log M)\) scratch but does not make produced output free. These are model-declared upper bounds, not measurements of the FLMV indexed multitape machine.

A lazy recomputing schedule may revisit shared gate DAGs exponentially often; one topological scheduled evaluation uses q gate operations and retains \(O(q)\) output-value bits. Exponentially large unrolled circuits or tableaux are **allowed** but necessarily cost at least their full explicit written/read lengths under this implementation. Neither an ideal logical inference count nor final-state scratch bounds the **all-phase** peak: \(\rho\) includes preprocessing, unrolling, indexing, construction, output materialization, verifier and any guest/ancestor simultaneously live buffers. There is no inferred square-root-space FLMV contraction.
