\section{Exact end-to-end physical-index theorem}
FLMV Section 2.1 \cite{flmv} grants a single-step indexed move that erases all cells of its index tape. Fix a finite-tape machine, blank-as-zero binary symbol coding, and fixed physical tape locations. Let $Z_t$ contain the actual work and index tape \emph{cell contents}, not a symbolic binary rendering of head positions or read-only input. Output is write-only and charged separately. Let $q$ bound direct non-erasure bit flips per native step, $Q$ their actual total, $E$ the number of live index one-bits erased atomically throughout the run, and $B_0$ initial live index ones. For disjoint chronological checkpoint intervals $[a_j,b_j]$ let $D=\sum_jd_H(Z_{a_j},Z_{b_j})$.

\begin{theorem}[Amortized index erasure, for every actual machine trace]
If the entire run has $T$ native transitions, then
\[
\boxed{D\le Q+E\le 2Q+B_0\le 2qT+B_0},\qquad
T\ge\max\{0,(D-B_0)/(2q)\}.
\]
This counts initial preparation and is independent of the SAT or UNSAT answer. In particular $B_0=0$ for initially blank index tapes. No analogous inequality with coefficient one holds on arbitrary interior reset intervals.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Hamming triangle inequality bounds each transition by its directly changed cell bits plus the bits erased in the atomic index reset. For live index-bit potential $\Psi_t$, let $U$ be direct $0\to1$ index changes and $V$ direct $1\to0$ changes. Then $\Psi_T=B_0+U-V-E\ge0$, whence $E\le B_0+U\le B_0+Q$. Since $Q\le qT$, the result follows. For asymptotic sharpness of factor 2, write $h$ index bits in $h$ charged steps, erase all in one indexed jump, obtaining cumulative adjacent XOR $2h$ for $h+1$ transitions. A lone reset interval has XOR $h$ for one transition, demonstrating why its prior preparation cannot be omitted.
\end{proof}
This is a direct application of the known potential method to the exact FLMV erasure primitive, not a novel general SAT hardness theorem. The FLMV work-space measure also charges far indexed work-tape positions; head/index operations, input reads, output, child tapes, selection, resets, failed strategies and retained ancestors are separately recorded, not hidden in Hamming distance.
