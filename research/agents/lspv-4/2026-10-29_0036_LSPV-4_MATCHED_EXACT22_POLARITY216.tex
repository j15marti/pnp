\begin{abstract}
We independently reconstruct a known unsatisfiable connected, proper,
monotone exact-(2 positive,2 negative)-3CNF with 198 variables and 264
clauses, and give a uniform connected, proper, monotone satisfiable
exact-(2,2)-3CNF control with exactly the same parameters and the same
7192-bit canonical encoded length. The negative construction is from
D\"ocker (2019). Its proof reduces to two finite unsatisfiable cores and
a six-variable outer skeleton, all exhaustively independently checked.
This is a useful same-model SAT/UNSAT correctness regression pair.
It does not give a lower bound on unrestricted SAT algorithms.
\end{abstract}

\section{PROPOSE: falsifiable model bridges}
The current bottleneck is the absence of an authentic UNSAT instance in
earlier exact-(2,2) signed-control experiments, whose sampled examples
were all satisfiable. We test:
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{P1.} A connected proper, signed, exactly two-positive/two-negative
three-CNF can be unsatisfiable. We construct an explicit source-backed example.
\item[] \textbf{P2.} An equally sized connected proper, monotone, exact-(2,2)
formula can be satisfiable, without artificial padding. We give a uniform family.
\item[] \textbf{P3.} Neither matching syntax nor equal encoded input length
implies matching decision time on all algorithms. Both controls are
recognizable; no lower bound follows.
\end{description}

\section{BUILD: negative and positive controls}
\begin{lemma}[Source-backed negative control]
There exists a connected, proper, monotone three-CNF $U$ with
198 variables, 264 distinct clauses, and exactly two occurrences
of each sign for each variable, such that $U$ is unsatisfiable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The explicitly transcribed 42-clause gadget $M$ contains 13 clauses
which, if $x_3=x_4=0$, reduce to an unsatisfiable nine-variable
formula $Y$. The other clauses imply $x_3\vee x_4$ and, after
forcing $x_1=1,x_2=0$, reduce to an unsatisfiable 15-variable
formula $Z$. Exhaustive finite checks examine all $2^9=512$
assignments to $Y$ and all $2^{15}=32768$ assignments to $Z$.
The derivation is explicit in the accompanying reproducer:
from $(x_1\vee x_2)$ and $(\neg x_2\vee\neg x_3)$,
$(\neg x_2\vee\neg x_4)$, $x_2=1$ contradicts $Y$;
thus $x_2=0$ and $x_1=1$. The $Y$ implication gives
$x_3\vee x_4$, then the clauses on $x_3,x_4,x_5,x_6$
give $\neg x_5\vee\neg x_6$; the clauses on
$x_5,x_6,x_7,x_8$ imply $x_7\vee x_8$.
The remaining clauses imply the $Z$ contradiction.

Take six disjoint copies of $M$, complementing every literal in
copies four through six. Augment the first three binary clauses in
each copy with a prescribed outer literal. Every satisfying assignment
to a copy consequently satisfies its induced outer clause.
Together with two standalone outer constraints the resulting
eight clauses on $a,b,c,d,e,f$ are
\begin{align*}
&(\neg a\vee\neg d\vee\neg f),\quad(b\vee d\vee e),\quad(e\vee\neg b),\\
&(d\vee\neg c\vee\neg f),\quad(a\vee\neg c\vee\neg e),\quad(\neg e\vee c),\\
&(\neg d\vee a\vee b),\quad(\neg a\vee f).
\end{align*}
All $2^6=64$ outer assignments violate at least one of these.
Ten additional three-clauses fill literal-occurrence quotas without
deleting old constraints. The reproducer checks the exact 198/264
counts, two-occurrences-per-sign, properness, monotonicity, uniqueness,
and primal-graph connectedness. The result is UNSAT without any
enumeration of $2^{198}$ assignments.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform matched SAT control]
For every integer $r\ge5$ there is a uniform connected proper
monotone exact-(2,2)-3CNF with $n=3r$ variables and $4r$ clauses
that is satisfied by a period-three assignment.
At $r=66$ this has exactly $(n,m)=(198,264)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $j\in\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z$ use variables $x_{j,0},x_{j,1},x_{j,2}$
and add four clauses:
\begin{align*}
C_j^+&=(x_{j,0}\vee x_{j,1}\vee x_{j,2}),\\
D_j^+&=(x_{j,1}\vee x_{j,2}\vee x_{j+1,0}),\\
C_j^-&=(\neg x_{j,0}\vee\neg x_{j,1}\vee\neg x_{j+1,2}),\\
D_j^-&=(\neg x_{j,0}\vee\neg x_{j+1,1}\vee\neg x_{j+2,2}).
\end{align*}
Each variable occurs precisely once in each positive family and once
in each negative family. The $C_j^+$ clauses connect each block and
the $D_j^+$ clauses connect successive blocks. Distinct clauses are
proper and monotone for $r\ge5$. Assign all $x_{j,0}=x_{j,1}=1$
and $x_{j,2}=0$; all positive and negative clauses hold.
\end{proof}

Use a 32-bit variable-count header, a 32-bit clause-count header,
then for each of the $3m$ literals one sign bit and
$w=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$ index bits. At $n=198,m=264$,
$w=8$ and both formulas have precisely
\[
N=64+3(264)(1+8)=\boxed{7192}\text{ bits}.
\]
Their different clause scopes can be recognized; this pairing
certifies no equal runtime requirement on the two answer classes.

\section{Model-native accounting}
The unrestricted SAT algorithm is one program operating on both
answer classes, without any free SAT/UNSAT bit.
The uniform positive generator is a deterministic linear-clause
construction: a fixed-$k$ read-only-input multitape version runs in
$O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)$ time with $O(\log N)$ writable cells
when output is append-only. Retaining output as work costs
$\Theta(N)$ cells. A conservative one-tape simulation has
\[
T_1=O(T_k(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2))),\qquad
P_1=O(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2)),
\]
so $O(N^2\operatorname{polylog}N)$ time and $O(N)$ physical
footprint are construction upper bounds, not lower bounds.
No precise universal-interpreter exponent or RAM lower bound
follows from these tape simulations. The 198-variable negative
control is a fixed example, not an asymptotic hardness family.
