\section*{Exact source-indexed lemma}
For a proper signed original 3CNF $F=\bigwedge_{j=1}^m C_j$, let $d_x$ be the number of occurrences of variable $x$ in distinct clauses and let $R(F)$ count distinct unordered source clause pairs sharing some variable. Define $S_j$ as all three literals TRUE and $U_j$ as all three FALSE, not as ordinary disjunctive clause satisfaction. For two clauses $i,j$, let $P_{ij}$ mean a same-sign shared variable and $Q_{ij}$ an opposite-sign shared variable.
\begin{theorem}[Local extremes, exact incidence ledger, restricted]
The four-way compatibility vector is
\[
(SS,SU,US,UU)=(\neg Q_{ij},\neg P_{ij},\neg P_{ij},\neg Q_{ij}).
\]
The nondefault entries have a sparse index of size
\[
R(F)\le\sum_x\binom{d_x}{2}.
\]
For any selected set $J$ of original clause indices and modes $\tau:J\to\{S,U\}$, all local extremes can hold together if and only if every pair of selected modes is compatible. An incompatible pair and its shared source variable provide a complete contradictory-demand certificate. This is an invariant of local extremal demands, not a SAT or UNSAT certificate for $F$.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\textbf{Proof.} Each local extreme is a conjunction of unary variable equalities. Opposite equalities on a shared variable are inconsistent, with the parity of the two modes determining whether same or opposite signed occurrences conflict. Every inconsistent collection of unary equalities contains a conflicting pair. Each $x$ contributes at most $\binom{d_x}{2}$ candidate incidence pairs, and duplicates are merged while retaining both conflict flags and proof provenance. A direct demand dictionary checks a selected $J$ in $O(|J|)$ word operations with $O(|J|\log n)$ explicit retained bits. \hfill$\square$

\section*{Fully charged three-way cost comparison}
A dense four-mode table must emit $4\binom m2$ cells, hence $\Omega(m^2)$ time, even if the formula is Horn, affine, or trivially SAT. Storing such a table on counted work tapes additionally requires $\Omega(m^2)$ bits. An explicit occurrence-index and sparse pair dictionary take $O(3m+\sum_x d_x^2)$ expected word operations plus source scanning and may retain $O((3m+R)\log m)$ bits; deterministic balanced lookup pays an extra logarithmic factor. If $d_x\le\Delta=O(1)$, the pair-update count is $O(m)$, but the explicit map is still potentially near-input-sized retained space and fails to prove ARC $\sigma=1/2$. A selected-pair evaluator on authenticated fixed-width records can instead obtain the six literals by charged indexed source reads in $\mathrm{polylog}(N)$ time and $O(\log N)$ scratch; for $r$ selected pairs its tariff is initial $O(N\mathrm{polylog}N)$ authentication plus $r\mathrm{polylog}N$ reads, subject to all native index-tape erasures, head movements, parser addressing and retained policy state. Without a bound on $r$ this is not a fast worst-case SAT algorithm. Variable-length DIMACS requires its own paid indexing or search. Neither the sparse-table constructor nor on-demand query is a universal ordinary child-bit accessor.
