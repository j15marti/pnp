\section*{Claim, quantifiers, and falsifiers}
Fix the \emph{same} 60-Resolution-occurrence dependency DAG $G$ of the source-signed bridge Tseitin proof LSPV-1/579 and LSPV-4/549--550, 41 original read-only SOURCE records, one globally paid and unused conservative AND2 gate owner, no recomputation, and five atomic whole-clause result registers. On a Resolution last-use of a paid parent $u$, overwrite is admissible only when its canonical JSON clause-array length $w(u)$ is no smaller than the resulting clause-array length $w(v)$; no expansion of that current clause slot is admitted. Every paid result is stored once, and its last-use deletion is free under this \emph{explicit abstract policy}, not an assertion about actual physical RAM.

Test two genuinely different additive schedule objectives: cumulative number of live paid results (slot-steps), and cumulative canonical JSON clause-array lengths of those results (byte-steps). Four falsifiers were prespecified:
\begin{enumerate}

\item[] F3. An optimizing witness changes a signed original SOURCE record, paid owner, any of the 60 oriented Resolution identities, parent ancestry, terminal contradiction, or $W=61$ count-once charge.
\item[] F4. A restricted slot/byte-area optimum is misrepresented as peak actual writable bits or as a lower bound against native GF(2), paid extensions, learned compatible branches, reconvergent DAGs, compact affine rows or any alternate refutation.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Exact configuration and recurrence}
For a downset $D\subseteq V(G)$ of already paid Resolution occurrences, set
\[
L(D)=\{u\in D:\exists v\notin D,\ (u,v)\in E(G)\},\qquad
s(D)=|L(D)|,\qquad b(D)=\sum_{u\in L(D)}w(u).
\]
For a ready occurrence $v$, define $r(D,v)=1$ exactly when $v$ has a paid parent $u\in L(D)$ whose \emph{sole remaining consumer} is $v$ and whose $w(u)\ge w(v)$; otherwise $r=0$. The five-slot transition is legal precisely if
\[
\boxed{s(D)+1-r(D,v)\le 5.}
\]
The two lexicographic shortest-path recurrences over these same legal edges are
\[
A_B(\varnothing)=(0,0),\quad
A_B(D\cup\{v\})=\min_{\rm lex}\left(A_B(D)+(b(D),s(D))\right),
\]
\[
A_S(\varnothing)=(0,0),\quad
A_S(D\cup\{v\})=\min_{\rm lex}\left(A_S(D)+(s(D),b(D))\right).
\]
The min is taken over all incoming legal edges and every downset is processed at most once. For the full set $V$ the pair $A_B(V)$ means first minimize byte-steps, then slot-steps; $A_S(V)$ reverses the priority.

\begin{theorem}[551.1: exact biobjective conflict, fixed W61 DAG]
In the declared five-slot, no-recomputation, nonexpanding-last-use whole-clause model,
\[
\boxed{A_B(V)=(1925,\ 157),\qquad A_S(V)=(156,\ 1930).}
\]
Consequently no legal schedule attains \emph{both} the global 1,925 byte-step minimum and global 156 slot-step minimum. The previous LSPV-4/550 schedule has $(\mathrm{slot},\mathrm{byte})=(156,1995)$ and is strictly dominated, for these two objectives, by the new $(156,1930)$ schedule. A second new $(157,1925)$ schedule saves another five canonical byte-steps at the price of one slot-step. All three certificates preserve $W=61$ paid work and five-slot peak.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At state $D$, all paid results with future consumers must remain present, since recomputation is disallowed. The next ready $v$ requires a fresh slot unless a last-use paid parent can be replaced without expanding its canonical clause-array length. Hence the displayed transition condition is necessary. Conversely the declared atomic-result model realizes every permitted transition: read the parents, reuse an admissible dying parent or allocate one free slot, commit the output and release dead parents. Its future legal schedules and both additive costs depend only on $D$, not on which prefix led to $D$. Thus paths from $\varnothing$ to $V$ are in bijection with legal topological schedules, and cardinality-ordered shortest-path induction establishes the recurrences.

Independently transcribed Python and JavaScript implementations exhaust the same $1{,}129{,}302$ reachable downsets, inspect $12{,}982{,}502$ ready transitions, and admit $4{,}107{,}280$ capacity-legal transitions. The byte-first search performs $2{,}480{,}565$ improvements and returns $(1925,157)$; the slot-first search performs $2{,}480{,}361$ improvements and returns $(156,1930)$. Each returns a complete 60-node ordering. The two new byte-exact witness JSON files are independently replayed at every chronological signed Resolution step, retain all source and paid-owner fields, use precisely the original 60 signed inference identities after parent remapping, and end in the empty clause. Their actual retained-cost measurements match the two recurrence outputs. The two lexicographic minima together exclude a common optimizer. \qedhere
\end{proof}
