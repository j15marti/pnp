\section*{Target and exact quantifiers}
Fix a deterministic SAT decider \(A\), static input \(x\), and realized challenge path \(\rho\). Let \(\operatorname{View}(A,x,\rho)\) contain exactly the information available to a proposed compiler from \(A\)'s extensional SAT behavior and realized CCSPC path: static input, realized challenges and responses, SAT-oracle query/answer pairs, and charged local state exposed by the declared interface. A realized-view black-box compiler is a deterministic map
\[
E:\operatorname{View}(A,x,\rho)\mapsto S
\]
whose output \(S\) purports to answer legal counterfactual future challenges.

\section*{PROVED/EXACT: OFFPATH1}
\textbf{Theorem.} Suppose the source specification permits two legal environments \(C_0,C_1\) such that (i) their complete static input and complete realized view on \(\rho\) are identical, and (ii) there is an off-path legal challenge \(b^\star\) for which the valid continuation-response sets in \(C_0\) and \(C_1\) are disjoint. Then no realized-view black-box compiler \(E\) is correct for both environments.

\textbf{Proof.} The two inputs to \(E\) are identical, so determinism gives the same output strategy \(S\). At \(b^\star\), correctness requires \(S(b^\star)\) to lie in two disjoint legal-response sets, impossible. \(\square\)

The theorem is conditional on the existence of the two legal environments. It is an interface theorem, not a claim that canonical CCSPC already supplies such a pair.

\section*{Corollary: required off-path information}
Any valid universal compiler to a non-path-faithful \(W_{\rm exact}\) must use information that separates all legal counterfactual completions relevant to its output. At least one of the following must therefore be explicit and charged:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an externally fixed source object containing the off-path legal-response relation;
\item queries or access to that off-path relation;
\item non-black-box code/source inspection that provably determines it;
\item a theorem proving that realized/static data uniquely determine every required off-path response.
\end{enumerate}
SAME_REALIZATION, canonical naming, provenance, or global-robustness labels do not supply this information.

\section*{Hostile consequences}

\paragraph{RECOMP and preprocessing.}
RECOMP stays inside the realized view by waiting for the actual challenge and recomputing only the demanded response. Full preprocessing helps only if the static input already exposes the off-path relation. If it does, representation/access costs must be charged; if not, preprocessing cannot distinguish \(C_0\) from \(C_1\).

\paragraph{Compact strategies and wrappers.}
A compact strategy circuit can compress a determined strategy but cannot acquire missing off-path truth from identical views. DEAD, COPY, and DELAY alter trace geometry without changing extensional realized information. INTERP can replace implementation history. These do not evade OFFPATH1.

\paragraph{Semantic and algebraic oracles.}
A maximal SAT semantic oracle does not distinguish \(C_0\) and \(C_1\) when their exposed SAT query/answer behavior agrees. The same indistinguishability proof survives an algebraic-oracle extension whenever the two environments agree on every algebraic query exposed to the compiler. This is a barrier guard, not a nonalgebrizing hinge.
