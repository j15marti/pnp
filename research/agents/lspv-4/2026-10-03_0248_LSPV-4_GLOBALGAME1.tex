\section*{Target and quantifiers}
RESPSEL1 closes polynomially many causal stages only when each legal current tuple has a polynomially bounded response accepted by a polynomial-time local predicate. The present target is the boundary between such stage-local total search and a globally coupled game.

Let a polynomial-round game have history \(h_i=(c_1,y_1,\ldots,c_i,y_i)\), polynomial-length challenges and responses, and polynomial-time terminal verifier \(V(x,h_T)\). For a prefix \(h\), define the continuation-winning predicate
\[
W(x,h,y)=1
\]
to mean that choosing response \(y\) at the current challenge leaves a continuation satisfying the required future game condition.

\section*{GLOBALGAME1 theorem}
\textbf{Theorem.} Assume \(P=NP\). Suppose that for every reachable legal prefix \(h\):
\[
\exists y,\quad |y|\le p(|x|),\quad W(x,h,y)=1,
\]
and membership in \(W\) is decidable in polynomial time. Then there is an \(FP\) online selector choosing a continuation-winning response at every stage. Consequently a polynomial number of stages can be played in polynomial total time.

\paragraph{Proof.}
For a current prefix \(h\), define the prefix-extension language
\[
L_W=\{(x,h,u):\exists v,\ |uv|\le p(|x|),\ W(x,h,uv)=1\}.
\]
Because \(W\) is polynomial-time decidable and \(v\) is polynomially bounded, \(L_W\in NP\). Under \(P=NP\), \(L_W\in P\). Standard bit-by-bit search-to-decision therefore constructs a response \(y\) with \(W(x,h,y)=1\) in polynomial time. Repeating for polynomially many stages preserves polynomial total time and, by the definition of \(W\), preserves a winning continuation. \(\square\)

\section*{Scope correction: local totality is not global viability}
RESPSEL1 must not be silently strengthened from a local response predicate \(R(x,h,c,y)\) to an arbitrary globally coupled winning condition. The implication
\[
\forall h\ \exists y\ R(x,h,c,y)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{greedy choices preserve a terminal win}
\]
is false in general: a locally valid move may destroy all winning continuations.

The correct load-bearing object for a globally coupled game is therefore the continuation predicate \(W\), not mere stage legality \(R\). If \(W\in P\) with polynomially bounded responses and totality on reachable prefixes, GLOBALGAME1 closes the route under \(P=NP\). If determining \(W\) requires additional alternation over future challenges, RESPSEL1 does not collapse that alternation merely by being applied repeatedly.

For polynomially many alternating future challenge/response rounds, the natural continuation statement can have polynomially many alternating quantifiers. Such a game can leave the fixed polynomial hierarchy and enter PSPACE-style territory. No claim that \(P=NP\) implies \(P=PSPACE\) is made or available. Thus globally coupled causal games are not retired by RESPSEL1 alone.

\section*{Hostile consequence for HEC, CRS-PPC, CCSPC, RCCN and Search-G}
A surviving causal proposal cannot obtain hardness merely by renaming local validity as future viability. It must write the exact continuation predicate and charge its acquisition. There are two hostile cases.

First, if the continuation-winning predicate is polynomial-time decidable, or more generally has an NP prefix-extension search relation under the stated polynomial balance, \(P=NP\) supplies the selector and the causal route closes.

Second, if continuation winning genuinely contains unresolved future universal choices, the proposal has escaped RESPSEL1 only into a stronger game problem. It still needs a model-preserving compiler proving that every hypothetical polynomial static SAT decider is forced to solve that game in the SAME\_REALIZATION. Inserting a PSPACE-like game into the protocol does not derive a SAT lower bound.

This is particularly important for CRS-B and Search-G. The exact question is no longer merely whether each stage has a valid witness. It is whether ordinary static SAT forces selection of a response preserving all required future audits, and whether that continuation property itself is available without importing a stronger problem.
