\section{Exact no-contraction theorem}
For a balanced proper 3-CNF $B$, write $m=|B|$ and suppose $x$ has exactly
$p\ge2$ positive and $p$ negative occurrences.  For $b\in\{0,1\}$, let
$R_b$ be the result of setting $x=b$, deleting satisfied clauses, deleting
the falsified literal in the other incident clauses, and densely renaming
the remaining variables.  Replace every binary clause $(a\vee b)$ of $R_b$
by the standard fresh-variable pair
\[
 (a\vee b\vee z)\ \wedge\ (a\vee b\vee\neg z),             \tag{2}
\]
using a distinct $z$ per binary clause.  Call the resulting proper 3-CNF
$P_b$.

\begin{theorem}[Decoded one-variable ARC is not a target contraction]
For every $B,x,b$ as above,
\[
 \SAT(B)\Longleftrightarrow \SAT(P_0)\lor\SAT(P_1),          \tag{3}
\]
and, for each branch,
\[
 |R_b|=m-p,\qquad |P_b|=m.                                  \tag{4}
\]
Moreover transparent balancing adds at least $2p$ seven-clause gadgets to
$P_b$.  Hence the actual compiler satisfies
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 v(U(B))&=9m,& M(U(B))&=12m,\\
 v(U(P_b))&=9(m+7g_b),&M(U(P_b))&=12(m+7g_b),
 \end{aligned}                                               \tag{5}
\]
where $g_b\ge2p$, and therefore
\[
 v(U(P_b))\ge9(m+14p)>v(U(B)),\quad
 M(U(P_b))\ge12(m+14p)>M(U(B)).                              \tag{6}
\]
Thus neither child is a smaller $L_{22}$ query in variables or clauses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In either restriction, $p$ incident clauses are satisfied and deleted, and
the other $p$ incident clauses become binary; all $m-2p$ nonincident clauses
remain.  This proves $|R_b|=m-p$.  Transformation (2) is equisatisfiable and
replaces each of the $p$ binary clauses by two ternary clauses, proving
$|P_b|=m$.  The ordinary restriction identity gives (3).

Each of the $p$ fresh repair variables occurs once positively and once
negatively.  The transparent balancer raises both signed counts to at least
two, requiring one positive-port and one negative-port seven-clause gadget
for each fresh variable.  Hence $g_b\ge2p$ even before accounting for any
deficits of surviving old variables.  IMAGE22DECODE1's audited compiler
identities on a balanced source are $v=9m_B$ and $M=12m_B$; here the balanced
intermediate has $m_B=m+7g_b$.  Equations (5)--(6) follow.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Encoding and recurrence consequence.}
Under the actual compact JSON encoding used by IMAGE22DECODE1, every added
clause contributes syntax and three signed indices, so the child is also
strictly longer in the tested realizations.  The structural theorem needs no
byte-ratio claim: (6) already denies the strict formula-size contraction
required by this ARC.  Querying an $L_{22}$ decider on both recompiled
children creates two \emph{larger} target queries.  Querying it directly on
$R_b$ is invalid because $R_b$ contains binary clauses and is outside the
fixed language.  Calling a general-SAT solver would silently change the
assumption.  The size-increasing $U$ is not a shrinker.

This theorem does not exclude a batch restriction with a different exact
repair, an orbit-valued child, an adaptive selector with a proved pruning
property, or a direct whole-machine argument.  Any such proposal must give
its own domain closure, exact encoded recurrence, and arbitrary-exponent
accounting.

\section{Literature, barrier, and transfer audit}
The two active obstacles are fixed-language closure under a decreasing
self-reduction and the still-missing implication from arbitrary uniform
computation to an unconditional lower-bound model.  SAT's usual
length-decreasing self-reduction acts on unrestricted formulas, not
automatically on $\mathcal C_{22}$; the exact repair calculation above is
the missing domain check.  Hemaspaandra's primary tutorial states the
ordinary two-child SAT identity and explicitly notes that self-reducibility
alone does not put SAT in P \cite{hemaspaandra}.  Chavrimootoo et al. identify
the same logical hazard in a claimed SAT lower bound: a desired algorithmic
structure cannot simply be imposed on every solver \cite{critique}.
D\"ocker supplies the fixed exact-occurrence NP-complete subclass, not a
closed decreasing self-reduction \cite{docker}.

The theorem in this report is an \textbf{EXACT OBJECT/THEOREM TRANSFER} from
the implemented compiler identities to this particular ARC.  Classical SAT
self-reducibility is a \textbf{USEFUL STRUCTURAL ANALOGY REQUIRING A NEW
TRANSFER THEOREM} because its children leave $L_{22}$.  Treating proof-system
hardness, Schur-square statistics, collision entropy, quadratic relations,
syzygies, cryptographic binding, or random-oracle behavior as arbitrary
machine time is a \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} here.  The visible algebraic
normal form may help an algorithm; it is not presumed hard.

The argument relativizes and algebrizes and defines no large constructive
circuit property.  It is a negative closure theorem, not a Natural-Proofs
escape.  Horn, 2SAT, affine/Gaussian, bounded-treewidth, compact arithmetic,
integer counting/flow, decoding, padding, products, wrappers and one-bit
output remain permitted hostile controls.  No semantic-state count is used
as time.
