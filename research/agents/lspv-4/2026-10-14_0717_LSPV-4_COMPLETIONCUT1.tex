\section{The user's actual construction and its recovered scope}
An algorithm completes an event with result $e_i$ at time $\tau_i$. The wrapper
samples $R_i$ from the continuously evolving external source and updates
\[
 h_i=H(h_{i-1},e_i,R_i),\qquad F_{i+1}=C(F_i,h_i).
\]
The event type must distinguish a completed SAT/UNSAT subroutine from a
primitive machine transition. Timestamps or internal configurations may be
additional hash inputs; their inclusion is recorded, not silently erased.

The controlling condition for the present probability statements is
\[
 \mathcal L(R_i\mid\mathcal H_i)=U(\{0,1\}^{\ell_i}),       \tag{1}
\]
where $\mathcal H_i$ is all information available when the event and any
preprediction are fixed, before the sampled block is exposed. This includes
the code, input, prior samples, current result and available internal state.
For a blind-between-snapshots IID stream, a block of fresh bits beginning
immediately after a stopping boundary satisfies (1). Sampling a current or
recent block at completion also needs (1), or an appropriate weaker entropy
bound; observing the stream to choose a favorable stopping bit violates it.
Overlapping previously exposed bits cannot be charged as fully fresh.
The experiments implement a fresh block latched after the boundary, charging
two sampling ticks. They do not assert that every physical sampling device
implements this rule.

The original CRS-PPC-SAT1 already establishes fresh snapshots, timing-sensitive
paths, reference replay and the solver-relative/static-problem distinction
\cite{crs}. LEAD-0016 proves deterministic execution-hash simulation closure
\cite{lead16}. LEAD-0017 places provenance in SRAS history, source lineage,
access and authority coordinates, rather than semantic SAT identity
\cite{lead17}. CORPUS-HASH-RECOVERY1 separates hash indexing from exact semantic
reuse \cite{recovery}. These are retained, not presented as new discoveries.

\section{Replay must retain the external sample inputs}
For a deterministic algorithm and controller, the correct live object is
\[
             P_A(F,G),\quad\text{not generally }P_A(F),     \tag{2}
\]
where $G$ is the actual external stream. Different sampled blocks can give
different records even for identical $A,F$. This qualifies the deterministic
replay theorem; it does not contradict its original hypotheses.

If the only stream access is through the recorded snapshots, an exact replay
can instead receive the finite event log
\[
 \mathcal R=((\tau_i,e_i,R_i))_{i=1}^k.
\]
For modeled deterministic step-count timing, the simulator can recompute the
events and their times from the code, input and samples. For externally
measured latencies, their actual observations must also be supplied. With
maximum timestamp $T$, the log length is
\[
 O\!\left(\sum_i |e_i|+\sum_i\ell_i+k\log(T+2)\right).    \tag{3}
\]
Unused stream bits are not required merely to replay this specified interface.
Equation (3) is not a method of authenticating historical samples: checking
that a recorded block really came from the external source at a claimed time
needs the trusted sampler/source record or an independently justified protocol.

The charged execution includes solver work, controller work, sampling access,
hash updates, any log output, replay and verification. Retained observer state
is distinct from the solver's own workspace. Under polynomial solver time,
polynomially many bounded-size events and polynomial per-update cost, replay
\emph{with the samples supplied} remains polynomial. No theorem says that
those external samples are reconstructible from $A,F$ alone.

For exact mathematical provenance, retain an injective event encoding or an
exact replay check. A short hash is a possibly colliding index. In particular,
for a fixed history $s$, let
\[
 \mu(s)=\max_y\Pr[H(s,R)=y\mid s].                        \tag{4}
\]
A precomputed list of $q$ digest values succeeds with probability at most
$\min\{1,q\mu(s)\}$. Without a proved bound on (4), raw-block entropy cannot
be assigned to the digest. No random-oracle or collision-resistance hypothesis
is used in this report.

\section{A precise contribution to simultaneous quantifiers}
\begin{theorem}[Common-stream preprediction bound]\label{thm:union}
Fix $M$ deterministic causal programs and one IID bitstream $G$. Program $p$
has its own almost-surely finite stopping boundary $\tau_p$, and fixes a list
$L_p$ of at most $q$ candidate $\ell$-bit blocks using only its available past.
The sampled block $R_p$ satisfies (1) at that boundary. Then
\[
 \Pr_G[R_p\in L_p]\le q2^{-\ell},\qquad
 \Pr_G[\exists p:\ R_p\in L_p]\le Mq2^{-\ell}.            \tag{5}
\]
If $Mq<2^\ell$, a common stream exists on which all $M$ prepredictions fail.
The programs may stop at different times and their samples may be mutually
correlated. No independence between the programs' success events is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Condition on the history fixing $\tau_p$ and $L_p$. At most $q$ of the
$2^\ell$ equiprobable fresh blocks are in the list. Average this conditional
bound and apply the union bound to the $M$ events. If their union has measure
less than one, its complement is nonempty.
\end{proof}

For all binary descriptions of length at most
$L_N=b_0+\lfloor\log_2(N+2)\rfloor$, the number of programs is at most
\[
 2^{L_N+1}-1<2^{b_0+1}(N+2).
\]
Thus $\ell\ge\lceil\log_2(Mq)\rceil+\lambda$ gives union probability at most
$2^{-\lambda}$. With polynomial $q$, a logarithmic-size fresh challenge
suffices for this finite-family preprediction property. This is a standard
probabilistic-method calculation applied to the recovered timing interface,
not a new complexity separation.

\paragraph{Three indispensable qualifications.}
The theorem bounds \emph{precommitted values}, not compact programs that compute
a response after reading $R_p$. The function $r\mapsto r$ defeats any attempt
to turn a large precomputed answer table into necessary work. Also, one common
stream is not necessarily one common SAT instance: hash-routed later formulas
can depend on each program's completion schedule. Finally, fixing a successful
stream as a static input can reveal precisely the information assumed hidden
in (1). A resource-preserving static transfer must address that change.

The oracle audit is exact. A fixed oracle independent of $G$ can be included
in the prior information without invalidating (1)--(5). Hence the bound also
holds with an equality oracle $P^O=NP^O$ supplied by Baker--Gill--Solovay
\cite{bgs}. This freshness argument alone is not the required nonrelativizing
hinge. Giving the oracle access to the future stream would instead violate
its independence assumption, rather than refute the lemma.

\section{Different paths can have identical event distributions}
\begin{theorem}[Clock-free event-law lemma]\label{thm:law}
Suppose a controller makes a fixed finite number $k$ of semantic decisions.
Its next state is a deterministic function of its prior logical state, the
correct decision bit, and a fresh independent block. The controller does not
consume completion times, implementation identity, or internal solver states.
All compared exact deciders halt at every stage. Then the logical event
transcript, including hash updates, has the same distribution for every such
decider, regardless of their completion-time schedules.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The initial state is common. Conditional on any common logical history, both
controllers ask the same decision question and obtain the same correct answer.
By (1), their newly sampled blocks have the same independent uniform law.
Applying the same state-update function gives the same next-state conditional
law. Induction proves equality of the entire $k$-stage distribution.
\end{proof}

This is equality in distribution, not pathwise equality on a shared physical
stream, and not free replay of a specified reference run. When time or internal
states feed the hash/controller, the hypothesis fails and this lemma cannot
be used. Subject to polynomial event count, query size and controller cost,
a SAT-oracle controller can generate the same clock-free law in polynomial
work. That observation does not reproduce reference timestamps.

A complementary event-level control computes a stage answer early and waits
until the prescribed release time. If the recorded event contains only the
answer, timestamp and sampled block, it can give the identical timed hash
record while spending different active work before release. A digest of every
primitive state would be a different interface, not covered by this control.
Even such a digest needs an independent theorem connecting mandatory ordinary
lineage to the original decision obligation.

\section{Where this attaches to the proof chain}
The available positive arrows are
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{completion boundary}\ \longrightarrow\
 \text{fresh source acquisition}\\
 \longrightarrow\ \text{preprediction bound and explicit replay inputs}.
 \end{gathered} \tag{6}
\]
They belong in SRAS $H,J,P,\mathsf A,\Omega_D,\Omega_G$ and a specified
provenance-sensitive consumer. They do not change the semantic continuation
quantity $\kappa_U$ merely by increasing the number of recorded histories.
Current main4/main5 accounting still needs source-relevant correctness,
acquisition, successor coverage and charged replay; (6) supplies no missing
contraction by itself. The fixed-language route is still allowed and needs
no mandatory universal implementation compiler.

A valid probabilistic route to the common-input UWI target would use a
\emph{single} instance generator $F(G)$ independent of the candidate program,
with satisfiable outputs of the declared encoded size, and prove
\[
 \forall p\in\mathcal P_N:\quad
 \Pr_G[p\text{ succeeds on }F(G)\text{ within the native budget}]
 \le\varepsilon_N,\qquad |\mathcal P_N|\varepsilon_N<1.   \tag{7}
\]
The union bound would then give one hard input; an efficient method for finding
the good seed is not necessary for a worst-case nonexistence proof. But (5)
is not (7): pre-release guessing is a different success event from solving a
fully available fixed instance. An external reduction from a hypothetical
polynomial decider into a prohibited live strategy would also suffice, if its
correctness, information access and all costs were proved. Neither transfer
is established here.

The next useful dependency is consequently a response predicate on the actual
residual source that remains costly \emph{after} the challenge is available,
or a proved reduction connecting a forbidden timing obligation to that source.
The present two-tree/shared-compatibility source in main.tex provides a fixed
problem to test; requiring its witness to carry our favorite historical hash
would not itself prove the connection. Exact source/witness maps and the older
EARLY22/SINGLE22 compilers remain valid only in their recorded scope.

\section{Bounded exhaustive tests}
The dependency-free program
\path{experiments/review/completion_cut_audit.py} runs three separately bounded
batches with a 25-second cooperative cap. All 15,820 assertions passed. The
summed measured runtime was 0.094733 seconds, excluding tool overhead.
No random seed is used: all finite source streams in each stated domain are
enumerated. Timing does not establish an asymptotic result.

Replay, scheduling and event distributions & 12,292 & 0.086339\\

\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Four precommitment settings use $(\ell,M,q)=(2,2,1),(3,4,1),(4,8,1),(5,8,2)$.
They enumerate respectively 32, 64, 128 and 256 full streams. In the
$(4,8,1)$ setting, all eight policies fail on 74 of 128 streams; the union
success probability is $27/64\le1/2$. Each policy has exactly $1/16$ success,
even though its completion time depends on the observed prefix.

The replay batch exhausts all 4,096 twelve-bit streams through three
hash-routed tiny 3-CNF decision stages. Correct SAT and UNSAT bits are computed
from actual three-variable clauses. Two different duration policies have
different logical paths on 4,032 streams, namely $63/64$, but identical logical
transcript distributions with 64 possible transcripts. Every sampled-block
replay matches its original timed record. All 4,096 early-compute/wait controls
match the reference completion record. Making the hash consume timestamps
changes the distribution, confirming the clock-free hypothesis is essential.
The active/wait counters are stipulated logical protocol costs, not measured
primitive CPU instruction counts or a fastest-solver comparison.

Eight exact digest-fiber tables enumerate 64 raw blocks for a two-bit truncated
SHA-256 output. No security assumption is inferred from the library primitive.
A selected-current-bit control stops at the first observed one among three
positions: that bit is one on 28 of 32 five-bit streams. In contrast, the next
two fresh bits have each possible value on exactly eight streams. This verifies
why ``sample at completion'' needs a genuine freshness condition.
