\section{Uniform fixed-width proper-3CNF family}
For even $q\ge2$, choose $A,B\subseteq[q]$ with $|A|=|B|=q/2$. Declare $q$ primaries $x_1,\ldots,x_q$ and $2q$ fresh helpers $(u_j,v_j)$, one pair per block. Define
\[
\operatorname{Pin}(\ell;u,v):=\bigwedge_{\epsilon,\delta\in\{-1,1\}}(\ell\vee u^\epsilon\vee v^\delta).
\tag{1}
\]
Write the $q/2$ blocks $\operatorname{Pin}(x_i;u_j,v_j)$ for $i\in A$ in sorted order first, followed by the $q/2$ blocks $\operatorname{Pin}(\neg x_i;u_j,v_j)$ for $i\in B$ in sorted order. Every helper pair is private and $u_j$ has identifier $q+2j-1$, $v_j$ identifier $q+2j$; denote the resulting ordinary formula by $J_q(A,B)$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact SAT, model count, and encoding; PROVED/EXACT]
All formulas $J_q(A,B)$ have $n=3q$ declared variables and $m=4q$ distinct proper ternary clauses. They satisfy
\[
J_q(A,B)\in\SAT\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A\cap B=\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B=[q]\setminus A,
\qquad
\# J_q(A,B)=\begin{cases}2^{2q}&\text{if SAT},\\0&\text{if UNSAT}.\end{cases}
\tag{2}
\]
Under two header words and four fixed-width words per clause, with $2|z|+\mathbf1_{z<0}$ as signed-literal code,
\[
w(q)=\left\lceil\log_2(64(7q+1))\right\rceil,\qquad
N(q)=(16q+2)w(q)=\Theta(q\log(q+2)).
\tag{3}
\]
All headers, arities, clause positions, helper IDs and \emph{literal signs} are fixed. Only the primary \emph{identifier records} vary.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The four clauses in (1) force $\ell$: if it is false, their residual binary clauses forbid all four assignments to $(u,v)$; if true, the helpers are free. Positive and negative blocks therefore require the same named primary to take opposite truth values exactly when $A\cap B\ne\varnothing$. On SAT inputs the equal sizes force the union to cover all $q$ primaries, fixing them uniquely, while $2q$ helpers remain free. Properness, distinctness, and record counts follow from the private helpers. Fixed sign positions and widths yield (3).
\end{proof}

\section{Full rank and a quantified pass-state theorem}
Let $\mathcal H_q=\{A\subseteq[q]:|A|=q/2\}$, $H_q=\binom q{q/2}$. The source SAT matrix
\[
M_q[A,B]=\mathbf1[J_q(A,B)\in\SAT]=\mathbf1[B=[q]\setminus A]
\tag{4}
\]
is a permutation matrix, so $\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F}(M_q)=H_q$ over \emph{every} field. This is a property of the source relation, not an arbitrary-decider runtime bound.

A deterministic \emph{sequential} controller reads the source in order, the $A$ blocks first and the $B$ blocks second; it may rewind and make up to $p$ forward passes. Every cache, learned datum, strategy, address, counter, current representation and continuation state is counted in $B$ live writable bits, with all finite machine control separately charged by $c_A=\lceil\log_2(|Q_A|+1)\rceil$ bits, where $Q_A$ is the fixed finite control-state set. No random source seek or external workspace is free. Method switching, propagation, SCC, Horn, affine elimination, branching, caching, rereading, and direct decision are unrestricted \emph{within} this channel. Halting/output data are explicitly charged by two extra bits per simulated boundary transmission.

\begin{theorem}[Identifier-only multi-pass SAT cut; PROVED/EXACT restricted]
For every even $q\ge2$, every exact deterministic controller correct on all the canonical $J_q(A,B)$, and every integer $p\ge1$ upper-bounding its complete forward passes,
\[
\boxed{(2p-1)(B+c_A+2)\ \ge\ \log_2 H_q
\ \ge\ q-\log_2(q+1).}
\tag{5}
\]
Consequently bounded-pass space is $\Omega(q/p)=\Omega(N/(p\log N))$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Alice simulates the first source half using $A$ and Bob the second using $B$. Every crossing of the public half boundary transmits the controller's complete portable configuration, with at most two extra bits for halt and answer. At most $2p-1$ transmissions suffice, each with $B+c_A+2$ bits. Padded deterministic transcripts with the same contents form rectangles in $(A,B)$-space. The $H_q$ positive diagonal pairs $(A,\overline A)$ form a fooling set: for distinct equal-cardinality $A,A'$, the crossed pair $(A,\overline{A'})$ is negative because $A\setminus A'\ne\varnothing$. No two distinct positive diagonal pairs can have identical transcripts. Thus $H_q\le2^{(2p-1)(B+c_A+2)}$. The central binomial coefficient satisfies $H_q\ge2^q/(q+1)$. This proof quantifies over arbitrary adaptive internal rules, but only in the explicitly restricted source-access model.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Nearly matching constructive upper bound.}
For every $1\le p\le q$, partition the \emph{name universe} $[q]$ into $p$ consecutive intervals of size at most $d=\lceil q/p\rceil$. During pass $j$, retain a $d$-bit mask of early-half names in the $j$th interval; reject when a late-half name hits a set bit. Otherwise continue and accept after all intervals. This decides both labels without prior label knowledge and uses
\[
B_{\rm upper}\le \lceil q/p\rceil+O(\log N),\qquad
W_{\rm upper}=O(pN\operatorname{polylog}N)
\tag{6}
\]
charged elementary bit operations for source reads, header/identifier parsing, mask addressing and updates, comparisons, control, rewinds and failed trials. A general-SAT implementation first pays for syntactic recognition and must use an exact fallback on noncanonical inputs, without an improved all-input bound.

\paragraph{BIT-RESOURCE1 distinctions.}
On positive inputs, the interval masks alone incur at most $q/2$ set-bit flips and $q/2$ clear-bit flips across all passes. This is \emph{not} a bound on the complete machine's state flips: counters, addressing, policy selection, head movement and caches are additional. Every write is charged even if a value is unchanged. Work, source/work reads and writes, complete-state flips, peak live bits, cumulative bit-time and program size remain distinct ledger coordinates. The rank $H_q$ requires portable state across a cut, not compulsory enumeration of $H_q$ assignments or a huge SAT-solving circuit.
