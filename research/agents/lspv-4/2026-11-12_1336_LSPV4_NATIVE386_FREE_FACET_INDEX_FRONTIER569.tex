\section*{Theorem 569.1: constant-work native byte separation}
Let $P$ be the fixed 58-record signed accepted /386 proof, with original records indexed 0 through 57, SOURCE/DEF prefix ending at 22 and terminal empty RES at 57. There are 35 references from the 35 RES steps to preceding paid RES records, and another 35 to SOURCE/DEF records. Each paid parent index lies in $[23,56]$. Let $F_k$ insert k copies of the original true unused defining facet \texttt{DEF([1,-8], owner 0)} immediately before old record 23. Let $L_k$ insert the \emph{same} k copies before old terminal record 57. Reindex all original parent pointers via $p\mapsto p+k$ exactly when $p$ is not earlier than the insertion gap; do not otherwise modify records. In both cases set \texttt{def\_views}=6+k$ and \texttt{live\_records}=58+k$.

For every finite k representable and executable by the unchanged original archived /386 checker, $F_k$ and $L_k$ are accepted and retain exactly
\[
 W_{386}=35_{\rm RES}+3_{\rm gate}=\boxed{38},\quad
 \#\mathrm{RES}=35,\quad \#\mathrm{SOURCE}=17,\quad
 R_{\rm clause\,scan}=790\ \mathrm{ASCII\ bytes}.
\]
The paid directed acyclic graph, including true paid-lemma sharing and the signed final empty clause, is identical after deleting the inserted unused facets and inverse-indexing. The original original-source clauses are \emph{byte-identical}. Yet for compact \texttt{JSON.stringify} ASCII serialization the exact length difference is
\[
 \boxed{B(F_k)-B(L_k)=\sum_{(i,p):\,i\ \mathrm{RES},\ 23\le p\le56}
 \bigl(d_{10}(p+k)-d_{10}(p)\bigr)}.
\]
Here the sum includes each \emph{parent use} exactly once; $d_{10}$ is the number of decimal digits of the nonnegative integer. There are exactly 35 summands. At $k=77$ all 35 parent indices cross from two to three digits, and the gap is \emph{exactly 35 bytes}; at $k=1024$ they become four digits and the gap is exactly 70 bytes. More generally the difference is $35\log_{10}k+O(1)$ as $k\to\infty$ in an unbounded index encoding, with finite-runtime/array limitations of the actual program.
