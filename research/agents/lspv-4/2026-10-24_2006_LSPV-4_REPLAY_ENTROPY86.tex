\section{A replay service as a typed interface}
For $R$ rounds, an exact fixed uniform deterministic service must emit $b_1,\ldots,b_q$ in the public order every round. It may switch policies and reread arbitrary sign fields adaptively. At every round boundary, its \emph{complete usable state} $Z_j$ has at most $B$ bits, including all mutable work/index information, control and head-address information, caches and compressed summaries. Write-only output may not be reread. A bounded-tape FLMV realization whose counted workspace is $S$ can have an additional $O(\log N)$ bits encoded in input-head positions; these must be included in $B$, not silently ignored. Public indices alone do not reveal orientations.
\begin{lemma}[Source-query decision-tree information]
If a deterministic policy begins with fixed $Z=z$ and exactly outputs a uniform source $X$ in a finite set $U$ of possible $q$-bit strings, then its expected number of indexed source-bit probes is at least $\log_2|U|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each complete source-read transcript follows a binary decision tree. Distinct source strings must end at distinct leaves: otherwise the policy would output the same $q$ bits for two different sources. Terminal transcripts are prefix-free codewords, so the average length is at least the entropy $\log_2|U|$ by Kraft's inequality. Non-query control steps cannot reveal new source bits.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Reread floor for any compressed $B$-bit cache]
Let $X$ be uniform over $\{0,1\}^q$, and assume the replay policy is exact for every source. If $A_j$ counts addressed sign-field source reads in round $j$, then
\[
\mathbb E A_j\ge H(X\mid Z_j)\ge(q-B)_+,\qquad
\mathbb E\sum_{j=1}^R A_j\ge R(q-B)_+.
\]
If $Z_1$ is source-independent, $\mathbb E A_1\ge q$. For each fixed parity class $\sigma\in\{0,1\}$ separately,
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E_{\oplus_i X_i=\sigma}\sum_{j=1}^R A_j
\ge R(q-1-B)_+.}
\]
Consequently, for every fixed $R$ there is at least one SAT source and one UNSAT source meeting the respective lower bound. This is an expectation/worst-case-existence result; it is NOT a per-source lower bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Condition on $Z_j=z$. Because $Z_j$ is a deterministic function of the source and prior computations, the originally uniform distribution remains uniform within each compatible fiber. The lemma gives expected probes at least the conditional entropy. Averaging yields $\mathbb E A_j\ge H(X\mid Z_j)\ge H(X)-H(Z_j)\ge H(X)-B$. Repeat for the uniform distribution conditional on the parity label, whose entropy is exactly $q-1$. Summing the expected costs is valid because each term refers to actual source reads, not sums of a hypothetical residual-potential quantity. Averaging within each nonempty parity class produces an input witnessing the bound.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{Near-matching implementation, with overhead counted.}
Read $q$ orientations once and retain any $C$ predetermined values. In each later sweep use retained values and reread the remaining $q-C$. The exact source-read total is
\[
A_{\rm cache}=q+(R-1)(q-C).
\]
Its complete writable state uses $C+O(\log(N+R))$ bits, including counters and indexed addressing. Thus a compressed cache cannot asymptotically improve the source-read order when $C=o(q)$. A direct, paid parity scan instead decides the \emph{single SAT bit} in $O(q)$ addressed field reads and $O(\log N)$ writable bits. It need not replay the source, count assignments, or produce witnesses. Since complete replay also writes $qR$ output bits, the lower bound is an interface-accounting result, not a post-acquisition time lower bound for arbitrary SAT.
