\section*{Theorem and proof}
\begin{theorem}[587.1: complete typed K4 in-place span--register frontier]
Within the above original-source, sound typed local-parity SOURCE/AND3/GF(2) K4 tree sublanguage, with exactly 15 paid binary operations and original all-SOURCE ancestry, the minimum possible logical span as a function of peak in-place paid-result registers $r$ is
\[
\boxed{
S_{\min}(r)=
\begin{cases}
\text{infeasible},& r<2,\\
6,&r=2,\\
5,&r=3,\\
4,&r\ge4.
\end{cases}}
\]
All three nondominated points $(S,r)=(6,2),(5,3),(4,4)$ are attained by actual original-checker-accepted \emph{same-source} 15-paid-record compact certificates, each of length exactly 3,090 bytes. The paid logical charge, source and parent-reference count are identically $15,16,30$ on all three realizations.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} The unchanged checker requires every one of sixteen distinct SOURCE records to be in the ancestry of the terminal contradiction. A connected two-parent ancestor network with sixteen source leaves and $k$ paid internal nodes has $2k$ incoming dependency arcs but at least $16+k-1$ arcs. Therefore $k\ge15$; because the accepted K4 packet has exactly fifteen paid nodes, every paid node is in the terminal ancestry, there is \emph{no sharing}, and the underlying undirected dependency graph is a full binary tree. A tree with sixteen leaves and binary fan-in requires logical height at least $\lceil\log_2 16\rceil=4$. Equality forces a perfectly balanced sixteen-leaf tree.

Two different K4 vertices have incident edge supports whose union has size five, so the checker-legal AND3 support-size-at-most-three rule cannot mix their SOURCE conditions. The conjunction of $j$ distinct local forbidden-pattern clauses, for $j=1,2,3,4$, has respectively $8-j$ satisfying local assignments. A nontrivial affine equation on three bits has exactly four satisfying assignments; constant affine equations have zero or eight. Consequently, an authenticated numeric-support AND3 node cannot present a checker-valid affine row to XOR until it has combined \emph{all four} clauses of one vertex. Four such local root rows require twelve AND3 nodes, leaving exactly three XOR nodes combining the four roots in a binary tree.

For a binary paid-operation tree with read-only SOURCE leaves, its exact no-recomputation in-place register need obeys the usual Sethi--Ullman recurrence: SOURCE leaf rank zero; at a paid node with child ranks $a,b$, the rank is $a+1$ when $a=b$, and $\max(a,b)$ otherwise. A four-SOURCE local row computed with rank one must be a comb of depth three; a depth-two balanced local row has rank two. A four-row \emph{balanced} XOR tree has rank at least three even if all local roots have rank one, so a two-register implementation must use a three-level \emph{comb} XOR tree. Its two deepest row leaves lie below three XOR steps; at least one of those two roots must have rank one, or their first XOR requires three registers. That row has local depth at least three, forcing total span at least $3+3=6$. Two registers are also necessary because the final XOR consumes two paid row-derived values. The ordinary local comb with an XOR comb attains $(6,2)$.

At span four the sixteen-leaf tree must be perfectly balanced. Its four local rows each have depth two and rank two; the two first XOR parents have rank three; the final XOR therefore has rank four. Hence three registers cannot reach span four. Four local combs with a balanced two-level XOR tree attain $(5,3)$, and four balanced local AND3 trees with balanced XOR attain $(4,4)$. Together these constructions and lower bounds prove every stated point. \(\square\)
