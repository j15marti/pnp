## Primary-source theorem, model and exact transfer
Dell and van Melkebeek, *Satisfiability Allows No Nontrivial Sparsification Unless the Polynomial-Time Hierarchy Collapses*, JACM 2014, ECCC TR10-038 revision 1 (2014), Definition 1 and Theorem 1, PDF https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2010/038/revision/1/download . The first participant has the entire n-variable d-CNF source and uses polynomial time, but the second is computationally unbounded and has NO source access. All bits sent from first to second are charged, even with arbitrary adaptive rounds; replies from second to first are free. For d>=3, communication cost O(n^{d-epsilon}) for epsilon>0 implies coNP subseteq NP/poly (and a polynomial-hierarchy collapse). The primary theorem even covers conondeterministic first participants. We use only the deterministic special case.

Let C be an effective **solver-independent**, polynomial-time preprocessing/oracle controller correct on **all** binary 3-CNF encodings, including SAT, UNSAT and malformed cases. It may branch adaptively and make multiple calls to an unbounded external solver, with ordinary responses free; every source-dependent bit sent externally, including query source descriptions, generator programs, indices and requested virtual bits, is charged. Suppose its TOTAL outgoing communication on a source of N bits is O(N^a(log(N+2))^b) for fixed a<1 and fixed b>=0. For an arbitrary n-variable 3-CNF, remove duplicate clauses and consistently renumber used variables. At most 8*C(n,3)+4*C(n,2)+2n+1 = O(n^3) distinct clauses remain, each with O(log n) bits under dense IDs and a fixed parser; hence N=O(n^3 log(n+2)). (The detailed fixed three-slot canonical encoding used in current project has b0=ceil(log2(128(n+m+3))), N=3*b0+1+3*m*(b0+2), and obeys the same bound.) Therefore outgoing cost is at most O(n^{3a}(log(n+2))^{a+b}), which is O(n^{3-epsilon}) for epsilon=3(1-a)/2>0, since log^c n=o(n^{epsilon}). Dell--van Melkebeek Theorem 1 gives:

$$
\boxed{\bigl(\exists C\text{ with all-input }B_{out}(N)=O(N^a\log^b N),\ a<1\bigr)\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathrm{coNP}\subseteq\mathrm{NP/poly}.}
$$

This is a **PROVED/RESTRICTED conditional implication** by explicit size composition, NOT an unconditional impossibility, SAT general-time lower bound, or R_mix341 proof-size lower bound. In particular, at a=1/2 the outgoing bit bound becomes O(n^{3/2}polylog n) and may be compared against the published n^{3-epsilon} exclusion. The lower-bound theorem says nothing about arbitrary algorithms with unrestricted local SAT work or uncharged retention of the original source at the oracle. When SAT is IN P, coNP subset NP/poly is automatically true; there is NO CONTRADICTION with Route A's SAT-in-P reductio antecedent. This correctly limits the utility of this barrier to identifying why any purported solver-INDEPENDENT shortcut is very strong. Source-dependent polynomial solver access or code-aware rewriting is a distinct architecture and the unresolved [P2-A2]/[P2-A5] question.

**Virtual child and adaptive branching qualification.** Sending a small virtual-child decoder to the oracle counts the decoder and all source-dependent advice. If the unbounded oracle can request individual input bits from the local participant, count every requested value, index and protocol message. If it can access the untransmitted parent source for free, the cited two-party theorem does not apply. Count total traffic across ALL branches actually executed, not only the largest child. Shared learned lemmas/branch DAGs can reduce communication but cannot make it uncharged.
