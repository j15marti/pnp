\section{Target, falsifiable mechanisms, and prior art}
The ultimate, still \OPEN{}, goal is, for one fixed NP-complete language
and encoding,
\[
\forall A\ {\rm(total,\ uniform,\ exact)}\;\forall c>0\;\forall N_0\
\exists F\ \bigl[|F|\ge N_0\land T_A(F)>|F|^c\bigr].
\]
This run tested three falsifiable mechanisms.
\par\noindent \textbf{M1 (\PROVED{} restricted):} an affine XOR3 plus
binary-OR family with $k=4q$ genuine ternaries, strong raw 2-CNF
backdoor $h=q$, and independently variable affine blocks admits
constant-cost exact local existential projection to 2-CNF, and therefore
a charged $O(q)$ adaptive decision algorithm.
\par\noindent \textbf{M2 (\PROVED{} counterexample):} one cross-block
equivalence invalidates independent existential projection.
For $q=2$ and $b=(1,0)$, the uncoupled formula is SAT, but adding
$x_0\leftrightarrow x_1$ makes it UNSAT despite an individually SAT
binary projection. By coupling all $x_i$, the SAT label collapses to
$b_0=0$; a single discovered global switch remains sufficient.
\par\noindent \textbf{M3 (\PROVED{} restricted; all-machine amplification
\OPEN{}):} a designated uncoupled UNSAT instance has $q$ independently
sensitive sign groups, forcing $q=\Omega(N/\log N)$ addressed-word reads
by every total exact deterministic decider. A source-aware $O(q)$ scan
matches this order. A nonpruning fixed backdoor enumeration makes
$2^q$ leaves, but that is a restricted-policy artifact.

\section{Canonical formula and exact encoded size}
Fix $q\ge1$, $b=(b_0,\ldots,b_{q-1})\in\{0,1\}^q$.
Use
$p_0,\ldots,p_q$, $x_0,\ldots,x_{q-1}$,
$y_0,\ldots,y_{q-1}$, and anchor variables $u,v$.
The binary clauses are
\[
(p_0\vee u)\land(p_0\vee\neg u)
\land(\neg p_q\vee v)\land(\neg p_q\vee\neg v)
\land\bigwedge_{i<q}\bigl[(x_i\vee y_i)
\land(\neg x_i\vee p_{i+1})\bigr].
\tag{1}
\]
For each $i$ add the standard four proper ternary clauses for
\[
x_i\oplus y_i\oplus p_i=b_i,
\tag{2}
\]
where $\oplus$ denotes addition modulo two. A canonical ordering
enumerates $(x_i,y_i)\in\{00,01,10,11\}$ and places the
$p_i$ sign last; toggling $b_i$ changes \emph{exactly four}
private signed-literal words but no identifiers, widths, headers or
encoded length.
Call the result $G_{q,b}$. The $q$ triples
$\{x_i,y_i,p_i\}$ are disjoint.
\[
n_G=3q+3,\quad m_{2,G}=2q+4,\quad
k_G=4q,\quad M_G=6q+4.
\tag{3}
\]
In a four-word-per-clause grammar with two header words,
$w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+M+1))\rceil$ and
\[
N_G=(24q+18)\lceil\log_2(64(9q+8))\rceil.
\tag{4}
\]
Every clause has two or three distinct variable identifiers.
The binary-only subsystem is SAT on \emph{both} labels
(e.g. $p_0=1$, $p_i=0$ for $i>0$, $x_i=0,y_i=1$);
the affine-only subsystem is also SAT for every $b$.
Thus the incompatibility, when present, is due to their interaction.

\section{Exact private gate projection}
\begin{lemma}[Two-boundary-bit identity; \PROVED]\label{lem:project}
For one gate, fix boundary bits $p,p'\in\{0,1\}$ and $b\in\{0,1\}$.
Then
\[
\begin{split}
&\exists x,y\
[(x\vee y)\land(\neg x\vee p')\land
 (x\oplus y\oplus p=b)]\\
&\qquad\equiv
p'\lor(p\oplus b).
\end{split}
\tag{5}
\]
The latter is $(\neg p\vee p')$ when $b=1$
and $(p\vee p')$ when $b=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $p'=0$, then the implication forces $x=0$ and
$(x\vee y)$ forces $y=1$; the XOR is possible precisely
when $p\oplus b=1$. If $p'=1$, for parity
$x\oplus y=0$ choose $x=y=1$; for parity one
choose $x=1,y=0$. Thus all $p$ are allowed.
These cases are exhaustive and provide a constructive witness map.
\end{proof}

Since the $x_i,y_i$ are genuinely private, their existential
quantifiers distribute over the conjunction. Eliminating the two
anchor variables additionally yields $p_0$ and $\neg p_q$.
Thus
\[
\exists x_0y_0\cdots x_{q-1}y_{q-1}\,u\,v\
G_{q,b}
\equiv
p_0\land\neg p_q\land
\bigwedge_{i<q}D_{b_i}(p_i,p_{i+1}),
\tag{6}
\]
where
$D_1(p,p')=\neg p\vee p'$ and $D_0(p,p')=p\vee p'$.
This is an exact \emph{decision} projection, not a parsimonious
one. For each satisfying boundary assignment $p$, the number of
target extensions is
\[
4\prod_{i<q}
\left(1+\mathbf1[
p_{i+1}=1\ \land\ (p_i\oplus b_i)=1]\right).
\tag{7}
\]
Here $u,v$ are freely chosen, and some private gate assignments
are nonfunctional. A SAT decider need not count these extensions.

\begin{theorem}[Labels, raw backdoor, and adaptive upper bound;
\PROVED]\label{th:family}
For every $q\ge1$ and $b\in\{0,1\}^q$,
\[
G_{q,b}\text{ is UNSAT}\iff b=1^q.
\tag{8}
\]
The minimum raw strong syntactic 2-CNF backdoor has size exactly
$q$, one variable from each disjoint ternary support. A
total uniform deterministic exact SAT algorithm recognizes the
canonical language in $O(q)$ word time, performs local truth-table
projections, invokes SCC on 2-CNF and optionally reconstructs and
checks a witness. On this language its time and peak live words are
$O(q)$, including all discovery, switching, source reads and replay.
Outside it the algorithm uses a complete exact DPLL fallback.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $b=1^q$, (6) contains the implication chain
$p_0\to p_1\to\cdots\to p_q$, contradicting
$p_0=1,p_q=0$. Conversely, let $j$ be the last zero
in $b$. Set $p_i=1$ for $i\le j$, and $p_i=0$
for $i>j$. Every $D_{b_i}$ holds; Lemma~\ref{lem:project}
constructs each private witness.
Every raw ternary is supported on one of the $q$ disjoint
triples, hence a strong syntactic width-two backdoor must
hit each; choosing $p_i$ for all $i<q$ suffices.
Canonical grammar comparison and per-gate 16-entry enumeration
use linear total operations and produce $q$ binary clauses.
SCC, back-substitution and source verification are linear.
DPLL fallback provides total exactness without preknowledge of
the label. None of these facts lower-bounds other algorithms.
\end{proof}

In the optional fully materialized quotient,
$d=2q+2$ syntactic variables (the private $x,y$ and
free $u,v$) are eliminated; $r=n-d=q+1$ and
$v=n/r=3$. The quotient has $q$ binaries and two units.
The implemented solver retains both binary anchor pairs,
giving $q+4$ binary clauses and SCC over the original
identifier range. The ratio $v=3$ is a syntactic count,
not a compulsory work multiplier.
The original $p$ values are retained. No uncharged
backward recovery, certificate parsing or proof DAG is required.

\section{One compatibility link changes the problem}
For $q\ge2$, add
\[
(\neg x_i\vee x_{i+1})
\land(x_i\vee\neg x_{i+1})
\qquad(0\le i<q-1)
\tag{9}
\]
and call this $H_{q,b}$. These binaries enforce a
single shared value $x=x_0=\cdots=x_{q-1}$.
The input still has $q$ physically disjoint ternary
supports, $k=4q$, and a raw minimum backdoor of $q$,
but now
\[
n_H=3q+3,\quad m_{2,H}=4q+2,\quad
M_H=8q+2,\quad
N_H=(32q+10)\lceil\log_2(64(11q+6))\rceil.
\tag{10}
\]
\begin{theorem}[Exact linked label and projection counterexample;
\PROVED]\label{th:link}
For all $q\ge2$,
\[
H_{q,b}\text{ is SAT}\iff b_0=0.
\tag{11}
\]
For $q=2,b=(1,0)$, the independent projection (6) says
SAT, but the linked formula is UNSAT.
For $q=2,b=(0,1)$, the linked formula is SAT.
After charged SCC discovery of the $x_i$ equivalence
class, assigning that single shared bit reduces all
$4q$ ternaries to width at most two, giving a correct
two-branch $O(q)$-time adaptive algorithm for $H$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $x=1$, each $(\neg x_i\vee p_{i+1})$
forces $p_q=1$, contradicting the final anchor.
If $x=0$, each $(x_i\vee y_i)$ forces $y_i=1$.
At $i=0$ the odd/even parity equation with $p_0=1$
is satisfiable exactly when $b_0=0$. If so, for
$i\ge1$ set $p_i=1-b_i$, set $y_i=1$ and
$p_q=0$; all equations and implications hold.
The two $q=2$ labels follow by taking $b=(1,0)$
and $(0,1)$. Local elimination of the $x_i$ in
isolation is unsound after (9) because the quantified
private scopes no longer factor. Branching on the
SCC quotient bit restores conditional independence,
and SCC completes each child.
\end{proof}
In particular, on $G_{q,1^q}$ every single $b_i$ flip
changes UNSAT to SAT, whereas on $H_{q,1^q}$ only the
$b_0$ flip changes the label. The $q$ input-orientation
sensitivity coordinates collapse to one while
$k$ and the raw syntactic backdoor remain unchanged.
This is a statement about orientation sensitivity on
a promised canonical family, \emph{not} permission for
a general SAT decider to skip validating its input.

and peak live workspace. The measured algorithm uses
the following label-blind transitions:

Neither a single fixed algebraic dimension nor a
preselected winning rule is imposed on the algorithm.
The parameter trajectory is
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
G:&(k,h,\text{interface arity})=(4q,q,2)
\ \longrightarrow\ (0,0,2)\quad\text{via exact projection},\\
H:&(4q,q,3)\longrightarrow(4q,1,3)
\longrightarrow(0,0,2)\quad\text{via SCC and one branch}.
\end{array}
\tag{12}
\]
The interface arity counts boundary bits of the
chosen gate factorization, not a universal computational
invariant; all required factorization discovery is charged.
Analysis parameters are not assumed visible to a solver
until their structure is recognized from source data.

\section{Lower bounds, transport and unrestricted endpoint}
On $G_{q,1^q}$, flipping any $b_i$ changes exactly four
signed-literal words in a private gate and produces SAT
at fixed input length. Hence every total deterministic
exact decider of the entire well-formed encoded language
must read at least one of those words for each $i$:
\[
T_A(G_{q,1^q})\ge q=\Omega(N_G/\log N_G)
\tag{13}
\]
in the addressed-literal-word model. If orientation bits
are densely packed into machine words, the precise number
of probes depends on packing; this is not an
encoding-independent $\Omega(N)$ time statement.
There is a matching $O(q)$ source-aware decision scan.
Direct inclusion retains only acquisition order, while
explicit source-to-target materialization writes
$4M+2=\Theta(q)$ addressed words, consuming the
linear source-query bound in a charged transport inequality.
No $2^q$ solving lower bound is implied.

For the prescribed baseline $F_b$ with
$n=q+3,m=q+4,N=(4q+18)\lceil\log_2(64(2q+8))\rceil$,
the two endpoint pairs force $z_0=1,z_q=0$.
It is UNSAT exactly when $b=1^q$, and every
deterministic orientation-query decider needs all $q$
fields on $1^q$. The sign-record map $b\mapsto
G_{q,b}$ preserves this \emph{label} with linear
source acquisition and output cost, not the source's
full witness fibers. The known general upper bound
for arbitrary 2-CNF plus $k$ ternaries is
$2^{|S|}\operatorname{poly}(N)$ for one selected variable
per ternary, $|S|\le k$. It is polynomial for
$k=O(\log N)$ and is no lower bound.
The unrestricted language with arbitrary binary OR
and ternary XOR constraints is NP-complete by
Schaefer's dichotomy theorem. Our canonical subclass
is easy despite belonging to that wider language.
AFFINE-NULLITY-BOUNDARY73 already proves the complementary
sharp per-component nullity-one polynomial versus
nullity-two NP-complete boundary. The present blocks
have nullity two but only two exposed private-block
boundary variables, and are easy.

\section{Reproducible experiment and failure controls}
The full Python standard-library auditor
\path{local_affine74.py} was run on 23 October 2026
with declared seed 202610240908 (no randomness
was needed in the enumerated families).
A single invocation completed 3,103 assertion-accounted
checks with zero failures in approximately 3.22 seconds.
It exhaustively checked all $2^q$ orientation vectors
for $1\le q\le7$ (all labels compared against independent
generic DPLL); all $2^q$ coupled orientations for
$2\le q\le8$, with generic DPLL checks for $q\le5$;
the two-gate false-positive; SCC, Horn, XOR, free-padding
and generic-fallback controls; and both labels through
$q=2048$ uncoupled and $q=512$ linked.
At $q=2048$, $N_G=1{,}032{,}570$ bits, the adaptive
projection used $32{,}768$ local truth tests and one
SCC solve. At $q=512$, the linked variant has
$N_H=311{,}486$ bits and uses at most two switch trials.
A deliberately nonpruning policy enumerated all
$2^{12}=4096$ complete raw backdoor assignments for
each label at $q=12$; the adaptive policy instead
performed 192 local truth tests and one SCC call.
The fixed policy is intentionally restricted and does
not bound unrestricted DPLL/CDCL or arbitrary algorithms.
Raw JSON records each stage's source reads,
recognition checks, local evaluations, branch attempts,
SCC calls, verification and conservative peak workspace.
The code is independently executable. Finite checks
corroborate, but do not replace, the symbolic proofs.
