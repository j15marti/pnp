\section{Claim card and exact scope}
The audited family is
\[
 G=(B_Xx=c)\ \wedge\ [q(Ax_C+r)=b_0]\ \wedge\
 [q(Ax_C+r+e_1)=b_1],                                    \tag{1}
\]
where $X=K_{s,s}$ for odd $s\ge3$, $T$ is the declared star-of-stars
spanning tree, $C=E(X)\setminus T$, $d=|C|=(s-1)^2$, and
\[
 q(z)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{d/2}z_{2i-1}z_{2i}.
\]
Coordinates in mathematics are one-based.  The charge $c\in\F^{2s}$ has
even parity, $r\in\F^d$, and $b_0,b_1\in\F$.  The fixed transform is
$A=I+uv^{\mathsf T}$, where $u$ is all ones and $v$ is the indicator of the
first $d/2$ coordinates.  Since $d$ is a multiple of four,
$v^{\mathsf T}u=0$, hence $A^2=I$.

\section{Primary-literature and duplication preflight}
De Colnet and Mengel's Tseitin results are DNNF/regular-Resolution theorems in
their declared representation and proof models, not arbitrary witness-search
lower bounds \cite{dcm}.  Bardet et al. study algorithms for systems of
quadratic Boolean equations under explicit algebraic assumptions \cite{bardet};
they do not supply an unconditional lower bound for (1).  Berthomieu,
Faug\`ere, and Perret give polynomial algorithms for a regular quadratic
isomorphism problem \cite{bfp}; their result is a useful warning that a dense
coordinate change can conceal an easy normal form, but the proof below is
elementary and deterministic.

\section{Particular solution, kernel basis, and encoding}
Let $a$ be the tree lift of cotree vector $0$ under charge $c$.  For
$j\in[d]$, let $L_j$ be the tree lift of cotree unit vector $e_j$ under zero
charge.  Leaf-to-root elimination gives
\[
 B_Xa=c,\qquad B_XL_j=0,\qquad \pi_C(L_j)=e_j.           \tag{2}
\]
Consequently every affine solution has the unique form
\[
 x=a+Ly,qquad y=x_C,                                    \tag{3}
\]
and (3) is also the direct spanning-tree lift of $y$.  Explicitly
materializing $L$ costs $d|E|$ bits, which is polynomial.  The witness
algorithm need not materialize it: it may assign $y$ to cotree edges and
perform one leaf-to-root pass.

The canonical JSON record has exactly the fields
\texttt{side}, \texttt{charge}, \texttt{phase}, \texttt{b0}, \texttt{b1},
\texttt{transform=rank\_one\_half}, and \texttt{shift=e0}.  Recognition
checks the key set, odd $s\ge3$, vector lengths and bit alphabets, even charge
parity, and the two tags, then reconstructs $K_{s,s}$, $T,C$, and $A$.
Malformed, inconsistent-charge, and off-image strings are rejected.  The
record has $O(s+d)$ bits; explicit graph construction, charge parsing,
elimination, selection, reconstruction, and verification are all polynomial
in its encoded length and in the materialized output length.

\section{Candidate exact upper-bound lemma}
\begin{theorem}[phase translates collapse]
For every valid record above, (1) is satisfiable and a full edge witness is
computable uniformly in polynomial time and space.  Both quadratic residuals
have the same polar matrix $A^{\mathsf T}JA$, of rank $d$, where $J$ is the
block diagonal symplectic matrix.  For the declared $A$, the off-diagonal
density of this matrix tends to $1/2$ as $d$ grows.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $z=Ay+r$.  The paired form satisfies
\[
 q(z+e_1)+q(z)=z_2.                                      \tag{4}
\]
Thus the two residual equations imply the single linear condition
$z_2=\delta:=b_0+b_1$.  They are solved explicitly as follows:
\[
 z^*=\begin{cases}
       b_0e_1+e_2,&\delta=1,\\
       0,&\delta=0,\ b_0=0,\\
       e_3+e_4,&\delta=0,\ b_0=1.
      \end{cases}                                        \tag{5}
\]
Since $d\ge4$, every case is defined; direct substitution gives
$q(z^*)=b_0$ and $q(z^*+e_1)=b_1$.  Set
$y=A^{-1}(z^*+r)=A(z^*+r)$ and lift $y$ through $T$ using (3).
Equations (2)--(5) verify the affine and both nonlinear constraints.

Translation does not change a quadratic's polar form, so both residuals have
$A^{\mathsf T}JA$.  Congruence by invertible $A$ preserves rank, hence the
rank is $d$.  Direct row counting for the rank-one transform gives
$d(d+2)/2$ off-diagonal ones among $d(d-1)$ positions, hence density
$(d+2)/(2(d-1))\to1/2$.  Density is diagnostic only and is not used by the
selector.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact boundary and useful repair.}
At $d=2$, the pair $b_0=b_1=1$ is unsatisfiable, so the $d\ge4$ hypothesis is
load-bearing.  More importantly, the collapse occurs because the two
equations have the same polar form: their difference is affine.  A useful
next obstacle must therefore use at least two genuinely independent polar
forms and survive simultaneous-normal-form and witness-selector attacks.
Even such independence would be a construction property, not a runtime
lower bound by itself.

\section{Counterexample scope and remaining implication}
This family defeats an attempted structural necessity, not the full residual
program.  It permits source access, polynomial workspace, compact evaluation
of $A$, input-assisted reconstruction, and complete preprocessing.  The
algorithm returns one witness and never builds a DNNF or expanded PC proof.
Program length is not workspace, and no state-count, cutwidth, proof-system,
pebbling, ETH, or cryptographic premise is used.

The still-missing claim is an unconditional whole-computation lower bound for
some explicit residual family, or the conditional UWI multiplier
$H(F_{j+1})\ge H(F_j)r(s_j)/B$ with $r(s)\to\infty$ after every parsing,
selection, replay, reconstruction, and joint-resource loss.  Neither follows
from high projected rank, high polar rank, density, or the mere presence of
two nonlinear equations.  Relativization/algebrization and Natural Proofs do
not directly decide this elementary upper bound; they become relevant only
after a genuine unrestricted transfer is proposed.
