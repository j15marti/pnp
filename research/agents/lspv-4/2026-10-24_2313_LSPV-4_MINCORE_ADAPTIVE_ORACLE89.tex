\section{Precise receiving gap and candidate mechanisms}
The ARC/FLMV program seeks a charged, uniform, all-input transition between simultaneous SAT time-space exponent pairs for one deterministic random-access Turing machine per pair. A finite sequence reaching the Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas (FLMV) forbidden box would contradict \(P=NP\); no all-input transition has been established. In particular, separately satisfying smaller children do not imply parent satisfiability.

\noindent\textbf{M1 (proved).} Could an \emph{arbitrarily adaptive} algorithm extract a distinguishing parent bit by querying the SAT status of different strict clause-deletion children? We prove an all-YES oracle transcript for an entire growing proper-3CNF promise family containing both SAT and UNSAT. This strengthens a fixed child-schedule counterexample in a precisely delimited oracle model.

\par\noindent\textbf{M2 (proved).} Can that oracle be combined with charged source reads and still enforce the original independently sensitive orientation fields? Yes: \(q\) independent source-field groups remain necessary on one UNSAT instance even with an unbounded number of free deletion-SAT queries.

\par\noindent\textbf{M3 (falsified as a general lower-bound transfer).} Does the oracle invisibility imply superlinear post-acquisition SAT computation, or a contradiction to ARC? No. One source scan and an SCC or direct implication-chain decision terminate in linear source-word work. The resulting \(\Omega(N/\log N)\) query lower bound is consumed by acquisition and leaves no positive target-work remainder.

\section{Declared encoding and exact canonical family}
For \(q\ge1\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}^{q}\), use original variables \(z_0,\ldots,z_q,u,v\). Put
\[
\begin{split}
F_b={}&(z_0\vee u)\wedge(z_0\vee\neg u)
\wedge(\neg z_q\vee v)\wedge(\neg z_q\vee\neg v)
\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1}^{q} C_i(b_i),\\
C_i(1)={}&(\neg z_{i-1}\vee z_i),\qquad
C_i(0)=(\neg z_{i-1}\vee\neg z_i).
\end{split}
\]
For each of its \(q+4\) proper binary clauses \(D_j=(a_j\vee c_j)\), create a fresh \(t_j\) and replace \(D_j\) with the pair
\[
P(D_j)=(a_j\vee c_j\vee t_j)\wedge(a_j\vee c_j\vee\neg t_j).
\]
Call the resulting proper ternary formula \(P_q(b)\). Every three-literal clause has three distinct variables. The fresh \(t_j\) is \emph{free} for every satisfying source assignment. The variable and clause counts are
\[
n=2q+7,\qquad m=2q+8,\qquad m-n=1;
\]
there are \(q+3\) original and \(q+4\) free auxiliary variables, no functional auxiliaries. The lift is clausewise uniform, assignment-wise equisatisfiable, and not parsimonious:
\[
\#P_q(b)=2^{q+4}\#F_b.
\]
On inputs with any zero orientation, \(F_b\) and \(P_q(b)\) are SAT. The unique all-one orientation gives UNSAT.

Use a read-only addressed fixed-width source record: two header words \(n,m\), followed by \([3,\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3]\) for every clause, with signed-literal record \(\ell=2|\mathrm{var}|+[\mathrm{negative}]\). Each word has
\[
w_q=\left\lceil\log_2(64(4q+16))\right\rceil
\]
bits; hence
\[
N=(8q+34)w_q=\Theta(q\log(q+2)).
\]
Flipping one \(b_i\) changes \emph{exactly two} sign-word records, one in each copy of its chain clause, with no effect on header, length, identifiers, or any other sign group. The \(q\) changed-word supports are disjoint. The unmodified padding form lives inside the fixed NP-complete language of proper 3-CNF, but this promise family is easy.

\section{Minimality and exact status-only oracle obstruction}
\begin{lemma}[PROVED/EXACT, minimum-deficiency core]
The formula \(F_{1^q}\) is minimally UNSAT, and so is the proper-3CNF lift \(P_q(1^q)\). Consequently every \emph{strict} clause-deletion subformula of \(P_q(1^q)\) is SAT.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first two clauses force \(z_0=1\); the last two force \(z_q=0\). All-one chain implications force \(z_0\le z_1\le\cdots\le z_q\), a contradiction. Delete a first-end clause: choose \(z_0=0\) and the auxiliary \(u\) satisfying the remaining end clause, then set every \(z_i=0\). Delete a last-end clause: set all \(z_i=1\) and choose \(v\) satisfying the surviving end clause. Delete the chain clause at \(i\): set \(z_j=1\) for \(j<i\) and \(z_j=0\) for \(j\ge i\), satisfying the untouched endpoints and implications. Thus each one-clause deletion of \(F_{1^q}\) is SAT; the deleted clause is necessarily false in that assignment, or the full formula would be SAT.

Now delete one of the two padded clauses corresponding to a binary source clause \(D_j\). Use a satisfying assignment for \(F_{1^q}\setminus\{D_j\}\). Its \(D_j\) is false, so choose \(t_j\) to satisfy the remaining one of its two ternaries. Every other binary source clause is true and imposes no restriction on its own \(t\). The padded formula with this single ternary removed is SAT. Monotonicity gives SAT for every smaller strict clause-subformula. All variables occur, and \(m-n=1\), attaining the known minimal-unsatisfiability deficiency bound, though deficiency is not a time lower bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT, unlimited adaptive deletion-oracle invisibility]
Fix \(q\). An oracle \(\mathcal O_b(S)\), for \emph{any} proper subset \(S\subsetneq[m]\) chosen at runtime, returns the SAT \emph{status only} of the ordinary self-contained CNF consisting of the indexed clauses \(\{P_q(b)_j:j\in S\}\). For every \(b\in\{0,1\}^q\) and every legal \(S\), \(\mathcal O_b(S)=1\). Thus an arbitrarily adaptive controller, using any number of these calls and any amount of internal computing but \emph{no source-bit observations}, receives exactly the same all-YES transcript on both labels. Adaptation of its query subsets does not change this fact.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(b\ne1^q\) the full parent is SAT, so every subformula is SAT. For \(b=1^q\) the preceding lemma proves that every strict subformula is SAT. The returned bit is therefore constantly one, regardless of \(S,b\). Induction on any adaptive sequence of oracle calls yields identical answer transcripts for every \(b\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT, source acquisition survives the stronger oracle]
Let \(A\) be a uniform deterministic exact decider on all canonical \(P_q(b)\), allowed arbitrary internal state, changing representations, caches, failed strategies, source rereads, and \emph{unbounded adaptive} use of the free oracle above, but allowed to learn actual input fields only through charged addressed source-bit probes. On \(P_q(1^q)\), it must probe at least one bit in each of the \(q\) disjoint orientation-change groups. In particular,
\[
Q_{\rm src}(A,P_q(1^q))\ge q=\Omega(N/\log N).
\]
The same lower bound holds for addressed-word probes, with groups consisting of two word records. This is \emph{not} a post-acquisition time lower bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose the run on \(P_q(1^q)\) never probes any bit from the two signed-literal words encoding a particular orientation \(i\). Flip only \(b_i\), keeping length and every other source word fixed. Every real source observation on both executions remains the same. At every adaptive oracle call, the same strict subset is selected and the oracle answers SAT on both inputs. Induction on the complete deterministic transcript therefore gives identical final output. But the all-one input is UNSAT while the flipped one is SAT, contradicting exactness. Repeat for every disjoint group.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Sharpness and scope.}
A uniform recognizer may scan and validate the complete signed-literal source and decide the canonical family by the OR of the \(q\) zero-orientation indicators, requiring \(O(q)\) addressed-word operations and \(O(\log N)\) writable bits. Unknown ordinary 3-CNFs go to an exact unrestricted fallback without any claimed polynomial bound. Adding pins or coupling clauses, querying the entire parent, requesting boundary witnesses, inspecting model counts, or letting the oracle observe an uncharged source-dependent query body lies \emph{outside} the status-only deletion model. The theorem therefore does not obstruct an all-input adaptive SAT decider.
