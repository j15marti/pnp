\begin{lemma}
Free deletion-only moves can be absorbed into the immediately following paid transition without changing terminal reachability. Consequently a reachability search over post-execution resident subsets, enumerating every possible retained subset and every permitted parent overwrite, is exhaustive.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Given a sequence containing any number of deletion-only moves, commute those deletions forward to immediately before the next execution; deletion does not create an available parent and cannot make any earlier execution legal that was illegal before. Conversely each enumerated pre-/post-retention pattern can be realized by free deletions, a charged legal execution, and free post-deletions. A terminal state containing output 34 requires no further deletion. Every legal path has such a normalized path.
\end{proof}

\section*{A concrete cost-soundness falsifier for an edge-forgetting abstraction}
Paid node 33 (original certificate RES index 56) requires paid parents 31 and 32. Illegally erasing either one of these genuine parent dependencies creates a three-register path. In the over-permissive overwrite model the dropped-edge \(33\leftarrow31\) abstraction reaches the sink using 21 paid executions, and \(33\leftarrow32\) using 17. These are \emph{spurious abstract paths}; the recorded signed RES at node 33 needs both parents. The exact original checker would reject a packet purporting to use the unaltered RES conclusion with one required antecedent deleted. The correct refinement is to retain the authenticated two-parent enabling condition, not to prune any legitimate proof or to charge arbitrary new abstract steps.

This illustrates a distinction: a permissive underapproximation of cost can be conservative for lower-bound \emph{values}, yet an abstraction that forgets producer availability cannot certify \emph{realizability}; the low-cost abstract witness must be replayed in the native checker before it may be treated as a concrete shortcut.
