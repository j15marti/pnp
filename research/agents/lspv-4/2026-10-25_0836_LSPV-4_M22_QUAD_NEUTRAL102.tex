\section{Exact receiving scope, recovered sources and falsifiable hypotheses}
The canonical M22-NONPAIRED1 class \(\CM\) comprises unique proper three-distinct-variable OR clauses, each wholly positive or wholly negative, with each active variable appearing exactly twice in each sign. Let \(\bar C\) reverse literal signs while preserving OR and put \(\CS=\CM\setminus\{F:\forall C\in F,\bar C\in F\}\). Individual complementary pairs, disconnected components, easy instances, and nonminimal UNSAT inputs remain allowed. \(L_\star\) accepts the satisfiable well-formed \(\CS\) encodings, rejecting malformed strings. For valid \(F\), \(3m=4k\), \(m_+=m_-=2k/3\), and \(N=\Theta(k\log(k+2))\). D\"ocker proved NP-completeness of \(\CM\)-SAT \cite{docker}, not a decision-time lower bound.

The original NEUTRAL-PORT100 \cite{port} repairs one removed clause with six new variables and nine new clauses. NEUTRAL-PAIR101 \cite{pair} repairs one positive and one negative clause jointly with six variables and ten clauses, exactly preserving the deletion residual but expanding the input. Three new falsifiable proposals were examined: (H1) two such pair repairs are necessary to repair two positive and two negative deletions (\textbf{FALSIFIED}); (H2) a universal four-clause neutral port repair can use fewer than six auxiliaries (\textbf{FALSIFIED} by a sharp capacity theorem); (H3) a perfectly grammar-restoring neutral repair necessarily preserves the original parent SAT label (\textbf{FALSIFIED}, including on an existing UNSAT M22 source). None of these hypotheses is an all-decider lower-bound statement.

\section{One six-variable gadget for four arbitrary signed port triples}
Choose \emph{two} original positive clauses and \emph{two} original negative clauses. List their positive old literal occurrences as \(x_1,\ldots,x_6\), and their negative variable occurrences as \(y_1,\ldots,y_6\), respecting multiplicity. The same old variable may occur at multiple ports; distinctness is required only inside each original clause. Delete these four clauses, keep all other original clauses unchanged, and introduce private fresh \(a,b,c,d,e,f\).

Insert the following six positive clauses:
\[
 (a\vee d\vee x_1),\quad(a\vee e\vee x_2),\quad
 (b\vee e\vee x_3),\quad(b\vee f\vee x_4),\quad
 (c\vee f\vee x_5),\quad(c\vee d\vee x_6).
\tag{1}
\]
Insert six negative clauses:
\[
\begin{split}
(\neg a\vee\neg d\vee\neg y_1),\quad
(\neg a\vee\neg f\vee\neg y_2),\quad
(\neg b\vee\neg d\vee\neg y_3),\\
(\neg b\vee\neg e\vee\neg y_4),\quad
(\neg c\vee\neg e\vee\neg y_5),\quad
(\neg c\vee\neg f\vee\neg y_6).
\end{split}\tag{2}
\]
Write \(D_4(F)\) for this ordinary replacement and \(F_{\rm del}\) for the four-clause deletion residual.

\begin{theorem}[Exact balanced quad repair]
For every \(F\in\CM\) and every two distinct original clauses of each sign,
\[
\boxed{D_4(F)\in\CS,\qquad
 \operatorname{SAT}(D_4(F))\Longleftrightarrow
 \operatorname{SAT}(F_{\rm del}),\qquad
 (k',m')=(k+6,m+8).}
\]
Every assignment to \emph{all} original variables extends to a satisfying assignment of the \emph{twelve new clauses}, using the same fresh setting
\[
 a=b=c=1,\qquad d=e=f=0.
\]
In particular this holds when signed port occurrences share original names.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each deleted old signed literal occurrence is replaced exactly once with the same polarity. In the six positives each fresh name appears exactly twice: the fresh pairs form the six-cycle
\((a,d),(a,e),(b,e),(b,f),(c,f),(c,d)\).
In the six negatives each fresh name also appears twice: the fresh pairs are
\((a,d),(a,f),(b,d),(b,e),(c,e),(c,f)\).
The old and new names are disjoint, and all six fresh pairs within each sign are distinct, so clauses are proper and unique even if old port names overlap. The old and fresh signed degrees are all \((2,2)\). Each positive clause contains a fresh true literal and each negative a fresh false variable, making its negated literal true. Projection to old variables preserves all retained clauses; the displayed extension proves the converse. Finally the positive clause with pair \((a,e)\) has no negative counterpart containing the pair \((a,e)\); no other gadget can use the fresh names. Thus the result is not fully complement-paired. The net clause increase is \(12-4=8\).
\end{proof}

\section{General sharp neutral-port capacity and optimality}
Suppose a replacement deletes \(d_+\) positive and \(d_-\) negative source clauses, retains every other original clause unchanged, retains all old active names, and introduces \(h\) fresh names with \(p\) positive and \(q\) negative new proper triples. Signed incidence conservation forces
\[
\boxed{h\equiv0\pmod3,\qquad
p=d_++\frac{2h}{3},\qquad q=d_-+\frac{2h}{3},\qquad
(k',m')=(k+h,m+4h/3).}\tag{3}
\]
For a \emph{universally neutral} interface, every independent assignment to all deleted old ports must extend to a satisfying assignment of the new clauses. The port sets are distinct in the lower-bound model, so one legal hostile assignment makes every old port literal in a new clause false.

\begin{theorem}[Port-capacity lower bound, sharp for balanced quads]
For such an independently assignable-port universal neutral replacement,
\[
\boxed{h\ge \frac32(d_++d_-).}\tag{4}
\]
More precisely, if \(t\) of the \(h\) new variables are true on the hostile port assignment, then
\[
\boxed{
2t\ge d_++2h/3,\qquad
2(h-t)\ge d_-+2h/3.}\tag{5}
\]
For \((d_+,d_-)=(2,2)\), at least \(h=6\) auxiliaries and \(p=q=6\) inserted clauses are necessary. Equations (1)--(2) attain all three minima. For balanced deletion of \(2r\) positive and \(2r\) negative original clauses, \(r\) independent copies of the quad gadget attain the universal floor \(h=6r\), \(p=q=6r\) even when the old source happens to share port names.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At the hostile old-port assignment, all \(p\) new positive clauses must be satisfied by true fresh literals. Each true fresh variable occupies exactly two positive occurrences, so \(p\le2t\). Similarly each negative clause needs a false fresh variable, giving \(q\le2(h-t)\). Inserting (3) proves (5), and summing gives \(d_++d_-+4h/3\le2h\), hence (4). For two plus and two minus deletions, (4) gives \(h\ge6\). At \(h=6\), (3) forces six clauses of each sign. The explicit gadget is a witness. Applying the same bound to \(d_+=d_-=2r\) gives \(h\ge6r\), met by disjoint private copies.
\end{proof}

The lower bound is \emph{not} asserted for specialized repairs exploiting incompatible source-port assignments, eliminating old variables, rewriting retained clauses, or departing from the universal port interface. Equation (5) further excludes a six-auxiliary neutral repair when \((d_+,d_-)=(3,1)\) despite four deleted clauses: it would require \(t\ge4\) and \(t\le3\). This is a source-interface restriction, not a lower bound on SAT deciders.

\section{Counterexample: type restoration is not parent reconstruction}
Every \(\CM\) input has \(k=3r\), and \(m_+=m_-=2r\), so its \emph{entire} original clause list can be partitioned into \(r\) balanced quads. Delete all old clauses and apply the gadget to each group. The output remains \(\CS\), and it is SAT for \emph{every} original input because the fixed fresh assignment satisfies all new clauses and no retained parent clauses remain. In particular, for D\"ocker's established \(198\)-variable, \(264\)-clause UNSAT source \(U\), the resulting ordinary \(\CS\) output has \((k',m')=(594,792)\), is SAT, and has \(N'=26168\) indexed bits instead of \(N=7154\) \cite{docker}. This is a symbolic consequence of the published UNSAT example and this construction, not a fresh exhaustive check of its \(2^{198}\) assignments.

Thus a type-correct family of individually satisfiable deletion children cannot, by itself, reconstruct the SAT label of the original parent. Compatibility, gluing and original-clause information are genuine extra obligations. Keeping an \emph{unchanged} UNSAT \(U\) component while repairing an independent \(Y\) component preserves UNSAT through two levels, but merely because \(U\) remains; this opposite-label control does not demonstrate semantic contraction.
