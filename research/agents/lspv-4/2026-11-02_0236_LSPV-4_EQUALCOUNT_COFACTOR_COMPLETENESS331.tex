\section{New exact promise-restricted coNP-hardness theorem}
For any canonical original $F$ and two coherent profiles $p_0,p_1$ differing only at the value of one pinned original name, define $\mathsf{EQ}_{\rm sib}(F,p_0,p_1)$ to ask whether the two cofactors are \emph{pointwise equal on every common future original assignment}. Impose the strong promise $\Pi$ that the two profiles have equal anonymous width histograms, neither contains an empty or unit residual source clause, and the two cofactors have equal \emph{total} model counts. The latter is a promise, not a freely acquired count oracle.

\begin{theorem}[coNP-hardness despite equal model counts and equal shape]
The promise-restricted one-original-variable-sibling problem $\mathsf{EQ}_{\rm sib}|_\Pi$ is coNP-hard under polynomial-time, promise-preserving many-one reductions; on promised inputs, NO-instances are polynomially checkable by a complete original-name continuation witness. Specifically, for every valid canonical original $G(y)$ form
\[
F_G(y,z)=G(y)\land Z_6(z)
\]
with six fresh original names and take $p_b=(z_1=b)$, for $b=0,1$. Both are coherent profiles of the \emph{same valid canonical original}, and
\[
\#(F_G|p_0)=\#(F_G|p_1)=9\,\#\mathrm{SAT}(G),
\quad
\eta(p_0)=\eta(p_1),
\tag{4}
\]
while
\[
\boxed{F_G|p_0\equiv F_G|p_1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad G\text{ is UNSAT}.}
\tag{5}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Variable-disjoint union preserves every original three-distinct, monotone, duplicate-free, two-positive and two-negative quota. Original source grammar is independently checked. The compiled forward circuit and both cofactors factor:
\[
C_{F_G}(y,z)=C_G(y)\land C_{Z_6}(z),\qquad
(F_G|p_b)(y,z')=G(y)\land h_b(z').
\tag{6}
\]
All $G$ clauses remain unpinned ternary source clauses, and the $Z_6$ siblings have the same profile (3), proving the profile promise without semantic work. The Cartesian model counts multiply, giving (4). If $G$ is UNSAT, both functions are identically FALSE. If $G$ is SAT, append the fixed witness in (2) to a $G$ witness and obtain unequal cofactor outputs. Thus (5) is exact. D\"ocker's canonical SAT is NP-complete, so its complement is coNP-complete, and this polynomial promise-preserving map proves coNP-hardness. To make the mapping total over malformed source encodings, first validate $G$ and replace an invalid input with a fixed valid canonical UNSAT source (e.g. the authenticated 198-variable source); this is a constant hardcoded output and still satisfies the promise. A NO-instance has a full continuation assignment on which the two forward circuits differ, a polynomial source-checkable witness. This gives coNP verification \emph{on promised instances}, without claiming that checking the equal-count promise itself is easy.
\end{proof}
