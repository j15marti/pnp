\section*{LEGALFLAT1 theorem}
Assume T(n) is polynomial, all source, challenge and response strings have polynomial total length, and there is a polynomial-size strategy description S with polynomial-time evaluator
\[
o_i=\operatorname{Eval}(S,x,R,i,c_{\le i},o_{<i}).
\]
For a complete proposed challenge sequence C, recursively generate the responses using S. Define Legal(C) to mean that every challenge encountered is legal relative to that generated prefix. Then S wins exactly when
\[
\forall C\;[\operatorname{Legal}(x,R,S,C)\Longrightarrow V(x,R,C,o_{\le T})].
\]
The bracketed predicate W is polynomial time. Hence existence of a polynomial-size source and succinct winning strategy is
\[
\boxed{\exists R,S\;\forall C\;W(x,R,S,C)},
\]
which lies in \(\Sigma_2^P\).

\paragraph{Proof.}
Fix x,R,S,C. Evaluate the strategy sequentially for at most T(n) rounds. Before each response evaluate the current legality predicate. If a challenge prefix is illegal, the universal branch is vacuous. Otherwise evaluate the prescribed response and continue. Polynomially many polynomial-time evaluations on polynomial-length strings take polynomial total time, so W is in P. If the displayed formula holds, every legal adaptive challenge path is accepted under S. Conversely, a winning S accepts every legal challenge path, so every C either first becomes illegal or remains legal and satisfies V. Thus the formulations are equivalent. \(\square\)

Under P=NP, PH collapses to P, so standard prefix search selects polynomially balanced valid (R,S) whenever one exists. This is a closure statement only.
