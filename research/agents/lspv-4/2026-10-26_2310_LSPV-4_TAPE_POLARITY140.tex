\section{Proof of the co-nondeterministic polarity firewall}
\begin{theorem}[One sequential input head, promised equality]
For $a,b\in\{0,1\}^m$, consider fixed-length source records $I_m(a,b)$ with an $a$-dependent left block, a fixed middle gap, and a $b$-dependent right block, of $N_m=\Theta(m\log(m+2))$ encoded bits. Assume the $j$th left sign bit $a_j$ and its right partner $b_j$ are discoverable in that order by one forward input scan in $O(N_m\operatorname{polylog}N_m)$ time and $O(\log N_m)$ work bits. On this exact promise, $\{I_m(a,b):a=b\}$ is recognizable co-nondeterministically in $O(N_m\operatorname{polylog}N_m)$ worst-branch time and $O(\log N_m)$ work space with $O(\log m)$ guessed bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Universally guess $j$ using $\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil$ binary bits. If invalid, accept. Otherwise scan forward to $a_j$, remember one bit, then scan forward to $b_j$; reject iff they differ and otherwise accept. Apply one uniform length-dependent clock and accept timeout branches. If $a=b$, every branch accepts. If $a\ne b$, at least one valid guessed index witnesses a mismatch and rejects. Index generation, parsing, input head movement, and counter cells are included. The certificate is the $O(\log m)$-bit mismatch index, and no deterministic discovery algorithm is asserted.
\end{proof}
