\section{Claim card and exact quantifiers}

Let $K\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$ be a canonically encoded family of SAT instances.
Assume there is a polynomial-time computable many-one reduction
$r:\{0,1\}^*\to K$ satisfying
\[
 x\in\SAT\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad r(x)\in\SAT.       \tag{1}
\]
The encoding is complete, and $N=|F|$ denotes its bit length.  For a total
uniform deterministic exact SAT decider $A$, $T_A(F)$ is the ordinary-machine
running time on that exact encoding.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf{WC}(K):\quad
 \forall A\in\operatorname{Dec}_{\rm uniform}(\SAT)\;
 \forall d\in\mathbb N\;\forall N_0\;\exists F\in K:
 |F|\ge N_0\ \land\ T_A(F)>|F|^d .                       \tag{2}
\end{equation}

The candidate attacked in this run was that (2), specialized to
$K=\Hsigned$, is an intermediate structural clean-core theorem that might be
derived before the separation.  The weakest useful outcome is an exact
classification of its strength.

\section{Exact equivalence theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{SIGNED-CLEANCORE-WORSTCASE-EQUIV1}]
For every family $K$ satisfying (1),
\[
 \mathsf{WC}(K)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \SAT\notin\mathsf P.
                                                               \tag{3}
\]
Consequently, because SAT is NP-complete,
\[
 \mathsf{WC}(K)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P\ne NP.          \tag{4}
\]
For $K=\Hsigned$, hypothesis (1) is supplied by D\"ocker's NP-completeness
theorem for unique proper sign-monochromatic three-clauses with exactly two
positive and two negative occurrences per variable \cite{Docker19}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $\mathsf{WC}(K)$ holds.  If SAT had a polynomial-time
exact decider $A$, there would be constants $c,k$ with
$T_A(F)\le c|F|^k+c$.  Choose an integer $d>k$ and then $N_0$ large enough
that $cN^k+c\le N^d$ for every $N\ge N_0$.  This contradicts (2).  Hence
$\SAT\notin\mathsf P$.

Conversely, suppose $\SAT\notin\mathsf P$ and (2) fails.  Quantifier
negation gives a total exact SAT decider $A$, an exponent $d$, and $N_0$ such
that
\[
 \forall F\in K\quad |F|\ge N_0\Longrightarrow
 T_A(F)\le |F|^d.                                             \tag{5}
\]
Construct a SAT decider $B$ by computing $r(x)$ and running $A(r(x))$.
Correctness follows from (1).  The reduction has polynomial output length,
say $|r(x)|\le q(|x|)$.  On outputs of length at least $N_0$, (5) bounds the
second stage by $q(|x|)^d$.  Outputs shorter than $N_0$ form a finite set; the
maximum running time of the fixed total machine $A$ on that set is a constant.
Thus $B$ is polynomial time, contradicting $\SAT\notin\mathsf P$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Bridge classification]
\texttt{SIGNED-CLEANCORE-WORSTCASE-AS-INTERMEDIATE1} is \RETIRED.  The
statement (2) itself remains \OPEN{} and is a valid final formulation of the
separation.  Any claimed proof of
\[
 \SAT\in\mathsf P\Longrightarrow\mathsf{WC}(\Hsigned)
\]
already derives a contradiction equivalent to $P\ne NP$; it is not a
solver-independent consequence available for free under $P=NP$.
\end{corollary}

This theorem does not infer runtime from semantic-state count.  It uses only
the exact reduction, the complete encoding, totality of $A$, and explicit
quantifier negation.  The finite exceptional set in (5) is charged rather
than hidden.

\section{Primary-literature and barrier preflight}

The first obstacle was whether the corrected clean family is genuinely
NP-hard rather than another recognizable easy island.  D\"ocker proves
exactly the needed NP-completeness statement for $\Hsigned$ \cite{Docker19};
this is a \textbf{directly transferable theorem}.  The second obstacle was
whether a complexity core or a restricted time--space theorem could supply
(2) without proving the separation.  Lynch's complexity-core theorem assumes
the language is already outside P \cite{Lynch75}; importing that hypothesis
for SAT is a \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER}.  FLMV supplies a genuine simultaneous
time--space exclusion for the stated random-access SAT machine
\cite{FLMV05}, but it transfers only after the same compiled realization is
placed in its forbidden region.  This is a \textbf{useful theorem requiring
a new model-matched transfer}, not a proof of (2).

The project has repeatedly and correctly called (2) ``separation-strength.''
The new point is the biconditional (3), including the finite-length and
reduction-cost details.  No current primary source changes the standard
barriers: the abstract proof relativizes whenever the reduction and decider
are relativized together, and it algebrizes under the analogous hypothesis.
It therefore supplies no Baker--Gill--Solovay or Aaronson--Wigderson escape
\cite{BGS75,AW09}.

\section{Hostile controls and SAME\_REALIZATION}

Horn, 2SAT, affine/Tseitin and bounded-treewidth islands may all be handled by
their own polynomial algorithms.  Independent products and the paired
$K_{3,3}$/prism products from TESTS-0020 give infinite recognizable all-YES
islands.  QGUARD1 correctly shows that these proper islands are compatible
with (2): the many-one reduction in (1) uses the whole signed family, and (5)
would require one decider fast on that whole family.

Padding, redundant clauses and alternate encodings can change $N$ and must be
included in the reduction bound $q$.  DIRECT, COPY, DEAD, DELAY, INTERP and
RECOMP wrappers change traces and constants but not the logical equivalence.
A recognize-first wrapper may skip a compatibility profile on a proper
island; it cannot be polynomial on all of an NP-hard $K$ unless SAT is in P.
The SAT output remains one bit, and neither direction of the theorem claims
that an arbitrary execution materializes $H(F)$, a witness, a transcript,
memory layout, communication protocol or reconstruction path.

SAME\_REALIZATION is explicit: in the reverse direction, $B$ includes the
reduction, output serialization, the actual run of $A$ on $r(x)$, and the
finite exceptional cases in one total ordinary program.  No resource of an
external protocol is charged to the original execution.

\section{Post-quantum and cross-domain ledger}

Schur squares, puncturing/shortening, quadratic relation ranks, Hilbert
functions, syzygies and graded Betti numbers are \textbf{FAILED TRANSFERS} to
(3): no code or algebraic representation is forced by a total SAT decider,
and exposed structure may be an algorithmic weakness.  Collision entropy is
an \textbf{EXACT OBJECT/THEOREM TRANSFER} for a declared map, but it does not
alter the reduction argument.  LWE-style black-box extraction,
special-soundness forks, observability, VLSI routing, communication cuts and
proof-search lower bounds are \textbf{useful structural analogies requiring a
new transfer theorem}.  Cryptographic binding and random-oracle assumptions
remain inadmissible in an unconditional separation.
