\section{Exact input/object types and algebraic audit}
Take a fixed-record input $x\in\{0,1\}^B$ to the LSPV-2 all-input verifier language with $q\ge3$ designated witness variables and $m\ge1$ ternary records. Set
\[
w=\lceil\log_2(q+1)\rceil,\quad B=m(1+3(w+1)),\quad
K=2^{\lceil\log_2(q+3m)\rceil},\quad h=\log_2K,\quad C_K=\frac{Kh(h+1)}4.
\]
The peer's explicitly parameterized Batcher sort--join--inverse verifier has the gate count
\[
G=C_K(13w+38)+19K+(K-1)(4w-1)+m(36w+16)-1.\tag{1}
\]
Its alias-safe proper-three-CNF compiler uses nine variables and thirteen clauses per Boolean gate, and separately pins each of the $B$ \emph{source bits} with three private variables and four proper clauses. With output/constant pins, the exact base counts are
\[
 n_0=9G+3B+q+8,\qquad m_0=13G+4B+12.\tag{2}
\]
The declared occurrence-ring $R_{15}$ signed exact-$(2,2)$ normalizer has the following two-level count equations \cite{sort166}:
\[
\begin{aligned}
n_1&=33m_0+48n_0,&m_1&=44m_0+64n_0,\\
n_2&=3036m_0+4416n_0,&m_2&=4048m_0+5888n_0.
\end{aligned}\tag{3}
\]
\begin{lemma}[Exact independent coefficient expansion]
For every declared $q,m$, regardless of the truth value of the fixed-record instance,
\[
\boxed{m_2=105616G+33856B+5888q+95680},\qquad
\boxed{n_2=79212G+25392B+4416q+71760}.\tag{4}
\]
In particular, $m_2\ge33856B$, $n_2\ge25392B$ and $3m_2=4n_2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Substitute (2) into (3) and collect coefficients. For the clauses, the gate multiplier is $4048\cdot13+5888\cdot9=105616$, the source-bit multiplier $4048\cdot4+5888\cdot3=33856$, and the constant $4048\cdot12+5888\cdot8=95680$. The variable expression is analogous: $3036\cdot13+4416\cdot9=79212$, $3036\cdot4+4416\cdot3=25392$, and $3036\cdot12+4416\cdot8=71760$. All omitted terms are nonnegative. Both identities satisfy the exact signed incidence relation $3m_2=4n_2$. No SAT-semantic assumption is used in this arithmetic proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pinned circuit output cannot be strictly shorter; fixed architecture only]
Suppose the peer's level-two target is \emph{actually represented as an ordinary, uncompressed proper signed exact-$(2,2)$ ternary CNF} with canonically renumbered variable identifiers $1,\dots,n_2$, and four literal occurrences per identifier. Let $M_2$ be its physical encoded \emph{bit length}. In canonical decimal-ID DIMACS form,
\[
\boxed{M_2\ge 32\sum_{i=1}^{n_2}\operatorname{digits}_{10}(i)
\ge32\sum_{i=1}^{25392B}\operatorname{digits}_{10}(i)
=\Omega(B\log B).}\tag{5}
\]
In a fixed-width binary-ID ordinary encoding, separately counting each of the $3m_2$ sign/name fields also gives
\[
 M_2\ge3m_2[1+\lceil\log_2(n_2+1)\rceil]
=\Omega(B\log B),\tag{6}
\]
ignoring all headers, terminators, validation, and other additional fields. Consequently, for this particular pinned and twice-regularized compiler, no claim $M_2\le B^{\lambda+o(1)}$ with \emph{fixed} $\lambda<1$ holds even on a single well-formed input family. As a source-to-ordinary-SAT child operation, the length exponent is at least one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A proper signed exact-$(2,2)$ target has four signed literal occurrences of each of its $n_2$ variable names. Every occurrence must print its own decimal identifier, and the print costs $8\operatorname{digits}_{10}(i)$ bits. Summing gives the first inequality. The second uses $n_2\ge25392B$. The decimal sum is $\Theta(z\log z)$. In a fixed-width binary representation, each signed literal field requires one sign bit plus the specified name field width; there are exactly $3m_2$ fields. The lower bound does not assume any particular guest algorithm, distinguish SAT from UNSAT, or convert an encoded child into a shorter compressed language. It is not a complexity lower bound on \SAT.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Transfer back to the canonical ordinary-SAT input measure $N$.}
Equations (1)--(6) use $B$ as the \emph{fixed-record} input length, never the canonical ARC $N$ silently. To convert an \emph{uncompressed canonical proper 3CNF} source of encoded length $N$ with $q$ legal numbered variables and $m$ actual ternary clauses to those fixed records, a direct literal-by-literal adapter with all records active has $B=\Theta(m\log(q+1))$ and $N=O(B)$ when headers and row terminators are counted. In particular, on the infinite disjoint signed exact-$(2,2)$ family built from four-clause SAT blocks $G$, $q=3k$, $m=4k$, and $B=\Theta(N)=\Theta(k\log k)$. Thus this \emph{specified syntactic adapter} has $M_2=\Omega(N\log N)$ on actual ordinary inputs. The same length conclusion is not asserted for unrestricted semantic preprocessing, which could recognize and solve the easy disjoint blocks first. An arbitrary ``ordinary SAT'' encoding requires its own parsing and encoding-size proof; this report does not silently cover compressed grammars or promise-only inputs.

\section{Sparse physical flips do not bound guest reads}
The peer proved a *different* localization statement: changing one fixed-record bit alters exactly four source-pin sign positions in $H_{q,m,x}$ and exactly sixteen distinct level-two sign positions in the regularized formula. Thus there are at most $16B$ \emph{source-dependent}\ sign positions. This counts syntactic \emph{influence}, not the length $M_2$ of the ordinary output, the number of input probes an arbitrary guest performs, nor the cost of random-access variable/consumer Rank/Select. The crucial converse is false.

\begin{theorem}[Scan-before-solve adversary; conditional on an exact solver]
Let $A$ be any fixed uniform total deterministic ordinary-SAT decider in the declared indexed read-only-input FLMV machine model with joint exponents $(d,s)$, $d\ge1$, $s\ge0$. Define $A_{\mathrm{scan}}$ as follows: on its complete physical $L$-bit input, scan each of its $L$ positions in order, charging all indexed-address operations and reads; then execute $A$ unchanged and return its answer. In the same model,
\[
T_{A_{\mathrm{scan}}}(L)\le L^{\max\{d,1\}+o(1)}=L^{d+o(1)},\quad
S_{A_{\mathrm{scan}}}(L)\le L^{s+o(1)},\tag{7}
\]
where logarithmic scan indices are included in writable/index space. This \emph{one fixed} all-input algorithm is just as SAT-correct as $A$ and necessarily makes at least $L$ input-bit probes \emph{on every input}. In particular, running it on a virtual level-two child forces at least $M_2$ correctly serviced virtual input probes even though only $16B$ child polarity fields depend on source bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The scan always terminates after visiting all $L$ bit positions; the result of the scan does not change the answer supplied by $A$. A sequential binary index can be maintained in $O(\log L)$ index/work bits, and the scan uses at most $O(L\operatorname{polylog} L)$ bit operations on the matched random-access Turing machine; the scan is fully charged, with no uncounted hardware word operation. Addition of the original $A$ computation preserves both upper exponents for $d\ge1$ and $s\ge0$. It also gives at least $L$ genuine input access requests by construction. If input is virtual, each request invokes its addressed-bit locator. No assumption that the original $A$ reads all positions is made.
\end{proof}
This is a \emph{counterexample to the claimed uniform implication} ``few source-dependent positions $\Longrightarrow$ every solver's read count is small'', not a lower bound on all solvers or on the existence of an alternative contractor. A compiler existentially tailored to a particular $A$ could choose a different policy, and a solver may simplify the instance without scanning. Conversely, if a contraction argument substitutes only $T_A(M_2)\le M_2^{d+o(1)}$ as its black-box call bound and $M_2=N^{1+o(1)}$ or larger, the recurrence does not certify a strict reduction of its $d$ time exponent. It cannot infer an actual $\Omega(N^d)$ running time from an upper bound.

\section{Exact resource ledger and forbidden-region comparison}
For child lengths $\{m_i\}$ representing \emph{complete ordinary SAT encodings}, the conditional volume lemma
\[
V=\sum_i m_i\le N^{b+o(1)},\quad M=\max_i m_i\le N^{\lambda+o(1)}
\Longrightarrow\sum_i m_i^d\le VM^{d-1}\le N^{b+\lambda(d-1)+o(1)}\tag{8}
\]
is valid for $d\ge1$, even for adaptive paths, provided every possible history obeys the bounds. If \emph{each} guest input probe requires worst-case $N^{u+o(1)}$ addressed-bit generation and local all-history work is $N^{r+o(1)}$, a safe conditional time upper bound is
\[
 c\le\max\{r,u+b+\lambda(d-1)\}\tag{9}
\]
\emph{only if} the virtual-input emulator truly invokes the guest for $m_i^{d+o(1)}$ steps with per-probe upper bound $N^u$, and all source/parser/index/generation work is included. If instead $N^u$ already bounds \emph{aggregate} access, the appropriate expression is $\max\{r,u,b+\lambda(d-1)\}$, not (9). Source-workspace read/write, unchanged writes, arithmetic/control, child headers/bodies, certificate production/printing, failed fallbacks, replay/cache and strategy switches are included in $r$ or explicit additional maxima. In particular, the $\Omega(B\log B)$ child from (5) is \emph{not} an $N^{\lambda}$ child with $\lambda<1$ in the direct canonical adapter on which $B=\Theta(N)$.

A simultaneous space guarantee for \emph{the same machine} must include the guest's $S_A(m_i)$, the child buffer when physically materialized, recursive locator workspaces, retained parent and ancestor buffers, all answer registers (including one bit per child if stored), index tapes, generator metadata, certificate access/replay state, output that is retained for random access, and other live scratch. One-way output cannot be substituted as a free read-only guest input. An on-demand indexed virtual-child oracle is a \emph{simulation design}, not a gratis model conversion. Circuit gates, wires, actual gate flips, hardware cycles and ordinary TM bit-steps are different ledgers.

FLMV Corollary~5.8, read in the original PDF \cite{flmv}, states
\[
\mathrm{NTIME}[n]\not\subseteq\mathrm{DTISP}[n^c,o(n^{\beta(c)})],
\quad\beta(c)=\frac{(c+2)/c^2-c}{2}.\tag{10}
\]
The designated strict endpoint is $c_*=241/200$, $\beta(c_*)=11642479/23232400$. A \emph{hypothetical} same-machine pair $(11/10,1/2)$ has exact strict gaps
\[
 c_*-11/10=21/200,\qquad \beta(c_*)-1/2=26279/23232400.\tag{11}
\]
The target $S\le N^{s+o(1)}$ implies $S=o(N^{\beta(c_*)})$ only for a \emph{fixed positive} gap $\beta(c_*)-s$, since the $o(1)$ term need not have a sign. Also the standard quasi-linear ordinary-SAT to $\mathrm{NTIME}[n]$ transfer requires its own matched-model polylog losses. No near-endpoint equality may be smuggled through an $o(1)$.

Van Melkebeek Theorem~1.3 is a \emph{two-machine} conondeterministic SAT time-$n^c$ plus deterministic SAT time-$n^d$/space-$n^e$ contradiction, subject to $(c-1)d<1$ or $cd(d-1)-2d+1<0$ \cite{vm}. A sound \emph{existential UNSAT proof verifier} can be complemented into the proper conondeterministic SAT polarity only with an \emph{all-branch clock}, including malformed and timeout branches. There is no free deterministic certificate acquisition. Theorem~6.3 instead uses \emph{full nonuniform SAT decision circuits}, together with a distinct small-space deterministic $\Sigma_2\mathrm{SAT}$ algorithm. Under the strictly EXTRA numerical assumption $d=901/500$, the separately conditional values
\[
 C(d)=\frac{2d-1}{d(d-1)}=\frac{651000}{361301}\approx1.801821749732,\quad
q_*=\frac{d^2}{d^2-1}=\frac{811801}{561801}\approx1.444997427915\tag{12}
\]
are exact arithmetic only. Neither a fixed-record witness verification circuit of exponent near one, nor a representation of a single instance with size exponent $1.29$, supplies the full decision circuit or the $\Sigma_2$SAT small-space hypothesis.

Bathie--Williams Theorem~1.4 and Definition~1.5 address input-aware polylogtime-uniform \emph{NAND formulas} of logarithmic depth, with gate address paths and source-dependent gate-description access charged \cite{bw}. Our peer's sorting--join--inverse verifier has potentially \emph{linear depth}; its $O(B\log^3B)$ \emph{gate count} cannot invoke the depth theorem. Nor can Pippenger--Fischer multitape circuitization, Allender--Kouck\'y threshold \emph{wire} counts, Borodin's \emph{depth} results, or Shalunov's supplied-circuit evaluation space $O(\sqrt{z\log z})$ be used as all-input FLMV construction/evaluation time and simultaneous space without proving and charging their model transfers \cite{shalunov}. Williams' cosine SAT theorem needs subpolynomial space, not an unqualified $t+s$ inequality. This report imports \emph{no} new bound from any of these differing models.
