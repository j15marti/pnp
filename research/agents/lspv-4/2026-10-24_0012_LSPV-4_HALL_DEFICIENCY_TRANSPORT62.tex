\section{Three falsifiable mechanisms}
\noindent\textbf{M1, PROVED/EXACT.} The canonical Horn-selector lift of every proper 3-CNF preserves maximum clause-variable deficiency exactly.
\par\noindent\textbf{M2, PROVED/EXACT plus KNOWN THEOREM.} If source maximum occurrence is four and exactly $h$ source variables occur four times, the maximum deficiency of source and target is at most $\lfloor h/3\rfloor$. Szeider's published SAT algorithm thus gives a $2^{\lfloor h/3\rfloor}\operatorname{poly}(N)$ decision upper bound.
\par\noindent\textbf{M3, FALSIFIED/RETIRED.} A connected, sign-balanced, proper 3-CNF prism family has maximum deficiency linear in $n$ yet is solved by an explicit linear-time source-aware algorithm.
\paragraph{Explicit model.} Two header words and four fixed-width words per source clause (arity, three signed IDs, zero padding for binaries). For $n$ variables and $M$ clauses,
\[
w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+M+1))\rceil,\qquad N=(4M+2)w.
\]
Charge source reads, input validation, unsuccessful matching and branching, witness maps, compiler output words, replay, caching, and peak live space. Allow uniform fixed local gadgets, renaming, matching and parsing, but no free decide-first reduction. A lower bound on this word access model does not automatically hold on arbitrary packed representations.

\section{Exact matching-deficiency transport}
For a finite CNF $F$, let $V(Q)$ denote the source variables appearing in a selected clause subset $Q$. Define
\[
\delta^*(F)=\max_{Q\subseteq F}\{|Q|-|V(Q)|\}.
\]
By the deficiency form of Hall's theorem, if $\nu(F)$ is the maximum clause-to-variable incidence matching size, then
\[
\boxed{\delta^*(F)=M-\nu(F)}.
\]
In particular, if $\delta^*=0$ a clause-saturating matching assigns distinct variables to satisfy all clauses, producing a polynomial-time SAT witness. Matching is sufficient, not necessary, for SAT.

For proper exactly-three-clause source
\[
\Phi=\bigwedge_{j=1}^m(\ell_{j1}\vee\ell_{j2}\vee\ell_{j3}),
\]
introduce fresh selectors $s_{j1},s_{j2},s_{j3}$ and construct
\[
H(\Phi)=\bigwedge_{j=1}^m
\left[(s_{j1}\vee s_{j2}\vee s_{j3})
\wedge\bigwedge_{a=1}^3(\neg s_{ja}\vee\ell_{ja})\right].
\]
The binary backbone is Horn, all $m$ positive ternaries have disjoint supports, and each selector occurs once with each sign. It has $n_H=n_0+3m$ variables, $m_2=3m$ binaries, $k=m$ genuine ternaries, $M_H=4m$ clauses, and
\[
N_H=(16m+2)\lceil\log_2(64(n_0+7m+1))\rceil.
\]
Selectors are existential choices, not functional gates or completely free pad bits.

\begin{theorem}[Exact deficiency and matching transport, PROVED/EXACT]
For every proper $\Phi$,
\[
\boxed{\delta^*(H(\Phi))=\delta^*(\Phi)},\qquad
\boxed{\nu(H(\Phi))=3m+\nu(\Phi)}.
\]
This includes both SAT and UNSAT sources, arbitrary sign patterns and shared source-variable identifiers.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For every source clause subset $J$, choose all four Horn-lift clauses in each gadget indexed by $J$. The resulting target has $4|J|$ clauses and $3|J|+|V_\Phi(J)|$ variables, so its deficiency is $|J|-|V_\Phi(J)|$. Hence $\delta^*(H)\ge\delta^*(\Phi)$.

Conversely fix any target subset $Q$. Let $d_j$ indicate whether its positive ternary from group $j$ is included and let $t_j\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ count included binary implications from that group. Let $U$ be all original variables mentioned by selected implications. The selected selector variables number three for $d_j=1$ and $t_j$ for $d_j=0$. Thus the exact count is
\[
|Q|-|V(Q)|=\sum_{j:d_j=1}(t_j-2)-|U|.
\]
Only groups $J_3=\{j:d_j=1,\ t_j=3\}$ contribute positively. Since $U\supseteq V_\Phi(J_3)$,
\[
|Q|-|V(Q)|\le |J_3|-|V_\Phi(J_3)|\le\delta^*(\Phi).
\]
Take the maximum to obtain equality. By Hall, $\nu(H)=4m-\delta^*(H)=3m+\nu(\Phi)$.
\end{proof}
For any original assignment $\alpha$, if $r_j(\alpha)$ literals of clause $j$ are true, the number of satisfying selector extensions equals $\prod_j(2^{r_j(\alpha)}-1)$. Consequently, $\Phi$ is SAT iff $H(\Phi)$ is SAT, and a target witness projects onto the original assignment. Canonical construction, recognition, original-source recovery, lifting and witness replay are all $O(n_0+m)$ word work/space, while the explicit target uses $16m+2$ output words.

\section{The degree-four quantitative rung}
\begin{theorem}[Source occurrence excess, PROVED/EXACT]
Suppose every source clause has three distinct variables, every variable has at most four total occurrences, and precisely $h$ source variables have degree four. Then
\[
\boxed{\delta^*(\Phi)=\delta^*(H(\Phi))\le\lfloor h/3\rfloor}.
\]
For $h\le2$ both source and target are necessarily SAT, with polynomial-time constructible matching witnesses.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every clause subset $J$ satisfies
\[
3|J|=\sum_{x\in V(J)}\deg_J(x)\le3|V(J)|+h.
\]
Subtract and maximize over $J$, then apply the previous theorem. For $h\le2$ the integer maximum deficiency is zero, so Hall yields a clause-saturating matching.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{FPT algorithmic consequence, KNOWN THEOREM.}
Szeider (2004) proves that SAT on $n$ variables with maximum deficiency $\kappa$ is decidable in $O(2^\kappa n^3)$ in the stated unit-cost combinatorial model, with a satisfying assignment or regular-resolution certificate. With polynomial source parsing and construction costs this yields
\[
T(\Phi),T(H(\Phi))\le2^{\lfloor h/3\rfloor}\operatorname{poly}(N),
\qquad h=O(\log N)\implies T=N^{O(1)}.
\]
The Szeider solver is not reimplemented in the attached audit; only the parameter identity is independently tested.
\paragraph{Structural sharpness.}
Take all four signed clauses on three variables whose number of positive literals is even. They are SAT and each variable occurs twice positively and twice negatively. Here $h=3$, $n=3$, $m=4$, $\delta^*=1=h/3$. Repeating disjointly gives scalable equality. The next control removes the disjointness escape.

\section{Connected large-deficiency family with an easy decision}
Use MAIN101's cubic prism $P_q=C_q\square K_2$, with $2q$ vertices, $3q$ edges, and one $x_e$ per graph edge. Its $2q$ vertex clauses contain the three incident edge variables, oppositely signed at endpoints. Order the $3q$ edges and introduce $y_0,\dots,y_{3q-1}$. For each $e_i$ add
\[
(y_i\vee\neg y_{i+1}\vee x_{e_i})\wedge
(y_i\vee\neg y_{i+1}\vee\neg x_{e_i}),
\]
indices cyclic. The connected source $G_q$ has $n=6q$, $m=8q$, every variable occurring twice in each sign and $h=6q$. The whole formula gives $\delta^*\ge m-n=2q$; occurrence excess gives $\delta^*\le h/3=2q$, so
\[
\boxed{\delta^*(G_q)=2q=h/3}.
\]
Orient each ring cyclically; set $x_e$ to satisfy the incoming ring-edge literal at its head, and every $y_i=0$. This is an explicit satisfying assignment. Canonical grammar recognition, source read, witness and replay are $O(q)$ word operations. A total uniform exact SAT decider first recognizes this family, decides it directly, and falls back to a generic exact SAT solver elsewhere. Therefore linear maximum deficiency does not impose exponential time on each instance, even for connected balanced no-pure-literal sources. The NP-complete worst-case lower bound remains OPEN.
