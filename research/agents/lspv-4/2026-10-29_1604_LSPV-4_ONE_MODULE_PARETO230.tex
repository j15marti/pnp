\section{BUILD: formula, connectivity, exact optimality}
Fix $r\ge2$ and indices modulo $r$. The $3r$ distinct variables are $a_i,u_i,v_i$ and the $4r$ clauses are
\begin{align}
 &(a_i\vee u_i\vee v_i),\qquad (a_i\vee u_{i+1}\vee v_i),\notag\\
 &(\neg a_i\vee\neg u_i\vee\neg v_i),\qquad
 (\neg a_i\vee\neg u_{i+1}\vee\neg v_i),\quad i=0,\ldots,r-1. \tag{1}
\end{align}
Each variable has two positive and two negative occurrences, with three distinct variable names in each clause. All clauses are distinct signed triples. The $u$--$v$ variable-only graph has cyclic edges $u_i-v_i-u_{i+1}$ and is connected. Every remaining $a_i$ attaches to that cycle.

\begin{theorem}[Full one-private-module realizable line]\label{thm:line}
For every $r\ge2$ and every nonempty $Y\subseteq\{a_i\}$ with $b=|Y|\le r$, regard the complement of $Y$ as one connected nonempty private module. Let $q$ be the minimum size of a syntactic hitting set for clauses \emph{not meeting $Y$}. Then
\[
\boxed{q=r-b,\qquad b+q=r=n/3.}\tag{2}
\]
Moreover, for \emph{every} assignment $\alpha$ to $Y$,
\[
\boxed{\exists\beta\ F_r(\alpha,\beta)=1.}\tag{3}
\]
Hence the exact projected boundary message is the constant TRUE relation, not a required $2^b$-bit table.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are precisely four clauses for every unpinned $a_i$, so $4(r-b)$ clauses avoid $Y$. Every private variable appears in at most four of these clauses, giving the hitting lower bound $q\ge 4(r-b)/4=r-b$. Choosing all unpinned $a_i$ hits all of them and attains equality. Removing any subset of $a$-variables leaves the private $u$--$v$ cycle connected; every unpinned $a_i$ is adjacent to it. Finally set all $u_i=1$ and all $v_i=0$, independently of all $a_i$: each positive clause is satisfied by its positive $u$ literal and each negative clause by its negative $v$ literal. This proves the full projection.
\end{proof}
\begin{proposition}[Tight k=1 structural inequality]
For any proper strict signed exact-(2,2)-3CNF with one private module and residual clause-hitting $B$ of size $q$, $n\le3(b+q)$. Every integer point of equality $1\le b\le n/3$ for $n=3r$ is realized by (1).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The $4n/3$ width-three clauses are all hit by $Y\cup B$. Each of its $b+q$ variables participates in only four clauses; hence $4n/3\le4(b+q)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:line} gives equality at every listed integer boundary.
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Scope fork.} The endpoint $b=r,q=0$ uses the \emph{residual} definition: the private module has $2r>0$ variables but no $Y$-free clauses. Under an alternate grammar requiring internally owned clauses or requiring $B$ to hit boundary-touching clauses, this endpoint is not valid. Do not conflate definitions.

\section{Exact proxy curves, crossing and realizability}
The prior relaxed multi-module necessary inequality yields
\[
q_{\rm rel}(n,b)=\max\left\{0,\left\lceil\frac{n-3b}{12b}\right\rceil\right\},\quad
t_{\rm rel}=b+q_{\rm rel},\quad s_{\rm table}=b.
\]
In its active continuous region $0<b<n/3$, $t_{\rm rel}^{\rm cont}=b+n/(12b)-1/4$, with derivative $1-n/(12b^2)$, positive second derivative $n/(6b^3)$, minimum at $b^*=\sqrt{n/12}$, and earliest $t_{\rm rel}=s_{\rm table}$ at $b=\lceil n/3\rceil,q=0$. These are \emph{permitted logs} of optional proxies, not physical complexity lower bounds.

At $n=1200$ the exact integer relaxed minimum is 20 at $b=9,10,11$, only $b=9$ nondominated in $(t_{\rm rel},s_{\rm table})$. Our \emph{same-source realized} one-module curve is instead $t_{\rm one}(b)=b+(400-b)=400$, $s_{\rm table}(b)=b$ for every integer $1\le b\le400$, with $dt_{\rm one}/db=0$. Thus $(t,s)=(400,400)$ is a genuinely realized first equality, but \emph{proxy dominated} by the same formula's $(400,1)$ point. The $t_{\rm rel}=20$ near $b=10$ is \emph{not realized by this $k=1$ family}. LSPV-2 has a different realizable many-module $n=1200$ point $b=32,q=24,t=56$; comparing these as same-input algorithm runtimes would be invalid.

At $b=\alpha\sqrt{n}$ the relaxed time logarithm divided by $\sqrt n$ tends to $\alpha+1/(12\alpha)$, with slope $1-1/(12\alpha^2)$; the realizable one-module time logarithm remains $n/3$. Canonical unpadded bit input length is $N=\Theta(n\log n)$, so both unoptimized proxies are asymptotically superpolynomial even though this recognized family is polynomially decidable.

\section{Same-realization native time, peak space and asymptotic firewall}
For $r=400$ the exact canonical ASCII DIMACS source has 25,188 bytes, or $N=201,504$ physical bits. A deterministic \emph{source authenticator} checks its header, every signed literal, ordering, delimiters and final length and emits the uniform witness $a_i=0,u_i=1,v_i=0$. On a bit-cost indexed RAM and on a fixed-$k$ sequential read-only-input multitape TM, a conservative implementation uses $T=O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)$ and additional work $S=O(\log N)$ bits. A two-fully-writable-tape machine can keep the input unchanged on tape 1 and use $O(\log N)$ auxiliary tape-2 bits. A one-head read/write TM obtained by conservative simulation has $T_1=O(N^2\operatorname{polylog}N)$ and $P_1=O(N)$ \emph{total} visited footprint, including source. A fixed UTM gives polynomial simulation with unclaimed exact exponents. The single-tape conversion $T_1=O(T_k(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2)))$ and $P_1=O(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2))$ is only an upper construction.

An implementation that actually materializes an explicit truth bit for each boundary row pays at least $2^b$ bits \emph{for that chosen representation}. A streaming brute-force hitting-set enumeration can omit the table and use polynomial work space; on this special recognized source the exact relation is simply TRUE and needs neither $2^b$ rows nor $2^{b+q}$ search. None of these costs creates a general SAT time lower bound or an FLMV contradiction. A single total correct SAT/UNSAT controller can authenticate the easy family first, then use complete exponential DPLL on other formulas; there is no supplied answer bit and no all-input polynomial guarantee. This promise contains only SAT instances, so it supplies no same-family NO resource bound. The recognizer and DPLL share a fixed program.
