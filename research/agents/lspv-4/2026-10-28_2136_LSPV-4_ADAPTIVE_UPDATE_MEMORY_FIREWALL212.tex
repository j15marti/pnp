\section{PROPOSE: four falsifiable bridges}
Our immediate bottleneck is the difference between a small one-time summary and an \emph{incrementally maintainable} semantic interface whose source may be modified during adaptive search. The four independently testable conjectures were fixed before source search.
\begin{description}
\item[]\textbf{H1, positive dynamic composition.} Constant-width Boolean-matrix chains admit uniform exact moving summaries with $O(m)$ retained matrix records and $O(\log m)$ matrix compositions per point replacement. \textbf{RESTRICTED PROVED.}
\item[]\textbf{H2, root-only constant state.} One total-product matrix alone suffices to maintain exact decisions after arbitrary replacement of a factor, without replay. \textbf{FALSIFIED}: two sources with identical root products have different post-update answers.
\item[]\textbf{H3, arbitrary editable small state.} A deterministic preprocessed state of $o(m)$ \emph{information bits} can handle all subsequent point overwrites and exact decision queries on the scalar subclass without rereading the source. \textbf{FALSIFIED}: $m-1$ bits are necessary.
\item[]\textbf{H4, RAM-to-tape cost preservation.} $O(\log m)$ tree updates on a word-RAM automatically mean $O(\log m)$ sequential-tape movements. \textbf{FALSIFIED} for the explicitly stored heap tree on a single head; a conservative sequential implementation is polynomial, but may take $\Theta(m)$ head travel per update. This is not a universal tape lower bound.
\end{description}

\section{BUILD: exact finite-semigroup model}
A relation is a Boolean matrix $M\in\{0,1\}^{d\times d}$ for constant $d=16$. Its product is composition over the Boolean semiring,
\[
(A\odot B)_{ij}=\bigvee_{u=0}^{15}(A_{iu}\land B_{uj}).
\]
For masks $I,F\subseteq\{0,\ldots,15\}$, the chain is SAT exactly when $I^\top\odot M_1\odot\cdots\odot M_m\odot F=1$. Ordinary 3CNF conversion uses four signed bits per state, forbids disallowed transitions by width-eight clauses, and replaces each width-eight clause by six proper ternary Tseitin clauses with five fresh auxiliaries. This exact existential conversion is explicitly checked in the reproducer for all $2^8$ assignments to the eight original literals (with all 32 auxiliary choices). Metadata, numbers, and regenerated clause bytes are charged.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical scalar ordinary-3CNF source]\label{lem:scalar}
Put $E=e_0e_0^\top$ and $Z=0_{16\times16}$. For bits $b_i\in\{0,1\}$ let $M_i=E$ if $b_i=1$ and $M_i=Z$ otherwise, with $I=F=\{0\}$. The canonical ordinary proper-3CNF chain $\Phi(b)$ satisfies
\[
\operatorname{SAT}(\Phi(b))\iff\prod_{i=1}^m b_i=1.
\]
Each formula has $1536m+60-6\sum_i b_i$ clauses and $1284m+34-5\sum_i b_i$ variables. In a standard unpadded ASCII DIMACS encoding, its complete length is $N=\Theta(m\log(m+2))$ bits.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$E\odot E=E$ and $Z$ absorbs all products; endpoints enforce state zero. For each transition there are $256-b_i$ disallowed pairs, each generating six proper ternary clauses and five auxiliaries. Each endpoint has fifteen excluded four-bit states, each generating two ternary clauses and one auxiliary. The count follows. Since each transition has at least 255 disallowed pairs, a constant fraction of its numeric literal identifiers have $\Theta(\log(m+2))$ decimal digits for large $m$; writing all clauses gives the matching $\Theta(m\log(m+2))$ length. No variable padding, oracle table, or hidden advice is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact dynamic summaries with retained leaves]\label{thm:positive}
For arbitrary $16\times16$ Boolean relations $M_1,\ldots,M_m$ and any online point replacements $M_j\gets M'_j$, a deterministic, uniform balanced binary product tree retains at most $2\cdot 2^{\lceil\log_2m\rceil}$ constant-size matrices. Each edit requires at most $\lceil\log_2m\rceil$ Boolean-matrix compositions; the root product is the exact current chain product, and SAT/UNSAT queries require one constant-size endpoint test. All intermediate states remain exact on both answer classes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pad to a power of two with identity matrices. The invariant at every internal node is left-child product followed by right-child product. Boolean-matrix multiplication is associative, so a changed leaf alters only its ancestor path. Recompute the path in order. There are exactly $\lceil\log_2m\rceil$ nontrivial levels. A rejected attempted edit followed by rollback is \emph{two} edits and both are charged. The algorithm never assumes an answer-class bit.
\end{proof}

The total-product root can be exported into one canonical relation child. For the static contraction in the earlier CHAIN-ORDINARY-CLOSURE213 report, group $m$ matrices into $k=\max\{1,\lfloor m^{1/4}\rfloor\}$ consecutive products, preserving both endpoints. The physical child length is $O(k\log(k+2))=O(N^{1/4}\log N)$, with closure on the restricted canonical grammar. The dynamic tree can compute each group product in $O(\log m)$ tree compositions, then explicitly write the $k$ relations and their ordinary 3CNF body. The \emph{retained parent tree costs $\Theta(m)$ bits}; it is not permissible to represent this dynamic editable interface as only its small physical child.

\section{New dynamic no-replay impossibility and answer-side probe costs}
\begin{theorem}[No-reread retained-state information lower bound]\label{thm:memory}
Suppose a deterministic preprocessing stage is given $\Phi(0,b_2,\ldots,b_m)$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:scalar}. It produces an internal state $z$ of $B$ bits. After this stage the algorithm may receive an arbitrary sequence of \emph{point-overwrite operations} $b_j\gets c$ and must correctly answer whether the resulting scalar chain is SAT, but it may not reread the original source, consult an uncharged external advice store, or retrieve emitted output. Then the collection of possible retained states satisfies
\[
\#\{z\}\ge2^{m-1},\qquad\boxed{B\ge m-1}.
\]
The information measure counts all persistent finite-control, head-position, saved ancestor, and auxiliary-output information, not just changed work-tape symbols.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All $2^{m-1}$ initial suffixes have the same initial answer UNSAT because $b_1=0$. Assume two distinct suffixes $v\ne w$ give identical retained state $z$. Choose $j\ge2$ where they differ. Issue one identical, predetermined overwrite stream setting \emph{every} coordinate except $j$ to one. This stream makes the final answer equal to the untouched bit $b_j$, which differs between $v$ and $w$. Equal retained states, a fixed deterministic program, and equal update streams cannot yield different answers. This contradicts correctness. Therefore the retained-state map is injective, requiring at least $m-1$ bits of distinguishability. In a fixed finite-alphabet, fixed-$k$ tape machine, the count of work-tape configurations includes head-position factors $O(k\log(S+2))$ and finite control: $m-1\le O(S)+O(k\log(S+2)+\log N)$. Thus $S=\Omega(m)$ for this \emph{no-reread dynamic model}. If the source is reread, the theorem does not apply; that access and time must instead be charged.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Root-only replay requires linear source probes on both answer classes]\label{prop:probes}
Suppose the preprocessing retains only the total scalar product and $m$ (not intermediate factors). After receiving the single update $b_1\gets1$ on the initially zero-first-bit family, a deterministic algorithm which retrieves suffix bits one by one from a read-only source oracle must make $m-1$ leaf probes on some SAT input and on some UNSAT input.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the suffix $b_2=\cdots=b_m=1$, every leaf must be queried: if index $j$ is unqueried, setting its bit to zero changes SAT to UNSAT while preserving the full observed transcript. Let $j$ be the last bit queried on this all-ones suffix. The suffix with $b_j=0$ and all other suffix bits one shares the first $m-2$ answers with the SAT run, so at least the $m-1$st probe is required on this UNSAT instance as well. Neither side receives its answer in advance. Preprocessing that stores other information escapes this \emph{root-only} restriction but incurs that storage cost.
\end{proof}

These are \emph{restricted dynamic-data-structure and oracle-interface lower bounds}. They do not say arbitrary SAT algorithms need $\Omega(m)$ post-acquisition work. Indeed a one-shot solver for the scalar family reads the inputs and evaluates their AND, and the broader matrix chain is tractable.

\section{Native resource ledgers: indexed RAM, two-tape OFF, one-tape, and UTM}
Let $N$ be the complete original canonical physical bit length, $U$ the total number of \emph{executed} overwrites (including failed adaptive attempts and rollbacks), $X=N+O(U\log(m+U+2))$ the total encoded source-and-update length, and $L_{\rm out}$ the actual written answer/child length. The RAM bound treats each 256-bit relation as $O(1)$ words at sufficiently large $X$, and charges word width and addressing. The OFF bound deliberately allows repeated input validation sweeps and physical work-tape travel. If an offline program emits a fully physical group-product child only once at the end, $L_{\rm out}=O(k\log k)$ in the restricted grammar; if one emits a new full child after \emph{every} update, all $U$ outputs contribute their complete length. A fixed two-fully-writable-tape simulator may leave its input tape unchanged and use the same upper bound but may not import a 2-OFF symbol-change lower bound. The one-tape output is either on counted writable cells or a charged separate stream, not a free indexed table.

In the declared heap-sequential layout, a leaf location and its root aggregate are $\Theta(m)$ cells apart. Any update that physically touches both therefore makes $\Omega(m)$ head movements on that single work head. This is \emph{architecture-specific}: alternate layouts, extra tapes, and symbolic update rules are not excluded. The single-tape simulation formula is a conservative construction arrow, not a reverse lower-bound transfer. Fredman--Saks cell-probe theorems have their own update/query and word-size assumptions and are not used to claim a new general SAT bound \cite{fredmansaks}.

\section{Polynomial-value rank firewall}
A rank with polynomial \emph{bit length} need not bound the total number of edits. A width-$w$ binary counter starting at $2^w-1$ and strictly decreasing by one takes $2^w-1$ updates while every state occupies $w$ bits. An adaptive algorithm with a nonnegative integer rank $r$ decreases in every step and with $r\le N^a$ in \emph{numeric value}, polynomially bounded intermediate full representations, and polynomial actual generation and access cost per step, does halt in polynomial time; the weaker hypothesis $\log(r+1)=O(N^{O(1)})$ does not. The reproducer checks $w=4,8,12$, giving 15, 255, and 4,095 edits.
