random-access Turing-machine model of Fortnow--Lipton--van
Melkebeek--Viglas (FLMV) \cite{FLMV}. Under \(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\)

\[
 c_*=\frac{241}{200}=1.205,\qquad
 d_*=\frac{11642479}{23232400}=\beta(c_*)>\frac12,\qquad
 \beta(c)=\frac12\left(\frac{c+2}{c^2}-c\right).
\]

\((t,s)\mapsto

\section{Primary-source model check}
FLMV Section 2.1 counts work/index tapes, permits read-only
indexed input, has write-only unidirectional output, and
excludes input/output tapes from writable-space usage
\cite{FLMV}. An input index must be \emph{written} and its contents
erased for an indexed access; no free random-access RAM, free
readable child-output tape or uncharged machine-state advice
is inserted. The full physical count includes source and work
tape reads, all writes \emph{including unchanged writes},
address arithmetic, loop/control/branch steps, representation
header and body bits, discovery, validation, source-to-child
renaming, gluing, failed routes, cache/replay and retained
ancestors/buffers. Actual changed-cell flips, cumulative bit-time,
hardware wires/transistors and program-description bits are
\emph{separate quantities}, not surrogate physical lower bounds.

FLMV Theorem 3.1 and Section 3.1 give near-linear locally
bit-computable reductions from fixed nondeterministic linear-time
languages; Corollary 5.8 gives the simultaneous exclusion
\(\NTIME[n]\not\subseteq
\DTISP[n^{c_*},o(n^{d_*})]\).
Williams \cite{Williams}, Section 2.1 and Theorem 2.1,

\section{Improved two-level source-probe composition}
Let an actual ordinary first child \(Y(F)\), for parent length \(N\),
have \(L_1=N^{\lambda+o(1)}\) binary bits, and let
\(Z(Y)\) have \(L_2=L_1^{\mu+o(1)}\) binary bits.
One fresh indexed first-bit service \(G_1(F,j)\) costs at most
\(N^{g+o(1)}\) total bit steps and
\(N^{\eta_1+o(1)}\) writable/index space, including all
root-address work and any repeated setup. The second-bit
service \(G_2(Y,i)\) carries out at most
\(L_1^{u+o(1)}\) \emph{local} bit steps, including issuance
and arithmetic for indexed \(Y\)-probe requests, but excluding
the cost of implementing those requests by \(G_1\);
it makes at most \(L_1^{p+o(1)}\) such requests and uses
\(L_1^{\eta_2+o(1)}\) live local work/index bits.
Here \(u,p,g,\eta_i\ge0\), and a bounded or polylogarithmic
number of \(Y\) requests means \(p=0\).
A fixed policy performs at most \(N^{a+o(1)}\) sequential final
calls to the \emph{same supplied} ordinary-SAT decider \(A\);
all setup, source validation and strategy selection use
\(N^{r+o(1)}\) time and \(N^{h+o(1)}\) space; retained
ancestors and caches contribute \(N^{\kappa+o(1)}\) peak bits.
Actual child semantic eligibility and correctness on both
labels are \emph{independent premises}, never consequences of this ledger.

\begin{theorem}[Separate local-work and virtual-probe upper ledger]
For the declared literal \emph{fully lazy} implementation, if
the policy is correct on its stated source class and every
ordinary \(Y,Z\) child is eligible for its declared guest, then
one sequential composed implementation has joint resource
upper exponents
\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \gamma_2&=\max\{\lambda u,\lambda p+g\},\\
 t'&\le\max\{r,\ a+\lambda\mu t+\gamma_2\},\\
 s'&\le\max\{h,\kappa,\eta_1,\lambda\eta_2,
                    \lambda\mu s\}.
 \end{aligned}}\tag{1}
\]
All policy or ancillary \(A\)-queries are separately charged;
\(N^b\) invocations at length \(N^\rho\) add time
\(b+\rho t\) plus fully nested access, guest space \(\rho s\),
and any separately live materialized \(N^\rho\) buffer.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each final guest takes at most \(L_2^{t+o(1)}\)
steps and therefore makes no more than that many
virtual input probes. Servicing one last-level probe costs
at most
\[
 L_1^{u+o(1)}
 +L_1^{p+o(1)}N^{g+o(1)}
 =N^{\max\{\lambda u,\lambda p+g\}+o(1)}.
\]
Multiplying by sequential guest count \(N^{a+o(1)}\),
including failed calls, and adding fully charged setup
gives (1). A depth-first indexed simulation stores the
single live guest work/index tapes, two live generator
states, the declared parent/policy/cached state, and
charged address and control bits. They belong to the
\emph{same execution}; finite sums give the displayed
maximal space exponent. Output/child buffers not mentioned
in the premise are \emph{not} free, so an eager variant
requires a new peak-buffer term. Rewriting identical
symbols and resetting index tapes are steps. The
result is a conservative upper bound for a specified
program, not a necessity or an all-algorithm lower bound.
\end{proof}
Taking \(u=p\) recovers the coarse nested-tax term
\(\lambda u+g\) of ARC-NESTED-ACCESS-TAX113.
If \(p<u\), the earlier multiplicative treatment of
local arithmetic as if every operation accessed the parent
can be strictly loose. For the \emph{arithmetic-only,
conditional} values
\[
 a=\frac35,\quad\lambda=\mu=\frac12,\quad
 t=\frac65,\quad g=\frac14,\quad
 u=\frac15,\quad p=0,
\]
the refined time exponent is \(9/10+1/4=23/20=1.15\),
whereas the coarse bound is \(9/10+1/10+1/4=5/4=1.25\).
With all other peak parameters at most \(1/2\) and
\(s=1/2\), the refined \emph{numerical} pair
\((23/20,1/2)\) lies in the strict box.
\textbf{No all-input machine, semantic child policy,
or hard-image alignment is exhibited by these values};
they cannot be promoted into \(E\).

\section{Two actual ordinary-CNF levels and one public probe}
For each \(q\ge3\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}^{q}\), construct
the ordinary cyclic 2CNF \(F_b\) enforcing
\(u_i\oplus u_{i+1}=b_i\), with indices cyclic.
It is SAT iff \(\bigoplus_i b_i=0\).
The explicit fixed-slot CNF binary encoding uses an arity
tag, unary field width \(w\), fixed-width variable and
clause counts, and each literal's sign plus \(w\)-bit
variable ID. Source length is \(N=\Theta(q\log q)\).
The source is a \emph{promised easy} affine/2SAT
family, not a hard-image or all-ordinary-SAT input
normalizer.

Let \(m_1=\max\{2,\lfloor\sqrt N/
\log_2(N+2)\rfloor\}\).
The first ordinary proper-3CNF \(Y_b\) contains
seven of the eight ternary signed clauses over
\(\{v_1,v_2,v_3\}\) whose falsified assignments run
from \(000\) through \(110\). Its eighth clause
is \((\neg v_1\vee\neg v_2\vee\neg v_3)\)
if source parity is odd and
\((v_4\vee v_5\vee v_6)\) if parity is even.
Append \(m_1\) disjoint independent positive ternary
clauses on fresh names \(7,\ldots,6+3m_1\).
The signed-clause enumeration kills \emph{all}
eight assignments iff parity is odd; otherwise
the all-ones assignment satisfies the first
three variables and the independent clauses can
all be satisfied. Thus the ordinary-CNF SAT
predicate is exactly preserved on \emph{both} labels.

Critically, the first sign bit of the eighth clause
in the fixed-slot encoded \(Y_b\) is a \emph{publicly
indexed marker} equal to \(1\) for UNSAT and \(0\)
for SAT, at
\[
 j_*=H_1+7\cdot3(w_1+1),\tag{2}
\]
where \(H_1\) is the first-child header length.
A direct polynomial-bit-time first-bit accessor
may inspect the full parent source and calculate
cycle parity on every query, then compute
the requested ordinary \(Y_b\) bit using fixed
arithmetic on the clause template.
It costs \(N^{1+o(1)}\) root bit work per query
and \(O(\log N)\) generation indexes, without
materializing an \(N^{1/2}\)-length guest tape.

Build the second ordinary proper-3CNF \(Z_b\)
with the \emph{same} signed-core rule and
\(m_2=\max\{2,\lfloor\sqrt{L_1}/
\log_2(L_1+2)\rfloor\}\) fresh independent
positive clauses. The second-level bit accessor
makes \emph{one} first-child request, at address
\(j_*\), and directly indexes its own fixed-slot
clause template to answer the requested bit.
The two genuinely ordinary lengths are
\[
 L_1=N^{1/2+o(1)},\qquad
 L_2=L_1^{1/2+o(1)}=N^{1/4+o(1)}.\tag{3}
\]
Consequently \(\SAT(F_b)=\SAT(Y_b)=\SAT(Z_b)\)
for both SAT and UNSAT, and each constructed
child is a valid \emph{ordinary} CNF input at
two consecutive levels. These are \emph{source
specific normalizers}, not calls to an all-input
ARC solver and not guaranteed members of one
FLMV-hard reduction image.

\section{Four fully distinguished implementation ledgers}
A hypothetical final guest which \emph{first
performs one complete sequential indexed input
scan} on \(Z_b\) demonstrates the following
\emph{access-service costs before its actual SAT
decision work}:
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\toprule
\text{declared implementation}&
\text{root input-bit reads / total leading work}&
\text{extra live readable space}\\
\midrule
\text{re-scan all }Y\text{ for every }Z\text{ bit}
 &\Theta(NL_1L_2)=N^{7/4+o(1)}
 &O(\log N)\\
\text{re-query one public }Y\text{ marker per }Z\text{ bit}
 &\Theta(NL_2)=N^{5/4+o(1)}
 &O(\log N)\\
\text{eagerly build and retain all }Y\text{ via literal }G_1
 &\Theta(NL_1+L_1L_2)=N^{3/2+o(1)}
 &L_1+O(\log N)\\
\text{acquire parity once, index }Z\text{ directly}
 &\Theta(N+L_2)=N^{1+o(1)}
 &O(\log N)\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}\tag{4}
\]
The third row uses \emph{literal repeated}
\(G_1\) for each eagerly emitted \(Y\) bit.
It is \emph{not} the optimized once-summary
implementation in the fourth row. The third
row has a counted retained \(N^{1/2+o(1)}\)
child buffer; the other rows do not. Header
serialization, every body field, parent and
work/index accesses, unchanged writes,
control and arithmetic all contribute to the
\(N^{o(1)}\) factors for these \emph{specific}
simple direct-index algorithms. Total guest
cost, branch/selector overhead, extra validation,
failed attempts, ancestor retention, cache
construction and gluing must be added before
any \emph{complete} SAT time-space claim.
No displayed bound is a worst-case lower bound
against arbitrary exact SAT algorithms.

\begin{lemma}[A publicly observable SAT-status bit is a decider]
Let \(\mathcal H\) be any SAT-input class and
suppose one \emph{uniform} indexed source
program \(G\) produces, at a public computable
address \(j(F)\), the exact bit
\(\SAT(F)\) or its complement for
\emph{every} \(F\in\mathcal H\).
If computing that bit, \emph{including} address
discovery, input reads, validation and the
program's actual workspace, costs at most
\(N^{g+o(1)}\) bit time and \(N^{\eta+o(1)}\)
writable/index space, then \(\SAT\) restricted
to \(\mathcal H\) is decided within those
\emph{simultaneous} bounds.
In particular, for one fixed FLMV-hard
locally indexable image \(\mathcal H^\dagger\),
\(g<c_*\) and \(\eta<d_*\) would contradict
the FLMV exclusion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On input \(F\), compute the public address
and run the stated bit procedure once;
complement its result if necessary.
No second solver is needed. The counted
resource bounds refer to that \emph{one}
execution. For \(\mathcal H^\dagger\),
compose with the original fixed FLMV
near-linear local SAT reduction. Its
polylogarithmic bit-access overhead and
logarithmic index-space accounting preserve
strict constant exponent slack and yield
the prohibited one-machine decider for the
fixed hard \(\NTIME[n]\) language.
\end{proof}
This is why the present marker optimization
works on the \emph{easy} cycle source: it
has intentionally exposed the SAT answer.
For \emph{general} ordinary SAT or the
hard image, an equally cheap all-input
public marker is itself a missing
complexity-theoretic breakthrough.
The same warning applies to \(A\)-dependent
selectors and status-guided source policies:
their real discovery and A-query costs
must be included.
