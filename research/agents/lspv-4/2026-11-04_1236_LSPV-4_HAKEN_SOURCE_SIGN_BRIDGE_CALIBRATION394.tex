\section*{Exact established family and restriction}
Let
\[
F_h=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{h+1}\left(\bigvee_{j=1}^h x_{i,j}\right)
\wedge\bigwedge_{j=1}^{h}\bigwedge_{1\le i<k\le h+1}
(\neg x_{i,j}\vee\neg x_{k,j}).
\]
It is UNSAT, with $h(h+1)$ variables, $(h+1)+h{h+1\choose2}$ clauses and exactly $L_h=h(h+1)^2$ literal occurrences. In a fixed-order signed-index binary source encoding with independent one-bit signs and fixed-width variable IDs, $N_h=\Theta(h^3\log(h+2))$ bits. Flipping one sign preserves the total encoded length. This is not the same as changing the length of a minus symbol in ASCII DIMACS.
\noindent\textbf{KNOWN (Haken 1985).} There are $c>0,h_0$ such that for all $h\ge h_0$ and \emph{every} ordinary unrestricted shared-DAG Resolution refutation $\pi$ of this explicit unary family, $|\pi|\ge2^{ch}=2^{\Omega((N_h/\log N_h)^{1/3})}$. A proof line is a clause derived by ordinary binary Resolution from original clauses; arbitrary pivots, width and sharing are allowed. This does not cover Extended Resolution, GF(2), Res($\oplus$), conservative auxiliary definitions, $R_{\rm mix341}$, or arbitrary SAT machines. Cook's 1976 polynomial Extended Resolution refutations are the mandatory countercontrol.
\begin{lemma}[Pivotality of every original source sign]
Every one-sign-flip neighbor $F_h^{(p)}$ is SAT.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $p$ belongs to a pigeon clause for pigeon $i$, leave pigeon $i$ in no hole and place each of the other $h$ pigeons in a distinct hole. The new negated literal satisfies the modified pigeon clause; all other clauses hold. If $p$ belongs to collision clause $(i,k,j)$, place $i,k$ in hole $j$ and biject the other $h-1$ pigeons onto the other holes. Only that one original collision clause is false and the flipped literal satisfies it. Original $F_h$ is UNSAT by pigeon counting.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Randomized representation-robust input acquisition, linear only]
Fix a total uniform adaptive randomized correct SAT decider $A$ with per-input error at most $\varepsilon<1/2$ on SAT, UNSAT and malformed input strings. All access to original source input bits is charged, even during preprocessing and lazy or guest computation. Then on the UNSAT input $F_h$,
\[
\mathbf E[\#\text{ distinct original sign positions queried}]\ge (1-2\varepsilon)L_h.
\]
When one native input probe reveals at most $w$ sign bits, its expected paid source reads are at least $(1-2\varepsilon)L_h/w$. The deterministic exact bound is $L_h$ sign reads. This is $\Omega(N_h/\log N_h)$ in the one-bit model, not an exponential SAT running-time bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Couple identical random tapes on $F_h$ and a one-sign-flip SAT neighbor $F_h^{(p)}$. Their transcripts and outputs can differ only if coordinate $p$ is queried. The probability of output UNSAT differs by at least $1-2\varepsilon$ because the correct labels differ. Hence the probability of querying $p$ on the original is at least $1-2\varepsilon$. Sum over all $L_h$ sign positions.
\end{proof}
