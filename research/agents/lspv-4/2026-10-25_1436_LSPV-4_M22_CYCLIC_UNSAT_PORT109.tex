\section{An unconditional UNSAT port permutation identity}
Let \(F_0,\ldots,F_{p-1}\) be variable-disjoint UNSAT CNFs. Choose \(C_i=(\ell_i\lor D_i)\) in \(F_i\), where \(D_i\) disjoins the remaining literals. For a permutation \(\pi\) moving entire same-signed literal expressions, define
\[
G_\pi=\bigwedge_{i=0}^{p-1}\bigl[(F_i\setminus\{C_i\})\land(\ell_{\pi(i)}\lor D_i)\bigr].
\]
\begin{theorem}[UNSAT port permutation]
\(G_\pi\) is UNSAT for every permutation \(\pi\), every disjoint UNSAT source collection and every selected clause. If the sources belong to \(\CM\), all selected literal occurrences have the same sign, the resulting clauses are proper and distinct, and no mixed signs are introduced, then \(G_\pi\in\CM\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A hypothetical satisfying assignment of \(G_\pi\) satisfies \(F_i\setminus\{C_i\}\) for each \(i\). Because \(F_i\) is UNSAT, this restriction must make every literal of its deleted \(C_i\) false, in particular \(\ell_i\) and each tail literal in \(D_i\). Every new clause \(\ell_{\pi(i)}\lor D_i\) is therefore false, contradiction. Each selected occurrence is lost at its original position and gained at its permutation destination, with the same signed literal. Signed occurrence counts and clausewise monotonicity persist; uniqueness and properness must be checked for the concrete source.
\end{proof}
