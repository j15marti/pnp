\section{A completely charged conditional fork-join lemma}
Fix $0<\lambda<1$, $\alpha,\gamma,\delta,\sigma,\beta\geq0$ and a fixed deterministic total ordinary-SAT decider $A$ on FLMV's machine, with actual simultaneous all-input ceilings $T_A(m)\le m^{K+o(1)}$ and $S_A(m)\le m^{L+o(1)}$. Suppose a total source-authenticated controller on an $N$-bit ordinary formula $F$ obtains $q\le N^{\gamma+o(1)}$ genuine ordinary-SAT children $F_i$, each of actual serialized length $m_i\le N^{\lambda+o(1)}$, with a \emph{proved} source-relative equivalence
\[
\operatorname{SAT}(F)\iff\bigvee_{i=1}^{q}\operatorname{SAT}(F_i).
\tag{1}
\]
Source inspection, discovery, failed policies and common validation cost $N^{\alpha+o(1)}$. For each attempted child, all policy setup and source lookups (including unsuccessful ones) cost at most $N^{\delta+o(1)}$, materializing its buffer costs $O(m_i)$, its \emph{actual} indexed guest-input adapter costs $N^{\beta+o(1)}$ per simulated guest transition and has scratch included in the $N^{\sigma+o(1)}$ live parent allowance, and scrubbing/restarting all guest tapes and index heads costs $O(m_i^{L+o(1)}\,\mathrm{polylog}\,N)$. The guest simulation itself must be proven on the same indexed-input model. Retained parent state, input-index tapes, output buffer, guest work tapes, head coordinates, scratch and any ancestor state are all counted; there is no hidden simultaneous sibling.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional serial-quiescence resource ledger]
Under precisely those premises, a sequential controller using one OR accumulator preserves both SAT and UNSAT labels and has
\[
T_{\Phi(A)}(N)\le N^{\max\{\alpha,\gamma+\delta,\gamma+\lambda,
 \gamma+\beta+\lambda K,\gamma+\lambda L\}+o(1)}, \tag{2}
\]
\[
S_{\Phi(A)}(N)\le N^{\max\{\sigma,\lambda,\lambda L\}+o(1)}. \tag{3}
\]
No multiplicative $q$ appears in (3). For an appropriate positive exponent $\sigma$ and geometrically shrinking hereditary stages $N_{i+1}\le N_i^{\lambda+o(1)}$ with total live frame $O(N_i^{\sigma+o(1)})$, the sum of all \emph{ancestor frames on one recursion path} is $N^{\sigma+o(1)}$, assuming a fixed small-instance base case.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equation (1) makes the OR of exact child decisions exactly the source decision. An early YES return is sound; after a complete run of NO answers the source is UNSAT. At any instant only one serialized child and one live guest are present; its predecessor has returned and the reset is charged before moving to the next child. Thus peak writable state is the \emph{sum} of a parent frame, a child buffer, guest work/index cells and scratch; its exponent is their maximum, yielding (3). Summing all $q$ child setup, writing, guest transitions (with adapter), and reset charges together with precomputation yields (2); no amortized cancellation is assumed. In the recursive case, after the first level, each frame has size $N^{\sigma\lambda^i+o(1)}$. There are $O(\log\log N)$ strict shrink levels above the fixed base threshold, and $\sum_{i\geq1}N^{\sigma\lambda^i+o(1)}=o(N^{\sigma+o(1)})$ for any fixed $\sigma>0$; hence the root frame dominates. The result only applies if the stated geometric contraction actually occurs. \qed
\end{proof}
The finite output buffer already costs $N^{\lambda}$ space. If the virtual child cannot be directly presented as FLMV's read-only indexed input, the adapter assumption is \emph{not} satisfied merely because the bytes were written. For $K\geq L$, the reset exponent in (2) is absorbed by the guest-call exponent; the reset operations remain present in the underlying time. If source scans are repeated once per child, then $\delta=1$ and $\gamma+\delta=8/5$ at the proposed $\gamma=3/5$, incompatible with the $c=241/200$ target even when space streams. If children are genuinely $N^{1/2}$ bits, their $q=N^{3/5}$ \emph{physical output volume} is $N^{11/10}$, at the proposed preprocessing allowance, unlike the earlier parent-sized direct-cofactor architecture.

\section{A faithful guest wrapper that defeats direct sequential sweeps}
\begin{theorem}[Restricted buffer-head tariff]
For a child byte string of length $m\ge2$ on one sequential work tape with a single reading head, $q$ alternating reads of addresses $0$ and $m-1$ require exactly $(q-1)(m-1)$ head moves if the head starts at $0$. A correct indexed-input guest can perform the same label-independent reads in $O(q\log(m+2)+m)$ time by charging binary index writing and initial length discovery. For any fixed polynomial exact SAT decider $A$, append the original unmodified call to $A$ after these dummy reads. The wrapper is correct on both SAT and UNSAT. Choosing $q=m^p$ with fixed $p$ larger than the time exponent of $A$ makes this direct sequential-sweep adapter incur $\Omega(m^{p+1})$ time on the dummy phase while the indexed guest uses $O(m^p\log m)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every change of endpoint traverses $m-1$ cells on a single sequential head; there are $q-1$ changes. The guest's indexed read-only input head can jump to a requested address after writing its $\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil$-bit address, paying all index-tape writes and indexed moves. It can find length in $O(m)$ initial steps. The returned dummy bits are discarded, so after the dummy phase $A$ reads the original child and decides its genuine SAT label. This is an \emph{adapter-specific} lower bound. Caching the two endpoint symbols or using a different indexed representation breaks the particular lower-bound premise; it is not an all-machine hardness theorem. \qed
\end{proof}
