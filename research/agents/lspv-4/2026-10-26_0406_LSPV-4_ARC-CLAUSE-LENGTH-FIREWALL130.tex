\section{Explicit proper exact-$(2,2)$ clause-preservation length barrier}
We say a transformation is \emph{clause-accountable} if, on every input in its declared domain, each of the $m$ original clause positions has a distinct actual proper ternary \emph{output clause position} assigned to it, so $m'\ge m$. This is a \emph{strong syntactic restriction}; neither arbitrary SAT-equivalence nor semantic rewriting implies it. The output is a \emph{physically printed, canonically renumbered} ordinary DIMACS proper signed exact-$(2,2)$-3CNF. All variable names are positive consecutive integers $1,\ldots,n'$, every variable has two positive and two negative occurrences, and clauses have three distinct variables. This does not claim to cover arbitrary compressed encodings or virtual formula descriptors.

\begin{theorem}[Clause-accountable normalized output length; PROVED/EXACT in stated model]
There is an infinite family of canonical unpadded, ordinary valid 3CNF inputs of $N$ encoded bits such that \emph{every} clause-accountable output satisfying the preceding exact-$(2,2)$ requirements has $N'=\Omega(N)$ printed output bits. Consequently no such construction has a worst-input guarantee $N'\le N^{\lambda+o(1)}$ for fixed $\lambda<1$, and emitting every output bit costs $\Omega(N)$ physical bit writes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $m=4h$ and source
\[
F_m=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}(x_{3j-2}\vee x_{3j-1}\vee x_{3j}).
\]
The canonical source has $n=3m$ consecutive names, three printed literals per clause, and full DIMACS header. Hence its exact ASCII length in bits is $N=\Theta(m\log m)$. If output has $m'\ge m$ clauses, counting signed incidences gives $4n'=3m'$, whence $n'\ge3m/4$. Every consecutive name $i\in\{1,\ldots,n'\}$ occurs four times, so merely printing variable \emph{digits}, before signs, spaces, terminators, newlines and the header, requires at least
\[
N'\ge 8\cdot4\sum_{i=1}^{n'}\mathrm{digits}_{10}(i)
\ge32\sum_{i=1}^{3m/4}\mathrm{digits}_{10}(i)
=\Omega(m\log m)=\Omega(N).
\]
All these are ordinary source/target encoded \emph{bits}. Thus $\lambda<1$ contradicts the exhibited worst-case family as $m\to\infty$. The proof says nothing about a semantic SAT contractor that detects these disjoint easy clauses and removes them before output, nor about optimal guest runtime on this family.
\end{proof}
A virtual indexed representation does not make the \emph{ordinary child length} $N'$ sublinear; it merely defers physical emission and must pay source selection and guest probes. If a contractor invokes the original $N^{d+o(1)}$ guest upper bound on an $\Omega(N)$ child, its \emph{black-box cost upper-bound recurrence} fails to establish an exponent smaller than $d$. This is not a lower bound on the actual guest's time.

\section{Exact two-level source-coherent countercontrol}
Let $G$ be the signed exact-$(2,2)$ four-clause \emph{SAT} formula
\[
G=(a\vee b\vee c)\wedge(a\vee\neg b\vee\neg c)
\wedge(\neg a\vee b\vee\neg c)\wedge
(\neg a\vee\neg b\vee c).
\]
Let $W_1$ be LSPV-3's \emph{UNSAT} proper signed exact-$(2,2)$ 15-variable, 20-clause source. For every $q\ge1$, set $F_{G,q}$ or $F_{W,q}$ to the disjoint union of $q$ renamed base copies, then build two actual successive ordinary inputs
\[
F^{(1)}=F^{(0)}\wedge \bigwedge_{i=1}^qG_i,\qquad
F^{(2)}=F^{(1)}\wedge \bigwedge_{i=1}^qG_i',
\]
with fresh canonical identifiers on each level. Every level retains exact-$(2,2)$ proper occurrence counts, both SAT and UNSAT labels are unchanged by $\wedge G$, and all headers, aliases, terminators, and literal signs are physically emitted and reparsed. Yet
$|F^{(0)}|_{\mathrm{bits}}<|F^{(1)}|_{\mathrm{bits}}<|F^{(2)}|_{\mathrm{bits}}$ on every checked $q$; in fact the lengths are $\Theta(|F^{(0)}|_{\mathrm{bits}})$, not $N^{1/2+o(1)}$. \section{Simultaneous time-space and circuit accounting}
For a hypothesized general exact adaptive procedure making ordinary SAT calls of lengths $m_i$, actual guest work is bounded by $\sum_i m_i^{d+o(1)}$ with the earlier exact size-spectrum lemma, only if the bounds hold over \emph{every} actual source, answer history, failed policy and branch. Setup $N^{r+o(1)}$, aggregate selector/input accesses $N^{u+o(1)}$, certificate printing and checking, child bit emission or per-probe regeneration, guest work/index tapes, generator metadata, retained answers and both ancestor levels must be charged \emph{on one machine}. For per-probe access exponent $u$ the bound becomes $\sum_i m_i^{d+o(1)} N^{u+o(1)}$; for an already aggregate access budget the exponents must not be charged a second time. Keeping all 3 serialized levels uses the sum of their \emph{physical} buffer lengths, even when they compute the same SAT bit. A generated circuit's size, its wires, the time needed to construct/evaluate it, and work/index-space are distinct quantities. In this countercontrol the children are longer, and no FLMV contraction follows.
