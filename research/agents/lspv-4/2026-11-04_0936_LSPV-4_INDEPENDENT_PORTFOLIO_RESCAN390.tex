\section*{Exact fully encoded family}
Let $n$ count \emph{ASCII source bytes}, and put $N=8n$ binary bits, $Q=\lfloor n^{3/5}\rfloor$, $B=\lfloor n^{1/2}\rfloor$. For every bit vector $b\in\{0,1\}^Q$ let
\[
F_b=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{Q}\left((x_i)\land(\ell_i(b_i))\right),
\qquad \ell_i(1)=x_i,\quad\ell_i(0)=\neg x_i.
\]
The fixed uniform source grammar uses a canonical header, explicitly signed ordered unit-pair lines and a fixed-padding comment to length $n$. Every sign position is independently mutable. The $i$th child $G_i$ consists of these two source units, with harmless fixed trailing comment to serialized length $B$ bytes. Because $O(Q\log Q)=o(n)$ and $O(\log Q)=o(B)$, such padding is possible for all sufficiently large $n$. This is a recognized subfamily of general signed CNF, not a replacement for the all-CNF target. The finite runner uses its own deterministic canonical parser and rejects malformed records; if an external DIMACS parser forbids explicit \texttt{+} signs, the sign field admits a fixed-length polarity-token reencoding with polynomial charged translation.

\begin{theorem}[Independent label vector and cache lower bound]
For every $b$,
\[
F_b\in\mathrm{SAT}\iff\bigwedge_i b_i=1,\qquad
G_i\in\mathrm{SAT}\iff b_i=1,\qquad
\mathrm{SAT}(F_b)=\bigwedge_i\mathrm{SAT}(G_i).
\]
Consequently the $Q$-child label vector realizes all $2^Q$ possibilities. A deterministic exact \emph{source-free} postprocessing cache that must answer any subsequently selected child index needs at least $Q$ bits of persistent machine information, up to a fixed finite-control constant. Every deterministic exact parent-SAT decider must probe all $Q$ independent sign positions on the all-positive parent. Any exact producer of all $Q$ child labels must read every sign.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The unit $x_i$ forces value one; its partner is consistent if and only if $b_i=1$. For cache injectivity choose two distinct vectors and the index on which they differ. Identical cached states without further source access cannot produce opposite answers. Hence at least $2^Q$ distinguishable states, or $Q$ bits. On $b=1^Q$, flip an unprobed sign: the decision changes SAT to UNSAT while the observed transcript stays unchanged, a contradiction. The same flip changes the emitted child label bit. These lower bounds are specific to source access and cache semantics, not general SAT decision time.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Streaming counterexample]
One fixed uniform program validates every member of the declared grammar, rejects malformed strings, computes the parent label and writes the whole child label vector on a write-only output using $O(\log(n+2))$ writable space and $O(n\,\mathrm{polylog}(n+2))$ bit steps on a sequentially readable immutable source. If the complete \emph{padded child formulas} must be emitted, their length and mandatory output work are $\Theta(QB)=\Theta(n^{11/10})$ bytes. Streaming their characters still uses $O(\log n)$ writable space. Materializing one full child on counted writable tapes costs $\Omega(B)$ simultaneous bytes; materializing all costs $\Omega(QB)$ simultaneous bytes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A first source scan uses a logarithmic counter to check header, ordered paired clauses, sign tokens, padding and retain one AND accumulator. The second scan writes the second sign of each pair as its output bit; both scans count all source reads, index movements and outputs. To emit padded child strings instead, enumerate characters by child number and output offset with logarithmic counters. No simultaneous child buffers are needed. An explicitly materialized buffer occupies its declared number of writable cells. The status-vector and source lengths are different resources.
\end{proof}
\noindent Arbitrary \emph{random-access} child-bit requests from a guest solver may force expensive source rescans, so this theorem does not provide a cheap indexed guest accessor or any all-input ARC transformation. Only the specified family is recognized in polynomial time; a general SAT decider must retain a complete possibly expensive fallback.

\section*{Hard anchor, fixed mixed calculus, quantifier discipline}
Haken's 1985 theorem: for standard explicitly signed unary pigeonhole CNFs with $h(h+1)$ variables, $(h+1)+h\binom{h+1}2$ clauses and natural binary input length $N_h=\Theta(h^3\log(h+2))$, there exist constants $c>0,h_0$ such that \emph{for every} $h\ge h_0$ \emph{every ordinary shared-DAG Resolution refutation} has at least $2^{ch}$ lines. This is neither Extended Resolution nor arbitrary deterministic SAT-machine time. Cook 1976 gives short Extended Resolution PHP proofs; authenticated GF(2) operations collapse odd cubic Tseitin in LSPV-2/341. The actual unchanged $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ comprises charged binary-premise RES, local AND3, native GF(2), authenticated unary LOCAL-CONVERT, and paid conservative two-input DEF; the byte-complete LOCAL-CONVERT syntax remains under audit. No known Haken lower bound carries to this combination.

The open bridge statements are
\[
\begin{array}{rl}
\mathrm{B1}:&\forall A\ \exists p_A\ \forall F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}:\quad W_R(F)\le p_A(N+T_A(F)),\\
\mathrm{B2}:&\forall A\ \exists p_A\ \forall N:\quad\bar W_R(N)\le p_A(N+T_A^{\mathrm{wc}}(N)),\\
\mathrm{B3}:&\mathrm{SAT}\in P\Longrightarrow\bar W_{R_{\mathrm{mix341}}}(N)=N^{O(1)}.
\end{array}
\]
Here $A$ is one fixed uniform total correct all-CNF decider handling SAT, UNSAT and malformed strings, and $T_A^\mathrm{wc}$ is the maximum over \emph{all} strings of length at most $N$. B1 implies B2 implies B3, none established. Cook--Reckhow's theorem gives \emph{some} polynomially bounded proof system under SAT in P, not the preselected $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$; LSPV-5/317 had already identified the missing fixed-system length simulation.
