\section{Claim card and exact quantifiers}
Let $\Dec$ be codes of total uniform deterministic exact ordinary-CNF SAT
deciders.  For $A\in\Dec$, let
\[
 V(A)=\{(K,D)\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}^2:
 T_A(n)\le n^{K+o(1)},\ S_A(n)\le n^{D+o(1)}\}.
\]
Changing rational witnesses to integer witnesses changes no conclusion.
By definition,
\[
 A\in\PolySAT\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad V(A)\ne\varnothing. \tag{1}
\]
For $v=(K,D)\in V(A)$ and finite compiler text $C$, let
$L(A,v,C)$ mean that the front end, $C$, and $A$ form one total uniform
ordinary random-access SAT decider satisfying the cited FLMV-forbidden
simultaneous-resource inequalities.  Parsing, generation, virtual access,
child calls, replay, output, and peak writable workspace belong to that one
execution.  This is the \same{} condition.

The model-matched endpoint gives the standing \KNOWN{} premise
\[
 \forall A\in\Dec\ \forall v\in V(A)\ \forall C\quad
 \neg L(A,v,C). \tag{2}
\]
The claim attacked is that a calibrated all-input compiler prefix is a
strictly weaker bridge than $P\ne NP$.  The two current obstacles are
(i) exact placement in one ordinary-machine forbidden region and (ii) a
nonvacuous construction theorem whose domain does not disappear when
$P\ne NP$.

\section{Exact quantifier lattice}
Define the following sentences:
\begin{align*}
U_{\exists V}&:\quad
 \forall A\in\Dec\,[V(A)\ne\varnothing\Rightarrow
 \exists v\in V(A)\ \exists C\ L(A,v,C)],\\
U_{\forall V}&:\quad
 \forall A\in\Dec\ \forall v\in V(A)\ \exists C\ L(A,v,C),\\
E&:\quad \exists A\in\Dec\ \exists v\in V(A)\ \exists C\ L(A,v,C),\\
I&:\quad [\PolySAT\ne\varnothing\Rightarrow E].
\end{align*}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{CALIBRATED-COMPILER-QUANTIFIER-LATTICE1}]
Under (2),
\[
 U_{\exists V}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ U_{\forall V}
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ I
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \PolySAT=\varnothing
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\ P\ne NP. \tag{3}
\]
Moreover $E$ is false unconditionally under (2).  The unguarded sentence
\[
 \forall A\in\Dec\ \exists v\in V(A)\ \exists C\ L(A,v,C) \tag{4}
\]
is false because $\Dec$ contains exact superpolynomial deciders with
$V(A)=\varnothing$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\PolySAT\ne\varnothing$, choose $A$ with $V(A)\ne\varnothing$ and then
$v\in V(A)$.  Sentence $U_{\exists V}$ yields some forbidden $L(A,v,C)$;
$U_{\forall V}$ does the same for the chosen $v$.  Both contradict (2).
Conversely, if $\PolySAT=\varnothing$, every antecedent in $U_{\exists V}$
is false and every set quantified over in $U_{\forall V}$ is empty, so both
sentences are true.  Premise (2) directly gives $\neg E$, hence the material
implication $I$ is true exactly when $\PolySAT$ is empty.  Cook's theorem
gives the last equivalence.  Finally, a brute-force exact decider belongs to
$\Dec$ but not to $\PolySAT$ under its displayed clock, so its $V(A)$ is
empty and (4) fails before any compiler is considered.
\end{proof}

KCALIB1's displayed prefix
``for every decider and every valid $(K,D)$, there exists a calibrated
compiler landing below FLMV'' has the form $U_{\forall V}$.  The theorem
therefore sharpens its prose label ``separation-strength'': as a semantic
sentence with endpoint (2), it is exactly separation-equivalent.  This does
not invalidate KCALIB1's composition ledger or its correction that the
compiler parameters may depend on a fixed $(A,K,D)$.
