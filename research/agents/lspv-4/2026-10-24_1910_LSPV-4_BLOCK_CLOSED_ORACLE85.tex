\section{Ordinary proper-CNF encoding}
Fix $q\ge3$ and orientation bits $b_0,\ldots,b_{q-1}$. Variables $x_0,\ldots,x_{q-1}$ form a cycle with edge equations $x_i\oplus x_{i+1}=b_i$, cyclic indices. Encode each as binary clauses:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
b_i=0:&(\neg x_i\vee x_{i+1}),\ (x_i\vee\neg x_{i+1}),\\
b_i=1:&(x_i\vee x_{i+1}),\ (\neg x_i\vee\neg x_{i+1}).
\end{array}
\]
For each binary clause $C_{i,h}$ introduce private $u_{i,h}$ and replace it by $(C_{i,h}\vee u_{i,h})\land(C_{i,h}\vee\neg u_{i,h})$. The resulting ordered proper three-CNF $P_{q,b}$ has $q$ originals, $2q$ free auxiliaries, $n=3q$, $k=m=4q$ distinct proper ternaries, connected incidence graph, and no functional auxiliaries. With fixed-width signed-literal records (two header words and four words per clause),
\[
w=\lceil\log_2(64(7q+1))\rceil,\qquad N=(16q+2)w.
\]
Changing a single orientation flips exactly four private source literal-sign fields, preserving metadata and input length. Witnesses lift by arbitrary assignments to all $u_{i,h}$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact full and proper edge-block counts]
For all $b$, $\#\operatorname{SAT}(P_{q,b})=2^{2q+1}$ if $\oplus_i b_i=0$, and zero otherwise. For every proper edge subset $S\subsetneq[q]$, retaining all four ternary clauses per chosen edge and preserving the declared $3q$-variable universe gives
\[
\boxed{\#\operatorname{SAT}(P_{q,b}[S])=2^{3q-|S|}}
\]
independently of every orientation. Thus all $2^q-1$ proper block-selected children have the same count on opposite-label parents, even for noncontiguous $S$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Summing the full cycle equations yields $0=\oplus_i b_i$; when consistent, choosing $x_0$ determines exactly two original assignments. All private auxiliaries remain free, giving the full count. A proper edge subset is a forest on $q$ vertices with $|S|$ independent affine equations, hence $2^{q-|S|}$ original assignments regardless of signs, times $2^{2q}$ free auxiliaries. Dropping unused variable identifiers in another ordinary child convention multiplies by a known structure-dependent power, still independent of $b$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Every strict individual-clause SAT query collides]
For odd parity, the complete $P_{q,b}$ is clause-minimally UNSAT: deleting any of its $4q$ ternary clauses makes it SAT. For even parity, the complete parent is SAT. Hence any source-blind deterministic state-adaptive controller asking arbitrary numbers of SAT-label questions about \emph{any strict subset} of individual ternary clauses receives an identical all-YES transcript on opposite-label parents and cannot correctly decide both.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Delete one twin representing one binary half of edge $j$. The other $q-1$ complete edges form a satisfiable path with two complementary original assignments. Under odd parity, both violate the missing XOR equation, but they falsify opposite binary halves. Choose the assignment falsifying the weakened half; its one remaining ternary twin can be satisfied by choosing the private auxiliary. All other twins hold. Further deletions preserve SAT. For an even parent all subsets remain SAT. Determinism and equal response histories imply the same eventual output.
\end{proof}
\section{Mixed-oracle acquisition and the sharp boundary}
\begin{theorem}[Exactly $q$ orientation observations with typed free oracles]
Suppose a deterministic controller knows all public unsigned incidence and metadata, can issue unlimited free SAT-label queries on strict individual-clause subsets and unlimited free exact-$\#\operatorname{SAT}$ queries on proper whole-edge-block subsets, and can additionally read signed source fields by address. To decide both labels exactly for all $b\in\{0,1\}^q$, it must read at least one sign field from each of the $q$ independent edge groups on every input. The bound is matched by a $q$-field XOR scan of a source-certified member.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All SAT-subset answers are YES by clause-minimality, and all permitted count answers are the public function $2^{3q-|S|}$. If a controller fails to inspect edge group's sign fields, flipping just that orientation preserves its full adaptive transcript, including both oracles, yet reverses the parent label. This contradiction applies to each independent edge. The lower bound counts addressed sign-field reads and does not force superlinear computation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[One half-edge exact-count query breaks count invariance]
Take $q-1$ full edge blocks and only the two proper ternary twins of \emph{one binary half} of omitted edge $j$. This strict clause-subset child has count $2^{2q+1}$ for even total parity and $2^{2q}$ for odd parity, and is SAT in both cases. A free exact counting oracle for \emph{arbitrary} strict clause subsets distinguishes the parent with one such query; a SAT-label oracle does not.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The $q-1$ edge path has two complementary assignments. Both satisfy the chosen closing half-clause if full parity is even; exactly one does if odd. All $2q$ private auxiliaries remain free. Constructing the child as a real ordinary encoded SAT instance would require paid source reads, addressing, copying and child headers.
\end{proof}
