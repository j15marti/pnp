\section{Four-resolvent theorem}
\begin{lemma}[One M22 pivot, exact positive/negative tails]
Let \(F\in\CM\) and \(x\) occur in
\((x\lor A_1),(x\lor A_2),(\neg x\lor B_1),(\neg x\lor B_2)\),
where the \(A_i\) each contain two positive literals and the \(B_j\)
each contain two negative literals. Remove those four clauses and
insert the non-tautological clauses \(A_i\lor B_j\), \(i,j\in\{1,2\}\).
Call the result \(D_x(F)\). Then, for every assignment \(a\) to
all other original variables,
\[
D_x(F)(a)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists b\in\{0,1\}:\ F(a,x=b)=1. \tag{1}
\]
If \(h_x\) counts the four ordered tail pairs with disjoint unsigned
supports, then, before optional subsumption,
\[
m(D_x(F))=m(F)-4+h_x\le m(F),\qquad
L(D_x(F))=3m(F)-12+4h_x, \quad 0\le h_x\le4, \tag{2}
\]
where \(L\) is the total literal-occurrence count.
Every surviving new clause is a proper mixed-sign four-clause,
and distinct tail pairs produce distinct surviving clauses.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equation (1) is the standard Davis--Putnam identity.
If all \(A_i\) are true, choose \(x=0\), satisfying both negative
parents. If some \(A_i\) is false, each resolvent forces every
\(B_j\) true, and choosing \(x=1\) satisfies all four parents.
The forward implication follows because every resolvent is
a logical consequence of its parents. A crossing of a positive
and negative tail sharing a variable is tautological.
Otherwise both tails have two distinct variables, have opposite
uniform signs, and their disjunction has four distinct signed
literals. Unique source clauses imply \(A_1\ne A_2\) and \(B_1\ne B_2\);
therefore two non-tautological crosses cannot duplicate.
A new four-literal mixed clause cannot duplicate an old
three-literal monotone clause. Four parents disappear and exactly
\(h_x\) new clauses remain, establishing (2).
\end{proof}

\section{Independent batches and two actual levels}
The primal graph joins two original variables occurring in a
common clause. An M22 name occupies four ternary clauses, hence
has at most eight neighbors. A greedy maximal independent set
\(I_1\) has cardinality \(q_1\ge k/9\). Its members never share an
original clause, so their four-parent resolvent neighborhoods can
be eliminated without introducing another member of \(I_1\)
into a resolvent.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform exact two-level ordinary-CNF projection]
For every \(F\in\CM\) with \(k\) active names and \(m=4k/3\) clauses
there exists a deterministic uniform, exact, source-consistent
two-stage ordinary-CNF transformation
\[
F\ \xrightarrow{\exists I_1}\ F_1\
\xrightarrow{\exists I_2}\ F_2
\]
satisfying, with \(v_i\) the number of \emph{occurring} variables,
\(m_i\) its number of clauses, and \(q_i=|I_i|\),
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
q_1\ge k/9,&v_1\le k-q_1\le8k/9,&m_1\le m,\quad
\operatorname{width}(F_1)\le4,\\
q_2\ge v_1/25,&v_2\le v_1-q_2\le64k/75,&
m_2\le m_1+8q_2,\quad\operatorname{width}(F_2)\le6.
\end{array}\tag{3}
\]
The first stage has at most four resolvents per pivot, and the
second at most sixteen. Each stage preserves the entire
existential projection onto its remaining named variables,
and hence preserves both SAT and UNSAT labels.
All intermediate ordinary encodings have \(O(N)\) bits.
With explicitly stored incidence indices, either fixed two-stage
procedure is implementable with \(O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)\)
charged elementary-bit work and \(O(N)\) peak writable/index bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The greedy independent-set bound is the elementary
\(|I|\ge |V|/(\Delta+1)\) bound on a graph with maximum degree
\(\Delta\). Every first-stage pivot has two parents of each polarity;
one old signed occurrence of an unselected variable can at most
double in the generated resolvents. Therefore each unselected name
has at most four occurrences of each polarity in \(F_1\), hence
total occurrence degree at most eight. Width is at most four,
so \(F_1\)'s primal degree is at most \(8(4-1)=24\).
A second greedy independent set has \(q_2\ge v_1/25\).
For any second-stage pivot, \(0\le p,q\le4\). Removing \(p+q\)
parents and adding at most \(pq\) resolvents costs
\(pq-p-q\le8\) clauses. The new clause width is at most
\(2(4-1)=6\). By (1), all elimination steps compute exact
existential projections, and independence prevents the second
pivot from appearing in another pivot's input clause.
At fixed depth two, clause counts and widths remain \(O(k)\)
and \(O(1)\), respectively, and their dense indexed lengths
are \(O(k\log(k+2))=O(N)\). Indexed occurrence lists and
greedy neighbor marking require \(O(N)\) stored bits, with
sorting/serialization and indexing within \(O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)\)
elementary bit work. This is a deliberately \emph{materialized},
not a virtual-addressed, implementation.
\end{proof}
\noindent The exact set of \emph{surviving active names} may shrink
further when clauses become tautologies or variables become unused.
No assertion is made that the first or second projected formula is
M22: its proper mixed four-/six-clauses normally lie \emph{outside}
\(\CM\) and \(\CS\). Consequently a target-only \(L_\star\) solver
cannot consume these children without a separate paid adapter.
This is an ordinary-CNF exact simplifier, not a target-hereditary contractor.

\section{A complete-state candidate-volume firewall}
\begin{theorem}[A specifically falsified post-acquisition potential]
Consider any proposal for a nonnegative potential \(\Phi\) on
\emph{complete legal machine states} that agrees with
\(\Phi=2^{v(G)}\) at all committed exact-CNF representation
checkpoints \(G\), including the source and the completed first
projection. Assume for every elementary transition of a
machine executing the above materialized construction
\[
\Phi(C)-\Phi(C')\le \Gamma(N)\,w(C,C'), \tag{4}
\]
where \(w\) counts actual charged elementary bit operations.
Then along this valid \emph{post-acquisition} path
\[
\boxed{\quad
\Gamma(N)\ \ge\
\frac{2^k-2^{8k/9}}
{O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)}.\quad}\tag{5}
\]
In particular, no polynomial \(\Gamma\) can certify (4) for
this \emph{particular} potential on unbounded M22 inputs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After reading or parsing the source, the machine performs the
first exact Davis--Putnam batch, paying at most
\(O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)\) bit operations. Its committed
representation has at most \(8k/9\) active variables. Summing
(4) over \emph{all} machine transitions in this interval
telescopes to at least \(2^k-2^{8k/9}\), giving (5).
The argument charges the construction and is not a free
semantic jump; it holds whatever potential values are assigned
to unfinished intermediate states, provided the two checkpoint
values agree as hypothesized.
\end{proof}

or proposed complete-state extensions matching those checkpoint
values. An independently initialized structural potential not
equal to \(2^v\), robust to source-aware DP and other adaptive
algorithms, could still exist. Large residual enumeration budgets
