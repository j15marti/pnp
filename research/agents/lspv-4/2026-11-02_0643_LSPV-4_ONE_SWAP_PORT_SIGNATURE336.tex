\section{Universal two-bit boundary signature}
Fix ANY two canonical originals $G(x)$ and satisfiable closed guard $K(z)$ with disjoint variable names and selected same-polarity original clauses
$C=(u\lor A)$ in $G$ and $D=(v\lor B)$ in $K$, where $u,v$ are signed \emph{literal truth values}, $A,B$ are disjunctions of the two respective other signed literals. Write $H=K\setminus\{D\}$ and exchange occurrences $u,v$:
\[
Q(x,z)=(G\setminus\{C\})(x)\wedge H(z)\wedge(v\lor A)(x,z)\wedge(u\lor B)(x,z).
\]
All signed occurrence quotas, distinct-name triple clauses, monotonicity and duplicate-freedom are retained, because the two original variable sets were disjoint. The swapped derived formula is itself a valid canonical original, though its existential projection is not necessarily $G$.
Define $W_{ij}=\#\{z:H(z)=1,\ v(z)=i,\ B(z)=j\}$ for $i,j\in\{0,1\}$. Define $E_{uA}$ as the number of satisfying extensions of the two swapped clauses and $H$, holding the original source literals at $(u,A)$.
\begin{theorem}[Exact one-occurrence-swap signature, all sizes and all original inputs]
The extension-count matrix is
\[
\boxed{
E=\begin{pmatrix}
W_{11}&W_{01}+W_{11}\\
W_{10}+W_{11}&W_{00}+W_{01}+W_{10}+W_{11}
\end{pmatrix}.}
\]
For every $x$, $\exists z\,Q(x,z)$ equals $(G\setminus\{C\})(x)\wedge P(u(x),A(x))$, where $P$ is the Boolean predicate $E_{uA}>0$. For arbitrary $H$, the only possible $P$ are $\bot,u\wedge A,u,A,u\vee A,\top$. For a satisfiable CLOSED guard $K=H\wedge(v\vee B)$ the only possibilities are $\top,u,A,u\vee A$. In particular:
\[
P=u\vee A\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
W_{11}=0,\quad W_{01}>0,\quad W_{10}>0;
\]
$W_{11}>0$ holds if and only if $P=\top$, i.e., the selected original clause disappears pointwise under existential projection. The iff concerns the \emph{local interface}; an already redundant source clause can conceal a changed interface on a particular $G$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $(u,A)=(0,0)$ both new clauses require $v=B=1$, giving $W_{11}$. For $(0,1)$ only $B=1$ is required, giving $W_{01}+W_{11}$. For $(1,0)$ only $v=1$ is required, giving $W_{10}+W_{11}$. For $(1,1)$ neither requirement remains, giving $\sum W_{ij}$. These inclusions force a monotone Boolean function of $u,A$, hence one of the six listed. If $K$ is satisfiable, at least one of $W_{01},W_{10},W_{11}$ is nonzero; this excludes $\bot$ and $u\land A$. The remaining classifications follow from the nonnegative count matrix. No all-input SAT-decision cost is assumed. \qed
\end{proof}
