\section{Exact universal opposite-sign batch gadget}
Take any $F\in\mathcal C_{\rm M22}$, one original positive clause $C_+=(x\vee y\vee z)$, and one original negative clause $C_-=(\neg u\vee\neg v\vee\neg w)$. The two triples may overlap in their old names. Introduce six private fresh variables $a,b,c,d,e,f$ and add five positive clauses
\[
(a\vee b\vee x),\quad(a\vee d\vee y),\quad(b\vee e\vee z),\quad
(c\vee d\vee f),\quad(c\vee e\vee f)
\]
and five negative clauses
\[
(\neg a\vee\neg d\vee\neg u),\quad
(\neg a\vee\neg d\vee\neg v),\quad
(\neg b\vee\neg e\vee\neg w),\quad
(\neg b\vee\neg c\vee\neg f),\quad
(\neg c\vee\neg e\vee\neg f).
\]
Denote the conjunction of those ten clauses by $G_{\pm}$ and put
$D_{\pm}(F)=(F\setminus\{C_+,C_-\})\wedge G_{\pm}$.
\begin{theorem}[Universal neutral pair; PROVED/EXACT, restricted]
For every valid $F$ and both selected opposite-sign source clauses,
\[
D_{\pm}(F)\in\mathcal C_\star,\qquad
\operatorname{SAT}(D_{\pm}(F))\iff
\operatorname{SAT}(F\setminus\{C_+,C_-\}),
\qquad(k',m')=(k+6,m+8).
\]
There is a single auxiliary assignment $a=b=c=1$, $d=e=f=0$ satisfying all ten added clauses for \emph{every} choice of the six old port values, including overlapping old names.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} Each old port supplies its one missing signed occurrence. Each fresh name occurs twice positively and twice negatively: in the positive clauses the pairs are $a:1,2$; $b:1,3$; $c:4,5$; $d:2,4$; $e:3,5$; $f:4,5$, and analogously in the negative list. All clauses are distinct, proper and monochromatic, regardless of whether positive and negative old ports overlap. Every added clause contains a literal satisfied by the displayed fixed auxiliary assignment. Therefore any satisfying assignment of the retained source clauses extends, and every target model restricts to a model of the retained clauses. The positive fresh-only clause $(c\vee d\vee f)$ lacks a negative counterpart, since the only fresh-only negative supports are $\{b,c,f\}$ and $\{c,e,f\}$. All other negative clauses contain an old name. Thus the output is never fully paired. The signed total counts are $m'_+=m_++4$ and $m'_-=m_-+4$, so $(k',m')=(k+6,m+8)$. \hfill$\square$

\section{Sharp floor for the six-independent-port universal interface}
\begin{lemma}[General signed incidence identities; PROVED/EXACT]
Suppose a valid M22 input loses $d_+$ original positive and $d_-$ negative clauses, every remaining original clause is unchanged, no original variable disappears, and a valid target is built by adding $h$ fresh names and $p,q$ new signed clauses. Necessarily
\[
h\equiv0\pmod3,\quad
p=d_++\frac{2h}{3},\quad q=d_-+\frac{2h}{3},\quad
k'=k+h,\quad m'=m+\frac{4h}{3}.
\]
More generally, if exactly $z$ old names disappear from the final active set and $h$ truly fresh names are introduced, then
\[
p=d_++\frac{2(h-z)}3,\quad q=d_-+\frac{2(h-z)}3,
\quad k'=k-z+h,\quad m'=m+\frac{4(h-z)}3.
\]
The second identity does not prohibit genuine contraction when sufficiently many old names are eliminated.
\end{lemma}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} Before deletion each sign has $2k$ literal incidences. Deleting one old signed triple removes three. The new formula has exactly $2(k-z+h)$ literal incidences of each sign. Solve $2k-3d_\pm+3a_\pm=2(k-z+h)$, with $a_+=p$, $a_-=q$, and use integrality. \hfill$\square$

\begin{theorem}[Sharp universal-port auxiliary floor; PROVED/EXACT, restricted]
For a replacement of exactly one positive and one negative clause on six \emph{independent} old ports, retaining all other original clauses and old variables, requiring every assignment to those six ports to extend to a model of the added gadget, and adding only unique proper monochromatic triples, at least six fresh names are necessary. The displayed $G_\pm$ attains this bound with ten inserted clauses.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} The incidence lemma gives $h=3r$. If $h=0$, exactly one positive and one negative clause must be added, and signed deficits force reinsertion of the original triples; choose all positive old ports zero and all negative old ports one, violating both. For $h=3$, three positive and three negative new clauses are mandatory. On that same hostile old assignment, let $t$ of the fresh bits equal one. Each fresh variable has only two positive and two negative occurrences, so satisfying all positive new clauses requires $2t\ge3$ and satisfying all negative new clauses requires $2(3-t)\ge3$. Thus $t\ge2$ and $t\le1$, a contradiction. The next possible value is $h=6$ and $G_\pm$ attains it. The result does \emph{not} exclude $h=3$ repairs specialized to sources where the hostile port assignment is incompatible with retained clauses, nor global rewriting or survivor elimination. \hfill$\square$
