\section{Exact quantified theorem}
Let $F$ be \emph{any} valid duplicate-free proper clause-monotone 3-CNF with exactly two positive and two negative occurrences of each of $n$ variables. Thus $n\equiv0\pmod3$ and $m=4n/3$. Let $p$ be \emph{any} coherent partial assignment with $k_0$ source variables set to zero and $k_1$ set to one, and $k=k_0+k_1$. Form the ordinary source-indexed cofactor $F|p$ by deleting satisfied clauses and then deleting falsified assigned literals. Let $e(p)$ be the number of \emph{unassigned} original variables with zero remaining occurrences and $r(p)=n-k-e(p)$.
\begin{equation}\boxed{e(p)\le2\min(k_0,k_1),\qquad r(p)\ge n-k-2\min(k_0,k_1).}\end{equation}
In particular, assigning only one sign ($k_0k_1=0$) never deletes an additional free variable, and \emph{every one-variable cofactor retains all $n-1$ other original variables}.
A \emph{source-identity-preserving canonical re-entry} is a valid target formula $H$ with an injection from all $r(p)$ surviving source-variable identities to distinct variables of $H$. It may add auxiliaries and change clause organization, but may not identify or eliminate those variables. Every such $H$ satisfies
\begin{equation}n_H\ge3\lceil r(p)/3\rceil.\end{equation}
For $k=1$ this implies $n_H\ge n$, $m_H\ge m$. In the fully declared gamma-header fixed-width indexed clause representation
\begin{equation}
E(n)=\gamma(n)+\gamma(4n/3)+(4n/3)(1+3\,\operatorname{bitlen}(n)),\quad \gamma(q)=2\operatorname{bitlen}(q)-1,
\end{equation}
the child is no shorter: $\boxed{E(n_H)\ge E(n)}$. For every $k=o(n)$, $E(n_H)=\Omega(E(n))$; no $E(n)^\lambda$ with fixed $\lambda<1$ can describe such children.

\paragraph{Proof.} Only a true assigned positive literal can delete a positive source clause; every assigned-true variable occurs in precisely two positive source clauses, so $D_+\le2k_1$. Likewise $D_-\le2k_0$. Each deleted clause contains an assigned literal and hence at most two unassigned occurrences. Each fully erased free variable contributes two positive and two negative occurrences, all deleted. Thus $2e\le2D_+\le4k_1$ and $2e\le2D_-\le4k_0$. Therefore $e\le2\min(k_0,k_1)$ and $r=n-k-e$. Every valid canonical target has variable count divisible by three, so an injection of $r$ distinct source variables forces $n_H\ge3\lceil r/3\rceil$. For one pivot $r=n-1$, and $3\mid n$, hence $n_H\ge n$ and $m_H\ge m$. The explicit encoding length is monotone for these valid cardinalities. All implications hold for both SAT and UNSAT parents. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Restricted multiplicity consequence.} If such a child has $n_H\le E(n)^\lambda$, then $k\ge(n-E(n)^\lambda)/2$. A naive complete Shannon enumeration of every one of the $2^k$ cubes, with no pruning or sharing, would use at least $2^{(n-E(n)^\lambda)/2}$ calls. This is solely a lower bound for that declared exhaustive source-faithful architecture, not for arbitrary SAT machines, adaptive pruning, global variable elimination, or semantic compression.
