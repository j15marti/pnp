\par
Fix a connected cubic bipartite graph on $H$ vertices, an odd charge, a matching of size $m=H/2$, and the original four strict 3-CNF parity clauses per vertex. In matching edge $j$, the left/right incident IDs are $2j+1+a_j,2j+1+b_j$; nonmatching edge IDs are greater than $H$. The resulting source has $4H$ original clauses and $N=3H+12+12H(\lceil\log_2(2H+1)\rceil+1)$ bits.
\par
On promised valid inputs, the lowest ID of each original vertex packet is its matching-edge ID. An indexed random-access decoder can scan all $m$ packets of both halves for each $j$, comparing the unique IDs of pair $j$. It returns SAT iff any differ. This takes $O(H^2\log^3N)$ time and $O(\log N)$ high-water workspace under a conservative binary-index simulation. If the packets are *only reordered* to correspond by matching index, time improves to $O(H\log^3N)$ with the same space. The clause multiset and ordinary Resolution hardness are preserved under that source reordering. These are promise results, not all-input SAT algorithms or a proof of FLMV transfer.
