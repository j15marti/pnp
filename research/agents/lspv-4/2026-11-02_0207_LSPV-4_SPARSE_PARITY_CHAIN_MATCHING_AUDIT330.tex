\section{Exact sparse parity theorem}
For complemented support-pairs $P(F)=M(F)$, each positive source support is one vertex of a loopless cubic multigraph; each original variable is a distinct labelled edge between its two positive-clause occurrences. There are $q=2n/3$ vertices and $n$ source edges. For coherent original-name pins $p$, let $t_v$ be pinned TRUE degree, $r_v$ the free incidence count and
\[
\ell_v=\max(0,1-t_v),\quad u_v=\min(r_v,2-t_v),
\quad d_v=r_v-\ell_v,\quad b_v=u_v-\ell_v.
\]
If $\ell_v>u_v$, the positive or negative original source clause is already false. Otherwise $(\ell_v,u_v)$ are the exact allowable free TRUE degrees. A source incidence vertex has $d_v\le2$ local dummy vertices, each linked to all $r_v\le3$ incident original-variable ports; each of $b_v\le1$ local slack terminals is linked to all the local dummies. For each unpinned original edge $e=vw$ the source-labelled ports $p_{e,v},p_{e,w}$ are joined by one cross edge. Distinct parallel source edges remain distinct labelled port pairs. This is the \emph{previously known} interval-factor gadget except for how unmatched slacks are linked.

\begin{lemma}[Triangulated path realizes exact parity]
Given slack terminals $s_1,\ldots,s_b$ and a desired parity $\pi\in\{0,1\}$, make nodes $q_0,\ldots,q_b$ and for every $i=1,\ldots,b$ connect $(q_{i-1},q_i)$, $(q_{i-1},s_i)$ and $(q_i,s_i)$. If $(b+1)\bmod2\neq\pi$, attach a pendant node $q_{b+1}$ linked only to $q_b$. For EVERY set $S$ of slack terminals remaining after external local dummy matches, the induced graph on $S$ and all $q$ nodes admits a perfect matching iff $|S|\bmod2=\pi$. The gadget has at most $b+2$ internal vertices and $3b+1$ edges.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Necessity is the parity of the exposed vertex count $b+1+\epsilon+|S|$, where $\epsilon$ is the optional pendant. Sufficiency follows by a left-to-right sweep. At stage $i$, at most the previous frontier $q_{i-1}$ remains unmatched. If $s_i$ is present, match $q_{i-1}s_i$ when $q_{i-1}$ is available, otherwise match $s_iq_i$; if $s_i$ is absent, match $q_{i-1}q_i$ when available, otherwise leave $q_i$ unmatched. Every earlier vertex is covered; the terminal $q_b$ is covered, or matched to its pendant, exactly when the total exposed vertex parity is even. The $b=0$ case is included.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Source-authenticated linear-edge paired decision]
For every valid complemented-pair original and EVERY coherent original-variable pin profile, there is an effective simple source-labelled perfect-matching graph $H_{F,p}$ with
\[
F|p\text{ SAT}\iff H_{F,p}\text{ has a perfect matching},\qquad
|V(H_{F,p})|\le\frac{14n}{3}+2,\qquad
|E(H_{F,p})|\le\frac{25n}{3}+1.
\]
Under $\Theta(\log n)$-bit node identifiers, explicitly materializing all graph edges requires only $O(n\log n)$ bits. SAT produces a verified full original-variable witness; UNSAT produces either an original-clause contradiction or a complete source-reconstructible Tutte odd-component certificate, using known deterministic general matching and Gallai--Edmonds methods. Auxiliary graphs are \emph{not} canonical original inputs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose the chosen cross edges corresponding to TRUE free variables form $X$. Dummies consume the other free original-variable ports; if $u_v-\deg_X(v)$ dummies are left after ports are consumed, exactly that many local slack terminals are taken. Local perfect matchability is equivalent to $\ell_v\le\deg_X(v)\le u_v$. Thus the \emph{remaining} slack terminals have size
\[
|S|=\sum_v\left(u_v-\deg_X(v)\right)=\sum_v u_v-2|X|
 \equiv\pi:=\sum_v u_v\pmod2.
\]
The triangulated-path lemma matches precisely those admissible slacks and no others. At each source vertex $d_v\le2$, $b_v\le1$, at most $8$ local dummy edges. Counting $n$ free cross edges, at most $2q$ ports, $2q$ dummies, $q$ slack terminals, $q+2$ internal chain vertices and $3q+1$ chain edges yields $|V|\le2n+4q+2$ and $|E|\le n+11q+1$, proving the constants with $q=2n/3$. A perfect matching defines each original Boolean input once and is verified clause-by-clause. If there is no perfect matching, Tutte's theorem yields a subset $A\subset V(H)$ with $\operatorname{odd}(H-A)>|A|$. The solver must acquire $A$ with paid matching work, and the certificate verifier rebuilds $H$, checks all original source IDs and counts odd components. No gratis NO oracle is assumed.
\end{proof}

\section{Smallest unmatched-support boundary and connected controls}
Let $P$ and $M$ be positive and negative original support sets and $U=P\triangle M$, $u=|U|$, $B=\bigcup_{C\in U}C$. Both sign families have the same cardinality and every variable has degree two in each. Therefore $u$ is even; $u=2$ is impossible because one unmatched positive triple and one unmatched negative triple with the same incidence degree vector would be identical. When $u=4$, each sign has precisely two unmatched triples, and the common intersection $K$ of those two triples is determined by the variables of support degree two. The positive and negative pairs have the same $K$. If $|K|=2$, their remaining single-occurrence elements uniquely determine the same two triples, impossible for distinct unmatched support sets. Hence $|K|\in\{0,1\}$ and
\[
 u=4 \Longrightarrow |B|=6-|K|\in\{5,6\}.
\]
This is an exact elementary source-grammar lemma. The \emph{previously known} factor-backdoor rule can enumerate at most $2^{|B|}\le2^{3u}$ boundary assignments, check all unmatched source clauses, and solve the remainder using the paired graph; its total work is polynomial when $u=O(\log N)$ with a fixed constant. For unbounded $u$, no polynomial aggregate-work bound is proved.

At adjacent $A_{00},B_{00}$ exchange two distinct \emph{positive source occurrence names} other than their common edge; negative original clauses remain unchanged. Each original variable's $2+$/$2-$ quota is unchanged, positive supports remain proper and distinct, and the resulting original is connected, nonpaired, $u=4$, $|B|=5$. Its negative-source primal graph contains the line graph of $H_k$ and has growing treewidth: contracting edges $A_{ij}B_{ij}$ in $H_k$ yields the $k\times k$ toroidal grid.
