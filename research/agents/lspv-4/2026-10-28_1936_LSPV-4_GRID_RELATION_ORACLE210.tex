\section{BUILD I: exponential raw feasible states in an easy exact-(2,2) family}
For $L\ge2$ put $n=3L^2$ variables $A_{rc},B_{rc},C_{rc}$ with cyclic indices modulo $L$. For every $(r,c)$ add
\begin{align*}
D^0_{rc}&=(A_{rc}\vee B_{rc}\vee C_{rc}),&
D^1_{rc}&=(A_{rc}\vee B_{r+1,c}\vee C_{rc}),\\
D^2_{rc}&=(\neg A_{rc}\vee\neg B_{rc}\vee\neg C_{r,c+1}),&
D^3_{rc}&=(\neg A_{rc}\vee\neg B_{r+1,c}\vee\neg C_{r,c+1}).
\end{align*}
All clauses are proper and every variable has two positive and two negative occurrences. The incidence graph is connected. An uncompressed row-major frontier dynamic program keeps a distinct tuple for each assignment of previously processed variables that still occur together with unprocessed variables in a clause, provided the tuple extends to satisfying assignments of processed clauses.
\begin{theorem}
Immediately after row $0$ is processed, there are at least $2^L$ distinct feasible frontier tuples, and an explicit raw list storing their $A$ fields needs at least $L2^L$ bits. In the canonical encoding $N=\Theta(L^2\log L)$ this is $2^{\Omega(\sqrt{N/\log N})}$. Nonetheless a uniform satisfying witness is $B_{rc}=1,C_{rc}=0$ with every $A_{rc}$ arbitrary, and the family is elementary to recognize and solve.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every $A_{0,c}$ is live due to $D^1_{0,c}$ containing the unprocessed $B_{1,c}$. Choose any $a\in\{0,1\}^L$ on these $L$ names, put every $B=1,C=0$, and assign all other $A$ arbitrarily. Then each positive clause holds by its $B$ literal and each negative clause by its $\neg C$ literal. Every $a$ extends to a full solution, hence contributes a distinct feasible raw frontier tuple. This proves the exact enumeration bound. There are $4L^2$ constant-width clauses using variable labels of $O(\log L)$ bits, giving the input-length conversion. The same witness provides a compact semantic decision summary.
\end{proof}
This is a counterexample to universal \emph{raw table} feasibility, not to polynomial SAT or to compact shared semantic circuits. Crucially it contains only SAT instances and proves nothing about an UNSAT-side resource lower bound.
\section{BUILD II: precise virtual recursive access}
Let $N_0=N,N_{i+1}\le N_i^\lambda$ above a fixed terminal threshold, $0<\lambda<1$. A uniform child-bit accessor at level $i$ executes at most $cN_i^\alpha$ local operations and makes at most $cN_i^\beta$ parent-bit calls. Let the original-input query cost on the declared model satisfy $C_0\le cN^\xi$. Every local cost includes index arithmetic, output header/body generation on request, discovery, failed strategy attempts, and book-keeping. Work frames are counted simultaneously.
\begin{theorem}
For a nested uncached evaluator with $C_{i+1}\le cN_i^\alpha+cN_i^\beta C_i$, the depth is $h=O(\log\log N)$ and
\[
C_h\le N^{\max(\alpha,\xi)+\beta/(1-\lambda)+o(1)}.
\]
With at most $cN_i^\sigma$ bits per retained frame and charged $O(\log N_i)$-bit addresses, peak frame space is $O(\sum_i N_i^\sigma+\sum_i\log(N_i+2))=N^{\max(\sigma,0)+o(1)}$. With constant frame data plus address counters this is $O(\log N)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Iterated power shrinkage gives $\log N_i\le\lambda^i\log N$, so $\prod_iN_i^\beta\le N^{\beta/(1-\lambda)}$. Unroll $C_h$: one base term $C_0\prod_i q_i$ and $h$ local terms $a_j\prod_{i>j}q_i$. Because $h=O(\log\log N)$, constant factors $c^h$ and $h$ contribute only $N^{o(1)}$. Bound each $a_j\le cN^\alpha$. Retained frames add rather than disappear; $\sum\log N_i=O(\log N)$. This is an \emph{upper} bound conditional on actually having the promised accessors, not a SAT algorithm.
\end{proof}
\section{BUILD III: why mere halving is insufficient for an uncached virtual interface}
Let $N=2^r$, $N_{i+1}=N_i/2$ until $2$, and compute
\[
X_{i+1}[j]=\bigoplus_{p=0}^{N_i-1}X_i[p]\oplus(j\bmod2).
\]
The explicitly defined uncached evaluator of one deepest bit makes exactly
\[
Q_{\mathrm{leaf}}=\prod_{i=0}^{r-2}N_i
=2^{r(r+1)/2-1}=N^{\Theta(\log N)}
\]
original-source bit probes. Materializing all intermediate strings by one scan of the parent per child bit instead needs $\sum_iN_iN_{i+1}=O(N^2)$ parent probes and $O(N)$ retained child bits. Caching parity can do still better. Therefore this is a resource counterexample for that \emph{specified implementation}, emphatically not an inherent query lower bound. Under power shrinkage, the local-size query product is polynomial instead. Roundings such as $\lceil\sqrt{N_i}\rceil$ add only $N^{o(1)}$ factors at logarithmic-logarithmic depth.
\section{Separate native ledgers}
For the \emph{materialized} parity example, a fixed-$k$ ($k\ge2$) sequential read-only-input multitape algorithm can rescan a parent and rewind once per output bit, charging output and work copies. Under square-root shrinkage it has $T_k=O(N^{3/2}\mathrm{polylog}N)$ and $S_k=O(N^{1/2}\mathrm{polylog}N)$. With halving it has $T_k=O(N^2\mathrm{polylog}N)$ and $S_k=O(N)$. The conservative one-read/write-tape conversion gives
\[
T_1=O(T_k(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2))),\quad
P_1=O(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2)).
\]
Thus the two illustrative \emph{upper} exponent pairs are $(t_1,p_1)\le(2.5,1)$ and $(3,1)$, respectively. The one-tape input is included in physical space; the $k$-tape input is read-only and excluded from work-space. A fixed universal interpreter's overhead must be separately compiled, not silently set to one. No sequential crossing lower bound transfers to unrestricted indexed RAM, and no reverse simulation implication is asserted.
