## Theorem 1: exact 5-state terminal composition

For a directed two-terminal source H=(V,E,s,t), define F_H(d) = #{T5 assignments with x_s=0 and x_t=d}. Translation x_v -> x_v+c is a bijection, so the fixed endpoint count depends only on d=x_t-x_s (mod 5). For internally disjoint H,J, exact original-vertex counts compose as

    F_parallel(d)=F_H(d)*F_J(d)
    F_series(d)=sum_{a in Z5} F_H(a)*F_J(d-a)

where series identifies H.t with J.s. For k otherwise disjoint modules sharing ONLY the same s,t:

    Z(union_i H_i)=5*sum_{d=0}^4 product_i F_{H_i}(d).

The five residues are an exact transfer representation, not an algorithm-independent information lower bound. Forward single-edge vector is (0,1,0,1,0); reverse single-edge vector is (0,0,1,0,1). An authenticated series/parallel expression can be evaluated bottom-up in polynomial time with 5 integers per node, each containing O(N) bits. This explicitly charges source recognition, terminal identities, source arcs, arithmetic, addresses, recomputation and output on the same sequential tape.

## Theorem 2: an exact Kp,p terminal signature

Orient every arc of K_{p,p}, p>=2, from left to right. Mark two distinct left-side terminals. Their common outneighbor sets for terminal differences d=0,1,2,3,4 are respectively {1,3}, empty, {3}, {1}, empty. Exact support inclusion/exclusion and free-color choices yield

    F_p=(3*2^(p-1)-2, 0, 2^(p-2), 2^(p-2), 0).

The independent original-vertex brute-force and support-enumeration implementations agree.

## Theorem 3: biconnected high-width easy family B(k,p)

For k>=2,p>=3 take k all-forward Kp,p modules sharing precisely the SAME two left-side terminals; every other source vertex and arc remains private. Then

    n=2+k(2p-2), m=kp^2, minimum degree=p,
    treewidth>=p, global missing-cross-edge cover tau=k(p-2),
    Z(B(k,p))=5*((3*2^(p-1)-2)^k + 2*(2^(p-2))^k).

B(k,p) has no articulation vertex: deleting either terminal leaves the other connecting all modules, while deleting any private vertex preserves its Kp,p module's connectivity. Treewidth is at least p from a Kp,p subgraph. The global complement-cover upper bound is all k(p-2) private left vertices. A matching of that size in the missing cross-edge graph pairs each private left vertex in module i with a distinct right vertex in module i+1 cyclically. Kőnig's theorem establishes equality. This family has no internal degree-two paths when p>=3. Its exact count is polynomial-time even as k and p grow.

This falsifies biconnectivity, minimum degree, growing source treewidth and global missing-edge cover as sufficient conditions for a same-realization superpolynomial lower bound.
