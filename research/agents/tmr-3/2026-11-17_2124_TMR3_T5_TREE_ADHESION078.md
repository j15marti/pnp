## Model, source and exact factor identity

Identify A,B,C,D,E with Z5. The fixed tournament relation is R(a,b) iff (b-a) mod 5 lies in {1,3}. The source has n vertices and explicit arcs; all labels, indices, lengths and end-of-input are authenticated, with O(poly(N)) parsing on a standard locally headed deterministic machine for binary input size N. Duplicated listed arcs represent one directed edge. A self-loop gives zero.

The recognized family is an explicit digraph with source hubs (indegree zero, including isolates), and private sinks (outdegree zero, indegree exactly three from distinct hubs). Every arc goes from hub to private sink. For one actual sink with three hub parents e=(u,v,w), the number of legal choices for its color is

f_e(a,b,c) = |Out(a) intersect Out(b) intersect Out(c)|,

where Out(a)={a+1,a+3} mod 5. The weight is 2 if all three parent colors agree, 1 if exactly two distinct colors differ by 2 modulo 5, and 0 otherwise. Multiple *different sink vertices* having identical triples remain independent distinct multiplicative factors. Summing their assignments gives the exact identity

Z(G) = SUM_(hub assignments x in Z5^h) PRODUCT_(original sinks e) f_e(x_u,x_v,x_w).

This is a full source-to-original-output equality, not a rank surrogate or a satisfying-assignment decision shortcut.

A valid nonfamily digraph is counted by a source-authenticated spanning-forest fallback: fix each connected root at color zero, enumerate the two allowed directed differences on each tree edge, recheck every actual original arc, and multiply valid canonical assignments by 5^c, where c is the number of weak components. Time O(poly(N)*(1+2^(n-c))), polynomial writable space. Malformed source records are rejected.

## Exact source-derived block-cut forest and cover search

Create the undirected primal graph on hubs by adding the three pairwise edges of every original parent triple. Discover its edge-biconnected blocks and articulation hubs by the classical depth-first lowpoint/edge-stack construction of Tarjan (1972). Since every triple is a triangle, it lies completely in ONE unique biconnected block. Retain each sink's unique identity and attribute its factor to that block. The incidence graph of blocks and articulation hubs is a forest. Isolated hubs never in any triple contribute a factor five.

Root each component at one block B. Let s_B be its parent articulation, if present; put delta_B=1 when s_B exists and zero otherwise. Find a smallest subset C_B of B other than s_B so that every original triple assigned to B contains at least two vertices in A_B=C_B union {s_B}. Let t_B=|C_B|.

Cover discovery is not an oracle. For budgets k=0,1,..., scan for a triple with fewer than two covered hubs. When it has one covered hub, branch on adding one of the other two; when it has none, branch on choosing two of its three. Each branch buys at least one new hub, and the fanout never exceeds three. A successful set is checked against every actual triple; selecting all block hubs always succeeds. Including unsuccessful budgets and source scans, the search costs O(poly(N)*(t_B+1)*3^t_B) sequential bit time, with polynomial live space (no exponential memo table).

## Five-entry articulation message induction

For a nonroot block define M_B(a) as the weighted number of valid assignments in its descendant subtree, conditional on the parent articulation hub s_B having color a, WITHOUT summing that hub. For a root block define a scalar total. At each other articulation v in B, multiply the conditional messages of its child blocks pointwise, producing one unary five-entry factor in color(v). Different children remain distinct factors, but their shared articulation is not independently summed.

Fix s_B (if present), and enumerate at most 5^t_B assignments to the local cover C_B. Every original triple has at least two colored parents, so it contributes a constant or a unary five-entry function in the sole unresolved parent. Every child subtree similarly contributes a unary factor in its attachment hub. Therefore, with F_v denoting the product of all unary factors for v, the exact local contribution is

constant * PRODUCT_(unfixed v in B) ( SUM_(a in Z5) F_v(a) ).

Sum this product over all cover colors to obtain M_B. This is valid for arbitrarily many child blocks and arbitrary depth; an articulation connected to many children still contributes only a unary table. Every hub belongs to a connected set of block nodes and is summed exactly ONCE at its highest block. Every original sink factor belongs to one block. Induction from leaf blocks gives the original Z(G) exactly, including NO instances, repeated original triples, disconnected components, and isolated hubs.

## Explicit infinite family outside the previous single-separator theorem

For r>=1, take hubs 0,...,2r and r genuine private sinks. Sink i has exactly the three parents {2i,2i+1,2i+2}. The primal graph is a path of r triangle blocks, adjacent pairs meeting in a different articulation hub. There are n_original=3r+1 vertices and 3r directed arcs. The root block has relative-cover size 2; every subsequent block has relative-cover size 1. Hence the new entire algorithm is polynomial in r.

Given the previously attached shared hub color, attaching each new triangle provides exactly eight weighted combinations of its two fresh hub colors and sink color. There are five choices for the first root color, so

**Z(chain_r)=5*8^r.**

At r=40, n=121, arcs=120, blocks=40, exact count 6646139978924579364519035301401722880. The executed run traversed 121 source-cover branch nodes and 1000 local color-combination evaluations. In the preceding /077 theorem any fixed-size global separator splits this long path into at most finitely many long runs, at least one requiring a relative local two-cover growing with r; fixed global b,t do not yield a polynomial guarantee. This family is also of treewidth two, so the construction does not pretend that standard treewidth methods were incapable of solving it.
