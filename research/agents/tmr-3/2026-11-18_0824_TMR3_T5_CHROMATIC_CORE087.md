## 087B: unbounded-girth local-consistency obstruction

For EVERY integer h>=3 there exists a connected simple directed source D_h with:
  (i) NO T5 coloring;
  (ii) underlying girth >h, minimum degree >=h+5, treewidth >=h+5;
  (iii) every induced subsource on v<=h vertices is an oriented forest and has EXACT count 5^c*2^(v-c), where c is the number of its tree components;
  (iv) no private (1,1) path compression and no singleton repeated-pair affine merge.

Proof: By Erdos's high-girth/high-chromatic existence theorem choose undirected H with girth>h and chi>=h+6. Choose a vertex-minimal induced H' with chi>=h+6; then chi(H')=h+6, H' is connected and vertex-critical, and its minimum degree is >=chi-1=h+5. Orient every edge exactly once. The 5-chromatic necessary condition makes D_h T5-UNSAT. All induced subsets of size at most h are forests. Each forest component has five root choices and two choices per subsequent directed edge (either orientation), proving 5^c*2^(v-c). No source internal vertex has total degree two, and every single original arc has two allowed endpoint differences without repeated-pair intersection; affine closure does not merge. Treewidth >=chi-1 follows by graph coloring along a tree decomposition. The existence is NONCONSTRUCTIVE; no efficient recognizer for these high-girth instances is claimed.

This proves that testing induced UNSAT witnesses up to any FIXED size cannot decide all T5 inputs. It does NOT rule out global propagation, polynomial local-consistency algorithms, Fourier methods, alternate graph representations, or general exact algorithms. It is NOT a same-machine computational lower bound.
