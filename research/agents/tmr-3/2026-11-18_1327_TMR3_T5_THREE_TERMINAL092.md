## Theorem 1: exact 3-terminal gluing with 25 relative colors

Let directed source modules H_1,...,H_k have precisely the same three labeled terminals s,t,u and disjoint other vertices, and no arcs between their private vertex sets. Define

    F_i(d,e) = #{T5 colorings of H_i with (x_s,x_t,x_u)=(0,d,e)}.

The simultaneous translation x_v -> x_v + c (mod 5) is an exact color-preserving bijection. Therefore

    Z(union_i H_i) = 5 * sum_{d=0}^4 sum_{e=0}^4 product_i F_i(d,e).

This handles arbitrary orientations and internal graphs, including contradictory modules. It is exact *counting* of original vertex assignments. The dimension is 25, independent of the number k of modules. With 25 certified module messages already calculated, 25(k-1) multiplications and 24 additions (plus final multiply by 5) suffice, with exact integers of O(n) bits. This does not assert each F_i is easy to calculate, nor that 25 states are necessary for any solver.

## Theorem 2: explicit three-terminal vector for all-forward K(p,p)

For p>=3, choose three distinct left-side terminals. Write F_p(d,e) for their fixed-color count. All entries are zero except:

    F_p(0,0) = a_p = 2^p + 2^(p-2) - 2,
    F_p(0,2) = F_p(0,3) = F_p(2,0) = F_p(3,0)
              = F_p(2,2) = F_p(3,3) = b_p = 2^(p-3).

Proof: a right-side color must be an outneighbor of every colored left vertex. A right color has exactly two in-neighbors in T5, so at most two distinct left colors can appear. Among pairs containing 0, exactly {0,2} and {0,3} share a common outneighbor. For all-equal terminals, all private lefts equal 0 gives 2^p choices for the p right vertices; each of two other compatible left colors contributes 2^(p-3)-1 further private-left assignments, with forced right color. For a compatible nonconstant terminal tuple, all p-3 private lefts freely choose between the two permitted colors, while all right colors are forced. All other terminal tuples give zero.

Independent support enumeration for p=3,4,5,6,7 exactly reproduces all 25 entries in the closed formula.

## Theorem 3: 3-connected large-treewidth easy family C(k,p)

Construct k>=2 disjoint all-forward K(p,p) copies, p>=4, and identify precisely the same THREE left-side terminals across copies, leaving all other vertices private. Then

    n = 3 + k(2p-3),       m = kp^2,
    vertex-connectivity = 3,
    minimum degree = p,   treewidth >= p,
    tau_missing = k(p-3),
    Z(C(k,p)) = 5 * [a_p^k + 6 b_p^k].

Proof of 3-connectivity: after deletion of any one or two vertices, at least one of the three common left terminals remains. Every remaining right vertex is adjacent to it. Every remaining private left vertex still has at least p-2 >=2 right neighbors, so the graph remains connected. Deleting all three common terminals disconnects the k modules. Each intact K(p,p) subgraph has treewidth p, implying treewidth at least p. For the bipartite missing-cross-edge complement, all missing edges touch the k(p-3) private left vertices, giving a vertex-cover upper bound k(p-3). A matching of that size assigns each private left vertex from module i to a distinct right vertex in module i+1 cyclically, giving the lower bound by Konig's theorem. Every private vertex has degree p, and no degree-one/two private-path interior exists for p>=4.

The count follows by substituting Theorem 2 into Theorem 1. This is a **3-vertex-connected** graph with unbounded treewidth, minimum degree, and global complement-cover size, while still having an exact polynomial-time source-authenticated counting algorithm.

**Example k=20,p=8:** n=263, m=1280, connectivity 3, minimum degree 8, treewidth >=8, tau_missing=100, and exact count:

    559129496820275798186261882797788214499516649308160

That integer has 51 decimal digits. The full original graph is not reduced to a 2-terminal composition; the construction has no separator of size <3.
