\section*{The strongest implemented decision wrapper}
In \texttt{double\_wrapper.py}, \texttt{inner} constructs an AND/OR DAG using
unit propagation, restrictions, splitting by connected variable components,
and exact normalized formula keys. The four strategies are flat, components,
separator and separator\_fast. Separator searches are heuristics, not invocations
of a published fixed-parameter theorem.

For a boundary variable set $B$, fixing the same assignment $a$ in every component
gives the exact decision identity
\[
\operatorname{SAT}(F)=
\bigvee_{a\in\{0,1\}^{B}}\ \bigwedge_j
\operatorname{SAT}(C_{a,j}),
\]
where components within one branch have disjoint remaining variable sets.
OR is Shannon restriction; AND is valid because component assignments can be
combined. Unit propagation preserves satisfiability. Local bijective variable
renaming preserves the SAT bit; merging exact equal normalized formulas is safe
for that decision service. Identical conjuncts and disjuncts are idempotent.
These identities explain correctness beyond the finite test corpus.

The inner worklist continues until each node is constant or a standard CNF leaf
of measured size at most $\max\{2,\lfloor M^{2/3}\rfloor\}$. The outer solver
repeats the construction on unresolved leaves and asserts strict size decrease.
Branching removes variables, and component splitting takes proper subproblems,
so this supplies a terminating exact decision procedure. There is no reference
SAT call in production. The encoding measure is the prototype's declared bit
measure, not a proof that Python objects have that storage size.

Consequently W1 decision semantics, the per-leaf cutoff of W2, and semantic W5
closure are addressed for this construction. Favorable W3 construction cost,
W4 aggregate branching, W6 simultaneous space, and quantitative recursive closure
are not established. An inner builder can solve much of SAT by enumeration before
emitting its small frontier. Even zero emitted leaves does not imply cheap work.
All stored intermediate formulas, internal gates, unsuccessful candidate trials,
sorting, keys and prime/hash arithmetic must enter a full resource analysis.

\section*{Variant catalogue}
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{Decision-guided and cycle wrappers.}
An answer-preserving route can be exposed with SAT calls. Replacing all probes
by recursive execution removes dependence on the original solver but does not
bound the resulting recursion tree. The cycle experiment distinguishes guided,
guided-one and branch-all. Its near-parent-sized recursive probes must remain
in the recurrence. The separate CYCLEGAIN1 theorem permits suitable typed
contracting cycles; it is not a bound for every such implementation.
\item[] \textbf{Double forward wrapper.}
This solves the specific dependency on a current-scale SAT answer before
constructing alternatives. It enforces small leaves by doing whatever branching
is necessary, with the aggregate price still unresolved.
\item[] \textbf{Equality quotient.}
The explicit two-clause equality certificates support union-find substitution.
This recovers equality-copy sources cheaply in its stated domain. Removing
padding is not power shrinkage relative to the unpadded source.
\item[] \textbf{Elimination, adaptive and guarded variants.}
Davis--Putnam elimination existentially projects a variable. The tested implementation
caps resolvent candidates and accepts size improvements. Adaptive selection scores
candidate descendants. The guard aborts a literal-visit budget and falls back to
a safe quotient; it does not cap all machine work, total depth or live memory.
These are actual implementation mechanisms, with no universal target bound.
\item[] \textbf{Charged backdoors and component trees.}
The existing recurrence already charges acquisition, residual solving and
combination. The unresolved part is favorable coverage and discovery on the
claimed general domain, not whether acquisition was conceptually charged.
\item[] \textbf{Disjunctive and lazy wrappers.}
Generate restriction cubes one at a time and stop on a SAT child. UNSAT requires
complete coverage. Answer caches contain answers actually computed. This reduces
unnecessary eager construction on some instances, but does not establish its
worst-case exponent.
\item[] \textbf{Parallel portfolios.}
An entire exact solver of the same parent can win with either SAT or UNSAT.
A residual branch can establish parent SAT but cannot alone establish parent
UNSAT. Recorded four-process races omit losing-process work and peak aggregate
memory. A finite portfolio needs uniform favorable coverage plus a joint
scheduling and memory guarantee.
\item[] \textbf{Virtual and indexed children.}
These implement exact child-bit access. The original virtual child may scan the
parent per query. Block indexing exchanges index storage for scanning work.
The recorded 36,687 bit checks validate finite encodings; index acquisition,
descriptors and nested access costs remain part of composition.
\item[] \textbf{Prime-lineage paired wrapper and wrapper-as-SAT.}
LEAD-0024's paired toy has 89 variables and 620 clauses; imposing a wrong lineage
identification gives 628 clauses and UNSAT. Its correct projection recovers the
source. Formula-internal consistency is therefore real, rather than merely an
external provenance demand. An arbitrary decision algorithm need not explicitly
store the satisfying execution, and universal-route obligations are not supplied
for free by an existential route variable.
\item[] \textbf{Precommit, ratchet and SAT-native reconvergence.}
These test fixed identity, delayed re-separation and consistency under later
queries. Binding is not the same as hiddenness or computational hardness.
LEAD-0022 shows re-separation also on Horn and 2-SAT, and polynomial replay under
the contradiction hypothesis. Such mechanisms constrain a declared service;
they do not establish unrestricted decision cost.
\item[] \textbf{Code-specialized macro DAG.}
LEAD-0030's DELAY guard defeats generic exact-configuration merging as a
K-independent compactor. Semantic source-dependent normalization remains a
candidate. Merely naming that quotient does not bound its acquisition cost.
\end{description}

\section*{Compatibility, exact quantifiers and joint realization}
The decision cache preserves $\operatorname{SAT}(F)$ after local renaming. A
lineage service may ask a later restriction using the original name map or a
prime label. Equal SAT bits do not preserve that service. For example, $x$ and
$\neg x$ are both SAT, but restriction $x=1$ distinguishes them. Combining these
interfaces requires retained translation/lineage data or a proof that the caller
needs only the SAT bit. That added data and its use must be charged.

For a finite family of complete correct solvers $W_i$, a useful coverage claim is
\[
\forall F\ \exists i\le r:\quad
T_{W_i}(F)\le B_T(|F|),\quad S_{W_i}(F)\le B_S(|F|).
\]
The same $i$ must satisfy both bounds. This still requires a scheduler with
proved joint bounds; unrestricted slow competitors may consume excessive space.
Input-dependent choices of winner are acceptable. Merely identifying a good
winner after measuring a finite sample proves no such universal statement.

For the one-type recursive contract, favorable bounds
$q\le M^{a+o(1)}$, child size $M^{b+o(1)}$, local work $M^{c+o(1)}$ and a valid
workspace exponent $s$ feed the existing conditional result
\[
C=\max\{c,a/(1-b)\},\qquad
C+\max\{s,b\}<2\cos(\pi/7).
\]
The point of this audit is to avoid reopening semantics already supplied by a
specific implementation, while also avoiding assembling time from one variant
and space from a different realization.

\section*{Tests and evidence}
Historical records: the double-wrapper report records 2,368 inner/outer checks;
the gap suite records 1,572 answer/replay checks and 1,920 projection checks;
the portfolio suite records 1,857 correctness checks. These are reviewed historical
results, not newly rerun totals. Among the 12 recorded random portfolio cases,
disj3 has lower literal-visit work than lazy. On the equality-copy control,
quotient costs 7,588 versus disj3's 15,144 and lazy's 68,641. On the independent
UNSAT-component control, disj3 costs 12,114 versus lazy's 3,371. Counts are
incomplete work proxies and do not support asymptotic rankings.

A fresh interoperability regression composes a fixed two-variable Shannon
cover with the existing flat and separator\_fast double-wrapper backends.
It covers 271 formulas: all 256 subsets of the eight full-width three-variable
clauses, eight stored random eight-variable cases, two pigeonhole cases, two
XOR cases, an independent product, and two planted separator cases. All four
boundary assignments are evaluated where present. Reference answers are obtained
before production execution; the reference entry point is then replaced by a
raising guard. All 542 combined checks pass, with zero guarded reference calls,
in approximately 0.13 seconds under a 25-second alarm. This is COMPUTATIONAL
interoperability evidence, not a new cheap-merge theorem. Code and output hashes
are recorded in the JSON. No new peak-space measurement is claimed.

\section*{Literature and barriers}
Williams, \emph{Self-Improvement for Circuit-Analysis Problems} (STOC 2024),
Section 5 explicitly leaves extension to the P=NP regime open:
\url{https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/stoc-self-improvement.pdf}.
Its circuit-size/input-parameter hypotheses cannot be replaced by ordinary CNF
encoded length without a charged transfer. This audit imports no new theorem
from it. The time-space endpoint and its model requirements remain those already
recorded in main4/main5.

As a cross-domain analogy, a software component preserving a Boolean result is
not interchangeable with one preserving an interactive protocol. This helps
state the decision/lineage mismatch but supplies no complexity theorem.
No nonrelativizing or nonalgebrizing hinge is established by these tests. Prime
labels use no cryptographic assumption. Natural Proofs is not newly invoked:
this audit does not construct a large useful circuit property.
