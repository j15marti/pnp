\section{Exact conditional accounting theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Critical-boundary recursive closure]
Fix $0<h<1$ and $0\le p<1$. Suppose one fixed total uniform deterministic
constructor $W$ maps every sufficiently large ordinary canonical CNF of
bit length $n$ to an exact OR of ordinary canonical CNFs. It sequentially
enumerates at most $n^{1-h+o(1)}$ children, each of length at most
$n^{h+o(1)}$, in total nonrecursive time $n^{1+o(1)}$ and retained local
workspace $n^{p+o(1)}$. The errors are worst-case uniform in length.
The enumeration can be suspended and resumed, and all scans, replay,
queries, indexing, decoding, failed routes and output are included.
The domain is recursively closed. Then a single fully recursive exact
decider has
\[
 T(n)\le n^{1+o(1)},\qquad
 S(n)\le n^{\max(p,h)+o(1)}.
\]
Space counts every descendant input and suspended enumerator, excluding
only the original read-only input. No free external storage is allowed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose fixed $h<\alpha<1$. Uniform shrinkage gives a fixed threshold $B$
above which every child has length at most $n^\alpha<n$. The algorithm
uses $W$ above $B$, and brute force below $B$. This fixes one algorithm
independently of any accuracy parameter. Its recursion depth is
$O(\log\log n)$. Exact OR semantics proves correctness by induction.

For arbitrary $\varepsilon>0$, choose $\delta>0$ with
$\delta<\varepsilon$ and
$\delta(2+\varepsilon)<\varepsilon(1-h)$.
Above a proof threshold $B_\varepsilon$, uniform bounds give
\[
 T(n)\le n^{1+\delta}
       +n^{1-h+\delta}T(n^{h+\delta}).
\]
This notation bounds each child separately; integer rounding is absorbed
by the uniform exponent slack. Under the strong-induction hypothesis
$T(m)\le C_\varepsilon m^{1+\varepsilon}$, the child sum is at most
\[
C_\varepsilon n^{1+h\varepsilon+\delta(2+\varepsilon)}
=o(n^{1+\varepsilon}).
\]
The local term is also $o(n^{1+\varepsilon})$. Enlarge the proof threshold
to absorb both terms, then choose $C_\varepsilon$ to cover its finite
prefix. Thus the same fixed algorithm is $O_\varepsilon(n^{1+\varepsilon})$
for every positive $\varepsilon$, or $n^{1+o(1)}$.
Importantly, $B_\varepsilon$ is an induction threshold, not a change to
the algorithm's fixed stopping threshold $B$.

For space, a depth-first traversal retains local states at scales at most
$n,n^\alpha,n^{\alpha^2},\ldots$, plus the explicit child inputs.
The first child costs at most $n^{h+o(1)}$ bits, and no descendant is
larger. For any fixed positive slack, every local state is bounded by
$n^{p+\text{slack}}$ above a fixed threshold. Summing at most
$O(\log\log n)$ terms proves the stated bound. Stack bookkeeping and
constant-size leaf work are subpolynomial. The assumed resumable interface
is essential: neither an entire child list nor repeated source scans are
free in this proof.
\end{proof}

\section{What this means for the proof spine}
Fortnow's matched deterministic random-access endpoint excludes SAT in
simultaneous $n^{1+o(1)}$ time and $n^{1-\epsilon}$ space for fixed
$\epsilon>0$ \cite{fortnow}. Therefore this all-input $W$ contract is
itself impossible on the matched model. The separation program must prove
\[
 P=NP\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists W\ \forall F\ \text{the above charged contract}.
\]
That implication is OPEN. Once $W$ is supplied, no polynomial seed or
exponent $K$ is needed. Its existence cannot be inferred from $P=NP$
merely because all required semantic decisions become polynomial.
This remains a conditional contradiction, not a separation proof.

\section{Hidden-cost audit and literature clue}
The KPATH1/KFIXGUARD1 record in the root-main snapshot gives
\[
C_*(K)=\max\{r,\chi,\delta+p,a+\beta K,(\eta+g)/(1-\theta)\}.
\]
A root-scale call to the seed decider is not removed by solving the
recursive term. These are bounds for a declared implementation, not
lower bounds against all SAT algorithms. The q-squared relation ledger
in main5 likewise charges representation, acquisition and composition.

Williams's Lemma 4.1 concerns circuit families and, under its stated
simulation hypotheses, motivates algorithmic self-improvement
\cite{williams}. Under self-composition its exponent ledger obeys
\[
a_{d+1}=\frac{a_db_d}{1+b_d},\qquad b_{d+1}=b_d^2.
\]
For fixed $b_0>1$, exact algebra gives
\[
b_d=b_0^{2^d},\qquad
a_d=\frac{a_0(b_0-1)b_0^{2^d-1}}{b_0^{2^d}-1}
\longrightarrow a_0\left(1-\frac1{b_0}\right)>0.
\]
The identity follows from
$\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}(1+b_0^{2^j})=(b_0^{2^d}-1)/(b_0-1)$.
Thus repeated use of this particular self-composition with a fixed
superlinear input exponent neither sends the search exponent to zero nor
controls input-size growth. This is not a limitation on every algorithm
in the paper. Its stronger quantifiers and near-linear hypotheses matter.
