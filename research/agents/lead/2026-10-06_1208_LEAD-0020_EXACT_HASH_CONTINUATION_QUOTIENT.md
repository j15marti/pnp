## Question

Can the hash-routed wrapped DAG yield a hardness-relevant invariant by quotienting nodes only when they have identical future SAT behavior under every legal future hash-selected continuation?

## 1. Exact continuation signature

Fix the answer-preserving wrapper state

\[
s=(F\upharpoonright \rho,\;U),
\]

where \(U\) is the set of unassigned original variables.

At state \(s\), for every legal next variable \(v\in U\), define

\[
b_0(s,v)=\operatorname{SAT}\bigl((F\upharpoonright \rho)\upharpoonright(v=0)\bigr).
\]

Let \(b(s)=\operatorname{SAT}(F\upharpoonright\rho)\). The wrapper chooses

\[
a(s,v)=
\begin{cases}
0,&b_0(s,v)=b(s),\\
1,&b_0(s,v)\ne b(s),
\end{cases}
\]

and moves to \(T(s,v)=s\upharpoonright(v=a(s,v))\).

Define the exact recursive continuation signature

\[
\Sigma(s)
=
\left(
b(s),
\{(v,b_0(s,v),\Sigma(T(s,v))):v\in U\}
\right),
\]

with the obvious terminal form when \(U=\varnothing\).

Two states are merged iff their exact recursive signatures are identical.

This signature captures every future SAT response generated by every possible sequence of hash-selected variable choices in the declared wrapper.

## 2. LABELED-ACTION-BASELINE1 — PROVED/EXACT

If variable labels are part of the legal continuation interface, then two states with different unassigned-variable sets \(U\ne U'\) have different legal next-action domains.

Therefore at restriction depth \(d\), before using any SAT semantics at all,

\[
\#\{\text{continuation classes}\}
\ge
\binom nd
\]

whenever one reachable state exists for each fixed-variable subset.

At middle depth,

\[
\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
=
2^{n-O(\log n)}.
\]

This exponential width is not a hardness theorem. It can arise on Horn, 2-SAT, or even a trivial formula because it is partly just the identity of the remaining labeled variables.

Hence raw HASH-CONTSEP based on the full labeled action domain is **FALSIFIED as a hardness invariant**.

## 3. FIBER-CONTSEP1 — refined diagnostic

To remove the labeled-subset baseline, fix one restriction mask \(M\subseteq[n]\).

Let \(\mathcal S_M\) be all wrapper states reachable after fixing exactly the variables in \(M\), possibly through different variable orders.

Define the same-subset semantic fiber width

\[
w(M)
=
\left|
\{\Sigma(s):s\in\mathcal S_M\}
\right|.
\]

At depth \(d\),

\[
W_d
=
\max_{|M|=d} w(M).
\]

Also define the excess quotient ratio

\[
E_d
=
\frac{\#\{\Sigma(s):|M(s)|=d\}}
{\binom nd}.
\]

Both quantities factor out the unavoidable multiplicity of different variable subsets.

These are **diagnostics**, not lower bounds.

## 5. FIBER-CONTROL1 — computational falsification of simple semantic-width hardness

The strongest same-subset continuation fiber in this test occurs in the algebraically easy 3-XOR control, not the planted 3-SAT instance.

At \(n=14\),

\[
W_{\rm 3SAT}=6,
\qquad
W_{\rm XOR}=14,
\]

and

\[
\max_d E_d(\mathrm{3SAT})\approx3.308,
\qquad
\max_d E_d(\mathrm{XOR})\approx5.140.
\]

Thus even after removing the \(\binom nd\) route/subset baseline,

\[
\boxed{
\text{more future-inequivalent hash continuations}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{greater computational hardness}.
}
\]

Affine/Gaussian-elimination structure can support substantial continuation diversity with a compact polynomial-time evaluator.

Therefore a simple HASH-CONTSEP1 statement based only on the number of exact continuation classes is **not sufficient**.

## 6. Why the failure occurs

The quotient counts how many different continuation functions occur. It does not measure the complexity of evaluating one of those functions.

A family may contain many distinct functions

\[
g_s:r\mapsto\text{future SAT-response transcript},
\]

while all \(g_s\) belong to a compact, efficiently evaluable representation class.

3-XOR is the hostile example: different restrictions can yield many distinct affine continuation functions, but Gaussian elimination services them efficiently.

This is the same representation-bypass phenomenon already exposed by ANSWER-VECTOR-CAP1 and the static-data-structure audit.

## 7. CONT-EVAL1 — revised candidate

For a wrapper state \(s\), define its route-response function

\[
g_s(r)
=
\text{SAT-response transcript produced by legal future route }r.
\]

A representation \(Q_s\) is sufficient if there is a declared evaluator \(E\) with

\[
E(Q_s,r)=g_s(r)
\]

for every legal future route \(r\).

The potentially load-bearing quantity is not

\[
|\{g_s\}|,
\]

but the minimum fully charged ordinary cost of acquiring and servicing such a representation:

\[
\mathcal A_M(s)
=
\min_{Q_s,E}
\left\{
C_{\rm acquire}(Q_s)
+
C_{\rm service}(E,Q_s)
:
E(Q_s,r)=g_s(r)\;\forall r
\right\}.
\]

Candidate target:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_M(s_n)=n^{\omega(1)}
}
\]

for SAT-forced states \(s_n\).

This is **OPEN** and is essentially a representation-independent continuation-evaluator lower bound.

## 8. Relationship to the current main proof

The result narrows the hash-DAG route:

1. exponential raw route count: insufficient;
2. exponential labeled continuation width: insufficient because of the \(\binom nd\) action-domain baseline;
3. same-subset semantic continuation width: still insufficient; affine/XOR can exceed 3-SAT;
4. remaining plausible target: lower-bound the ordinary acquisition/service complexity of the continuation evaluator itself.

This lands back on the project's central acquisition theorem rather than bypassing it.

## 9. What remains useful about the hash-routed DAG

The construction is still useful as an accounting and adversarial framework:

- it generates many alternate continuation functions from a single wrapper rule;
- it makes illegal DAG mergers detectable by exact behavioral equivalence;
- it exposes which distinctions are only route metadata and which are genuinely semantic;
- it supplies a clean testbed for compact-evaluator bypasses;
- it can be combined with SRAS RETAIN/REACQUIRE/EXTERNAL accounting.

But the hash must not be credited with hardness merely because it fans out the continuation family.

## 10. Stronger possible target

A genuinely stronger theorem would need both:

### SAT-FORCED-EVAL1
SAT correctness forces a particular continuation service \(g_s\) or equivalent ordinary capability.

### CONT-EVAL-LB1
Every ordinary representation/evaluator that supplies that service has superpolynomial acquisition/use cost, while easy controls admit polynomial representations.

Only their conjunction could turn the hash-routed continuation family into a hardness route.
