\section{What remains open, according to the recovered source}
Removing a retained formula is not a bound on all retained information.
The realized system may hold linear-sized clause-ID scopes, ancestor
assignment masks, component grouping structures and normalization maps.
Residual access still reads the original source. A smaller semantic residual
does not by itself make the access cost depend only on that residual's length.
A capped frontier can fall back to exponentially many Shannon branches.

Thus the corrected target is to prove favorable joint total work and
workspace for an actual all-input realization, or a suitable covered system
or achievable-region improvement. It is not to invent source-backed replay,
bounded caching or their concrete combination again.

For example, sufficient critical-boundary bounds remain
\[
q(N)\le N^{1-h+o(1)},\quad
m(N)\le N^{h+o(1)},\quad
A(N)\le N^{1+o(1)},\quad S_{\mathrm{local}}(N)\le N^{p+o(1)}
\]
with $0<h<1$, $p<1$, compatible resumable access and all replay charged.
The already-established closure then gives
$T(N)\le N^{1+o(1)}$ and
$S(N)\le N^{\max(p,h)+o(1)}$.
The recovered implementation does not establish those inequalities on every
input. Its general correctness and its finite test evidence are not a proof
of that quantitative premise.
