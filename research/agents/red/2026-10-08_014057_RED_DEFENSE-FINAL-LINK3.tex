\begin{abstract}
This audit stress-tests the current WRAPPER claims against padding, variable
renaming, independent conjunction products, Horn, 2-SAT and XOR controls,
source-backed descriptors, fixed-width hashes, malformed generated encodings,
and recurrence boundary cases.  The executable completed 208,853 checks in
3.97 seconds.  The explicit generator--shrinker equivalence and finite exponent elimination survive
after their strict encoding and accounting hypotheses are made explicit.
Six tempting shortcuts fail.  The all-input acquisition of the first strict
successor or favorable typed epoch remains \Open, so this audit does not prove
$P\ne NP$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Disposition}

The defensible statement after this audit is narrow and useful.  If a uniform
constructor really emits a self-contained generated exact cover within the
declared exponent bounds, a strict total verifier rejects malformed words,
unused selectors map to a valid padded UNSAT instance, and all source access
and construction are charged, then the generator--shrinker equivalence remains valid.  If a
genuine strict successor with $h<1$ is available, the finite recurrence that
removes fixed machine exponents $K$ and $\sigma$ also remains valid.

The following are not valid substitutes for that successor: measuring size
only after padding; unioning independent-component covers; returning a short
handle while retaining the parent; merging on a fixed hash alone; accepting
malformed generator outputs permissively; or checking only that a typed cycle
contracts.

\section{Padding and normalization}

Let the semantic source have length $M$ and let a representation inflate it
to $N=M^p$.  A returned object of size $N^h$ has length
\[
       N^h=M^{ph}.
\]
Consequently a sublinear exponent relative to $N$ is a strict semantic
successor relative to the original source only if $ph<1$.  Three exact
boundary witnesses are
\[
 (p,h,ph)=(3/2,2/3,1),\qquad
 (2,2/3,4/3),\qquad
 (4/3,3/4,1).
\]
The finite audit also appended tautological clauses to SAT and UNSAT formulas
and confirmed semantic preservation.  This is exactly why padding is a valid
representation change but cannot manufacture contraction.  The original
input reading and padding construction must be measured relative to the same
un-padded parameter used by the lower-bound endpoint.

\section{Renaming control}

For a bijection $\pi$ on variables and a set of polarity flips $s$, transport
an assignment by
\[
 y_{\pi(i)}=x_i\oplus s_i.
\]
Literal evaluation is preserved term by term, so
\[
 \SAT(F)=\SAT(\pi_s(F)).
\]
The program checked this identity both at formula level and for every
assignment of each sampled formula.  It performed 13,053 checks over 350
formulas, with no mismatch.

This exact symmetry does not make a raw hash semantic.  It also does not
preserve every portfolio label.  For example,
\[
 (x_1)\wedge(\neg x_1\vee x_2)\wedge(\neg x_2\vee x_3)
\]
is Horn, but flipping only $x_1$ produces a clause
$x_1\vee x_2$, so the transformed presentation is not Horn.  A wrapper that
normalizes signs must rerun the base-class recognizer or transport a verified
certificate; it may not inherit the old class label merely from SAT
invariance.

\section{Independent-product control}

For formulas on disjoint variables,
\[
 \SAT(F\wedge G)=\SAT(F)\wedge\SAT(G),
\]
not the corresponding OR.  If
\[
 F\equiv\bigvee_{i\le q_1}F_i,
 \qquad
 G\equiv\bigvee_{j\le q_2}G_j,
\]
then distributivity gives
\[
 F\wedge G\equiv
 \bigvee_{(i,j)\in[q_1]\times[q_2]}(F_i\wedge G_j).
\]
The audit checked this Boolean identity on 900 finite cover pairs and checked
AND semantics on 25 CNF pairs.  The smallest recorded union failure combines
the empty true CNF with $(x)\wedge(\neg x)$: the conjunction is UNSAT while
the proposed union answer is SAT.

For two equal independent components, a componentwise cover with fanout
$M^{a+o(1)}$ becomes a naive Cartesian fanout $M^{2a+o(1)}$.  At
$(a,b)=(1/3,2/3)$, its explicit aggregate volume exponent is
\[
        2a+b=\frac43.
\]
This is a scoped no-go for independently composing two disjunctive covers.
It is not a lower bound on a source-global compiler, which may exploit
structure across the whole conjunction.

\section{Tractable-family controls}

The runner independently implemented forward chaining for Horn CNF,
implication-graph SCCs for 2-CNF, and Gaussian elimination over $\mathbb F_2$
for affine systems.  It compared each answer with exhaustive assignments for
variables up to seven.  All 840 Horn, 840 2-SAT, and 840 XOR cases agreed.

These results defend the portfolio interpretation: once charged recognition
certifies one of these languages, a wrapper may terminate in a VALUE leaf.
They do not support extrapolation from residual counts, hashes, or ease on
these families to general CNF.  Recognition still reads the represented
source, and an all-input favorable coverage theorem remains necessary.

\section{Source carry and compact descriptors}

Let a descriptor have physical scale $M^b$, $0<b<1$, but require an $M$-bit
parent to recover the ordinary residual.  As a self-contained typed state its
scale is
\[
       \max\{1,b\}=1.
\]
Equivalently, decoding from length $M^b$ back to length $M$ has scale $1/b$,
and the apparent contraction has closed round-trip product
\[
       b\left(\frac1b\right)=1.
\]
The audit verified these identities at 323 rational values of $b$.  Sharing
one parent may reduce duplication, but it does not remove the scale-one live
dependency.  To claim an ordinary successor, the returned object must be
self-contained.  To use a parent-aware typed route instead, the parent state,
rereads, decoder traffic, and complete macro-cycle must remain in the Bellman
ledger.

\section{Fixed hash collision controls}

The runner generated two certified families of legal CNFs: satisfiable
formulas with an explicit all-true witness, and unsatisfiable formulas
containing the complete contradictory two-variable clause set.  It then
hashed their canonical textual encodings with SHA-256.  Exact cross-answer
prefix collisions appeared as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{ccl}
4\text{ hex digits} & 820\text{ inputs} & \texttt{09e1},\\
6\text{ hex digits} & 13{,}427\text{ inputs} & \texttt{8414ac},\\
8\text{ hex digits} & 106{,}334\text{ inputs} & \texttt{ac905e47}.
\end{array}
\]
For the eight-digit witness, the full digests are
\begin{align*}
&\texttt{ac905e472f8e06e33118cfaa7d6307a34627ba7b461bcf9b75156f0e0d0da0b0},\\
&\texttt{ac905e4746494261fafd31dac693730f20f4cf35e6a48fd92d30fdac556045cc}.
\end{align*}
The first formula is UNSAT and the second is SAT.  Thus hash-only merging is
\Retired{} at all three tested widths.  A digest remains useful as a bucket
index when exact equality, semantic verification, or source replay resolves
collisions and its cost is charged.  The test is finite; its asymptotic point
is the elementary pigeonhole requirement that a fixed-length digest cannot be
an injective name for an unbounded formula domain.

\section{Malformed generated encodings}

The generator--shrinker equivalence needs a total strict verifier.  To expose the obligation,
the audit exhaustively enumerated a 16-bit padded unit-CNF encoding.  The
strict decoder checks the header, variable bounds, active record count,
inactive slots, and zero trailer.  A deliberately permissive decoder clamps
headers, drops invalid literals, and ignores the trailer.

Among 65,536 words, only 71 are well formed.  Of the malformed words, 63,685
are interpreted as satisfiable by the permissive decoder.  The first witness
is the all-zero word.  The strict decoder rejects it, while the permissive
decoder treats it as the empty SAT formula.  An unused selector leaf carrying
this word therefore creates a false SAT branch when the source is UNSAT.

This does not refute the generated-cover theorem.  Its definition already
requires padded ordinary CNFs and a valid UNSAT value for unused selectors.
For defense, the proof should retain the operational formulation:
\begin{description}
\item[] every verifier path rejects a malformed child;
\item[] every declared output and inactive selector has the fixed legal
length and padding discipline;
\item[] every unused selector maps to a valid padded UNSAT encoding;
\item[] variable identifiers, clause boundaries, and padding are checked
inside the charged $\widetilde O(m)$ bounded-fan-in verifier.
\end{description}

\section{Recurrence edge cases}

For $h=2/3$, the audit ranged over $1\le K\le40$ and six values of $\sigma$.
For each pair it selected the least relevant fixed depth
\[
 d\ge
 \max\left\{
  \min\{j:K(2/3)^j\le1\},
  \min\{j:\sigma(2/3)^j\le2/3\}
 \right\}.
\]
All 240 parameter cells landed exactly at
\[
       (t,s)=\left(1,\frac23\right),
       \qquad t+s=\frac53.
\]
This point is not in the general Williams product region because
$5/3>\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$.  It is instead excluded by Fortnow's theorem
against simultaneous $N^{1+o(1)}$ time and $N^{1-\varepsilon}$ space:
choosing $\varepsilon=1/4$ leaves space slack
$3/4-2/3=1/12$.  This confirms only the finite exponent-elimination
arithmetic for fixed $K$ and $\sigma$.  It assumes the strict successor; it
does not construct one \cite{williams2010,fortnow2000}.

Three boundary guards also passed.  A unit-scale zero-weight cycle preserved
an inherited exponent $17/2$ after 100 rounds.  A contracting cycle with
$\theta=9/10$ and weight $1/5$ has least time exponent
$(1/5)/(1-9/10)=2$; with space $1/2$ its sum is $5/2$, so contraction alone
does not meet the general $t+s<\varphi$ product endpoint.  Finally, two
pointwise pairs $(21/20,1/2)$ and $(4/5,4/5)$ each have sum below
$\varphi$, but taking the uniform worst-case time and space yields
$(21/20,4/5)$ and fails.  This is why
the same-realization maxima must be taken before invoking the lower bound.
