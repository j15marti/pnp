\begin{guardbox}
\textbf{Disposition.}
The OR-multiplexer Boolean identity is \EXACT.  Its bit-length bound needs a
polylogarithmic correction, which does not change any exponent.  The compact
generated-cover implication is also correct after the encoding obligations
below are made explicit.  It does not, however, derive the missing bridge
from one fixed polynomial SAT decider.  More strongly, the assumed generator
already contains a sublinear SAT-preserving successor and can be compiled
without the decider $A$.  Consequently the remaining assertion
``$P=NP$ forces such a generator'' is the separation hinge itself, not a
consequence presently supplied by OR multiplexing.
\end{guardbox}

\section{Objects audited}

This report audits the fixed-machine proof report and its companion Python
audit and JSON output under \path{research/agents/proof/}.
The audit concerns the exact mux identity, ordinary-CNF bit size, selector
width, padding, construction uniformity, recursive closure, simultaneous
time and space, and the quantifier needed to turn $P=NP$ into the proposed
generator.

Let $L(X)$ denote the number of bits in an ordinary explicit encoding.  This
distinguishes bit length from gate count, clause count, and literal-occurrence
count.  Polylogarithmic factors are written with $\widetilde O$ and are
absorbed only when an exponent statement explicitly uses $o(1)$.

\section{The explicit OR multiplexer}

\begin{proposition}[Corrected OR-MUX]
Fix a deterministic Boolean circuit $C:\{0,1\}^m\to\{0,1\}$ and explicit
strings $x_1,\ldots,x_q\in\{0,1\}^m$, where $q\ge1$.  There is a circuit
$D$ with $\ell=\lceil\log_2 q\rceil$ free selector bits such that
\[
 \exists z\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}:D(z)=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \bigvee_{i=1}^{q}C(x_i)=1.
\]
Unused leaves are mapped to a fixed rejecting word.  In gate count,
\[
 |D|=|C|+O(qm+\log q).
\]
If circuit wires, gate identifiers, and CNF variable identifiers are encoded
in binary, the ordinary Tseitin 3-CNF $Q$ has
\[
 L(Q)=\widetilde O\bigl(L(C)+qm+\log q\bigr).           \tag{1}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Pad to $q'<2q$ leaves.  For each of the $m$ bit positions, a balanced tree of
$q'-1$ two-input multiplexers selects the corresponding bit.  Each
multiplexer uses constantly many bounded-fan-in gates.  Feed the selected word
into one copy of $C$.  The Tseitin clauses enforce the unique value of every
internal gate, while the output unit clause enforces acceptance.  A textual
CNF needs $O(\log |D|)$ bits per variable identifier, which yields (1).
\end{proof}

The Boolean identity has status \EXACT.  The displayed $O(m^{K+o(1)}+qm)$
bound in the proof report is exact only in a unit-cost gate, clause, or
literal-occurrence measure.  For ordinary bit length it should be
$\widetilde O(m^{K+o(1)}+qm)$, equivalently
$m^{K+o(1)}+(qm)^{1+o(1)}$ when the parameters are polynomially bounded.
The exponent formula $\max\{bK,v\}$ therefore survives.

The finite test in \path{fixed_machine_successor_audit.py} does not construct
a mux circuit or a Tseitin formula.  Its variables named \texttt{separate}
and \texttt{selector} both evaluate the same family with Python
\texttt{any}.  The 5,904 checks are therefore a tautological sanity check of
the identity, not an implementation, size, padding, or encoding test.  The
symbolic proof above is sufficient; the computational count should not be
described as independent verification of a mux compiler.

\section{Selector, padding, and uniformity corrections}

The following repairs are mandatory but routine.

\begin{itemize}
\item[] \textbf{Selector width.}
The successor formula contains all $\ell$ selector variables.  Its bound must
therefore include $\ell$.  If the definition of $|E_{A,F}|$ counts every
input terminal and its description, then $\ell\le |E_{A,F}|$ and the charge
is already absorbed.  This convention must be retained in every use of the
theorem; standard gate count alone does not guarantee it.

\item[] \textbf{Output width.}
Every one of the $m$ output terminals must also be counted.  Hence a circuit
of description length $M^{h+o(1)}$ automatically has
$m\le M^{h+o(1)}$.  A convention allowing an uncharged bus or an external
source pointer would invalidate the contraction.

\item[] \textbf{Padded language.}
An arbitrary binary string of exactly $m$ bits is not automatically an
ordinary CNF encoding.  Fix once and for all a total padded encoding with a
length field and an ignored zero trailer, or a canonical padding by dummy
variables and tautological clauses.  Define the decider and the universal
verifier to reject malformed words.  Map every unused selector leaf to a
valid padded UNSAT instance, rather than relying on an unspecified malformed
word.  The conversion to the Williams endpoint encoding must be linear or
quasilinear and charged.

\item[] \textbf{Circuit uniformity.}
For a fixed clocked multitape machine $A$, a standard uniform circuit
simulation can be generated from $m$ and the hardwired transition table.
The construction time and live workspace must be bounded by the emitted
circuit description.  If $A$ is instead introduced as a RAM program, the
claimed near-$T$ circuitization is not automatic; either first choose a
multitape polynomial decider under $P=NP$, or prove the appropriate RAM
simulation.

\item[] \textbf{Identifier logarithms.}
Gate and variable identifiers add logarithmic factors to both the circuit
description and the ordinary 3-CNF.  These are harmless in
$M^{\alpha+o(1)}$ notation but prevent a literal $O(S)$ bit-length claim.
\end{itemize}

No item above supplies the missing semantic object.  They only make the
compiler source-checkable.

\section{The generator already is the shrinker}

The most important audit result is that the fixed polynomial SAT decider is
not needed to compile a generated exact OR cover.

\begin{theorem}[UNIVERSAL-VERIFY-COMPILE1]
Fix a reasonable explicit padded CNF encoding.  Suppose a uniform constructor
$G$ maps every length-$M$ CNF $F$ to a bounded-fan-in circuit $E_F$ with
selector $z$, $m$ output bits, and
\begin{align*}
 L(E_F)&\le M^{h+o(1)}, & m&\le M^{b+o(1)},\\
 \operatorname{time}_G(F)&\le M^{r+o(1)}, &
 \operatorname{space}_G(F)&\le M^{p+o(1)},
\end{align*}
such that every output is a valid padded CNF and
\[
 \SAT(F)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists z\;\SAT(E_F(z)).                              \tag{2}
\]
Then, without calling or unrolling any SAT decider, one can construct an
ordinary 3-CNF $Q_F$ satisfying
\begin{align*}
 \SAT(Q_F)&=\SAT(F),\\
 L(Q_F)&\le M^{\max\{h,2b\}+o(1)},                     \tag{3}\\
 \operatorname{time}(F\mapsto Q_F)
   &\le M^{\max\{r,h,2b\}+o(1)},                       \tag{4}\\
 \operatorname{space}(F\mapsto Q_F)
   &\le M^{\max\{p,h,2b\}+o(1)}.                      \tag{5}
\end{align*}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
There is a fixed deterministic verifier circuit $V_m(x,y)$ of size
$m^{2+o(1)}$ which rejects malformed $x$ and, for a valid $m$-bit CNF
encoding, satisfies
\[
 \exists y\;V_m(x,y)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\SAT(x)=1.
\]
A quadratic bound is conservative.  The verifier guesses truth values for at
most $m$ occurring variables, parses at most $m$ encoded bits, and checks
literal values by a quadratic scan.  Standard uniform circuitization only
adds polylogarithmic factors.

Form the composed circuit
\[
 D_F(z,y)=V_m(E_F(z),y).
\]
By (2), $\exists z,y\;D_F(z,y)=1$ exactly when $F$ is satisfiable.  Apply the
Tseitin transformation and constrain the output to one.  The circuit and
ordinary CNF bit lengths are
\[
 \widetilde O\bigl(L(E_F)+m^2\bigr)
 =M^{\max\{h,2b\}+o(1)}.
\]
Generating the fixed verifier topology and streaming the Tseitin clauses
gives (4).  Retaining the materialized successor together with the
constructor workspace gives (5).
\end{proof}

This implication is \EXACT under the displayed encoding convention.  At the
proof report's proposed values
\[
 h=\frac23,\qquad b=\frac{1}{3K},\qquad K\ge1,
\]
we have $2b\le h$.  Therefore the same $M^{2/3+o(1)}$ successor follows
without $C_{A,m}$, without the condition $bK\le h$, and without assuming
$P=NP$.  The generator $E_F$ is already a sublinear nondeterministic circuit
for the statement $\SAT(F)$: its witnesses are $(z,y)$.  Tseitin compilation
merely makes that fact explicit.

The theorem does not falsify the mux lemma.  It shows that muxing through the
assumed deterministic decider is not the source of the desired progress.

\section{Exact quantifier gap}

Let $\mathsf{PolySAT}(A)$ state that $A$ is a fixed uniform polynomial-time
SAT decider, and let $\mathsf{Gen}(A,G)$ denote the all-input generated-cover
contract.  The current proof establishes
\[
 \forall A\,\forall G\;
 \bigl(\mathsf{PolySAT}(A)\wedge\mathsf{Gen}(A,G)\bigr)
 \Longrightarrow \mathsf{FastSAT}.                     \tag{6}
\]
The known endpoint excludes $\mathsf{FastSAT}$ in the claimed simultaneous
region.  To refute $P=NP$, however, one needs
\[
 \bigl(\exists A\;\mathsf{PolySAT}(A)\bigr)
 \Longrightarrow
 \exists A\,G\;
 \bigl(\mathsf{PolySAT}(A)\wedge\mathsf{Gen}(A,G)\bigr).\tag{7}
\]
Nothing in OR-MUX-COMPILE1, fixed-code hardwiring, or the finite experiment
proves (7).  The source code of one fixed machine can specialize a uniform
constructor, but it does not imply the input-dependent semantic summary (2)
within near-linear time and sublinear space.

Moreover, UNIVERSAL-VERIFY-COMPILE1 removes $A$ from the semantic content of
$\mathsf{Gen}$.  The subscript $A$ can influence constants and the chosen
number $b$, but once $E_F$ exists, it independently yields the missing
SAT-preserving contraction.  Thus (7) is not a weak normalization lemma.  It
is the remaining separation hinge.

The phrase ``the generator is open'' should be qualified.  Given the
Williams endpoint and the compilation above, the conjunction
$\mathsf{PolySAT}(A)\wedge\mathsf{Gen}(A,G)$ at the proposed exponents is
inconsistent.  What remains \OPEN is the implication (7); proving it would
complete the contradiction.  Labeling an unconditional existence claim for
$A$ and $G$ as merely open obscures this logical distinction.

Equation (2) by itself does not merely restate SAT: the trivial generator can
output $F$ and has linear size.  The sublinear description, tiny output,
near-linear acquisition, and all-input closure are the substantive content.
Together they restate the missing structured compression theorem closely
enough that no independent bridge has yet been gained.

\section{Recursive closure and simultaneous resources}

Conditional on a valid all-input shrinker, the fixed-depth composition is
sound.  The following clauses must remain in the theorem statement.

\begin{itemize}
\item[] \textbf{All-input domain.}
The constructor must accept every ordinary CNF produced by the previous
Tseitin level, not only the original benchmark distribution or formulas with
a promise inherited from $F$.

\item[] \textbf{One encoding.}
Every successor must be converted to the same padded language used by the
next invocation.  Header, padding, and variable-renaming costs contribute
only polylogarithmic factors if implemented explicitly.

\item[] \textbf{Fixed depth.}
After choosing the hypothetical machine and certified constants, the depth
$d$ may be hardwired.  For fixed $d$, the accumulated $o(1)$ terms remain
$o(1)$.  Inputs below all asymptotic thresholds can be handled by a fixed
finite table or direct computation.

\item[] \textbf{Same realization.}
Materialize $F_1$, then generate $F_2$ while retaining $F_1$, discard the
predecessor after completion, and continue.  This single implementation has
time dominated by the first construction and live generated storage
$M^{h+o(1)}$.  The original input may remain on a read-only tape.  The final
machine call or universal-verifier successor must be charged on that same
run.

\item[] \textbf{No hidden source access.}
Neither $E_F$ nor a child may contain an uncharged pointer, oracle gate, or
random-access wire to the $M$-bit parent.  Counting source terminals and
wiring bits is essential.
\end{itemize}

Under these clauses, the recurrence calculation is \EXACT as a conditional
resource statement.  None of the clauses proves the acquisition premise.

\section{Required revision to the proof spine}

The honest strongest spine should state:
\begin{resultbox}
If $P=NP$ forced a near-linearly constructible, all-input generated exact OR
cover of sublinear circuit size and sufficiently small output width, then the
cover could be compiled directly, without the assumed SAT decider, into an
ordinary strict-power SAT successor.  Fixed-depth reapplication would reach
the forbidden Williams region.  The forcing implication remains open.
\end{resultbox}

OR-MUX-COMPILE1 remains a useful exact sharing lemma for explicit portfolios.
It removes a factor $q$ from repeated copies of a fixed circuit.  It does not
remove the explicit cover volume, and the compact-generator variant removes
that volume only by assuming the missing semantic compression in circuit
form.
