\begin{abstract}
This report tests whether four, six, or eight hexadecimal digits of a hash of
a normalized CNF residual can safely replace the residual in a WRAPPER cache.
It constructs one explicit parent CNF with $2^{20}$ distinct normalized
residuals, exactly half satisfiable, and searches bounded prefixes of that
family for truncated SHA-256 collisions.  Cross-answer collisions occur at
all three widths.  A cache that reuses an answer on digest equality therefore
returns incorrect answers.  Full-key verification restores exactness, but
retains the full residual and incurs comparison work.  Compact source replay
is exact on this family only because its history-to-residual map is explicitly
injective; it shifts the cost to history storage, regeneration, and equality
checking.  A pigeonhole-formula control shows that normalization can also
perform genuine exact symmetry merges.  The results are computational finite
models, not an asymptotic lower bound and not a proof that $P\ne NP$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Status and question}

The status of every numerical result in this report is
\textbf{COMPUTATIONAL}.  The exact family facts are elementary consequences
of the construction and are verified independently in the accompanying
program.  The experiment addresses a narrow cache question:
\[
  \text{Can a short digest serve as an exact identity for a normalized
  residual?}
\]
The answer is no for digest-only reuse on the finite witnesses below.  This
does not rule out a stronger source-checkable canonical representation.  The
construction and verification cost of such a representation would have to be
charged separately.

\section{A common parent with delayed history}

Fix $k=19$.  The parent has a control selector $p$, tag selectors
$x_1,\ldots,x_k$, disjoint tag variables $y_j,z_j$, and core variables
$a,b,c$.  For each tag index $j$, include
\[
 T_j=(x_j\lor y_j\lor z_j)\land
     (\neg x_j\lor\neg y_j\lor\neg z_j).
 \tag{1}
\]
After setting $x_j=0$, this gadget leaves $y_j\lor z_j$.  After setting
$x_j=1$, it leaves $\neg y_j\lor\neg z_j$.  Neither residual clause is a
unit, and the tag pairs are disjoint.

Let $X(u,v;r)$ be the standard two-clause CNF expressing
$u\mathbin{\oplus}v=r$.  Define
\begin{align*}
 E&=X(a,b;0)\land X(b,c;0)\land X(a,c;0),\\
 O&=X(a,b;0)\land X(b,c;0)\land X(a,c;1).
\end{align*}
The parent contains $p\lor C$ for every clause $C$ of $E$, and
$\neg p\lor C$ for every clause $C$ of $O$.  Thus $p=0$ activates $E$ and
$p=1$ activates $O$.

For a history $h=(b_0,b_1,\ldots,b_k)\in\bits^{k+1}$, set $p=b_0$ and
$x_j=b_j$, then run the same restriction, unit propagation, variable
renaming, clause sorting, and duplicate removal used by the tested WRAPPER.
Call the resulting normalized formula $R_h$.

\begin{lemma}[Family exactness]
The formulas $R_h$ are pairwise syntactically distinct.  Moreover,
\[
 \SAT(R_h)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b_0=0.
 \tag{2}
\]
Consequently the common parent produces $2^{20}=1{,}048{,}576$ distinct
normalized residuals, exactly half SAT and half UNSAT.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If two histories differ in tag bit $j$, one residual has
$y_j\lor z_j$ and the other has $\neg y_j\lor\neg z_j$ at the corresponding
normalized variables.  If they differ only at $b_0$, their core clause sets
are different.  Hence the normalized tuples differ.  Every isolated tag
clause is satisfiable.  The core $E$ is satisfiable by $a=b=c$, while $O$ is
contradictory because its first two equalities imply $a=c$ and its last
constraint requires $a\ne c$.
\end{proof}

The generated parent has 50 clauses and 61 variables.  Every $R_h$ has 25
clauses, 41 variables, and 20 connected components.  Eight boundary histories
were reconstructed both directly and through the parent restriction pipeline.
The two cores were independently evaluated on all eight assignments to their
three variables.

\section{Cache policies and charged work}

The comparison workload retains up to 96 collision buckets, adds at most 256
singleton keys, and makes four passes.  It is intentionally hostile and must
not be read as an average-case error rate.  Five policies are compared.
No-cache evaluation is the exact baseline.  Hash-only reuse stores one answer
per digest and performs no identity verification.  Verified hash buckets
retain complete normalized formulas and compare them structurally.  Full-key
reuse uses the normalized formula itself as the dictionary key.  Source replay
stores the common parent and a history descriptor, regenerating residuals to
reject a collision.

Short output does not make digest acquisition free.  The four-, six-, and
eight-digit workloads feed $948{,}200$, $474{,}100$, and $336{,}232$
serialized input bytes to the hash computation.  The verified-bucket policy
then pays the structural equality work in Table~\ref{tab:cache}.  It stores
slightly more logical payload than a full-key table because it retains the
full key and adds a digest index.  Thus this verified hash is an exact index,
not a compression theorem.

The source-replay policy stores the 747-byte common parent once, a three-byte
history descriptor and one answer byte per residual, and digest buckets.  Its
regeneration output is $584{,}896$, $35{,}872$, and $10{,}944$ formula tokens
for the respective workloads.  The reduced retained payload is real for this
family, but its exactness rests on the proved injectivity of the fixed
history-to-residual map.  A general WRAPPER would need a uniform source record
and replay theorem, with parent storage, history length, regeneration,
comparison, and recursion all charged.

\section{Equal present answers do not imply equal state}

Digest collisions can also be dangerous when both residuals have the same
current SAT answer.  Suppose histories $h$ and $h'$ differ at tag bit $j$.
Apply the same later restriction
\[
 y_j=0,\qquad z_j=0.
 \tag{4}
\]
The positive tag clause becomes empty, while the negative tag clause is
satisfied and disappears.  When both original residuals are SAT, one
restricted residual is UNSAT and the other remains SAT.

Among the bounded examples retained by the audit, 24 such SAT pairs were
recorded at four digits, 5 at six digits, and 10 at eight digits.  Therefore
``same present SAT bit'' is weaker than interchangeable residual state.  Any
cache intended to support later restrictions or recursive continuation needs
a congruence strong enough for that continuation.

\section{Pigeonhole control}

The pigeonhole audit supplies a positive control for exact normalization.  In
$\operatorname{PHP}(9,8)$, there are
\[
 8\cdot7\cdot6\cdot5=1{,}680
\]
legal histories placing four pigeons into distinct holes.  They produce 70
raw source-labelled restrictions but one residual after the tested
simplification and normalization pipeline.  This is a valid symmetry merge,
not a hash collision.

A second control enumerates all 1,024 assignments to a ten-variable prefix of
$\operatorname{PHP}(5,4)$.  The pipeline produces 33 distinct normalized
residuals, and 52 histories simplify immediately to false.  No four-, six-,
or eight-digit collision occurs among this small 33-residual set.  Every
residual is nevertheless UNSAT because it restricts an UNSAT parent.  Hence a
test that observes only the current SAT bit cannot detect an erroneous
identity merge in this control.

\section{Consequences for the WRAPPER gap}

\paragraph{Digest-only identity fails.}
The explicit cross-answer pairs prove that equality of a four-, six-, or
eight-digit digest is not an exact identity relation for these normalized
residuals.  Increasing the fixed width only makes finite search harder; it
does not turn the digest into a mathematical certificate of equality.

\paragraph{Verification repairs correctness but not contraction.}
A full-key comparison rejects every false collision and preserves all exact
reuse.  It must retain or reconstruct the information needed for that
comparison.  In the tested full-key policy, the verified hash table uses more
storage than the direct full-key table and also performs equality replay.

\paragraph{Source replay is the narrow opportunity.}
Compact histories can replace stored formulas when a source-checkable replay
map is uniform, exact, and cheap.  The finite family demonstrates this design
pattern, but it does not establish favorable all-input bounds.  In particular,
the common source, every history, all regenerated output, rejected
comparisons, and recursive reuses must be included in the claimed resources.

\paragraph{Normalization helps only where a theorem justifies it.}
The pigeonhole control shows substantial exact symmetry reduction.  The
independent tag family shows that the same syntactic normalization can still
leave exponentially many states.  Neither behavior alone supplies a general
all-input merge bound.

The narrow remaining arrow is therefore a uniform, source-checkable residual
identifier or replay rule that simultaneously proves exact continuation
equivalence and the needed construction, retained-state, verification, and
recursive resource bounds.  A short digest is not that rule by itself.

\section{Exact limitations}

The searches are bounded chosen-input experiments.  They are not asymptotic
collision lower bounds.  They test syntactic normalization by variable
renaming and simplification, not every possible semantic canonical form.  The
collision-focused workload deliberately increases the frequency of false
reuse and gives no average-case estimate.  SAT labels come from the proved
structure of this family rather than a general SAT solver.  Logical bytes and
tokens are reproducible accounting proxies, not resident-memory measurements.
The strongest 32-bit witnesses found within the limit are pairs; no larger
32-bit multicollision is claimed.  The largest 16-bit and 24-bit buckets have
9 and 3 members.  None of these finite facts proves or disproves $P=NP$.
