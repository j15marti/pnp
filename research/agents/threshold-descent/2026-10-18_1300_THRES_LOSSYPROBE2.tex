\section{CONSTRUCTION OR PROOF ATTEMPT}
Put $N=2^n$, $s=\lfloor N^\beta\rfloor$, and define
\[
 B=\lceil\log_2(s+1)\rceil+\lceil\log_2(n+s)\rceil+
 s(2+2\lceil\log_2(n+s)\rceil).
\]
There are at most $2^B$ size-$s$ Boolean circuits.
\begin{theorem}[LOSSY-PROBE-NOGO2]
Any deterministic exact decider for MCSP[$s$] using adaptive
source-bit probes must inspect at least $N-B$ distinct bits
on the all-zero truth table. A stand-alone lossy many-one
reduction to any target language has the same limitation
when all its source information comes from those probes.
\end{theorem}
\noindent Proof: If fewer probes are read, the unexamined
positions support $2^{N-q}>2^B$ distinct truth tables.
One has circuit complexity $>s$ and agrees with zero
at every observed bit. The full adaptive transcript and
downstream target question agree, contradicting exactness.
For fixed $\beta<1$ the bound is $N-O(N^\beta\log N)$.
\begin{theorem}[Randomized source access]
For worst-case error at most $1/3$, put $t=2B+2\le N$.
Any adaptive bit-probe decider needs
$q\ge N/(3t)=\Omega(N/(s\log N))$ probes.
\end{theorem}
\noindent Proof: choose a uniform $t$-subset and fill its
bits independently and fairly, zero elsewhere. The
probability of a size-$s$ circuit is at most $2^{B-t}\le1/4$.
Couple randomized execution with zero; transcripts
differ with probability at most $qt/(2N)$.
Correctness forces a rejection-probability difference
at least $1/6$, hence the bound.
