\section{BUILD: proper three-clause modules}
Let a designated clause in module $i$ be $C_i=(a_i\lor b_i\lor c_i)$; its puncture is $a_i$ and its remainder is $d_i=b_i\lor c_i$. We delete $C_i$ temporarily and define the exact two-coordinate profile of the remaining module by existential quantification of its private variables. Use the following two standard \emph{proper ternary} gadgets, with fresh variable names:
\[
\operatorname{Pad}(\ell,r;w)=(\ell\lor r\lor w)\land(\ell\lor r\lor\neg w),\qquad
\operatorname{Pin0}(v;u,z)=\bigwedge_{\epsilon,\delta\in\{-1,1\}}(\neg v\lor\epsilon u\lor\delta z).
\]
The first existentially equals $\ell\lor r$; the second enforces $v=0$. Let $A_E$ contain $\operatorname{Pad}(\neg a,b;w_1)$, $\operatorname{Pad}(a,\neg b;w_2)$ and $\operatorname{Pin0}(c;u,z)$. Let $A_X$ instead contain $\operatorname{Pad}(a,b;w_1)$, $\operatorname{Pad}(\neg a,\neg b;w_2)$ and the same $\operatorname{Pin0}(c;u,z)$. Both have seven variables and eight clauses before $C_i$. Their exact profiles are
\[
\boxed{P_E=\{(0,0),(1,1)\},\qquad P_X=\{(0,1),(1,0)\}.}\tag{1}
\]
Both one-coordinate projections are $\{0,1\}$, both have cardinality two, and both have identical per-variable positive/negative occurrence counts and identical unsigned clause scopes. Let the terminal $B$ module contain three independent $\operatorname{Pin0}$ gadgets, pinning $a_0,b_0,c_0$ to zero. Its puncture profile is $P_B=\{(0,0)\}$.

Order $B,A_1,\ldots,A_k$, use disjoint variable names and perform a \emph{cyclic same-sign exchange} of the designated positive $a_i$ occurrences. The new clauses are
\[
 C_1'=(a_0\lor b_1\lor c_1),\qquad
 C_i'=(a_{i-1}\lor b_i\lor c_i)\quad(2\le i\le k),\qquad
 C_0'=(a_k\lor b_0\lor c_0).
\]
Denote the resulting formula by $F_k(e_1,\ldots,e_k)$, where each $e_i\in\{E,X\}$. Put $U_k=F_k(X,\ldots,X)$ and let $S_{k,j}$ be $F_k$ with exactly the $j$th module changed to $E$.

\begin{theorem}[Source-matched opposite labels; PROVED/EXACT restricted]\label{th:main}
For every $k\ge1$ and $1\le j\le k$, $U_k$ and $S_{k,j}$ are connected proper 3CNFs with $n=7k+9$ variables and $m=9k+13$ clauses. Every variable occurs in at most five clauses. Their unsigned clause-scope multisets and their signed incidence vector $\bigl(\#v,\#\neg v\bigr)_{v=1}^n$ coincide. They have identical independent one-coordinate puncture marginal supports for every corresponding module. Nonetheless
\[
\boxed{U_k\in\UNSAT,\quad S_{k,j}\in\SAT}.\tag{2}
\]
In the fixed-record signed-literal encoding, the two sources differ in exactly \emph{four} bits of equal physical length. These are not strict signed exact-$(2,2)$ sources.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each $A$ gadget uses three data variables, two distinct binary-padding names, and two pinning names, hence seven variables; it contributes $4+4+1=9$ clauses after the exchanged clause is counted. Module $B$ uses three data variables, six pinning names, and $12+1=13$ clauses. Every $a_i,b_i,c_i$ occurs in at most four gadget clauses and in exactly one exchanged clause. Each fresh padding or pin variable occurs at most four times. Every clause has three distinct names. The ring of exchanged clauses joins consecutive modules, so their complete incidence graph is connected. Flipping $X\to E$ changes the signs of $a_j$ in exactly four padded literal positions, while $a_j$ retains two positive and two negative gadget occurrences. No unsigned scope changes and all other occurrence vectors remain fixed.

For an $X$ module, $c_i=0$ and $b_i=1-a_i$; for $E$, $c_i=0$ and $b_i=a_i$. The terminal has $a_0=b_0=c_0=0$. Thus $C_1'$ forces $b_1=1$, and $C_0'$ forces $a_k=1$. If all modules are $X$, $b_1=1$ forces $a_1=0$; inductively $C_i'$ forces $a_i=0$, contradicting $a_k=1$. If exactly module $j$ is $E$, choose $a_i=0$ for $i<j$ and $a_i=1$ for $i\ge j$, with $b_i=1-a_i$ except $b_j=a_j=1$, and set all $c_i=0$. Every exchanged clause is then true. Auxiliary assignments exist by the gadget equivalences. This proves both labels without a SAT oracle. The four differing polarity bits are the low bits of the four independently encoded literal words, hence the physical Hamming distance equals four.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact resource firewall for one deterministic decision program}
Let $\mathcal P_k=\{U_k,S_{k,1},\ldots,S_{k,k}\}$ be a \emph{promise of fully authentic fixed-record source strings}, with $k$ known. A deterministic indexed \emph{bit-probe} decision procedure sees only queried source bits and must distinguish the one NO input from every YES input. For each $j$, the set $D_j$ of four physical positions where $U_k$ and $S_{k,j}$ differ is disjoint from $D_h$ for $h\ne j$.
\begin{theorem}[Two answer-side indexed probe costs]
For the same fixed correct deterministic promise program,
\[
\boxed{Q_-(k)=Q_+(k)=k}\tag{3}
\]
for the optimal achievable worst-case number of indexed bit queries over the respective UNSAT and SAT inputs. The lower bound holds for \emph{any} deterministic exact bit-query tree; an explicit address-computing test of one polarity bit from each module meets it.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Run any correct decision tree on $U_k$. If it never reads a bit in $D_j$, the entire transcript is identical on $S_{k,j}$, contradicting correctness. Hence it probes at least one bit from each disjoint $D_j$, costing at least $k$ on $U_k$. Choose a module whose first distinguishing probe occurs last among the $k$ modules along this no-instance transcript. Its $S_{k,j}$ transcript coincides with the no-instance transcript through the previous $k-1$ distinguishing probes, and through the $k$th distinguishing probe; it costs at least $k$. Conversely each module has one fixed sign flag that is $X$ or $E$. Probe that flag in order, return YES at the first $E$, and NO after all $k$ are examined. The worst-case costs on both answer sides equal $k$. The algorithm does not receive an answer-class bit.\end{proof}
This is a \emph{source-acquisition lower bound within the certified promise}, not a post-acquisition processing bound and not an FLMV lower bound for unrestricted SAT. The physically encoded input length is
\[
 w=\left\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\right\rceil,\quad N=(4m+2)w=\Theta(k\log(k+2)),
\]
so $Q_+=Q_-=\Theta(N/\log N)$ in this indexed-bit-probe promise. The class is easy: the program itself can recognize its syntactic mode, and a generic SAT decider has unrestricted alternative strategies.

\section{Constructive positive control, output closure and three machine ledgers}
Let the closed restricted encoded class consist of the exact $U_k$, the $S_{k,j}$ for all $k,j$, and two fixed proper-3CNF base strings: one positive ternary and all eight signed ternaries on three variables. A deterministic parser first authenticates the header, field width, every literal and the complete promised clause sequence. It checks the all-$X$ candidate and the $k$ one-$E$ candidates. Failed matches, source reads, address arithmetic, sign switches, and final output serialization are charged. On a recognized source, return a \emph{single} 54-bit YES or 340-bit NO child, respectively. The base strings are directly decided. For $k<4$, decide at the bounded source size directly; for $k\ge4$, $N_{\rm child}\le340\le N^{3/4}$ holds. The output remains in the closed class. This is a constructive contraction only because the restricted promise is already decidable.

\paragraph{Actual Python implementation.} The reproducer stores and compares entire clause arrays; its peak storage is $O(N)$ bits up to representation overhead, and the reported counts are Python-level controls, not measured native tape transitions. A separate conservative \emph{streaming} implementation generates each candidate record on demand and uses $O(\log N)$ work bits with $O((k+1)N\operatorname{polylog}N)\subseteq O(N^2\operatorname{polylog}N)$ bit work. On a deterministic fixed-$k$ multitape machine the source is read-only and work-head motion/repositioning and all failed candidate scans are charged in that bound. A deterministic one-read/write-tape simulation, with source in the footprint, satisfies the deliberately conservative ledger
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_{\rm one}&=O\bigl(T_{\rm multi}(N+S_{\rm multi}+\log(T_{\rm multi}+2))\bigr)\\
&=O(N^3\operatorname{polylog}N),\\
P_{\rm one}&=O(N+S_{\rm multi}+\log(T_{\rm multi}+2))=O(N).
\end{aligned}
\]
This simulation accounts for input and work-head movement and constant-size virtual output; it is not the Hennie--Stearns fast two-tape theorem. Head crossings and symbol changes are not claimed as experimentally measured. The fixed universal-machine interpreter uses a constant program and a charged polynomial simulation, with no unproved numerical exponent or uncharged oracle access. Circuit gate count is not inferred from $3m$ literal wire occurrences, and no witness-verifier construction is called a full decision circuit.
