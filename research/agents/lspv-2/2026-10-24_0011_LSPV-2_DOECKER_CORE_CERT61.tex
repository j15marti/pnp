\section{Exact 3-CNF input and reverse-selector semantics}
The signed source has $n=198$ original variables and $m=264$ proper unique monotone clauses, with exactly two positive and two negative occurrences of each source. Its SHA256 compact-JSON digest is
{\small\begin{center}\texttt{4ed9031a7a1b1fa9fcb752f9feece1ea7201df6b610059b90793517ecd874001}.\end{center}}
The fixed four-word clause-record encoding, $w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil=15$ bits, has $N=(4m+2)w=15,870$ bits. The complement-occurrence graph contains $4n=792$ vertices and $4n=792$ conflict edges in 198 disjoint $K_{2,2}$; adding the clause triangles yields 1,584 edges, degree four at each vertex. For any proper 3-CNF, output TRUE from the AND of clause OR gates iff there is a selection of one true literal \emph{occurrence} per clause with no source variable selected in opposite signs. One selected literal is a witness, not an exactly-one-TRUE constraint on the source. This is a standard exact SAT equivalence. With OR2 and AND2 fan-in-two assignment-evaluation gates, counts are $3m-1$ gates and $6m-2$ directed input references; adding shared source NOT1 gates adds $n$ to both counts. Neither bound applies to arbitrary circuits deciding SAT encodings.

\section{Certified six-module projection}
Let $Y=\{193,194,195,196,197,198\}$ and $U_i=\{32i+1,\ldots,32i+32\}$ for $0\le i<6$. The 264 original clauses partition into six mutually local 42-clause $G_i(U_i,Y)$, two all-global clauses at source indices 0 and 1, and ten cross-module balancing clauses at indices 254 through 263. Dropping the ten cross-module clauses is permitted to prove UNSAT, not to preserve all assignments. The exact existential projections of the six $G_i$ are:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
C_1=(\neg y_{194}\vee y_{197}),&
C_2=(\neg y_{195}\vee y_{196}\vee\neg y_{198}),\\
C_3=(y_{193}\vee\neg y_{195}\vee\neg y_{197}),&
C_4=(y_{195}\vee\neg y_{197}),\\
C_5=(y_{193}\vee y_{194}\vee\neg y_{196}),&
C_6=(\neg y_{193}\vee y_{198}).
\end{array}
\]
The two original global clauses are
\[
C_7=(\neg y_{193}\vee\neg y_{196}\vee\neg y_{198}),\qquad
C_8=(y_{194}\vee y_{196}\vee y_{197}).
\]
All $U_i$ are disjoint; every occurrence of a global $Y$ source retains the same identifier. Consequently, MAIN64's exact join applies:
\[
\exists U_0\cdots\exists U_5\ \left[\bigwedge_iG_i(U_i,Y)\wedge C_7\wedge C_8\right]
\equiv \bigwedge_{j=1}^{8} C_j(Y).
\]
\begin{theorem}[Explicit finite source certificate; PROVED/EXACT within supplied source]
The 254-clause subformula, and hence the 264-clause published input, is UNSAT. The eight-clause global quotient is deletion-minimal UNSAT.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each $G_i$, the machine-readable certificate contains one complete binary-split/unit-propagation refutation tree at the one forbidden boundary assignment and explicit satisfying local source assignments at every permitted boundary row. The independent Python verifier checks original source clause indices at every conflict leaf, both branches of every split, all 30 local satisfying witnesses, and every source identifier. This certifies $\exists U_i G_i\equiv C_i$. Checking all 64 assignments to $Y$ rejects the eight-clause conjunction. After removal of individual clauses $C_1,\ldots,C_8$, its numbers of accepted assignments are respectively $8,2,4,8,4,6,4,4$, proving deletion-minimality. The omitted ten original clauses can only preserve UNSAT.
\end{proof}

\section{Matched exact-language SAT control}
\begin{theorem}[Source-level signed-positive transposition; PROVED/EXACT, explicit instance]
Exchange literal $197$ in original zero-based clause 2, $(1,2,197)$, with literal $196$ in clause 44, $(33,34,196)$. The new clauses are $(1,2,196)$ and $(33,34,197)$. The modified source is SAT while preserving 198 variables, 264 unique proper monotone clauses, two positive/two negative appearances per variable, and the exact 15,870-bit input length.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The exchange preserves both positive occurrence counts, changes no negative occurrence or clause type, and creates no repeated clause. The explicit 198-bit assignment supplied with the research package is replayed by an independent verifier against all 264 modified signed source clauses. No MILP/SAT solver is trusted for this check.
\end{proof}
Both source labels have zero complementary-clause pivot pairs, $\delta=264$, and zero identical positive/negative support pairs, $t=264$. Large syntactic $t$ or $\delta$ therefore does not force a solver to run for time $2^t$ or $2^\delta$ on every input. The polynomial-source-decider constructed by recognizing this fixed template is only a restricted-instance shortcut, not an all-input polynomial SAT algorithm.

\section{Charged ledger and independently reproducible controls}
The independent checker, using the actual 264 signed source clauses and the 12,067-byte certificate, checks 834 proof nodes (414 binary splits and 420 source-labelled conflicts), 1,884 unit assignments, 30 positive boundary witnesses, 64 core assignments, and full SAT-control source replay. Recorded charges include 58,674 source-clause scans and 176,022 source-literal reads during proof replay; 3,780 literal reads for positive local witnesses; 512 eight-clause core row evaluations; 264 source-clause and 792 literal reads in SAT-control replay. Additional source parsing requires 1,058 input record words, including 792 literal fields. No auxiliary SAT sources or uncharged equality between occurrences are introduced. There is no DAG reuse in the supplied refutation trees. Verification is polynomial in explicit source and certificate size, but general acquisition of an equivalent small separator/interface for arbitrary SAT inputs is \textbf{OPEN} and not given for free. The independent verifier passes in about 0.032 seconds on the local machine. A separate scipy/HiGHS MILP solve independently classified the full original as infeasible and the matched modified source as feasible, but the proof does not depend on that solver.
