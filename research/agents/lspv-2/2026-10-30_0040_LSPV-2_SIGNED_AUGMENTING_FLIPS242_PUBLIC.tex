\section{Primary exact clause-indexed experiment}

The local all-literals-true target $S_j$ demands $x_i=1\oplus\nu_{ij}$ at all three occurrences. Its full inversion gives $U_j$, the all-literals-false target, which demands $x_i=\nu_{ij}$. \section{A constructive witness without signed-dual balance}
\begin{theorem}
Let $D_1,\dots,D_r$ be proper signed clauses of width at least three, and suppose every ORIGINAL Boolean variable occurs at most twice across them. Beginning at the coherent global assignment $0^n$, a deterministic algorithm constructs $a$ satisfying
\[
1\le t_p(a)\le |D_p|-1\quad (p=1,\dots,r)
\]
after at most $r^2$ single-original-variable flips. With indexed adjacency and an explicit source encoding $N$, the algorithm uses $O(N+r^2)$ word-RAM operations and $O(N+r\log N)$ working bits if its source-indexed trace is streamed. Keeping the complete trace in memory requires up to $O(r^2\log N)$ further bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Call $D_p$ bad if all its literal truth values agree, good otherwise. Maintain its true-literal count. From a bad clause, flip any variable not equal to the incoming variable on the current augmenting path (there are at least two choices because width is at least three). This makes the bad clause good. Because the source variable occurs at most twice, at most one other clause $D_q$ changes. If $q$ is absent, was already bad, or remains good, the total number of bad clauses decreases. Otherwise $q$ was good and became bad; transfer a single bad token to $q$, record the incoming edge, and continue without immediately backtracking.
When a token leaves a bad clause, the departure flip makes it good with exactly one literal of the minority truth value. Its only original variable whose immediate flip could make it bad again is that departure variable. Before the first repeated clause along this token path, no already visited clause has changed again. If a path first revisited some clause $q$ and made it bad, its incoming variable would have to equal the old departure variable of $q$. But that variable leads to the earlier successor of $q$, which would then have been repeated first (or would constitute the excluded immediate backtrack). Thus a first revisit cannot transfer badness; it decreases the number of bad clauses. Hence an augmentation succeeds in at most $r$ flips. The bad count starts at most $r$ and decreases by at least one per augmentation, giving at most $r^2$ flips. Counts and adjacency updates cost constant word operations per flip. A full bad-clause scan per augmentation costs $O(r^2)$ total. All flips act on ORIGINAL variables and can be streamed with their source clause identifiers. Signs never enter the correctness argument.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}
Every proper strict signed exact-$(2+,2-)$ 3CNF that admits an exact pairing of its ORIGINAL clauses as $\bigwedge_p(D_p\land\bar D_p)$ is satisfiable by a deterministic polynomial-time source-preserving construction, regardless of the signed dual cycle parity.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every paired occurrence contributes one positive and one negative original occurrence. Strict signed degree four therefore means each variable occurs precisely twice among the $D_p$. Each pair $(D_p,\bar D_p)$ is satisfied exactly when $D_p$ has one or two true literals. Use the theorem. Full syntactic pairing, quota validation and source-index reconstruction are polynomial-time computable by deterministic sorting of normalized clause signatures, and failure returns UNKNOWN, not UNSAT.
\end{proof}
\section{An exact odd-cycle hostile control}
Take original first source clauses
\[
D_1=(x_1\vee x_2\vee x_3),\quad
D_2=(\neg x_1\vee x_4\vee x_5),\quad
D_3=(x_2\vee x_4\vee x_6),\quad
D_4=(x_3\vee x_5\vee x_6)
\]
and append their four all-sign-complements as original clauses 5--8. This connected six-variable eight-clause formula is strict signed exact-$(2,2)$, and $D_1,D_2,D_3$ have a signed-odd-cycle inconsistency if all three are required to be S or U extremes. Global coherent $0^6$ and $1^6$ both have $N_0=N_3=3$, yet the full original assignment $(1,0,1,1,0,0)$ has per-source-clause true-literal counts $(2,1,1,1,1,2,2,2)$ and is SAT. Augmenting source-variable flips $1,4,3$ reduce the base bad-clause count $3\to1\to0$. Original source clauses 1 and 2 resolve on $x_1$ to $(x_2\vee x_3\vee x_4\vee x_5)$; falsifying all four literals provably forbids that entire partial assignment cube.
The width-two NAE odd cycle NAE$(x_1,x_2)$, NAE$(x_2,x_3)$, NAE$(x_3,x_1)$ is UNSAT even though variables occur twice, proving width at least three is necessary. The separate 15-variable 20-clause connected strict22 W1 source is independently UNSAT and is not complement-paired, proving the pairing grammar is a genuine restriction.
