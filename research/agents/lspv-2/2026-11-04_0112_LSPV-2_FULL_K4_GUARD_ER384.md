## Cost and literature firewall

The **128** count is a *logical upper certificate*, not physical runtime. The producer materializes a **16,191-byte** proof and must pay for input parsing, definition construction, source/index access, resolution candidate generation, parent reference and hashing, serialization, verification and all-phase simultaneous storage. Proof optimizer discovery cost is separate from minimum proof cost. The small finite result supplies neither an empirical bound on growing expanders nor a universal time-space domination inequality.

**Published theorem anchor:** Urquhart's 1987 Tseitin lower bound applies to **every unrestricted ordinary shared-DAG Resolution refutation** of a suitable infinite bounded-degree expander family with odd charges, giving `2^Omega(h)` Resolution lines measured in vertices `h`. A degree-three signed CNF has `4h` width-three clauses and `Theta(h log h)` binary-indexed DIMACS input bits. That theorem does **not** prove a lower bound for this stronger fixed calculus, Res(XOR), Extended Resolution, or arbitrary correct uniform SAT algorithms. Gaussian elimination is a polynomial affine shortcut. Haken's 1985 pigeonhole lower bound and Cook's 1976 polynomial Extended Resolution proof are cross-system countercontrols [1–3].
