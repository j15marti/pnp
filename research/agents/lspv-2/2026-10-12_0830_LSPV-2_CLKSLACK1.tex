\section{Claim card and exact target}

Let $\mathcal H^{\pm}_{2,2}$ be the duplicate-free proper signed Monotone
$3$SAT-$(2,2)$ family used by the current corpus: every clause has three
distinct literals of one sign and every variable occurs twice positively
and twice negatively.  D\"ocker proves NP-completeness of this family
\cite{Docker20}.  The input measure is always the canonical encoded bit
length $N=|\operatorname{enc}(F)|$.  For
$F=C_1\wedge\cdots\wedge C_m$, define the semantic compatibility matrix
\[
 H(F)_{a,j}=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a\models C_j .
\]
The one-bit decision asks whether $H(F)$ has an all-one row.  No decider is
therefore required merely by its specification to expose $H(F)$, an
assignment, proof, trace, table, or canonical representation.

The separation-strength target remains
\[
 \forall A\in\operatorname{Dec}_{\rm unif}(\mathcal H^{\pm}_{2,2})\;
 \forall d\;\forall N_0\;\exists F\in\mathcal H^{\pm}_{2,2}:\quad
 |\operatorname{enc}(F)|\ge N_0\ \land\
 T_A(\operatorname{enc}F)>|\operatorname{enc}F|^d . \tag{UCORE-TIME1}
\]
It is \OPEN.  The candidate attacked here is narrower: a strict power bound
relative to a supplied valid source clock is, by itself, a quantitatively
meaningful instance of TDLG1's \textsf{CrossRegime} law.

\section{Primary-literature and obstacle preflight}

The two current obstacles are (i) finding a clock-description-invariant law
with genuine content on an unconditionally known exact SAT implementation,
and (ii) specializing that law to one fully charged ordinary realization in
a model-matched forbidden time--space region.  Blum's axiomatic complexity
theory makes the complexity measure and its program index explicit
\cite{Blum67}; it does not identify loose upper-bound annotations with
realized running time.  FLMV gives a simultaneous SAT time--space lower bound
only after one ordinary random-access realization meets its precise bounds
\cite{FLMV05}.  Baker--Gill--Solovay and Aaronson--Wigderson rule out treating
a relativizing or merely algebrizing ledger as the missing P-versus-NP hinge
\cite{BGS75,AW09}.

The exact transferable content is limited: valid upper bounds are closed
under widening, and the FLMV endpoint applies only to its own machine model.
VLSI clock slack, amortized potentials, observability, communication cuts,
pebbling, proof search, and coding-theory distinguishers are useful
structural analogies but provide no arbitrary-SAT transfer theorem.  In
particular, a hardware clock-frequency analogy is a \textbf{misleading
analogy / failed transfer}: the supplied complexity clock here is a
mathematical upper bound, not a physical oscillator.

\section{Clocked domain and exact theorem}

Let $z=(A,t,s)\in\ExactSAT$ mean that $A$ is a total uniform exact SAT
decider and $t,s:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ are total valid time and space
clocks.  Thus, for every input $x$ of length $n$,
$T_A(x)\le t(n)$ and $S_A(x)\le s(n)$.  A clock annotation is descriptive;
it need not be tight and a one-bit decider need not validate it during the
decision computation.

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{CLOCK-SLACK-CONTRACTION-GAUGE1}]
For every $z=(A,t,s)\in\ExactSAT$, every rational
$\alpha\in(0,1)$, and every integer $q\ge\lceil 1/\alpha\rceil$, define
\[
 t_q(n)=\max\{2,t(n)\}^{q}. \tag{1}
\]
Then $z_q=(A,t_q,s)\in\ExactSAT$ is a valid clocked description of the
identical implementation, and the identity transformation
$\mathcal I(z_q)=A$ satisfies
\[
 \forall x\qquad
 T_{\mathcal I(z_q)}(x)\le t_q(|x|)^{\alpha}. \tag{2}
\]
The input/output Boolean function, machine transitions, actual time, actual
space, and all exposed semantic objects are unchanged.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $n=|x|$ and $u=\max\{2,t(n)\}$.  Since $t$ is valid,
$T_A(x)\le t(n)\le u$.  Since $q\alpha\ge1$ and $u\ge2$,
\[
 T_A(x)\le u\le u^{q\alpha}=(u^q)^\alpha=t_q(n)^\alpha.
\]
Moreover $t_q(n)\ge t(n)$, so it is a valid clock for $A$.  The
transformation returns the same program $A$ and changes only the supplied
upper-bound description.  Hence every realized resource and semantic output
is unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Existential favorable-clock laws are vacuous]
Fix any rational $\alpha\in(0,1)$.  The statement
\[
 \forall A\ \forall t\text{ valid for }A\ \exists\widehat t\ge t:\quad
 T_A\le \widehat t^{\alpha} \tag{3}
\]
holds for every total computation, not only SAT, with the identity output.
Consequently (3) cannot be the missing solver-independent obstruction.
\end{corollary}

The theorem does \emph{not} refute a law quantified over every valid clock,
a law stated as an absolute charged bound in $N$, or a theorem tied to an
independently proved tight reference cost.  It proves that choosing a
favorable valid annotation and reporting a smaller power of that annotation
has no runtime content.

\section{Barrier, uniformity, and post-quantum audit}

The theorem is algebra plus closure of upper bounds and therefore
relativizes and algebrizes.  It supplies no barrier escape.  Natural Proofs
is not triggered because no constructive large circuit property is proposed.
The integer $q$ depends only on fixed $\alpha$, so the clock wrapper is
uniform; no input-dependent advice or nonuniform family is used.

Schur squares, shortening/puncturing, quadratic-relation ranks, Hilbert
functions, syzygies and Betti numbers are \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER}: the
clock fiber is not a code and no such object is forced by a one-bit decider.
Collision entropy of the many clock descriptions is an
\textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE needing a new bridge}; a large annotation fiber
measures representational redundancy, not computation.  LWE-style external
black-box extraction is likewise an \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}: it would
need a worst-case, all-solver, fully charged transfer absent here.
Special-soundness/collision extraction and collapse binding are
\textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} without a construction yielding two valid openings.
Hash and random-oracle methods are inadmissible as unconditional proof
premises.  Hidden affine or algebraic structure remains potentially
algorithmically exploitable rather than a hardness source.
