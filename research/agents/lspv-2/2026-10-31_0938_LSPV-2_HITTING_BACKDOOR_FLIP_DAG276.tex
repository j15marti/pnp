\section{Exact theorem with source-indexed signed clauses}
Let $F=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{m}C_j$ be an ordered ordinary CNF over original variables $V$. Source occurrence $x_{i,j}$ denotes the signed occurrence of original $x_i$ in original $C_j$, not a new variable. Choose a tested set $T\subseteq V$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:backdoor}
\forall j,\quad |\operatorname{vars}(C_j)\setminus T|\le 2.
\end{equation}
Set $R=V\setminus T$. A controller must either discover such $T$, paying for the search, or fail the policy and retain a complete fallback. For any $b\in\{0,1\}^{T}$, let $F_b=F\upharpoonright (T=b)$ be the ordinary restricted CNF over $R$. Condition (\ref{eq:backdoor}) certifies that $F_b$ is 2-CNF, including unit and empty clauses. The condition is a syntactic strong 2-SAT backdoor, not a semantic hardness or optimality measure.

\begin{theorem}[Flip-cube resolution lift and complete both-label join]\label{th:lift}
For every such $F,T$ and every $b$, the following can be computed exactly using the original ordered source clauses.
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{YES profile:} If $F_b$ is SAT, a 2-SAT witness on $R$ joined with $b$ is a complete source assignment satisfying every original clause.
\item[] \textbf{NO profile:} If $F_b$ is UNSAT, a finite ordinary-resolution DAG whose leaves are \emph{original full source clauses} derives a non-tautological clause $D_b$ on $T$ alone, falsified by $b$, and $F\models D_b$. Thus it rejects the entire coherent flip subcube $T=b$ without expanding any of its $2^{|R|}$ complete-assignment leaves.
\item[] \textbf{Complete join:} If every one of the $2^{|T|}$ profiles is NO, the DAGs for $D_b$ extend by ordinary resolution on $T$ to a full original-source refutation ending in the empty clause. Otherwise an actual YES profile reconstructs a complete witness. Hence the policy preserves both labels for the same source.
\end{description}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $b$. Each original clause containing a true literal on $T$ disappears. Every remaining clause $C_j$ has all its $T$ literals false under $b$, so its restriction $c_j$ has at most two $R$ literals. A source-indexed restricted 2-SAT decision is therefore exact. On the YES side, replacing $c_j$ by the found $R$ assignment satisfies every surviving original clause, and the disappearing clauses were already satisfied by $b$.

For the NO side, apply complete Davis--Putnam resolution elimination of all $R$ variables to the restricted 2-CNF. Every resolvent remains width at most two \emph{in $R$}. The final zero-variable clause set must contain the empty clause, because existential quantification preserves the NO label. Build an ordinary source DAG in parallel. Its initial nodes are the complete unmodified source clauses $C_j$ associated to surviving restricted clauses $c_j$. For a restricted resolution of $c_p$ and $c_q$ on $r\in R$, resolve their \emph{full} parent clauses on the same $r$, retaining all outside literals. The resulting full clause restricts under $b$ to the exact restricted resolvent. Each outside literal in a live node is false under the fixed $b$, so two such outside literals cannot be complementary. All restricted tautological resolvents are discarded, and every retained resolution step is legitimate ordinary non-tautological resolution on original source descendants. At the restricted empty-clause node, the corresponding full clause $D_b$ contains only $T$ literals, all false under $b$. As each DAG node follows by sound resolution from original source leaves, $F\models D_b$.

Suppose all profiles are NO. For each $b$ there is a derived $D_b$ false under $b$. The CNF $\bigwedge_b D_b$ excludes every assignment to $T$, so it is UNSAT; resolving away its remaining $T$ variables yields the empty clause. Appending these ordinary-resolution steps to the original-source DAGs gives the required refutation. No unsuccessful bounded repair is used as an UNSAT rule.\qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact finite-cost parameterization.} For $k=|T|$, $n=|V|$ and $m$ original clauses, exhaustive discovery of a size-\(\le k\) syntactic backdoor by subsets may inspect $\sum_{i=0}^{k}\binom ni$ candidates, each costing at least $\Omega(\sum_j |C_j|)$ occurrence probes under a direct scanner. Once a $T$ is certified, up to $2^k$ residual 2-SAT solves are required. Deduplicated resolution elimination of 2-CNF residuals retains only $O(|R|^2)$ distinct residual clauses at a stage, though source DAGs can accumulate wider guard literals and require polynomially many source-provenance records. This is an FPT-style \emph{conditional on discovery and representation} bound, not a uniform subexponential or FLMV result. Actual source tape read positions, memory allocation, parent/child retention, all head moves, and guest query interleaving require additional machine-model audit.

\section{Small source-original example and a source proof}
Let $P(x;p,q)=\bigwedge_{u\in\{p,\neg p\}}\bigwedge_{v\in\{q,\neg q\}}(x\vee u\vee v)$. It has four proper signed 3-clauses and is equivalent to $x$ after private existential quantification. Choosing $T=\{x\}$, $R=\{p,q\}$, $b(x)=0$ leaves the four binary clauses $(p\vee q),(p\vee\neg q),(\neg p\vee q),(\neg p\vee\neg q)$, which are UNSAT. Resolution over $q$ yields $(x\vee p)$ and $(x\vee\neg p)$, and resolution over $p$ derives $(x)$ from \emph{the original full clauses}. Thus the entire $x=0$ flip subcube is forbidden by one learned original-source implication. Nevertheless the whole parent is SAT at $x=1$; endpoint failure of $x=0$ is not a global refutation.

The matched NO source $P(x;p,q)\wedge P(\neg x;r,s)$ produces $(x)$ from the $x=0$ subcube and $(\neg x)$ from the $x=1$ subcube, then resolves them to the empty clause. The resulting full DAG is an ordinary-resolution refutation with original indexed leaves. These are exact witnesses to both the YES and NO sides, not a classification inferred from local clause extremes.
