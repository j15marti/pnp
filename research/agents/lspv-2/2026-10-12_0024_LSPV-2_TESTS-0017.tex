\section*{Executive result}

\status{PROVED/EXACT} (independent review pending): on the paired-$K_{3,3}$ clean-core product family, ordinary satisfying assignments give exponentially many mutually distinct valid openings for one fixed formula/address.  Hence the generic inference
\[
  \operatorname{Open}(F,a,y)\wedge \operatorname{Open}(F,a,y')\wedge y\ne y'
  \Longrightarrow \text{a forbidden witness}
\]
is false when $\operatorname{Open}$ means only ``is a satisfying assignment.''  A construction-specific transcript relation may still be binding; that is a different and currently \status{OPEN} theorem.  This result concerns semantic multiplicity, not an algorithmic time, space, or communication lower bound.  P versus NP remains open.

\section*{Claim card and exact quantifiers}

Let $B$ be the twelve-clause paired-$K_{3,3}$ block on variables
$x_1,x_2,x_3,y_1,y_2,y_3,e_{12},e_{23},e_{31}$ used in the verified lower-bound corpus.  Direct enumeration gives
$|\operatorname{SAT}(B)|=102$.  For $k\geq1$, let
\[
 F_k=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{k} B_i,
 \qquad W_k=\{w:w\models F_k\},
 \qquad L_k(w)=\star,
\]
where the $B_i$ have disjoint variables and $\star$ is one fixed child/acceptance address.  Define the ordinary opening relation
\[
  O_k(F_k,\star,w)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w\in W_k.
\]
The attacked universal premise is
\[
  \forall k\ \forall w\ne w'\in W_k:\quad
  O_k(F_k,\star,w)\wedge O_k(F_k,\star,w')
  \Longrightarrow \bot.
\]
It is refuted for every $k\geq1$: every distinct $w,w'\in W_k$ is a pair of valid openings of the same source and address.  The encoded input length in the corpus encoding is $N_k=\Theta(k\log k)$, while
\[
  |W_k|=102^k=2^{\Theta(N_k/\log N_k)}.
\]
This equation is a witness-count statement only.

\section*{Exact collision theorem}

\paragraph{Theorem.}
For every $k\geq1$,
\[
 |W_k|=102^k,
 \qquad |\operatorname{im}L_k|=1,
 \qquad \operatorname{Coll}(L_k)
 =|\{(w,w'):L_k(w)=L_k(w')\}|=102^{2k}.
\]
The collision Cauchy--Schwarz inequality is tight:
\[
 |\operatorname{im}L_k|
 \geq \frac{|W_k|^2}{\operatorname{Coll}(L_k)}=1.
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
The blocks are variable-disjoint, so restriction to blocks is a bijection
$W_k\cong\operatorname{SAT}(B)^k$ and $|W_k|=102^k$.
The map $L_k$ is constant, hence its image has size one and every ordered
pair in $W_k^2$ collides.  Substitution gives equality in the displayed
Cauchy--Schwarz bound. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Consequence.}
The large collision set is not itself a contradiction: it consists of valid
SAT witnesses.  Therefore \status{ORDINARY-WITNESS-BINDING1} is
\status{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}.  The narrower
\status{SEMANTIC-OPENING-BIND1} target remains \status{OPEN}: it must define
additional, construction-specific opening semantics under which two
conflicting valid openings yield an explicit forbidden algebraic object, and
then prove that every arbitrary SAT decider must realize or pay for that same
relation.

\section*{Canonicalization and the $P=NP$ audit}

A total polynomial-time machine that outputs the lexicographically least
satisfying assignment or $\bot$ decides SAT by testing whether its output is
$\bot$.  Conversely, if SAT is in P, standard variable-by-variable
self-reduction produces that canonical assignment in polynomial time.
Thus demanding efficient canonical witness generation is equivalent to the
missing separation rather than an independently established obstruction.
Valiant--Vazirani isolation supplies randomized uniqueness under randomized
reductions; it does not provide deterministic, pointwise binding at the same
source/address.  Under the assumption SAT$\in$P, the recognize-first wrapper
plus the polynomial fallback remains polynomial and yields no model-matched
unconditional contradiction.

\section*{Primary-literature and barrier preflight}

The two major obstacles are (i) transferring a representation-level opening
relation to every uniform deterministic SAT decider and (ii) obtaining a
nonrelativizing, nonalgebrizing lower bound for that same machine model.
The preflight checked the original Valiant--Vazirani isolation paper, the
Baker--Gill--Solovay relativization barrier, Aaronson--Wigderson algebraization,
Razborov--Rudich Natural Proofs, the Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas
time--space framework, and primary bounded-occurrence monotone 3SAT literature.
No cited theorem supplies the missing arbitrary-decider transfer.

The present collision counterexample relativizes: adding any oracle does not
alter the truth that two ordinary satisfying assignments can open the same
formula/address.  It also algebraizes in the relevant weak sense; no
arithmetization step prevents the collision.  It is not a Natural-Proofs
property against circuit size.  FLMV-style time--space lower bounds require a
fully matched computational model and realization; witness counts alone do
not instantiate their hypotheses.

\section*{SAME\_REALIZATION audit}

The objects $W_k$, $L_k$, and $O_k$ are semantic sets and relations.  They are
not machine configurations, memory cells, messages, or branches.  An arbitrary
uniform deterministic decider may compute only the output bit, use an
alternative encoding, or exploit a direct recognizer.  Therefore no time,
space, memory, communication, or state-exposure lower bound follows without a
new theorem mapping every decider on the same encoded input to the same
opening resource with all extraction costs included.  That arrow remains
\status{OPEN}.

\section*{Precise handoff}

The next load-bearing target is:
\[
\begin{split}
\text{find }&O^\dagger(F,a,t)\text{ and an explicit extractor }E\text{ such that}\\
&O^\dagger(F,a,t)\wedge O^\dagger(F,a,t')\wedge t\not\sim t'
  \Longrightarrow E(F,a,t,t')\in\mathcal{B},\\
&\text{where membership in }\mathcal{B}\text{ is unconditionally impossible, and}\\
&\text{every uniform deterministic SAT decider is proved, on the same encoded}
  \text{ inputs, to realize or pay for }O^\dagger.
\end{split}
\]
The equivalence relation $\sim$, transcript alphabet, address $a$, extraction
cost, and forbidden class $\mathcal B$ must all be explicit.  Ordinary
satisfying assignments cannot fill this role.
