\section{BUILD I: explicit source and controlled balanced mutation}
Let $W$ be the proper strict signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3CNF on $15$ variables with the following twenty source clauses. Each triple denotes a disjunction, a negative integer denotes a negative literal, and positions below are indexed from one:
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{rrrr}
\toprule
$C_1=(-1,3,-14)$ & $C_2=(2,-8,9)$ & $C_3=(10,-13,14)$ & $C_4=(3,7,-13)$\\
$C_5=(-4,-9,-11)$ & $C_6=(5,8,-14)$ & $C_7=(4,-8,-11)$ & $C_8=(1,7,13)$\\
$C_9=(1,-7,10)$ & $C_{10}=(-5,-6,-15)$ & $C_{11}=(-2,-6,12)$ & $C_{12}=(-4,9,-10)$\\
$C_{13}=(-2,5,-12)$ & $C_{14}=(-3,6,-15)$ & $C_{15}=(6,-7,11)$ & $C_{16}=(2,-9,11)$\\
$C_{17}=(4,8,-10)$ & $C_{18}=(-3,12,15)$ & $C_{19}=(-5,-12,15)$ & $C_{20}=(-1,13,14)$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The source $W$ is the previously authenticated $W_1$ minimally UNSAT example; all twenty clauses and every signed occurrence can be checked directly. Define $M$ by simultaneously replacing $C_8=(1,7,13)$ with $(-1,7,13)$ and $C_{20}=(-1,13,14)$ with $(1,13,14)$. Every other clause is untouched. These two opposite sign switches preserve exactly two positive and two negative appearances of variable 1, leave the unsigned incidence scopes unchanged, and make $M$ SAT.

For a block $T\in\{W,M\}$, write $U_T=T\setminus\{C_1\}$, and denote the \emph{truth value} of the negative port literal $-x_1$ by $a\in\{0,1\}$. Replace the negative $x_1$ occurrence in $C_1=(-x_1\lor x_3\lor\neg x_{14})$ by an external negative port literal whose truth value is $b$. The exact module relation is
\[
 R_T(a,b)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \exists x_2,\ldots,x_{15}:\ U_T\land(a=x_1^{\mathrm{neg}})\land(b\lor x_3\lor\neg x_{14}). \tag{1}
\]
Here the symbol $a=x_1^{\mathrm{neg}}$ means $a=\neg x_1$, not the positive $x_1$ bit.

\begin{lemma}[Exact local port profiles with auditable finite certificates]\label{lem:profiles}
Writing the two arguments $(a,b)$ as row and column bits, the exact relation support is
\[
 R_W=\{(0,1)\},\qquad R_M=\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,1)\}=\{(a,b):a\le b\}.\tag{2}
\]
The number of local assignments extending each pair, counted over the $15$ internal variables, is
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr|r}
& (0,0)&(0,1)&(1,0)&(1,1)&\#\{\text{models of }U_T\}\\\hline
W&0&140&0&0&140\\
M&84&140&0&84&224
\end{array}\tag{3}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The negative entries of (3) have independent, exact resolution certificates. Introduce a formal incoming variable $z$ in the first clause $(-z\lor x_3\lor\neg x_{14})$, keep $U_T$, and eliminate $x_2,\ldots,x_{15}$ by two-parent non-tautological resolution with checked subsumption. The publicly supplied reproducer generates 147 derivation lines for $W$, with two final clauses $(x_1)$ and $(\neg x_1\lor\neg z)$, and 112 derivation lines for $M$, with final clause $(x_1\lor\neg z)$. Every line is checked against literal source clause IDs or two earlier source-derived parents by an independent checker, not accepted as an opaque semantic claim. For $W$, one further resolution yields $\neg z$, forcing $a=0,b=1$. For $M$, $(x_1\lor\neg z)$ forbids only $a=1,b=0$. The permitted pairs have explicit 15-bit models checked against all twenty local clauses, with concrete masks in the raw data. An independent complete enumeration of $2^{15}$ assignments per template yields (3); these numerical model counts are \textbf{COMPUTATIONAL} while the binary support statements are certified by resolution plus checked witnesses.\end{proof}

\section{BUILD II: connected global cyclic stress and necessary bridge cycle}
For integer $k\ge2$, create $k$ disjoint named copies of a local source $T_i\in\{W,M\}$, using variables $x_{15i+1},\ldots,x_{15i+15}$ for $i=0,\ldots,k-1$. In the first clause of block $i$, replace just the original occurrence $-x_{15i+1}$ by $-x_{15((i+1)\bmod k)+1}$. Call this single actual source clause $E_i$; the other $19$ clauses remain unchanged. No new variables or clauses are introduced. Let $S_k$ use all $T_i=M$, and let $U_k$ use $T_0=W$ and all $T_i=M$ for $i>0$.

\begin{theorem}[A signed exact22 connected matched SAT/UNSAT cycle]\label{th:cycle}
For every $k\ge2$, the sources $S_k$ and $U_k$ satisfy the following exact assertions.
\begin{description}
\item[] Both are connected proper signed exact-$(2,2)$-3CNFs on $n=15k$ variables and $m=20k$ clauses, with $N=\Theta(k\log(k+1))$ physical bits in canonical DIMACS. They have identical unsigned variable-clause incidence hypergraphs.
\item[] $S_k$ is SAT and $U_k$ is UNSAT. In $U_k$, \emph{deleting any one} of the $k$ named cross-module source clauses $E_0,\dots,E_{k-1}$ yields a SAT formula, with an explicit globally checked assignment.
\item[] Every unsatisfiable subset of $U_k$'s original clauses must contain \emph{all} $k$ crossed clauses. Nevertheless $U_k$ has a completely source-ID-authenticated resolution refutation with exactly $113k+36$ derived lines of maximum width seven, plus the original $20k$ clauses. Hence the bridge-support bound supplies neither a superpolynomial proof-size lower bound nor a decision lower bound.
\end{description}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each block has exactly four incidences per local name, two per polarity. Every selected negative-literal occurrence is moved to the next block in one cyclic permutation. Thus the total signed degree of each name is unchanged. Clause supports are proper and distinct, because each moved literal has a variable name absent from the other two literals in its recipient clause; every module's nineteen original clauses and their cross clause induce a connected incidence graph. The unchanged block skeleton is connected because each cross clause meets its local two names and one port of the next block, forming a ring.

By Lemma~\ref{lem:profiles}, satisfying assignments for $S_k$ correspond exactly to cyclic port assignments with $a_i\le a_{i+1}$ for every $i$ modulo $k$; all zeros work. In $U_k$, the distinguished block requires $a_0=0$ and $a_1=1$. Each other block imposes $a_i\le a_{i+1}$, so $a_1=1$ propagates around the remainder of the ring to $a_0=1$, a contradiction. There is no externally supplied SAT/UNSAT answer bit: the same canonical parser and two-state transfer program computes both.

Delete $E_j$. If $j=0$, set every port to zero, satisfy all $M$ transitions, and choose any $U_W$ model for block zero. If $j>0$, set $a_0=0$, set $a_1=\cdots=a_j=1$, and set all later ports to zero. The sole forbidden $1\to0$ transition occurs across the deleted $E_j$. For each remaining block select a preverified local model for its allowed pair; for the deleted block select a model of $U_{T_j}$ at its required own-port bit. These independent local assignments agree on the port names and satisfy all surviving original clauses. Therefore no clause subset lacking an $E_j$ can be UNSAT.

For a concrete source-derived resolution refutation, generate the fixed W local lines concluding $x_1$ and $(\neg x_1\lor\neg x_{16})$, resolve to get the unit $\neg x_{16}$, and for every $M$ block derive $(x_{15i+1}\lor\neg x_{15((i+1)\bmod k)+1})$. Resolve that unit successively through the $k-1$ remaining M clauses, obtaining $\neg x_1$, and resolve against W's $x_1$. The local W proof uses 147 lines, each local M proof uses 112 lines, and the unit/chain/final operations use $k+1$ lines. Total $147+112(k-1)+k+1=113k+36$. All parents and pivots are explicitly verified against the actual source ID and prior derived clauses in the JSON certificate. The length is $O(k\log k)$ when physically serialized, since all literals and IDs need $O(\log k)$ bits.
\end{proof}

\section{BUILD III: affine relaxation is not the complete signed interface}
The $\FF$-affine hull of $R_M$ is all $\FF^2$, because the differences of $(0,1)$ and $(1,1)$ from $(0,0)$ are independent. Thus its affine-hull codimension is zero, even though its exact binary implication $a\le b$ is essential. The hull of singleton $R_W$ has codimension two. In $U_k$, replace each $R_M$ by its affine hull and keep the W singleton: this relaxed system has a solution $a_0=0,a_1=1$ with the remaining ports arbitrary. The exact joined system is UNSAT. This \emph{specific affine-only summary} is unsound; retaining the literal 2CNF implication would make the case easy. It does not challenge Arvind--Guruswami's complete 2CNF-plus-affine decision algorithm or the LSPV-5 path-composition theorem.

The cycle has a constant one-bit shared port at each module boundary and one independent cycle in the module-interface graph. Static syntactic clause-hitting backdoors are a distinct parameter; the earlier connected-interface capacity inequality $n\le3b+12bq$ remains valid under its fixed-boundary assumptions and does not preclude these moving relations. The exact input language is a promised, source-authenticated family; recognizing arbitrary hidden block permutations or discovering semantic profiles in an arbitrary strict22 input is \textbf{OPEN}.

\section{Separation versus construction and exact open lemma}
Under $P=NP$, the canonical family remains polynomial and there may exist a general polynomial SAT solver; the present exact interface algorithm is not an unconditional reduction for arbitrary SAT. Under $P\ne NP$, this family is still polynomial and its bridge necessity does not yield a contradiction; it supplies only $k$ necessary original bridge \emph{clauses}, while the input has $20k$ clauses. The explicit $O(k)$ resolution refutation is a strong hostile control against lower bounds inferred from connectedness, signed balance, absence of a local UNSAT block, or global cycle length alone.

\textbf{Next LSPV-2 lemma:} For arbitrary connected strict22 sources, test whether a \emph{polynomial-time discovered} sequence of semantic operations can produce a cover by $O(\log N)$-bit moving interfaces with exact, source-authenticated relations and \emph{polynomial total} update, join and reencoding cost. The smallest credible falsifier is a family with certified semantic profile growth despite all adaptive representational choices, not just a high-width raw clause graph. In parallel, use controlled M/W and unrelated balanced-sign mutations to separate compatibility cost from cost of finding the decomposition. Neither $P=NP$ nor $P\ne NP$ is resolved.
