\section{Effective-dimension threshold}
Write the hypothetical effective lifting dimension as
\(\nu_{\rm eff}=t^s\), rescale the gadget as \(b=t^\alpha\), and let
\(0<\delta<1\).  The recovered sufficient bounds become
\[
 \alpha\delta\ge 2s,\qquad q=4\alpha+c+1,\qquad h>q,        \tag{2}
\]
and the known-proof exponent must fit under the cap:
\[
 \alpha(h+4)+h\le \alpha(1-\delta)(h+c).                  \tag{3}
\]
Equation (3) is equivalent to
\[
 h(1+\alpha\delta)\le \alpha\bigl(c(1-\delta)-4\bigr).   \tag{4}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Effective-dimension squeeze]
For fixed \(c>0\) and \(s>0\), there exist parameters
\(\alpha>0\), \(0<\delta<1\), and an integer \(h\) satisfying (2)--(3)
for all sufficiently large rescalings if and only if
\[
                         c>8+8s.                          \tag{5}
\]
Equivalently, a specialized-dimension repair must satisfy
\(s<(c-8)/8\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put \(x=\alpha\delta\).  If a feasible \(h\) exists, then the upper bound
from (4) strictly exceeds \(4\alpha+c+1\).  Hence
\[
 c(1-\delta)-4
   >4(1+x)+\frac{(c+1)(1+x)}{\alpha}.
\]
Since \(x\ge2s\), dropping positive terms yields
\(c>8+4x\ge8+8s\), proving necessity.

Conversely suppose \(c>8+8s\).  Set \(x=2s\) and choose
\(\delta=x/\alpha\).  As \(\alpha\to\infty\), the lower and upper bounds
for \(h/\alpha\) tend respectively to
\[
             4 \quad\hbox{and}\quad \frac{c-4}{1+2s}.
\]
Condition (5) makes the second limit strictly larger than the first.
Therefore, for sufficiently large integer \(\alpha\), the interval between
\(4\alpha+c+1\) and the right-hand side of (4) has length greater than one
and contains an integer \(h\).  Then \(0<\delta<1\),
\(\alpha\delta=2s\), and all inequalities hold.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Actual recovered dimension]
For \(s=c+1\), condition (5) becomes \(c>8c+16\), which fails for every
positive \(c\).  Thus the generic source dimension cannot be repaired merely
by retuning \(\alpha,\delta,h\) within this ledger.
\end{corollary}

The theorem is \EXACT{} for the displayed exponent conditions.  It neither
proves that a specialized theorem with \(s<(c-8)/8\) exists nor changes the
validity of the cited source theorem.  It converts an open-ended ``try a
smaller dimension'' suggestion into a quantitative, falsifiable target.
