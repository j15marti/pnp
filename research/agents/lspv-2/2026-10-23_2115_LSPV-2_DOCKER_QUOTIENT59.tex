\section{Post-acquisition zeta join theorem}
For a source-certified separator $S$, conditional source assignment $\alpha$, and three internally disjoint components with complete $b$-bit port feasibility sets $R_1(\alpha),R_2(\alpha),R_3(\alpha)$, suppose their only cross clauses are the $b$ bitwise ternary OR conditions. Exact compatibility requires
\[
\exists x\in R_1,\ y\in R_2,\ z\in R_3:
\quad x\vee_{\rm bit}y\vee_{\rm bit}z=2^b-1.
\]
PROVED/EXACT restricted: once the exact three tables are provided, a deterministic superset-zeta witness array on $R_3$ can be built in $O(b2^b)$ word time and $O(2^b)$ words, after which examining at most $|R_1||R_2|\le2^{2b}$ ordered pairs finds a compatible third witness or proves nonexistence. For $g$ independent groups under $s$ shared source bits the continuation bound is $O(2^sg(2^{2b}+b2^b))$, plus witness replay. Acquiring a correct interface and generating exact local feasibility tables are separate and potentially expensive. No uniform all-input compiler is claimed.

For the paper instance, the source-checked canonical tables have $b=5$: 256 local boundary candidates, 15,640 DPLL nodes, 1,206,150 clause visits, 2,734,922 literal reads, and 120 feasible mask entries across eight entrance types. The global join checked all 64 external assignments and was UNSAT. The much shorter 611-node plus 13-step shadow proof bypasses the full table construction. An independent test compared the superset join to full enumeration on 560 mask-table triples with $1\le b\le7$ (347 SAT and 213 UNSAT), with zero failures.
