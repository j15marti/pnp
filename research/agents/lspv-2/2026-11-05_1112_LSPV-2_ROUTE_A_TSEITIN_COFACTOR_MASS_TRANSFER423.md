## Theorem 423.1: exact direct-cofactor residual
For any consistent partial assignment $\rho$ fixing $k$ original edge variables, let $s_j(\rho)$ be the number of original source clause $C_j$ literals already made TRUE. Form $F\upharpoonright_\rho$ **only** by dropping clauses with a TRUE assigned literal and removing FALSE assigned literals elsewhere, retaining original clause IDs and any empty clauses. Define
$$
\Omega(\rho)=\sum_{j=1}^{4H}\max\{0,s_j(\rho)-1\}.
$$
Then
$$
\boxed{m_{\rm residual}(\rho)=4H-4k+\Omega(\rho)\ge4H-4k.}
$$
**Proof.** Each original edge variable has four positive and four negative occurrences, so assigning it makes exactly four source literal occurrences TRUE; hence $\sum_j s_j=4k$. A clause is removed iff $s_j>0$, and $\mathbf1_{s>0}=s-(s-1)_+$. Summing gives removed source IDs $D=4k-\Omega$, as claimed. For any matching of assigned graph edges, no source clause has more than one assigned variable; $\Omega=0$, so equality is attained. The exact local parity-gadget residual has $2^{d-1}$ clauses of width $d$ when $d\in\{1,2,3\}$ incident edges remain unset. When $d=0$, it has zero clauses if charge consistent, otherwise precisely one empty clause. This is a direct *source-cofactor* theorem, not a theorem about all legal simplifications.

## Theorem 423.2: adaptive complete-tree mass (transport of LSPV-3/261)
Consider a full binary **tree** of original-variable assignments (no uncharged branch merges). Leaves are P (correctly certified UNSAT partial cubes), S (certified fully satisfying cubes), or E (explicit source-cofactor SAT guest of at most $r$ source clause IDs). Let $q=|E|$, $\mu(X)=\sum_{\ell\in X}2^{-|\rho_\ell|}$, and $k_*=\max\{0,\lceil(4H-r)/4\rceil\}$. Then
$$
\boxed{\mu(P)+\mu(S)+\mu(E)=1,\quad\mu(E)\le q2^{-k_*},\quad\mu(P)+\mu(S)\ge1-q2^{-k_*}.}
$$
Proof: all E leaves have depth at least $k_*$ by Theorem 423.1; apply Kraft equality to all complete-tree leaves. For odd-charge UNSAT, $\mu(S)=0$. Therefore, **if there are no P or S leaves**, all power-short $N^{1/2+o(1)}$-bit children require $q\ge2^{H-o(H)}$, contradicting a $N^{3/5+o(1)}$ child budget. With $q\le N^{3/5+o(1)}$, P certificates must cover mass at least $1-2^{-H+o(H)}$. This is a necessary condition on one **specified pure direct-cofactor decomposition class**, not a lower bound on acquiring P certificates. Shared GF(2) reasoning can prune the odd member at the root; no arbitrary SAT-machine hardness is inferred.
