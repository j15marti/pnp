\section{Ordinary source construction and exact Boolean join}

\begin{theorem}[Source-coherent OR--AND equivalence, PROVED/EXACT restricted]
For every choice of ordinary leaf formulas,
\[
\operatorname{SAT}(H_b(F_{11},\ldots,F_{bb}))
\iff \bigvee_{i=1}^{b}\bigwedge_{j=1}^{b}\operatorname{SAT}(F_{ij}).
\]
Every group $G_i$ and leaf $F_{ij}$ is an ordinary complete CNF and an eligible input to the \emph{same all-input} exact SAT decider. The correct equation holds for both SAT and UNSAT root instances, with global source-name compatibility.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pointwise, $\exists a[(\neg s\vee u\vee a)\wedge(\neg a\vee v\vee w)]\equiv(\neg s\vee u\vee v\vee w)$: for $s=0$ take $a=0$, and for $s=1$ the clause is exactly the original ternary existential condition. At least one selector must be true. All leaf original-name blocks are disjoint, so the witness extensions of a selected group combine without conflict. A satisfying root therefore selects a group with all satisfiable leaves, and any such group produces an explicit root extension. This covers rejection as well.
\end{proof}
The exact identity relies on source-name disjointness and verified guard parsing. A generic shared-variable OR-of-AND assembly would need a separate compatibility join; separate child labels would not suffice.

\section{Two actual nested ordinary solver levels and decision-tree lower bound}
A first-layer group decider receives the \emph{ordinary CNF} $G_i$, parses its declared consecutive leaf records and calls a total ordinary-SAT solver on each self-contained $F_{ij}$ until either one is UNSAT or all pass. An outer decider calls each group procedure until one group is SAT or all fail. This gives real semantic recursion at root $\to$ group $\to$ ordinary leaf, not assumed SAT answer bits. The reference executable also independently runs exact DPLL on every full group and root, and compares all labels.

\begin{proposition}[Restricted answer-only adversary, PROVED/EXACT]
A deterministic controller receiving solely individual independent leaf-SAT bits for the Boolean function $\bigvee_i\bigwedge_j z_{ij}$ requires $b^2$ separate queries in its worst case; worst-case full transcripts exist for both root labels.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Within each group answer $1$ on its first $b-1$ distinct queries and $0$ on its last. To reject the root, all $b$ groups must each yield a zero, found only on their last query. For an accepting path, use the same answers except that the last completed group receives $1$ on its last query, thus becoming the first all-one group. Until that final answer there are compatible SAT and UNSAT bit-vector completions. Independent SAT/UNSAT leaf constructions realize the bit vectors. The result is \emph{not} a general SAT time or query lower bound: a source-aware solver may recognize the simple leaves, solve the root directly, or use a different representation.
\end{proof}
