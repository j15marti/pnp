\section{Source-indexed exact theorem}
Let $C_j$ be a proper original clause with support $S$, and let $u(C_j)\in\{0,1\}^{S}$ be its local all-literals-false $U_j$ target; it bans exactly that coherent assignment on $S$. If the original clauses contain every one of the $2^{|S|-1}$ sign patterns with $|u|\bmod 2=p$, their conjunction is equivalent to
\[
\bigoplus_{x\in S}x=1-p.
\]
Proof: every assignment of parity $p$ falsifies its corresponding source clause, and every assignment of parity $1-p$ satisfies all of them. This is exact on both SAT and UNSAT sources and requires all original clauses and global variable consistency; occurrences $x_{i,j}$ are not independent variables. The rest of the source CNF is retained.

Let extracted authenticated equations be $(M_i,b_i)$. Any XOR sum of rows gives the consequence $(\triangle_{i\in I}M_i,\bigoplus_{i\in I}b_i)$. Conditioning by a coherent pin set $\alpha$ produces a reduced row. A $0=1$ row is a certified conflict for that residual; $x=b$ forces an original variable; setting the remaining support to the opposite parity forbids a complete partial cube. The checking certificate identifies each full set of original-clause indices, selected equation indices, coherent pins, and resulting row. The proof is linear addition of source consequences followed by substitution, and does not promote one UNSAT cofactor to parent UNSAT.

\paragraph{Original-clause witness and conflict examples.} Complete source clauses for $x_1\oplus x_2\oplus x_3=0$, $x_1\oplus x_2\oplus x_4=0$, and $x_3\oplus x_4=1$ produce the source-authenticated contradiction $0=1$. Complete blocks for $x_1\oplus x_2\oplus x_3=0$ and $x_1\oplus x_2=0$ yield $x_3=0$ without enumerating flips. A source model reconstructed from the affine system is explicitly checked against every original clause.

\paragraph{Falsifier of affine-only classification.}
Let $G$ be the four original clauses equivalent to $x_1\oplus x_2\oplus x_3=0$ plus $(x_1)$. Append $(\neg x_1\vee x_4)\wedge(\neg x_4\vee x_5)$ and choose last clause $(\neg x_5\vee x_1)$ or $(\neg x_5\vee\neg x_1)$. Both inputs have the \emph{same} fully authenticated extracted parity equations; the first is SAT and the second UNSAT. Therefore this is a pruning module only, unless all clauses are covered or the rest are decided by another correct terminal.
