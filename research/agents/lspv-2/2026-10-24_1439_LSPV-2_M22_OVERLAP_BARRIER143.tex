\section{Uniform regular source ensemble}
Take $k\ge6$ divisible by three, $h=2k/3$, and $M=\binom{k}{3}$. Let $\mathcal H_k$ be the finite collection of simple $3$-uniform hypergraphs on labeled vertices $[k]$, each with $h$ distinct edges and degree exactly two at every vertex. Draw $P,Q$ independently and uniformly from $\mathcal H_k$ and sign $P$ positive, $Q$ negative. This produces $\CM$ and is in $\CS$ precisely if $P\ne Q$. It is an experimental/adversarial control and is not assumed hard.

\begin{theorem}[Exact mean and maximal-defect probability, PROVED/EXACT]\label{th:mean}
For $H=|P\cap Q|$, one has
\[
\boxed{\mathbb EH=\frac{h^2}{M}
=\frac{8k}{3(k-1)(k-2)}.}
\]
In particular $\Pr(q=h)\ge 1-\mathbb EH=1-O(k^{-1})$. For the conditional uniform distribution on $\CS$, the failure probability in either bound increases by at most a factor two.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The vertex-permutation group acts transitively on triple supports. Every random $P$ contains exactly $h$ of the $M$ supports, so $\Pr(e\in P)=h/M$ for each triple $e$, and independently the same holds for $Q$. Summing $\Pr(e\in P,e\in Q)$ over all supports gives $h^2/M$. Markov gives $\Pr(H\ge1)\le\mathbb EH$. The fully paired event is $P=Q$, probability $1/|\mathcal H_k|$. For $k\ge6$ divisible by three, $|\mathcal H_k|\ge2$; therefore conditioning on $P\ne Q$ multiplies every unconditional failure bound by at most $(1-1/|\mathcal H_k|)^{-1}\le2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact branch-count crossover]\label{cor:cross}
Define $\delta=2/3-\log_3 2>0$. Then
\[
\boxed{3^q<2^k\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H>\delta k,\quad
\delta\approx0.0357369.}
\]
Moreover
\[
\boxed{\Pr(3^q<2^k)\le
\frac{8}{3\delta(k-1)(k-2)}=O(k^{-2}).}
\]
Conditioning on $\CS$ costs at most a factor two. Thus with probability $1-O(k^{-2})$ the \emph{choice between these two advertised upper budgets} selects exhaustive $2^k$ rather than the $3^q$ factor route. This is not an optimal-runtime comparison or any arbitrary-algorithm lower bound.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Take logarithms of $3^{2k/3-H}<2^k$ and rearrange to obtain $H>\delta k$. Markov gives $\Pr(H>\delta k)\le\mathbb EH/(\delta k)$. Substitute Theorem~\ref{th:mean} and condition as above.
\end{proof}

At maximal defect, $3^{2k/3}=2^{(2\log_2 3/3)k}>2^k$, so this \emph{particular} factor-branch upper budget is not favorable. A uniform adaptive decider can still recognize formulas, find graph matchings, run affine/Horn/SCC reductions, cache shared work, or decide directly. Existing MAXDEFECT-FACTOR101 and MATCH-EXPANDER100 explicitly furnish maximum-$q$ easy SAT examples. Source frontier, graph expansion and $q$ do not force decision work.
