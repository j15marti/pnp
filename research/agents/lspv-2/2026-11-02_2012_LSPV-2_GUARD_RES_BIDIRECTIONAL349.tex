\section*{Preselected syntax and prior results}
Retain the existing fixed $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ with free authenticated original clauses, unit-cost binary Resolution, AND3, GF$(2)$ XOR, paid conversions and conservative source-independent VARDEF. We study its \emph{subsystem} Resolution here, not a replacement of the common mixed calculus. Let $W_{\mathrm{Res}}(F)$ count distinct derived inferences in the best shared Resolution refutation DAG, with free optimal proof selection. This is a proof-size measure, not a solver-time lower bound.

For arbitrary general CNF $F(x)=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}C_i(x)$, fresh $z$, define
\[
G_z(F)=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}(z\vee C_i)\ \wedge\ \bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}(\neg z\vee C_i).
\]
For every $(x,z)$, $G_z(F)(x,z)=F(x)$. The compiler does not inspect the SAT label. Previous LSPV-2 GUARD344 established $\mu(G_z(F))=2\mu(F)$ and an exact guard lift when $F$ has a source-once $(m-1)$-node Resolution tree. This run supplies the arbitrary shared-DAG upper translation and the converse \emph{lower-bound-direction} proof translation.

\begin{theorem}[uniform guarded Resolution transport]
For \emph{every} valid UNSAT $F$, even with arbitrary Resolution-DAG sharing,
\[
\boxed{W_{\mathrm{Res}}(F)\le W_{\mathrm{Res}}(G_z(F))
       \le 2W_{\mathrm{Res}}(F)+1.}
\]
With a canonical signed-decimal DIMACS serializer, old header length $H$, new header length $H'$, $m$ original clauses, $N$ original encoded bytes, and $d$ decimal digits in $z=n+1$, exact new source length is
\[
\boxed{N'=2(N-H)+H'+m(2d+3).}
\]
Thus $N'=O(N+m\log(n+2))=O(N^2)$ for arbitrary syntactically valid DIMACS headers. Under canonical dense numbering in which every declared variable occurs, $n\le N$, giving $N'=O(N\log(N+2))$. Both proof transformations work uniformly and effectively for all $m,n$; neither uses an instance-answer oracle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the upper direction, lift every original source and derived Resolution clause $D$ to $z\vee D$. Every original pivot remains valid because $z$ is fresh. The proof DAG is copied with the same sharing, once for $+z$ and once for $-z$. The two roots are $(z)$ and $(\neg z)$; one final Resolution inference on $z$ yields the empty clause. If the source proof had $q$ derived nodes, the lifted proof has exactly $2q+1$, without unfolding a shared DAG into a tree.

For the lower direction, take an \emph{arbitrary} $q'$-node Resolution refutation of $G_z(F)$, and restrict every clause by $z=0$. Positive-guard sources become original $C_i$, while negative-guard sources become TRUE and disappear. Standard restriction closure of Resolution holds with no increase in inferred nodes: restricted inferences either remain Resolution, become TRUE, or can be bypassed by the restricted parent that subsumes the restricted resolvent. Propagating these stronger clauses through the proof DAG gives an ordinary Resolution refutation of the surviving source formula $F$ in at most $q'$ nodes. Hence $W_{\mathrm{Res}}(F)\le q'$; minimizing $q'$ gives the left inequality. For byte accounting, prefixing each original line with signed $z$ adds $d+1$ bytes in the positive branch and $d+2$ in the negative branch; duplicate the old clause block and update the header.
\end{proof}

\section*{Four explicit falsifiers}
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{F1, PROVED/EXACT.} A guard can make a hard source arbitrarily easier in pure Resolution. This is excluded by a size-nonincreasing restriction compiler.
\item[] \textbf{F2, PROVED/EXACT.} A shared source DAG needs exponential unfolding when lifted. The compiler preserves each branch's sharing, exactly $2q+1$ paid nodes.
\item[] \textbf{F3, OPEN.} The lower inequality holds for the full combined mixed calculus without further hypotheses. Resolution restriction closure does \emph{not} by itself pay for semantic AND3/XOR retyping, fresh auxiliary restrictions or conversion. No mixed lower transport is claimed.
\item[] \textbf{F4, UNSUPPORTED.} The guarded construction proves a superpolynomial bound for mixed SAT or B3. It is an exact \emph{Resolution-only} transport, not a SAT decision lower bound.
\end{description}
