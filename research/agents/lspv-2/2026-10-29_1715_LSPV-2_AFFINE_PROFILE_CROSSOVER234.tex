\section{PROPOSE: one missing lemma, three falsifiable mechanisms}
\noindent\textbf{Missing lemma.} Does a growing \emph{aggregate exact semantic interface rank}, even with connected strict signed exact-$(2,2)$ source constraints, force a corresponding unavoidable cost in the \emph{actual} simultaneous time-space Pareto frontier of arbitrary uniform SAT deciders? The intended universal implication is unproved. We test three restricted mechanisms, without supplying an answer-class bit.
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{M1: high-rank source.} Build a connected, degree-four signed exact-$(2,2)$ family whose authenticated boundary projection is not rank zero but has independent affine codimension $e=k=\Theta(n)$. \textbf{PROVED/RESTRICTED} below.
\item[] \textbf{M2: rank forces exponential exact-profile storage.} A rank-$k$ boundary relation requires $2^{\Omega(k)}$ bits in \emph{every} exact representation. \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED as a general representation claim}: one ordered BDD uses exactly $5k$ nonterminal nodes; an affine-row list is linear size in words. A dense explicit table still has exponential output size.
\item[] \textbf{M3: high aggregate rank forces decision hardness.} This same rank implies a superpolynomial decision cost. \textbf{FALSIFIED as an inference on the constructed promise}: an unconditional polynomial canonical-promise recognizer, accompanied by an explicit SAT witness or an exact fixed UNSAT certificate, decides both labels. The \emph{general} implication for source-independent algorithms remains OPEN.
\end{description}

\section{BUILD: exact22 parity blocks and constant-size connector}
\noindent Let $P(a,b,c)$ be the four ternary clauses that prohibit exactly those assignments with $a\oplus b\oplus c=1$. Explicitly,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parity}
P(a,b,c)=(a\vee b\vee\neg c)\land(a\vee\neg b\vee c)
\land(\neg a\vee b\vee c)\land(\neg a\vee\neg b\vee\neg c).
\end{equation}
Each of $a,b,c$ occurs twice positively and twice negatively and the formula is proper, connected and satisfied exactly when $a\oplus b\oplus c=0$.

\noindent The \emph{source-authenticated} conjunction connector $J(H,K)$ of CONNECTOR231 adds two fixed 15-variable guard gadgets, hence $30$ variables and $40$ clauses; it preserves strict quotas and connectedness and has the pointwise identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:connector}
\exists Z\,J(H,K)(X,Y,Z)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H(X)\land K(Y).
\end{equation}
The guards use three negative-literal exchanges and their finite local gate identity is independently checked by exhausting $2^{15}$ assignments and the 16 four-port Boolean inputs. The identity is a theorem \emph{about this fixed gadget}, not an assumed unrestricted SAT oracle. Taking $k$ independent copies of (\ref{eq:parity}) and joining them left-associatively gives $C_k$. Let $G$ be the fixed 15-variable SAT core with 272 satisfying models and $W_1$ the original 15-variable UNSAT core certified by all $2^{15}$ assignments. They have identical unsigned clause scopes and differ in two signed literal occurrences. Put
\[F_k^+=J(C_k,G),\qquad F_k^-=J(C_k,W_1).\]
The canonical source family includes both signs and all physical guard variables. Recognition is by complete source reconstruction and comparison, not supplied answer advice.

\begin{theorem}[Connected parity-core SAT/UNSAT twins]\label{thm:twins}
For every integer $k\ge1$, $F_k^+$ and $F_k^-$ are proper, connected strict signed exact-$(2,2)$-3CNFs with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sizes}
 n=33k+15,\qquad m=44k+20=\tfrac43n.
\end{equation}
They have identical unsigned clause scopes, differ at two literal signs, and have opposite satisfiability labels. There exists a constructible tree decomposition of each with width at most $35$. Let $Y$ comprise the original $3k$ parity-block variables, excluding all newly generated guards and anchor variables. Then, as exact Boolean relations on $Y$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proj}
\exists (V(F_k^+)\setminus Y)\,F_k^+
=\bigwedge_{i=1}^k [y_{i,1}\oplus y_{i,2}\oplus y_{i,3}=0],
\qquad
\exists (V(F_k^-)\setminus Y)\,F_k^- =\bot.
\end{equation}
The SAT relation is an affine subspace of dimension $2k$ and codimension and aggregate coefficient-row rank $e=k$ in $\mathbb F_2^{3k}$, with exactly $2^{2k}$ valid boundary assignments.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each parity block is a complete four-clause XOR3 formula. Every connector application preserves signed occurrence quotas and adds precisely $30$ variables and $40$ clauses by three paired same-polarity exchanges. Joining $k$ blocks takes $k-1$ connectors and the anchor takes one more, giving $3k+15+30k=33k+15$ and $4k+20+40k=44k+20$. The fixed $G/W_1$ difference is two opposite sign changes away from selected connector ports, so corresponding sources remain unsigned-scope identical with the same signed quotas. By induction on (\ref{eq:connector}) and then existentially projecting the anchor, the exact projected relations are (\ref{eq:proj}). An independent triple equation has coefficient row $\{3i-2,3i-1,3i\}$; these rows have disjoint supports and hence rank $k$. A witness for each satisfying triple is lifted through the fixed connector's known extension table. On the UNSAT side, the $W_1$ anchor makes the complete conjunction inconsistent. For the width bound, give each three-variable source block a bag of size three and the anchor a bag of size 15. At every join introduce a central bag containing the selected clause's three original variables from each child and all 30 fresh guard variables, at most 36 names. Attach that bag to child bags containing the selected clauses. The running-intersection property and clause coverage are preserved inductively; the maximum width is $36-1=35$.
\end{proof}

\section{Syntactic $b,q$, exact finite optima, and genuine semantic rank}
\noindent On these physically connected formulas the specified global boundary has $b=3k$ variables. Let the \emph{single} private module comprise all $30k+15$ remaining variables. Its syntactic hitting parameter $q$ is the minimum number of private names meeting every clause with no global boundary name. There are exactly $39k+20$ such clauses, and every private name has degree at most four. Thus the following family-wide rigorous, albeit loose, bound holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qbound}
 \left\lceil\frac{39k+20}{4}\right\rceil\le q\le30k+15.
\end{equation}
This is an \emph{actual} one-module choice, and $Y\cup B$ hits all clauses. The earlier static architectural condition $n\le3b+12bq$ consequently applies but is far from tight here. In separate finite integer-program runs, HiGHS reported optimal $q=16,27,38,49,60,71,82,93,104$ for $k=1,\ldots,9$, respectively. Every returned primal hitting set was replayed on all private-only clauses; proof-quality integer dual certificates were not independently obtained. Consequently $q=11k+5$ is \textbf{COMPUTATIONAL} on these nine tests, not a proved asymptotic identity. The rank $k$ is the \emph{semantic parity-row rank} of the SAT boundary projection, not the rank of the binary syntactic clause-incidence matrix, not a matrix rank of arbitrary eight-bit truth-table rows, and not a hardness guarantee.

\section{Physical bit frontier and first representation crossover}
\noindent Fix the exact encoded interface alphabet, $b=3k$ variable names and $w=\lceil\log_2(b+1)\rceil$ header/name bits. The dense truth table stores one bit for every boundary assignment; an affine list stores one right-hand-side bit and three $w$-bit indices per parity row; an ordered reduced BDD uses $h=\lceil\log_2(5k+2)\rceil$-bit node pointers. Each receives the same $2w$-bit header. The following are explicit physically realizable encoding lengths for the SAT projection:
\begin{align}\label{eq:encoding}
 D(k)&=2w+2^{3k},\\
 A(k)&=2w+k(3w+1),\\
 B(k)&=2w+5k(w+2h).
\end{align}
These count named bits of the specified encoders, not minimum descriptions over all possible representations. The BDD nodes are topologically numbered; the root is the last node, so it needs no extra pointer field.
\begin{lemma}[Five nodes per parity block]\label{lem:bdd}
With grouped variable order $y_{1,1},y_{1,2},y_{1,3},\ldots$, the reduced ordered BDD representing (\ref{eq:proj}) on the SAT side has \emph{exactly} $5k$ nonterminal nodes and two terminals.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At the beginning of each parity triple there is one node. After its first variable, the two branches encode the distinct prefix-parity states; each requires a distinct node on the second variable. After the second variable, the two states again require distinct nodes on the third variable; exactly one value of the third variable permits proceeding to the next triple and the other reaches false. These are five distinct reduced nodes for the triple. Continuations use variables of larger index, so nodes from different triples cannot merge. The last triple terminates at true/false. The construction is both upper and lower for this fixed variable order.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[First exact physical size crossover]\label{prop:cross}
For $k=1,2$, the dense table in (\ref{eq:encoding}) is shorter than the stated BDD encoding. For all integers $k\ge3$, the BDD is shorter than the dense table. Thus the first realized $B(k)<D(k)$ point is \(k=3\), with $(D,B,A)=(520,218,47)$ bits and source $n=114$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Direct substitution gives $(D,B)=(12,44),(70,116),(520,218)$ at $k=1,2,3$. For $k\ge3$, $w\le k+1$ and $h\le k+2$, so $B(k)\le 15k^2+27k+2+2(k+1)$, which is below $8^k$ at $k=3$ and remains below it by induction, since the exponential is multiplied by eight while the polynomial grows by a smaller factor. Hence $B(k)<2w+8^k=D(k)$ for all $k\ge3$.
\end{proof}

\section{Native resource firewall and both complexity universes}
\noindent The canonical input has $N=(4m+2)\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil=\Theta(k\log k)$ source bits. A uniform parser/reconstructor of canonical templates and a width-35 decomposition both run in polynomial time and polynomial work bits with full output size and source matching charged, on standard deterministic Turing machines. On fixed-$t$ read-only-input multitape TMs a conservative repeated-scan implementation requires $O(N^3)$ transitions and $O(N)$ writable space. A conventional one-read/write-tape simulation yields $O(N^4)$ transitions and $O(N)$ total visited footprint including source, child and output. On two fully writable tapes, both input and auxiliary symbol changes must be counted; the same polynomial upper constructor need not minimize write count. On indexed RAM, source parsing and physically storing the source costs $\Omega(N)$ bits, and separate $O(N^3)$ bit-operation upper accounting is conservative. A fixed UTM has an additional fixed-program interpretation and input-layout overhead; no small-space FLMV endpoint follows from this easy restricted family. The physical exponentially long dense table, when actually produced, must be charged separately and is \emph{not} part of the polynomial recognizer.

\noindent In the hypothetical $P=NP$ universe, the displayed easy family and its finite-size representation crossings remain valid, while an unknown generic polynomial SAT algorithm may choose other representations. In the $P\ne NP$ universe, they remain equally valid: the matched SAT/UNSAT pair is a recognizable promise family, and neither its growing aggregate rank nor its low treewidth furnishes a universal decision lower bound. No free label bit and no alternative recursive SAT solver are assumed.
