\begin{theorem}
A complete assignment placing each original clause in $S_j$ (all
literals true) or $U_j$ (all false) exists precisely when the
signed clause-incidence multigraph has no cycle of odd parity.

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

occurrence $a_i=z_j\oplus\sigma_{ij}$. Shared variables impose
$z_j\oplus z_k=\sigma_{ij}\oplus\sigma_{ik}$.

choose a root label per component and propagate labels through
a spanning forest; the resulting variable demands are consistent.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
Let a proper width-$r\ge2$ source have $n$ variables, each with
$d/2$ positive and $d/2$ negative occurrences (even $d$),
and $m=dn/r$ original clauses.
If a partial assignment makes $k>(dn/2-m)/(r-1)$
original clauses all-true, saturated ordinary unit propagation
refutes it. For strict signed exact-$(2,2)$-3CNF this is
$k>m/4=n/3$. Each satisfying model has $N_3\le m/4$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose conflict-free UP saturates after fixing $f$ variables.
Let $k'\ge k$ clauses be fully true, and let $h$ other clauses
contain an assigned true literal. There are exactly $df/2$
assigned-true and $d(n-f)$ unassigned literal occurrences.
Therefore $rk'+h\le df/2$.
Every other clause has at least two unassigned literals, so
$d(n-f)\ge2(m-k'-h)\ge2m+2(r-1)k'-df$.
Rearrange for the contradiction.
The SAT bound follows by counting at least one true literal
in every non-all-true original clause.
\end{proof}
