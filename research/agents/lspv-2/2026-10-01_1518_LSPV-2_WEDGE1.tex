\paragraph{Two obstacles and literature preflight.}
The two active LSPV-2 obstacles remain (i) acquisition of exact reconstruction-compatible legal Hadamard slice spaces \(S_v\), and (ii) deciding whether their common quadratic kernel is large or zero. Recent HRS and twisted Reed--Solomon work again computes Schur-square dimensions from construction-specific determinant/product identities rather than generic locality or list-decoding properties. The vMS targeted-generator literature supplies the surrounding construction but no Boolean common-kernel theorem. These are object-level guides only, not SAT lower bounds.

\paragraph{PROVED/EXACT: exterior-square polar constraint.}
Let \(V=\mathbb F_2^m\). For every legal Hadamard state \(v\), let \(S_v\le V\) be its linear output slice. Define
\[
W=\operatorname{span}_{v}S_v\le V,\qquad
U=\operatorname{span}_{v}\bigwedge\nolimits^2 S_v\le\bigwedge\nolimits^2 V.
\]
For a zero-constant Boolean quadratic
\[
q(x)=\ell(x)+\sum_{i<j}\beta_{ij}x_ix_j,
\]
let \(b_q\) be its alternating polar form. Alternating bilinear forms on \(V\) are canonically dual to \(\bigwedge^2V\). Hence the POLAR1 equations \(b_q|_{S_v\times S_v}=0\) for every \(v\) are equivalent to
\[
\boxed{b_q\in U^\perp.}
\]
Therefore the space of possible polar parts of common quadratic relations has dimension at most
\[
\boxed{\binom m2-\dim U.}
\]
This is exact for the declared SAME\_REALIZATION Hadamard family.

\paragraph{PROVED/EXACT: full exterior span collapses the quadratic part.}
If \(U=\bigwedge^2V\), then every \(q\in K_2(H)\) has \(b_q=0\). A zero-constant Boolean polynomial of degree at most two with zero polar form has no quadratic monomials, so it is linear. Consequently
\[
\boxed{U=\bigwedge\nolimits^2V\Longrightarrow K_2(H)=W^\perp},
\qquad
\boxed{\dim K_2(H)=m-\dim W.}
\]
In particular,
\[
\boxed{W=V\ \text{and}\ U=\bigwedge\nolimits^2V
\Longrightarrow K_2(H)=\{0\},\quad \rho(H)=1+m+\binom m2.}
\]
Thus full quadratic rank has a two-level subspace certificate: legal slices span all linear directions and their within-slice wedges span all pair directions.

\paragraph{PROVED/EXACT: incremental certificate accounting.}
If \(S_v\) has dimension \(r_v\), one new state can increase \(\dim W\) by at most \(r_v\) and \(\dim U\) by at most \(\binom{r_v}{2}\). Hence any certificate satisfying the sufficient full-span conditions obeys
\[
\sum_v r_v\ge m,\qquad
\sum_v\binom{r_v}{2}\ge\binom m2.
\]
These are necessary counting conditions for this exterior-square certificate, not necessary conditions for \(K_2(H)=0\) in general, because value equations can rule out a nonzero polar form even when \(U\ne\bigwedge^2V\).

\paragraph{Algorithmic exploitability and exact computation.}
Given bases for explicit legal slices, \(W\) is obtained by row reduction and \(U\) by row reducing wedge vectors \(s_{v,a}\wedge s_{v,b}\). Thus the polar obstruction is efficiently computable once slices are acquired. If \(U\) is deficient, a basis of \(U^\perp\) explicitly exposes candidate alternating matrices; one then solves the POLAR1 basis-value equations for compatible linear parts. Hidden exterior-square structure therefore aids acquisition and diagnosis rather than constituting hardness by itself.
