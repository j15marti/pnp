\section{Exact four-to-three-occurrence constructor}
\begin{lemma}
For a formula \(F\in\mathcal C_{\rm M22}\), replace the two positive occurrences of each original \(x_i\) by a fresh variable \(v_i\); add the ordinary binary implication clause \((\neg v_i\vee x_i)\). Call the resulting ordinary CNF \(G(F)\). Then \(\operatorname{SAT}(G(F))\iff\operatorname{SAT}(F)\), every old or new variable occurs exactly three times, \(n(G)=2k\), \(m(G)=m+k=7k/3\), and the total literal occurrence count is \(6k\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Old \(x_i\) retains its two negative occurrences and gets one positive implication occurrence. Fresh \(v_i\) has two positive occurrences and one negative implication occurrence. Extend any model of \(F\) by \(v_i=x_i\). Conversely, \((\neg v_i\vee x_i)\) forces \(v_i\Rightarrow x_i\), so each positive clause true after replacement stays true after original-variable projection. Negative old clauses are unchanged. Every model of \(G(F)\) therefore projects to a model of \(F\).
\end{proof}
Output clauses include binaries and mixed signs, so \(G(F)\notin\CS\), but it is a legitimate \emph{ordinary CNF} input for the published generic degree-three solver. Its dense serialization has \(\Theta(N)\) bits, with multiplicative rather than power shrink. Two successive ordinary source-variable restrictions retain maximum degree at most three and therefore preserve that generic solver eligibility without inventing a \(\CS\)-specific adapter. This is a valid typed construction \emph{outside} ARC-EDGE-90.
