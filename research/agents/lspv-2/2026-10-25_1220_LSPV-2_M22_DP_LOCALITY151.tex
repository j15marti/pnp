\section{Canonical M22 target, machine model and terminal convention}
Every $F\in\CM$ is a duplicate-free ordinary OR-CNF with exactly three distinct names per clause, each clause all-positive or all-negative, every active original variable appearing exactly twice positively and twice negatively. For a clause $C$, $\bar C$ flips literal signs but retains OR. Define
\[
\mathcal P_{\rm pair}=\{F\in\CM:(\forall C\in F)\ \bar C\in F\},\quad
\CS=\CM\setminus\mathcal P_{\rm pair},\quad
L_\star=\{\operatorname{enc}(F):F\in\CS,\ \SAT(F)\}.
\]
Only \emph{fully} paired formulas are excluded; partially paired, disconnected and easy instances are allowed, and malformed inputs are rejected. With $k=n(F)$ active names, $m$ clauses, and dense indexed bit length $N=|\operatorname{enc}(F)|$,
\[
3m=4k,\quad m_+=m_-=2k/3,\quad N=\Theta(k\log(k+2)).
\]
D\"ocker's Theorem~1 establishes NP-completeness of $\CM$-SAT and its printed fully-paired special case is always SAT \cite{docker}. The established paired-input adapter to fixed eligible satisfiable $Y$ proves $L_\star$ NP-complete, not superpolynomial deterministic hardness.

For any CNF $G$ and named variable $x$ write
\[
P_x=\{x\lor A_i\}_i,\quad Q_x=\{\neg x\lor B_j\}_j,\quad
\mathrm{DP}_x(G)=(G\setminus(P_x\cup Q_x))\wedge
 \bigwedge_{i,j}^{\mathrm{non\text{-}taut}}(A_i\lor B_j).
\]
Generated tautological resolvents and repeated clauses may be omitted, with no other substitution, subsumption, propagation, or global simplification. An empty clause denotes FALSE and a formula with no clauses TRUE. Pivots are \emph{distinct original input names}; their list is supplied or chosen by a separately paid computable policy. The theorem does not grant an arbitrary policy a free selection oracle.

\section{Fixed-depth source-patch theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Fixed-depth DP source locality; restricted exact]
Let $F\in\CM$ have $k$ original active names and $m=4k/3$ clauses. For any $d\le k$ and any fixed list of $d$ distinct original pivots $(x_1,\ldots,x_d)$, write $G_0=F$ and $G_j=\mathrm{DP}_{x_j}(G_{j-1})$. Define the numerical constants
\[
a_0=0,\qquad a_{j+1}=a_j+(a_j+2)^2,\qquad w_j=2^j+2.
\]
For every $j\le d$:
\[
\boxed{G_j(z)=\exists x_1\cdots\exists x_j\,F(x_1,\ldots,x_j,z)}
\quad\text{for every surviving assignment }z;
\]
\[
\boxed{m-4j\le |G_j|\le m+a_j,\qquad
\max_{C\in G_j}|C|\le w_j.}
\]
Moreover, $G_j$ consists of all original clauses not involving any of $x_1,\ldots,x_j$, together with at most $a_j$ new clause records; the original clauses removed are among at most $4j$ input records. Duplicates and tautological generated clauses may only improve these bounds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The single-pivot identity is the elementary resolution projection: after fixing the surviving variables, either all positive remainders $A_i$ hold, permitting $x=0$, or some $A_i$ fails, in which case all resolvents force every $B_j$ and $x=1$ works. Original clauses not mentioning $x$ persist unchanged. Iteration proves the exact pointwise existential statement, including both SAT and UNSAT cases.

Inductively represent $G_j$ as \emph{all} original clauses whose supports avoid the already chosen pivots, plus a multiset of at most $a_j$ generated clauses. When eliminating an additional original name $x$, at most two positive and two negative clauses can come from the untouched original part, because $F\in\CM$ has exactly two of each sign. The generated part contains at most $a_j$ records total. Thus each signed parent list has size at most $a_j+2$, yielding at most $(a_j+2)^2$ new resolvents. The surviving generated records number at most $a_j$. Hence $a_{j+1}\le a_j+(a_j+2)^2$. Any removed original clause must mention one of the pivots and therefore lies among at most $4j$ original clause records. This yields both clause bounds.

Initially all clauses have width three. A resolvent of two clauses of width at most $w_j$ has width at most $2(w_j-1)$; the recurrence $w_{j+1}=2(w_j-1)$ with $w_0=3$ solves to $w_j=2^j+2$. Removing duplicates, tautologies or repeated literals cannot increase width.\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No finite-pivot power shrink in ordinary indexed encoding]
Fix a constant $d$ independent of $k$. In a self-delimiting dense indexed clause-list representation with $\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil$-bit source identifiers, one sign bit per literal, clause-width tags, and unary or comparable $O(k)$-bit headers,
\[
\boxed{\ |\operatorname{enc}(G_d)|=N\left(1+O_d\left(\frac1{\log(k+2)}\right)\right).\ }
\]
Therefore no fixed number of such bare-DP steps produces ordinary encoded children of length $N^{\lambda+o(1)}$ for any fixed $\lambda<1$. A finite chain of constant-pivot bare-DP transformations remains at input exponent one. This does \emph{not} exclude a different adaptive preprocessing algorithm, nonlocal compression, arbitrary existential construction, or a route whose number of actual eliminations grows with input length and has separately proved paid uniformity.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At least $m-4d=4k/3-O_d(1)$ original proper three-literal clauses survive. All but $O_d(1)$ source variables appear in these untouched clauses: the at most $4d$ touched originals collectively contain at most $12d$ variable-name positions. At most $a_d=O_d(1)$ extra clauses appear, each with bounded width $2^d+2$. The surviving original body hence contains $4k-O_d(1)$ literal identifier fields, each of width $\log_2k+O(1)$, and the generated part adds only $O_d(\log k)$ bits. Dense relabeling and header changes can save or add at most $O_d(k)$ bits, including an identifier-width threshold crossing. The original length is $N=4k\log_2k+O(k)$. Therefore the difference is at most $O_d(k)$ and the asserted relative bound follows. For every $\lambda<1$, $N^{\lambda+o(1)}=o(N)$, impossible for these descendants.\end{proof}

\section{Paid implementation and exact model boundary}
For fixed $d$, the changed-record set, eliminated source record IDs, generated constant-width clauses, original/derived variable rank exceptions, and $d$ pivot names occupy $O_d(\log N)$ writable bits. Repeated passes over the read-only indexed source can find the at most $4d$ touched originals and construct the $a_d$ patch with $O_d(N\operatorname{polylog}N)$ elementary bit work, including selection if the pivot policy is itself an explicitly paid logarithmic-space rule (e.g., first $d$ indexed names). One-way emission of an \emph{ordinary explicit} child is paid per output bit, and the original clauses still require $\Theta(N)$ emitted bits. Materializing that child on a writable tape consumes $\Theta(N)$ live bits.

A virtual indexed child accessor can instead retain the constant patch and map requested output-bit addresses back to the original read-only source, charging source reads, source-to-child address arithmetic, tags, skipped records and relabeling. For fixed $d$ and an indexed fixed-width source clause layout, a query requires $O_d(\operatorname{polylog}N)$ bit work after the paid patch is built, and $O_d(\log N)$ writable bits. This is a \emph{source-backed representation}, not a new independently present FLMV read-only input. Multiple live consumers, nested calls, caches, ancestor state, output buffering and the actual source access model must be charged together. No SAT solver call or all-input resource improvement is supplied.
