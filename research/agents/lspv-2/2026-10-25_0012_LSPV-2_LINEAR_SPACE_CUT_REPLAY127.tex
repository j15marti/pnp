\section{Declared restricted objective and exact optimality}
Let $L\ge 1$ events form a supplied or fully validated closed scoped stream. Each variable has one named OPEN and CLOSE and is live throughout its occurrence interval. Let $S_i$ denote actual live original names at cut $i$, $w_i=|S_i|$, $w_0=w_L=0$, $W=\max_iw_i$. Let $2\le b\le L$ and $d=\lfloor b/2\rfloor$. An admissible edge $(i,j)$ has $1\le j-i\le b$ and $j-i\ge d$ unless $j=L$. Every $0$-to-$L$ path of admissible edges is a legal partition. Its \emph{algorithm-specific} cost is
\[
C(P)=\sum_{(i,j)\in P}2^{w_i+w_j}.
\]
This is an exhaustive boundary-pair \emph{upper proxy}, not a necessary number of SAT calls for arbitrary algorithms.

\begin{theorem}[Low-memory optimal scheduling with streamed traceback]
A globally minimum-cost legal cut path can be \emph{emitted in increasing source order}, without storing its entire predecessor array. Using access to the original indexed event source, the algorithm performs at most
\[
O\!\left(L(W+1)\left(1+\log(1+L/b)\right)+b\right)
\]
integer additions/comparisons/deque operations and $O(L(1+\log(1+L/b)))$ source event accesses, and requires
\[
O(b+W+\log L)
\]
live numerical/index entries, excluding emitted output, certified source material and separately charged child compilation/decision work.
Every path-cost integer needs at most $B=\lceil\log_2(L+1)\rceil+2W+2$ bits. With indexed bit decoding and finite-precision charged arithmetic the conservative bounds are
\[
U_{\rm bit}=O\!\left(L(W+1)(1+\log(1+L/b))B\operatorname{polylog}(L+2)\right)
\]
and
\[
S_{\rm writable}=O\!\left((b+W)(B+\log(L+2))+\log^2(L+2)\right)
\]
bits for this controller. These are upper bounds for an implementation with reusable buffers, not a claim that all SAT solvers spend this amount.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the globally optimal last-edge recurrence from OPTIMAL-WINDOW-CUT126. For a subinterval $[a,c]$ with compulsory endpoint cuts, let $D^a_i$ be the minimum cost of an admissible path from $a$ to $i$, and $R^c_j$ the minimum cost from $j$ to $c$. All edges retain the \emph{global} final-cut exception only when their endpoint is $L$. Take $h=\lfloor(a+c)/2\rfloor$. Every admissible path crosses the midpoint in one edge $(u,v)$ with $u\le h<v$; therefore
\[
\operatorname{OPT}(a,c)=
\min_{\substack{u\le h<v\\1\le v-u\le b\\v-u\ge d\ {\rm or}\ v=L}}
\left(D^a_u+2^{w_u+w_v}+R^c_v\right).
\]
The forward values need only the $b$ indices immediately before $h$, and the reverse values only the $b$ immediately after $h$. As in CUT126, for each live width $k$ maintain a monotone queue of the eligible minima $D_i$ (respectively $R_j$). The queued minima are sufficient because equal-width predecessors make identical successor increments, and later-inserted entries expire no earlier in the scan direction. They produce all required forward/reverse values in $O((c-a)(W+1)+b)$ arithmetic operations and $O(b+W)$ entries. Apply the same width-bucket scanning to the crossing search; its two endpoint windows have at most $b$ indices and need $O(b(W+1))$ operations. At the one global final edge, lengths below $d$ are checked explicitly. Store only the selected crossing edge and its two endpoint widths, release both forward/reverse buffers, recursively emit the left optimal path, emit the crossing edge, then emit the right optimal path. Each child interval has length at most $\lceil(c-a)/2\rceil$. For $c-a\le2b$, calculate the local predecessor array and emit its exact shortest path in $O(b(W+1))$ arithmetic steps and $O(b+W)$ entries. Since buffers are released before recursion, the additional stack is $O(\log L)$ index/width entries and the total arithmetic work satisfies the stated bound.

A random-access $w_i$ table need \emph{not} be stored: on the forward sweep update the current count by $+1,0,-1$ for OPEN, CLAUSE, CLOSE. On the reverse sweep subtract the corresponding delta. Each recursive endpoint receives its certified endpoint width from the just-computed crossing. Thus every requested width comes from a charged original-source event read and $O(b)$ rolling width entries. The original named-source validity and consistency checks must either be paid or supplied as a formal promise; they are not consequences of the width-only calculation. The global cost has at most $L$ edges each of weight at most $2^{2W}$, which gives $B$. Using ordinary addressed-bit arithmetic and indices yields the displayed bit-work and peak-bit upper bounds.
\end{proof}

\section{Reproducible hostile test and adaptive state ledger}

It covered 90 source formulas, 540 budget configurations \emph{per label}, 175 additional random balanced width trajectories each with four budgets, and 4,267 assertion groups, with zero mismatches. The adaptive test chooses by observed source grammar, never by a supplied truth label. A complete source scan may establish the eight-clause base plus harmless private positive leaves, yielding direct SAT or UNSAT; otherwise the policy switches to an exact named frontier-state DP. For 45 matched pairs plus 40 tampered-source controls of each label, the observed decisions were 45 recognized SAT, 45 recognized UNSAT, 40 frontier SAT and 40 frontier UNSAT, all correct. The fixed frontier method recorded 89,034 inspected clause states; the instrumented hybrid policy recorded 20,016 scanned-event and state-visit proxy operations, \emph{not} a machine-time speed ratio.
