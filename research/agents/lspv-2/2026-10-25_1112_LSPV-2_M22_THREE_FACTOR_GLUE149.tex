\section{Exact three-factor matching-glue theorem}
For $F\in\CM$, let $P$ and $Q$ be its unsigned positive and negative support families, each of size $2k/3$. Use disjoint coordinate sets $V=\{v_1,\ldots,v_k\}$ and $X=\{x_1,\ldots,x_k\}$, and define
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_F(V)&=\bigwedge_{S\in P}\ \bigvee_{i\in S}v_i,\\
B_F(X)&=\bigwedge_{S\in Q}\ \bigvee_{i\in S}\neg x_i,\\
I_k(V,X)&=\bigwedge_{i=1}^k(\neg v_i\vee x_i),\\
G(F)&=A_F(V)\wedge B_F(X)\wedge I_k(V,X).
\end{aligned}
\]
All $k$ clauses of $I_k$ form \emph{independent, variable-disjoint} Horn implications. Both $A_F$ and $B_F$ are monotone 3-CNF of one polarity, and each of their own variables occurs exactly twice. Every variable of $G(F)$ occurs exactly three times. The output has $2k$ variables, $m_G=7k/3$ clauses, $m$ proper ternaries and $k$ binary implications.

\begin{theorem}[Exact matching-glue identity and all proper-factor feasibility]
For \emph{every} $F\in\CM$ and every assignment $(v,x)\in\{0,1\}^{2k}$,
\[
G(F)(v,x)=1\iff
 \bigl(\forall S\in P:S\cap\{i:v_i=1\}\ne\varnothing\bigr)
 \land
 \bigl(\forall S\in Q:S\nsubseteq\{i:x_i=1\}\bigr)
 \land
 \{i:v_i=1\}\subseteq\{i:x_i=1\}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{\SAT(G(F))\iff\SAT(F).}
\]
Furthermore each of $A_F$, $B_F$, $I_k$, $A_F\wedge B_F$, $A_F\wedge I_k$, and $B_F\wedge I_k$ is satisfiable for \emph{every} $F$, including every UNSAT $F$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each positive ternary says its support meets $V_1=\{i:v_i=1\}$; each negative ternary says its support is not contained in $X_1=\{i:x_i=1\}$; an implication says $V_1\subseteq X_1$. A model $(v,x)$ of $G$ projects to the original assignment $x$: each positive source triple meets $V_1\subseteq X_1$ and each negative source triple has a zero $x$ coordinate, so $x\models F$. Conversely if $x\models F$, set $v=x$ to satisfy all three factors. For local and pairwise feasibility, use the following uniform explicit total assignments:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{subformula}&(v_1,\ldots,v_k;x_1,\ldots,x_k)\\
\hline
A_F\wedge B_F&(1^k;0^k)\\
A_F\wedge I_k&(1^k;1^k)\\
B_F\wedge I_k&(0^k;0^k)
\end{array}
\]
Each single factor is contained in a satisfiable pair. No oracle, global witness table, or SAT/UNSAT label is needed to construct these local witnesses.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[NP-complete canonical three-factor gluing; conditional algorithmic significance]

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A canonical image has $2k$ coordinates, source positive/negative ternaries of the prescribed two-regular support type, one identified matching implication per original variable, and the non-fully-paired source condition. Its grammar and recovered source $F$ are checked in polynomial time using sorting and explicit source identifiers. Its decision problem is in NP. If $F\in\CS$, emit its canonical $G(F)$; otherwise emit the fixed canonical image $G(U)$ of D\"ocker's publicly specified 198-variable UNSAT source $U\in\CS$. Hence $L_\star\le_m^p L_{\rm match}$. The local status statement is the preceding explicit-witness argument.
\end{proof}
