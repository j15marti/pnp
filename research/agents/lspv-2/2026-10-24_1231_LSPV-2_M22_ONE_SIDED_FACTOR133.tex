\section{Exact target, source accounting and falsifiable recovery hypotheses}
A $\CM$ formula is a duplicate-free CNF whose clauses each have three distinct original source variables, all positive or all negative, each active variable occurring exactly twice with each sign. Let $\bar C$ flip literal signs while retaining disjunction. Define
\[
\mathcal P_{\rm pair}=\{F\in\CM:(\forall C\in F)\ \bar C\in F\},\quad
\CS=\CM\setminus\mathcal P_{\rm pair},\quad
L_\star=\{\operatorname{enc}(F):F\in\CS,\ \SAT(F)\}.
\]
Invalid encodings are rejected. Partial pairs, disconnected formulas, easy examples and nonminimal UNSAT remain permitted. With $k$ original active variables, $m$ clauses and actual dense indexed bit length $N$,
\[
3m=4k,\quad m_+=m_-=\frac{2k}{3},\quad N=\Theta(k\log(k+2)).
\]
D\"ocker's Theorem~1 and fully-paired special case imply NP-completeness of $L_\star$ by the previously published polynomial M22-NONPAIRED1 replacement of fully paired SAT inputs by fixed $\CS$-SAT instance $Y$. This is a completeness adapter, \emph{not} an all-decider lower bound.

Three falsifiable hypotheses were selected from REVERSE-SELECTOR57, BLOSSOM-FACTOR60, M22-DEFECT-ADAPTER131 and INTERVAL-FACTOR134.
\begin{description}
\item[H1.] Selecting a TRUE witness only for each unmatched \emph{positive} triple leaves a residual general signed interval-cardinality instance in which every free source name has occurrence-degree at most two. \textbf{FILLED, restricted}, by the exact signed negative-occurrence argument below.
\item[H2.] Those branches give an exact $3^q$-call solver for $\CS$, instead of first assigning all $|D|$ variables with up to $2^{|D|}$ branches or choosing witnesses for all $2q$ unmatched clauses. \textbf{FILLED, restricted}, with a uniform factor constructor and complete branch semantics.
\item[H3.] This reduced branch count alone produces power-smaller \emph{ordinary encoded SAT children}, a simultaneous favorable exponent edge for all ordinary SAT, or a superpolynomial lower bound. \textbf{FALSIFIED} for the direct two-level pin/serialize constructor; arbitrary adaptive all-input closure remains \textbf{OPEN}.
\end{description}

\section{Existing canonical decomposition and new one-sided continuation}
Represent a positive or negative clause by its unsigned three-variable support. Let $P$ and $Q$ be the positive and negative support sets. Define
\[
H=P\cap Q,\qquad P'=P\setminus Q,\qquad Q'=Q\setminus P,\qquad
q=|P'|=|Q'|,\qquad D=\bigcup P'=\bigcup Q'.
\]
Here the equality of the final \emph{sets} follows from the equality of residual per-variable occurrence counts. For every $x$, $\deg_{P'}(x)=\deg_{Q'}(x)\le2$; consequently $q=0$ or $q\ge2$, and $\CS$ means $q\ge2$. This is \emph{previously proved} by DUALPAIR61 and M22-DEFECT-ADAPTER131, not new here. Each $S\in H$ contributes precisely the matched constraint
\[
\mathrm{NAE}(S):\quad1\le\sum_{x\in S}x\le2,
\]
equivalent to the conjunction of its positive and negative OR clauses.

\begin{theorem}[One-polarity residual degree bound; PROVED/EXACT within $\CM$]
Fix any $F\in\CM$ and any consistent choice of at least one TRUE source occurrence in \emph{each} of the $q$ positive unmatched clauses $P'$. Let $\pi$ be the resulting source assignment, assigning all selected source names value one. Discard those positive clauses and retain the matched $\mathrm{NAE}(S)$ constraints for $S\in H$ and the $q$ unpaired negative OR clauses for $Q'$, under the fixed pins $\pi$. Every unpinned source variable occurs in \emph{exactly two} residual constraint records, counted as one occurrence in a matched NAE record and one in a negative-unmatched OR record whenever appropriate. The resulting residual is a signed interval-cardinality SAT instance of source occurrence-degree at most two.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each $x$ has exactly two \emph{negative} source occurrences. A matched support containing $x$ contributes one negative literal occurrence in the original formula and one residual NAE record containing $x$. A negative-unmatched support containing $x$ contributes its original negative occurrence and one residual signed OR record containing $\neg x$. These are disjoint and exhaust the original negative clauses. Therefore the unpinned residual occurrence count is $\deg_H(x)+\deg_{Q'}(x)=\deg_Q(x)=2$. The positive-unmatched clauses are all satisfied by selected TRUE witnesses and can be discarded; every other original constraint is represented exactly. Fixed pins are substituted into the intervals with all original-source identities retained. No condition depends on an advance SAT/UNSAT label.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[One-sided factor continuation; PROVED/EXACT restricted]
There exists a \emph{single fixed uniform deterministic total decider} for valid $\CS$ instances with
\[
\boxed{T_{\CS}(N,q)=O(3^q\operatorname{poly}(N)),\qquad
 S_{\CS}(N,q)=\operatorname{poly}(N)\ {\rm simultaneously}.}
\]
More precisely, exact satisfiability is equivalent to the existence of one of at most $3^q$ choices of one source-TRUE occurrence in each $P'$ clause whose induced pinned signed degree-two interval instance has a feasible $(g,f)$ factor. Each accepted branch reconstructs a globally coherent original assignment; every rejected branch is exact. One can adaptively choose the cheaper previously known $2^{|D|}$ support-assignment method or ordinary $2^k$ enumeration, giving $O(\min\{3^q,2^{|D|},2^k\}\operatorname{poly}(N))$ time with polynomial space.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each $S\in P'$, choose one of its three named positive literals and require its Boolean source value be TRUE. These choices are always sign-consistent, although repeated names may occur. Thus at most $3^q$ branches result, with every $P'$ clause satisfied. If the original formula is SAT, choose one of its TRUE source literals in every positive unmatched clause. The resulting branch pins are compatible with the original satisfying assignment, and all remaining matched NAE and unpaired negative OR constraints hold. Thus a factor exists for that branch. Conversely, the source assignment reconstructed from a feasible degree-two factor plus its pins satisfies all matched positive/negative clauses, all unpaired negative clauses, and all selected positive witnesses, hence the original formula.

The source-degree-two signed interval-cardinality to $(g,f)$-factor theorem is already established in INTERVAL-FACTOR134, and polynomial $(g,f)$-factor feasibility is classical. It handles same-polarity double occurrences as a shared source edge, opposite-polarity doubles by a mandatory choice vertex, and singly occurring names by leaves. It neither evaluates a semantic decision for free nor assumes a bipartite support graph. A deterministic factor algorithm, source parser, support sorter, branch enumerator, constraint writer, pin substitute and witness checker are all polynomial bit-work and polynomial peak writable bits per branch. Stream branches sequentially and clear prior graph state. Include \emph{all} failed branches, selection, source reads, graph/witness reconstruction and full source replay. The optional three-way minimum rule reads the structural parameters and invokes one chosen method, and its charge is polynomial plus the chosen exponential continuation. Invalid encodings terminate after paid validation with NO.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Scope.} The bound becomes polynomial when $q=O(\log N)$, but canonical $\CS$ is NP-complete and does not impose such a global restriction. Even for $q=2$, an adaptive recognizer may choose an entirely different cheaper method; the bound is an upper bound, not a mandatory work budget. The mirror algorithm selects one FALSE source variable per unmatched \emph{negative} clause and retains paired NAE plus unmatched positive OR; it has the same bound.

\section{Direct post-acquisition lower bound and ordinary-SAT ARC compatibility}

\[
\Phi(C)-\Phi(C')\le\Gamma(N)\,\mathrm{charged\_bit\_work}(C,C')
\]
for every adaptive transition, plus a separately proved sufficiently large initial $\Phi$. Then and only then does telescoping prove post-acquisition work at least $\Phi_{\rm initial}/\Gamma$. ADAPTIVE-COVER75 bounds future distinct input reads by $\tau\le N$ and cannot be summed over visits or transferred to post-acquisition computation. PHASE-SANDWICH136 explains why an arbitrary decider may compute before its final input read.
