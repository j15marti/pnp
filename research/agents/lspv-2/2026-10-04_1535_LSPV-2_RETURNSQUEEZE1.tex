\section*{PRIMARY LITERATURE}
Primary literature was refreshed before the theorem construction.

Williams' STOC 2024 self-improvement theorem for Circuit-SAT shows that genuine iterative speedup phenomena exist when the algorithm can be represented sufficiently efficiently inside instances of the analysis problem. The paper explicitly asks whether self-improvement can extend toward the P versus NP regime and records bottlenecks below subexponential time. Its oracle version also shows that self-improvement as such need not be nonrelativizing.

Williams' alternation-trading framework gives the unconditional SAT time-space product lower bound
\[
t(n)s(n)=\Omega\!\left(n^{2\cos(\pi/7)-\varepsilon}\right)
\]
for polynomially bounded time and space, for every \(\varepsilon>0\). Buss--Williams characterize the ceiling of the then-current alternation-trading method in the subpolynomial-space regime.

Fortnow--Santhanam and Dell--van Melkebeek show that strong SAT compression/sparsification would imply major class collapses. Those results are conditional barriers to compression and are not terminal contradictions under the counterfactual \(P=NP\).

Allender--Kouck\'y show that self-reducibility can amplify restricted circuit lower bounds. This is a structural precedent, not a transfer to unrestricted SAT time.

\section*{DUPLICATION / CONTRADICTION AUDIT}
SQUEEZE1 already proposed a scalar recurrence \(K'\le a+bK\) with \(0<b<1\), and CONTRACT1 correctly falsified ordinary SAT downward self-reduction as the missing contraction: the standard search reduction has slope one and adds an exponent in the charged \(n\)-call form.

ENVELOPE1 and TOTALENV1 already give the verified reconstruction-time envelope for one fixed exact SAT decider exponent \(K\):
\[
D(K)=\max\{a,q+bK\},\qquad
R(K)=\ell+c_0K^2,\qquad
F(K)=\max\{f,c_FK\},
\]
\[
E(K)=\min\{D(K),R(K),F(K)\},
\qquad
H_T(K)=\max\{G,E(K)\}.
\]
The new point is to separate a contractive reconstruction dependence from the missing return-to-SAT map and to state the exact size condition under which their composition would contract.

\section*{Claim card}
\textbf{Target.} A repeatable SAT-to-reconstruction-to-SAT transformation whose time-space fixed point lies in a known unconditional forbidden region.

\textbf{Quantification.} For every exact deterministic SAT decider \(A\) in the declared random-access model with
\[
T_A(n)\le n^{K+o(1)},\qquad S_A(n)\le n^{\Sigma+o(1)},
\]
construct an exact SAT decider \(A'\) with uniformly controlled exponents \(K',\Sigma'\).

\textbf{SAME\_REALIZATION.} The same source-normalized reconstruction instance, compiler, query semantics, SAT decider, and return reduction must be used throughout one transformation step. Iteration requires the output decider \(A'\) to satisfy the same eligibility hypotheses.

\textbf{Falsification target.} Show that any proposed return bridge carries the full original SAT input into a downstream SAT query, or that generic computation-to-SAT encoding multiplies rather than contracts the inherited exponent.

\section*{PROVED/EXACT accounting: reconstruction has a candidate linear slope}
From TOTALENV1,
\[
H_T(K)=\max\{G,E(K)\}\le \max\{G,D(K)\}.
\]
Let
\[
A_D=\max\{G,a,q\}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{H_T(K)\le A_D+bK.}
\]
Likewise, with
\[
A_F=\max\{G,f\},
\]
\[
\boxed{H_T(K)\le A_F+c_FK.}
\]
Hence for either individually legal route \(i\in\{D,F\}\),
\[
\boxed{H_T(K)\le A_i+\beta_iK}
\]
with \((A_i,\beta_i)=(A_D,b)\) or \((A_F,c_F)\).

This is an upper-bound accounting statement. It becomes a contraction ingredient only if some source-certified route has \(\beta_i<1\). The actual vMS/SGL values of \(b\) and \(c_F\) remain OPEN.

\section*{PROVED/EXACT conditional theorem: fine-grained return bridge}
Fix a legal reconstruction route satisfying
\[
H_T(K)\le A+\beta K.
\]
Assume a uniform return reduction from SAT inputs \(x\) of length \(n\) to reconstruction instances of length
\[
N=n^{\lambda+o(1)}
\]
such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item nonquery return-reduction work is at most \(n^{r+o(1)}\);
\item the reduction makes at most \(n^{\eta+o(1)}\) sequential calls to the reconstruction solver;
\item the answer to SAT is recovered exactly from those calls; and
\item the same constants \(A,\beta,\lambda,r,\eta\) apply after each transformed decider, with all construction and writing costs charged.
\end{enumerate}
Then the resulting exact SAT decider has exponent
\[
K'\le
\max\{r,\eta+\lambda H_T(K)\}
\le
\max\{r,\eta+\lambda A+\lambda\beta K\}.
\]
Writing
\[
C_T=\max\{r,\eta+\lambda A\},
\]
we obtain
\[
\boxed{K'\le C_T+\lambda\beta K.}
\]
Therefore the composite transformation is contractive in the scalar exponent exactly when
\[
\boxed{\lambda\beta<1.}
\]
If it is repeatable, then
\[
\boxed{
K_j\le
(\lambda\beta)^jK_0+
C_T\frac{1-(\lambda\beta)^j}{1-\lambda\beta}
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{j\to\infty}K_j
\le
K_\star:=\frac{C_T}{1-\lambda\beta}.
}
\]

The proof is direct substitution and the geometric-series recurrence. Status: \textbf{PROVED/EXACT as conditional exponent accounting}. The existence of the return reduction is \textbf{OPEN}.

\section*{PROVED/EXACT obstruction: full-input carry kills contraction}
Define a return bridge to be \emph{full-input carrying} if, on infinitely many \(n\), at least one inherited SAT query produced in the chosen reconstruction route has explicit bit length
\[
n^{1-o(1)}.
\]
Suppose the reconstruction-instance length is
\[
N=n^{\lambda+o(1)}
\]
and every inherited SAT query has length at most
\[
N^{b+o(1)}.
\]
Then full-input carry implies
\[
n^{1-o(1)}
\le
N^{b+o(1)}
=
n^{\lambda b+o(1)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{\lambda b\ge1.}
\]
Consequently the direct-route coefficient \(\lambda b\) cannot be contractive.

Thus any direct-route Boundary-Squeeze with \(\lambda b<1\) must avoid carrying an explicit linear-size copy of the original SAT input into any inherited SAT query. It needs a genuinely finer self-embedding, decomposition, or source-specific representation theorem. Merely renaming, padding, or wrapping the full original formula cannot supply the contraction.

This theorem is purely an input-size obstruction. It does not prove that every non-full-input-carrying bridge is impossible.

\section*{FALSIFIED/RETIRED return candidate: generic computation-to-SAT self-encoding}
Suppose the only return from a reconstruction algorithm \(B_A\) of runtime
\[
N^{H_T(K)+o(1)}
\]
to SAT is a model-matched Cook--Levin/Robson-style encoding whose SAT-instance length is
\[
N^{H_T(K)+o(1)}
\]
up to polylogarithmic factors. Running the same SAT decider \(A\) on that encoding costs
\[
N^{K\,H_T(K)+o(1)}.
\]
For \(K\ge1\) and any nontrivial polynomial-time reconstruction exponent \(H_T(K)\ge1\), this route has inherited exponent at least \(K\). It does not yield a strict contraction of \(K\).

Accordingly, \textbf{generic encode-the-improved-computation and call \(A\) again is FALSIFIED/RETIRED as the missing contraction mechanism}. This does not rule out a problem-specific self-embedding whose instance size is substantially smaller than the full computation encoding.

\section*{PROVED/EXACT conditional space envelope for the direct route}
Now keep the same exact SAT decider with space exponent \(\Sigma\):
\[
S_A(s)\le s^{\Sigma+o(1)}.
\]
Assume the direct reconstruction oracle computation has nonquery workspace
\[
N^{u+o(1)}
\]
and explicitly writes SAT queries of maximum length
\[
N^{b+o(1)}.
\]
Sequential SAT calls do not sum their workspaces. A call to \(A\) on a query of length \(N^{b+o(1)}\) uses
\[
N^{b\Sigma+o(1)}
\]
space. Including an explicit query buffer and a common-space floor \(G_S\), the reconstruction space exponent obeys
\[
\boxed{
H_S(\Sigma)
\le
\max\{G_S,u,b,b\Sigma\}.
}
\]
Let
\[
C_S=\max\{G_S,u,b\}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
H_S(\Sigma)\le\max\{C_S,b\Sigma\}.
}
\]

If the return reduction uses \(n^{r_S+o(1)}\) workspace and scales instance length by \(N=n^{\lambda+o(1)}\), the transformed SAT decider satisfies
\[
\Sigma'
\le
\max\{r_S,\lambda C_S,\lambda b\Sigma\}.
\]
Let
\[
C'_S=\max\{r_S,\lambda C_S\}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\Sigma'\le\max\{C'_S,\lambda b\Sigma\}.
}
\]
If \(\lambda b<1\) and the transformation is repeatable,
\[
\boxed{
\limsup_{j\to\infty}\Sigma_j\le C'_S.
}
\]
Again, this is conditional resource accounting, not a constructed SAT transformation.

\section*{Conditional collision with the Williams time-space frontier}
Williams proves that for polynomially bounded SAT time \(t\) and space \(s\),
\[
t(n)s(n)=
\Omega\!\left(
n^{c_W-\varepsilon}
\right)
\qquad
\forall\varepsilon>0,
\]
where
\[
c_W=2\cos(\pi/7).
\]
Therefore an exact SAT algorithm with exponent pair \((K,\Sigma)\) cannot satisfy
\[
K+\Sigma<c_W.
\]

Under the fine-grained return-bridge hypotheses above, a sufficient contradiction condition is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{C_T}{1-\lambda\beta}+C'_S<c_W.
}
\]
This is \textbf{CONDITIONAL}. None of \(C_T,\lambda,\beta,C'_S\) has yet been source-certified so as to satisfy it.

In the idealized one-call, size-preserving, subpolynomial-space-floor case
\[
\lambda=1,\qquad C_T=1,\qquad C'_S=0,
\]
the criterion becomes
\[
\frac{1}{1-\beta}<2\cos(\pi/7),
\]
or equivalently
\[
\boxed{
\beta<
1-\frac{1}{2\cos(\pi/7)}.
}
\]
This exact expression is the correct screening threshold for that idealized normalization. It is an example condition, not a source-derived vMS parameter.

\section*{Literature transfer classification}
\textbf{KNOWN THEOREM.}
Williams 2024 proves self-improvement for Circuit-SAT/\#Circuit-SAT/Q-Circuit-SAT in large-circuit regimes and explicitly identifies efficient self-modeling inside problem instances as central. Williams 2010 gives the SAT time-space product lower bound above. Buss--Williams give limitations of the existing alternation-trading framework. Fortnow--Santhanam and Dell--van Melkebeek give conditional SAT compression/sparsification barriers. Allender--Kouck\'y amplify restricted circuit lower bounds via self-reducibility. Robson gives near-linear-overhead RAM-to-SAT computation encoding in the compatible model.

\textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE.}
The Williams 2024 OR/self-embedding trick is the closest positive analogue for a return bridge, but its present theorem lives in an exponential/large-circuit parameter regime and the paper itself records bottlenecks toward the P-versus-NP regime. A source-specific LSPV return bridge remains a new theorem.

\textbf{FAILED TRANSFER.}
Conditional kernelization/compression lower bounds cannot be used as the terminal contradiction under the assumption \(P=NP\), since the associated PH-collapse consequences are then compatible with the counterfactual. Generic self-reduction and generic computation-to-SAT encoding do not provide the needed contraction.

\section*{Hostile-control and barrier audit}
\textbf{DEAD/COPY/DELAY.} Padding or duplicated representation can change \(\lambda\) and \(b\) without changing semantics. The meaningful quantity is the composite coefficient \(\lambda\beta\), measured after normalization. Artificially inflating the intermediate instance to make \(b\) appear small is invalid.

\textbf{INTERP/RECOMP.} A solver may use an alternative representation or recompute on demand. The return bridge must be representation-robust or explicitly charge the chosen representation.

\textbf{Horn/2-SAT/affine/treewidth.} Easy families can admit sublinear decompositions or compact state. Any proposed \(\beta\)-based hardness reading must distinguish those controls or remain only an upper-bound transformation theorem.

\textbf{Semantic/algebraic oracles.} The recurrence algebra, input-carry obstruction, and Williams-style self-improvement analogy do not by themselves cross relativization or algebrization. Williams' self-improvement theorem itself has an oracle formulation. Therefore the needed nonrelativizing/nonalgebrizing hinge must occur in a source/solver-obligation or return-bridge line that fails under sufficiently strong oracle replacement. That hinge remains OPEN.

\textbf{Natural Proofs.} No constructive large circuit property is asserted here, so the Natural Proofs barrier is not the active obstruction for these accounting lemmas.

\section*{STALE OR OVERSTATED CLAIMS FOUND}
Any statement that \(b<1\) alone yields a SAT self-improvement theorem is overstated. The correct composite condition is \(\lambda\beta<1\), together with a return reduction and repeatability. Any statement that a small \(b\) obtained only by padding the intermediate size \(N\) is meaningful is also overstated; full-input carry forces \(\lambda b\ge1\).
