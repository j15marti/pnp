\section{Fixed-$B$ residual-entry predicate}
Let $F=\bigwedge_j C_j$ be proper signed CNF and $B$ a fixed set of \emph{original} Boolean variables. For each original source index $j$, set $D_j=C_j\cap \mathrm{Lit}(B)$ and $R_j=C_j\setminus D_j$. Let $\beta$ be one coherent assignment to all source occurrences of $B$ (never independent occurrence assignments). Define
\[
P_{B,H}=\bigwedge_{j:\#\mathrm{pos}(R_j)>1}D_j,\quad
P_{B,D}=\bigwedge_{j:\#\mathrm{neg}(R_j)>1}D_j,\quad
P_{B,K}=\bigwedge_{j:|R_j|>2}D_j.
\tag{1}
\]
Every $D_j$ is a signed clause over $B$, possibly empty, retaining source-parent label $j$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact syntactic projection, PROVED/EXACT restricted]
For all $F,B,\beta$ as above and $\mathcal C$ equal to Horn, dual-Horn or Krom, $F[\beta]\in\mathcal C$ if and only if $\beta\models P_{B,\mathcal C}$. This determines only entry into the tractable syntactic class, not satisfiability of $F$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A source clause disappears exactly when some literal in $D_j$ is true under $\beta$. Otherwise the surviving clause is exactly $R_j$. It violates the desired class precisely when its unassigned positive, negative, or total literal count exceeds the class threshold. Thus every violating $R_j$ must be deleted by satisfying $D_j$, which is exactly (1). The converse is identical.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp fixed-candidate entry classification, PROVED/EXACT restricted]
Given $(F,B)$, deciding whether some $\beta$ enters Horn or dual-Horn is in linear indexed-word RAM time for maximum source width four, but NP-complete for maximum source width five. Krom entry is linear at width five and NP-complete at width six. Source-list acquisition and all memory are charged; search for $B$ is not included.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An offending outside-$B$ residual has at least two literals for Horn/dual-Horn and at least three for Krom. Thus at the stated tractable widths all included $D_j$ have length at most two. Formula (1) is explicit source-indexed 2CNF. SCC decides it, returning a full $\beta$ or paths $x\leadsto\neg x$ and $\neg x\leadsto x$ labeled by source clauses. For NP-hardness take any proper three-clause CNF $H=\bigwedge_j D_j$ on variables $B$ and fresh $p,q,r$ outside $B$. The width-five source $\bigwedge_j(p\vee q\vee D_j)$ has $P_{B,Horn}=H$ exactly, although the parent is SAT by $p=1$. For dual-Horn replace $p,q$ with $\neg p,\neg q$; the parent is SAT by $p=q=0$. For width-six Krom use $\bigwedge_j(p\vee q\vee r\vee D_j)$, whose Krom-entry projection is exactly $H$ and whose parent is SAT by $p=1$. All clauses remain proper; membership is in NP by guessing $\beta$. This is NP-completeness of a \emph{specific policy-discovery problem}, not a SAT lower bound for those easy parents.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Unsound branch promotion counterexample.}

Hence an UNSAT terminal \emph{cofactor} cannot certify original UNSAT. In $(x\vee y\vee z)\land(x\vee y\vee\neg z)$, candidate $B=\{z\}$ cannot enter Horn because its policy projection is $(z)\land(\neg z)$, yet the original is SAT. A four-clause width-five reduction induces a source-tagged 2SAT implication contradiction, again while the parent is trivially SAT.
