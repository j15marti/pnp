\section{Proof obligation, model and preregistered falsifiers}
The ultimate ARC set $E$ contains $(t,s)$ only when one \emph{fixed},
\emph{uniform}, \emph{total}, \emph{correct} deterministic ordinary-SAT
decider on all encoded inputs of physical length $N$ simultaneously
runs in $N^{t+o(1)}$ time and $N^{s+o(1)}$ writable/index space in the
indexed, read-only-input, random-access Turing-machine model of
Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas~\cite{flmv,vm}.  Under $P=NP$
some unknown finite polynomial diagonal is available, but the separate
$d=1.802$, $s=o(1)$ seed is not supplied.  We study only a fixed
\emph{witness verifier's} combinational circuit and its proper-CNF
compiler.  This does not decide SAT on all $N$-bit inputs.

Four testable routes were stipulated before reviewing primary papers and
repository code during this run:
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{H1.} Nonfinal forward record-output OR gates have
algebraically calculable signed direct consumers, with no writable
incidence table.  \textbf{PROVED/EXACT restricted}.
\item[] \textbf{H2.} The direct-consumer map yields actual proper
13-row CNF variable-occurrence Rank/Select, respecting gate input
clone polarities and physical row order. \textbf{PROVED/EXACT restricted}.
\item[] \textbf{H3.} The output side of one comparator remains the
input side at its consumer comparator. \textbf{FALSIFIED} already for
$K=4,w=2$; a corrected next-stride calculation repairs H1.
\item[] \textbf{H4.} The virtual two-level signed regularization could
by itself make ordinary-SAT children $N^{\lambda}$ bits,
$\lambda<1$. \textbf{FALSIFIED for the published explicit architecture}
by the independently computed output floor~\cite{floor131}; no new

\section{Exact comparator netlist and indexed location arithmetic}
Fix $w\ge2$ and $K=2^h\ge4$.  In the original
\texttt{CIRCUIT-OBLIVIOUS-INVERSE-SORT166} program, each record has
$D=w+3$ wires: $w$ identifier bits, two kind bits and one payload.
Every forward comparator consists of
\[
 H=7(w+2)\quad\text{comparison gates followed by}\quad
 2D\text{ three-gate MUXs},\qquad
 F=H+6D=13w+32.
\]
The bitonic network has $J=h(h+1)/2$ comparator layers, each
with $K/2$ pairs and $F$ gates per pair.  Layer $\ell$ has the
standard fixed pair stride $s_\ell=2^{b_\ell}$ and network
size $z_\ell=2^{a_\ell}$; the order is
$a=1,\ldots,h$ and, within each $a$, $b=a-1,\ldots,0$.
These parameters are computed from $\ell$ by at most $O(h)$
short-integer iterations.  Pair rank $r\in[0,K/2)$ has
\[
 i_\ell(r)=2s_\ell\left\lfloor\frac{r}{s_\ell}\right\rfloor
              +(r\bmod s_\ell),\qquad
 j_\ell(r)=i_\ell(r)+s_\ell.                              \tag{1}
\]
The global Boolean-gate index of that pair's first gate is
\[
 b_{\ell,r}= \ell(K/2)F+rF.                               \tag{2}
\]
The comparator's forward output field $u\in[0,2D)$ has
\emph{output} gate
\[
 g_{\ell,r,u}=b_{\ell,r}+H+3u+2,                          \tag{3}
\]
an OR gate.  Its current side is
$\delta=\lfloor u/D\rfloor$, its record coordinate is
$c=u\bmod D$, and its physical output record position is
\[
 p=i_\ell(r)+\delta s_\ell.                              \tag{4}
\]
For $\ell<J-1$, let the \emph{next} layer have stride
$s'=s_{\ell+1}$ and size $z'=z_{\ell+1}$.  Compute
\[
 i'=p\mathbin{\&}\neg s',\quad
 r'=s'\left\lfloor\frac{i'}{2s'}\right\rfloor+(i'\bmod s'),
 \quad b'=(\ell+1)(K/2)F+r'F,                           \tag{5}
\]
\[
 \epsilon=[p\mathbin{\&}s'\ne0],
 \qquad \mathrm{up}=[i'\mathbin{\&}z'=0].                 \tag{6}
\]
The bit mask $\neg s'$ in (5) clears only the stride bit of the
$O(\log K)$-bit index. Crucially, $\epsilon$ is the \emph{next
comparator input side}, and is not generally $\delta$.

\section{Exact direct-consumer theorem}
The comparison's compound key is
$(\mathrm{kind}_0,\mathrm{kind}_1,
\mathrm{id}_0,\ldots,\mathrm{id}_{w-1})$ in little-endian
low-to-high order. For record coordinate $c<w+2$ its
comparator iteration index is
\[
 t(c)=\begin{cases}w-1-c,&c<w,\\2w+1-c,&w\le c<w+2.\end{cases}    \tag{7}
\]
In iteration $t$, the seven gates local to the \emph{next}
comparator are the two prefix-update terms, the less OR,
two XOR ANDs, its OR, and its equality AND.  The three
direct occurrences of the record bit in comparison are:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccc}
 \text{role}&(7t+0)&(7t+3)&(7t+4)\\ \hline
 x&(0,-)&(0,+)&(0,-)\\
 y&(1,+)&(1,-)&(1,+)
 \end{array}                                              \tag{8}
\]
where $(0,\pm)$ means signed operand slot zero and $(1,\pm)$
slot one. If the next comparator is ascending,
$x$ is its upper input record; if descending, $x$ is its lower
record. Precisely, the current field uses row $x$ of (8) iff
$\epsilon=\mathrm{up}$; otherwise it uses row $y$.

The two direct uses in the next comparator's forward MUXs are:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
\epsilon=0 &(H+3c+0,1,+)&(H+3(D+c)+1,1,+)\\
\epsilon=1 &(H+3c+1,1,+)&(H+3(D+c)+0,1,+)
\end{array},                                               \tag{9}
\]
where every gate index in (8)--(9) is augmented by $b'$.
Equation (9) holds for all $c<D$; comparison rows (8) occur only
for $c<w+2$.  Sort these at most five triples by global consumer
gate index and input slot.  Call the resulting list $U(g)$.

\begin{theorem}[Forward nonfinal record-output consumer selector;
\textbf{PROVED/EXACT, restricted}]\label{th:sel}
For every $h,w,\ell,r,u$ as above with $\ell<J-1$, the
signed direct consumers of the Boolean gate
$g_{\ell,r,u}$, in physical gate-input order, are
\emph{exactly} $U(g)$ computed by (1)--(9).
For an identifier or kind field the direct fanout is exactly
five; for a payload field exactly two. A fixed uniform
bit-address program computes $\Select_{\rm gate}(g,j)$,
its inverse $\Rank_{\rm gate}(g,a,k)$, and the degree using
$O(\log^3 G)$ elementary bit operations and
$O(\log G)$ total work/index bits, where $G$ is the
total comparator-prefix gate count.  No stored consumer table,
prior full gate traversal, or input-dependent source sorting
is needed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At every bitonic layer, each record position participates in
exactly one comparator, with the pairing (1).  Its output
record position (4) enters precisely one comparator in the
next layer, whose low partner, rank and direction are (5)--(6).
The exact source emitter constructs the seven-gate
less/equality block in the order
\[
 q_0=(-x)\wedge y,\quad q_1=e\wedge q_0,\quad
 q_2=\ell\vee q_1,\quad
 q_3=x\wedge(-y),\quad q_4=(-x)\wedge y,\quad
 q_5=q_3\vee q_4,\quad q_6=e\wedge(-q_5).
\]
Thus precisely the three occurrences in (8) apply per
key field.  It then constructs
$\operatorname{MUX}(\sigma,A_c,B_c)$ and
$\operatorname{MUX}(\sigma,B_c,A_c)$ as
$\operatorname{OR}(\operatorname{AND}(-\sigma,\cdot),
\operatorname{AND}(\sigma,\cdot))$. These create exactly the
two occurrences in (9), with no additional use before the
scan. The distinct gate positions exhaust all direct
references to the output wire.

Recovering layer parameters by scanning the $O(h)$
triangular stage indices uses $O(\log K)$-bit counters.
Schoolbook multiplication and division on these counters
take $O(\log^2 G)$ bit operations, so a conservative
$O(\log^3G)$ bound covers location, signs, sorted constant
list, Rank and Select.  Only a constant number of such
integers are live, giving $O(\log G)$ bit workspace,
including source-index address and control registers.
This accounts for negative operands; it is not a gate-count
claim about the entire circuit's evaluation.
\end{proof}

\section{Actual thirteen-clause proper-3CNF occurrence theorem}
Each Boolean AND/OR gate has three distinct logical source
names $u,v,y$ and private padding variables in the
project's thirteen-row compiler.  Its output source variable
$y$ occurs in five rows of its own block. For an OR gate,
these are the negative row $(13g+8,2,-)$ and four
positive rows $(13g+9,\ldots,13g+12,1,+)$. Every
signed reference of $y$ in operand slot $k\in\{0,1\}$
of a downstream gate $a$ contributes exactly four
clone-equality rows, with middle-literal polarities
$(\tau,\tau,-\tau,-\tau)$, where
$\tau$ is the sign of that reference:
\[
 (13a+4k+j,\;1,\;
     \begin{cases}\tau,&j<2\\-\tau,&j\ge2\end{cases}),
 \quad j=0,1,2,3.                                      \tag{10}
\]
All gate outputs are assigned distinct module identifiers.
No aliasing of circuit operands breaks source properness,
because fresh operand-clone variables are used.

\begin{theorem}[Source occurrence Rank/Select;
\textbf{PROVED/EXACT, restricted}]\label{th:cnf}
Let $v_g$ be the physical compiler output variable for a
gate satisfying Theorem~\ref{th:sel}.  Its exact ordered
signed proper-CNF occurrence list consists of its five
own-block rows followed by the four clone rows (10)
for every element of $U(g)$, sorted by absolute clause
number and field. Thus
\[
 \#\operatorname{Occ}(v_g)=
 \begin{cases}25,&c<w+2,\\13,&c=w+2,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 \#(+v_g)=4+2f,\quad\#(-v_g)=1+2f,\quad
 f\in\{5,2\}.                                          \tag{11}
\]
The $\operatorname{OccSelect}(v_g,r)$ and
$\operatorname{OccRank}(v_g,c,k)$ source indices, clauses,
literal fields and polarities are computable uniformly
in $O(\log^3G)$ bit operations and $O(\log G)$ peak
writable/index bits. A fixed-width physical output-bit
position is then recovered by arithmetic from the
source header length and 3-literal field width.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The compiler's padded output relation gives exactly
one negative and four positive own occurrences.
An upstream source output name is never used as a
padding or clone variable elsewhere; its other uses
are precisely signed operand references in the Boolean
DAG. Each reference appears twice positively and twice
negatively in the two copy-equivalence binary clauses
widened by private padding names, independently of
operand sign. Theorem~\ref{th:sel} exhausts all such
references, and $g<a$ for every consumer gate $a$,
so the prescribed physical clause order is immediate.
Every list has at most 25 entries; arithmetic (10)
supplies an explicit inverse without any materialized
occurrence table. The fixed-width header and field
arithmetic requires at most additional polylogarithmic
bit work and logarithmic address storage. The theorem
does not include the unresolved rank/select of arbitrary
source variables such as constants or the R15 transport.
\end{proof}

\section{Smallest counterexample to a tempting incorrect simplification}
\begin{counterexample}[\textbf{FALSIFIED: old-side equals new-side}]
Let $w=2$, $K=4$, current layer $\ell=0$, pair rank
$r=0$, record-output field $u=5$ (upper record's
first identifier bit). The current stride is $s=1$,
giving $\delta=1$ and physical position $p=1$.
The \emph{next} layer has $s'=2$, so its input side
is $\epsilon=0$, not $1$. The correct five direct
consumer triples (global gate, operand slot, sign) are
\[
 (181,1,+),\ (184,1,-),\ (185,1,+),\
 (202,1,+),\ (218,1,+).
\]
The incorrect side-preservation rule predicts instead
\[
 (181,0,-),\ (184,0,+),\ (185,0,-),\
 (203,1,+),\ (217,1,+).
\]
Both lists are computed on the same exact emitted netlist.
The corrected (5)--(9) matches the actual list.
\end{counterexample}
This is an exact program-level falsifier and a local repair,
not a SAT-complexity separation.

\section{Charged resource ledger and proof-type firewall}
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{Input and direct query.} The fixed-record
source of report 166 has
$B=m[1+3(w+1)]$ physical bits,
$G(q,m)=O(B\log^3 B)$ \emph{witness-verifier} gates
for $q\le B$. The theorem above takes arithmetic
gate and source-variable IDs and performs $O(\log^3G)$
charged bit operations plus $O(\log G)$ combined live
work/index bits per \emph{nonfinal} Select or Rank
request. Registers, addresses, bit shifts/divisions,
signed slots and emitted output bits are all charged.
\item[] \textbf{Whole circuit.} Reconstructing and writing
all $G$ Boolean gate records costs at least their
physical encoded length in time and, if stored, live
space. Repeated virtual source queries multiply the
per-query overhead. A gate-count upper bound gives
neither a small-space evaluator nor full decision-circuit
compression. Comparator swaps and scan carry remain
data-dependent Boolean \emph{values}, despite the
fixed source topology.
\item[] \textbf{Signed transport.} For a proper signed
source with $n_0$ names and $m_0$ clauses, the exact
claimed $Q_{15}/J$ regularizer gives after two levels
$m_2=4048m_0+5888n_0$. The peer's independent
physical-size floor is $m_2\ge33856B$ in the
particular full fixed-pin architecture. These are
expansions, not $B^\lambda$ children with
$\lambda<1$~\cite{floor131}. A source-bit change
affecting 16 output polarities does not bound a guest
decider's unchanged-position reads.
\item[] \textbf{Unsolved links.} This result does not
give efficient inverse consumers for the last forward
layer into the scan, reversed one-bit MUX routing,
high-fanout source constants/alias bits, the full
gate checker, or source incidence in the virtual R15
regularizer. It does not normalize arbitrary
variable-width or malformed ordinary SAT source strings.
\item[] \textbf{Decision versus verifier.}
$V_B(x,z)$ computing whether $z$ satisfies the
formula is not a full deterministic SAT decision
circuit $C_{A,N}(x)$ for \emph{every} $N$-bit
ordinary source. Eliminating the existential
witness or compressing a single gate trace is
a separate unproved transformation. In particular,
no $q=1.29$ instance-specific relation is promoted
to a small full SAT decision circuit.
\end{description}
The Williams square-root-space simulation for
\emph{multitape} time~\cite{williams} and
Pippenger--Fischer's circuit compilation for
one-dimensional tapes~\cite{pf} have distinct
input/model hypotheses. Bathie--Williams'
\emph{DeMorgan depth} results~\cite{bw} are not
general gate-count bounds for an arbitrary full
SAT decision family. None eliminates the
input-index, output-construction and guest-query
charges required for an ARC transition.
