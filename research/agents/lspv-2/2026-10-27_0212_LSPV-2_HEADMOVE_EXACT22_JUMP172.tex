\section{Quantifiers, four pre-search falsifiers and primary model}
Fix one deterministic algorithm and one encoding, and require correctness on
all canonical images of the declared family; do not take the time from one
algorithm and space or head reversals from another.  The following
hypotheses were written before consulting the new primary literature and
project HEADMOVE-1 handoff:
\begin{description}
\item[] H1: the 20-row W1 fixed-support exact22 DISJ gadget has complete,
physically ordered, locally indexed $\Rank/\Select$ without a stored
occurrence map. Test malformed polarity pairs and sparse-ID positions.
\item[] H2: a separate-input-head, one-work-tape equality \emph{circuit}
can be simulated transition for transition on a shared read/write tape
with input-inclusive physical footprint; reject any uncharged end-to-start
head teleportation.
\item[] H3: expand every bounded input jump without introducing an input-head
\emph{reversal}; transfer both the transport and reversal forbidden faces
using the complete-work crossing theorem, paying offset counters.
\item[] H4: reject the inference ``locally indexable signed exact22 source
$\Rightarrow$ a power-smaller ordinary SAT child or a full SAT decision
circuit''.  Count all physical gate and output widths.
\end{description}
The experiment adopts the project's \emph{signed} proper exact-$(2,2)$
family. Every variable occurs in exactly two positive and two negative
literals, and each clause has three distinct names. Clause-monotone
exact-$(2,2)$ is an additional restrictive family and is \emph{not}
asserted here.

\section{Reconstructed source with explicit bit-length and locality}
Take the exact 15-variable, 20-clause UNSAT source $W_1$ verbatim from
\path{experiments/review/early22_compiler_redteam_audit.py}.  Using
zero-based clause/literal positions, the two independent polarity controls
flip precisely
\[
 A=((6,0),(11,0))\quad\text{and}\quad
 B=((17,2),(9,2)).                                             \tag{1}
\]
Both pairs refer to one variable each, respectively variables 4 and 15,
with one initially positive and one negative occurrence. Put the unflipped
pair at control value $1$; flip the two signs for value $0$. Call the
20-clause output $H(a,b)$. Direct exhaustive enumeration of its
$2^{15}$ assignments gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
(a,b)&(0,0)&(0,1)&(1,0)&(1,1)\\\hline
\#\mathrm{models}&772&272&272&0.
\end{array}                                                       \tag{2}
\]
Flipping a sign pair leaves every variable quota and every three-name
support unchanged; (2) therefore gives the exact constant-sized semantic
identity
\[
                 \SAT(H(a,b))\iff \neg(a\land b).            \tag{3}
\]
For $m\ge1$, use $m$ disjoint variable-renamed copies with clause order
consisting first of all $2m$ A clauses, next all $16m$ unmodified
clauses, last all $2m$ B clauses.  Denote the result by
$F_m(a,b)$. It has
\[
 V=15m,\quad C=20m,\quad
 \SAT(F_m(a,b))\iff \bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}\neg(a_i\land b_i).
                                                                        \tag{4}
\]
The proof of (4) is disjoint-conjunction semantics using (3), not a
hypothesis about general SAT algorithms. The middle $16m$ clauses
are independent of $a,b$.

For this report define a \emph{canonical physical binary clause-list
encoding} with a 32-bit fixed magic word, three $w$-bit unsigned fields
$(V,C,m)$, and $20m$ consecutive three-literal clauses. Each literal is
one sign bit followed by a $w$-bit variable ID, with
\[
 w=\lceil\log_2(20m+1)\rceil,\qquad
 N=32+3w+60m(w+1).                                               \tag{5}
\]
The sign at A's first clause and B's first clause is exactly the
respective control bit. Its indexed physical positions are
\begin{align*}
 p_A(i)&=32+3w+2i\cdot 3(w+1),\\
 p_B(i)&=32+3w+(18m+2i)\cdot 3(w+1)+2(w+1),
 \qquad 0\le i<m.                                           \tag{6}
\end{align*}
All other positions are either constant or the complement of a designated
control.  The two members of each sign-swap pair are checked together.
The middle physical gap contains $16m\cdot3(w+1)$ bits, asymptotically
$4N/5$ of the input. The number of source variables/clauses is thus
$\Theta(N/\log N)$. These are newly specified canonical bytes for
this \emph{promise image}, not a parser for every ordinary SAT encoding.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical input bit and occurrence selectors; restricted]\label{th:index}
There is one fixed deterministic bit-address program which, for every
$m\ge1$, every $a,b\in\{0,1\}^m$, every physical index $p<N$, every
source name $v\in[15m]$ and every $r\in\{0,1,2,3\}$,
correctly computes the physical output bit of $F_m(a,b)$ and the $r$th
\emph{physical clause-order} signed occurrence of $v$. It supports the
inverse $\Rank(v,c,k)$ including literal slot $k$, polarity and physical
bit address. Its source-dependent queries read at most the two control
sign positions (6) belonging to one component. For each request,
conservative bit-operation time is $O(\log^2 N)$ and total live
writable-plus-index storage $O(\log N)$ bits. No input-wide header table,
variable incidence list, or occurrence sorting table is stored.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $i=\lfloor(v-1)/15\rfloor$ and
$t=1+((v-1)\bmod15)$. The fixed 20-row W1 program is finite-control
read-only data. Visit those 20 rows and three fields, retaining only
those whose absolute variable equals $t$. Exactly four occur because W1
is exact signed $(2,2)$. Their global physical clause numbers are
$2i+\ell$ for the two A positions, $2m+16i+\ell$ for the sixteen
unmodified positions, and $18m+2i+\ell$ for the two B positions,
where the finite lookup $\ell$ is the position in its corresponding
fixed list. Read $a_i,b_i$ from (6) if the signed results are requested;
the source signs follow (1). Sort the four $O(\log N)$-bit pairs
$(\mathrm{clause},\mathrm{slot})$ and return rank $r$.
The inverse compares the four candidates with the supplied physical
position. Formula (5) converts the winning field to a bit address.
Only a constant number of $O(\log N)$-bit registers coexist.
Schoolbook integer arithmetic conservatively costs
$O(\log^2N)$ bit operations. This is not a theorem for arbitrary
ordinary SAT incidence arrays or for the remaining bitonic scan and
reverse MUX circuits.
\end{proof}

On this \emph{canonical promise}, a word-RAM algorithm of logarithmic
word width reads at most $2m$ controls, with $O(m)$ arithmetic/word
operations after header arithmetic, and $O(\log N)$ workspace.
An indexed bit-TM pays $O(\log N)$ to write each binary address,
so $O(m\operatorname{polylog}N)$ bit time suffices.
Full \emph{template membership}, including magic, headers, signed pairs,
all literal IDs, and malformed strings of the same length, is checked
by testing all $N$ bits against the virtual template, with
$O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)$ bit time and $O(\log N)$ live bits.
It rejects noncanonical sources but is not a full SAT decider on
noncanonical ordinary CNF strings.

\section{Two native sequential tape ledgers and a complete tiny circuit}
For $m$ Boolean equality-control pairs, a direct binary fanin-2 NAND
circuit uses exactly $5m$ gates for per-pair XNOR and $2(m-1)$ gates
for the AND chain:
\[
                         G_{\rm NAND}=7m-2.                 \tag{7}
\]
Its primary inputs are $2m$ wires, its gate outputs are $7m-2$
additional wires, and its gate operand-reference count is
$2(7m-2)$ with the repeated input to a NAND counted in both slots.
This is an \emph{equality promise circuit}, \underline{not}
$C_{A,N}$ deciding unrestricted length-$N$ SAT inputs.

An independent physical machine $M_k$ receives the compact control
string $a\#0^{2m+3}\#b\$ $ as a read-only sequential input and uses one
sequential work tape. It copies $a$ into work cells, reverses its work
head to the left marker, scans the middle gap, then compares the right
bits against the stored left bits. It writes one result bit.
On the valid compact inputs it computes the circuit (7). The compact
representation is separate from the canonical full $20m$-clause
encoding in (5); this small example validates a \emph{tape compiler},
not a full binary-CNF parsing simulation.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit conservative one-tape simulator]
Let $M_k$ be the displayed compact equality machine using input
$X=4m+6$ physical symbols, one read-only input head and one work head,
work footprint $S_k\le m+O(1)$ and time $T_k$. A single read/write
tape uses a fixed product alphabet to hold source symbols, work symbols,
and both movable head markers. Each simulated transition is implemented
by \emph{four charged full sweeps}: gather both marked symbols,
update work and negative-moving markers, update positive-moving
markers, and return the physical head to the first cell. If
$L=X+1$ includes the right blank sentinel, exact abstract counters are
\[
 T_1=4LT_k+1,\quad H_1=C_1=4(L-1)T_k,\quad P_1=L,\quad
 \mathrm{outputBits}=1.                                          \tag{8}
\]
This implements the same transition sequence and satisfies the general
conservative upper bounds
\[
 T_1=O(T_k(X+S_k+\log(T_k+2))),\qquad
 P_1=O(X+S_k+\log(T_k+2)).                                    \tag{9}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At the first pass, finite control retains the source symbol under the
marked input head and work symbol under the work marker. The machine's
finite transition rule yields the new state, two head directions and new
work symbol. The backward pass updates the marked work cell and shifts
left-moving marker bits one cell; the next forward pass shifts the
right-moving marker bits one cell. Thus precisely one cell is marked on
each track and both virtual heads move as directed. The fourth pass
\emph{must} return the physical head to the left end before the next
first pass. Every pass touches $L$ cells, crosses $L-1$ boundaries,
and has one finite-alphabet operation per cell. The additional terminal
result write occurs at physical cell zero. Equations (8) follow.
The source and work tracks stay within $L$ cells on the tested machine;
no input-inclusive footprint is deducted. The more general simulator
uses $O(X+S_k+\log T_k)$ visited tape cells, encoding the fixed work
heads as markers and rescanning the entire active segment for each
transition; (9) follows from the standard construction, not from a
Hennie--Stearns two-tape shortcut. The finite reference replay checks
both full track contents and both marker locations after every step.
\end{proof}

A tempting three-pass variant is \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}: it ends at
the rightmost physical cell and silently begins its next pass at the
leftmost cell. The fourth return sweep is essential and was added
before publication. Machine-space, time, probes, cell writes and head
crossings are separately measured; probes/crossings are
\emph{subcounters of time}, not additional time charges.

\section{A reversal-preserving bounded-jump model transfer}
Consider a \emph{different} model $M_J$ with exactly one two-way
read-only \emph{input} head and fixed $k$ sequential work tapes.
An input-head command gives a signed binary displacement $d$ with
$|d|\le J(N)$, reads only the target input symbol, and pays for
writing and interpreting the offset. The work tapes have no indexed
reads. Let $T_J,S_J,R_J$ be simultaneous worst-case bounds on one
fixed total deterministic solver and define $R_J$ to count sign
changes between consecutive \emph{nonzero} input displacements.

\begin{lemma}[Jump unrolling preserves input reversals; exact]
For every such $M_J$ and every fixed input, a one-sequential-input-head,
fixed-work-tape simulator can produce exactly the same output while
using
\[
 H_0=\sum_{\text{jump commands }u}|d_u|\le JT_J,
 \quad R_0=R_J,\quad
 S_0\le S_J+O(\log(N+T_J+J)),                               \tag{10}
\]
with conservative time
$T_0=O(T_J(J+1)\operatorname{polylog}(N+T_J+J))$.
These inequalities hold jointly on the \emph{same simulated input}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Store $|d|$ and its sign in an auxiliary sequential counter tape,
using $O(\log(J+1))$ cells. Move the ordinary input head exactly
$|d|$ unit steps in that sign direction, ignoring intermediate read
symbols. Process the original transition's work-tape updates and
read only the final target symbol in the source simulated control;
offset setup and decrement operations are charged. Every nonzero
command becomes a constant-sign block of unit moves, hence the
nonzero direction sequence and its reversal count are identical.
The work tapes are all sequential, and the auxiliary tape adds one
fixed head, not a number of heads growing with input size. A
straight binary decrement has $O(\log N)$ worst-case overhead per
unit movement, yielding the displayed conservative time bound.
The derived head-distance, reversal and space claims need no faster
simulation assumption. There is no free RAM input probe in $M_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[New conditional jump and reversal surfaces]
Assume the exact one-input-head HEADMOVE-1 crossing theorem for the
canonical family (4), proved with complete visited-work-cell
configurations and the same input selected by double averaging. If
$M_J$ correctly decides all $F_m(a,b)$ for every $m$ and $a,b$,
then, with $N$ from (5),
\begin{align*}
 T_J J\bigl(S_J+\log(N+T_J+J)+1\bigr)
     &=\Omega(N^2/\log N),\\
 (R_J+1)\bigl(S_J+\log(N+T_J+J)+1\bigr)
     &=\Omega(N/\log N).                                  \tag{11}
\end{align*}
For polynomial worst-case ceilings
$T_J=N^{t+o(1)}, S_J=N^{s+o(1)},J=N^{j+o(1)}$ and
$R_J+1=N^{r+o(1)}$, this implies
\[
                 \boxed{t+s+j\ge2,\qquad r+s\ge1}.         \tag{12}
\]
Here $s,j,r\ge0$. This theorem is \textbf{CONDITIONAL}
on the separately declared source crossing theorem; it has
no implication for unrestricted indexed-input RAM or for P versus NP.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
HEADMOVE-1, for one sequential input head, supplies on one common
SAT instance $F_m(a,\neg a)$ the two lower bounds
\[
 H_0(S_0+1)=\Omega(N^2/\log N),\qquad
 (R_0+1)(S_0+1)=\Omega(N/\log N).
\]
For clarity, its common-input quantifier follows by fixing any of
the $\Theta(N)$ middle-gap cuts, injecting the $2^m$ distinct YES
instances into complete crossing transcripts, averaging the
$\Omega(m/(S_0+1))$ crossings at each cut over all $a$, summing over
cuts and selecting one $a_*$ of large aggregate movement. For that
\emph{same} input some cut has at least the average number of
crossings; alternating directions give the reversal inequality.
Insert (10), enlarging the $S_0$ factor only by the charged
$O(\log(N+T_J+J))$ counter space and bounding $H_0$ above by
$JT_J$. This yields (11). Polylogarithmic factors vanish at the
exponent level, yielding (12). No lower bound from a
\emph{one shared writable tape} was transferred backward to a
more powerful machine; the transferred source is the sequential
read-only-input crossing theorem.
\end{proof}

The original unrestricted RAM/RAT model may have indexed work
storage and arbitrary input probes, so $r+s\ge1$ and
$t+s+j\ge2$ are not RAM lower bounds. At $j=1$, this jump
model reaches the near-linear-time promise comparator with logarithmic
workspace; the bound (12) is then compatible with $t=1,s=0$.
For fixed $j<1$ a batching strategy on this \emph{easy promise}
performs $O(m/B)$ left/right passes with $B$ buffered control
bits, and limited-radius jumps give transport time exponent
$\max(1,2-s-j)$ up to polylogarithmic overhead.
The reversal exponent remains $\max(0,1-s)$, matching the second
face. All source reads, index commands, buffer updates and jump
offsets have been charged, but no universal SAT solver is produced.
