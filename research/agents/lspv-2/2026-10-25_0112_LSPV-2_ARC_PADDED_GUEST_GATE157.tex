\section*{An actual polynomial-solver padding family}
Assume \(P=NP\) and let \(B\) be any \emph{fixed} uniform total exact ordinary-SAT decider of time and writable/index space at most \(N^{K_0+o(1)}\) for some integer \(K_0\ge1\). Define \(A_D=\operatorname{Pad}_D(B)\), for every integer \(D>K_0\), as: on every ordinary input \(x\), execute at least \(|x|^D\) real counted dummy steps \emph{before making any content-dependent input access or computing a SAT answer}, and then execute \(B(x)\). Use \(D\) fixed finite logarithmic-bit counters to implement the delay. For \(N\ge2\), \(A_D\) is correct and total, has
\[
  \Omega(N^D)\le T_{A_D}(N)\le N^{D+o(1)},\qquad
  S_{A_D}(N)\le N^{K_0+o(1)}\le N^{D+o(1)}.
\]
The delay counter itself needs \(O_D(\log N)\) writable/index bits. Hence \((D,D)\in E\) \emph{under the stated conditional existence of \(B\)}. Invalid SAT encodings are rejected by \(B\) after the delay. This statement does not assert the existence of such a polynomial \(B\) unconditionally.

\begin{theorem}[Padded-guest query-length floor, restricted delegation model]
Let \(Q_D\) be any proposed all-input transformed decider that is required, for every sufficiently large integer \(D\), to have
\[
 T_{Q_D}(N)\le N^{q(D)+o(1)},\qquad
 q(D)=\max\{11/10,\,3/5+D/2\},
\]
when supplied the \emph{actual} guest \(A_D\) above. Suppose \(Q_D\), on some unbounded sequence of source lengths, \emph{actually invokes and waits for} \(A_D\) on at least one ordinary child \(y\) of length \(|y|\ge N^{\theta+o(1)}\). Then necessarily \(\theta D\le q(D)\). In particular, for every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), such an invocation of length \(N^{1/2+\varepsilon+o(1)}\) is impossible for all sufficiently large \(D\) satisfying the claimed bound. A parent-scale actual \(A_D\) call fails the claimed successor for \(D>6/5\).
If, more strongly, at least \(N^{b+o(1)}\) \emph{sequential genuinely executed} guest invocations each have length at least \(N^{\theta+o(1)}\), necessarily \(b+\theta D\le q(D)\). At \(\theta=1/2\) and \(D>1\) this forces \(b\le3/5\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each completed guest invocation spends at least \(|y|^D\) physically charged machine steps before returning its SAT bit. A deterministic machine that waits for the answer must incur these steps in its own execution; distinct sequential \emph{executed} calls add their costs, including changed and unchanged writes, address operations, and any copied child tape. Thus
\(T_{Q_D}(N)\ge N^{\theta D+o(1)}\), or \(\ge N^{b+\theta D+o(1)}\) under the multiplicity hypothesis. Compare with \(q(D)\). For \(\theta=1/2+\varepsilon\), \(\theta D>3/5+D/2\) for \(D>3/(5\varepsilon)\). The parent-scale claim follows from \(D>3/5+D/2\) once \(D>6/5\). This argument is a lower bound on an \emph{executed guest subroutine}, not a lower bound on SAT itself or on another implementation which avoids these calls.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Actual trace access.}
A controller observing fewer than \(|y|^D\) steps of this chosen \emph{pre-answer delay prefix} sees a clock depending on the length, not on the SAT label; source content is available to its own charged reads, but no new label-sensitive state is supplied by the executed guest prefix. A compiler may instead inspect the known source code of \(B\), strip delays, cache answers, or solve \(y\) itself. Those are \emph{outside} the theorem's actual completed-guest-call hypothesis, not contradictions. This scope is distinct from LSPV-4 ARC-EXT-SELECTOR-NOGO109's oracle elimination and LSPV-1 ARC-WINNER-ORACLE157's winner-index hardness.

\section*{Positive all-input but solver-syntax-restricted transformation}
\begin{theorem}[Syntactic unpadding; an assumption-dependent constructive closure fragment]
For the \emph{recognizable source grammar} \(A=\operatorname{Pad}_{D_h}(\cdots\operatorname{Pad}_{D_1}(B)\cdots)\), a finite uniform offline compiler may remove an outer syntactically explicit padding operation and emit the inner fixed machine verbatim. The resulting machine is total and exact on \emph{every ordinary SAT encoding}, retaining both SAT and UNSAT labels, while charging all of the inner program's actual source reads, work, writable bits, and index operations. For nested two wrappers, two genuine \emph{compiler} iterations give \(\operatorname{Pad}_{D_2}(\operatorname{Pad}_{D_1}(B))\to\operatorname{Pad}_{D_1}(B)\to B\). If \(D_1\ge2K_0\) and \(D_2\ge2D_1\), both exponent improvements obey the \emph{numerical} proposed successor inequalities at their declared diagonal pairs \((D_2,D_2)\), \((D_1,D_1)\), and \((K_0,K_0)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The marked prefix performs no semantic operations and the inner code is available as an exact finite source substring. Thus deleting this prefix changes no accepted language, on valid or invalid encodings. Compiler work is fixed finite code manipulation, not an uncharged \emph{online SAT} call; the resulting ordinary decider directly executes the inner code with its actual resource costs. For \(D_i\ge2D_{i-1}\) and \(D_1\ge2K_0\), each next exponent is at most \(D_i/2\), below \(\max(3/5+D_i/2,11/10)\), while space is at most \(D_i/2\). Every claimed pair belongs to its own resulting machine. No child or recursive SAT call is fabricated.
\end{proof}
This is \textbf{FILLED/RESTRICTED} by program syntax, though it is \emph{all-input by SAT instance}. It does \emph{not} create power-short ordinary SAT subcalls, is not hereditary on arbitrary solver code, and stops at the unpadded \(B\), whose exponent need not enter FLMV's box. In particular, this is \textbf{not} the missing all-decider ARC edge, and two compiler iterations are \emph{not} the two real recursive solver levels required for a proposed all-input splitter.

\section*{Two actual ordinary-SAT levels and finite controls}
An independent Python test actually constructs and reparses DIMACS children at each of two assignment levels on original variable identifiers \(x_1,x_2\), then runs \emph{real} exact SAT decisions on four ordinary leaf CNFs and checks OR reconciliation against direct SAT. Both SAT and UNSAT, empty clauses, Horn, 2SAT, affine-style and M22-\(Y_6\) controls are included. Auxiliary wrappers with exponents \(2,1\) are peeled twice, preserving every final label. The six-case repository mini-reproducer performs 24 real leaf calls; a ten-case extended local script performs 40. Its padding counter is a \emph{symbolic logical operation count}, not timed physical FLMV steps, and its Python buffer proxies are not machine workspace measurements. The children remain \emph{ordinary SAT}; they are not asserted to remain in clausewise-monotone exact-\((2^+,2^-)\) \(C_\star\), which excludes only \emph{fully} complement-paired M22 inputs.

\section*{Charged successor ledger and precise residual gap}
For an \emph{independently proved} all-input ordinary-SAT decomposition with at most \(N^{a+o(1)}\) actual calls of size \(N^{\lambda+o(1)}\), local bit work \(N^{r+o(1)}\), per-child-output-bit overhead \(N^{g+o(1)}\), local live workspace \(N^{\eta+o(1)}\), and live ancestors \(N^{\kappa+o(1)}\), eager complete-child buffers give
\[
 t'\le\max\{r,a+\lambda+g,a+\lambda t\},\
 s'\le\max\{\lambda,\lambda s,\eta,\kappa\}.
\]
Fully lazy, charged per guest indexed input probe gives
\[
 t'\le\max\{r,a+\lambda t+g\},\
 s'\le\max\{\lambda s,\eta,\kappa\}.
\]
These are \textbf{CONDITIONAL} ledgers, not semantic constructions. The guest-padding theorem adds a \emph{necessary charged query-scale} test for transformations required to work on \emph{every} syntactically padded hypothetical polynomial SAT machine. The unpadding theorem gives a precise hypothesis-dependent \emph{source-syntax} construction but no ARC child contraction. Discovery, switching, failed policies, read/write traffic including unchanged bits, child headers, source rereads, cache live bits, reconstruction, and simultaneous ancestor state may not be omitted. One output bit is not a free semantic solver.
