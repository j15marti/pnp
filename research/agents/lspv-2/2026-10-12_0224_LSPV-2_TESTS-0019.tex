The attacked statement was:
\begin{quote}
For every uniform deterministic exact decider of a pointed SAT language, a
unique binding pointer $u$ of length $p(N)$ must be retained, exposed, or
communicated at positive scale after validation.
\end{quote}
Its quantifier over every decider makes one uniform erasure implementation a
counterexample.  This is deliberately distinct from the true claim that an
exact validator may have to inspect every explicit pointer coordinate on a
worst-case malformed input.

\section{Bit-local unique-pointer erasure theorem}

\begin{definition}[Bit-local pointer]
Let $g:\{0,1\}^*\to\{0,1\}^*$ have output length $p(N)$ on inputs of
length $N$.  It is $(t_g,s_g)$ bit-local if one fixed deterministic machine,
given read-only $x$ and $j<p(N)$, returns $g(x)_j$ in time $t_g(N)$ and
workspace $s_g(N)$.  Define
\[
 L_g=\{\langle x,u\rangle:u=g(x)\text{ and }x\in L\}
 \tag{1}
\]
under a self-delimiting pairing of total length $M$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{BITLOCAL-UNIQUE-POINTER-ERASURE1}]
For every uniform deterministic exact decider $A$ for $L$, there is a uniform
deterministic exact decider $B_{A,g}$ for $L_g$ with
\[
 T_B(M)\le O(M)+p(N)t_g(N)+T_A(N)+O(1),
 \tag{2}
\]
and
\[
 S_B(M)\le
 \max\{S_A(N),s_g(N)\}+O(\log M).
 \tag{3}
\]
After validation, $B_{A,g}$ retains no pointer bit and its remaining trace is
independent of the representation of $u$.  Equations (2)--(3) hold even when
$g$ is injective, so that the valid pointer is unique and exactly binds the
source.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Parse the two self-delimiting fields and verify their lengths.  For
$j=0,\ldots,p(N)-1$, read the $j$th supplied pointer bit, run the bit-local
machine on $(x,j)$, and reject if the bits differ.  Only $j$, the current
supplied bit, the generated bit, and the generator workspace are live.  Once
the loop succeeds, discard these temporary values and simulate $A(x)$ on the
unchanged read-only source field.  Accept exactly when $A$ accepts.

Correctness is immediate from (1).  The sequential phases give the maximum,
not the sum, of their workspaces.  The time bound charges parsing, every
pointer read, every regeneration, source access, the complete execution of
$A$, and the final output.  No pointer-sized buffer, witness, compatibility
row, or reconstruction is present.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Injective repetition control]
Let $e(x)$ be an injective canonical source encoding with random-access bits
and let $r(N)$ be polynomially bounded.  For
\[
 g_r(x)=e(x)^{r(N)},
 \tag{4}
\]
the pointer is unique, injective, and has length $r(N)|e(x)|$, but exact
validation uses only $O(\log M)$ control workspace beyond $A$ and retains zero
pointer bits afterward.  If $A$ and $r$ are polynomial, the complete decider
is polynomial time.
\end{corollary}

Thus \texttt{UNIQUE-BINDING-POINTER-PERSISTENCE1} is
\RETIRED.  Binding and persistence are different properties.  If pointer
validity is not part of membership, MODELBRIDGE-0020's stronger cylinder
erasure skips validation altogether.  If validity is part of membership,
Theorem 1 pays for validation but still defeats the persistent-state claim.

\section{What the theorem does not erase}

The theorem does not establish sublinear input time.  On an explicit
sequential pointer, an adversary can flip the last bit, so the displayed
validator reads all $p(N)$ bits on that input.  It also does not apply when
the source is destroyed before a later challenge, when pointer bits are
received by a remote party that cannot access the source, or when the claimed
resource is an independently proved online communication transcript.  Those
are different models and require a model-preserving compiler from ordinary
static SAT.

Under the counterfactual $P=NP$, take a polynomial SAT decider $A$ in
Theorem 1.  Every polynomial-length bit-local unique-pointer language (1) is
then decided in polynomial time by the same construction.  This is compatible
with $P=NP$ and yields no unconditional contradiction.  Requiring $g$ itself
to need superpolynomial generation would insert the desired hardness into the
premise unless that lower bound and the SAT-to-$g$ transfer were independently
proved.

\section{Literature, barrier, and cross-domain transfer audit}

The two obstacles were identified before the construction: binding does not
imply persistent state on a random-access machine, and a restricted online or
communication lower bound does not constrain ordinary static SAT without a
compiler.  Primary literature was checked first.  Yao's communication model
formalizes distributed information cost \cite{Yao79}; it is not the present
one-machine model with both fields locally readable.  Classical pebbling and
sorting time--space tradeoffs constrain declared graph or sequential models,
not arbitrary SAT implementations \cite{Pippenger81,BorodinCook82}.  FLMV is
a directly usable SAT endpoint only after the same ordinary random-access
machine satisfies its simultaneous hypotheses \cite{FLMV05}.

The transfer taxonomy is:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Directly transferable theorem.}  Bitwise regeneration and
sequential comparison apply literally; Theorem 1 proves their full accounting.
\item \textbf{Useful structural analogy requiring a new transfer theorem.}
Streaming validators, read-only recomputation, proof labels, observable
control states, and communication cuts suggest interfaces.  A bridge must
prove that every SAT computation inherits the interface after the source is
removed or partitioned.
\item \textbf{Misleading analogy / failed transfer.}  Cryptographic binding,
random-oracle hashes, VLSI routing, proof-system lower bounds, and
communication equality cannot be imported as persistent-memory lower bounds
for a local random-access decider.
\end{itemize}

The construction relativizes and algebraizes: replace $A$ by a semantic or
algebraic oracle and the same streaming validation still works.  It therefore
retires a route rather than escaping Baker--Gill--Solovay or
Aaronson--Wigderson \cite{BGS75,AW09}.  It defines no large constructive truth-
table property, so Natural Proofs supplies no positive bridge.

For the post-quantum ledger, exact injective binding is stronger than a
collision-resistant-hash analogy but still supplies no persistent-state
theorem.  For the source-to-pointer map, collision count is minimal on the
tested finite source set, yet validation remains streaming; collision entropy
therefore does not promote to memory.  This is an
\textbf{EXACT OBJECT/THEOREM TRANSFER} for the map and a
\textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} to arbitrary computation.  Special soundness,
collapse binding, Schur squares, quadratic relations, Hilbert functions, and
syzygies require additional protocol or algebraic representations and are
\textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE} or \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} here.

\section{SAME\_REALIZATION audit}

The validator, source field, pointer field, bit generator, supplied SAT
decider, output bit, elapsed time, and peak workspace belong to one machine.
There is no external lookup or uncharged canonicalizer.  Pointer generation
is not charged to the input decider because the pointer is already part of
the input; every bit needed for validation is regenerated and charged.  If a
future compiler must itself write the pointer, its $p(N)$ output work is an
additional charged term, strengthening rather than weakening the negative
result about persistence.

\section{DUPLICATION / CONTRADICTION AUDIT}

The lower-bound spine already states qualitatively that uniqueness of an
auxiliary witness does not force its construction.  MODELBRIDGE-0020 proves
cylinder erasure and observes that a polynomial validity predicate can be
checked.  The new contribution is the exact bit-local theorem (2)--(3), its
post-validation zero-retention statement even for injective binding maps, and
the exhaustive corruption/state audit.  It strengthens rather than
contradicts those reports.  It does not duplicate TESTS-0018: fork padding
attacks multiplicity; this report attacks the proposed uniqueness repair.

\section{WHAT CHANGED RELATIVE TO ROOT MAIN}

Root MAIN correctly leaves the pointed source-compiler endpoint \OPEN and
warns that compiler provenance is not solver exposure.  The LSPV-2 region is
now sharpened: making a pointer unique and semantically validated still does
not force pointer-sized persistent state when the pointer is bit-local from a
readable source.  Validation time stays charged.  The repaired target must
remove or partition the source before a genuinely later obligation, then
prove a model-preserving bridge from ordinary SAT.

\section{STALE OR OVERSTATED CLAIMS FOUND}

Any wording that upgrades ``unique binding pointer'' to ``the SAT solver must
store the pointer'' is overstated.  Any communication or VLSI statement that
forgets both fields are local is a model mismatch.  No newest root theorem
requires reversal; the added guard narrows how the pointed repair may be used.

\section{Precise handoff}

The next target is \texttt{SOURCE-SEMANTIC-ONLINE-OBLIGATION1} (\OPEN): define
one fixed ordinary-SAT compiler in which a source-derived object becomes
necessary only after the source is no longer freely reread, prove this timing
or partition semantically rather than by solver-relative instrumentation, and
charge preprocessing, pointer writing, validation, communication, rereads,
recomputation, reconstruction, output, and peak space in the same realization.
It must survive STREAM\_ERASE, RECOMP, affine/Tseitin, Horn, 2SAT,
bounded-treewidth, products, padding, alternate encodings, semantic oracles,
and algebraic oracles.  Until that bridge and a forbidden endpoint are proved,
P versus NP remains \OPEN.
