\begin{lemma}[Signed literal/constant substitution closure]
For a substitution \(\sigma\) taking variables to constants or signed literals, a Resolution DAG concluding \(C\) maps to a Resolution DAG, with no more inference nodes, concluding a subclause of \(\sigma(C)\) when \(\sigma(C)\) is not tautological.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Process original lines topologically. A source maps to its substituted clause, discarding tautologies. Suppose \(C=\mathrm{Res}_x(A\vee x,B\vee\neg x)\), and \(\sigma(C)\) is non-tautological. If \(\sigma(x)\) is a constant, one parent becomes true and the other subsumes the target. Otherwise \(\sigma(x)\) is signed literal \(u\). If an already-derived stronger parent lacks its required pivot \(u\) or \(\neg u\), it itself subsumes the target. If both have opposite pivots, one strict Resolution on \(u\) yields a subclause of the target. Any tautological parent can be discarded: its complementary pair must involve the pivot, as a complementary pair away from that pivot would also make the target tautological; then the other parent subsumes the target. A shared original inference creates at most one derived node. For the empty conclusion, its only subclause is empty.
\end{proof}
