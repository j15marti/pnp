\section{Theorem: arbitrary randomized semantic messages}
A one-way encoder observes the original and $\beta$, does arbitrary charged computation, then irreversibly sends at most $s$ bits $M$; later a decoder receives a singleton context $\sigma=e_i$, coins and $M$, but cannot reread the earlier pins/history. All public coins $R$ are independent of $\beta$. For every $\beta,i$, the answer error is at most $\epsilon<1/2$.
\begin{theorem}
Every such randomized encoder/decoder satisfies
\[
\boxed{s\ge(1-h_2(\epsilon))t},\quad h_2(p)=-p\log_2p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p).
\]
For $\epsilon=0$, $s\ge t$, achieved by transmitting $\beta$. At $\epsilon=1/3$, the coefficient is $0.0817041659455\ldots$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
With $\sigma=e_i$, every product factor except $i$ is strictly positive; the completed SAT label is the complement of $\beta_i$. Hence a correct SAT decoder is a random-access decoder of every requested bit $\beta_i$. Let $X$ be uniform on $\{0,1\}^t$. Binary Fano inequality gives $H(X_i\mid M,R)\le h_2(\epsilon)$. By conditional entropy subadditivity, $H(X\mid M,R)\le t h_2(\epsilon)$. Thus
\[
t(1-h_2(\epsilon))\le I(X;M\mid R)\le H(M\mid R)\le s.
\]
Private encoder or decoder randomness is subsumed by the joint distribution/error guarantee. This is a classical random-access-code/INDEX argument applied to a source-authenticated SAT residual family.
\end{proof}
