\section*{Exact restricted-gate theorem}
\begin{theorem}
With two distinct independent input variables \(a,b\), free negation of signed wire \emph{views} and a signed final output, XOR and XNOR require exactly three conservative signed AND2 definition gates, even if constants are permitted as gate inputs. This is a minimum for \emph{circuit realization in the restricted gate basis}, not a shortest refutation in full \(R_{\mathrm{mix341}}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The three paid gates
\[
u=a\land\neg b,\quad v=\neg a\land b,\quad w=\neg u\land\neg v
\]
have signed output \(\neg w=a\oplus b\). A one-gate function or its complement is a literal, constant, or signed conjunction of literals, not the diagonal XOR truth table. In a two-gate circuit, if the final gate does not use the first gate, it reduces to the one-gate case. Otherwise the final gate ANDs a signed first-gate wire with a signed original input or a constant, or uses only signed views of the first wire. In the former case the final output (or its complement) is constant on one full original-input halfcube, whereas XOR/XNOR is nonconstant on both halfcubes. In the latter case the output reduces to a signed first-gate wire or a constant. Thus two gates are impossible.
\end{proof}
