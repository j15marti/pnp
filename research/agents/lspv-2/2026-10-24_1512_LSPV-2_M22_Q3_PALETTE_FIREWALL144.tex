\section{Exact \(q=3\) counterexample}
Let source names be \(1,\ldots,6\), and set
\[
 P=\{123,124,356,456\},\qquad

\]
As usual, \(abc\) abbreviates the unsigned support \(\{a,b,c\}\).
\begin{theorem}[Fixed-color coarsening fails at defect three]
The signed formula \(F(P,Q)\) belongs to \(\CS\), with \(k=6,m=8,q=3\), and is SAT and NAE-satisfiable. Nevertheless its valid positive-primal four-coloring
\[
 c(1),\ldots,c(6)=(0,1,2,2,0,1)
\]
fails under \emph{all three} partitions of four colors into two unordered pairs. This coloring is the lexicographically first proper four-coloring in the stated variable order and colors \(0<1<2<3\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every source name appears in exactly two supports of \(P\) and two of \(Q\), with all supports proper and distinct. \(P\cap Q=\{123\}\) and \(P\ne Q\), so \(F\in\CS\) and \(q=3\). Each positive support is rainbow under \(c\), proving it is a proper four-coloring of \(G_P\). To verify lexicographic minimality, scan vertices \(1,\ldots,6\): the earliest legal colors are successively \(0,1,2,2,0,1\), because adjacent earlier vertices forbid all smaller alternatives.

For the color partition \(01\mid23\), the negative support \(256\) has color multiset \(1,0,1\), entirely on the first side. For \(02\mid13\), the negative support \(145\) has colors \(0,2,0\), entirely on the first side. For \(03\mid12\), the negative support \(346\) has colors \(2,2,1\), entirely on the second side. Each coarsening therefore falsifies a negative OR clause. But the original truth vector \(001100\) satisfies all signed clauses, and \(001110\) makes every support in \(P\cup Q\) nonmonochromatic. A different positive-primal four-coloring \((0,1,2,2,0,3)\) \emph{does} work with \(03\mid12\), yielding NAE witness \(011100\). This last observation is essential: the counterexample rejects an arbitrary \emph{preselected} coloring, not all recoloring policies or SAT algorithms.
\end{proof}
