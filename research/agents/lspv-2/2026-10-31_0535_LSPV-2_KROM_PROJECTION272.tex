\section{Exact same-source compiler and proof}
Let the source be an arbitrary finite CNF $F$ with explicit signed original clause identifiers. Define $H$ to be all source clauses of width greater than two; let $B$ be the set of variables appearing in $H$; let $K$ be the remaining source clauses, each of width at most two, and let $P=\mathrm{vars}(K)\setminus B$. Thus, by construction, $F=H(B)\land K(B,P)$ and no private variable occurs in $H$. An original source clause is used only at its authenticated source identifier, not as a free semantic assertion. No external module separator is supplied.

For a pivot $p\in P$, let $K^0_p$ be clauses containing neither $p$ nor $\neg p$, $K^+_p$ contain $p$, and $K^-_p$ contain $\neg p$. Define
\[
\mathsf{DP}_p(K)=K^0_p\land\bigwedge_{C\in K^+_p,\,D\in K^-_p}
\operatorname{Res}_p(C,D),
\]
where tautological resolvents are deleted, equal clauses are memoized, and each retained resolvent records both parent proof-node IDs and signed pivot. All clauses remain of width at most two. Repeatedly eliminate the computed private-variable list $P$; denote the resulting boundary CNF by $Q(B)$. Remap the boundary identifiers to $1,\ldots,|B|$ and physically serialize the ordinary-CNF child $G=H\land Q$ in exact DIMACS notation.

\begin{theorem}[Source-preserving mixed Krom projection]
For every input CNF $F$, every boundary assignment $\beta\in\{0,1\}^{B}$, and the above fully executed compiler,
\[
 G(\beta)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists\pi\in\{0,1\}^{P}\;F(\beta,\pi)=1.
\]
Every child SAT assignment has a reconstructible full source witness. Every emitted child clause has an explicit ordinary resolution DAG whose leaves are indexed original source clauses. For each UNSAT source the exhaustive elimination certificate, when successfully constructed, terminates in an empty clause over original source indices. Budget failure or an unacceptable child-size test must route to an exact original-source fallback, never to a guessed UNSAT answer.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a pivot $p$ and an assignment $\tau$ to every other variable. If $\tau$ extends to a model of $K$, every surviving nonpivot clause and every resolvent is true, giving the forward direction. Conversely, suppose every nonpivot clause and resolvent is true. A positive-$p$ clause whose other literals are all false forces $p=1$; a negative-$p$ clause whose other literals are all false forces $p=0$. Both demands cannot occur, since their resolvent would be false under $\tau$. Therefore a legal value of $p$ exists. This is the Davis--Putnam existential-elimination equivalence. Induction over the authenticated pivot list proves the displayed boundary identity, including both YES and NO. In reverse pivot order, the source-indexed saved positive/negative clauses determine each forced private value, defaulting to zero when unforced. The entire reconstructed assignment is checked on each original clause. Each resolvent is checked by one signed pivot cancellation from two earlier proof nodes; therefore its deduction and every derived forbidden partial cube are consequences of the original source. A second elimination of the remaining variables in an UNSAT instance yields the empty clause with the same kind of ordinary original-source resolution certificate. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Explicit symbolic skipped region.} Original source clauses $C_1=(\neg a\lor p)$ and $C_2=(\neg p\lor b)$ imply the source-indexed resolvent $D=(\neg a\lor b)$ in one step. The entire original-source partial cube $a=1,b=0$, regardless of the remaining private assignments, is excluded without expanding any of its complete-assignment leaves. This is established resolution, not a new unrestricted SAT shortcut.

\paragraph{Finite representation and accounting bound.} For $v$ active Krom variables there are at most $2v^2+1$ distinct normalized non-tautological clauses of width at most two, including the empty clause. Each pivot has at most $2v-1$ positive and $2v-1$ negative distinct clauses, hence at most $(2v-1)^2$ resolvent trials. Naive source-indexed memoized elimination therefore has an $O(m+v^3)$ word-operation bound plus encoding and verification, and $O((m+v^2)\log(m+v))$ retained bits for the clauses and proof histories in a conventional explicit model. These bounds are intentionally far weaker than the proposed $N^{11/10}$ time and $N^{1/2}$ workspace ARC thresholds. The checked indexed-character accessor stores sorted output clauses and their prefix lengths, not a concatenated child string; it answers each address using a binary offset search and formatting one clause, but its retained table and construction cost are fully charged. The research harness additionally materializes the full byte string solely to check all indexed positions, and this allocation remains in its measured host-language peak. It does \emph{not} fix the missing general strict-(2,2) indexed emitter.

\section{Quadratic auxiliary-free output firewall}
Let $a_1,\ldots,a_k,b_1,\ldots,b_k$ be boundary variables and $p$ private, and put
\[
K_k=\bigwedge_{i=1}^k(\neg a_i\lor p)
\land\bigwedge_{j=1}^k(\neg p\lor b_j).
\]
Then exact elimination yields
\[
\exists p\,K_k\equiv\bigwedge_{1\le i,j\le k}(\neg a_i\lor b_j).
\]
\begin{lemma}[Necessary quadratic auxiliary-free 2-CNF interface]
Every 2-CNF on precisely the $2k$ boundary variables equivalent to $\exists p K_k$ requires at least $k^2$ distinct binary clauses. This is a representation-specific lower bound, not a SAT runtime lower bound.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each pair $(i,j)$, take the boundary assignment with $a_i=1$, $b_j=0$, every other $a_h=0$, and every other $b_\ell=1$. This assignment violates only the pair clause $(\neg a_i\lor b_j)$ in the displayed projection. Any equivalent 2-CNF must contain a clause falsified by this assignment and entailed by the projection. The projection entails no nontrivial units. Its only non-tautological implied binary clauses are the displayed pairs: this follows directly from its implication graph, whose only nontrivial reachability edges are $a_i\to b_j$ and their contrapositives $\neg b_j\to\neg a_i$. Hence the necessary falsifying clause is exactly $(\neg a_i\lor b_j)$. Distinct pairs demand distinct clauses, proving $k^2$. Auxiliary variables can evade this bound, and keeping $p$ is already a linear-size extended representation. \qedhere
\end{proof}
