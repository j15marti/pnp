\section{BUILD: explicit source grammars and independent labels}
Let $W$ be the following known 15-variable signed exact-$(2,2)$ UNSAT source, one clause per semicolon, with clauses indexed $0,\ldots,19$ in the reproducer:
\begin{align*}
W={}&(-1,3,-14);(2,-8,9);(10,-13,14);(3,7,-13);(-4,-9,-11);\\
   &(5,8,-14);(4,-8,-11);(1,7,13);(1,-7,10);(-5,-6,-15);\\
   &(-2,-6,12);(-4,9,-10);(-2,5,-12);(-3,6,-15);(6,-7,11);\\
   &(2,-9,11);(4,8,-10);(-3,12,15);(-5,-12,15);(-1,13,14).
\end{align*}
Integer sign denotes literal polarity, not variable value. Define $V$ by reversing only the signs of variable $9$ in source clauses $4$ and $11$. Each reverse replaces one positive and one negative occurrence, preserving every exact signed quota and all unsigned scopes. Exhaustive independent evaluation of all $2^{15}=32768$ valuations gives
\[
 \#\mathrm{SAT}(W)=0,\qquad \#\mathrm{SAT}(V)=54,
 \quad\text{first satisfying low-bit integer}=616.
\]
These are exact \emph{finite computational certificates}; the first zero statement can be converted into a finite disjunctive proof without assuming an oracle. The companion fixed guard $G$ is $W$ with the signs of variable $4$ reversed at clauses $6,11$ and has $272$ models.

For each $L\ge6$ divisible by $6$, write $x_{r,c}$ for $(r,c)\in\mathbb Z_L^2$ and define $T_L$ with two offset-$0/1$ horizontal positive triple partitions and two offset-$0/1$ vertical negative triple partitions. Explicitly, for $o\in\{0,1\}$, $j=0,\ldots,L/3-1$, include
\[
(x_{r,o+3j}\vee x_{r,o+3j+1}\vee x_{r,o+3j+2}),\quad
(\neg x_{o+3j,c}\vee\neg x_{o+3j+1,c}\vee\neg x_{o+3j+2,c})
\]
for all rows $r$ and columns $c$, with modular indices. This is the earlier ARC210 torus, whose $L^2$ variables each have exactly two positive and two negative occurrences. Its certificate $x_{r,c}=[r\equiv c\pmod3]$ is true in every source clause. Its unsigned primal graph contains the $L\times L$ grid.

Use the \emph{one fixed deterministic} connector from CONNECTOR231, selecting the first available negative source occurrence on each input, and define
\[
 S_L=J(T_L,V),\qquad U_L=J(T_L,W).
\]
Both are output by uniform $O(N)$ source scans and $O(N)$ additional output-bit writes at fixed physical word length; in canonical DIMACS, decimal conversion and buffering are charged by polynomial overhead. No SAT-side bit or label is supplied to the connector.
\begin{lemma}[15-bit gate and pointwise conjunction]\label{lem:gate}
The guard $G$ with two negative incidence slots removed defines a local four-input signature. For port bit $x$, residual clause-bit $a$, and guard outputs $u,v$, the exact counts of internal extensions (columns $uv=00,01,10,11$) are
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrr}
xa &00&01&10&11\\\hline
00&0&153&0&0\\
01&178&153&170&34\\
10&0&0&0&0\\
11&25&0&85&34
\end{array}.
\]
Two guard copies crossed at their $u$ ports have a legal extension exactly when $(\neg x\vee a)\land(\neg y\vee b)$ holds. Thus for all complete assignments $\xi,\eta$, 
\[
\exists Z\ J(H,K)(\xi,\eta,Z)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H(\xi)\land K(\eta).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Evaluate the 18 retained clauses of the displayed fixed guard $G$ for every one of its $32768$ valuations and filter by the two changed port clauses. The full deterministic Python loop is in the accompanying source. Its exact frequency table gives the stated four-row relation. The two cross clauses have Boolean forms $(v_1\vee u_2)$ and $(v_2\vee u_1)$; iterate the $2^4=16$ input patterns to verify the gate table. This is a fixed finite proof certificate, not asymptotic sampling. Each exchange of identically signed literal occurrences preserves two-positive/two-negative quotas and does not introduce duplicate literals in a clause. Graph connectivity is checked on the fixed 35-node guard incidence graph after removing only two designated incidence edges; these two same-port direct attachments connect the two sources through the two guards. All original source assignments are retained, so both directions follow by existential extension.
\end{proof}
\section{An infinite matched two-answer structural counterexample}
For a signed strict exact-$(2,2)$ source $F$ with $m$ clauses, define the \emph{signed clause-occurrence graph} $C_\sigma(F)$ for $\sigma\in\{+,-\}$: the clauses are graph vertices; each source variable appears exactly twice with sign $\sigma$ and gives an edge between those two clauses, retaining parallel edges. Let $\mathcal S_\sigma(F)$ denote the \emph{entire multiset of connected component sizes}, not merely its number of components. Let $M_\sigma$ be the $m\times n$ binary incidence matrix of occurrences of sign $\sigma$, and let $h_j(F)$ be the number of clauses with exactly $j$ positive literals.
\begin{theorem}[Two opposite labels with matched signed structural summaries]\label{thm:matched}
For every multiple $L$ of six with $L\ge6$, the pair $(S_L,U_L)$ satisfies all of the following:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&|\operatorname{var}|=n=L^2+45,\qquad m=4L^2/3+60;\\
& S_L\in\operatorname{SAT},\qquad U_L\in\operatorname{UNSAT};\\
&\text{both are connected, proper, strict signed exact-$(2,2)$-3CNF};\\
&\operatorname{scope}(S_L)=\operatorname{scope}(U_L)\quad\text{clausewise};\\
&\sum_{j=0}^3|h_j(S_L)-h_j(U_L)|=0;\\
&\mathcal S_\sigma(S_L)=\mathcal S_\sigma(U_L)\quad(\sigma\in\{+,-\});\\
&\operatorname{tw}(\operatorname{primal}(S_L)),\operatorname{tw}(\operatorname{primal}(U_L))\ge L-2.
\end{aligned}
\]
In fact, their literal encodings differ only by the \emph{two sign bits} of the two occurrences of the same source variable, and for both signs and both labels,
\[
 c_\sigma=\tfrac23L^2+L+18,\qquad
 \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}(M_\sigma)=\tfrac23L^2-L+42.
\]
This is a counterexample to the inference that unsigned scopes, per-clause polarity counts, the complete signed component-size multisets, and their associated linear ranks jointly determine satisfiability.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pointwise correctness of $J$ and the independently verified source labels give opposite answers; the periodic witness for $T_L$ and the 15-bit SAT witness for $V$ extend by the guard table to an explicitly tested assignment of every $S_L$ variable. Both connectors select the same negative source slot because the $9$-flip never touches the first negative source occurrence, so their unsigned scopes are identical clause-by-clause and exactly two literal fields change sign. One flips positive-to-negative and the other negative-to-positive in clauses which both originally have two negative literals, so the multiset $h_j$ is unchanged.

We give a size-independent certificate for the signed spectra. In the positive signed clause graph, no positive torus occurrence is altered by the negative-port connector. Torus horizontal row components have exactly $2L/3$ clause vertices per row and each negative vertical torus clause is an isolated positive-graph node. The 60 clause vertices of the joined $15+30$ source/guard core form a fixed, separately calculated positive graph. Its component-size histogram, \emph{identical} for source $V$ and $W$, is
\[
\{1^{8},3^{3},4^{1},5^{1},6^{3},8^{2}\},\qquad \sum\text{sizes}=60,\quad \#\text{components}=18.
\]
Here exponents denote multiplicities, not numeric powers.

For the negative graph, each torus column has $2L/3$ clauses linked as one cyclic/bridgeless component by the two shifted triple partitions. Removing the single chosen negative occurrence in a column leaves that column connected. Contract this distinguished column component to one marked vertex. The remaining 60 source/guard clauses and the marked vertex form a \emph{fixed} 61-vertex negative graph, independent of $L$; its complete component-size histogram for \emph{both} source signs is
\[
\{1^{3},2^{8},3^{2},5^{1},6^{4},7^{1}\},\qquad \sum\text{sizes}=61,\quad \#\text{components}=19.
\]
The marked vertex belongs to the unique component of size five, as certified by an explicit 61-vertex deterministic adjacency/BFS list in the reproducer for both $V$ and $W$. Expanding the marked vertex to the $2L/3$-vertex original connected column component changes that component's size from 5 to $4+2L/3$ in the \emph{same way} for both labels. The other $L-1$ negative torus columns give independent size-$2L/3$ components, while the $2L^2/3$ positive torus clauses are isolated in the negative graph. This establishes the full signed spectra equality for arbitrary allowed $L$, not just the checked numerical sizes.

Counting components yields $c_+=2L^2/3+L+18$ and $c_-=2L^2/3+(L-1)+19$. Because each column of $M_\sigma$ has precisely two ones on distinct clause rows, $M_\sigma$ is the incidence matrix of the possibly multiedged graph $C_\sigma$, and standard binary row elimination yields $\operatorname{rank}(M_\sigma)=m-c_\sigma$.

Only one negative torus clause scope loses an incidence in the original torus. Remove its column and the last row from the original torus grid. The remaining $(L-1)\times(L-1)$ rectangular grid is still contained in the primal graph of both outputs. Standard row-column cross arguments imply its treewidth is $\ge L-2$. All signs and scopes of this grid are unaffected by the core switch. Every claim is therefore exact given the displayed fixed 15-bit table and 60/61-vertex spectra certificates, subject to independent source-level referee.
\end{proof}
\section{Hostile algorithmic control: this stress promise is in P}
\begin{proposition}[Bounded-degree symmetry recognition, not SAT hardness]\label{prop:easy}
Let $\mathcal O$ be the language of all signed formulas obtainable from some $S_L$ or $U_L$ by arbitrary source-variable renaming, clause reordering, literal reordering, and even arbitrary independent variable complementation. Given the promise $F\in\mathcal O$, its satisfiability is decidable by one uniform deterministic polynomial-time algorithm.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First check the strict signed exact-$(2,2)$ grammar and solve $n=L^2+45$ for a positive admissible integer $L$; only one value is possible. Construct the two canonical templates in time polynomial in encoded $|F|$. Represent any formula as a vertex-colored bounded-degree incidence graph. Each clause is a clause-color node of degree 3. Every variable is a center-color vertex of degree 2, joined to its two polarity-port vertices. Each polarity-port vertex has degree 3: one link to the center and exactly two to the source clause nodes of that literal polarity. For plain renaming, positive and negative ports have two different colors; for the promised independent complementation symmetry, give the ports one common color and allow their interchange. Clause order and literal order disappear in this representation. The graph has maximum degree 3, with a fixed color alphabet. Fixed-color bounded-degree graph isomorphism is in P by the algorithm of Luks \cite{luks}, after a constant-size color gadget if necessary. Test isomorphism against $S_L$ and $U_L$; the two equivalence classes cannot overlap because their SAT labels differ and all allowed operations preserve satisfiability. Correctness is promise restricted. A generic input outside the two orbits does not receive an answer guarantee. Source parsing, both template constructions, isomorphism attempts and rejected matches are all charged. No claim is made that the VF2 Python package used for practical matching provides the theorem's worst-case guarantee.
\end{proof}
The bounded-degree graph recognition is the decisive easy-family falsifier for any inference of superpolynomial decision time merely from two-answer ambiguity, the signed spectra, or growing treewidth. No unconditional lower bound for any all-input SAT decider follows. Neither this proof nor the finite benchmarks is a general $N^\lambda$ contraction.

\section{Native machine and information ledgers}
The direct constructor reads all $N$ source bits and writes the $\Theta(N)$ original clauses plus $O(1)$ guard clauses. On an indexed RAM its source construction is polynomial, with $\Theta(N)$ materialized output bits and at least $\Omega(N)$ read/write activity; its workspace and address bits are charged. The fixed 60/61-node profile is constant, but only for the authenticated family, not a secret extra semantic oracle. A fixed-$k$ read-only-input multitape TM simulates parsing, template generation, model verification, the (theoretically) polynomial bounded-degree GI algorithm and the finite source profiles with polynomial time and space. The one-tape read/write TM counts the $N$ source cells in total footprint and has the conservative upper simulation
\[
 T_1=O\!\left(T_k(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2))\right),\qquad
 P_1=O(N+S_k+L_{\rm out}+\log(T_k+2)),
\]
where $T_k,S_k$ are the \emph{full} multitape realization and output is not free. Two fully writable tapes charge both input-tape modifications and other writes; their previously studied DISJ promise lower bounds do not transfer here. A fixed universal Turing machine charges program/interpreter and all source normalization; because the chosen program is fixed and polynomial, the class remains polynomial with some unspecified exponent. No published FLMV/Williams bound is violated, and no side-specific time lower bound has been established.

Static global clause-hitting sets for any strict exact22 width-three source require at least $m/4=n/3$ variables by four signed occurrences per variable; this is a \emph{syntactic} constraint only. The torus grid's growing width and this clause-hitting bound do not prevent rapid recognition by symmetry. The matched degree/sign signatures are useful tests for adaptive structural classifiers but not correctness certificates.
