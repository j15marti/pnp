\paragraph{PROVED/EXACT: polarization criterion on one linear slice.}
Let
\[
q(y)=\ell(y)+\sum_{1\le i<j\le m}\beta_{ij}y_i y_j
\]
be a zero-constant Boolean polynomial of degree at most two on \(\mathbb F_2^m\). Define its polar form
\[
b_q(x,y)=q(x+y)+q(x)+q(y).
\]
Then \(b_q\) is alternating bilinear. Let \(S\le\mathbb F_2^m\) have basis \(s_1,\ldots,s_r\). Then
\[
\boxed{
q|_S\equiv0
\iff
q(s_a)=0\ \forall a
\quad\text{and}\quad
b_q(s_a,s_b)=0\ \forall a<b.}
\]
Indeed, for \(x=\sum_a\lambda_a s_a\),
\[
q(x)=\sum_a\lambda_a q(s_a)+
\sum_{a<b}\lambda_a\lambda_b\,b_q(s_a,s_b),
\]
so the displayed conditions are sufficient, and necessity follows by evaluating on \(s_a\) and \(s_a+s_b\).

\paragraph{PROVED/EXACT: common-kernel reduction for declared Hadamard realization.}
For a legal Hadamard source state \(v\), HADSQ1 identifies the output slice
\[
S_v=\operatorname{im}X_v\le\mathbb F_2^m.
\]
The global degree-two annihilator space is therefore exactly
\[
\boxed{
K_2(H)=\{q:q|_{S_v}=0\text{ for every legal }v\}.}
\]
Choosing a basis \(s_{v,1},\ldots,s_{v,r_v}\) for each \(S_v\), membership in \(K_2(H)\) is equivalent to the basis-local linear equations
\[
q(s_{v,a})=0,\qquad
b_q(s_{v,a},s_{v,b})=0\quad(a<b)
\]
for every legal \(v\). Thus one state contributes at most
\[
r_v+\binom{r_v}{2}
\]
homogeneous constraints on the \(m+\binom m2\) nonconstant quadratic coefficients. Restoring the constant feature recovers the HADSQ1 slice rank
\(1+r_v+\binom{r_v}{2}\).

\paragraph{PROVED/EXACT: alternating-matrix formulation.}
Associate to the quadratic part of \(q\) the alternating matrix \(A_q\) defined by
\[
b_q(x,y)=x^\top A_qy.
\]
If the columns of \(X_v\) span \(S_v\), every common relation satisfies
\[
\boxed{X_v^\top A_qX_v=0\quad\text{for every legal }v,}
\]
together with the basis-value equations \(q(s_{v,a})=0\). Equivalently, every legal slice is totally isotropic for the same alternating form. This is a properly typed quadratic-relation matrix space. Minimum nonzero matrix rank is therefore an exact invariant of the surviving relation space once the legal slices are fixed, but no low-rank survivor is presently proved to exist.

\paragraph{Algorithmic exploitability guard.}
For any explicit finite collection of legal slice bases, all surviving quadratic relations are found by Gaussian elimination on at most
\[
\sum_v\left(r_v+\binom{r_v}{2}\right)
\]
linear equations in \(m+\binom m2\) unknown nonconstant coefficients. Hence solving the explicit relation system cannot itself be the hardness source. Any lower-bound route must charge acquisition of the legal slices, prove an all-state intersection statement, or supply a separate nonrelativizing resource theorem. This is a hostile correction: hidden algebraic structure can make reconstruction easier.
