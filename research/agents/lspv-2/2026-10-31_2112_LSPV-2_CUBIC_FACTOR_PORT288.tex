\section{Exact symbolic census theorem}
Let $P,Q$ be the four unsigned triples in the positive and negative original clauses on six variables. For $H=P,Q$, create a loopless 4-vertex cubic clause-dual multigraph, with its edges labelled by the six original variable names. The edge multiplicities on three opposite vertex-pair matchings are $(a,b,c)$ on one side and the same $(a,b,c)$ on the opposite side. The four degree equations imply equality of opposite multiplicities and $a+b+c=3$. Uniqueness of clause triples excludes $(3,0,0)$, leaving, up to permutation, $(1,1,1)$ or $(2,1,0)$. Thus $p(H)=a^2+b^2+c^2$ is respectively 3 or 5.
\begin{theorem}[Six-variable exact count and port completion]
Let $h=|Q\cap\{[6]\setminus C:C\in P\}|$. For every valid six-variable canonical target,
\[
\boxed{\#\mathrm{SAT}(F)=12+h+p(P)+p(Q).}
\]
The satisfying weights 2,3,4 contribute $p(P),12+h,p(Q)$, respectively. Every prescribed assignment to at most two original variables has a complete satisfying extension acquired by at most six same-source constant-size candidates, following grammar validation and certificate checking.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At weight two, negative clauses are automatically true and the true variables form a 2-edge perfect matching in the positive four-vertex dual iff all positive clauses are satisfied. Its three opposite-pair types contribute $a^2+b^2+c^2$. Complement the argument for weight four. At weight three, each of the four positive clauses excludes exactly the complementary true triple, while each of the four negative clauses excludes its own triple; the overlaps number $h$, giving $20-8+h=12+h$. Weights zero, one, five and six cannot satisfy because each variable hits only two same-sign clauses. On each opposite edge-pair type, pair the labelled edges in an arbitrary bijection. This produces three disjoint pairs of original variables, each pair covering all four positive source clauses (analogously negative clauses). Two pinned zeros can block at most two of the three positive pairs, so an unblocked pair gives a weight-two witness. Two pinned ones use an unblocked negative pair as the only zeros. Mixed pins admit six weight-three candidates; at most two are excluded by the positive side and at most two by the negative side, leaving at least two. One or zero pins reduce to two pins. All proposed witnesses are checked against original clauses.
\end{proof}
