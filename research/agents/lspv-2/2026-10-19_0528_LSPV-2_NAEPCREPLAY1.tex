\section{EXPLICIT LEGAL-STEP LEMMA}
Write a signed literal as $\ell\in\{x,1-x\}$.  From any derived polynomial
$P$, multiplication by $\ell$ is a legal two-step operation at worst:
derive $xP$ by the \PC{} multiplication rule and then form
$P-xP$ if $\ell=1-x$.  Thus multiplication by a product of two signed
literals uses at most four new lines and raises degree by at most two.

\begin{lemma}[ordinary-ring local replay]
Let $a,b$ be arbitrary signed source literals, put
$r=1-a$, $s=1-b$, and $p=rs$.  Over every field:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $abp=ab(1-a)(1-b)$ has a \PC{} derivation from one source Boolean
axiom of degree at most four;
\item $p^2-p$ has a \PC{} derivation of degree at most four and at most nine
recorded lines (including its two Boolean input axioms);
\item multiplication of a derived $P$ by $p$ or $1-p$ is simulated by legal
\PC{} steps with degree at most $\deg P+2$;
\item in \PCR{}, $u+\bar u-1$ maps to zero and $u\bar u$ maps to the same
derived $p(1-p)$ relation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For either polarity, $\ell^2-\ell=x^2-x$.  Hence
$a(1-a)=-(x_a^2-x_a)$; multiply by $b$ and $1-b$ using the legal literal
simulation.  For the splitter use the integral identity
\[
p^2-p=(r^2-r)s^2+r(s^2-s).
\]
Each parenthesized term is an underlying-variable Boolean axiom, and the
remaining signed-literal factors are introduced by the preceding legal
simulation.  The largest displayed degree is four.  For a general line $P$,
derive $Prs$ sequentially and obtain $P(1-p)=P-Prs$ by a linear combination.
Finally map the complement rail to $1-p$; its sum and product axioms become
zero and $p(1-p)$, respectively.  Every coefficient is integral, so the proof
also holds in characteristic two.
\end{proof}

Combining this lemma with NAEPC2D1's axiom images gives the factor-two degree
replay.  This report independently validates the local proof DAG; it does not
claim to replace independent review of the global source theorem.
