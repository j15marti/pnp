\section{PROPOSE: exact missing lemma and falsifiers}
The new five-agent coordination directive, \url{https://github.com/j15marti/pnp/issues/2}, asks whether expensive first-step opposite-occurrence enumeration can be filtered without losing unit-propagation consequences, and whether raw opposing scores track actual clause equity. We registered three specific hypotheses before testing:
\begin{description}
\item[]\textbf{H1.} Every non-tautological resolvent can strengthen full unit-propagation at some partial assignment. \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}; non-merge resolvents are a known redundant class.
\item[]\textbf{H2.} When original parents remain stored, keeping only \emph{merge} one-step resolvents preserves full saturated UP behavior at every partial prefix. \textbf{PROVED/EXACT, restricted to the declared preprocessing model}, as a direct application of \cite{Babka2013}.
\item[]\textbf{H3.} The raw additive opposition weight $\sum 2^{-|R|}$ lower-bounds marginal clause equity or the measure of distinct forbidden assignments. \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED} by Theorem~\ref{th:dup}; neither inference is valid.
\end{description}

\section{Occurrence-indexed syntax and complete-complement diagnostics}
Let source clauses be $C_j$ and $x_{i,j}$ denote the \emph{occurrence}, not another independent Boolean variable. Its polarity $\sigma_{ij}\in\{-1,+1\}$ determines its preferred satisfying value $b_{ij}=(1+\sigma_{ij})/2$. Under a full assignment $a$, the literal occurrence is true exactly when $a_i=b_{ij}$. Under partial $p_i\in\{0,1,\bot\}$ it has one of $\{\mathrm{T},\mathrm{F},\bot\}$. A full complementation $\bar a_i=1-a_i$ swaps occurrence truth values.
For proper 3-CNF define $N_r(a)=|\{j:C_j\text{ has exactly }r\text{ true literals under }a\}|$, $0\le r\le3$. Consequently
\[
 N_r(\bar a)=N_{3-r}(a),\qquad E(a)=N_0(a)+N_3(a)=E(\bar a),\qquad D(a)=N_3(a)-N_0(a)=-D(\bar a).
\]
These are exact diagnostics, \emph{not} SAT/UNSAT oracles. In particular $E=|D|$ only says one of the two orientations satisfies all source clauses, not which orientation. The interpreter records original source indices and polarity and does not silently reclassify derived 4-CNF as strict22 input.

\section{Resolution, certified cubes, and the merge firewall}
Write opposite source occurrences $C_j=(x\lor A)$ and $C_k=(\neg x\lor B)$, where $x$ is absent from $A,B$. If the union $A\cup B$ contains both $z$ and $\neg z$, discard the tautological resolvent; otherwise let $R=A\lor B$. Every assignment falsifying $R$ falsifies $C_j$ if $x=0$ and $C_k$ if $x=1$. Hence the partial cube falsifying all literals of $R$ is a proof-carrying impossible completion cube. Its unrestricted full-assignment density is $2^{-|R|}$, \emph{not} its marginal search benefit. Overlap between resolvent cubes is counted by exact union, not independent summation.

\begin{lemma}[KNOWN THEOREM: non-merge resolvent UP redundancy]\label{lem:nonmerge}
Suppose $A\cap B=\varnothing$ as sets of \emph{signed literals} and $R=A\lor B$ is non-tautological. Let $H$ be any CNF containing both parent clauses. For every partial assignment $p$, the fully saturated unit-propagation outcomes of $H\land p$ and $(H\land R)\land p$ are the same, up to irrelevant order of propagation, with conflict treated as a single outcome. The same holds when all one-step non-merge resolvents of retained source clauses are added simultaneously, even when arbitrary other learned merge clauses are also present.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $s$ be a nonconflicting fixed point of UP on $H\land p$. If $R$ were falsified, the two retained parents would be the contradictory units $x$ and $\neg x$. If $R$ were unit with remaining literal $\ell\in A$ (the symmetric case $\ell\in B$ is identical), uniqueness of this signed literal between $A$ and $B$ makes every literal of $B$ false. The second parent forces $\neg x$ (or contradicts an existing assignment $x$), and the first parent then forces $\ell$, contradicting saturation with $\ell$ unset. Thus $R$ is already satisfied or has at least two unset literals at $s$. The saturated old state remains saturated after adding $R$, and monotonically iterated UP reaches the same literals and conflict status. The argument applies individually to all retained-parent non-merge resolvents, hence to their simultaneous addition. It also works when $H$ includes arbitrary other clauses. This is the non-merge / non-empowering observation in \cite[Lemma~3.3]{Babka2013}, not a claim of new proof-theoretic novelty.
\end{proof}

For shared signed support $A\cap B\ne\varnothing$ the conclusion can fail: $C_1=(x\lor a\lor u)$, $C_2=(\neg x\lor a\lor v)$ resolve to $a\lor u\lor v$. With $u=v=0$, original UP is inconclusive but the derived clause forces $a=1$. This is not an UNSAT answer and must pay discovery and verification costs. The converse is only that a merge is \emph{capable} of improving UP in its two-parent subsystem; other source clauses may make it redundant globally. For disjoint-support resolvents, the raw cube is sound even though it adds no saturated-UP pruning strength.

\section{Exact equity inversion and fully overlapping cubes}
For source clause $C_j$, exact clause equity is
\[
 Q_j(F)=\#\{a\in\{0,1\}^n: C_j(a)=0,\ \bigwedge_{h\ne j}C_h(a)=1\}.
\]
This is a \emph{diagnostic} that the reproducibility script evaluates by exhaustive assignments; it is not supplied to the solver as a polynomial-time oracle. Define the naive raw pair weight $W(F)=\sum_{(j,k,x):R_{jk}\text{ non-taut.}}2^{-|R_{jk}|}$. It counts pair occurrences with multiplicity.
\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT: unbounded false opposition equity]\label{th:dup}
For every integer $k\ge2$, let
\[
 F_k(a,b,x_1,\ldots,x_k)=\bigwedge_{r=1}^{k}\bigl[(x_r\lor a\lor b)\land(\neg x_r\lor a\lor b)\bigr].
\]
Each $F_k$ is a connected proper width-3 CNF (but \emph{not} strict exact-$(2,2)$). Every clause has exact equity $Q_j(F_k)=0$. Its $k$ opposite-occurrence pairs have the identical non-tautological resolvent $R=a\lor b$. The naive raw opposition score is $W(F_k)=k/4$, whereas the exact union of all forbidden resolvent cubes has density $1/4$. The ratio between the raw score and union density is $k$ and is unbounded.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each $r$, the conjunction $(x_r\lor a\lor b)\land(\neg x_r\lor a\lor b)$ is equivalent to $a\lor b$, so $F_k\equiv a\lor b$. If $k\ge2$, deleting any one source clause leaves another intact pair that still entails $a\lor b$; hence the removed clause cannot be uniquely violated while all other clauses hold, and $Q_j=0$. Each $x_r$ has precisely one positive and one negative occurrence, yielding $R=a\lor b$. Variables $a,b$ occur only positively, so there are no other opposing pairs. The resolvent is falsified exactly by $a=b=0$, independently of the $k$ other variables, so the forbidden full-assignment union has size $2^k$ out of $2^{k+2}$; its density is $1/4$. Summing $k$ repeated weights $2^{-2}$ instead yields $k/4$. Every formula is connected because each $x_r$ shares $a,b$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Caution:} This is a counterexample to a heuristic transfer, not a lower bound on SAT, and the failure does not automatically transfer to signed exact22 without an explicitly proven quota-preserving compiler. Conversely, any SAT/UNSAT-labeled instance below is independently checked on its original source grammar.
