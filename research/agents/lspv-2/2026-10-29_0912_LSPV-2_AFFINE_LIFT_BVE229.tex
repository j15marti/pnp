\section{BUILD I: an unbounded connected exact22 linear-incidence family}
Write $P=\mathbb F_3^2$, indexed by $(a,b)\mapsto3a+b$, with the 12 affine lines given by $a=c$, $b=c$, $b-a=c$, and $b-2a=c$ for $c\in\mathbb F_3$. Each line has three points, each point lies on exactly four lines, and every pair of distinct points lies together on exactly one line. For integer $r\ge1$, let $P_r=P\times\mathbb Z_r$ and let the clauses be 12$r$ lifted lines $L_{d,c,t}$. Each point $(p,t)$ on base line $L_{d,c}$ participates in $L_{d,c,t}$, except that the incidence between base point $p=(0,0)$ and base line $L_{0,0}$ is shifted to layer $t+1\bmod r$. Give every literal on line type $d=0,1$ positive sign, and every literal on line type $d=2,3$ negative sign. Denote this 3CNF by $F_r$.
\begin{theorem}[Infinite connected linear exact22 family]\label{th:design}
For every $r\ge1$, $F_r$ has $n=9r$ variables and $m=12r=4n/3$ distinct proper ternary clauses, exactly two positive and two negative occurrences per variable, a connected incidence graph, and at most one clause containing any particular pair of distinct variables.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The base affine plane has four parallel classes, each containing three 3-point lines and each point appearing once per class. The lifted incidence assignment is a permutation on the $r$ clones of one source incidence and the identity on all others; degrees, signs, sizes and line distinctness are unchanged. Two different lifted clauses sharing the same two names would project to two distinct base lines containing the same pair of base points (impossible), or to different lifts of one base line which cannot share a cloned point, since incidence-lift maps are bijective. The base incidence graph minus the shifted edge is connected: that edge lies on a cycle through two other base points/lines. Hence each untwisted layer is connected; the shifted edges cyclically join all $r$ layers. This proves connectedness. Each clone meets each of the four line directions once, two positive and two negative.
\end{proof}

\section{BUILD II: four unavoidable local width-four resolvents}
\begin{theorem}[Exact local width and local CNF barrier]\label{th:pivot}
For \emph{every} variable $x$ of \emph{every} $F_r$, its four containing clauses, with eight pairwise distinct neighboring variables, can be written
\[
 G=(x\vee A_1)\land(x\vee A_2)\land(\neg x\vee B_1)\land(\neg x\vee B_2),
\]
where $A_i,B_j$ are 2-literal ORs on disjoint supports. The local projection has exactly four distinct, non-tautological width-four Davis--Putnam resolvents:
\[
 \exists x\,G=\bigwedge_{i,j\in\{1,2\}}(A_i\vee B_j)
 = (A_1\wedge A_2)\vee(B_1\wedge B_2). \tag{1}
\]
For the isolated local projected function in (1), no exactly equivalent \emph{auxiliary-free} 3CNF exists on those eight variables. Its four displayed width-four clauses are all prime implicates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every variable belongs to four lines by Theorem~\ref{th:design}. Each pair of lines through it intersects only at that variable; otherwise the two lines share another pair of variables. Thus its eight co-variables are pairwise different. There are two positive and two negative source clauses. Resolution produces four distinct 4-literal clauses with no repeated or complementary variable. Existentially setting $x=0$ or $x=1$ gives the disjunction in (1), and distributivity gives the four resolvents.

Complement individual co-variable coordinates where needed so that the eight displayed literal values become independent positive Boolean coordinates. The resulting function is monotone. For each pair $(i,j)$, make all four literals in $A_i\vee B_j$ false and all other four true. This is a minimal falsifying assignment: turning any of its four zeros to one makes at least one conjunction branch in (1) true, because the other $A$ and $B$ groups already hold. Consequently $A_i\vee B_j$ is prime. Every CNF for a monotone function can be replaced without growing its number of clauses by deleting negative literals: if the function implies a clause, monotonicity implies its positive part. In a positive CNF, the minimal falsifying assignment for $(i,j)$ forces some clause contained in those four zeros. Prime-ness forces that clause to contain all four. Hence all four width-four clauses are needed and no pure width-three CNF over these eight independent inputs represents the \emph{isolated} projection. This assertion does not say the four clauses remain globally essential after adjoining the other clauses of $F_r$.
\end{proof}

\section{BUILD III: exact charged conversion back to ordinary ternary SAT}
For each resolvent $C=(\ell_1\vee\ell_2\vee\ell_3\vee\ell_4)$, introduce its own new name $u_C$ and replace $C$ with
\[
(\ell_1\vee\ell_2\vee u_C)\land(\neg u_C\vee\ell_3\vee\ell_4),
\quad \exists u_C[\cdots]\;\equiv\; C.\tag{2}
\]
Use the unchanged surviving clauses, the eight new 3-clauses, and a charged dense renumbering of all live source and auxiliary identifiers. This defines a \emph{particular} deterministic local normalized elimination scheme $\mathcal E$; no call to a SAT decider, global subsumption, or semantic oracle is hidden in it.
\begin{theorem}[Every source pivot physically grows under $\mathcal E$]\label{th:growth}
For every $F_r$ and each of its $n$ source pivots, $\mathcal E(F_r,x)$ is answer-equivalent, is a proper 3CNF, and has exactly
\[
 n'=n-1+4=n+3,\qquad m'=m-4+8=m+4,\qquad m'=\frac43n'.
\]
It is \emph{not} strict signed exact22: the eight source neighbors have five occurrences each and each fresh auxiliary has two. In the explicit four-words-per-clause encoding with
\[
w(n,m)=\big\lceil\log_2[64(n+m+1)]\big\rceil,\qquad N(n,m)=(4m+2)w(n,m),
\]
we have $N(n',m')-N(n,m)\ge16w(n,m)>0$ for every $r$. The local transformation therefore does \emph{not} supply even one strict physical-bit descent step, let alone a recursive $N^\lambda$ contraction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{th:pivot} gives exactly four width-four resolvents and no other deleted clauses. Each application of (2) replaces one 4-clause with two 3-clauses and one fresh variable, proving the counts. The exact projection identity, source-preserving resolution, and (2) establish $\SAT(F_r)\Leftrightarrow\SAT(\mathcal E(F_r,x))$. Each neighbor loses one occurrence in a deleted $x$-clause and gains two occurrences in the two new resolvents containing that neighbor, hence has degree five. Each fresh auxiliary occurs once per polarity. The displayed ratio $m'/n'=4/3$ holds numerically, yet the per-variable grammar is violated. Dense re-encoding charges all renamed literal fields and headers. Since $w(n',m')\ge w(n,m)$ and $4m'+2=4m+18$, subtracting yields at least $16w(n,m)$ additional physical bits.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[A two-step auxiliary fixed point, rather than progress]\label{prop:aux}
Choose any one of the four fresh $u_C$ names. Eliminate it by resolution and immediately re-encode the resulting width-four clause by (2) with a fresh replacement name. Up to renaming this gives back the same pair of 3-clauses and the same encoded size. In particular, the rank ``number of current variables'' need not decrease at all after a full elimination-and-reentry step.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The positive $u_C$ and negative $u_C$ clauses of (2) resolve to exactly $C$. All other clauses are untouched because the auxiliary occurs nowhere else. Reapplying (2) regenerates the original pair with a renamed auxiliary. $n,m$ and their bit encoding are unchanged after dense renumbering.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{Circuit accounting and escape.} The isolated projected function in (1) has four binary OR gates to produce $A_i,B_j$, two binary AND gates, and one binary OR gate, thus 7 internal fan-in-two gates and eight source-literal inputs. The original four triple clauses use 8 binary OR gates plus 3 AND gates, thus 11 internal gates and 12 literal occurrences, counting repeated $x$. The 3CNF realization (2) uses 8 ternary clauses and 24 literal pins. These are distinct resource coordinates; gate reduction does \emph{not} imply physical ordinary-SAT output shrink, and switches, fanout and source selection are not free. A shared DAG may avoid the particular CNF inflation; its uniform discovery and repeated SAT re-entry remain unproved.

\section{Charged models, two universes and exact residual obstruction}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,labelwidth=0pt,itemsep=3pt]
\item[] \textbf{Fixed two-tape read-only input TM:} parsing, connectivity, counts, signed quotas, dense-ID remapping and physical materialization of one chosen child can be carried out by simple repeated full scans in $T_2=O(N^3)$ steps using $S_2=O(N)$ writable cells, $O(N^3)$ input-head moves and $O(N)$ output symbols; the $(4m+2)w$ source is fully accounted. This is a conservative explicit upper bound, not a lower bound or measurement of Python runtime.
\item[] \textbf{One shared read/write tape TM:} an elementary track simulation gives $T_1=O(T_2(N+S_2+\log(T_2+2)))=O(N^4)$ and total footprint $P_1=O(N+S_2+\log(T_2+2))=O(N)$ for the chosen single-child construction, with input, child output and head crossings charged. These exponents are bounds on a simple implementation, not optimal native exponents.
\item[] \textbf{Fixed universal interpreter:} compile the same finite parser and transform program, charge universal simulation overhead, instructions, head movements and its own simultaneous peak tape footprint. No free oracle table or arbitrary instantaneous random access is supplied.
\item[] \textbf{Indexed RAM comparison:} preprocessing plus construction is polynomial with $O(N)$ charged bits and explicit output; the large workspace does not meet a hypothetical $N^{o(1)}$ small-space premise. Machine-specific crossing claims cannot be transplanted to RAM.
\item[] \textbf{World $P=NP$:} an unknown polynomial-time SAT decider can choose a direct fixed YES/NO child. The fact that $\mathcal E$ expands is consistent with this world and does not constrain that unknown solver exponent $K$ or space.
\item[] \textbf{World $P\ne NP$:} $\mathcal E$ still expands, but a syntactically restricted procedure need not represent every adaptive deterministic decider; no superpolynomial time, space, query or bit-switch lower bound follows for either SAT or UNSAT. A single source-authenticated correct algorithm must serve both labels; there is no free label bit.
\end{description}
The proper next theorem would prove an affordable and exact \emph{shared} semantic projection whose \emph{complete physical representation and recursive successor class} are bounded on \emph{all} SAT inputs, including hostile high-incidence-width inputs and both answer classes, or refute a sufficiently precisely stated proposed schema. Local width-four prime implicates and the static interface coverage obstruction are not the unrestricted theorem.
