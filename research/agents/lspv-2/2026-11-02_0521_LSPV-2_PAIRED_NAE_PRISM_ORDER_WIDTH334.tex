\section{Obstruction and falsifiers}
An evaluator $C_F(x)$ running in linear gate count on \emph{given} $x$ is not an algorithm for deciding $\exists x\,C_F(x)=1$. The four tested mechanisms were: (i) large cofactor spectra imply SAT decision hardness, \textbf{FALSIFIED}; (ii) a bad order cannot be escaped within OBDDs, \textbf{FALSIFIED}; (iii) continuation equivalence always requires a SAT oracle, \textbf{FALSIFIED on this restricted rim-prefix family}; (iv) a compact circuit necessarily reduces total decision work, \textbf{FALSIFIED on same-original controls}.

\section{The canonical original}
For each even integer $k\ge4$ introduce $3k$ original variables
$T_i,B_i,S_i$ for $i\in\mathbb Z/k$. At each top vertex $i$, include the two distinct source clauses
\[
 (T_{i-1}\lor T_i\lor S_i),\qquad
 (\neg T_{i-1}\lor\neg T_i\lor\neg S_i);
\]
include their bottom analogues using $B_{i-1},B_i,S_i$.
This defines $4k$ clauses, all proper, same-sign, and duplicate-free.
Every variable occurs in exactly two positive and two negative clauses, and the variable-clause incidence graph is connected. It is therefore a valid D\"ocker Monotone 3-SAT-(2,2) original, not an arbitrary signed $(4,4)$ surrogate.
The source-clause pair at each prism vertex is $\mathrm{NAE}_3$. A three-gate XOR/XOR/OR evaluator per pair plus an AND tree uses exactly $8k-1$ fan-in-two gates in this \emph{declared architecture}, and its unique-trace output-one Tseitin encoding has $28k-2$ clauses. Neither number is an unrestricted circuit minimum, and Tseitin is not a canonical child.

\begin{theorem}[Bad-order lower bound]
For the variable order consisting of all $T$ wires, all $B$ wires, then $S_1$ and the remaining spokes, every reduced OBDD for this original has at least $2^{k/2}$ nodes at the level labeled $S_1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each $q\in\{0,1\}^{k/2}$, fix $T_{2j}=T_{2j+1}=q_j$ and $B_i=i\bmod2$. All bottom constraints become tautological on the remaining spokes. Top vertex $2j+1$ forces $S_{2j+1}=1-q_j$; top vertex $2j$ either forces a consistent value on $S_{2j}$ or leaves it free. Every resulting residual is satisfiable, each depends on $S_1$, and residual functions for different $q$ disagree on one forced odd spoke. Thus they require distinct reduced OBDD nodes at the next decision level.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Good-order upper bound]
For the interleaved order $(T_0,B_0,S_0,\ldots,T_{k-1},B_{k-1},S_{k-1})$, the reduced OBDD width is at most $146$, with at most $438k$ nonterminal nodes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After $j$ complete triples, $1\le j<k$, a surviving residual is determined by the two wrap-edge constraints left at vertex zero (each free, forced zero or forced one) and the two last assigned rim bits $(T_{j-1},B_{j-1})$. Thus it has at most $3^2 2^2=36$ nonzero boundary summaries; every other suffix clause is source-identical. Before completing the next triple at most two new bit values have been read, giving at most $4\cdot36=144$ summaries, plus two terminals. Finite-state updates are obtained by checking the local original clause pairs and do not require a free semantic-equality oracle. Standard bottom-up BDD redundancy elimination cannot increase width. There are $3k$ levels.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Decision bypass and same-original opposite labels]
The original formula is SAT by $T_i=B_i=i\bmod2$ and arbitrary spoke values, verified in $O(k)$ source-clause word operations after authentication. Two coherent rim contexts in the \emph{same original}, $q=0^{k/2}$ and $q'=(1,0,\ldots,0)$, are both currently SAT, but the common future pin $S_1=1$ preserves SAT for the first and makes the second UNSAT. Its original negative source clause at top vertex one (ID $2k+2$) is already false.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every top and bottom vertex then has two incident rim bits of opposite values, so both source NAE clauses hold. For the opposing rim context, $T_0=T_1=S_1=1$ falsifies $(\neg T_0\lor\neg T_1\lor\neg S_1)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Work, independent tests, and adverse controls}
A rim-prefix continuation has a source-computable $(M,V)$ signature: each spoke is either free or forced to one value by its two incident NAE pairs; conflicting forces yield FALSE. Every signature is computed by $O(k)$ source-local reads, not an oracle. Equality of two nonempty such signatures is exactly equality of their continuation functions. However, explicit full-prefix enumeration costs $2^{2k}$ rows, so this experiment never treats the semantic quotient as free.
