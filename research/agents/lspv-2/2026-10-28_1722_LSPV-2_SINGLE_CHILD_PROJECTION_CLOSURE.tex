\section{Propose: four falsifiable mechanisms}
The sought lemma is a uniform deterministic all-input map from ordinary
SAT to one SAT child of physical length at most $N^\lambda$, fixed
$0<\lambda<1$, with polynomial fully charged generation, exact finite
bases and repeated closure.
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{H1.} Recognize a large canonical auxiliary-free
cross-product and make one truly smaller SAT child.
\textbf{PROVED/RESTRICTED.}
\item[] \textbf{H2.} The same canonical expanded source class remains
NP-hard after allowing linear-size bounded-width contexts.
\textbf{PROVED/RESTRICTED.}
\item[] \textbf{H3.} A small factoring circuit guarantees smaller
ordinary SAT output even compared with the original selector.
\textbf{FALSIFIED}; count all gate-input wires, Tseitin clauses,
fresh names and output bits. The $t^2$ auxiliary-free lower bound
is \textbf{PROVED/RESTRICTED} but an already-known type of phenomenon.
\item[] \textbf{H4.} The child grammar is automatically closed,
or a pair can be discarded without reading shared context.
\textbf{FALSIFIED} by a concrete UNSAT-to-SAT counterexample.
\end{description}

\section{Precise construction and pointwise correctness}
Fix $t\ge1$ and disjoint original pairs of variable names
\[
 A_i=(a_i\vee b_i),\qquad B_j=(c_j\vee d_j),\qquad 1\le i,j\le t.
\]
Let
\begin{align}
 P_t(X)&=\bigwedge_{i,j}(A_i\vee B_j),\\
 F_t(X,z)&=\bigwedge_i(z\vee A_i)\ \wedge\
                         \bigwedge_j(\neg z\vee B_j).
\end{align}
There are $t^2$ width-four clauses and $4t$ original variables
in $P_t$, versus $2t$ width-three clauses and one fresh selector
variable $z$ in $F_t$. Let $R(X)$ contain at most $2t$ clauses,
each of \emph{width at most three}, over the original $4t$ variables.
Let $\mathcal E$ be the canonically encoded DIMACS instances
$P_t\wedge R$, with the $P_t$ prefix in increasing $(i,j)$ order.
The header $n=4t$ gives $t$. Recognition checks every header field,
literal, row and context-width bound; it cannot skip malformed input.

\begin{lemma}[Exact context-preserving existential projection]
For every $X$ and every admissible $R$,
\[
 R(X)\wedge P_t(X)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
               \exists z\,[R(X)\wedge F_t(X,z)].
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At $z=0$, $F_t$ requires all $A_i$; at $z=1$, it
requires all $B_j$. Thus $\exists z F_t=(\bigwedge_i A_i)
\vee(\bigwedge_j B_j)=\bigwedge_{i,j}(A_i\vee B_j)$.
Since $R$ is independent of $z$, it commutes with projection.
This is a pointwise equivalence, stronger than SAT equisatisfiability.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Genuine sublinear one-child reduction,
\textbf{PROVED/RESTRICTED}]
A deterministic polynomial-time algorithm on all valid inputs in
$\mathcal E$ outputs exactly one actual ordinary-SAT child
$F_t\wedge R$, preserving both SAT and UNSAT without an answer-class
hint. With the stated bounded-width context,
\[
 N_{\rm in}=\Theta(t^2\log(t+2)),\qquad
 N_{\rm child}=O(t\log(t+2))
             =O(\sqrt{N_{\rm in}\log N_{\rm in}}).
\]
For each fixed $1/2<\lambda<1$ and sufficiently large $N_{\rm in}$,
the child has at most $N_{\rm in}^{\lambda}$ physical bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Parse the complete input, recover $t$, verify all $t^2$ canonical rows
and the at-most-$2t$ bounded-width context rows, introduce
$z=4t+1$, output all $2t$ selector clauses and every $R$ clause,
and update the DIMACS header. Correctness follows from the lemma.
A constant fraction of the $t^2$ rows contains identifiers of
$\Theta(\log t)$ bits, and all rows have constant width.
The output has only $O(t)$ constant-width clauses with $O(\log t)$-bit
IDs. In particular
$t\log t=O(\sqrt{N_{\rm in}\log N_{\rm in}})$.
For any $\lambda>1/2$ the remaining polylogarithmic factor
is dominated by $N^{\lambda-1/2}$.
This is a complete ordinary-CNF output, not an oracle or compressed
circuit in place of a SAT child.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[NP-complete recognized source language,
\textbf{PROVED/RESTRICTED}]
SAT on the polynomial-time recognizable source class $\mathcal E$
is NP-complete under ordinary many-one reductions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Given any proper 3CNF $G$ with $m\ge1$ clauses and at most $3m$
active names, choose $t=2m$. The $2t=4m$ right-side variables
$c_1,d_1,\ldots,c_t,d_t$ hold an injectively renamed copy of all
variables of $G$. Put in $R$ the $t$ positive units $a_i$ and
the $m$ renamed clauses of $G$. Then
$|R|=t+m=3m\le 2t$ and every $A_i$ is true.
Therefore $P_t$ is automatically true, so
$\SAT(P_t\wedge R)=\SAT(G)$. The full expansion has
$O(m^2\log m)$ bits and is constructible without solving $G$.
Recognition and NP membership are immediate.
\end{proof}

\section{Sharp output representation barrier, not a SAT lower bound}
\begin{lemma}[Critical-pair clause bound]
Every equivalent auxiliary-free CNF over the same $4t$
variables as $P_t$, with arbitrary signs and widths, requires
at least $t^2$ clauses.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each $(i,j)$ let $u_{ij}$ make exactly
$a_i,b_i,c_j,d_j$ false and all other original variables true.
It falsifies $P_t$, while flipping any one of the four false
bits makes $P_t$ true. An equivalent CNF has some clause
$C_{ij}$ false at $u_{ij}$ and true at each of those four
upward neighbors. Thus it contains the four corresponding
positive literals. Such a clause is true at every other
$u_{i'j'}$, so no clause can serve two distinct witnesses.
There are $t^2$ witnesses. This is a previously known type
of restricted clause-output lower bound; it says nothing
about arbitrary SAT decision runtime.
\end{proof}
The factored circuit
$(\bigwedge_i A_i)\vee(\bigwedge_j B_j)$
has $4t-1$ fan-in-two AND/OR gates and $8t-2$
gate-input references. Full Tseitin equivalences plus
one root pin yield $12t-2$ clauses, $28t-6$
literal entries and $4t-1$ fresh names. In contrast, original
selector $F_t$ has only $2t$ ternary clauses and $6t$
literal occurrences. Thus factoring and Tseitin prevent
quadratic expansion but \emph{do not} imply a child shorter
than the already compact selector. In d4's forgetting code
the unsimplified pivot has $t^2$ new resolvents against
$2t$ old clauses, and the explicit no-growth test
$t^2\le2t$ holds only for $t\le2$.

\section{Falsifiers of repeated closure}
\begin{counterexample}[Output grammar is not closed]
The child $F_t\wedge R$ is selector form and lacks the
$t^2$-row canonical prefix required for membership in
$\mathcal E$. Re-expanding it creates the original larger
$P_t\wedge R$ and a representation cycle. Therefore the
single valid contraction does \emph{not} justify
$T(N)\le N^c+T(N^\lambda)$.
\end{counterexample}
\begin{counterexample}[Shared context invalidates pair dropping]
For $t=2$, set
$R=(\neg a_1)\wedge(\neg b_1)\wedge
(\neg c_1)\wedge(\neg d_1)$.
The full selector $F_2\wedge R$ is UNSAT.
Deleting the first A and B pair while retaining $R$
leaves a SAT formula. Thus no free dynamic pair deletion
or answer-side hint is permitted.
\end{counterexample}
If a local rewrite must copy an arbitrary large context $R$
without changing it, take fixed $t=1$ and let $|R|\to\infty$.
The child remains length $N-O(1)$, contradicting every
universal strict-power bound $N^\lambda$, $\lambda<1$.
This only rules out that pass-through rewrite model.

\begin{lemma}[Full-SAT target is equivalent in existence to P=NP]
If a uniform deterministic polynomial-time map shortens
every ordinary SAT input to exactly one SAT-equivalent
ordinary child of length $\le N^\lambda$ above a fixed
threshold, with direct finite base cases, then SAT is in P.
Conversely, if SAT is in P, deciding the input and outputting
one of two fixed constant-size YES/NO instances is such a map.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first direction follows by repeated, strictly
decreasing single-child recursion. The recurrence has
polynomial total running time and terminating fixed bases.
The converse explicitly relies on the hypothetical
polynomial SAT decider and pays its computation; it is
not the unconditional builder demanded here.
\end{proof}

\section{Finite checks and physical machine accounting}
The independent Python reproducer in the report's local package
and GitHub standalone JavaScript reproducer checked all
$16+256+4096+65536=69,904$ original assignments
against $\exists z F_t$, the $t^2$-clause projection,
the gate circuit and the full Tseitin extension.
Four $t=4$ SAT/UNSAT contexts add $262,144$ pointwise
comparisons. Thirty critical pair witnesses and their
$120$ upward neighboring assignments were verified.
The actual canonical DIMACS parser rejected altered rows.
The unsafe-deletion UNSAT-to-SAT control was reproduced.
SAT/UNSAT examples from seven/eight ternary sign patterns
were embedded in complete sources. All checks passed.

A fixed-$k$ machine with sequential read-only input
and output tape charged as visited work can validate with
two passes in $T_k=O(N\operatorname{polylog}N+N')$,
$S_k=O(N'+\log N)$ visited writable output/work cells,
$Q_k=O(T_k)$ probes and crossing events, and exactly
$N'$ emitted bits. Materialized RAM output likewise
costs at least $N'$ live bits. An append-only external
sink can avoid retained work bits but still charges
each emitted bit. The Python checker materializes
lists and does \emph{not} establish a small-space TM
performance certificate.

Conservative standard one-read/write-tape simulation,
counting the initial input in footprint, gives
\[
T_1=O(T_k(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2))),\quad
P_1=O(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2)).
\]
Physical input-head motion, work-head motion and output
are within the transition count, not double-charged.
Indexed read-only-input RAM requires charged binary
addresses and output storage; no one-head tape
crossing lower bound is imported into RAT.
Signed exact-(2,2) transport remains separate and
is not clause-monotone or output compression for free.
The same uniform map handles SAT and UNSAT with
side-specific costs only measured as restrictions of
one program; no answer bit is supplied.

\section{Exact remaining gap}
The next decisive lemma must transform every compact
child $F_t\wedge R$, including those containing an embedded
arbitrary 3SAT $G$, into a genuinely shorter ordinary SAT
child with paid parsing, construction and memory, and
must do so repeatedly under one closed grammar.
No such second step was found or proved.
An unknown hypothetical SAT exponent $K$, full decision
circuit generation, ARC-EDGE-90, FLMV contraction,
$P=NP$ and $P\ne NP$ all remain \textbf{OPEN}.
