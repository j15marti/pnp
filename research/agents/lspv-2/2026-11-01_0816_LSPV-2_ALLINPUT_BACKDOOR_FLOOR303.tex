\section{Theorem: raw backdoor depth, including arbitrary polarity renaming}
Let $F$ be any valid original on $n>0$ variables, with $m=4n/3$ clauses and exactly two occurrences per sign of each variable. A coherent raw restriction $F\upharpoonright_p$ assigns $b$ original variables, deletes clauses made true and deletes false pinned literals from other clauses. Source clause IDs are retained. For a variable polarity renaming $\rho\in\{0,1\}^n$, complement chosen variable names in the entire formula before restriction; this does not change any variable's two-positive/two-negative quota.

\begin{theorem}[All-input syntactic depth floor]\label{thm:floor}
For every valid canonical original $F$, every coherent pin assignment $p$ of size $b$, and every polarity renaming $\rho$, the following necessary conditions hold:
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
F^\rho\upharpoonright_p\text{ is Horn}&\Longrightarrow& b\ge n/6,\\
F^\rho\upharpoonright_p\text{ is dual-Horn}&\Longrightarrow& b\ge n/6,\\
F^\rho\upharpoonright_p\text{ is 2-CNF (Krom)}&\Longrightarrow& b\ge n/3.
\end{array}
\]
In the original unrenamed source orientation, the stronger bounds $b\ge n/3$ hold separately for Horn, dual-Horn and Krom. These are necessary depth bounds, not sufficient recognition conditions or branch-count lower bounds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For Krom, every original width-three clause must have at least one pinned variable on its support: otherwise that exact original source clause survives with three free literals. Each pinned original variable occurs in exactly four clauses, hence $4b\ge m=4n/3$ and $b\ge n/3$. The argument survives renaming.

For unrenamed Horn, every all-positive original clause must contain a pinned variable; otherwise its surviving three-positive clause violates the Horn grammar. There are exactly $2n/3$ positive source clauses and each pinned variable occurs in precisely two of them. Therefore $2b\ge 2n/3$. The dual-Horn statement follows by exchanging signs.

For arbitrary renaming, let $q_j$ be the number of positive literals in the $j$th renamed source clause. Each variable remains positive exactly twice, so $\sum_jq_j=2n$. Define the nonnegative Horn excess
\[
E_\rho(F)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(q_j-1)_+
\ge\sum_{j=1}^{m}(q_j-1)=2n-m=2n/3.
\]
Assign pinned variables one at a time, retaining only clauses not yet satisfied. When a newly assigned variable is TRUE in the renamed coordinate, its two positive occurrences can satisfy at most two surviving clauses, each with Horn excess at most two. Removing its two FALSE negative literal occurrences does not lower the positive count of surviving clauses. Thus the excess decreases by at most four. When the variable is FALSE, its two negative occurrences can satisfy at most two clauses, each having at most two positive literals and thus excess at most one. The two false positive occurrences can each lower excess by at most one in the clauses that survive. Again the excess decreases by at most four. Charges for clauses removed earlier can only reduce the bound. Hence
\[
 E_\rho(F^\rho\upharpoonright_p)\ge E_\rho(F)-4b\ge 2n/3-4b.
\]
Horn requires zero final excess, giving $b\ge n/6$. Replacing ``positive'' by ``negative'' gives the dual-Horn bound, since every variable has exactly two negative occurrences as well. This proof covers all legal sources, both truth labels, arbitrary original clause permutations and arbitrary polarity renamings.\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The lower bound applies only to \emph{syntactic raw cofactor} recognition, including after paid variable renaming. It does not constrain exact resolution, proof learning, Gaussian elimination, treewidth/matroid or K4 certificates, semantic compilation, direct model construction, early SAT and UNSAT certificates, or a particular arbitrary decider $A$. In particular it does not establish that $2^{n/6}$ branches are necessary. Repeated clause/variable pinning, heuristic preference, parallel search, and memoization may reuse work, and successful terminals may occur before the bound. A strong backdoor is a set whose \emph{all} assignments yield base-class formulas; the theorem also lower-bounds every individual weak syntactic escape, and hence every strong backdoor to the corresponding class.
\end{remark}
