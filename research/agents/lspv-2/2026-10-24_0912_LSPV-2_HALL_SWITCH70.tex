\section{Target, scope and falsifiable mechanisms}
Fix the unique proper signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3CNF language: every original clause has three distinct variables and every original source variable occurs exactly twice with each polarity. Let $n$ be source variables, $m$ original clauses, and $N=(4m+2)w$ encoded bits for $w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil$. Necessarily $4n=3m$. We investigate whether an arbitrary exact decider necessarily constructs a rich backward selector representation or pays comparable alternate symbolic cost. We do not assume this claim.
\begin{description}
\item[] M1: A paid complementary-pair contraction can enable a clause-saturating matching certificate despite impossibility of matching the original clauses to distinct variables. \textbf{PROVED/EXACT, using known matching.}
\item[] M2: No initially tautological eligible pivot implies exponential backward-selection work. \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED} by an explicit connected shifted-permutation family.
\item[] M3: Merely having as many source variables as reduced clauses guarantees Hall matching. \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED} by a source-valid both-label counterexample.
\end{description}
All results concern a specified adaptive algorithm, not every uniform SAT decider.

\section{Reverse semantics and occurrence graph}
Output TRUE of a conjunction of three-literal OR gates requires every clause TRUE. Selecting one satisfying literal \emph{as a witness} per clause is not the assertion that exactly one original literal is TRUE. A backward selector is consistent exactly when no shared original variable is demanded in both polarities. Such a selector exists iff the source is SAT, but constructing all such selectors is optional.
The strict signed occurrence graph has $4n=3m$ occurrence vertices, clause blocks of size three, and $n$ disjoint opposite-polarity conflict graphs $K_{2,2}$, each with four conflict edges. Adding the triangles of individual clauses gives a special degree-four graph, not arbitrary degree-four independent set. Gate counts for evaluating a \emph{specified} assignment are not decision-circuit lower bounds.
\section{Hall certificate after exact source contraction}
For certified complementary pairs, use the pointwise identity
\[
(a\vee b\vee x)\wedge(a\vee b\vee\neg x)\equiv(a\vee b).
\]
PAIRRIGID72 ensures distinct pairs in the declared grammar. If $p$ pairs are contracted, $\delta=m-2p$ original ternaries remain. The reduced formula $R=B\wedge T$ contains $m'=p+\delta=m-p$ clauses. Build a bipartite graph $H_R$ with a left vertex for each reduced clause and a right vertex for each original variable, joining a clause to the variables occurring in it.
\begin{theorem}[Source-coherent Hall certificate; PROVED/EXACT]
If $H_R$ admits a matching saturating all reduced clauses, then the original source $F$ is SAT. Validation, pairing, matching, witness construction and original-source replay run in
\[
O(m\log(m+2)+(n+m)\sqrt{n+m})
\]
addressed-word operations and $O(n+m)$ live words. Failure to find such a matching has \emph{no} UNSAT implication.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For every reduced clause, choose its matched original source variable and set that variable to make its matched literal TRUE. Matching ensures no original variable is assigned contradictory values. Assign all unmatched variables arbitrarily. Every reduced clause is TRUE; since $F\equiv R$ pointwise, every original clause is TRUE. The bipartite graph has at most $3m$ edges. Sorting and exact signed-source validation cost $O(m\log(m+2))$ on the specified RAM; Hopcroft--Karp costs $O((n+m)\sqrt{n+m})$, including unsuccessful augmenting searches. Charge actual rule tests, writes and a complete witness replay. All arrays, queues and read buffers use $O(n+m)$ words.
\end{proof}
Originally $m=4n/3>n$, so no clause-saturating injection exists. After pairing, $m'=m-p\le n$ becomes numerically possible for $p\ge n/3$. This global inequality is \emph{necessary but not sufficient}: Hall's inequalities apply to all subsets of reduced clauses.
\section{Explicit connected shifted-permutation family}
For $q\ge2$, use sources $a_i,b_i,c_i,x_i,y_i,z_i$ for each $i\in\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z$, together with permutations $\sigma,\tau$ of these indices. Add eight clauses for each $i$:
\begin{align*}
 &(a_{i+1}\vee b_i\vee x_i),\quad
 (a_{i+1}\vee b_i\vee\neg x_i),\\
 &(\neg a_i\vee c_i\vee y_i),\quad
 (\neg a_i\vee c_i\vee\neg y_i),\\
 &(\neg b_i\vee x_i\vee z_i),\quad
 (\neg b_i\vee\neg x_i\vee\neg z_{\sigma(i)}),\\
 &(\neg c_i\vee y_i\vee z_i),\quad
 (\neg c_i\vee\neg y_i\vee\neg z_{\tau(i)}).
\end{align*}
\begin{theorem}[Shifted source-safe switch; PROVED/EXACT on the family]
Every such formula is connected, proper, unique and signed exact-$(2,2)$, with $n=6q$, $m=8q$, $p=2q$, $\delta=4q$. Its $6q$ reduced clauses have a saturating matching. If $\sigma$ and $\tau$ have no fixed points, no eligible one-use pivot $x_i,y_i$ produces a tautological resolvent between its two unmatched ternaries. A uniform policy can nevertheless find a polynomial-time SAT certificate by switching from failed pivot tests to bipartite matching.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every $a_i$ occurs twice positively in the preceding module and twice negatively in its own; every $b_i,c_i$ appears twice in a paired clause and twice with the opposite sign in unmatched clauses; every pivot $x_i,y_i$ appears once per sign in its pair and once per sign unmatched. The $\sigma,\tau$ bijections give each $z_i$ two positive and two negative occurrences. The cross-module $a_{i+1}$ makes the source connected. Contract the first two pairs per module. Match the resulting binaries to $b_i,a_i$ and the four unmatched ternaries respectively to $x_i,z_{\sigma(i)},y_i,c_i$. These $6q$ chosen source variables are distinct. They generate the satisfying assignment
\[
a_i=c_i=z_i=0,\qquad b_i=x_i=y_i=1.
\]
A tautological pivot resolvent for $x_i$ requires $z_{\sigma(i)}=z_i$, and for $y_i$ requires $z_{\tau(i)}=z_i$. Both are precluded when the permutations have no fixed points.
\end{proof}
Source renaming, source-wide polarity complementation and clause shuffling preserve this result. This is a recognized easy family, not a polynomial algorithm for the NP-complete language.
\section{Global cardinality does not imply Hall, on both labels}
Take the 198-variable, 264-clause signed exact-$(2,2)$ sources $U,V$ with identical unsigned supports. The UNSAT label of $U$ depends on the previously verified DOECKER-CORE-CERT61 proof; it is \emph{imported} rather than re-established here. The known satisfying assignment of $V$ was replayed on all 264 original clauses. Neither source has complementary pairs. Disjointly adjoin $q$ copies of the six-variable, eight-clause, three-pair SAT gadget established in PAIRPATH73. The result has
\[
 n=198+6q,\quad m=264+8q,\quad p=3q,\quad m'=264+5q.
\]
At $q=66$, $m'=n=594$, yet the 264 core clauses see only 198 core source variables. This subset violates Hall by 66. Both $U$-based and $V$-based sources fail the certificate, while their opposite labels are retained by conjunction with SAT gadgets. The examples are intentionally disconnected; no connectedness is claimed.
