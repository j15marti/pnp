\section{New restricted theorem: bounded-degree indexed triangle enumeration}
An input source $F$ is an arbitrary well-formed, duplicate-free, proper, clausewise-monotone D\"ocker Monotone 3-SAT-(2,2) encoding. Every variable appears exactly twice positively and exactly twice negatively. Let $p$ be any coherent assignment to original variables, and let $G_p$ be the labeled multigraph whose edge $e_j=(u,v,j)$ corresponds to an uncovered original clause $C_j$ with precisely two free original variables $u\ne v$. Different original clauses retain distinct $j$ even if their residual endpoints coincide.
\begin{theorem}[Deterministic original-source triangle acquisition]
For any canonical source $F$ with $m$ clauses and any coherent partial assignment $p$, the binary residual multigraph $G_p$ has maximum degree at most four. If $E=|E(G_p)|$, all distinct source-ID triangles can be listed in lexicographic order of sorted edge-ID triples using at most $9E$ endpoint-neighbor comparisons, $O(m+n+E)$ indexed word operations and $O((n+E)\log(n+m))$ writable bits. The number of distinct source-ID triangles is at most $3E$. The canonical lexicographic edge-disjoint greedy packing can be acquired in the same asymptotic bounds; its output is \emph{identical}, including tie-breaking, to the previous $\binom E3$ enumeration.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each variable's four source occurrences bound its degree in every uncovered binary-clause multigraph by four. Build a direct array of $n+1$ adjacency heads, storing at each endpoint all $(j,\text{neighbor})$ records, and traverse original clause IDs $j$ in increasing order. For $e_j=(u,v)$, form a candidate triangle only from two other edges $a=(u,w)$, $b=(v,w)$ having $a>j$ and $b>j$, distinct IDs and common third vertex $w$. At most $(\deg u-1)(\deg v-1)\le9$ candidate pairs are examined. Every source-ID triangle has a unique smallest edge ID $j$ and is emitted exactly once at that iteration. Sorting the at most nine local candidates makes the global sequence exactly lexicographic on sorted edge-ID triples. Keep an $m$-bit used-ID vector and accept a triple iff its three bits are zero. This reproduces the previous greedy packing, even with parallel residual edges and mixed original polarity. Counting each triangle against its three constituent edges and at most nine witnesses per edge yields $3T\le9E$, so $T\le3E$. Direct adjacency arrays and the used vector have the claimed size, and a fixed bounded number of operations is paid per source edge and local pair. The original source and every clause index remain intact.\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Model firewall.} The theorem is deterministic indexed word-RAM, not a bound on an arbitrary randomized/FLMV machine. Sequentially reading all input bits costs $\Omega(N)$ time; arbitrary-order duplicate/malformed grammar validation is charged separately (e.g., deterministic record sorting, $O(m\log m)$ comparisons and nonconstant bit cost). Each source-ID/vertex comparison and indexed read needs its native bit/address cost on an indexed TM; allocating $O(E\log N)$ bits is \emph{not} a proof of $N^{1/2}$ space. The earlier exact affine-union theorem still contributes $O(3^\nu\operatorname{poly}(N))$ time for triangle packing.
