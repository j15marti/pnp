\section{Falsifiable mechanisms}
The three tests were whether arc/path consistency gains information on a proper reverse-selector 3CNF CSP, whether an UNSAT signed exact-$(2,2)$ six-variable source can exist, and whether linear unmatched-pair defect forces large continuation work. \section{Exact backward representation}
For a proper 3CNF $F=\bigwedge_{j=1}^m(\ell_{j1}\vee\ell_{j2}\vee\ell_{j3})$ with named original sources, introduce a three-valued selector $s_j$. Define $R_F(x,s)=\bigwedge_j[\ell_{j,s_j}(x)=1]$. Then $F(x)=1$ iff $\exists s\,R_F(x,s)=1$, and $F$ is SAT iff there is a set of one selected occurrence per clause with no complementary original-source pair. A clause may contain multiple true literals; exactly one \emph{selected} literal is only a witness. Reconstruct original values from consistent selected signs and replay all clauses.

The fan-in-two $\mathrm{OR2}/\mathrm{AND2}$ evaluation circuit has $2m$ OR gates, $m-1$ AND gates, $3m-1$ gates total and $6m-2$ directed gate-input wires. Functional Tseitin constraints plus output TRUE use $9m-2$ clauses. With $n$ shared NOT gates on source wires, the counts become $n+3m-1$ gates, $n+6m-2$ wires, and $9m-2+2n$ CNF clauses. Trace auxiliaries are uniquely determined by source bits. The circuit evaluates assignments; it does not decide SAT on formula encodings.

For strict signed exact-$(2^+,2^-)$, every original source variable occurs twice per sign, hence $4n=3m$. Its complement-occurrence graph has $4n$ vertices and $4n$ edges, being $n$ disjoint $K_{2,2}$. Clause triangles add $3m=4n$ edges, making an exact four-regular graph with $8n$ edges. An independent set of cardinality $m$ means exactly one selected occurrence per clause and is equivalent to SAT.

\section{Theorem 59A: Local consistency is initially vacuous}
\begin{theorem}
For every proper width-$h$ CNF, $h\ge2$, the associated one-occurrence-per-clause binary selector CSP is strongly $h$-consistent before conditioning. In particular, every proper 3CNF, SAT or UNSAT, is initially arc- and path-consistent, so ordinary arc/path consistency cannot delete any original domain choice or allowed binary pair.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix at most $h-1$ mutually compatible previously selected occurrences in different clauses. Since a target clause contains $h$ different original variable IDs, each existing choice can contradict at most one of its $h$ candidate occurrences. At most $h-1$ candidates are forbidden, leaving a compatible selection. For $h=3$, the $t=1$ and $t=2$ cases establish arc consistency and pair support in every third domain, respectively.
\end{proof}
The threshold is sharp: three compatible negative witnesses $\neg x,\neg y,\neg z$ from distinct clauses jointly block every witness of $(x\vee y\vee z)$. A fully exact-$(2,2)$ shifted-prism source below has this obstruction even though the formula itself is SAT. For $h=2$, the four-clause UNSAT 2CNF over two variables remains selector-arc-consistent, while ordinary implication/SCC decides it in linear time. This is a restricted local-consistency failure, not a time lower bound for SAT.

\section{Theorem 59B: No six-variable signed exact-(2,2) UNSAT source}
\begin{theorem}
Every proper signed exact-$(2^+,2^-)$ 3CNF on $n=6$ original variables is satisfiable. A strict UNSAT instance therefore needs $n\ge9$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The occurrence identity forces $m=4n/3=8$. Each proper clause is false on exactly $2^{6-3}=8$ of the 64 complete source assignments. If all eight clauses were UNSAT jointly, their falsifying sets would cover all 64 assignments; because their sizes sum to 64, the sets would have to be pairwise disjoint. Any two clause-falsifier sets are disjoint only if their source clauses contain complementary occurrences of some common variable. Thus all $\binom82=28$ distinct clause pairs must receive a complement occurrence edge. But every original variable contributes exactly $2\cdot2=4$ opposite-sign occurrence edges, total $4n=24$. Each edge belongs to one clause pair. Since $24<28$, the requisite disjointness is impossible. At least one satisfying original assignment exists.
\end{proof}
For $n=3$, $m=4$, the elementary clause union bound already gives SAT. This finite-size theorem does not bound general signed exact-$(2,2)$ worst-case decision time.

\section{Theorem 59C: Charged bucket elimination}
\begin{theorem}
Given a certified selector-variable elimination order of induced width $\omega$, exact backward-SAT decision and original-source witness reconstruction take $O(m^2 3^{\omega+1}+N/w)$ charged word operations with a straightforward table implementation. Computing a deterministic min-fill order by a direct polynomial method adds at most $O(m^4)$ word operations. Retained tables and backpointers use $O(m3^{\omega+1}+m^2)$ words. Neither bound is a lower bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compile a binary forbidden-value factor for each complementary pair of clause occurrences, respecting shared original variable identifiers. Process each selector in the supplied order. For each assignment to its at most $\omega$ current neighbors, enumerate its three possible choices and retain the neighbor assignment iff some choice satisfies every incident factor, recording the choice. Each step is existential projection of its bucket. An empty factor proves UNSAT; otherwise reverse stored choices, reconstruct one shared original-source assignment, and replay $F$. The conservative factor-table counting proves the upper bounds. A verifier recomputes the table projections and full source replay.
\end{proof}
This is an exact instantiation of standard bounded-width CSP elimination, not a newly discovered general CSP theorem.

\section{Fully unpaired monotone exact-(2,2) application}
For $q\ge4$, let $a_j,b_j,c_j$ be source variables with indices modulo $q$. For every column $j$ include
\[
(a_{j-1}\vee a_j\vee c_j),\quad(b_{j-1}\vee b_j\vee c_j),
\]
\[
(\neg a_{j-1}\vee\neg a_j\vee\neg c_{j+1}),\quad
(\neg b_{j-1}\vee\neg b_j\vee\neg c_{j+1}).
\]
All $n=3q$ original variables appear twice positively and twice negatively; all $m=4q$ clauses are proper and distinct. No positive/negative support pair matches, and no complementary single-pivot pair exists; the Role-1 PAIRKERNEL73 unmatched defect is $\delta=4q$. Let $C_j$ contain the four selector blocks of column $j$. Every complement-conflict factor is within one or two adjacent columns. The path bags $C_0\cup C_j\cup C_{j+1}$, $1\le j\le q-2$, have size at most 12 and give induced width at most 11. Recognition of the canonical grammar and complete source correspondence is polynomial, and this certificate yields a $O(q3^{12})$ restricted table algorithm.

An even simpler direct source-aware shortcut must be stated: set all $a_j=b_j=0$ and all $c_j=1$. It satisfies the whole source in $O(q)$ reconstruction plus replay. Thus neither $\delta=\Theta(q)$ nor a high theoretical enumeration count compels real cost here.
