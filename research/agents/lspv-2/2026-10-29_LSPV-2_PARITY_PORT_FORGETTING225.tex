\section{BUILD I: exact parity projection in $4b$ proper ternary clauses}
Fix $b\ge2$, $t\in\{0,1\}$, and free Boolean inputs $x_1,\ldots,x_b$. Introduce $y_2,\ldots,y_b$ and two additional variables $a,c$. For each $i=2,\ldots,b$, impose the parity equation $y_i=y_{i-1}\oplus x_i$, where $y_1=x_1$. Encode each $w=u\oplus v$ by precisely four proper ternary clauses
\[
 (u\vee v\vee\neg w)\land(u\vee\neg v\vee w)
 \land(\neg u\vee v\vee w)\land(\neg u\vee\neg v\vee\neg w).
\]
Enforce $y_b=t$ by the four clauses $(\ell\vee\pm a\vee\pm c)$ for all independent polarity pairs, where $\ell=y_b$ if $t=1$ and $\ell=\neg y_b$ if $t=0$. Call the resulting proper 3CNF $P_{b,t}(X,Y,a,c)$.

\begin{theorem}[Linear proper 3CNF representation and exact projection]\label{thm:parity}
$P_{b,t}$ is connected, has precisely $2b+1$ variables and $4b$ distinct proper ternary clauses, and satisfies the pointwise identity
\[
\boxed{\forall X:\quad \exists Y,a,c\;P_{b,t}(X,Y,a,c)
 \iff \bigoplus_{j=1}^{b}x_j=t.}
\]
For every $j\in\{1,\ldots,b\}$ and every assignment to $X\setminus\{x_j\}$,
\[
\boxed{\exists x_j,Y,a,c\;P_{b,t}(X,Y,a,c)\iff\top.}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The four XOR clauses exclude exactly the assignments where the output is not the XOR of its two inputs. Hence the internal $y_i$ values are uniquely determined by $X$, and $y_b=\oplus_j x_j$. The four terminal clauses are jointly satisfiable over $a,c$ exactly when $\ell$ is true; otherwise they exclude all four $(a,c)$ assignments. The chain and terminal clauses are connected. There are $b-1$ XOR gates and one four-clause terminal, giving $4(b-1)+4=4b$ clauses; variables are $b+(b-1)+2=2b+1$. For each fixed assignment to $b-1$ inputs, exactly one choice of the last unassigned parity input makes the XOR equal $t$. That choice extends uniquely to the internal chain values. Thus forgetting even one free bit yields TRUE.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Optimal auxiliary-free CNF for parity]\label{thm:noaux}
Every CNF formula over $X$ alone that represents the parity relation $\bigoplus_j x_j=t$ must contain at least $2^{b-1}$ distinct non-tautological clauses, each of width $b$. The bound is attained by taking for every forbidden parity assignment $\alpha$ the full-length clause whose unique falsifying assignment is $\alpha$. Consequently no auxiliary-free CNF of width at most three expresses parity on $b\ge4$ variables.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every non-tautological implicate of parity must include every input variable. Otherwise its falsifying partial assignment leaves one free bit whose value can always be chosen to satisfy parity; the clause would incorrectly exclude a satisfying assignment. A full-width non-tautological clause has exactly one falsifying full assignment. Since $2^{b-1}$ assignments fail parity, at least one distinct full-width clause is required per forbidden assignment. The canonical set of such clauses meets the bound.\end{proof}

\paragraph{SAT-side and UNSAT-side control.} Define $U_b=P_{b,0}(X,Y_0)\land P_{b,1}(X,Y_1)$, using disjoint private variables but shared free boundary $X$. For every boundary assignment the two projected parity equations contradict; thus $U_b$ is UNSAT, and remains so under existentially forgetting any subset of boundary variables. A polynomially recognizable parity grammar lets one common program distinguish these two structured outcomes without a free side bit, via ordinary GF$(2)$ reasoning. This is an easy-family hostile control, not a SAT-hard lower bound.

\section{BUILD II: connected strict signed exact22 embedding and retained-port floor}
Apply the previously verified all-port pointwise normalizer $C$ from report224 to the canonical ordinary 3CNF $P_{b,t}$. That compiler retains every original input name as a physical unquantified port, balances signed occurrences using the previously published $J$ and $Q$ gadgets, and produces a connected proper strict signed exact-$(2,2)$ formula. By composition, the exact external boundary relation is still parity. This introduces only $O(b)$ variables and clauses, hence $O(b\log b)$ ordinary DIMACS bits, but with a large multiplicative constant. The tests below construct the actual strict clauses for $b\in\{3,4,6\}$ and verify signed occurrence quotas, incidence connectedness, and valid lifted positive witnesses. For negative boundary pins, the implication rests on the independently established source-compiler projection lemma rather than brute-force search over hundreds of auxiliary bits.

\begin{proposition}[Quantitative physical retention obstruction]\label{prop:floor}
Any strict signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3CNF encoding that retains at least $r$ distinct physical free variable names contains at least
\[
\boxed{32\sum_{j=1}^{r}\bigl(1+\lfloor\log_{10}j\rfloor\bigr)=\Omega(r\log(r+1))\text{ bits}}
\]
in ASCII decimal variable digits alone. On source families with $N=\Theta(b\log b)$, an output length $N_{\rm child}\le N^\lambda$ for fixed $0<\lambda<1$ implies
\[
 r=O\!\left(\frac{b^\lambda}{(\log b)^{1-\lambda}}\right)
\quad (r\to\infty).
\]
This implication requires the child to retain all $r$ designated names as distinct physical free ports; it is \emph{not} a decision-only SAT lower bound and does not hold for a compiler that truly forgets those ports.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each retained physical name appears in two positive and two negative literal occurrences. Among $r$ distinct positive integer names, the total number of decimal digits is minimized by $1,\ldots,r$. Four copies of each digit string, eight bits per ASCII byte, give the displayed bound; signs, spaces and other literals only add bits. If $N=\Theta(b\log b)$, $r\log r=O((b\log b)^\lambda)$ and, for growing $r$, inversion of this monotone function gives the stated order.\end{proof}

\paragraph{Essential firewall.} There is no contradiction between Proposition~\ref{prop:floor} and Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}. Retaining $b-1$ independent physical ports in a strict exact22 child has a linear-like representation floor even when the resulting semantic relation is TRUE. The correct \emph{decision-only} successor in this easy case may instead discard every port and output a fixed YES base. No theorem permits doing this for arbitrary SAT instances without discovering the correct answer.
