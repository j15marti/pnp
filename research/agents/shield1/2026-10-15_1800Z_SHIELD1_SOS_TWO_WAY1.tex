\section{Exact interface}
For disjoint proper source 3-CNFs $A,B$ with $m_A,m_B\ge1$, the gadget $\mathcal O(A,B)$ emits $(s\vee a\vee y_C),(\neg y_C\vee b\vee c)$ per left clause $C=(a\vee b\vee c)$ and $(\neg s\vee d\vee z_D),(\neg z_D\vee e\vee f)$ per right clause $D=(d\vee e\vee f)$. With $q=m_A+m_B$, output has exactly $2q$ proper distinct clauses and $v_A+v_B+q+1$ variables. It is satisfiable exactly if $A$ or $B$ is satisfiable. Substitutions $(s,y_C,z_D)=(0,\neg a,0)$ and $(1,0,\neg d)$ send clauses to the corresponding leaf or tautologies. In dense fixed-record encoding with signed literal width $w(v)=1+\lceil\log_2(v+1)\rceil$, gamma headers, and $v=v_A+v_B+q+1$, encoded length is $|\gamma(v+1)|+|\gamma(2q+1)|+6qw(v)=\Theta(q\log(q+2))$ when source variables are active. Local clause streaming uses $O(\log(q+2))$ generator scratch; sparse identifiers and any source normalization must be charged independently.
\section{New theorem}
An equality-style real SoS certificate is
\[
-1=\sum_i q_i^2+\sum_C h_Cp_C+\sum_x r_x(x^2-x),
\qquad p_C=\prod_{\ell\in C}(1-\ell).
\]
Degree is the maximum formal degree of each complete summand.
\begin{theorem}
For UNSAT proper source 3-CNFs on disjoint variables,
\[
\boxed{\max\{D_{\rm SoS}(A),D_{\rm SoS}(B)\}
\le D_{\rm SoS}(\mathcal O(A,B))
\le \max\{D_{\rm SoS}(A),D_{\rm SoS}(B)\}+2.}
\]
For $\Phi=\mathcal O(\mathcal O(A,B),\mathcal O(C,D))$ with four UNSAT leaves,
\[
\boxed{\max_iD_{\rm SoS}(F_i)\le D_{\rm SoS}(\Phi)\le\max_iD_{\rm SoS}(F_i)+4.}
\]
The constructive upper transformation has polynomial bit cost for explicitly encoded rational-coefficient source certificates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $B_s=s^2-s$. For either selector guard $g\in\{s,1-s\}$, there is a \emph{formal} identity
\[
\boxed{gq^2=(gq)^2-B_sq^2}.
\]
It is essential to retain the selector Boolean axiom. For a left clause $C=(a\vee b\vee c)$, let $u=(1-s)(1-a)$, $v=(1-b)(1-c)$, $P=u(1-y_C)$ and $Q=y_Cv$ be its two split-clause polynomials. Then
\[
\boxed{(1-s)p_C=vP+uQ},
\]
and the right analogue uses $u'=s(1-d)$ to give $sp_D$. All multipliers have degree at most two. Multiply a degree-$d_A$ source certificate for $A$ by $1-s$, replacing every resulting guarded square and guarded clause polynomial with these identities. Source Boolean terms remain target Boolean-ideal terms, and the new selector corrections are $-B_sq_i^2$. This gives a valid SoS identity with left side $-(1-s)$ and degree $\le d_A+2$. Repeat for $B$ with guard $s$ to obtain an identity with left side $-s$ and degree $\le d_B+2$. Add them to obtain $-1$. For the lower bounds, project any target SoS identity using the legal literal/constant branch substitutions. Squares remain squares; all axiom images are source clause polynomials, signed Boolean polynomials, or zero, and degree cannot increase. Compose two OR layers for the four-leaf result. In explicit rational encoding, expanding $q_i^2$ costs at most quadratic monomial overhead and polynomial coefficient bits; no real-arithmetic unit-cost assumption is used.
\end{proof}
\section{Adversarial audit and limits}
The shortcut $gq^2=(gq)^2$ is false as a formal equality: take $s=1/2,q=1$. Finite symbolic auditing constructed nonzero-square source certificates for two independent eight-clause contradictions and translated both through all 32 split-clause ideal terms, two squares and four Boolean terms; the expanded residual over the integers was identically zero. Twelve additional selector-square identities, plus negative controls omitting the correction, were checked. Auditing code: \texttt{shield1\_sos\_guard\_audit.py}, available with the full local manuscript. These checks do not establish external proof lower bounds. The theorem covers \emph{equality-style} SoS, not arbitrary inequality-style semialgebraic certificates. Published parity/Tseitin SoS lower bounds must be matched to the exact clause-equation encoding before their import. As earlier EXHAUST1 and wrapper adversaries show, proof-system lower bounds do not force work by arbitrary SAT deciders. No unrestricted resource contradiction follows.
