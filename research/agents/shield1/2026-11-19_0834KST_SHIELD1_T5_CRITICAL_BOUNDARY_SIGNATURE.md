## Theorem A: 5-entry orbit quotient, 3-bit critical support (PROVED/EXACT)
For source G with ordered terminals s,t, put F_G(d)=number of T5 witness colorings with x_s=0, x_t=d. Rotating every color by the same element of Z5 gives exactly #Hom(G,T5)=5 sum_{d=0}^4 F_G(d), and the number with any fixed terminal colors (a,b) equals F_G((b-a) mod 5). Let B_G={d:F_G(d)>0}. The exact two-terminal Boolean state consists of at most five bits. If H is UNSAT but H-e is SAT with e=(s,t), B_{H-e} is nonempty and contained in {0,2,4}; otherwise a witness to H-e would satisfy e, contradicting H's UNSAT status. Thus only three support bits can be nonzero for critical deletions, but computing those bits requires real work.

For source-authenticated series and parallel compositions with disjoint interiors:
F_parallel(d)=F_1(d)F_2(d);
F_series(d)=sum_{r mod 5} F_1(r)F_2(d-r).
The corresponding Boolean signatures compose by intersection and mod-5 sumset. This is a rotation reduction of an exact 5-by-5 boundary matrix to FIVE integers, or FIVE Boolean bits for SAT. It is NOT valid across an unproved separator or overlapping interiors. To construct full witnesses one must record or recompute compatible intermediate offsets, recover each interior assignment, align its rotation, verify every original arc, and pay output cost. A single critical-deletion witness does not reveal the other two possible bits. Pinning endpoints to query the other bits may leave the critical-deletion promise.

## Theorem B: small summary does not imply cheap acquisition (PROVED conditional only on known T5 NP-completeness)
Computing whether a nonempty five-bit boundary signature exists on arbitrary connected two-terminal T5 inputs is NP-hard. Given arbitrary T5 instance D, take one vertex v_i from each of its undirected components, add fresh q with q->v_i, and add degree-one terminals s->q->t. If D is SAT, independently rotate each component so x(v_i)=1; set x(q)=0, x(s)=4, x(t)=1. This extends every component witness and satisfies all new arcs. Conversely a witness on the new connected graph restricts to one on D. Empty sources are handled by the fresh terminal path alone. Thus nonemptiness of a tiny terminal state decides general T5 CSP. This reduction DOES NOT preserve edge-criticality or growing girth, and establishes no lower bound independent of P versus NP.

## Four-vertex critical counterexample (PROVED/EXACT finite exhaustive check)
The connected acyclic H4 with arcs (0,1),(0,2),(2,1),(3,0),(3,1) is UNSAT. Exhaustive 5^4 enumeration confirms ALL five edge deletions SAT. Their five-bit signatures, indexed by differences 0,1,2,3,4, are 00100, 10100, 10100, 10100, 00101. The corresponding gauge-fixed count vectors are (0,0,1,0,0), (1,0,1,0,0) three times, and (0,0,1,0,1). In the first deletion there is only one rotation-orbit of witnesses: exactly five original-label witnesses. Therefore intrinsic criticality does not force a full {0,2,4} support or large witness count. This control has undirected girth three and does not settle growing-girth hardness.

## Direct Boolean-hash collision (FALSIFIED count preservation)
Let P_k be k internally vertex-disjoint, directed two-edge paths s->u_i->t, for k=2,3. Their two-terminal exact count vectors are (0,1,1,0,4) and (0,1,1,0,8). Both have the SAME Boolean support {1,2,4}. Closing each with a genuine reverse arc t->s yields respectively 25 and 45 full original-label witnesses, verified by independent brute force over 5^4 and 5^5 labelings. Hence a Boolean hash can be exact for SAT feasibility of proper two-terminal compositions but is NOT a source-preserving exact-count quotient. Retaining only the mask also discards internal witness pointers.
