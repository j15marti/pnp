\section{Claim card}
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{Claim ID:} PROJECTED-SOLUTION-COVER-GATE1.
\item[] \textbf{Exact quantifiers:} for every $m\geq1$, fixed width $k\geq1$,
finite family size $q$, finite auxiliary sets $Z_1,\ldots,Z_q$, and width-$k$
CNFs $G_j(X,Z_j)$, if
\[
 \sol_X(F_m)=\bigcup_{j=1}^{q}\proj_X\sol(G_j),\qquad
 F_m=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}(x_i\vee y_i),
\]
then every satisfiable $G_j$ has at least one original-variable occurrence
from each block $\{x_i,y_i\}$, at least $m$ such occurrences, and at least
$\lceil m/k\rceil$ clauses.
\item[] \textbf{Model and measure:} explicit width-$k$ CNF, globally indexed
source variables or a locally indexed child with its charged source-projection
map, and ordinary encoded bit length.
\item[] \textbf{SAME\_REALIZATION object:} one emitted child together with the
map needed to interpret its original-variable coordinates.
\item[] \textbf{Weakest useful theorem:} extend RED-0001's child-size lower
bound to existential auxiliary projection without claiming a decision-only
lower bound.
\item[] \textbf{Falsifier:} a satisfiable bounded-width child whose projected
solutions lie inside $\sol(F_m)$ while one original block is absent.
\end{description}

\section{Proof}
Fix a satisfiable child $G_j$.  Exact union equality gives
$\proj_X\sol(G_j)\subseteq\sol_X(F_m)$.  Suppose $G_j$ contains no occurrence
of $x_i$ or $y_i$ for some block $i$.  Choose a satisfying assignment to
$X\cup Z_j$ and change only $x_i,y_i$ to zero.  Every literal occurring in
$G_j$ keeps its value, so the child remains satisfied with all auxiliary
values fixed.  Its projected $X$-assignment now falsifies the $i$th clause of
$F_m$, contradicting containment.  Thus each of the $m$ blocks supplies an
original-variable occurrence.  A width-$k$ clause contains occurrences from
at most $k$ blocks, proving the clause and occurrence bounds.

With global source indices, the $m$ original-variable references require
$\Omega(m\log m)$ bits.  If the child renames variables locally, its charged
interface must identify the $m$ source coordinates used by the projection;
that explicit map has the same asymptotic size.  Since the ordinary encoding
of $F_m$ is $\Theta(m\log m)$, no satisfiable child has fixed-power encoded
shrink.  A compressed implicit map changes the representation and falls
outside the lemma rather than refuting it.

\section{Hostile controls and limitations}
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{Easy family and independent products:} $F_m$ is a disjoint
product of width-two clauses and is easy for propagation/component methods.
The result is a representation lower bound, never a machine-time lower bound.
\item[] \textbf{Padding, renaming, and auxiliaries:} unused auxiliaries and
signed block renamings preserve the proof; an auxiliary-only clause cannot
repair an omitted original block.  Auxiliary variables may still help other
functions or non-cover interfaces.
\item[] \textbf{Source carry and current-scale calls:} no SAT call occurs in
the proof.  Carrying an implicit source-coordinate decoder is not free; if it
is compressed, that is a different representation requiring its own access
and return theorem.
\item[] \textbf{Oracle and algebraic replacement:} a semantic SAT oracle may
emit only a constant answer and thereby escapes witness preservation.  The
lemma therefore supplies no nonrelativizing or nonalgebrizing hinge.
\item[] \textbf{Finite versus uniform depth:} this is a one-application
obstruction.  It neither constructs nor rules out a growing-depth uniform
algorithm, and it does not change ARC's finite existential iteration theorem.
\end{description}
