\section{Exact statement and repaired proof}
Let $y\in\{0,1\}^m$ be a complete actual ordinary SAT input with blank reads at addresses $h\ge m$. A deterministic guest uses read, ordinary left/right moves and indexed jump$(j)$. Fix an honest upper bound $B\ge0$ on the TOTAL number of ordinary moves in the remaining run. Indexed jump addresses and guest tapes are charged separately. Define
\[
q_B(h)=\begin{cases}h,&h<m+B,\\\mathrm{FAR},&h\ge m+B.\end{cases}
\]
The abstract machine updates finite coordinates on ordinary moves; FAR remains FAR under ordinary moves; \emph{every indexed jump, including from FAR, resets the state to} $q_B(j)$.
\begin{theorem}[Jump-reset bisimulation, restricted head model]
Every deterministic guest read transcript is preserved by this quotient for every source and any interleaving of jumps, reads and at most $B$ ordinary moves. The quotient needs $m+B+1$ states, hence at most $\lceil\log_2(m+B+1)\rceil$ additional coordinate bits. If all other guest state is transported exactly, both guest SAT and UNSAT decisions are preserved.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct over guest instructions, maintaining either an exact physical head or FAR with actual coordinate at least $m+B$. FAR reads are blank, since the physical head cannot move left across the $B$-cell margin with at most $B$ ordinary moves. Exact coordinates update exactly or saturate. On an indexed jump, both real and simulated heads RESET to $j$ and $q_B(j)$, even if the preceding head was FAR. Thus a jump may reenter the physical input, but both machines agree after it. Read agreement preserves deterministic subsequent control. There are $m+B$ exact coordinates and one sentinel. The statement is confined to a head subsystem and does not claim free index-tape access or any child-size compression.
\end{proof}
The prior HEAD-HORIZON255 proof asserted no valid read can ever follow a FAR entry. This is \textbf{false}: jump$(m+B+100)$, read blank, jump$(0)$, read $y[0]$ contains no ordinary move. Its previously specified \emph{jump-reset transition implementation}, however, is correct; this repairs the explanation, not its restricted result. An absorbing sticky FAR on jumps is incorrect.

\section{Native index-tape source of an uncharged tariff}
FLMV's machine model associates an index tape with the read-only input and erases its CONTENTS on a jump. Its published definition does not explicitly say that erasure resets the INDEX-TAPE HEAD. Therefore the following is a conditional machine-variant falsifier, not an asserted lower bound on all FLMV simulators.
\begin{theorem}[Conditional full-rescan penalty]
If erasure preserves index-tape head coordinate $H$, a correct SAT guest wrapper can make $q$ indexed jumps addressing $2^H$ in $H+2q+O(1)$ guest operations, while an implementation that fully scans address cells $0,\ldots,H$ anew per jump performs at least $q(H+1)$ address-cell inspections. With $q=H$, this is quadratic versus linear in $H$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Move the initially blank index-tape head $H$ cells right. Repeat: write one high-position 1; index-jump, which erases contents but by hypothesis retains the head coordinate. Then invoke any correct ordinary SAT decider $A$ and return its decision unchanged. The wrapper is correct on YES and NO. Each scan costs $H+1$, while each loop iteration costs only a write and a jump. Better incremental simulators are not excluded.
\end{proof}
Thus a hypothesis that explicit address bits arrive one per charged step cannot be silently transferred to the native erasing index-tape model. Every guest tape, erased-content behavior, source probe and virtual child-bit access must be accounted in the same realization. The whole construction remains within the integrated ARC/FLMV program, not a competing hardness route.
