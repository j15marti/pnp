\section{One-sided PV-reflection criterion}
Let $T$ be a fixed feasible arithmetic theory whose proofs of universal bounded polynomial-time predicates translate uniformly into polynomial-size Extended Frege proofs, as Cook proved for the appropriate PV formulas. Assume signed two-input ER definitions and their facets embed with polynomial source-labelled overhead in the unchanged $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$.
\begin{theorem}[One-sided sufficient bridge, CONDITIONAL]
Suppose a fixed, total, polynomial-clock binary SAT classifier $A$ rejects every valid UNSAT input. Suppose $T$ has a fixed \emph{formal proof} of only
\[
\forall F,a\quad
\bigl(\operatorname{ValidCNF}(F)\wedge \operatorname{Eval}(F,a)\bigr)
\longrightarrow A(F)\ne\mathrm{UNSAT}. \tag{OS}
\]
Then there exists a polynomial $p_A$ such that for every UNSAT $F$,
$W_{R_{\mathrm{mix341}}}(F)\le p_A(|\operatorname{encode}(F)|)$; hence B3's conclusion follows under these additional assumptions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a valid UNSAT original CNF $F$ of $N$ bits. Actual execution of $A$ produces UNSAT by semantic completeness; its polynomial clock gives a polynomial-size grounded circuit and an authenticated local evaluation of that output, without using correctness of $A$. Apply the \emph{fixed} proof of (OS) in $T$ to the polynomial-time circuit predicates for valid input, original-clause evaluation and $A$'s output. The feasible propositional translation gives a polynomial-size EF derivation of the Boolean statement that rejection excludes an assignment satisfying $F$. Ground the program's already computed rejection bit and the validity of the original input; obtain an EF derivation of $\neg\operatorname{Eval}(F,a)$. Conversely, from each \emph{actual original source clause} derive its Boolean evaluation output by paid gate definitions and strict Resolution on the clause literals; combine those outputs with paid AND gates to derive $\operatorname{Eval}(F,a)$. This contradicts the preceding EF theorem. Standard EF-to-ER polynomial simulation and the assumed authentic paid ER-to-native encoding produce a shared native contradiction DAG with polynomially many charged nodes and polynomial encoded bits, using the original clause IDs. The compiler is uniform given the fixed $T$ proof and circuit templates; its polynomial depends on $A$ and the fixed theory proof, never on $F$. Maximize over $N$.
\end{proof}
This is a noncircular \emph{conditional} transfer, not the required B3 implication from bare SAT in P: neither SAT in P nor completeness of the native rules supplies a $T$ proof of (OS) or resolves the historical DEF facet syntax. A proof of two-sided machine correctness is unnecessary, but the one-sided formal proof is an unproved extra hypothesis.

\section{An explicit Godel countercontrol to all-decider reflection}
Let $T$ be consistent, recursively axiomatized, able to formalize polynomial-time syntax verification and fixed program execution, and subject to the ordinary second incompleteness theorem. Assume its authentic proof predicate $\operatorname{Prf}_T(p,\bot)$ is polynomial-time checkable in the \emph{length of the proposed proof} and that $T$ proves the simple source-coding and proof-checker implications used below. Let
\[
\Sigma(p)=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{|p|}(z\vee \ell_i(p_i)),\qquad
\ell_i(1)=u_i,\quad \ell_i(0)=\neg u_i.
\]
These are canonically recognizable, proper distinct signed binary source clauses. The explicit assignment $z=1$ satisfies $\Sigma(p)$ for every bit string $p$.

\begin{proposition}[Failure of all-decider automatic reflection]
Conditional on SAT in P, for each correct total polynomial-time SAT decider $A$ there exists a total correct polynomial-time SAT decider $B$ which agrees with $A$ on \emph{all} valid and malformed inputs, but $T$ cannot prove its one-sided universal rejection soundness (OS).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The fixed machine $B$ recognizes a canonical $\Sigma(p)$ and checks the candidate string $p$ as a formal $T$ proof of $\bot$. If the check succeeds $B$ returns UNSAT, otherwise it runs $A$. On other strings it runs $A$. This is polynomial time for fixed $A$. Since $T$ is actually consistent, there is no accepted $p$ and $B$ agrees extensionally with $A$ on every string. Within $T$, however, for arbitrary $p$, $\operatorname{Prf}_T(p,\bot)$ would imply $B(\Sigma(p))=\mathrm{UNSAT}$, while $T$ proves $\Sigma(p)$ has witness $z=1$. Thus $T\vdash\mathrm{OS}(B)$ would entail $T\vdash \forall p\,\neg\operatorname{Prf}_T(p,\bot)$, its own standard consistency sentence, contradicting Godel II under the stated hypotheses.
\end{proof}
The countercontrol rules out a strategy that requires (OS) to be provable for \emph{every} semantically correct polynomial-time SAT decider. It does not refute the existence of a specially selected one with a $T$ proof and does not refute B1, B2 or B3. This refines project LSPV-1/415 (soundness-index complexity) and /417 (dead-branch audits), rather than claiming a new general incompleteness theorem.
