\begin{theorem}[Uniform proof generator]\label{t1}
Fix a polynomial-time sound complete original-source proof verifier $V(F,\pi)$. Assume SAT in P and one fixed polynomial bound $L(N)$ such that every valid UNSAT $F$ has an accepted proof of at most $L(|F|)$ encoded bits. Then a single uniform deterministic polynomial-time total generator outputs genuine $V$ proofs on every UNSAT input and no accepted proof on any SAT or malformed input. Conversely a total polynomial-time producer of authentic proofs on all UNSAT inputs implies a polynomial bit-length bound and SAT in P.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First increase the polynomial bound by $\lceil\log_2(L(N)+1)\rceil+2$ bits, use a fixed-width certificate-length header and check a zero trailer; rename the resulting polynomial cutoff $L(N)$. This makes padding uniquely decodable without assuming prefix-free native proof syntax. For each source $F$ and candidate prefix $u$, the relation
\[
E(F,u,1^{L(N)})\iff\exists v,\ |uv|=L(N)
\ \land\ V(F,\mathrm{unpad}(uv))=1
\]
is in NP by guessing $v$ and running the fixed authenticated verifier in time polynomial in the fully written evidence. SAT in P gives one fixed P-time decider for this NP language. Beginning with empty prefix on UNSAT $F$, choose 0 if the corresponding prefix has an extension, otherwise 1. At most $L(N)+1$ tests recover a complete valid proof; verify and output it. On SAT or malformed input no accepted extension exists by soundness. The polynomial $L$ and one decider are fixed globally, not chosen per input length or family. For the converse, a total polynomial-time generator has polynomial output length and its polynomial-time checker decides UNSAT exactly.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[B3 optimizer-gap corollary]\label{t2}
Assume additionally that a single polynomial $p$ satisfies: every $q$-paid-node proof of $F$ in the \emph{same fully specified Rmix341 grammar} has some authentic proof with at most $p(|F|+q)$ bits. Then under SAT in P, polynomial $\overline W_R(N)$ is equivalent to a uniform polynomial-time source-exact R proof generator.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A polynomial worst-case node bound plus the existential bit-normalizer gives a polynomial bit bound and Theorem~\ref{t1}. A P-time generator outputs polynomially many proof bits, which dominate its number of paid nodes. Neither direction computes a shortest derivation.
\end{proof}
