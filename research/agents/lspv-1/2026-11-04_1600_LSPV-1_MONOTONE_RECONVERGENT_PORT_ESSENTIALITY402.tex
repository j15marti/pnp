\section*{Exact theorem and proof}
\begin{theorem}
Given an explicitly encoded topological fan-in-two monotone AND/OR shared DAG and a named positive input $t$, deciding whether $\exists z:C(0,z)\ne C(1,z)$ is NP-complete under logspace many-one reductions, even though every gate's formal Boolean operator depends on both input bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Membership in NP follows from guessing $z$ and evaluating both cofactors. For a proper nonempty-literal signed 3CNF $H$ with variables $v_1,\ldots,v_n$, introduce freely independent positive flag inputs $a_i$ (true) and $b_i$ (false). Define
\[
G=\bigwedge_i(a_i\lor b_i),\qquad B=\bigvee_i(a_i\land b_i).
\]
The false flag for an original literal is $f(v_i)=b_i$ and $f(\neg v_i)=a_i$. Let
\[
Q=B\lor\bigvee_{D\in H}\bigwedge_{\ell\in D}f(\ell),\qquad
C_H(t,a,b)=Q\lor(t\land G).
\]
Every gate is a positive fan-in-two AND or OR; the explicit circuit is $O(n+|H|)$ gates and is logspace constructible with binary gate indices. Since $C_H(0)=Q$ and $C_H(1)=Q\lor G$, the port $t$ is essential iff some flags satisfy $G=1,Q=0$. $G=1$ requires at least one of $a_i,b_i$ per variable, while $B=0$ forbids both, giving exactly one sign per variable. Requiring all original-clause falsity terms to be zero says every clause of $H$ has a true literal. Conversely every satisfying assignment to $H$ supplies such flags. Therefore $\operatorname{Ess}_t(C_H)\iff H\in\mathrm{SAT}$. Standard SAT NP-completeness completes the reduction.
\end{proof}
