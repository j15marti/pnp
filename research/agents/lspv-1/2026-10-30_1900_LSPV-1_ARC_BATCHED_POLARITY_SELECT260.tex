\paragraph{Exact model.}
Let $F$ be an ordered signed CNF of physical bit length $N\ge2$, encoded with $O(\log N)$-bit variable identifiers and explicit clause delimiters. Let $G(i)$ for $0\le i<q$ enumerate $q\le n$ distinct \emph{original} variable names with $(\log N)^{O(1)}$ generator time and $O(\log N)$ generator space per name. Generator construction and execution are charged. For $v$, define original-source signed counts
\[
p_v=\#\{(j,\ell):\ell=x_v\in C_j\},\quad
u_v=\#\{(j,\ell):\ell=\neg x_v\in C_j\}.
\]
The deterministic policy selects the largest numbered candidate with $p_v\ge2$ and $u_v\ge2$, or $G(0)$ if no such candidate exists. This is a literal occurrence predicate, not an equity/valuation measure.

\begin{theorem}
For any $1\le B\le q$, a deterministic uniform exact selector computes the above policy using
\[
T=O\left(N\lceil q/B\rceil(\log N)^{O(1)}+q(\log N)^{O(1)}\right),
\quad S=O(B\log N+\log N)
\]
counted work bits, including source scans, candidate creation, address/reset handling, sorting, searches, tests of unsuccessful candidates, and winner retention. Thus with $q\le N^\gamma$, $0<\sigma\le\gamma<1$, choosing $B=\max(1,\lfloor N^\sigma/(c\log N)\rfloor)$ gives $T\le N^{1+\gamma-\sigma+o(1)}$, $S\le N^{\sigma+o(1)}$. In particular $\gamma=3/5$, $\sigma=1/2$ yields the formal parent-stage pair $(11/10,1/2)$ for this selector alone.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Create one sorted batch of $B$ distinct variable names, holding only two $O(\log N)$-bit signed counters per name. During a full read-only source scan, binary-search each signed occurrence in the sorted batch, and increment its unique matching counter when present. Sorting and generating one batch use $B(\log N)^{O(1)}$ bit operations and $O(B\log N)$ work bits. The indexed machine's source reset and address arithmetic are explicitly counted in the polylogarithmic term. Inspect all batch counts, retain only the largest qualifying name in an $O(\log N)$-bit global register, and discard the batch. Repeat for $\lceil q/B\rceil$ batches. Each candidate belongs to exactly one batch, so its counters are exact. Memory never includes a persistent $q$-element table. Summing the source scans and candidate computations proves the bounds. No SAT answer oracle or uncharged source index is used.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Same-source SAT/UNSAT semantics.}
If the chosen variable is used merely as a first DPLL pivot, the ordinary cofactor identity
$\operatorname{SAT}(F)=\operatorname{SAT}(F|_{x=0})\lor\operatorname{SAT}(F|_{x=1})$
preserves SAT and UNSAT when both needed branches are completed. The theorem does not bound subsequent DPLL search time, memory, child physical length, recursive applicability, guest solver cost or learned-clause authentication. A dynamically dependent sequence of candidates need not be batchable. The full source's clause labels and variable identifiers remain original, not independent indexed occurrences.

A deterministic independent replay used seed 202610301900 on 52 source formulas, with 36 SAT and 16 UNSAT. Controls comprised all-eight and seven-of-eight signed 3CNF, Horn, Krom, affine XOR, dense 3CNF, polarity switching and 40 randomized proper 3CNFs, at most seven variables per source. Width-one/two control clauses use their actual width in the complement identity.

Independent checks passed on all 52 SAT/UNSAT decisions, 28,132 pair extreme modes, 3,776 triple modes and 2,349 original signed occurrences. The batched selector performed 208 source scans, 9,524 signed-occurrence probes, 19,048 conservative binary-search comparison events and 228 candidate generations. Standard UP-DPLL used 281 nodes and 11,396 instrumented selected events; first-pivot batched selection plus UP-DPLL used 292 nodes and 40,662 events. The producer loads source CNF into Python and serializes full raw output; this host bookkeeping is not the bounded-space theoretical implementation.
