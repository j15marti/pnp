\begin{theorem}[Finite-horizon clipping with exact re-entry]
Fix any complete physical child $y$ of length $m$, any deterministic guest, and any \emph{honest} upper bound $B\ge1$ on the number of remaining guest head instructions in this run. An exact simulator can represent the logical read-only input head using only $m+B+2$ possible states, hence
\[
\boxed{\,\lceil\log_2(m+B+2)\rceil\ \text{writable head-state bits}.\,}
\]
On a jump to $j\le m+B$, retain $j$; on a jump to $j>m+B$ use a single special $\mathrm{FAR}$ state. For an ordinary right move leaving $m+B$, enter $\mathrm{FAR}$; ordinary moves from $\mathrm{FAR}$ remain there. Reads from $\mathrm{FAR}$ return blank. All valid reads, and every possible re-entry into $0,\ldots,m-1$, are exact throughout this $B$-step run.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Until $\mathrm{FAR}$ occurs the represented head equals the guest's physical head by induction, including each indexed jump and ordinary move. If $\mathrm{FAR}$ first arises at guest step $t$ from an indexed jump or right move, the real head is at least $m+B+1$. There are at most $B-t$ subsequent head moves. Therefore its smallest possible later coordinate is $m+B+1-(B-t)=m+t+1>m-1$, so every subsequent read is blank. No physical valid-cell re-entry can occur within this run. The special state is thus observationally equivalent to all such far positions. Earlier valid re-entry from $m+1$, for instance, is tracked by the exact finite coordinate and succeeds. The proof permits the guest to adapt its next instruction to any previous read; read agreement inductively implies identical control choices. Reading an arbitrarily long indexed address is \emph{not free}: streaming the binary digits and saturating the accumulator at $m+B+1$ requires each delivered address bit to be processed, with $O(\log(m+B+2))$ temporary bits.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exponent-oblivious geometric replay]
Suppose the guest halts in $T\ge1$ steps, but its polynomial exponent $K$ is unknown. Restart its deterministic simulation with budgets $1,2,4,\ldots$ until the first completed epoch, using the theorem's honest budget for each epoch. The final answer and all observed input symbols are exact, with
\[
\sum_{\mathrm{epochs}}\text{guest steps}<4T,\quad
B_{\max}<2T,\quad
\text{head-state bits}=O(\log(m+T)).
\]
If $Q$ denotes the \emph{final execution's} number of explicitly supplied jump-address bits and $R$ the worst charged child-symbol-access cost, a conservative bit-operation upper tariff is
\[
O\!\bigl((T+Q)\log(T+2)(\log(m+T+2)+R)\bigr)
\]
plus the fully charged cost of resetting the guest's writable tapes and reconstructing any non-head source state. Under an interface requiring address-bit delivery in charged individual steps, $Q=O(T)$; without that interface this conclusion is \emph{not} claimed. Peak additional head/replay counter space is $O(\log(m+T))$, apart from the guest, source, accessor, and retained ancestor states.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first budget at least $T$ is below $2T$. The geometric sum of elapsed step limits is less than twice that budget, and therefore below $4T$. Every epoch starts from an unchanged deterministic guest initial state. The theorem guarantees the same observations as a physical guest up to that epoch's cutoff. The first completed epoch therefore returns the physical guest's answer without requiring knowledge of $K$. Each earlier epoch is a prefix of the final run; even the worst case re-reading and re-decoding all final-run material in every epoch incurs at most $O(\log T)$ multiplicity. Updating a bounded binary counter and decoding a delivered binary indexed address takes $O(\log(m+T))$ elementary bit work per charged digit or move. Resetting a guest state requires real work and retained source/tape costs, which the statement explicitly excludes from the head-only bound and charges separately. \end{proof}
