\section{Target, quantifiers, and four preregistered falsifiers}
The target is \emph{necessary charged source authentication}, one arrow of a proposed universal adaptive UNSAT certificate system, not certificate existence. The quantified computational assertion is: for every fixed machine $V$ whose accepting (respectively rejecting coSAT) computation is sound \emph{on every ordinary SAT input}, for every accepting UNSAT transcript on the explicit source $F_h$ below, every position in a designated set of original physical sign bits was read. A separate promise assertion covers machines sound on \emph{all proper signed exact-$(4,4)$ 3CNFs}. Computations have a fixed nondeterministic choice stream when replayed; deterministic deciders are a special case.

The following four falsifiable mechanisms were set before searching the original project and primary literature this run:
\begin{description}
\item[] H1: On a 3-vertex-connected cubic odd-Tseitin source, changing \emph{any one} literal polarity produces an ordinary SAT formula on the \emph{same} physical clause list. \textbf{PROVED/EXACT RESTRICTED} below.
\item[] H2: In the same source, \emph{all} polarity occurrences can be partitioned into disjoint pairs whose simultaneous flips restore the exact signed occurrence quotas and give SAT. \textbf{PROVED/EXACT RESTRICTED}, by an opposite-sign matching at each original graph edge.
\item[] H3: A verifier may certify UNSAT of these sources while skipping at least one sign bit, provided it has a short syntactic or affine proof. \textbf{FALSIFIED} for sound ordinary SAT/UNSAT verification. A short proof may exist, but \emph{source authentication} of it still obeys the input-probe floor.
\item[] H4: The resulting source-probe floor proves a superlinear random-access SAT time bound or transports immediately to signed exact-$(2,2)$. \textbf{FALSIFIED AS INFERENCE}: the floor is $\Omega(N/\log N)$, these graphs have exact signed $(4,4)$, and their algebraic proofs are short.
\end{description}

\section{Source and provenance: prior clause criticality versus the new bit theorem}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a simple cubic graph, and let $b\in\{0,1\}^V$ have odd parity. Give each edge $e$ one original Boolean name $x_e$. At a vertex $v$, enumerate its three incident edges in fixed ascending order. For each $a\in\{0,1\}^3$ of parity $1-b_v$, form the proper clause $C_{v,a}$ which is false exactly when the local edge assignment is $a$. Thus $C_{v,a}$ contains $x_e$ when $a_e=0$ and $\neg x_e$ when $a_e=1$. Define
\[
T(G,b)=\bigwedge_{v\in V}\;\bigwedge_{a:\oplus a=1-b_v}C_{v,a}.
\tag{1}
\]
Every vertex contributes four different three-distinct-name clauses. Every original edge name occurs in the two endpoint quartets, twice positively and twice negatively \emph{per endpoint}, hence has global signed quota $(4,4)$. XORing the vertex parity equations gives $0=\oplus_v b_v=1$, so $T(G,b)$ is UNSAT. Its clause-variable incidence graph is connected when $G$ is connected. These are not clause-monotone exact-$(2,2)$ sources.

Role-3's earlier TSEITIN-CLAUSE-CRITICALITY125 and VERTEX-CUT-CRITICALITY134 already proved that deleting any clause from this formula makes it SAT when $G-v$ is connected for every $v$. This run does \emph{not} claim clause-criticality or the characteristic-two short proof as new. In fact, the individual sign-bit SAT sensitivity follows immediately from prior per-clause essentiality: a witness satisfying all clauses except one is converted into a full satisfying witness by flipping any false literal in that clause. The added proof obligations are (a) the explicit every-accepting-path bit-probe consequence for sound source-authenticated verifiers and (b) a perfect pairing of \emph{two-sign} SAT mutations retaining the signed occurrence promise, with a separate promised-path hitting bound.

For explicit growing sources choose the prism $G_h=C_h\square K_2$ for $h\ge3$. It has $2h$ vertices, $3h$ edges, and $8h$ clauses. It remains connected after removing any one or any two vertices, as is seen by retaining at least one rung joining the two surviving rail paths or by connectivity of the remaining full rail. Assign charge $1$ to one vertex and $0$ elsewhere. Write $F_h=T(G_h,b)$.

For an unbounded uniform family use a self-delimiting binary header specifying the name width $w$, variable count $3h$, and clause count $8h$, followed by a fixed-length record of one sign bit and $w$ identity bits per literal. A fixed magic word plus Elias-gamma fields suffice, with header length $H_h=O(\log(h+2))$ and a uniform polylogarithmic-bit decoder. Its length and exact count of original sign-bit positions obey
\[
 w_h=\lceil\log_2(3h+1)\rceil,\quad
 N_h=H_h+24h(w_h+1)=\Theta(h\log h),
 \quad |\mathcal P_h|=24h.\tag{2}
\]
The finite executable below instead uses a fixed 88-bit header (16-bit magic, 8-bit $w$, and two 32-bit counts), valid for all \emph{tested} values $h=3,4,5,6,8$; the exact \emph{finite} bit lengths in the results table use $N_h^{\rm test}=88+24h(w_h+1)$. Extrapolating that fixed-size count header to arbitrary $h$ would be incorrect. The theorem applies to the scalable header, with the same literal records, semantic sign flips, and $\Theta(h\log h)$ bit lengths. Here $\mathcal P_h$ comprises all literal sign positions, not headers or variable-name bits. A sign flip produces another \emph{valid encoded ordinary proper} 3CNF of identical length, headers, clause count, and distinct-variable supports. This precise binary encoding is a declared language representation, not the claim that the FLMV theorem automatically transfers to that particular exact-$(4,4)$ promise.

\section{Exact completion theorem and single-bit sensitivity}
\begin{lemma}[Pinned odd-parity completion]\label{lem:pinned}
If $G-v$ is connected, then for every forbidden local word $a$ of parity $1-b_v$ there is a full edge assignment $z$ which equals $a$ on the three edges at $v$ and satisfies the parity equations at \emph{all} $u\ne v$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix the three edges at $v$ to $a$. On the connected graph $G-v$, the remaining equations are the vertex-edge incidence linear system over $\mathbb F_2$. Its right-hand-side XOR is $\bigoplus_{u\ne v}b_u\oplus\bigoplus_{e\ni v}a_e=(1\oplus b_v)\oplus(1\oplus b_v)=0$. The incidence matrix of a connected graph has image all even-parity vertex vectors. Therefore the system is solvable. At $v$ the pinned triple has the wrong parity, as required.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Every original literal sign bit is SAT-sensitive]\label{th:single}
For every $h\ge3$ and every position $p\in\mathcal P_h$, flipping \emph{only} source bit $p$ changes the \emph{ordinary} 3CNF label of the fixed UNSAT source $F_h$ to SAT. In particular there are $24h$ distinct single-bit SAT neighbors in the same physical proper ternary input format.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Bit $p$ names one literal in a specific original clause $C_{v,a}$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:pinned} to produce $z$ which falsifies the local word $a$ at $v$ and satisfies all other vertex equations. Exactly $C_{v,a}$ is false under $z$: the four forbidden local patterns are distinct, while every quartet away from $v$ is satisfied. The chosen literal in $C_{v,a}$ is false under $z$. Flipping its sign makes it true, leaving every other clause unchanged and already true. The resulting formula is SAT, with witness $z$. No clause loses its three distinct identities, and the physical encoding differs in exactly that sign bit.\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[All-sign input-authentication floor on a single NO source]\label{cor:source}
Let $D$ be any fixed uniform deterministic total ordinary-SAT decider in a bit-probe model, or let $V$ be a nondeterministic verifier whose acceptance is sound only for UNSAT \emph{on all ordinary inputs}. On input $F_h$, every rejecting computation of $D$ and every accepting computation of $V$ inspects all $24h$ designated original sign positions. Equivalently, if SAT is recognized by a co-nondeterministic machine, every rejecting branch on $F_h$ must inspect them. Therefore its source-bit-probe count on the \emph{same NO input and same path} is
\[
                         Q(F_h)\ge24h=\Omega(N_h/\log N_h).\tag{3}
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If a designated bit $p$ were never read along the declared computation, hold fixed all nondeterministic choices, the proof, and all other source bits, and replay that entire path on the SAT one-bit mutant from Theorem~\ref{th:single}. Until termination the observed symbols and machine configurations are identical, so the same acceptance or rejection would occur with the wrong label. Contradiction. The argument is about the soundness of \emph{each path}; it makes no assumption about the proof grammar, resolution length, witness circuit, or certificate generation time.\end{proof}

\section{A perfect matching of quota-preserving SAT mutations}
The preceding one-bit mutant is outside the signed exact-$(4,4)$ promise because it changes the quota of the flipped variable. A stronger construction preserves that promise. For an original graph edge $e=uv$, the edge name appears in exactly four clause literals at each endpoint, two positive and two negative. Pair the two positive occurrences at $u$ with the two negative occurrences at $v$ bijectively, and the two negative occurrences at $u$ with the two positive occurrences at $v$. Do this independently for every original graph edge. The resulting set $\mathcal M_h$ of ordered pairs partitions \emph{every} sign bit into disjoint pairs, with
\[
                   |\mathcal M_h|=4|E|=12h,\qquad
                   \bigcup_{\{p,q\}\in\mathcal M_h}\{p,q\}=\mathcal P_h.\tag{4}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Two-endpoint completion]\label{lem:two}
Let $G$ be cubic and suppose $G-\{u,v\}$ is connected for the endpoints of an edge $e=uv$. Let $a$ be a forbidden local word at $v$, and let $c$ be a forbidden local word at $u$ whose value on $e$ is \emph{opposite} to that of $a$. There is a full edge assignment $z$ which realizes $a$ at $v$, realizes the \emph{bitwise complement} $\bar c$ at $u$, and satisfies the parity equations at every remaining vertex.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The two pinned local words are consistent on the one common edge: $a_e=1-c_e=(\bar c)_e$. Vertex $v$ has wrong parity $1-b_v$, vertex $u$ has correct parity $b_u$ because complementing three bits reverses parity. Fixing all five distinct edges incident to $u$ or $v$ leaves the connected incidence system on $G-\{u,v\}$. Summing its adjusted right-hand sides yields
\[
\bigoplus_{w\notin\{u,v\}}b_w\ \oplus\ \bigoplus_{f\in\delta(\{u,v\})}z_f
=(1\oplus b_v\oplus b_u)\oplus(1\oplus b_v\oplus b_u)=0.
\]
Here $\delta(\{u,v\})$ includes only the four edges running to the residual graph, not the internal edge $uv$ counted twice. The standard connected-incidence solvability criterion gives the completion.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Perfect quota-preserving SAT-neighbor matching]\label{th:matching}
For every $h\ge3$, every $\{p,q\}\in\mathcal M_h$ has the property that flipping \emph{exactly} $p$ and $q$ in the UNSAT input $F_h$ gives a SAT proper signed exact-$(4,4)$ 3CNF of the same length and the same clause-variable supports. The mutation pairs are pairwise sign-position disjoint. Every deterministic exact signed-$(4,4)$-SAT promise decider, and every sound exact-$(4,4)$-UNSAT accepting computation, reads at least one position from each of the $12h$ pairs on this \emph{same} NO source:
\[
                          Q_{(4,4)}(F_h)\ge12h=\Omega(N_h/\log N_h).\tag{5}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
One paired sign belongs to the clause $C_{v,a}$ and one to $C_{u,c}$ on their common edge. Opposite literal signs mean $a_e\ne c_e$. Use Lemma~\ref{lem:two} to choose $z$: it falsifies $C_{v,a}$ and satisfies all other clauses before mutation. Flipping the $v$ literal makes that unique false clause true. At $u$, its selected original clause $C_{u,c}$ has all three literals true under $z$, because $z$ is the coordinatewise complement of its forbidden assignment $c$; flipping just its $e$ literal leaves \emph{two} other true literals. Every other clause remains true. Thus the double-mutant is SAT. As one literal of each sign is flipped for the same original edge name, that name's positive and negative totals do not change. Distinct-three-variable support also does not change. Mutated local clauses encode a newly forbidden right-parity triple and do not duplicate any other original forbidden triple in the same quartet; quartets at different graph vertices use different sets of three original edges. Hence clause uniqueness is preserved. The number and disjointness of pairs follow from the endpoint matching construction. If any accepting computation skipped both sign positions of one pair, replay on its SAT mutant would violate soundness, exactly as in Corollary~\ref{cor:source}.\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope of the promise conclusion.} This theorem is about \emph{signed exact-$(4,4)$}, not signed exact-$(2,2)$. The source has $8$ occurrences per original name, not $4$. Nor is it a clause-monotone construction. Role-2 must provide an occurrence-managed exact-$(2,2)$ translator before one may claim this property on the target family. For all ordinary SAT the stronger single-bit bound (3) applies directly.

\section{Actual two-level controls, circuit identities, and all-input gap}
For an arbitrary genuine original CNF $F$ and original variable $x$, the identities $\SAT(F)=\SAT(F\vert_{x=0})\lor\SAT(F\vert_{x=1})$ and their second-level analogues are exact, but they do not imply a power reduction in \emph{encoded input length}. The reproducer emitted and reparsed two consecutive actual ordinary-CNF restrictions on the original UNSAT $F_h$, an even-charge SAT control, and a double-mutant SAT control for each tested $h$. The chosen UNSAT descendants remain UNSAT at both levels; SAT controls choose pins from explicit satisfying assignments and stay SAT. All thirty descendants were physically serialized. These controls have no hypothetical guest-$A$ calls and no ARC shortening claim.

A full length-$N$ SAT \emph{decision} circuit $C_{A,N}$, a particular instance's witness verifier, and a source-authenticated parity \emph{boundary relation} are three different objects. The small characteristic-two polynomial-calculus proof of odd Tseitin is an independent \emph{easy control}, not a universal SAT circuit. It may prove semantic UNSAT cheaply while authentication still reads the source signs. Previous project ARC184 and Role-3 affine terminals already demonstrate this distinction. An arbitrary hypothetical uniform total SAT solver $A$ supplied by $P=NP$ has unknown polynomial time exponent $K$ and simultaneous space exponent $s$ on \emph{one machine}; the extra $K=1.802,s=o(1)$ is not implied. Our linear-exponent source floor is compatible with any $K\ge1$ and never approaches the strict FLMV box
\[
                  t<241/200,\qquad s<11642479/23232400.
\tag{7}
\]
A certificate scheme whose source authentication takes $o(N/\log N)$ bit probes on \emph{every} UNSAT input is now \textbf{FALSIFIED}; an $N^{1+o(1)}$ verifier is \emph{not} falsified. The missing arrow is a sound \emph{and} complete, uniformly length-clocked all-UNSAT source-authenticated certificate family with fully charged deterministic acquisition and two genuinely shorter hereditary guest inputs, or a separate uniformly constructed arbitrary-$K$ simultaneous ARC successor. Neither exists in this report. No van Melkebeek two-algorithm premise, coNTIME small-space machine, or nonuniform decision-circuit premise is asserted.
