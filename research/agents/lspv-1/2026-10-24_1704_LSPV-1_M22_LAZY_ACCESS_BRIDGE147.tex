\section{Charged materialization floor}
\begin{theorem}[Materialization firewall]
Suppose a uniform SAT transformation produces a string $R(x)$ of
$M(N)\ge N^{a-o(1)}$ bits, and at some instant all $M(N)$ bits are
simultaneously held in counted writable/index storage so that a subsequent
random-access target simulation may read them. Then peak counted space
$S(N)\ge M(N)$. If $a\ge b_\star$, this implementation cannot achieve
a fixed $s<b_\star$. Full materialization of a $\Theta(N)$-bit
ordinary encoded child is therefore excluded by the strict FLMV space
budget, regardless of its later erasure.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At the materialization instant all $M(N)$ writable bits are live.
The peak counts this moment. For fixed $s<b_\star\le a$,
$N^{s+o(1)}=o(N^{a-o(1)})$. Erasure or reuse does not lower the peak.
An output-only tape cannot silently become a free indexed read-only
input. A never-materialized virtual accessor is not ruled out.
\end{proof}
This is a restriction on \emph{that storage implementation}, not
a lower bound on any ordinary SAT decider, any streaming construction
or arbitrary virtual reductions.

\section{One-machine lazy-access transport}
\begin{theorem}[Addressed input simulation]
Assume a fixed uniform exact SAT-label-preserving reduction $R$ for
all ordinary inputs of length $N$ into the target language, with
$|R(x)|\le N^{a+o(1)}$. Charge validation/setup
$N^{v+o(1)}$ work and $N^{u+o(1)}$ live counted space. Assume a fixed
indexed-address routine computing every requested $R(x)[j]$ uses
at most $N^{\gamma+o(1)}$ actual elementary bit work and
$N^{\delta+o(1)}$ additional live writable/index space, including
source reads, headers, variable names, arithmetic, failed recognition,
cached data, read traffic and source rereads. Let ONE fixed exact total
target decider $B$ run in simultaneous $M^{t+o(1)}$ time and
$M^{s+o(1)}$ space. In the matched random-access machine model,
the inlined original-input algorithm achieves
\[
 \boxed{T(N)\le N^{\max\{v,at+\gamma\}+o(1)},\quad
 S(N)\le N^{\max\{u,as,\delta\}+o(1)}.}\tag{1}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Inline guest machine $B$ and service each indexed guest input-bit query
with the source-aware routine, without storing the virtual target.
All guest steps and queries number at most $M^{t+o(1)}$; a query
is charged at most $N^{\gamma+o(1)}$ elementary work. Setup is
charged separately. Writable guest registers, setup retained state,
source-address indices, nested accessor buffers and live ancestors
are counted simultaneously, so space is their sum and has exponent
their maximum. The fixed programs and all-input bit accuracy provide
uniformity and exactness; this assertion depends on model-matched
simulation of each guest operation.
\end{proof}
\emph{Receiving mismatch:} NP-completeness of $\CS$ does not furnish
an accessor satisfying these precise hypotheses, and the construction
above supplies no new general source-to-$\CS$ SAT adapter.

\begin{corollary}[Two virtual levels, no free intermediate input]
Suppose first-stage $R_1$ has length exponent $a_1$, addressed
work exponent $\gamma_1$, extra space exponent $\delta_1$ relative
to original $N$. Suppose second-stage $R_2$ has respective
$a_2,\gamma_2,\delta_2$ relative to its input length, with
$\gamma_2$ counting the number of lower-level input requests.
Then a two-stage virtual input has length exponent $a_1a_2$,
conservative addressed-work exponent $\gamma_1+a_1\gamma_2$,
and added space exponent $\max\{\delta_1,a_1\delta_2\}$.
Without further setup the guest bound is
\[
 t_{\rm out}\le a_1a_2t+\gamma_1+a_1\gamma_2,\quad
 s_{\rm out}\le\max\{a_1a_2s,\delta_1,a_1\delta_2\}.\tag{2}
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
One outer bit invokes at most $N_1^{\gamma_2+o(1)}$
lower-level bits, each costing $N^{\gamma_1+o(1)}$,
where $N_1=N^{a_1+o(1)}$. Combined work is bounded by
$N^{\gamma_1+a_1\gamma_2+o(1)}$.
Nested live buffers coexist but their leading exponent is
the maximum, not a sum of space exponents. Substitute into (1).
\end{proof}
This is an exact \emph{conditional} composition theorem, not
two recursive steps of a proven SAT contraction.

\section{Direct post-acquisition potential ceiling}
The imported known bounded-occurrence SAT algorithm gives
$O^*(1.1199^{(d-2)k})$ on $k$-variable maximum-$d$-occurrence
CNF. For target $d=4$, a fixed uniform exact upper-algorithm
uses $\operatorname{poly}(N)b^k$ bit work, with
\[
 b=1.1199^2=1.25417601,\qquad
 \kappa=\log_2(b)\approx0.326739828949.
\]
Its space exponent has \emph{not} been inferred from this time
bound.
\begin{theorem}[Upper algorithm rejects overly large universal potentials]
Suppose a proposed independently defined complete-state potential
$\Phi_A$ is nonnegative, ends at zero and obeys
$\Phi_A(C)-\Phi_A(C')\le\Gamma(N)w(C,C')$
at every legal charged transition, with $\Gamma(N)=N^{O(1)}$.
When applied to the fixed sparse-SAT solver
$A_{\rm sparse}$ after completed source acquisition,
\[
 \boxed{\Phi_{A_{\rm sparse}}(C_{\rm postscan})
 \le \Gamma(N)\operatorname{poly}(N)b^k.}
\]
Hence a claim that \emph{every} solver must start with
$\Phi_A\ge2^{\alpha k}$ on arbitrarily large valid target inputs
for fixed $\alpha>\kappa$ is false.
The exact all-algorithm worst-case lower bound with a smaller
positive rate, and any noncircular $\Phi$, remain \textbf{OPEN}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Telescope the per-transition drop along the actual
$A_{\rm sparse}$ computation. Its terminal potential is zero,
so initialization is no larger than $\Gamma(N)$ times actual
postscan bit work, itself no larger than total sparse-SAT time.
Since $N=\Theta(k\log k)$, polynomial factors are $2^{o(k)}$.
An $\alpha>\kappa$ lower initialization would eventually
contradict the upper bound. This uses no hypothetical
optimal-time function or free semantic operation.
\end{proof}
