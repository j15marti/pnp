\section{The exact streaming interface}
A \emph{closed scoped ordinary CNF event stream} comprises
$\mathrm{OPEN}(v)$, $\mathrm{CLAUSE}(\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r)$ and
$\mathrm{CLOSE}(v)$ events, with $r\le3$, each clause referring only to
currently live original names. Each name is opened and closed exactly once;
its lifetime is contiguous. Invalid encodings have a declared uniform
rejection action. The maximum number of simultaneously live names is $w$.
Let $M$ be the bit length of the emitted event stream. The normalizer can
write events only once in forward order; the consuming DP reads this stream
online, without saving all $M$ bits. This sequential output requirement is
essential. Random-access clause-bit provision does NOT imply that a
good scope ordering can be generated at comparable cost.

At a live boundary $Y$, represent the feasible assignments by a dense
$2^{|Y|}$-bit truth-table bitset. On an OPEN, copy every currently feasible
assignment into its two bit extensions. On a CLAUSE, clear bits not satisfying
that clause; on CLOSE, existentially OR the two bits that differ only in the
closing coordinate, then compact. Every step costs $O(2^w\operatorname{polylog}M)$
primitive bit/index operations with at most $O(2^w+\operatorname{polylog}M)$
writable/index bits. No free semantic SAT oracle is used. The invariant is
that a bit for live assignment $u$ equals one exactly when some values of
already closed variables satisfy every processed clause and agree with $u$.
Thus the final singleton bit is the exact SAT answer, including UNSAT.

\begin{theorem}[Curved normalizer impossibility, source-only interface]\label{thm:frontier}
Let constants $\mu\ge1$, $p\ge\mu$, $\sigma\ge0$, $\kappa\ge0$ be fixed.
Suppose a single deterministic uniform \emph{source-only} total normalizer
maps \emph{every ordinary SAT instance} of $N$ encoded bits, preserving both
SAT and UNSAT, into a valid closed scoped CNF stream with
\[
M\le N^{\mu+o(1)},\quad
w\le\kappa\log_2N+o(\log N),\quad
T_{\rm gen}\le N^{p+o(1)},\quad
S_{\rm gen}\le N^{\sigma+o(1)}
\]
in the matched FLMV machine, with all source reads, validation, output writes,
controller and addressing already charged. Then ordinary SAT is in
\[
\boxed{\DTISP\bigl[N^{\max\{p,\mu+\kappa\}+o(1)},
N^{\max\{\sigma,\kappa\}+o(1)}\bigr].}
\]
Consequently such a normalizer CANNOT exist if, for
$c=\max\{p,\mu+\kappa\}>1$ and $s=\max\{\sigma,\kappa\}$,
\[
\boxed{s<\beta(c)\quad\text{and }c\text{ is in the positive range of Corollary 5.8}.}
\]
The same conclusion holds for an image-only normalizer if its image is a
\emph{fixed locally indexed FLMV-hard image} and all reduction and online
output access costs are included in the displayed $(p,\sigma,\mu)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compose the normalizer with the direct live-variable bitset DP. On each
emitted event, DP touches at most $2^w\operatorname{polylog}M$ dense table
bit slots. Total DP time is
$M\,2^w\operatorname{polylog}M\le N^{\mu+\kappa+o(1)}$;
peak DP workspace is $2^w\operatorname{polylog}M\le N^{\kappa+o(1)}$.
The generator and consumer remain simultaneously resident, so maximum live
writable/index space is at most their \emph{sum}, with the larger exponent
$\max\{\sigma,\kappa\}$. Local source work and online output are already
charged in $p$ and event operations in $\mu+\kappa$. There is no full
materialized output tape. This yields the boxed same-machine certificate.
If $s<\beta(c)$, choose strictly larger rational $c_1>c$ near $c$ and
$s_1$ between $s$ and $\beta(c_1)$; FLMV Corollary 5.8 combined with
Theorem 3.1 then forbids these simultaneous SAT bounds, contradiction.
For a hard image, include its fixed matched local reduction in the same
stream-producing program; the same argument applies without a promise
recognizer. \end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact slice of the curved forbidden frontier]
For the near-linear case $\mu=1$ with $p\le1+\kappa$ and
$\sigma\le\kappa$, the normalizer above is impossible for every
$0\le\kappa<\kappa_*$, where $\kappa_*$ is the unique positive zero of
\[
\boxed{3\kappa^3+7\kappa^2+4\kappa-2=0,\qquad
\kappa_*=0.309774739896032565\ldots.}
\]
This is the exact cutoff for THIS one-parameter subfamily under the stated
$k=2$ FLMV curve; it is not claimed optimal among all time-space lower
bounds or different machine implementations.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Here $c=1+\kappa$ and $s=\kappa$. Straight algebra gives
\[
\beta(1+\kappa)-\kappa=
\frac{2-4\kappa-7\kappa^2-3\kappa^3}{2(1+\kappa)^2}.
\]
The numerator is strictly decreasing on nonnegative $\kappa$, positive at
zero and negative at $1/2$, so has a unique positive zero. The formula's
strict positive interval is exactly $[0,\kappa_*)$. At $\kappa=0$, the
space exponent zero denotes logarithmic/polylogarithmic tape overhead;
choose any small positive slack in the FLMV theorem.
\end{proof}
