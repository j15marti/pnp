## THEOREM 494.1: opposite-characteristic same-source backtranslation impossibility

Fix a constant r>=1 and any infinite family F_h meeting BOTH the /341 declared-semantic GF(2) upper construction and Pang's characteristic-three lower bound. There is **no** map C, even noncomputable, that for every valid declared-semantic R_mix341 refutation pi of every such ORIGINAL F_h produces an explicit, valid same-source Res(PC_r/F3) refutation and satisfies

$$
S_{\mathrm{Res(PC_r/F_3)}}(C(F_h,\pi))
\le p(N_h+|\pi|)
$$

for a SINGLE polynomial p and a standard explicit encoding |\pi| of native nodes/parents/masks.

**Proof.** Choose the source-authenticated /341 witness pi_h with 4h-1 unit-paid derived nodes, maximum literal/edge identifiers O(h), and O(h log(h+2)) explicit bits. Its source has N_h=O(h log(h+2)). The proposed output bound becomes p(O(h log h))=h^{O(1)}. Pang requires >=exp(kappa h) DAG nodes for the SAME F_h, for fixed kappa>0 and infinitely many suitable h. For sufficiently large h, exp(kappa h) exceeds every fixed polynomial in h log h. Contradiction. This uses neither a proof-search lower bound nor any claim about the converter's runtime. QED, **conditional on the exact overlap with the cited hard-family theorem**.

The same argument rules out a cost-only bound S_out <= poly(N_h+W_mix(F_h)) for these *particular optimal exhibited* refutations. In indexed source bits the target output lower bound is exp(Omega(N_h/log N_h)). It does **NOT** say all mixed proofs require exponential work, does not show a retraction to ordinary Resolution is possible, and does not show no other lower-bound invariant exists.

**Critical scope check:** If the actual original /341 byte grammar fails to accept the exhibited GF(2) proof, Theorem 494.1 remains valid for the declared-semantic calculus only; it must NOT be promoted to an accepted-byte historical claim. If Pang's precise family has an unproved encoding or bridgelessness overlap, the statement is conditional on that overlap. The original-source dependence is explicit.
