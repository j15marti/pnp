\section{Primary-literature and obstacle preflight}
The two obstacles are (i) constructing an all-input transformation with
content on already known nonpolynomial exact deciders, and (ii) obtaining a
simultaneous ordinary-machine landing to which an unconditional SAT
time--space theorem applies.  FLMV supplies the second theorem only after the
same encoded ordinary machine is shown to lie in its forbidden
region~\cite{FLMV05}; it does not construct the transformation.  Cook's
theorem supplies $\SAT\in P\Leftrightarrow P=NP$~\cite{Cook71}.  BGS and
Aaronson--Wigderson show that a relativizing or algebrizing ledger is not a
barrier escape~\cite{BGS75,AW09}.

Blum's machine-independent complexity theory is a useful warning that program
complexity and clock descriptions must be fixed explicitly, but it does not
transfer a speedup theorem into a SAT lower bound~\cite{Blum67}.  VLSI
area--time, communication cuts, pebbling, proof-system size and coding
invariants are \textbf{misleading/failed transfers} without an exact compiler
to the FLMV model.  The only direct transfer used below is the already
declared endpoint exclusion.

\section{Clocked domains and the exact theorem}
Let $\ExactSAT$ be the semantic set of triples
$z=(A,t,s)$ where $A$ is a total uniform exact ordinary-CNF SAT decider and
$t,s$ are valid total time/space clocks for $A$.  It is unconditionally
inhabited by a clocked exhaustive-search decider.  Let
\[
 \PolySAT=\{(A,t,s)\in\ExactSAT:t,s\text{ are polynomial}\}. \tag{2}
\]
Because polynomial time entails polynomial space,
\[
 \PolySAT\ne\varnothing\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\SAT\in P. \tag{3}
\]
The set of valid polynomial exponent witnesses for one fixed polynomial
implementation is upward closed; adding loose clocks creates more
descriptions but no faster execution.

Let $Y$ be codes of exact ordinary SAT deciders together with their charged
construction data.  Write $B(z,y)$ when $y$ is a valid exact output for
$z$.  The identity transformation witnesses
\[
 \exists\mathcal T:\ExactSAT\to Y\quad
 \forall z\in\ExactSAT\ B(z,\mathcal T(z)). \tag{4}
\]
Let $L(z,y)$ mean that this output, including all construction costs, is the
one \same{} ordinary SAT machine inside one fixed model-matched
unconditionally forbidden region.  The endpoint theorem says
\[
 \forall z\in\ExactSAT\ \forall y\in Y\quad \neg L(z,y). \tag{5}
\]

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{TOTAL-DOMAIN-LANDING-GUARD1}]
Under (5), for every total transformation $\mathcal T$ satisfying (4),
\[
 \forall z\in\PolySAT\ L(z,\mathcal T(z))
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \PolySAT=\varnothing. \tag{6}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \exists\mathcal T\,[\forall z\in\ExactSAT\ B(z,\mathcal T(z))\ \land\
 \forall z\in\PolySAT\ L(z,\mathcal T(z))]
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P\ne NP. \tag{7}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\PolySAT$ is nonempty, choose $z$ in it.  The left side of (6) gives
$L(z,\mathcal T(z))$, contradicting (5).  If $\PolySAT$ is empty, the
universal quantifier in (6) ranges over the empty set, so the statement is
true.  For (7), the identity map supplies the base condition (4), and (3),
(6) give the equivalence.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Added resource laws]
Let $R(z,\mathcal T(z))$ be any additional runtime-sensitive law.  A theorem
asserting a total $\mathcal T$ with $R$ and the guarded landing in (6) implies
$P\ne NP$.  Its converse need not hold because $R$ may fail even when
$\PolySAT$ is empty.  Therefore such a theorem is at least
separation-strength, not automatically an intermediate consequence of
total-domain inhabitedness.
\end{corollary}

This corollary does not say that a total runtime-sensitive law is useless.
It says that its nonvacuous content must be stated before the forbidden
predicate is attached.

\section{Smallest surviving repair}
The proposed construction should be split into two independently auditable
claims:
\begin{align*}
\textsf{CrossRegime}:\quad &
 \exists\mathcal T\ \forall z\in\ExactSAT\ R(z,\mathcal T(z)), \tag{8}\\
\textsf{Specialize}:\quad &
 \forall z\in\PolySAT\,[R(z,\mathcal T(z))\Rightarrow
 L(z,\mathcal T(z))]. \tag{9}
\end{align*}
For (8) to be a nonvacuous intermediate, $R$ must have quantitative,
checkable content on the known exhaustive decider and must not define itself
by membership in the forbidden endpoint.  It must be stable under loose
clock annotations or specify a canonical witness policy.  Equation (9) is
the separation-bearing specialization.  Both are \OPEN.  Calling their
conjunction merely a ``total compiler'' hides the quantifier guard exposed
by (6).

\section{Barrier, uniformity and post-quantum audit}
The theorem is a quantifier consequence of an already forbidden endpoint and
therefore relativizes whenever its endpoint premise is postulated relative
to the oracle.  It gives no nonrelativizing hinge.  Low-degree/algebraic
oracle access does not change the empty-subdomain logic and gives no
nonalgebrizing hinge.  Natural Proofs is not triggered because no
constructive large circuit property is proposed.  A finite program and
clock may be hardwired after existential selection without becoming
nonuniform; input-dependent advice is not allowed.

Schur squares, quadratic relation spaces, Hilbert functions, syzygies and
collision entropy are \textbf{FAILED TRANSFERS}: no code or map is forced.
LWE-style total black-box reductions and special-soundness extraction are
\textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE needing a new bridge} for (8).  Cryptographic
binding and random-oracle assumptions are inadmissible.  Hidden algebraic
structure may help rather than obstruct an algorithm.
