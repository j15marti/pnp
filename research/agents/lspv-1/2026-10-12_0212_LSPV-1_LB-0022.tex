\section{Explicit clean-core family and encoded length}

For $r\ge3$, let $P_r$ be the prism graph with two $r$-cycles and a perfect matching between corresponding cycle vertices.  It is connected and cubic, with
\[
 v=|V(P_r)|=2r,\qquad e=|E(P_r)|=3r.
 \tag{1}
\]
Associate a Boolean variable $x_e$ with every edge.  For a charge vector $c\in\{0,1\}^{2r}$, impose at every vertex $u$ the equation
\[
 \bigoplus_{e\ni u}x_e=c_u.
 \tag{2}
\]
Encode each degree-three equation by the four proper 3-clauses excluding the four assignments of the wrong parity.  Write the resulting formula as $T_r(c)$ and fix the canonical encoding in vertex order, template order, and signed binary literal-index format.

\begin{definition}[Canonical Tseitin clean core]
\[
 \mathcal T=\{T_r(c):r\ge3,\ c\in\{0,1\}^{2r}\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Every $T_r(c)$ has $n_r=3r$ variables and $m_r=8r$ clauses.  Each variable occurs exactly four times positively and four times negatively, so (3) is a proper exact-$(4,4)$ 3-CNF family.  Its encoded bit length is
\[
 N_r=\Theta(r\log r).
 \tag{4}
\]
\end{definition}

For any 3-CNF $F=C_1\wedge\cdots\wedge C_m$, define the semantic compatibility matrix
\[
 H(F)_{\alpha,j}=1\Longleftrightarrow \alpha\models C_j,
 \qquad H(F)\in\{0,1\}^{2^n\times m}.
 \tag{5}
\]
Then $F\in\SAT$ iff $H(F)$ has an all-one row.  For $T_r(c)$, (5) has
\[
 2^{3r}=2^{\Theta(N_r/\log N_r)}
 \tag{6}
\]
rows.  The proposed universal target under attack asserted that every uniform deterministic polynomial-time exact SAT decider, on infinitely many formulas from (3), must expose or pay positive-scale time, memory, communication, or state for a compatibility row or an equivalent object.

\section{Exact parity shortcut}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{H44-TSEITIN-PARITY-SHORTCUT1}]
For every $r\ge3$ and $c\in\{0,1\}^{2r}$,
\[
 T_r(c)\in\SAT
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{u\in V(P_r)}c_u=0.
 \tag{7}
\]
If (7) holds, then $T_r(c)$ has exactly
\[
 2^{e-v+1}=2^{r+1}
 \tag{8}
\]
satisfying assignments; otherwise it has none.  There is a uniform deterministic machine that, on the canonical encoding (3), validates the source and decides (7) in $O(N_r)$ time and $O(\log N_r)$ workspace, returning one bit and never materializing $H(T_r(c))$ or a witness.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Summing all vertex equations (2) over $\mathbb F_2$ cancels every edge variable exactly twice, so consistency requires the right-hand sides to have even total parity.  Conversely, the vertex-edge incidence matrix of a connected graph has rank $v-1$ over $\mathbb F_2$, and its image is precisely the even-parity subspace.  This proves (7).  When consistent, the affine solution space has dimension $e-(v-1)=e-v+1$, proving (8).

In each four-clause degree-three template, every incident variable occurs twice in each polarity.  Each edge has two endpoints, hence four occurrences in each polarity.  A canonical streaming recognizer uses counters for the current vertex, edge indices, template position, and one parity accumulator.  It validates all clause templates and graph positions, toggles the accumulator when the decoded charge is one, and returns its complement as the SAT bit.  Its counters use $O(\log N_r)$ bits; no assignment row or compatibility table is formed.
\end{proof}

Equation (6) therefore does not imply work proportional to the number of semantic rows.  Even (8), an exponential witness set, is compressed by the source equations to one decision bit.  The exact object correspondence to coding theory is favorable to the algorithm: the satisfying assignments form an affine coset of the cycle space of $P_r$.  Hidden algebraic structure is a weakness, not a hardness certificate.

\section{Wrapper transfer to arbitrary exact SAT deciders}

Let $A$ be any uniform deterministic total exact SAT decider, without assuming a running-time bound.  Define
\[
 W_A(F)=
 \begin{cases}
 \bigl[\bigoplus_u c_u=0\bigr],&\text{if $F$ validates as canonical $T_r(c)\in\mathcal T$},\\
 A(F),&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{9}
\]

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{RECOGNIZABLE-ISLAND-WRAPPER1}]
$W_A$ is a uniform deterministic total exact SAT decider.  On every input in $\mathcal T$ it uses $O(N)$ time and $O(\log N)$ workspace beyond fixed control, returns only the decision bit, and its semantic work after recognition is one parity accumulator.  If $A$ runs in polynomial time and space, so does $W_A$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The branches in (9) are disjoint and exhaustive.  Correctness on the recognized branch is Theorem 1; correctness elsewhere is correctness of $A$.  Validation charges every source read and does not trust a promise.  The resource bounds are the maximum of the recognizer and $A$ off the island.  On the island, $A$ is never invoked.
\end{proof}

Consequently, under the counterfactual $\SAT\in P$ (so $\PolySAT\ne\varnothing$), choose any $A\in\PolySAT$ and construct $W_A\in\PolySAT$.  This single exact polynomial decider falsifies an assertion quantified over \emph{every} member of $\PolySAT$ that demands compatibility-row extraction, witness exposure, or $\Omega(r)$ work-state on (3).  The argument does not assume $P\ne NP$; it holds precisely inside the $P=NP$ counterfactual where the universal target was meant to create a contradiction.

The conclusion is deliberately limited.  Sequential validation costs $O(N)$ time.  The theorem proves no sublinear probe bound and no superlinear time lower bound.  It does not show that a full SAT decider uses logarithmic space on arbitrary formulas: off (3), $W_A$ inherits $A$.  Nor does a proof-system lower bound transfer to (9).  Tseitin contradictions can be hard for resolution, regular resolution, DNNF, or bounded-depth proof systems while their canonical decision bit remains the parity in (7).

\section{Memory, communication, encoding, and one-bit audits}

\paragraph{Memory.}
The live summary after each validated vertex block is one parity bit plus logarithmic indices.  Thus semantic witness count does not force linear memory.  A claim about retaining an explicit $\Theta(r)$ charge vector is false on the canonical stream.

\paragraph{Communication.}
If vertex blocks are streamed by separate producers under the validated canonical interface, each contributes one charge bit and the consumer XORs them.  This is a declared protocol fact, not a lower bound on arbitrary SAT communication.  Changing the partition or requiring adversarial consistency needs a new model.

\paragraph{Alternative encodings.}
Clause and literal permutations preserve (7), but an on-line $O(\log N)$ recognizer is proved here only for the canonical order.  A noncanonical encoding may require sorting, repeated scans, or more workspace; it does not revive a representation-independent compatibility-extraction theorem.  Comment padding changes $M$ and input-reading cost without changing the parity bit.  A trusted global answer annotation trivially collapses the island further, while an untrusted annotation must be validated.

\paragraph{Affine elimination and semantic shortcuts.}
Full Gaussian elimination solves arbitrary XOR systems in polynomial time.  Connected Tseitin structure makes even that unnecessary: rank and consistency reduce to (7).  Horn, 2SAT, bounded-treewidth formulas, and independent products provide parallel hostile controls in which large semantic relations also coexist with specialized polynomial algorithms.

\paragraph{One-bit output.}
$W_A$ produces exactly one bit.  Extracting an assignment by self-reduction is a different execution with additional calls and cannot be charged to (9) under SAME\_REALIZATION.

\section{Barrier-transfer and post-quantum ledger}

The two blocking obstacles are (i) deriving a fixed favorable all-input resource bound from arbitrary SAT computation without selecting a chosen representation, and (ii) reaching an unconditional lower bound in the same ordinary random-access realization with all construction, validation, replay, failure, output, and peak-space costs charged.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Directly transferable theorem.}  The graph incidence rank, parity consistency criterion, and affine model count transfer exactly to (2).  This is the algorithmic shortcut proved above.  FLMV transfers only after one ordinary machine meets its simultaneous time-space hypotheses; it supplies no acquisition theorem.
\item \textbf{Useful analogy needing a new transfer theorem.}  VLSI cut/routing bounds, distributed parity, control observability, locally decodable codes, and proof-complexity interpolation all require a declared layout, cut, observation map, decoder, or proof system.  To constrain every SAT decider, one must prove that arbitrary computation preserves that interface and pays its costs.
\item \textbf{Misleading or failed transfer.}  Resolution/DNNF lower bounds for Tseitin formulas do not lower-bound unrestricted SAT decision.  Large $H(F)$, high proof width, expansion, or many witnesses do not imply time or state exposure.  Relativizing simulation cannot alone resolve $P$ versus $NP$, and algebrizing extensions remain blocked.
\end{itemize}

For the post-quantum prompts: the exact solution object is an affine code coset, so Schur squares, duals, quadratic relations, Hilbert functions, and syzygies may reveal more exploitable structure; no hardness transfer follows.  For the map $c\mapsto\bigoplus c$, each of two fibers has size $2^{2r-1}$ and
\[
 \operatorname{Coll}=2\,(2^{2r-1})^2=2^{4r-1},\qquad |\operatorname{im}|=2.
\tag{10}
\]
This is an \textbf{EXACT OBJECT/THEOREM TRANSFER} of the collision ledger, and it diagnoses extreme lossiness as the reason for the shortcut.  LWE-style external extraction, special soundness, collapse binding, hashes, and random oracles remain \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE} or \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER}; no unconditional arbitrary-decider bridge results.
