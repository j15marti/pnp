\section{Current proof DAG and model-matched endpoint}
Define $E$ to consist of pairs $(t,s)$ for which there exists \emph{one fixed uniform deterministic total exact ordinary-SAT decider} correct on \emph{every} ordinary $N$-bit input in the indexed-read-only-input random-access Turing-machine model of Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas (FLMV), simultaneously satisfying
\[
T_A(N)\le N^{t+o(1)},\qquad
S_A(N)\le N^{s+o(1)},
\]
where $S_A$ counts writable and index-tape bits. Writes, including unchanged writes, and address arithmetic are time operations; flip count, hardware cost and cumulative bit-time are different resources. FLMV Corollary 5.8 yields, after its SAT transfer, a strict forbidden box
\[
c_*=\frac{241}{200},\qquad
d_*=\frac{11642479}{23232400}=\beta(c_*),\qquad
\beta(c)=\frac12\left(\frac{c+2}{c^2}-c\right),
\quad t<c_*,\ s<d_*.
\]
The old universal $t+s<2\cos(\pi/7)$ endpoint is \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}; it does not substitute for this simultaneous matched-model statement.

The active logical chain is
\[
\boxed{P=NP}\ \Longrightarrow\
\boxed{(K,K)\in E\text{ for some finite integer }K}\
\xrightarrow[\text{OPEN}]{\text{fully charged finite machine route}}\
\boxed{\text{one joint strict-box ordinary-SAT decider}}\
\Longrightarrow\ \boxed{\text{FLMV contradiction}}.
\]
The middle arrow, \textbf{ARC-EDGE-90}, remains \OPEN. The numerical illustration
$(t,s)\mapsto(\max\{11/10,3/5+t/2\},\max\{1/2,s/2\})$
is not a machine-producing theorem. Fixed route depth may depend on the initial machine and $K$, but not on $N$. Distinct stages may use distinct genuinely justified rules.

\paragraph{Cofinal finite-hit implication (elementary, conditional).}
If some unbounded set of integers $\mathcal K$ has the property that, for each
$K\in\mathcal K$ with $(K,K)\in E$, a finite fixed sequence of source-checkable same-machine total constructions yields a machine strictly inside the displayed forbidden box, then $P\ne NP$. Under $P=NP$ fix a polynomial total SAT decider; its one time-space pair yields $(K_0,K_0)\in E$. Upward closure using \emph{that same machine} makes every $(K,K)$ for $K\ge K_0$ achievable. Choose $K\in\mathcal K$ and apply its finite fixed route. This contradicts the known endpoint. The hypothesis asserting the existence of such routes is precisely not proved here.

\section{Quantified ordinary-input retention theorem}
Fix a conventional complete literal-index encoding of ordinary 3CNF with
$O(\log(n+1))$ bits per literal and $O(\log(n+m+2))$ header overhead. Renumbering live variables densely is permitted. A \emph{full Shannon cube} on $k$ distinct variables means every one of the $2^k$ truth vectors is represented by an ordinary restricted CNF; it is not allowed to prune branches using separate semantic decisions or discard untouched clauses using a stronger simplifier.

\begin{theorem}[Two-level bounded-degree full-cube barrier]\label{th:retain}
Let $F_m$ be any sequence of ordinary formulas with $m$ non-tautological ternary clauses, $n=\Theta(m)$ variables, each variable in at most a fixed $D$ clauses, and original encoding size $N=\Theta(m\log m)$. At the first level fix $k_1$ distinct variables and enumerate all assignments. At the second level fix $k_2$ additional variables along each first-level branch and enumerate every assignment; the selected second-level names may depend on the first-level branch. Suppose every root-to-grandchild path has exactly $k=k_1+k_2$ distinct pins and neither level otherwise performs semantic clause deletion. Every resulting grandchild retains at least
\[
m-Dk
\]
completely untouched ternary clauses. Consequently, if the total number $B=2^k$ of grandchildren obeys $B\le N^{a+o(1)}$ for fixed finite $a$, every grandchild has ordinary encoded length $\Theta(N)$, even after full dense renumbering. In particular, this architecture has child-length exponent $\lambda=1$, not any fixed $\lambda<1$.

Conversely, obtaining length at most $N^{\lambda+o(1)}$ for any fixed $\lambda<1$ on this family by \emph{full-cube literal restriction alone} requires $k=\Omega(m)$ and hence $B\ge2^{\Omega(m)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each pin touches at most $D$ clauses. Across one path of $k$ pins, the union of touched clauses has size at most $Dk$, so at least $m-Dk$ original clauses have all three literal occurrences unchanged. These clauses survive every assignment, including assignments making other clauses empty. The full-cube hypothesis gives $B=2^k$; $B\le N^{a+o(1)}$ implies $k\le(a+o(1))\log_2 N=o(m)$. Thus $m-Dk=\Theta(m)$ three-literal clauses remain. Bounded occurrence means they collectively involve at least $3(m-Dk)/D=\Theta(m)$ distinct still-live variables. Dense relabeling therefore still needs $\Omega(\log m)$ bits for each literal index, hence every grandchild has $\Omega(m\log m)=\Omega(N)$ bits. Restriction and dense renumbering cannot increase the asymptotic number of clauses or variable-name width above $O(m\log m)$, giving the matching $O(N)$ bound.

If instead a grandchild has $N^{\lambda+o(1)}$ bits with fixed $\lambda<1$, it cannot contain $\Theta(m)$ untouched ternary clauses by the same counting. Necessarily $m-Dk=o(m)$, so $k\ge (1-o(1))m/D=\Omega(m)$. The complete assignment cube then has $2^{\Omega(m)}$ leaves.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope and loopholes.}
This theorem is a \emph{syntactic budget statement}, not a decision-time or general compression lower bound. It does not apply to a pruned adaptive tree, SAT-dependent simplification, a source-recoverable compressed symbolic representation, a large shared memoized subproblem, variable-elimination resolvents, an already-easy input family, or a padded promise instance. The adaptive comb report supplies an explicit counterexample to overgeneralizing the Cartesian premise: a depth-$k$ pruned spine can have $k+1$ leaves. The present controls intentionally include easy disjoint clauses, and a better solver may decide their SAT status without emitting any child.

\section{Two-real-level correctness and joint resource ledger}
For every formula $F$ and pin $x$, the Shannon identity
\[
\SAT(F)=\SAT(F|_{x=0})\vee\SAT(F|_{x=1})
\]
holds for both SAT and UNSAT. Apply the identity a second time to each actual ordinary first child; no child status is furnished free. The descendants are \emph{ordinary complete encodings} and may be fed to one fixed total guest SAT decider. This is an exact all-input semantic identity, but it has $\lambda=1$ on Theorem~\ref{th:retain}'s family. It is not an ARC successor. By contrast, the shared-name pair $(x)$ and $(\neg x)$ has individually SAT children but UNSAT conjunction: detached witnesses need a compatible named-interface join.

For a declared eager implementation enumerating all $N^{a+o(1)}$ leaves, producing their readable, writable ordinary buffers and calling the original $A$ on each without semantic short-circuit, Theorem~\ref{th:retain} gives only the certificate
\[
t'\le\max\{r,a+1+g,a+t\},\qquad
s'\le\max\{1,s,\eta,\kappa\}.
\]
Here $r$ pays all noncall branching, parsing, source reads, unchanged and changed writes, indices, arithmetic, selection and errors; $g$ is per-bit generation overhead; $\eta$ is work and $\kappa$ is live ancestor state. The displayed bound is an \emph{upper resource envelope} for this particular implementation; its inability to certify contraction does \emph{not} give an unconditional lower bound on the runtime of another SAT algorithm. In this eager architecture a held readable $\Theta(N)$-bit child costs $\Omega(N)$ writable space and each full original-machine call remains parent scale. An output-only tape cannot secretly double as a free random-access child.

\section{Polynomial image-inflation firewall}
\begin{corollary}[Declared full-stream reduction transport]\label{cor:inflate}
Let an ordinary source of length $L$ be mapped by a fixed reduction to an image of length $N(L)=L^{p+o(1)}$. Any declared transport that actually emits \emph{every} output bit by a counted sequential step before or during its SAT decision has time $\Omega(N(L))=L^{p-o(1)}$ on that source. If $p\ge c_*=241/200$, this particular full-stream pipeline cannot establish an all-source bound $L^{t+o(1)}$ with $t<c_*$, regardless of a fast decider on the padded image.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A deterministic machine emitting $N$ output bits performs at least $N$ counted output steps. The exponent inequality contradicts any fixed strict smaller time bound as $L\to\infty$. This proves a limitation of full emission, not of arbitrary local indexed or on-demand reductions.
\end{proof}

ARC-ADAPTIVE-COMB170's particular polynomial image has $N=L^{4+o(1)}$ and can have genuine $N^{1/2}$ and $N^{1/4}$ children on \emph{promised} padded instances. Full image streaming costs $L^{4+o(1)}$ and cannot transport that promise certificate to the strict \emph{source-length} FLMV box. A purely lazy reduction must instead pay every actual source bit probe, nested accessor, cache and source/ancestor state; Corollary~\ref{cor:inflate} deliberately makes no lower-bound claim about such different implementations. A hard image with a bit-local near-linear reduction remains a different, still OPEN path.
