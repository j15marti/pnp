\section{The endpoint-witness crossing theorem}
For arbitrary $r\ge2$, write $p=(p_1,\ldots,p_r)$ for designated Boolean port variables and $y$ for private Boolean auxiliaries. A proper pure-sign CNF $G(p,y)$ has only all-positive or all-negative OR clauses; there is no restriction on auxiliary signed degrees until stated explicitly. Define the exact \emph{existential port projection}
\[
\operatorname{Proj}_pG=\{p:\exists y\ G(p,y)=1\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint witnesses force singleton-port crossings]\label{th:cross}
Suppose $\operatorname{Proj}_pG=\{0^r,1^r\}$. Fix \emph{any} two extending witnesses $y^0,y^1$ with $G(0^r,y^0)=G(1^r,y^1)=1$, and define the directed switching set
\[
D=\{u:y^0_u=1,\ y^1_u=0\}.
\]
For every port $i$, the formula necessarily has:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{(P$_i$)}&\text{a positive clause containing }p_i
\text{ and no other port, whose auxiliary literals}\\
&\text{are all FALSE at }y^1\text{ and include a TRUE literal at }y^0;\\[2mm]
\text{(N$_i$)}&\text{a negative clause containing }\neg p_i
\text{ and no other port, whose auxiliary literals}\\
&\text{are all FALSE at }y^0\text{ and include a TRUE literal at }y^1.
\end{array}
\]
In particular each of these $2r$ clauses contains an auxiliary name from $D$, and $D\ne\emptyset$. If each auxiliary occurs in at most $d_+$ positive and at most $d_-$ negative clauses, then
\[
\boxed{\quad |D|\ge\left\lceil
 \frac{r}{\min\{d_+,d_-\}}\right\rceil.\quad}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At $(0^r,y^0)$ every positive clause is satisfied. Change only port $p_i$ from zero to one, retaining $y^0$. Positive clauses remain satisfied since they are coordinatewise monotone increasing. This nonconstant port assignment is excluded by the projection hypothesis; therefore some negative clause becomes false. It was true at $(0^r,y^0)$ and is false at $(e_i,y^0)$, so it contains $\neg p_i$ and no other negative port literal: any other port remains zero and would satisfy that negative clause. All its negative auxiliary literals are false at $y^0$, hence the included auxiliary bits are all one there. That same negative clause must be true at $(1^r,y^1)$, although all its port literals are false there, so one of its auxiliary bits must be zero at $y^1$. This auxiliary lies in $D$, proving (N$_i$).

Symmetrically, at $(1^r,y^1)$ every negative clause is satisfied. Lower only port $p_i$ to zero, retaining $y^1$. Negative clauses remain satisfied. Because the resulting port word is excluded, a positive clause must become false; its only port literal is $p_i$, and all its auxiliary bits are zero at $y^1$. At $(0^r,y^0)$ the same positive clause is true, though its port is zero, so an auxiliary bit is one at $y^0$. This is (P$_i$) and another occurrence of a name in $D$.

The forced positive clauses are distinct for different ports because each contains exactly one port; likewise the negative clauses. Each meets $D$. Counting incidence with auxiliaries in $D$ gives $r\le d_+|D|$ and $r\le d_-|D|$. The claimed bound follows. No future truth label, choice of solver, or computation transcript is used.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Important interpretations.}
The set $D$ counts \emph{differences between two static gadget extension witnesses}. It is not a temporal complete-machine-state flip count, a read/write charge, communication entropy, or decision time. If $y^0\le y^1$ coordinatewise, the same value $y^0$ extends every port word: all positive clauses hold because ports rise from $0^r$, and all negative clauses hold because $(p,y^0)\le(1^r,y^1)$. Thus equality projection forces $D\ne\emptyset$, with the stronger cardinality bound above.

\section{Specialization to the canonical three-port M22 gadget}
Each of the three ports has one positive occurrence already used outside the block. Inside the block the required port degrees are exactly $(1^+,2^-)$; each of $a$ private auxiliaries must have $(2^+,2^-)$. All block clauses have three distinct names and one polarity. A \emph{standalone} equality projection must be exactly $\{000,111\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Forced pure-sign incidence histogram]\label{th:quota}
Under these hypotheses, every positive gadget clause containing a port contains \emph{exactly one} port. There are exactly three such positive clauses, one per port. Writing $P_t,N_t$ for the counts of positive and negative block clauses containing exactly $t$ ports, necessarily
\[
(P_0,P_1,P_2,P_3)=\left(\frac{2a}{3}-2,3,0,0\right),
\]
and the only possible negative histograms are
\[
(N_0,N_1,N_2,N_3)=
\left(\frac{2a}{3}-4,6,0,0\right)
\quad\text{or}\quad
\left(\frac{2a}{3}-3,4,1,0\right).
\]
Moreover every pair of endpoint auxiliary witnesses satisfies
\[
|\{u:y^0_u=1,y^1_u=0\}|\ge2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{th:cross} supplies one separate singleton-port positive clause for every port. Each port has exactly one positive gadget occurrence, leaving no other positive port-containing clause, and fixing $P_1=3,P_2=P_3=0$. The signed degree quotas give $3\sum_tP_t=3+2a$ and $3\sum_tN_t=6+2a$, yielding the positive expression. A negative all-three-ports clause is false at $(111,y^1)$ and therefore forbidden, so $N_3=0$. The negative singleton clauses force $N_1\ge3$, and the port quota forces $N_1+2N_2=6$. Thus $N_2\in\{0,1\}$ with the displayed counts. Theorem~\ref{th:cross} with $r=3$ and $d_+=d_-=2$ implies the witness crossing has at least two auxiliary coordinates.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Endpoint capacity bound.}
For $a=9$ the gadget requires exactly seven positive and eight negative clauses. At $(000,y^0)$ every positive clause must have a TRUE \emph{auxiliary} literal, while each $y^0_u=1$ auxiliary can cover at most two such positive clauses; hence $\operatorname{wt}(y^0)\ge4$. At $(111,y^1)$ every negative clause must have a TRUE negative \emph{auxiliary} literal, so at least four auxiliaries must be zero at $y^1$: $\operatorname{wt}(y^1)\le5$. Together with directed crossing, the orbit signature
\[
(n_{00},n_{01},n_{10},n_{11}),\qquad
 n_{bc}=|\{u:(y^0_u,y^1_u)=(b,c)\}|
\]
must satisfy
\[
\boxed{
n_{10}\ge2,\quad n_{10}+n_{11}\ge4,\quad n_{00}+n_{10}\ge4,\quad
n_{00}+n_{01}+n_{10}+n_{11}=9.
}
\]
There are exactly 81 admissible signatures among all $\binom{12}{3}=220$ possible orbits. The six-variable SAT fixture $Y$ and arbitrary unrestricted SAT deciders have \emph{no} reason to satisfy these gadget-only conditions; applying the count to their computation is invalid.
