\section{Compatibility and explicit balanced layer}
For a CNF \(F\) on variables \(V(F)\) and a partial assignment
\(\alpha\in\{0,1,*\}^{V(F)}\), let
\[
 H_F(\alpha)=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \alpha\text{ extends to a satisfying assignment of }F.       \tag{1}
\]
Use the six-variable satisfiable block \(S\) with positive supports
\[
 123,145,246,356
\]
and the same four negative supports.  The assignment \(001100\) satisfies
\(S\).  D\"ocker's Proposition~1 supplies a fixed unsatisfiable
\(U\in\Core\) with 198 variables and 264 clauses~\cite{Doecker21}.  Put
\[
 B_1=S^{\sqcup 33},\qquad B_0=U.                              \tag{2}
\]
Both blocks have 198 variables and 264 clauses; \(B_1\) is satisfiable and
\(B_0\) is not.

Let \(G_0,G_1\) share the positive supports above and have negative supports
\[
 \begin{split}
 N_0&=\{135,146,236,245\},\\
 N_1&=\{136,145,235,246\}.
 \end{split}                                                   \tag{3}
\]
Every variable occurs twice with each sign, every clause is proper and
unique, and \(001011\) satisfies both formulas.  For
\(\beta=(0,*,0,*,0,*)\), exhaustive extension gives
\[
 H_{G_0}(\beta)=1,\qquad H_{G_1}(\beta)=0.                    \tag{4}
\]

For \(b\in\{0,1\}\) and \(u\in\{0,1\}^k\), define
\[
 E_{b,u}=B_b\sqcup G_{u_1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup G_{u_k},
 \qquad \mathcal E_k=\{E_{b,u}:b\in\{0,1\},u\in\{0,1\}^k\}.
                                                                    \tag{5}
\]
Disjoint variable intervals and fixed clause order make all encodings
distinct.  Each member has \(198+6k\) variables and \(264+8k\) clauses.  With
\(w_k=\lceil\log_2(198+6k+1)\rceil\), the declared literal-index encoding has
\[
 N_k=h(k)+(792+24k)(1+w_k)=\Theta(k\log k),                  \tag{6}
\]
where \(h(k)=O(\log k)\).  Hence every member of one layer has exactly the
same encoded length.

\section{Exact theorems}
\begin{lemma}[\texttt{UNSAT-COMPATIBILITY-COLLAPSE1}]
If \(F\) is unsatisfiable, then \(H_F\) is the constant-zero function.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An extension satisfying a restriction of \(F\) would also satisfy \(F\), a
contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{BALANCED-COMPATIBILITY-DIVERSITY-NO-COST1}]
For every \(k\ge1\), \(\mathcal E_k\subseteq\Core\) has \(2^{k+1}\) members,
exactly \(2^k\) satisfiable and \(2^k\) unsatisfiable.  The layer realizes
exactly \(2^k+1=2^{\Theta(N_k/\log N_k)}\) distinct functions \(H_F\).
Nevertheless there is a uniform deterministic recognizer \(R\) that decides
SAT on \(\mathcal E_k\) in \(O(N_k)\) time without evaluating or representing
any \(H_F\).

Moreover, for every total exact SAT decider \(A\), the wrapper
\[
 W[A](x)=
 \begin{cases}
 b,&x\text{ is the canonical encoding of }E_{b,u}\in\mathcal E_k,\\
 A(x),&\text{otherwise}
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{7}
\]
is a total exact SAT decider.  If \(A\) is polynomial time, so is \(W[A]\).
On the \same{} execution of \(W[A]\) on \(E_{b,u}\), parsing costs \(O(N_k)\),
output costs \(O(1)\), and fallback calls, compatibility queries, extraction,
and reconstruction are all zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Disjoint union preserves the clean-core conditions.  Since every \(G_i\) is
satisfiable, \(E_{b,u}\) is satisfiable exactly when \(b=1\), proving the
balanced count and recognizer correctness.

For a satisfiable member, let \(\alpha_i\) restrict only payload block \(i\)
according to \(\beta\) and leave all other variables unset.  Equations (4)--(5)
give
\[
 H_{E_{1,u}}(\alpha_i)=1-u_i.                                \tag{8}
\]
Thus the \(2^k\) YES members have pairwise distinct compatibility functions.
Every NO member has the one constant-zero function by the lemma, and that
function differs from every YES function already at the empty restriction.
This proves the exact total \(2^k+1\).  Equation (6) gives the stated
asymptotics.  A single left-to-right parse verifies the fixed macroblock
grammar and reads \(b\), proving all remaining claims and cost bounds.
\end{proof}

The exact negative quantifier statement is
\[
 \exists(\mathcal E_k)_{k\ge1}\ \forall A\in\mathsf{Dec}(\mathrm{SAT}):
 \quad W[A]\equiv\mathrm{SAT},\quad
 \mathrm{SAT}\in P\Rightarrow W[A]\in P,                    \tag{9}
\]
while \(W[A]\) pays none of the proposed compatibility costs on every member
of every layer.  This does not assert that the original \(A\) takes a shortcut;
it refutes an all-decider necessity through the equivalent \(W[A]\).

\section{Primary literature and barrier-transfer preflight}
D\"ocker's fixed UNSAT construction is a \textbf{DIRECTLY TRANSFERABLE
THEOREM} at the formula level: its proper, unique, exact-\((2,2)\) clauses are
exactly the declared core~\cite{Doecker21}.  It supplies \(B_0\), not a
machine lower bound.

Levin's distributional framework and the Bogdanov--Trevisan survey require an
explicit input distribution and algorithmic success criterion; bare 50/50
truth-label balance is not average-case hardness~\cite{Levin86,BT06}.
Impagliazzo's hard-core theorem converts a quantified existing failure rate
into a hard set~\cite{Impagliazzo95}; importing that premise for arbitrary SAT
deciders would assume the desired lower bound.  These are \textbf{DIRECTLY
TRANSFERABLE DEFINITIONS/THEOREMS} only after their hardness hypotheses are
proved.

Worst-case/average-case reduction architecture from LWE is a \textbf{USEFUL
STRUCTURAL ANALOGY REQUIRING A NEW TRANSFER THEOREM}.  Bogdanov--Trevisan's
barrier for nonadaptive black-box NP reductions is a caution, not an
impossibility theorem for every conceivable SAT bridge~\cite{BT06b}.
Branching-program, OBDD, communication, VLSI, proof-system, and online-causal
lower bounds are \textbf{FAILED TRANSFERS} without a compiler that places the
same execution in their model.  The present counterexample relativizes and
algebrizes; Baker--Gill--Solovay and Aaronson--Wigderson remain barriers, not
premises~\cite{BGS75,AW09}.
