Define two prefixes equivalent when every possible suffix selector gives the same SAT bit. Distinct bit matrices give distinct equivalence classes, so the contextual quotient has $2^{q^2}$ classes.
\textbf{PROVED/EXACT one-pass theorem.} Every deterministic uniform exact machine making one sequential pass, without random access, replay, input-dependent advice or external uncharged storage, must retain at least $q^2$ bits of persistent state at the prefix/suffix boundary. Otherwise two different prefix matrices have the same state; choose a position where they differ, append identical suffixes and obtain contradictory SAT labels. Restricted amortization: after any fixed $t$ independently selectable source fields, at least $t$ state bits are required to support every later selector. This measures a declared source-closed storage task, not arbitrary SAT-solving work.

\textbf{PROVED/EXACT countertheorem.} Two charged sequential passes suffice with $O(\log N)$ workspace: validate the canonical source and read the selector on pass one; revisit its designated record and output the opposite activation bit on pass two. This takes $O(N/w)$ total word reads. Random access allows direct suffix and selected-prefix reads. Thus the one-pass space bound does not imply unrestricted SAT time hardness, and a claimed inequality $s+R\ge q^2$ for unrestricted replay is false.
