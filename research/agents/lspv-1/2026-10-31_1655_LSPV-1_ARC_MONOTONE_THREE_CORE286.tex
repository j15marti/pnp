\section{A complete bounded-local theorem}
For a coherent partial assignment $\rho$, form the usual residual $F|\rho$: remove satisfied clauses and delete falsified literals from unsatisfied original clauses. Every surviving clause remains all-positive or all-negative, possibly shorter or empty. Its original clause identifier is retained as proof provenance. A \emph{core} is a conjunction of selected surviving clauses; a \emph{minimal core} is unsatisfiable and every proper selected subcollection is satisfiable.
\begin{theorem}[Complete classification of monotone residual cores with at most three clauses]
Any unsatisfiable subcollection of at most three monotone residual clauses has at least one of these certified obstructions:
\begin{description}
\item[] $\mathbf{E}$: an empty clause;
\item[] $\mathbf{P}$: opposite unit clauses $\{x\}$ and $\{\neg x\}$;
\item[] $\mathbf{T}$: three clauses $\{x\}$, $\{y\}$ and $\{\neg x,\neg y\}$ for distinct $x,y$, or the sign-dual $\{\neg x\}$, $\{\neg y\}$, $\{x,y\}$.
\end{description}
Conversely each of $\mathbf E,\mathbf P,\mathbf T$ is unsatisfiable. A minimal three-clause core is exactly pattern $\mathbf T$, up to renaming and simultaneous polarity inversion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For one or two nonempty monotone clauses, a contradiction is possible only for opposite units: if $A$ is positive and $\neg C$ negative, choose a variable in $A$ true and one in $C$ false; the choices are compatible unless both clauses are units on the same variable. Empty clauses yield $\mathbf E$.
Suppose a smallest unsatisfiable subcollection consists of three nonempty clauses. All clauses cannot have the same sign. By sign complementation, write the three as positive variable sets $A,B$ and one negative variable set $\neg C$, where $A,B,C$ are nonempty. For every choice $a\in A$ and $b\in B$, consider the assignment making only $a,b$ true and every other source variable false. This satisfies both positive clauses. Because the triple is UNSAT, it must falsify the negative clause. Hence
\[
 \forall a\in A\ \forall b\in B:\qquad C\subseteq\{a,b\}.\tag{$*$}
\]
If $|C|\ge 3$, $(*)$ is impossible. If $C=\{x\}$, condition $(*)$ says $x$ belongs to every choice pair, forcing $A=\{x\}$ or $B=\{x\}$; this gives a forbidden unsatisfiable proper pair. If $C=\{x,y\}$ with $x\ne y$, condition $(*)$ forces $\{a,b\}=\{x,y\}$ for every such pair; fixing either choice then forces $A=\{x\},B=\{y\}$ or vice versa. This is exactly $\mathbf T$. The negative-positive dual is identical. Direct inspection proves the converse and minimality.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Linear-scan authenticated detector]
After a certified, correctly computed residual is available, one can find every unsatisfiable core involving at most three residual clauses, or correctly report none, in $O(n+m)$ word operations and $O(n+m)$ words with fixed-size constant-width clauses. Record empty original clauses, signs/IDs of unit clauses, and for each residual binary clause test whether both opposite signed units are present. Emit the original clause indices and $\mathbf E$, $\mathbf P$ or $\mathbf T$ proof. The detector is a complete \emph{bounded-local refuter}, not a complete SAT decider.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
One clause/literal scan records all empties, a sign-indexed unit table with provenance, and the original-indexed residual binaries. For each binary $\ell_1\vee\ell_2$, check that unit $\neg\ell_1$ and unit $\neg\ell_2$ both exist; duplicates and signs are checked. The theorem supplies soundness and completeness for the declared core size. The original source scan, residual construction, table initialization and all physical bit/index operations are additional charged work; no unit or table is supplied for free.
\end{proof}

\section{Same-source valid target three-core counterexample}
Use the duplicate-free valid six-variable target source
\[
\begin{split}
C_0&=(x_1\vee x_2\vee x_3),\quad C_1=(x_1\vee x_4\vee x_5),\\
C_2&=(x_2\vee x_4\vee x_6),\quad C_3=(x_3\vee x_5\vee x_6),\\
C_4&=(\neg x_1\vee\neg x_2\vee\neg x_4),\\
C_5&=(\neg x_1\vee\neg x_3\vee\neg x_6),\\
C_6&=(\neg x_2\vee\neg x_3\vee\neg x_5),\\
C_7&=(\neg x_4\vee\neg x_5\vee\neg x_6).
\end{split}
\]
It is SAT (20 of the 64 complete assignments) and every original variable appears exactly twice per sign. Fix the \emph{coherent} source restriction
\[
\rho:\quad x_2=0,\ x_3=1,\ x_4=0,\ x_5=0;\qquad x_1,x_6\text{ unassigned}.
\]
The original-indexed triple $C_1,C_2,C_5$ reduces to
\[
 (x_1),\qquad(x_6),\qquad(\neg x_1\vee\neg x_6).
\]
Every pair is satisfiable, whereas all three are contradictory. Resolve original $C_1$ with $C_5$ on $x_1$, then with original $C_2$ on $x_6$:
\[
 (x_4\vee x_5\vee\neg x_3\vee\neg x_6),
 \quad (x_2\vee x_4\vee x_5\vee\neg x_3).
\]
The second is an original-source authenticated non-tautological clause $R$ implied by $F$ in two resolution steps. Its falsifying cube is precisely $\rho$, so every complete assignment extending $\rho$ is forbidden. The certificate proves $F|\rho$ UNSAT, not $F$ UNSAT; the source is SAT. The three original clauses supply proof provenance and the source-variable identities remain shared, not per-occurrence independent.
