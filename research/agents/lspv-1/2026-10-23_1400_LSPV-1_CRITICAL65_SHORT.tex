P versus NP remains OPEN. For a graph $G=(V,E)$ define
\[
\Delta(G)=
\bigwedge_{v\in V}(x_{v,0}\vee x_{v,1}\vee x_{v,2})
\wedge
\bigwedge_{uv\in E}\bigwedge_{c=0}^2
(\neg x_{u,c}\vee\neg x_{v,c}).
\]
It has $n=3|V|$ variables, $k=|V|$ ternary clauses and
$m=|V|+3|E|$ total clauses. Local at-most-one clauses are absent.
\textbf{Lemma (exact).} $\Delta(G)$ is satisfiable iff $G$ has a
3-coloring. Indeed, every model assigns each vertex a nonempty
color set, adjacent sets are disjoint, and choosing any color
per vertex yields a proper coloring.
\textbf{Theorem (exact).} $\Delta(G)$ is clause-minimally UNSAT
iff $G$ is 4-critical. If $G$ is critical, deletion of a
positive vertex clause permits a 3-coloring of $G-v$ with $v$
uncolored; deletion of an edge-color clause permits a coloring
of $G-uv$ whose endpoints share a color. Permute that common
color to the deleted clause color. Conversely, any witness
after such a deletion gives a coloring of $G-v$ or $G-uv$.
Thus the equivalence holds for every simple graph.
For an odd wheel $W_h$ with rim length $h\ge3$, the graph is
4-critical, $n=3(h+1)$, $k=h+1$, $m=7h+1$.
Use fixed four-word clause records with word width
$w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil$ and
$N=(2+4m)w=(28h+6)w$ input bits.
Each clause has one designated signed-literal word whose
one-bit polarity flip gives an opposite-label SAT formula:
use a satisfying deletion witness, where the removed clause
is false, then flip one of its literals.
Define independent sensitivity set $S(F)$ as these positions,
and the charged potential $\Phi_F(R)=|S(F)\setminus R|$
for read positions $R$.
\textbf{Amortization (exact, restricted).} Every total uniform
deterministic exact SAT decider with addressed-word source
access must read each designated position on the odd-wheel
UNSAT input. Otherwise it has an identical deterministic
transcript on the opposite-label one-word flip. One read
reduces $\Phi$ by at most one, so
\[
T_A(\Delta(W_h))\ge m=(N/w-2)/4=\Omega(N/\log N).
\]
The fixed unit/pure/subsumption calculus eliminates $d=0$
variables, hence $r=n-d=3(h+1)$ and $r/\log N\to\infty$.
Disjoint ternary supports give direct-assignment strong
2-CNF backdoor $k$. Nevertheless wheel templates and
their one-sign-flip neighbors are decided in $O(m)$ word time
using $O(\log N)$ auxiliary bits; an exact exhaustive fallback
makes this a total SAT decider. Wheel treewidth is three.
Therefore full clause-minimality, large residual, and large
backdoor do not establish exponential decision hardness.
For the graph template, $k=4$ is the first UNSAT case,
witnessed by $K_4$; arbitrary 2-CNF plus $k$ ternaries
can be UNSAT for $k=0$.
