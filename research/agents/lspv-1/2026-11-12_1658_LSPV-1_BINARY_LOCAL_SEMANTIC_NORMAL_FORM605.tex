\section*{Exact normal-form theorem}
Identify normalized, non-tautological clauses with sets of signed literals, including the empty clause. For $p\in A$ and $-p\in B$ define $R_p(A,B)=(A\setminus\{p\})\cup(B\setminus\{-p\})$.
\begin{theorem}[Binary semantic implicates]
For all finite normalized non-tautological clauses $A,B,C$,
\[
\boxed{A\land B\models C\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A\subseteq C\ \lor\ B\subseteq C\ \lor\
 \exists p\in A\cap(-B):
 (R_p(A,B)\text{ non-tautological}\ \land\ R_p(A,B)\subseteq C).}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both weakening cases are immediate, and a resolvent included in $C$ proves $C$ by one ordinary Resolution inference and weakening. Conversely, fix every literal of $C$ to false. Because $C$ is non-tautological, this partial assignment is consistent. The residual conjunction of the two input clauses is UNSAT. If one residual clause is empty, its original clause contained only literals of $C$, giving one of the first two disjuncts. If no residual clause is empty, and one is TRUE, then the conjunction cannot be UNSAT without the other clause being empty, already treated. Otherwise both residuals are nonempty clauses, jointly unsatisfiable. Such a pair must be complementary singletons: if two nonempty clauses have any two non-complementary literals, the two literals can be satisfied simultaneously. Write the singletons $\{p\}$ and $\{-p\}$. All other original literals were fixed false, and thus belong to $C$. Therefore their resolvent is a subset of $C$ and cannot be tautological.
\end{proof}

The theorem gives an \emph{exact restricted local-MAY transition characterization} for two explicit clause parents. It does not prove free native weakening, preserve any historical tariff, or permit dropping source identity, contexts, assumptions, paid-node histories or rank. GF(2) rows, AND3 factors, extension wires, DEF facets and discharged assumptions are not covered. Unknown genuine native rules must remain overapproximated.
