\section*{Theorem 587.1: exact weak saturation}
Fix a finite or infinite original-source-bound configuration set $\mathcal S_F$, legal transition relation
$s\xrightarrow{w}_R t$ with $w\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}$, and a set $G_F$ of \emph{genuine complete refutations}. A state includes the entire original-source-authenticated shared deduction DAG with distinct paid occurrence identities; the globally paid set; all assumption contexts and discharge certificates; source, definitions, freshness, exported/private wires, provenance, and exact rule side conditions. A fresh paid node is charged according to the fixed native weight; reuse of that \emph{same} already-paid occurrence is zero. Logical charge is not parser time, certificate bits, proof discovery, span, or peak physical memory.

Define the directed free reachability set
\[
Z(s)=\{t:\;s\xrightarrow{0*}_R t\}.
\]
The \emph{saturated} states are the distinct sets $Z(s)$, with concretization $\gamma(Z)=Z$ and abstraction $\alpha(s)=Z(s)$. A saturated state is terminal exactly when $Z\cap G_F\ne\varnothing$. For each $w>0$ put
\[
Z(s)\xrightarrow{w}_{\#}Z(t)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(\exists u\in Z(s))\;u\xrightarrow{w}_R t.
\]
In this construction every original zero-charge edge is \emph{absorbed into closure}, not deleted from the mathematical semantics. Then for every $s\in\mathcal S_F$
\[
\boxed{
\inf_{\pi:\,s\leadsto G_F}\sum_{e\in\pi}w(e)
=
\inf_{\widehat\pi:\,Z(s)\leadsto G_\#}\sum_{e\in\widehat\pi}w(e).
}
\]
If no finite goal path exists both infima are $+\infty$.

\noindent\textbf{Proof.}
Given a finite concrete goal walk, divide it into maximal finite zero-charge subwalks separated by positive transitions. Each positive edge $u\xrightarrow w t$ produces the saturated edge from the current free closure to $Z(t)$; the last closure contains its concrete goal. Hence the saturated infimum is no larger than the concrete infimum.
Conversely, a saturated positive edge has an explicit witness $u\in Z(s)$ followed by $u\xrightarrow w t$. From the concrete representative $s$ one can traverse zero-charge steps to $u$ and then take the paid edge. The next abstract state is precisely $Z(t)$; inductively each later edge has an independently liftable zero-charge prefix. When the abstract goal is reached, a final zero-charge suffix reaches an actual $G_F$ state. This lifts every finite saturated goal walk with \emph{equal} accumulated charge, so the concrete infimum is no larger. The two inequalities prove equality. $\square$

\noindent\textbf{Concrete-step inclusion.} For each free edge $s\xrightarrow0t$, $Z(t)\subseteq Z(s)$, hence $\operatorname{Post}_0(\gamma(Z))\subseteq\gamma(Z)$. For every paid edge from any $u\in\gamma(Z)$ its exact-cost abstract successor is present. Every concrete goal belongs to an abstract goal closure. This is a \emph{weak} transition simulation, not a claim that $\alpha(s)$ equals $\alpha(t)$ for each one-way free edge.

\section*{Theorem 587.2: explicit failure modes}
First, a zero transition $A\to B$, a cost-one transition $A\to G$, and a cost-ten transition $B\to G$ give distance $d(B)=10$ but $d(A)=1$. Merging $A$ and $B$ by identical labels or one-way reachability manufactures the false \emph{exact} distance one from $B$. Such a MAY quotient can remain a conservative underestimator, but it destroys exactness. The correct directed closures are $Z(A)=\{A,B\}$ and $Z(B)=\{B\}$.

Second, in a declared paid-owner model a single cost-one fresh owner makes two goal facets available by separately free reuse transitions. A proof needs only one globally paid owner, not the sum of two branch/facet charges. The correct initial optimum is one. If an additional actually legal zero-charge transition from the initial state straight to the goal is omitted from saturation, the calculated value remains one even though the true optimum becomes zero. Thus the assertion of a \emph{positive} lower bound requires authenticated coverage of every free transition. An UNKNOWN full historical rule cannot be silently assigned an impossible transition; allowing it conservatively with zero charge may collapse the bound.
