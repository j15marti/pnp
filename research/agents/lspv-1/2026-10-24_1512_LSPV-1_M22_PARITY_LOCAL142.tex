\section{Constructive marginal theorem}
For a proper OR3 clause $C$ on named bits $(x_a,x_b,x_c)$ let $z_C$ be its unique falsifying three-bit tuple. Write $p_C=z_{C,1}\oplus z_{C,2}\oplus z_{C,3}$. Define $\mu_C$ to be uniform on the four tuples with parity $1-p_C$. For all-positive clauses it is uniform on $001,010,100,111$; for all-negative clauses it is uniform on $000,011,101,110$.
\begin{theorem}[Pairwise-compatible satisfying local measures, PROVED/EXACT]
For every proper 3-CNF with no duplicated \emph{unsigned} three-variable support, every $\mu_C$ is supported on satisfying local assignments, and its marginal on any $s\le2$ named coordinates is exactly uniform, probability $2^{-s}$ per row. Consequently the $\mu_C$ and $\mu_D$ agree on their entire overlap for every pair of clauses. The conclusion holds on every pair-free $\mathcal C_\star$ formula, even if the formula is globally UNSAT.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The sole falsifying triple has parity $p_C$, and the chosen parity class excludes it. Fixing $s\le2$ coordinates leaves precisely $2^{2-s}$ of the four parity-class tuples, hence probability $2^{-s}$. Different three-name supports intersect in at most two names, so their projected marginals agree. No joint global satisfying distribution is asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Two cubic edge covers, PROVED/EXACT]

For each source variable $x$, its two positive occurrences are endpoints of the edge labeled $x$ in $G_+$; its two negative occurrences are endpoints of the edge labeled $x$ in $G_-$. Both are loopless cubic multigraphs, each with $2k/3$ clause vertices and $k$ labeled edges; parallel edges are permitted. For the true-name set $X$,
\[
F(X)=1\iff
\big(\forall v\in V(G_+):\deg_{X}(v)\ge1\big)
\land\big(\forall w\in V(G_-):\deg_{X}(w)\le2\big).
\]
Thus $X$ covers all vertices of $G_+$ and its complement covers all vertices of $G_-$. This recovers the original clause lists exactly, but supplies no automatic algorithmic contraction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Exactly two occurrences of each polarity give two distinct endpoints per labeled edge. Three distinct variables per clause give cubic degree. A positive OR requires at least one true incident edge; a negative OR requires at least one false edge. Edge labels together with clause vertices recover the original source.
\end{proof}
