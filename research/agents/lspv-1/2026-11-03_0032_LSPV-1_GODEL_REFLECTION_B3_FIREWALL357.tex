\section*{One-sided bounded-arithmetic sufficient condition}
Let $A$ be \emph{one} fixed externally correct uniform total P-time general CNF decider, including MALFORMED. Let $\mathrm{Valid}(s)$ be the total syntax predicate, $\mathrm{Eval}(s,y)$ its satisfying-assignment predicate, and $\mathrm{Reject}_A(s)$ denote return of UNSAT. All are P-time. The bounded universal sentence
\[
\mathrm{RS}_A:\quad \forall s,y\,(\mathrm{Valid}(s)\land\mathrm{Eval}(s,y)\longrightarrow\neg\mathrm{Reject}_A(s))
\]
states only \emph{rejection soundness}, not all of algorithm correctness.
\begin{proposition}[One provable P-policy suffices]
Suppose $\mathrm{RS}_A$ is provable in a fixed suitable bounded-arithmetic theory whose universal bounded propositional translations have uniformly constructible polynomial-size Extended Frege proofs (for example the usual $S^1_2$/PV correspondence in its appropriate formula class). Then for the preselected $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ every valid UNSAT $F$ of N bits has an $\mathrm{ER}_2$ refutation of $N^{O_A(1)}$ charged nodes. Proofs can be constructed uniformly in polynomial time using $A$ and its one fixed formal rejection-soundness proof.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Cook's bounded-arithmetic translation produces a polynomial Extended Frege proof of the universal finite-length circuit instance of $\mathrm{RS}_A$. Specialize the formula-index bits to the \emph{actual signed} source $\langle F\rangle$. Compute $A(F)=\mathrm{UNSAT}$ and discharge this concrete circuit evaluation and the actual parser bits by polynomial propositional reasoning. The result is an EF proof of $\neg\mathrm{Eval}(\langle F\rangle,y)$, a tautology in arbitrary witness bits. Standard definitional expansion and polynomial EF-to-ER simulation yield a signed original-clause ER refutation of F. Compile all Boolean extensions into fresh paid two-input signed VARDEF nodes and every pivot into strict paid RES. No mixed-rule restriction theorem or hidden oracle is invoked. All constants are fixed by A and its fixed arithmetic proof, not F. \end{proof}
\noindent This is a \textbf{CONDITIONAL} nonrelativizing sufficient criterion, not B3 from SAT$\in P$. The implication SAT$\in P\Rightarrow\exists A\, [T\vdash\mathrm{RS}_A]$ has not been proved. The hypothesis is stronger than mere external correctness, but weaker than demanding a full arithmetic proof of every part of A.

\section*{A total correct but internally uncertifiable SAT program}
Let $T$ be a consistent recursively axiomatized classical arithmetic theory with a polynomial-time checkable proof predicate $\mathrm{Prf}_T(p,\ulcorner 0=1\urcorner)$, enough reasoning about elementary polynomial computations and the second incompleteness property $T\not\vdash\mathrm{Con}(T)$. Peano Arithmetic with an ordinary proof encoding is an example. Let B be \emph{any} fixed uniform total correct general SAT decider, possibly exponential. Given arbitrary binary p of length r, form a canonical signed DIMACS CNF
\[
E(p)=(z_1)\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1}^{r}
\begin{cases}(z_{i+1}),&p_i=1,\\(\neg z_{i+1}),&p_i=0.\end{cases}\tag{1}
\]
Every $E(p)$ is SAT with an explicit unit-sign assignment. The encoding is injective, polynomial-time recognizable/invertible with $O((r+1)\log(r+2))$ source bits, and is never malformed. Define the single fixed wrapper
\[
 A_T(x)=\begin{cases}
 \mathrm{UNSAT},&x=\langle E(p)\rangle\text{ canonically and }\mathrm{Prf}_T(p,\ulcorner0=1\urcorner),\\
 B(x),&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}\tag{2}
\]
This checks \emph{one decoded candidate} p from x, not an unbounded proof search. Every nonmatching valid SAT/UNSAT or malformed input uses the correct fallback B.
\begin{theorem}[Godelian one-sided reflection firewall]
If T is consistent, A_T is a single uniform total correct general SAT decider computing exactly the same function as B on \emph{all} binary inputs, with
\[
T_{A_T}(x)\le T_B(x)+\operatorname{poly}(|x|).
\]
Thus if SAT is in P and B is a P-time decider, A_T is another correct P-time decider. Nevertheless
\[
\boxed{T\not\vdash\mathrm{RS}_{A_T}.}
\]
It is therefore unsound to require a T-correctness proof for \emph{every} correct SAT decider as an argument for B1; this does \emph{not} refute B1, B2 or B3.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Consistency means that no actual p is accepted by the correct T-proof checker as a proof of $0=1$. The special branch of (2) is never executed, making A_T extensionally identical to B, including all SAT, UNSAT and malformed labels. Checking the unique candidate p is polynomial in the source length. Internally, T proves the elementary syntactic statements $\mathrm{Valid}(\langle E(p)\rangle)$, $\mathrm{Eval}(\langle E(p)\rangle,y_p)$ for the explicit unit witness and
\[
\mathrm{Prf}_T(p,\ulcorner0=1\urcorner)\longrightarrow \mathrm{Reject}_{A_T}(\langle E(p)\rangle).
\]
Therefore T proves $\mathrm{RS}_{A_T}\longrightarrow
\forall p\neg\mathrm{Prf}_T(p,\ulcorner0=1\urcorner)=\mathrm{Con}(T)$.
If T proved $\mathrm{RS}_{A_T}$ it would prove its own consistency, contradicting Godel's second incompleteness theorem. The theorem applies even to \emph{one-sided} rejection soundness, regardless of whether B itself has a T-proof. \end{proof}
\noindent The construction is an application of classical incompleteness, not an original incompleteness theorem. It separates \emph{external semantic equality of algorithms} from \emph{internal provability of their code's correctness}. B3 asks for one hypothetical P-decider, not formal soundness of all of them, so the bridge remains OPEN.
