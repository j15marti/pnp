\section{CONSTRUCTION OR PROOF ATTEMPT}
\begin{theorem}[Global lexicographic output obstruction]
On explicit indexed proper positive 2-CNF clause streams of encoded length $N$, a uniform general sequential transducer required to emit \emph{all clauses globally sorted} cannot have simultaneously time $N^{11/10+o(1)}$ and workspace $N^{1/2+o(1)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any sorting input of pairwise distinct $y_1,\ldots,y_n\in[n^2]$ construct, in the original order, $C_i=(x_{y_i}\lor x_{n^2+i})$. Each clause has two distinct positive literals, and the complete sparse indexed encoding has $N=\Theta(n\log n)$ bits. Global lexicographic clause sorting orders by increasing $y_i$. The second literal $n^2+i$ reveals the original position, so output recovers the full sorting permutation, including ranks. An indexed clause input is simulated by reading one original integer, charged at polylog bit time and logarithmic scratch; output decoding is equally cheap. The sorting algorithm inherits both time and space costs from this one transducer, plus polylogarithmic overhead. The resulting $n^{1.1+o(1)}$ time times $n^{0.5+o(1)}$ workspace violates the Borodin--Cook $n^{2-o(1)}$ product lower bound. The input clauses are all satisfiable by the all-TRUE assignment; this proves no SAT decision lower bound.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Exact source-order-free residual multiset quotient]
For any explicitly encoded width-at-most-three CNF $F$ with complete length $N$, boundary $B$ of $b$ variables, $R=2^b$, and $D$ distinct projected clause bodies after removing boundary literals, one deterministic algorithm with a user-provided cap $D_{\max}$ either returns UNKNOWN on exceeding it or computes exactly the equivalence relation among all $R$ boundary assignments induced by identical residual clause \emph{multisets}. Successful cost is $T=O(NR\operatorname{polylog}N)$ bit time and $S=O((DR+R+b+1)\log N)$ bits on one machine. Thus $R\le N^{1/10+o(1)}$ and $D\le N^{2/5+o(1)}$ fit the proposed local budget independently of clause order.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Define the projected clause key $D_B(C)$ by removing literals on variables in $B$. Under boundary assignment $\alpha$, retain this key exactly when none of those removed literals satisfies $C$. Include an empty key, which represents a contradiction. A deterministic balanced dictionary stores every projected key (at most three signed variable indices) and an array of $R$ integer multiplicities. Scan every original clause exactly once; for every $\alpha$ increment the corresponding key count only if the clause survives. Each count uses $O(\log N)$ bits, and dictionary keys and pointers are $O(\log N)$ bits each. The final row vector for $\alpha$ records the exact multiplicity of \emph{every} residual clause. Two residual multisets are equal if and only if these row vectors match, so sorting the $R$ short vectors exactly yields the required equivalence classes. All comparisons, counters, reads, and output are charged. On key-cap failure halt with UNKNOWN and make no decision. This is not an all-input SAT contractor.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Fingerprint counterexample]
For $A=\{0,3,5,6\}$ and $B=\{1,2,4,7\}$, $\sum_{a\in A}a^j=\sum_{b\in B}b^j$ for $j=0,1,2$, but the multisets differ and their $j=3$ sums are respectively 368 and 416.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first three sums equal $(4,14,70)$ by integer arithmetic. Modular reductions for any primes cannot distinguish exactly equal integer moments. A Horn CNF with boundary $z$, positive gate $z$ on clauses $(z\lor\neg x_{10+a})$ for $a\in A$, and gate $\neg z$ on $(\neg z\lor\neg x_{10+b})$ for $b\in B$ realizes the two different residual clause sets while all instances remain trivially satisfiable. This refutes only the bounded-moment proposal.
\end{proof}
