## Main theorem (PROVED/RESTRICTED)
Let $X=(u_1,v_1,\ldots,u_g,v_g)\in\mathbb F_2^{2g}$ and $y_i=u_iv_i$ for $i=1,\ldots,g$. Let $\mathcal H$ be **any covering** of the full Boolean cube by nonempty affine subspaces, allowing overlaps and arbitrary affine equations. If each coordinate function $y_i$ is affine when restricted to each $H\in\mathcal H$, then
$$
\boxed{\dim H\le g,\qquad |\mathcal H|\ge2^g.}
$$
Both inequalities are tight: fixing each $u_i$ yields precisely $2^g$ disjoint cells, each with dimension $g$, on which $y_i$ is either $0$ or $v_i$.

**Proof.** Fix an affine cell $H=a+Lt$ with $t\in\mathbb F_2^d$ and $\mathrm{rank}(L)=d$. Write $u_i|_H=a_i+U_i t$ and $v_i|_H=b_i+V_i t$. If $U_i,V_i$ are linearly independent, choose two directions $r,s\in\mathbb F_2^d$ producing $(U_i r,V_i r)=(1,0)$ and $(U_i s,V_i s)=(0,1)$. The second affine difference of $(a_i+U_it)(b_i+V_it)$ over the parallelogram $t,t+r,t+s,t+r+s$ equals $1$, whereas an affine function's second difference is $0$. Thus for each $i$, $\mathrm{rank}\binom{U_i}{V_i}\le1$. The rows of $L$ comprise these $g$ pairs, so $d=\mathrm{rank}(L)\le\sum_{i=1}^g\mathrm{rank}\binom{U_i}{V_i}\le g$. Every cell contains at most $2^g$ points; covering $2^{2g}$ points requires at least $2^g$ cells, even if overlapping. Fixing all $u_i$ achieves equality. QED.

**Scope:** The theorem applies to **full-input affine-subspace covers linearizing all named gate outputs**. It does not cover nonaffine leaf regions, source-constrained domains, alternative inferential representations, or compressed shared reasoning. It proves tightness of the *cell-count factor*, NOT mixed-refutation size, an all-algorithm lower bound, or any historical native-rule authentication.
