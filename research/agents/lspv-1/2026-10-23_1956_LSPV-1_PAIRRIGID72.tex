\section{Language, machine, backward circuit}
Let $L_{2,2}$ contain the satisfiable, valid, densely named, proper, pairwise distinct signed 3-CNFs for which each original variable occurs exactly two times with each polarity. Invalid encodings are rejected. Thus $4n=3m$. This language is NP-complete because it contains the known proper sign-monochromatic exact-$(2,2)$ NP-complete subclass (D\"ocker), subject to the stated validation/encoding. A uniform deterministic RAM has word width $w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil$ and the fixed two-header/four-word-per-clause source length $N=(2+4m)w$ bits. Each source read, word operation, output write and replay has unit word cost; widths and source identifiers are never free.
\par\noindent For the AND of $m$ clause-ORs with shared source identities, use one shared NOT gate per variable, two binary ORs per ternary, and $m-1$ binary AND gates. This declared evaluator has $n+3m-1=5n-1$ gates and $n+6m-2=9n-2$ directed gate-input references. These are not lower bounds on SAT-solving circuitry. Backward propagation from TRUE requires every AND child and at least one true literal occurrence in each OR. \section{New complementary-pair rigidity}
Call two distinct ternary clauses a complementary pair when their signed literal sets are $A\cup\{u\}$ and $A\cup\{\neg u\}$ with $A=\{a,b\}$ a pair of \emph{identical signed literals} on two distinct variables. They can be contracted exactly:
\[
(A\vee u)\wedge(A\vee\neg u)\equiv A.
\]
Form a graph on the original clause records with one edge for each such pair.
\begin{theorem}[Pair graph is a matching; PROVED/EXACT]
On every valid signed exact-$(2,2)$ proper 3-CNF with unique clauses, every clause vertex has complementary-pair degree at most one. Consequently, the complete list of pairs is unique and disjoint, and all available pairs can be contracted simultaneously with no matching-selection problem.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $C$ has distinct partners $D,E$. A partner differs from $C$ in exactly one literal's polarity. If both partners flip the same variable, they are identical clauses, violating the unique-clause rule. Otherwise, flips occur in different variables, so their third literal is identically signed in $C,D,E$. This gives three occurrences of one signed source literal, contrary to the exact-two-occurrences promise. Hence two distinct partners cannot exist.
\end{proof}
\par\noindent A uniform detector validates the source signs and count promise, indexes each signed literal's two clause IDs, and tests candidate pairs; source validation and pair detection cost $O(n+m)$ after a possible $O(m\log m)$ duplicate/normalization sort. No hidden general matching algorithm is required. This rigidity concerns complementary pairs, \emph{not} Role-2's different paired-monotone-support graph or the earlier RESOLVE-MATCH mediator graph.
\section{Strong positive theorem: all paired instances are SAT}
Let $p(F)$ be the number of edges of this uniquely determined pair graph and define the \emph{unmatched-clause defect}
\[
\delta(F)=m-2p(F).
\]
It is a noncircular property of the explicit signed source, not the observed runtime or the size of a supposed mandatory full-relation compilation.
\begin{theorem}[Fully paired SAT with a source witness; PROVED/EXACT]
If $\delta(F)=0$, then $F$ is satisfiable. After charged source validation, a satisfying original-variable assignment is constructible by a degree-two cycle orientation in $O(n+m)$ additional RAM word operations, followed by original-source replay.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Contract all disjoint complementary pairs. A variable that is the flipped pivot of a pair consumes one positive and one negative occurrence there. Its two remaining positive/negative occurrences cannot constitute the \emph{same-sign doubled} nonpivot appearances required by any contracted pair. Thus a variable either occurs as a pivot in two pairs, disappearing entirely, or occurs as a nonpivot exactly once in a positive pair and once in a negative pair. In the latter case its original two positive occurrences lie in one pair and its two negative occurrences in another, and contraction leaves precisely one positive and one negative literal in two different binary clauses.
\par\noindent Create a multigraph with one vertex per contracted binary clause and an edge for each surviving source variable between its positive and negative clause. Each clause vertex has degree exactly two (no tautological binary because each original clause was proper on distinct variables). Hence every component is a cycle, permitting parallel edges. Orient each cycle consistently. For each edge variable choose its Boolean value to make the corresponding signed literal TRUE at the \emph{head} of that directed edge and false at the tail; this assigns one global truth value to each original source variable. Every binary clause vertex has one incoming edge and thus at least one true literal (in fact exactly one). Variables used only as pivots are unconstrained and may be set arbitrarily. The contracted 2-CNF and, by the exact pair identities, the original proper 3-CNF are satisfied. Complete source replay verifies the witness.
\end{proof}
\par\noindent This is strictly stronger than using SCC after pairing: on the full-pair grammar, a SAT witness is guaranteed; there is no UNSAT branch. It applies to \emph{signed} exact-$(2,2)$ formulas and does not prove easy satisfiability of the NP-complete strictly sign-monochromatic class.
\section{Exact parameterized positive algorithm and amortization}
\begin{theorem}[Defect-parameterized exact decision; PROVED/EXACT]
Every valid signed exact-$(2,2)$ formula $F$ is decidable deterministically, with original-source witness replay on YES, in
\[
T(F)=O\bigl(m\log(m+2)+\delta(F)^2+2^{h(F)}(n+m)\bigr),\qquad
h(F)\le\delta(F),
\]
using $O(n+m)$ words of reusable peak workspace. In particular $\delta(F)=O(\log N)$ implies polynomial-time decision.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Validate and sort the entire source, form and simultaneously contract all forced pairs, keeping their exact signed source mapping. The resulting formula consists of $p$ binary clauses and $\delta$ unpaired ternaries. Construct $H$ by choosing at least one original variable from each residual ternary support, deduplicating choices; $|H|=h\le\delta$. For all $2^h$ assignments to $H$, simplify every clause and solve the remaining 2-CNF by implication-graph SCC. Source coherence is maintained because every occurrence shares its original variable ID. If a branch is SAT, reconstruct all remaining values and replay-check the original CNF; if none is SAT, return UNSAT. All failed branches and SCC calls are charged. Finding $H$ by a naive deterministic scan takes at most $O(\delta^2)$ work; the contracted formula and each reused SCC graph take $O(n+m)$ words.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Restricted amortization; PROVED/EXACT]
Fix the \emph{explicit pair-contraction calculus}: before continuation it must validate each contracted pair and emit one explicit binary-clause record per pair, with no other transformations allowed to eliminate such a pair. After $j$ contractions define
\[
\Psi_j=m-2j,\qquad 0\le j\le p.
\]
Each pair operation decreases $\Psi$ by exactly two, costs at least one inspected pair and one emitted binary record, and $p=(m-\delta)/2$ pair operations are compulsory \emph{within this calculus}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Disjointness of pair edges shows each operation consumes exactly two original ternary records, replaces them by one explicit binary record, and never changes any other pair. Therefore exactly $p$ such operations are necessary and sufficient to eliminate every pair in the declared output format.
\end{proof}
\par\noindent\textbf{FIREWALL:} neither $\Psi$ nor $\delta$ forces an arbitrary exact decider to construct pair contractions, produce all witnesses, count models, or answer arbitrary contexts. The lemma is a lower bound for an explicit normalization task, not an unrestricted SAT-decision lower bound. A hypothetical polynomial decider may decide first; it cannot be charged for optional compilation work.
