\section{Exact source grammar}
Every ternary clause must occur in one of the four signed 3CNF clauses exactly encoding an XOR3 equation; the remaining clauses have length at most two. Each recognized quartet preserves original clause IDs and physical byte offsets. Reversible GF(2) row operations, pivoting affine-private variables before binary-clause-visible ones, yield either a source-indexed contradiction $0=1$ or the \emph{complete} affine solution parametrization
\[
 x_i=a_i\oplus\bigoplus_{j\in S_i}t_j,\qquad 1\le i\le n,
\]
with $r=n-\operatorname{rank}(A)$ free bits. For the set $V_C$ of variables appearing in the unary/binary part, define
\[
 J=\bigcup_{i\in V_C:|S_i|\ge2}S_i,\qquad k=|J|.
\]
This $k$ is a polynomially computable property of the fixed canonical basis. It is not a basis-invariant optimum, and alternate-basis search is not free.
\begin{theorem}[Source-authenticated affine support selector]
One fixed deterministic total polynomial-time \emph{partial} SAT decider correctly decides every well-formed input in this grammar with $k\le\lfloor\log_2N\rfloor$, where $N$ is the physical encoded source length. It emits a source-checked satisfying assignment on YES, and on NO a custom complete source-verified UNSAT certificate consisting of an affine contradiction or a contradictory 2SAT implication cycle for each of the at most $2^k$ conditional branches. All discovery, failed branches, proof records and checking, physical output, caches and simultaneous peak storage are polynomially bounded. On other sources the algorithm may return UNKNOWN, without guessing.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix values of the free coordinates indexed by $J$. Any clause-visible $x_i$ with $|S_i|\ge2$ becomes constant, because $S_i\subseteq J$. Every other visible coordinate becomes constant or a signed literal in at most one remaining $t_j$. Therefore every residual original unary/binary clause remains of width at most two and is decided by 2SAT. All affine assignments are represented exactly once by the parametrization, so exhaustive branching covers all possible solutions. A 2SAT UNSAT proof records a path from $t_j$ to $\neg t_j$ and a reverse path, or a source clause reduced to false. Each edge carries an original clause ID, and the checker recomputes the authenticated Gaussian basis and every branch to validate the proof. A SAT witness is expanded and checked against all original clauses. With $k\le\log_2N$, at most $N$ branches and polynomial-size outputs are produced. The same one program handles both truth labels, without any answer-class advice.
\end{proof}

\end{proposition}
