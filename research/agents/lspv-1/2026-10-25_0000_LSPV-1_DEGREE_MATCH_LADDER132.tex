\section{The exact source language and adaptive parameters}
An input \(H\) is a positive exact-one formula with \(m\) triples \(C_i=(x_{i1},x_{i2},x_{i3})\), all three variables distinct, demanding
\[
 \sum_{x\in C_i} x=1 \quad\hbox{over ordinary integers for every }i.
\]
Write \(\deg_H(x)\) for the \emph{source occurrence count}, defined independently of solver time, and
\[
 k(H)=|\{x:\deg_H(x)\ge 3\}|.
\]
This is a promise/source-language parameter, not a parameter of arbitrary 3CNF after Tseitin encoding. A solver may choose a different representation or strategy during its computation. No SAT-label oracle is supplied.

Every local exact-one triple has exactly three feasible signatures and an affine hull of dimension two:
\[
 E(a,b,c)\equiv(a\oplus b\oplus c=1)\land(\neg a\vee\neg b).
\]
These facts were already established in LSPV-3 EXACTONE-JOIN-CYCLE95 and independently checked in LSPV-5 THREE-STATE-JOIN-FIREWALL130; we do not relabel them as new. The nontrivial problem is global sharing of the source variables.

\section{Polynomial decision and witness reconstruction at degree two}
\begin{theorem}[Occurrence-two matching algorithm; restricted PROVED/EXACT]\label{th:match}
For every positive exact-one instance \(H\) in which each source variable occurs in at most two triples, there is a uniform polynomial-time exact SAT/UNSAT decider that also returns a witness when one exists. The algorithm makes no SAT-oracle calls and does not enumerate three local signatures per clause.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Construct a graph with one vertex \(v_i\) for every source clause \(C_i\). If variable \(x\) occurs in distinct clauses \(C_i,C_j\), add the edge \(v_iv_j\), labelled \(x\); if \(x\) occurs only in \(C_i\), add an edge \(v_i\ell_x\) to a private leaf \(\ell_x\), also labelled \(x\). Each edge represents setting exactly its source variable true, thereby satisfying exactly one or two clause vertices. A set of true variables satisfies every exact-one source clause if and only if its labelled edges form a matching saturating every clause vertex. In one direction, exactly one true occurrence at every clause makes the corresponding edges pairwise nonincident at clause vertices; private leaves cannot conflict. In the other direction, a matching saturating clause vertices selects a consistent true-variable set: source variables have been represented by one edge, so a clause is satisfied by exactly its one incident selected edge.

Parallel edges between the same clause pair, arising from different source variables, are interchangeable for the existence and construction of one matching. Preserve one representative label for each; none can be simultaneously selected. Assign weight two to clause--clause edges and weight one to clause--private-leaf edges. Every selected matching edge then covers exactly its weight in distinct clause vertices. Thus a maximum-weight matching has weight \(m\) if and only if all \(m\) clauses can be satisfied. Standard weighted matching in general graphs is polynomial-time; alternatively, polynomial cardinality matching can be applied to an appropriate cardinality-constrained construction. Parsing, occurrence counting, edge generation, matching, and witness reconstruction are all polynomial and explicitly charged. No assumption about planarity, acyclicity, graph cycle count or SAT labels is required.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[High-occurrence fixed-parameter algorithm; restricted PROVED/EXACT]\label{th:fpt}
For every positive exact-one instance \(H\), exact decision and optional witness extraction take \(2^{k(H)}\operatorname{poly}(|H|)\) time and polynomial writable space by one fixed uniform algorithm. All branch choices and failed branches are charged.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Enumerate the assignments to the \(k(H)\) source variables occurring at least three times. Reject an assignment when a clause has two or more true high-degree occurrences. For each clause already satisfied by exactly one true high-degree variable, force every other occurrence false and discharge that clause. For every remaining clause, remove variables forced false and retain an exact-one requirement. Empty remaining clauses reject that branch. Every remaining free variable originally had source occurrence at most two, hence has degree at most two in the residual, which Theorem~\ref{th:match} decides with a polynomial matching algorithm. A global satisfying witness selects exactly one high-degree branch and a residual matching. Conversely every accepting branch constructs an assignment satisfying all original source clauses. There are at most \(2^{k(H)}\) branches, each charged polynomial work, with polynomial peak space when processed sequentially.
\end{proof}
For \(k(H)=0\), the result is unconditional polynomial exact decision, even for connected cyclic source graphs. For general \(k\), the bound is an \emph{upper} bound, not a lower bound. Other source-aware algorithms may improve it.

\section{The degree-three hardness boundary}
\begin{theorem}[Source occurrence-three threshold; KNOWN THEOREM transferred]\label{th:hard}
Positive exact-one SAT restricted to source instances in which each variable appears exactly three times is NP-complete, even under the planar cubic incidence restrictions in Moore--Robson. Therefore the degree-two polynomial result does not extend to degree three by a blanket polynomial-time algorithm unless \(P=NP\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Moore and Robson prove NP-completeness of planar cubic monotone 1-in-3-SAT, where each clause has three positive source variables and each source variable occurs three times. Their source problem is exactly the degree-three sublanguage just defined. Satisfying assignments are polynomially verifiable, giving NP membership. The theorem is a \emph{known restricted-language NP-completeness result}; it does not show a superpolynomial lower bound for arbitrary exact SAT deciders.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact ordinary proper-3CNF transport}

\[
 (\neg a\vee\neg b\vee z)\land(\neg a\vee\neg b\vee\neg z)

for one fresh private \(z\). This uses six proper three-variable clauses and one fresh auxiliary per source triple, all source names retained. For a valid \(H\) with \(n\) occurring source variables, the compiled ordinary SAT input has

 n'=n+m,\qquad m'=6m,\qquad
 N'=(4m'+2)\left\lceil\log_2(64(n'+m'+1))\right\rceil

under the project's fixed-record bit convention. For every assignment \(a\) to source variables,
\[
 E(a)\iff\exists z:\text{the six corresponding proper 3CNF clauses hold}.

\section{Matched SAT/UNSAT controls and rejection of width-only costs}
When every source variable has degree exactly two, the clause-interaction graph is cubic; the source exact-one decision is precisely its perfect-matching decision. Two connected order-16 cubic graphs have \(m=16\) clause vertices, \(n=24\) original variables, \(24\) edges and cycle rank \(24-16+1=9\), but opposite SAT labels.

The SAT graph is the circular ladder on eight rungs (16 clause vertices), with a perfect matching. The UNSAT graph joins a central cubic vertex by one bridge to the subdivided edge of each of three separate \(K_4\) lobes. Each lobe after subdivision has five vertices and requires its bridge to be used in every perfect matching, since an unmatched external edge leaves an odd isolated lobe. The hub has degree three and can be matched across at most one bridge, contradiction. Hence UNSAT, despite the same degree, vertex count, edge count and cyclomatic number as the SAT graph. The 3-bridge example graph and its no-perfect-matching argument were \emph{already used by LSPV-1 CUBICCOVER75 in another source language}; this report transfers it to the distinct positive exact-one source and does not claim novelty of the graph.

Both formulae compile to ordinary proper 3CNF with \(40\) variables and \(96\) clauses, equal fixed-record bit length. A second matched source-degree-three SAT/UNSAT pair has six variables and six exact-one triples in each instance, and demonstrates that the elementary divisibility requirement \(m\equiv0\pmod 3\) is not a label test. The exact clauses and independently checked statuses are stored in the public repro and raw results.
