\section{Claim card and proposals before literature}
The universal separation target fixes one encoded NP-complete language and quantifies over \emph{every} total uniform deterministic exact decider $A$:
\[
\forall A\,\forall c>0\,\forall N_0\,\exists F_A:\quad
|F_A|\ge N_0\ \land\ T_A(F_A)>|F_A|^c.
\]
The $F_A$ may depend on $A$. No universal time lower bound has been proved.
We proposed three falsifiable mechanisms prior to literature inspection.

\noindent\textbf{M1 (PROVED/EXACT; standard decision-tree mechanism).}
Even unlimited learning, source rereads and switching cannot avoid querying a disjoint opposite-label input perturbation, with an error-robust $(1-2\epsilon)$ coefficient for randomized deciders. The parameter is defined \emph{independently} of runtime and of the solver's current method.

\noindent\textbf{M2 (PROVED/EXACT conditional on an earlier certified source).}
Role 4's genuine signed exact-$(2,2)$ two-sign SAT/UNSAT pair yields $p$ disjoint sensitive literal-word groups on a $p$-block SAT product. The resulting lower bound covers arbitrary adaptive deterministic and bounded-error randomized source-query deciders in the model below, but is at most input-linear.

\noindent\textbf{M3 (FALSIFIED/RETIRED as a universal transfer).}
The sensitive-word parameter must force superlinear computation or construction of the rich reverse-Circuit-SAT compatibility relation. A direct fixed-template recognizer gives a matching input-linear SAT decision algorithm; hence this implication is false on the exhibited family. The hypothesis that \emph{some other} parameter yields a global superpolynomial lower bound remains OPEN.

\section{Declared input machine and an independently defined parameter}
Fix a valid-input domain $D_N$ for one encoding and label $L:D_N\to\{0,1\}$. Input is read-only and partitioned into $M$ addressed words of width $w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil$. Any read reveals one addressed word and is charged one operation. An arbitrary randomized uniform algorithm may retain the source, revisit it, cache observations, construct DNNF or not, run SCC/Horn/affine elimination, branch, restart, learn clauses, replace representations, and perform any finite internal computation. Random coins are independent of the input and the algorithm has worst-case per-input error at most $\epsilon<1/2$ on all valid inputs. Each query and every other machine operation is paid; this theorem counts \emph{only} queries.

For $x,y\in D_N$, write
$\Delta_w(x,y)=\{j\in[M]:x_j\ne y_j\}$.
Define the \emph{analysis-only} opposite-label word-block sensitivity
\[
\beta_w(x)=\max\bigl\{t:
\exists y_1,\ldots,y_t\in D_N,\
L(y_i)\ne L(x),\
\Delta_w(x,y_i)\cap\Delta_w(x,y_j)=\varnothing\ (i\ne j)\bigr\}.
\]
It is defined by the language, source and packing, not by a decider's measured time, representation choice or eventual answer. Computing $\beta_w$ need not be inexpensive, and the solver is never handed its value or the true label.
\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT: adaptive error-robust source probes]
For every randomized adaptive exact-input decider $A$ with error at most $\epsilon<1/2$ on each legal input, and every legal source $x$,
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[Q_A(x)]\ge(1-2\epsilon)\beta_w(x).}
\tag{1}
\]
Here $Q_A$ counts source-word probes (repeated probes are charged, while the lower bound uses only first probes). For zero-error randomized or deterministic exact algorithms the coefficient is $1$. The same theorem applies to nonuniform fixed programs without input-dependent advice, but no conclusion is claimed for a free external SAT oracle or an operation returning arbitrarily many words.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take a maximum family $y_i$ whose nonempty word-difference supports $\Delta_i$ are disjoint. Couple the runs on $x$ and $y_i$ using exactly the same random coin sequence. Until the run on $x$ reads a word in $\Delta_i$, all observed words, random choices, internal states, learned constraints, cache contents, switches and outputs are identical on both inputs. Let $E_i$ be the event that the run on $x$ queries some word of $\Delta_i$. Since opposite labels require different correct outputs,
\[
1\le\Pr(E_i)+\Pr(A(x)\ne L(x))+
\Pr(A(y_i)\ne L(y_i))
\le\Pr(E_i)+2\epsilon.
\]
The $E_i$ require distinct word addresses because their supports are pairwise disjoint. Thus $Q_A(x)\ge\sum_i\mathbf1_{E_i}$ pointwise, giving (1) by linearity of expectation. No assumption about the solver's representation or fixed rule was used.
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Sharp ceiling.}
By disjointness $\beta_w(x)\le M\le N$; for this fixed-width encoding $M=2+4m=\Theta(N/\log N)$. Therefore a bound from (1), \emph{even with perfect algorithm coverage}, cannot be superlinear in the input-word count. In particular it cannot prove $P\ne NP$. Distinguish this from unrestricted physical time, which might exceed source probes by an unknown amount.

\section{Application to the certified signed exact-$(2,2)$ source}
Take Role~2's signed exact-$(2,2)$ proper unique $U$, with $198$ variables and $264$ ternary clauses. Its UNSAT certificate was reported in DOECKER-CORE-CERT61. Role~4's TWO-SIGN-SWAP64 reports a SAT source $V$ on \emph{identical ordered unsigned supports} obtained by swapping the polarities of $x_3$ in original clauses 3 and 13 (zero-based). The two altered literal words have distinct addresses; all original signed occurrence budgets remain two positive and two negative per variable. The SAT witness is imported and source-checked in the earlier Role~4 run. We do not claim an independent full UNSAT verification here.

For $p\ge1$ take disjoint source-renamed blocks $B_{p,b}$, choosing $V$ for orientation $1$, $U$ for orientation $0$. Then
\[
L(B_{p,b})=\bigwedge_{i=1}^p b_i,\quad
n_p=198p,\quad m_p=264p,\quad
w_p=\left\lceil\log_2(64(462p+1))\right\rceil,
\quad N_p=(1056p+2)w_p.
\]
Every $B_{p,b}$ is a legal signed exact-$(2,2)$ formula with the same number of encoded records. On $x=B_{p,1^p}$, the $p$ one-$U$ neighbors have disjoint two-word difference sets, proving $\beta_{w_p}(x)\ge p$.
\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT with imported U/V SAT labels]
For any uniformly correct total deterministic exact decider of the fixed full signed exact-$(2,2)$ language, $Q_A(B_{p,1^p})\ge p$. More generally every bounded-error randomized decider in the addressed-word model satisfies
\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\,Q_A(B_{p,1^p})\ge(1-2\epsilon)p
=\Omega_\epsilon(N_p/\log N_p).}
\tag{2}
\]
No source-independent superlinear or post-acquisition bound follows.
\end{theorem}
The reverse Circuit-SAT evaluation representation has $n_p+3m_p-1=990p-1$ NOT1/OR2/AND2 gates and $n_p+6m_p-2=1782p-2$ gate-input incidences under the stated shared-source basis. Backward output TRUE requires each clause OR to have some true literal with no contradictory shared-source choices. These counts describe \emph{evaluation}, not the size or runtime of a SAT-solving circuit. A one-bit decider need not materialize the independent-transversal relation or the output-gate backward paths.

\section{Tightness, adaptive ledger and hostile controls}
On the abstract $p$-field orientation promise $\{0,1\}^p$ with answer $\bigwedge b_i$, a randomized policy obtains a fair-enough random number and, with probability $\epsilon$, guesses FALSE immediately; with probability $\epsilon$, guesses TRUE immediately; otherwise it performs the \emph{exact} source scan, returning FALSE on the first zero and TRUE only after all $p$ fields. Its per-input error is exactly $\epsilon$ and on $1^p$ it makes exactly $(1-2\epsilon)p$ expected field queries. Thus the coefficient in (1) is sharp for this designated promise interface. The guessing phases are not exact deterministic SAT algorithms; they are valid for the declared bounded-error experiment. On full CNF encodings, sound template recognition and validation require additional charged reads and a generic exact DPLL fallback for unrecognized sources, giving an $O(p)$ upper bound rather than an exact $p$-query upper bound.

The event $E_i$ in the proof is unchanged by any costed switch between propagation, SCC, Horn, affine, DPLL, learnt-clause, restart, decomposition, caching or source replay; none produces an unread input word for free. This is the limited sense in which (1) covers all adaptive policies. After source acquisition, the rich representation can be bypassed by a linear recognizer. An all-decider \emph{time} lower bound beyond input acquisition remains OPEN.
