\section{Target, falsifiers and prior work}
The language is D\"ocker's NP-complete duplicate-free proper monotone 3-SAT-(2,2), each clause three distinct same-sign literals, every original variable exactly twice positive and twice negative. Invalid encodings must be rejected. Four falsifiers: (F1) $q>2$ dangerous negative clauses makes matching certification impossible (FALSE, $q=4,\tau=2$ below); (F2) dangerous-clause count instead of overlap is decisive (FALSE, transversal parameter); (F3) failed matching proves UNSAT (FALSE, a nine-variable source has 18 SAT models but zero safe positive matchings); (F4) a restricted matching terminal supplies hereditary arbitrary-$A$ ARC (FALSE, it generates no child and needs unproved FLMV model transport).
For each original positive clause create a vertex of $G_+(F)$; for original variable $x_i$ add edge $e_i$ joining the two distinct source positive clauses containing it. This is a loopless cubic multigraph. Original negative clause $D=(\neg x_a\vee\neg x_b\vee\neg x_c)$ is \emph{dangerous} if $e_a,e_b,e_c$ are pairwise vertex-disjoint. Let $\mathcal D$ be the indexed dangerous support triples and $q=|\mathcal D|$. Set $\tau=\min\{|Q|:Q\cap D\ne\varnothing,\ \forall D\in\mathcal D\}$, with $\tau=0$ for empty $\mathcal D$.
\begin{theorem}[Exact matching-witness semantics]
For every valid $F$ and every perfect matching $M$ in $G_+$, the coherent complete original assignment $x_i=[e_i\in M]$ satisfies $F$ iff $D\nsubseteq M$ for every $D\in\mathcal D$. Such a matching-derived SAT witness exists iff some choice of one label $f(D)\in D$ for every dangerous $D$ leaves a perfect matching in $G_+-\{f(D):D\in\mathcal D\}$. The latter can be tested by at most $3^q$ general-graph perfect-matching calls. Exhaustion means UNKNOWN, not UNSAT.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each original positive clause vertex is incident with precisely one matched edge and is satisfied. For a nondangerous negative clause two labelled edges share a positive vertex and cannot both lie in a matching, so its negative disjunction is satisfied. A dangerous negative clause is falsified precisely if its three edges all belong to $M$. If $M$ is safe select from each dangerous $D$ an edge absent from $M$; deleting the selected edges preserves $M$. Conversely any perfect matching after one deletion per dangerous $D$ avoids falsifying every original clause. Enumerate all three choices per original negative dangerous clause.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Bridgeless two-edge transversal SAT terminal]
If $G_+$ is bridgeless and $\tau\le2$, a deterministic polynomial procedure produces and source-verifies a satisfying assignment of $F$. The symmetric result applies to $G_-$ after global complement. Always $q\le2\tau$, with equality $q=4,\tau=2$ attainable in the canonical NP-complete target.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Find $Q$ of size at most two by testing at most $1+n+\binom n2$ subsets and all indexed dangerous clauses; charge failures. In a bridgeless loopless cubic multigraph the vector $z_e=1/3$ satisfies the degree-one and odd-cut inequalities of Edmonds' perfect-matching polytope, because every odd cut has at least three edges. Thus it is a convex combination of perfect matchings. For random $M$ from this combination $\mathbb E|M\cap Q|=|Q|/3<1$, so some perfect matching avoids all of $Q$. Compute one by polynomial-time matching in $G_+-Q$. The previous theorem then certifies every original clause. Each variable occurs in exactly two negative source clauses, so $\tau$ selected edges hit at most $2\tau$ dangerous clauses. The $q=4$ example below reaches this limit.
\end{proof}
