\section{Scope, hypotheses, and recovered project state}
The question is whether every exact SAT decider must construct a rich backward relation or pay comparably for some other symbolic method. We do not assume that premise. Three falsifiable attempts: (A) test a connected, signed exact-(2,2) counterexample with exponential cut rank and easy SAT; (B) formalize an amortized rank-one static-service lower bound and challenge its transport to unrestricted decision; (C) seek a genuine polynomial algorithm for a recognizable balanced subclass, then test that its grammar excludes the strict monotone NP-complete endpoint.

\section{Precise representation, backward construction, and counts}
Fix proper signed exact-(2,2) CNF with distinct clauses of exactly three distinct literals. Each variable appears exactly twice positively and twice negatively, so $4n=3m$. Let $N$ be encoded bits, $n$ source variables, $m$ clauses. Use two header words and four words per clause with $w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil$ bits each, giving $N=(2+4m)w$. Uniform deterministic random-access word-RAM cost charges reads, parsing, matching discovery, rejection attempts, child construction, replay, output, and peak space. The full language is NP-complete even with sign-monochromatic clauses by D\"ocker's result. This is KNOWN THEOREM, not a decision-time lower bound.
\par\noindent Construct the ordinary forward evaluation circuit with $n$ shared NOT gates, $2m$ two-input OR gates, and $m-1$ two-input AND gates. It has $n+3m-1=5n-1$ evaluation gates and $n+6m-2=9n-2$ gate-input arcs. This is a declared NOT/OR/AND basis; no SAT-solving circuit or transistor lower bound is implied. Backward propagation from output TRUE requires one satisfied input of each clause's OR and all children of each AND. Choosing one literal occurrence per clause without choosing both polarities of any variable is equivalent to SAT. The opposite-polarity conflict graph is a disjoint union of $K_{2,2}$ blocks; clause occurrence triples partition its $4n=3m$ vertices. This is a compact exact reformulation, not mandatory work for a decider.
\section{Connected high-rank family}
For $g\ge1$, put $z_{g+1}=z_1$ and
\[
H_g=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{g}\left[
(x_i\vee\neg y_i\vee z_i)
\land(x_i\vee\neg y_i\vee\neg z_i)
\land(\neg x_i\vee y_i\vee z_{i+1})
\land(\neg x_i\vee y_i\vee\neg z_{i+1})\right].
\]
\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT, independently red-team review pending]
The source $H_g$ is proper signed exact-(2,2) with $n=3g$, $m=4g$, connected incidence graph and unique clauses. It has exactly $2^{2g}$ models and an $O(g\log g)$-word canonical-source decision, but its cut matrix $M_g[x,(y,z)]=[H_g(x,y,z)]$ has rank $2^g$ over every field.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both $x_i$ and $y_i$ occur twice under each polarity. The cyclic $z_i$ appears once under each polarity in block $i$'s first pair and once under each polarity in block $i-1$'s second pair. It connects neighboring blocks in the incidence graph. All clauses have three distinct variables. The propositional identity
$(A\vee u)\land(A\vee\neg u)\equiv A$
reduces $H_g$ to
$\bigwedge_i(x_i\vee\neg y_i)\land(\neg x_i\vee y_i)$, namely $x_i=y_i$ with all $z_i$ free. The model count follows. Every row of $M_g$ indexed by $x$ is nonzero exactly on columns with $y=x$. These $2^g$ supports are disjoint, so the rows are linearly independent over any field. A charged source recognizer can check the paired clauses then use linear-time 2-SAT SCC; for a canonical ordering, sorting costs $O(g\log g)$. In particular, all-zero is a source-verifiable witness.
\end{proof}
For the independently fixed unit/pure/subsumption normalization, $d=0$, $r=n-d=3g$, and $r/\log N\to\infty$. Neither $r$ nor $\operatorname{rank}M_g$ forces exponential SAT decision on this family.
\section{Exact restricted amortization, and countertheorem}
\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT, explicit static rectangle service]
Suppose a source-closed service represents the whole cut matrix as an explicitly emitted sum $M_g=\sum_{j=1}^{s}R_j$ with $\operatorname{rank}R_j\le1$. Then $s\ge2^g$. For emitted partial sums $P_t=\sum_{j\le t}R_j$, the independently defined potential $\Phi_t=2^g-\operatorname{rank}P_t$ decreases by at most one per charged insertion. A reduced OBDD with all $x$ variables ordered before any $y,z$ variable has width at least $2^g$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Rank subadditivity gives $2^g=\operatorname{rank}M_g\le\sum_j\operatorname{rank}R_j\le s$. A rank-one update increases rank by at most one. The $2^g$ assignments to the $x$ prefix induce pairwise different nonempty residual functions $[y=x]$, requiring distinct OBDD states.
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED universal transfer.} Neither explicit rank-one summation nor that OBDD variable order is mandatory in SAT decision. Interleaving $x_i,y_i$ gives a linear-size OBDD, the original formula already is a small circuit, and all-zero is a direct witness. A total exact uniform algorithm for the entire NP-complete language can check this recognizer (or the matching island below) and use exhaustive SAT search otherwise. It runs in polynomial time on $H_g$. Thus high cut rank does not imply a per-input lower bound for every exact decider. It says nothing about worst-case hard inputs elsewhere.
\section{Positive polynomial recognition and strict-promise firewall}
On any proper signed exact-(2,2) input, construct the graph of clauses, connecting two if they have the same two signed literals and opposite signs on the third literal. If it has a perfect matching, replace each matched pair
$(a\vee b\vee u)\land(a\vee b\vee\neg u)$ by the binary clause $(a\vee b)$. All replacements preserve the complete solution relation, with no new variables. A general-graph perfect matching algorithm plus SCC yields an exact uniform decision procedure on this recognizable sublanguage in $O(m^3+N/w)$ word time with polynomial peak space. Discovery, unsuccessful matching choices, all input reads and witness replay are charged. If no matching exists, the full-language total decider can fall back to exhaustive search.
\par\noindent\textbf{Limit.} A sign-monochromatic 3-CNF has no eligible complementary pair: changing one literal's sign creates a mixed-sign clause. Thus this matching algorithm does not settle the known NP-complete strict monotone exact-(2,2) sublanguage. A separately constructed signed six-variable, eight-clause balanced example with all distinct clause supports also has no eligible pairs.
\section{Compact intensional algebra and charged extraction}
Let $\mathcal S_n$ be the semiring of sets of consistent partial assignments, with union as addition and consistent merge as multiplication. A literal maps to its singleton partial assignment. The unexpanded straight-line program
$\mathcal P_F=\prod_{C\in F}(\sum_{\ell\in C}\{\ell\})$
uses $2m$ additions and $m-1$ multiplications for proper 3-CNF. Its value is nonempty iff there is a consistent independent clause-choice transversal, iff SAT. Its small \emph{syntax} does not imply polynomial-time evaluation of semiring elements or zero testing.
\par\noindent Given an arbitrary total exact decider $A$ for the fixed NP-complete balanced language, ordinary decision self-reduction plus a uniform polynomial-time many-one reduction back to the language recovers one witness for a satisfiable $n$-variable CNF using at most $n+1$ charged calls to $A$, polynomial reduction time and final source verification. The restricted children may leave the balanced grammar before re-encoding. This is a many-call \emph{upper bound}, not a rich state extracted from one run. Sizes, calls, failed attempts and replay are not free.
