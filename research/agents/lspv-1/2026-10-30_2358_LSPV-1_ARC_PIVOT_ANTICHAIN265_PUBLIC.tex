\section{Exact original-source theorem}
For coherent partial source cube $\rho$ leaving $x$ unassigned, consider source clauses $C_j=x\vee A_j$, $C_k=\neg x\vee B_k$. Their nontautological resolvent is $R_{jk}=A_j\vee B_k$. Define $\rho_R$ as the partial cube falsifying every literal of $R$, and $\mathcal R_x$ as the distinct nontautological original-clause resolvents for pivot $x$.
\begin{theorem}
The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[] (a) Under $\rho$, two original source clauses immediately reduce to units $x$ and $\neg x$, before derived unit propagation.
\item[] (b) Some $R\in\mathcal R_x$ has $\rho_R\subseteq\rho$.
\item[] (c) All nonpivot literals of some opposite-pivot-sign original-clause pair are false in $\rho$.
\end{enumerate}
Each such cube is UNSAT under $F$ and is excluded by a one-resolution-step proof using the two indexed source clauses. The inclusion-minimal rejected cubes for this one-step fixed-pivot system are precisely $\{\rho_R:R\in\min_{\subseteq}\mathcal R_x\}$, duplicates discarded. For proper original 3-CNF, these blockers fix at most four variables.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An original $x\vee A_j$ reduces to $x$ iff all $A_j$ literals are false under $\rho$, and analogously for $\neg x\vee B_k$. Coherence prevents simultaneous falsification if the resolvent is tautological; otherwise the conjunction of falsified nonpivot literals is exactly $\rho_R\subseteq\rho$. The two source clauses resolve to $R$, giving $F\models R$ and, if the cube is further restricted, $F\models B_\rho$. Cube-inclusion is reversed by resolvent-literal inclusion; removing subsumed and duplicate resolvents characterizes the minimal rejected cubes. Each pair of 3-clauses contributes at most four nonpivot literals.
\end{proof}
The converse applies ONLY to immediate original-source opposing units, not arbitrary semantic UNSAT or deeper resolution. The four-mode SS/SU/US/UU compatibility matrix tests local ALL-literals-true/false targets, not ordinary disjunctive SAT. Assignment complementation obeys $t_j(\bar a)=3-t_j(a)$ for proper ternary clauses.
