\section*{Exact grammar and quantifiers}
Fix a strict computable canonical binary signed-CNF parser; assign SAT-label zero to malformed strings. Let $\mathcal T$ be the decidably presented class of explicitly polynomial-clocked total transducers $T_P$ parameterized by a clocked polynomial-time Boolean predicate $P$. They copy every original clause verbatim, then append nothing or the two fresh opposite unit clauses $(z_F),(\neg z_F)$ using the first unused variable identifier. On malformed input they output the original malformed string. All source reads, predicate execution, memory, fresh-index discovery and output bytes are charged. Formally,
\[
 T_P(F)=\begin{cases}F\wedge(z_F)\wedge(\neg z_F)&
 \text{valid $F$ and $P(F)=1$},\\
 F&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
\]
Let $\mathrm{GOOD}=\{\langle P\rangle\in\mathcal T:
\forall x\in\{0,1\}^{*}\;[\mathrm{SAT}(T_P(x))=\mathrm{SAT}(x)]\}$.

\begin{theorem}[SOURCE-RETAINING-SAT-PRESERVATION403]
$\mathrm{GOOD}$ is $\Pi^0_1$-complete under computable many-one reductions, even with the stated copied-source and two-fresh-unit restrictions. In particular no total computable algorithm determines whether every valid instance of an arbitrary such polynomial-time transducer preserves SAT and UNSAT.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The complement of GOOD is recursively enumerable: enumerate every binary input $x$, execute the supplied clocked transducer, parse source and output, and determine their finite SAT labels by exhaustive assignments. Halt upon a mismatch. Thus GOOD is $\Pi^0_1$.

For $\Pi^0_1$ hardness reduce NONHALT. Given any Turing machine $M$ and word $w$, fix the canonical satisfiable formula
\[
U_n=(x_1)\wedge\cdots\wedge(x_n),\qquad n\ge1.
\]
Its strict signed binary-index encoding has length $\Theta(n\log(n+2))$. Define a single uniform clocked predicate $P_{M,w}$: accept exactly such a byte-exact $U_n$ if a universal simulation shows $M(w)$ halts within $n$ steps; reject otherwise. Since $n\le |U_n|$ and $M,w$ are fixed in the generated program, a standard bounded universal simulation has polynomial running time in the source length. Syntactic recognition and malformed-input rejection are polynomial too.

If $M(w)$ never halts, then $P_{M,w}$ is false on every input and $T_{P_{M,w}}$ is the identity: it belongs to GOOD. If $M(w)$ halts after $t$ steps, choose any $n\ge\max\{t,1\}$. The satisfiable $U_n$ maps to
\[
U_n\wedge(x_{n+1})\wedge(\neg x_{n+1}),
\]
which is UNSAT. Hence $T_{P_{M,w}}\notin\mathrm{GOOD}$. The reduction from NONHALT is computable and uses one fixed all-length transducer per $(M,w)$, not an input-tailored or per-$N$ oracle. Therefore GOOD is $\Pi^0_1$-complete.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One-sided retention is not source retraction]
For every $P$ and every UNSAT source $F$, the appended-clause output $T_P(F)$ remains UNSAT. The reverse implication, from UNSAT of the output back to UNSAT of the original input, is false for some $P$. Consequently copying source clauses is not proof of a sound retraction of output refutations.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Conjoining clauses cannot create a satisfying assignment. But any activated $P_{M,w}$ maps a satisfiable $U_n$ to an explicitly contradictory output. Its source clauses were copied in full and still do not justify the new contradictions.
\end{proof}
