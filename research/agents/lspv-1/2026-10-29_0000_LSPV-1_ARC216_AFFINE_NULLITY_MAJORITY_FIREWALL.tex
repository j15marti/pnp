\section{BUILD I: exact affine-nullity algorithm}
Let $F=C(X,Z)\wedge A(Y,Z)$ be an explicitly encoded mixed 2CNF/affine instance, with pairwise disjoint sets $X,Y,Z$ and $|Z|=b$. No answer bit is supplied. Parse all occurrences and use the known exact polynomial-time componentwise projection:
\[
 C_Z(z)\equiv\exists x\,C(x,z),\qquad A_Z(z)\equiv\exists y\,A(y,z).
\]
Each private 2CNF variable is removed by width-at-most-two Davis--Putnam resolvents with deduplication; the affine subsystem uses ordinary row elimination over the private columns. At most $O(b^2)$ distinct binary clauses and at most $b$ independent affine rows remain. The explicitly serialized child has $O(1+b^2\log(b+2))$ physical bits, not necessarily smaller than its parent. The proof is pointwise, including contradictory empty clauses/rows.

\begin{theorem}[Exact affine-nullity parameterization]
There exists one fixed uniform deterministic mixed-SAT decider $D$ that, for every well-formed $F=(C,A)$ of physical bit length $N$, first constructs the exact projection on $Z$, rejects immediately if $A_Z$ is inconsistent, and otherwise computes $r=b-\mathrm{rank}(A_Z)$. It decides both satisfiable and unsatisfiable cases in time
\[
 T_{D,\mathcal M}(N)=N^{O(1)}+2^r N^{O(1)}
\]
and polynomial peak workspace, on each fixed standard deterministic machine model $\mathcal M$ after model-native implementation. It uses one program, one input, no advice, no separate SAT/UNSAT solver and no uncharged oracle. Consequently the promise class $r=O(\log N)$ is uniformly polynomial-time decidable, without requiring $b=O(\log N)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For consistent $A_Z$, deterministic Gaussian row reduction returns a particular solution $z_0\in\mathbb F_2^b$ and independent kernel vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_r$. Its complete solution set is precisely
\[
 \bigl\{z_0+\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_i v_i:\alpha\in\mathbb F_2^r\bigr\}.
\]
Enumerate every $\alpha$, construct its explicit $b$-bit shared assignment, and evaluate every clause of $C_Z$. Accept when one passes and reject after exhausting all $2^r$ candidates otherwise. Exact projection and affine solving prove
\[
 D(F)=1\iff\exists z\,[C_Z(z)\wedge A_Z(z)]\iff\exists x,y,z\,[C(x,z)\wedge A(y,z)].
\]
Input parsing, private-variable discovery, pair-generation even for unsuccessful resolvents, row operations, candidate enumeration, indexed/pointer accesses, source rereads, memory traffic and final YES/NO output are implemented by ordinary deterministic operations. Deduplicated 2CNF has polynomially many clauses, affine rows have polynomially many bits, and the algorithm streams candidate assignments rather than storing $2^r$ copies. Thus each native model has a fixed polynomial preprocessing and per-candidate bound and a polynomial simultaneous footprint. No time exponent from one model is asserted for another.\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Large shared interface with small nullity]
For arbitrarily large $b$ and every $0\le r<b$, there is an explicit $F_{b,r}$ on $b+2r$ variables, with exactly $b$ variables shared between its languages, whose affine projection has nullity $r$. It is UNSAT and the enumerator tests exactly $2^r$ candidates. The construction is simple and not intended as a lower bound for other algorithms.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $i\le b-r$, include the 2CNF unit $\neg z_i$ and affine unit $z_i=0$, except force $z_1=1$ for an explicit contradiction. For each remaining $z_i$, include private 2CNF auxiliary $u_i$ with $(z_i\vee u_i)$ and private affine auxiliary $v_i$ with equation $z_i\oplus v_i=0$. Every $z_i$ appears in both input languages. Eliminating $u_i,v_i$ erases their respective constraints, leaving $r$ free $z$ bits, while the contradictory first coordinate makes every candidate fail. All construction and enumeration costs are explicit.
\end{proof}

\section{BUILD II: majority-closure falsifier}
\begin{lemma}[2CNF relations are majority closed]
If three Boolean assignments satisfy a 2CNF, their coordinatewise majority also satisfies it. Every relation obtained by existentially projecting private variables from a 2CNF is majority closed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If the majority falsified a clause of width two, each of its two literals would be false in at least two of the three assignments. Those two sets of cardinality at least two intersect, so one original assignment would falsify both literals, a contradiction. The unit and empty cases are simpler. For existential projections, lift each of three satisfying visible assignments to a full satisfying assignment and take their coordinatewise majority; the visible coordinates are the desired majority.\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Gaussian substitution does not preserve 2CNF]
The relation
\[
 f(u,v,w)=(u\oplus v)\lor w
\]
is not definable by any 2CNF on $(u,v,w)$, even with existentially quantified private 2CNF auxiliaries. It results from the 2CNF clause $(z\vee w)$ and the affine row $z\oplus u\oplus v=0$ after eliminating $z$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Assignments $(0,1,0),(1,0,0),(0,0,1)$ satisfy $f$. Their coordinatewise majority is $(0,0,0)$, which falsifies $f$. The preceding lemma excludes any 2CNF existential encoding. The original $(z\vee w)$ is 2CNF and $z\oplus u\oplus v=0$ is affine, so the exact substitution yields the asserted relation.\end{proof}

The counterexample concerns language closure, not the possibility of keeping a separate affine gate or introducing a richer mixed representation. The latter is precisely what must be charged in a proposed adaptive compiler.
