\section*{Exact cost-reflecting splice theorem}
A \emph{certified clause-addition stage} takes a valid CNF $F_{i-1}$ to $F_i=F_{i-1}\wedge G_i$, together with a finite shared ordinary Resolution DAG $D_i$ deriving each added clause of $G_i$ later used as a premise from the clauses of $F_{i-1}$. Charge $d_i$ for distinct derived nodes of $D_i$; the old clauses remain free leaves. All inference pivots, clause identities, and source links must be valid. A terminal shared Resolution refutation $\pi_t$ of $F_t$ has $q$ paid nodes.

\begin{theorem}[All-input, all-certified-chain additive transfer]
For every valid CNF $F_0$, every finite $t\ge0$, every certified chain $(F_i,D_i)$, and every terminal shared Resolution refutation $\pi_t$,
\[
\boxed{W_{\rm Res}(F_0)\le q+\sum_{i=1}^t d_i.}
\]
The bound counts distinct inference nodes once and allows temporary enlargement, adaptive choices, arbitrary intermediate proof length and shared premises.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take one copy of each certificate DAG and the terminal proof, renaming node identifiers to disjoint namespaces. Replace every added source-leaf reference in $\pi_t$ by the corresponding derived clause node in $D_t$, and recursively replace earlier added-leaf references by nodes in $D_{t-1},\ldots,D_1$. After expansion all free leaves belong to the original source $F_0$. Each retained inference still has the same two clauses and pivot; chronological stage order ensures an acyclic union and a topological sort. Shared derived clauses are represented once and referenced arbitrarily many times. Pruning unused nodes reduces cost. There are at most $q+\sum d_i$ distinct paid nodes, and the terminal empty clause is unchanged.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Exact Haken restricted transfer]
For any such source-addition certificates above the canonical $P_h$ and every final pure-Resolution refutation,
\[
\boxed{q+\sum_i d_i\ge 2^{ch}-m_h=2^{\Omega(h)}}
\]
for all sufficiently large $h$. This lower-bounds the \emph{combined certified conversion plus final Resolution proof}, not $q$ alone. An uncertified semantic encoder lies outside its hypotheses.
\end{corollary}

\section*{Tight finite case and source controls}

Two nodes attain the minimum: derive $(y)$ from the first two clauses, then resolve with $(\neg y)$ to obtain the empty clause. Certify the redundant $(y)$ by one node and add it to $F_1$; the final refutation of $F_1$ costs one node. Splicing gives the exact two-node optimum of $F_0$. \section*{Resource and bridge accounting}
For pure Resolution, every derived clause uses variable names occurring in the source, so each clause has polynomial encoded length in the original input length $N$. A $Q=q+\sum d_i$ node merged DAG has a polynomial-length serialization and polynomial-time verification in $N+Q$, including source links and pivots, provided the actual output is produced and checked. When $Q$ is exponential, evidence materialization and validation are also potentially exponential, not forbidden. Node count is not machine runtime; proof-search, source reads, index/head movement, failed choices, output and simultaneous memory remain separately charged.
