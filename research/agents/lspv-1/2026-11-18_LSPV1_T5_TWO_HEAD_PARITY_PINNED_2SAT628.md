## Model and two-head parity theorem
Domain Z5. R(a,b)=1 iff (b-a) mod 5 belongs to {1,3}. R(a,a)=0. Q(h,u,v)=R(h,u) XOR R(h,v). Every clause has three distinct variable indices, but colors may tie. The head count k is the number of distinct FIRST-position variable indices, not the number of clauses.

**Theorem 1 (PROVED/EXACT).** For every unpinned Q formula with k<=2, SAT(F) iff each head-specific follower graph G_h, having one undirected edge {u,v} per clause Q(h,u,v), is bipartite. Both labels and a complete SAT witness are computed in O(n+m) word operations and O(n+m) words, including parsing and original-clause verification; bit storage is O((n+m)log(n+m)). Any translation to a different physical model must charge indexed access and pointer overhead.

**Proof.** Let p_h(v)=R(color(h),color(v)); each Q clause requires p_h(u) XOR p_h(v)=1, implying bipartiteness. For sufficiency assign colors 0 and 2 to the two heads. The complete signature table is

    tail color       0   1   2   3   4
    (R(0,c),R(2,c)) 01  10  00  11  00

and therefore covers ALL four Boolean pairs. Orient the bipartition bit of the component of G_0 containing the other head color 2 to require R(0,2)=0; orient the independent component of G_2 containing the first head color 0 to require R(2,0)=1. All other components can use either phase. Own-head tails cannot occur under the distinct-name grammar. Any nonhead vertex's at most two required comparison bits are simultaneously realizable by the signature table; choose an explicit corresponding tail color. This gives an original-label model, satisfying every triple. For one head, color it 0, choose colors 1 and 0 as witnesses to comparison bits 1 and 0; no heads is vacuous. BFS, reconstruction, input scans and output verification are linear. QED.

The head-count boundary is SHARP for parity sufficiency: the five-clause Q(a,b,c), Q(a,b,d), Q(b,a,c), Q(b,c,d), Q(c,a,d) has three heads, passes all head-parity checks, yet is UNSAT (independently verified from the supplied archive). ## Pinned child theorem (real global compatibility)
**Theorem 2 (PROVED/EXACT).** For k<=2 and any subset of variables pinned to absolute letters, SAT/UNSAT and a complete pin-respecting witness are obtainable in O(n+m) word time, O(n+m) word space, with fixed constants independent of n,m, by a sound exact 2-SAT compilation.

**Proof.** Enumerate all <=5^k<=25 head-letter assignments compatible with pins (no free rotation normalization once absolute pins are present). First detect an odd follower graph. For every bipartite follower component C of every head h, fix BFS offsets delta_(h,v) and introduce one Boolean phase t_(h,C) so that each required comparison equals t_(h,C) XOR delta_(h,v). For a pinned follower v, its literal color supplies the exact unit requirement R(color(h),pin_v). For each unpinned nonhead v appearing as follower under r<=2 heads, compute S_v={ (R(color(h_1),a),...,R(color(h_r),a)):a in Z5 }. Every Boolean tuple NOT in S_v is excluded by a clause of width r<=2 on these phase variables; at most four such exclusions per vertex. The entire phase formula is 2-CNF. Solve by SCC [1]. Any satisfying phase model lifts independently at every unpinned vertex to a genuine five-letter color with the required signature; verify source triples and all pins. Conversely every original model induces one enumerated head assignment and phase model. Thus no false SAT/UNSAT decisions occur. Every step and all at most 25 head cases cost O(n+m). QED.

Pins make parity alone incomplete. For Q(h,u,v), distinct indices h,u,v with pin colors h=4,u=1,v=4, both comparisons are non-wins, so UNSAT despite a bipartite one-edge follower graph. The phase 2-CNF correctly rejects this pinning.
