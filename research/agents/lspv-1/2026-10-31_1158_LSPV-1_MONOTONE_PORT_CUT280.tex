\section{The exact canonical two-port theorem}
Let F have a connected original primal graph, n variables, m=4n/3 original clauses. Let B be a nonempty proper source-variable boundary, b=|B|. Let P_1,...,P_k be the nonempty private components of G(F)-B. For each P_i, let v_i=|P_i|, m_i^+ and m_i^- be counts of positive and negative source clauses touching P_i, and e_i^+ and e_i^- the positive and negative boundary \emph{occurrences} in those same source clauses. Let h denote all-boundary original clauses.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical two-port and size obstruction]
Each component satisfies
\[
3m_i^+=2v_i+e_i^+,\quad
3m_i^-=2v_i+e_i^-,\quad
e_i^+\equiv e_i^-\pmod3,\quad e_i^++e_i^-\ge2.
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{2k\le 4b-3h,\qquad k\le2b.}
\]
For b=1 and an articulation boundary variable, precisely two private components exist, each receiving exactly one positive and one negative boundary occurrence.

At most 4b original clauses touch B. Every coherent boundary assignment leaves at least 4n/3-4b \emph{unchanged} source clauses, and one component retains at least
\[
\boxed{\frac{2n}{3b}-2}
\]
such clauses. On an explicit indexed clause-list source of N=Theta(n log n) bits, any child emitting the unchanged source clauses has length Omega(N/(b log N)). A logarithmic boundary cannot yield power-square-root children for \emph{every} connected canonical source by this raw emitter. To require O(N^lambda) bits per child, lambda<1, this method demands b=Omega(N^(1-lambda)/log N); enumerating all 2^b boundary assignments costs superpolynomial time.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each private name has two original occurrences of each sign, and every original clause is uniformly signed with three positions. Counting all positions of clauses touching P_i gives the two equalities and the mod-three relation. Since the original primal graph is connected, every nonempty P_i shares at least one source clause with B. Equal nonnegative residues modulo three cannot sum to one, so at least two boundary occurrences touch each private component. There are 4b boundary occurrences total and 3h appear in all-boundary clauses. No other boundary occurrence touches more than one private component. Sum and divide by two. When b=1 the sum is four and each component consumes two; the congruence gives the sign distribution. At most four original clauses per boundary name are touched, and every untouched original clause is wholly contained in one private component and survives all boundary assignments. Pigeonhole over k<=2b gives the clause count. An explicit encoding must retain each of these clauses, yielding the string-length bound. The entire result is conditional on original-clause-retaining child emission, not arbitrary semantic compression or correct terminal policies.
\end{proof}
