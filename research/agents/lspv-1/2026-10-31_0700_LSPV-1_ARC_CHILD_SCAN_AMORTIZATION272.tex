\section{Falsifiers and mathematical result}
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{F1 (tested and rejected within the stated architecture).} A hereditary compiler can generate $\Omega(N^{3/5})$ children and independently cold-scan all $N$ source characters before each child while still satisfying (1) at every iteration, regardless of unknown starting $K$.
\item[] \textbf{F2 (countercontrol).} The same impossibility applies if a streaming parser shares a single source scan across children, or caches a reusable indexed summary. It does \emph{not}: the $qN$ lower bound requires actual repeated reads, not mere logical dependence on all source bits.
\item[] \textbf{F3 (tested).} Independent source-cofactor children may be joined by \emph{AND} for SAT or \emph{OR} for UNSAT. False: correct total cofactors obey OR for SAT and AND for UNSAT.
\item[] \textbf{F4 (tested).} A decrease in DPLL nodes alone pays for scan/re-encode cost. The instrumented controller has more selected total events even where nodes are close.
\end{description}

\begin{theorem}[Hereditary cold-source-rescan gate]\label{thm:scan}
Fix $0<\lambda<1$, $\gamma>0$ and $\alpha<1+\gamma$, with
\[
K_*=\max\{\alpha,\gamma/(1-\lambda)\}<1/\lambda.
\tag{2}
\]
Suppose a fixed uniform successor $\Phi$ satisfies, for every correct polynomial-time SAT decider $A$ and every iteration, a total-machine time-exponent bound
\[
K(\Phi(A))\le\max\{\alpha,\gamma+\lambda K(A)\}.
\tag{3}
\]
Suppose additionally that at every successor level and on infinitely many input lengths $N$, some source forces the actual machine to create at least $cN^\gamma$ ordinary child attempts, each of which \emph{really executes} at least $c'N$ original-input read transitions in addition to any retained cached information, for constants $c,c'>0$ independent of $N$. Then these assumptions are inconsistent. The contradiction is internal to this \emph{repeated cold-scan architecture}, not a hardness statement for SAT.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each scanned original-source bit consumes at least one machine transition. On the corresponding worst-case sources, the total realized time is therefore at least $cc'N^{1+\gamma}$, regardless of child labels, policy efficiency, memoization or the cost of the guest, so the successor's worst-case exponent is at least $1+\gamma$. By (2), the max-affine map $f(K)=\max\{\alpha,\gamma+\lambda K\}$ has fixed point $K_*$ strictly less than $1/\lambda$. Iterating (3) from any finite starting $K_0$ gives
\[
K_j\le K_*+\lambda^j\max\{0,K_0-K_*\}.
\tag{4}
\]
For some finite $j$, $K_j<1/\lambda$. Then
\[
K_{j+1}\le\max\{\alpha,\gamma+\lambda K_j\}<1+\gamma,
\tag{5}
\]
where the final inequality uses $\alpha<1+\gamma$ and $\lambda K_j<1$. Yet the stipulated cold-source reads for $\Phi(A_j)$ enforce $K_{j+1}\ge1+\gamma$, contradiction. The argument uses an infinite sequence of lengths for the same successor, so polylogarithmic and $N^{o(1)}$ corrections cannot remove a strict exponent gap. No assumption about the output SAT/UNSAT labels was used.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Instantiated ARC coefficients]
At $(\alpha,\gamma,\lambda)=(11/10,3/5,1/2)$, the asserted time recurrence converges to $6/5$. At any level where the parent upper exponent satisfies $K<2$, the successor upper exponent is strictly below $8/5$, whereas $\Omega(N^{3/5})$ independent full source scans force at least $N^{8/5}$ transitions. The checked rational recurrence exhibits strict collisions after parent levels $1,2,4,5,6,7$ when the initial bounds are $K_0=2,4,8,16,32,100$, respectively.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[What the theorem does not prove]
A legal compiler may have fewer children; share one source scan; index, memoize or replay source facts at a charged cost; use source-aware recognition; terminate with checked SAT/UNSAT; preempt a guest; or choose non-cold policies. Such algorithms do \emph{not} satisfy the theorem's extra per-child-scan hypothesis. The theorem cannot be transferred to arbitrary polynomial-time SAT deciders. It also does not establish that any all-input power-short child system exists.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact ordinary-child polarity and adversarial original-clause controls}
For a complete binary partition on $b$ variables, each source cube $\rho\in\{0,1\}^{b}$ gives the materialized ordinary child $F|_\rho$ (write header, clauses and terminators). Pointwise
\[
F(a)=\bigvee_{\rho\in\{0,1\}^{b}}\bigl([a\succeq\rho]\wedge (F|_\rho)(a)\bigr),
\qquad
\SAT(F)=\bigvee_\rho\SAT(F|_\rho),
\quad
\neg\SAT(F)=\bigwedge_\rho\neg\SAT(F|_\rho).
\tag{6}
\]
The right side is true for UNSAT only when \emph{all} children are UNSAT. A child-falsifying assignment or failed complete endpoints are not UNSAT certificates for the source.

Each original signed occurrence $x_{i,j}$ in $C_j$ is the \emph{same} source variable $x_i$. Its local $S_j$ target makes the literal TRUE; its inverse gives its $U_j$ target. Independent local clause targets are never merged into an illegal global assignment. For coherent full $a$, $t_j(\bar a)=|C_j|-t_j(a)$; for proper ternary clauses this is $3-t_j(a)$. The signed-clause compatibility matrix for distinct $C_i,C_j$ satisfies: $SS,UU$ iff no shared opposite-polarity variable, and $SU,US$ iff no shared same-polarity variable. The full test re-evaluates all four modes by coherent exhaustive assignments, records $0^n$, $1^n$, every single-flip forward/backward trail and the four true-literal count bins. All source-derived pivot resolvents $(x\vee A),(\neg x\vee B)\Rightarrow A\vee B$ are independently checked as prohibited cubes. Horn, dual-Horn, 2-SAT, affine, renaming, dense clauses and the mixed-sign zero-extreme-compatible pair are retained as adverse controls. None is treated as a lower bound.

\section{Literature and handoff}
The actual source machine is Fortnow, Lipton, van Melkebeek and Viglas's indexed-input random-access Turing model, with a read-only original input, write-only one-way output and charged work/index tapes~\cite{FLMV}. Section 2.1 explicitly describes the indexed tape-erasure semantics and a possible sparse-tape polylog simulation; the cold-scan gate is a lower bound solely on \emph{executed input-read transitions}, robust to such re-encodings.
