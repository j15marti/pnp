\section{Quantitative forest theorem}
Call two original clause IDs $j,h$ \emph{$UU$ compatible} if their all-false source cubes $E_j,E_h$ intersect, and let $G_U(F)$ be the graph of compatible pairs, the complement of the ordinary conflict graph.
\begin{theorem}[Source-certified, constructive; restricted]
If $F$ is proper uniform width-$k$ CNF on $n$ original variables with exactly $m=2^k$ original clauses, and $G_U(F)$ has no isolated vertices, then
\[
\boxed{\#\operatorname{SAT}(F)\ge 2^{n-k-1}.}
\]
This class is recognizable in polynomial time by original signed occurrence comparisons, and a uniform deterministic algorithm constructs and verifies a full SAT assignment in polynomial time. In particular, every proper strict signed exact-$(2+,2-)$ 3CNF with $n=6$, $m=8$ has at least four models. This numerical bound is sharp.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any spanning forest $T$ of the original clause indices, Hunter's known event-union inequality gives
\[
\Pr[F(a)=1]\ge B_T :=
1-\sum_j\Pr(E_j)+\sum_{jh\in E(T)}\Pr(E_j\cap E_h).
\tag{1}
\]
The proof orders each tree parent before its children; every new union increment is at most $\Pr(E_j)-\Pr(E_j\cap E_{\rm parent})$. The statement also holds after any coherent partial assignment $\rho$. A graph with $m$ nonisolated vertices has at most $m/2$ components, so its spanning forest has at least $m/2$ edges. Each all-false clause cube has probability $2^{-k}$, and each $UU$-compatible pair pins at most $2k$ distinct variables, hence intersects with probability at least $2^{-2k}$. As $m=2^k$,
\[
 B_T\ge 1-m2^{-k}+(m/2)2^{-2k}=2^{-k-1}.
\]
Multiply by $2^n$. For construction, compute $B_T(\rho)$ exactly by scanning signed source occurrences as dyadic rationals. At every free original variable $x$, conditional averaging gives $B_T(\rho)=[B_T(\rho,x=0)+B_T(\rho,x=1)]/2$, so retain a positive child. After at most $n$ choices a positive $B_T(a)$ implies $F(a)=1$, because at a complete assignment the forest bound does not exceed its zero-one satisfaction indicator. Pair discovery, failed checks, forest construction, every conditional probability, bit arithmetic, witness reconstruction and replay have polynomial bit cost and peak space. This is not a universal SAT theorem.
For the exact-$(2+,2-)$ corollary, each of a clause's three signed literals can conflict with at most the two opposite-sign occurrences of its variable. Its conflict degree is at most six among seven other clauses, so $G_U$ has no isolates, and $2^{6-3-1}=4$.
\end{proof}
