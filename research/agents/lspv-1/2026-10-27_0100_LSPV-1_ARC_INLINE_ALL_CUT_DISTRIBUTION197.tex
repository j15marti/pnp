\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{.94\linewidth}{
\textbf{PROVED/EXACT, RESTRICTED; independent referee review PENDING.}
For the previously authenticated signed exact-$(2,2)$ DISJ family, a
single fixed, globally sound, deterministic sequential-input interpreter
obeys an exact \emph{distributional} common-input lower bound even when
each satisfiable input selects its own arbitrarily long inline program.
If $A$ is a common upper bound on full crossing-event labels,
$B_A(h)=\sum_{j=0}^h A^j$, and $G$ is the number of fixed middle
cuts, at most $G B_A(h)$ diagonal YES sources admit \emph{any} sound
inline program with prescribed simultaneous resource ceilings and
at most $h$ crossings at \emph{some} middle cut. Thus when
$G B_A(h)\le2^{m-2}$, at least $3/4$ of the $2^m$ YES sources
force $h+1$ crossings at \emph{every} middle cut on every eligible
accepting run, or have no eligible accepting run. This strengthens
the fixed-portfolio/existence counting result, not the asymptotic
restricted exponent and not an unrestricted SAT lower bound.
No ordinary all-UNSAT certificate contraction, ARC-EDGE-90, or
$P\ne NP$ is proved.}}
\end{center}

\section{Target and preregistered falsifiers}
The single target is the quantifier arrow from a sound, possibly
input-dependent inline-program \emph{portfolio} to the cardinality of
YES inputs with a cheap accepting execution. Three falsifiable
mechanisms were fixed \emph{before} the source audit.
\begin{description}
\item[]\textbf{F1.} Distinct program prefixes may require
a factor $2^{b+1}$ even when physically on the same left
source shore. \textbf{FALSIFIED as necessary:} the complete
cross-program splice, independently identified in LSPV-5
INLINE--PREFIX--SPLICE--FIREWALL175, keeps each left program
on its own left hybrid.
\item[]\textbf{F2.} A worst-input time bound may fail to imply
that many inputs are individually hard \emph{at all} fixed
cuts. \textbf{FALSIFIED for the declared sound inline model:}
a union bound over low-crossing histories proves a
$3/4$-density assertion, Theorem~\ref{th:allcuts}.
\item[]\textbf{F3.} The same factor-free theorem might hold
for a separately moving read-only proof/advice tape.
\textbf{FALSIFIED as a model transfer:} a source-only
cut transcript does not authenticate future proof symbols;
a charged two-head proof-stream control supplies a countermodel.
\end{description}

\section{Source gadget, quantifiers and same-machine interface}
The original 15-variable, 20-clause proper signed
exact-$(2,2)$ module $H(a,b)$ has verified model counts
\[
(\#H(0,0),\#H(0,1),\#H(1,0),\#H(1,1))
=(772,272,272,0).
\]
Make $m$ disjoint copies, ordered all Alice-changing clauses,
all 16$m$ fixed clauses, then all Bob-changing clauses:
\[
 \SAT(F_m(a,b))\iff a\wedge b=0^m,\quad
 n_{\mathrm{var}}=15m,\quad n_{\mathrm{cl}}=20m,\quad
 N_m=\Theta(m\log(m+2)),\quad G_m=\Theta(N_m).
\]
Choose $G_m$ fixed bit-boundaries wholly within the common
middle source, excluding any possible varying header/prefix.
For each $a\in\{0,1\}^m$ write $X_a=F_m(a,\bar a)$.
Then $X_a$ is SAT, while for $a\ne a'$ at least one of
$F_m(a,\bar a')$, $F_m(a',\bar a)$ is UNSAT.

Fix \emph{one} deterministic finite-control interpreter $U$
with one two-way sequential source input head, fixed alphabet,
and $k$ fixed sequential work heads/tapes initially blank.
Its physical read-only input is $p\#F_m(a,b)$, with the
entire program prefix $p$ left of every designated cut.
Every \emph{eligible} program is globally sound:
\[
\forall p\in\mathcal P\;\forall F\in\mathrm{3CNF},\qquad
 U(p,F)\downarrow\mathrm{accept}\Longrightarrow\SAT(F).
\]
Completeness of each individual $p$ on YES is \emph{not}
assumed. Programs may be selected with perfect hindsight and
their bit length is not bounded in this theorem. This
assumption does not give free program reading; all movements,
parsing and execution contribute to the transition count
$T_U$. On every single accepting run, visited work cells
$S_U$, actual work-symbol changes $D_U$, reversals $R_U$,
input probes, caches and output are charged on \emph{that
same realization}. A proof on a separate tape is excluded.

At a middle cut, a \emph{complete} crossing label contains
control state, crossing direction, all work contents, and
the positions of \emph{all} work heads. For ceilings
$S_U\le S_0,D_U\le D_0$ let
\[
 h_0=2S_0+3,\quad
 A=A_U(S_0,D_0):=
 2q h_0^k
 \sum_{j=0}^{\min(D_0,kh_0)}
 {kh_0\choose j}(g-1)^j,\qquad A\ge2.       \tag{1}
\]
Here $q,g,k$ are \emph{fixed} finite constants for $U$.
This is an upper bound on possible full crossing labels.
It assumes blank work initialization. An initial $I_0$
nonblank work cells require the peer's $D_0+I_0$ correction.

\begin{lemma}[Cross-program fixed-cut injectivity; previously known]
\label{lem:splice}
For every fixed middle cut $c$, choose \emph{at most one}
eligible sound accepting computation on each $X_a$ with
ceilings $S_0,D_0$. Its complete crossing transcript
$\tau_c(a)$ is injective as a function of the selected $a$,
even when the selected inline programs differ in contents
and length.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $\tau_c(a)=\tau_c(a')$, paste the left shore of
the first accepting run to the right shore of the second;
and conversely. Crossing labels include every mutable
work configuration, so induction on successive crossings
gives accepting computations for both crossed initial
inputs. The left shore includes the full selected program
prefix, so the hybrids are
$p_a\#F_m(a,\bar a')$ and
$p_{a'}\#F_m(a',\bar a)$.
Unequal prefix lengths translate the right suffix along
a translation-invariant source tape but do not change
its symbols or distance to the relative cut. At least
one crossed formula is UNSAT, contradicting global
soundness of its retained left-shore program.
This is precisely the cross-program repair of
LSPV-5 report 175; it is a dependency, \emph{not
a new lemma here}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[ALL-CUT distributional capacity, exact finite]
\label{th:allcuts}
For every $m\ge1$, every fixed $U$ above, ceilings
$S_0,D_0$, every integer $h\ge0$, and any choice
of eligible globally sound programs, define the set
$\mathcal B_h$ of diagonals $a$ for which there
\emph{exists} a sound accepting execution within
$S_0,D_0$ on $p\#X_a$ whose complete transcript
at \emph{some} of the $G_m$ fixed cuts has length
at most $h$. Then
\[
 \boxed{|\mathcal B_h|\le
   \min\{2^m,\;G_m B_A(h)\}},\qquad
 B_A(h):=\sum_{j=0}^h A^j
       =\frac{A^{h+1}-1}{A-1}.                 \tag{2}
\]
Consequently, if $G_mB_A(h)\le2^{m-2}$, at least
$3\cdot 2^{m-2}$ different diagonal YES sources
have the following \emph{universal} property:
\[
 \forall p\in\mathcal P,\quad
  U(p,X_a)\downarrow\mathrm{accept}
  \ \wedge\ S_U\le S_0,\ D_U\le D_0
 \Longrightarrow
   \bigl(\forall c\in [G_m],\ |\tau_c|\ge h+1\bigr)
 \Longrightarrow T_U\ge G_m(h+1).           \tag{3}
\]
This is a promise-family theorem about fixed-$U$
\emph{interpreted} time, not every native implementation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each $a\in\mathcal B_h$, pick one eligible accepting
program/execution and one cut $c(a)$ witnessing a transcript
of length at most $h$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:splice}, for
each fixed cut all such transcripts of selected inputs
are distinct. The map $a\mapsto(c(a),\tau_{c(a)}(a))$
is therefore injective. There are $G_m$ choices for
$c(a)$ and exactly $B_A(h)$ strings of length at most
$h$ over an alphabet of cardinality at most $A$.
This proves (2) without any sum over programs.

For $a\notin\mathcal B_h$, \emph{every} eligible
accepting run must have more than $h$ crossings at
each cut. Each crossing advances the same single
source head across one boundary and belongs to
actual transition time, so summing over pairwise
distinct middle cuts proves $T_U\ge G_m(h+1)$.
Complementing (2) yields the density claim.
No cutwise maxima are added across different
source inputs or different programs.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Quantified tail with arbitrary inline programs]
\label{cor:tail}
For $m$ sufficiently large that
\[
 h_*=\left\lfloor\frac{m-\log_2(4G_m)}
                       {\log_2 A}\right\rfloor-1\ge0,
\]
at least $3/4$ of the diagonal YES sources, uniformly
against \emph{all} sound inline programs satisfying the
same $S_0,D_0$ ceilings, need
\[
 T_U\ge G_m(h_*+1).                           \tag{4}
\]
If $\log_2 A=o(m)$, then on this fraction of sources
\[
 T_U=\Omega\left(
  \frac{G_m m}{\log_2 A}\right).
\]
For fixed finite-control one-shared-tape $U$, a crossing
label is only finite control and orientation
($A=2q$), giving
$T_1=\Omega(N_m^2/\log N_m)$ on at least $3/4$
of the diagonal SAT sources, even with arbitrarily long
input-dependent inline program prefixes.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since $A\ge2$,
$B_A(h)\le A^{h+1}$. For the displayed $h_*$,
$G_m B_A(h_*)\le2^{m-2}$; apply (3).
For the asymptotic clause $\log G_m=o(m)$, and
$\log A=o(m)$ ensures a positive linearly growing
ratio. On a shared read/write tape the input is
charged in footprint; the same crossing-sequence
proof uses only the fixed machine's finite state
and its tape's crossing histories.
\end{proof}

\section{Separate-proof countermodel and other hostile controls}
\begin{counterexample}[An external proof stream is another channel]
On the \emph{tagged, easy} separated DISJ word interface
$a\#0^{G}\#b$, $G\ge m$, a second independent,
two-way \emph{read-only proof head} receives
$\pi=a$ of length $m$. A finite-control verifier first
compares $a$ with $\pi$ while both heads advance,
rewinds its proof head during the fixed gap, and
compares $\pi$ with $b$, rejecting if any coordinate
contains two ones. It is sound on all NO words of this
declared language, recognizes every YES word when
$\pi=a$, has $S=D=0$, and uses
$O(N+m)$ charged head movements.
It does \emph{not} instantiate the one-source-head
inline program model because the proof head is
independently positionable and the entire future
proof stream need not cross the source cut. This
does not prove an all-ordinary-3CNF verifier or
challenge the gadget's signed exact-$(2,2)$ property.
\end{counterexample}

\paragraph{Barriers and model-native ledger.}
For $M_1$, the single shared tape counts the original
input in $P_1\ge N$, while time counts all crossings.
For $M_k$ here, $S$ counts all visited work cells and
$D$ actual symbol changes; $A_U$ in (1) applies to
one bounded sequential read-only input head and fixed
work tapes. The indexed RAM/RAT model has direct
address probes, and none of (2)--(4) transfers to it.
Conservative simulation
$t_1\le t_k+\max(1,s_k)$ for decision-only runs
is only an upper construction, not a reverse lower
bound; physically retained outputs add their length
to $P_1$. Fixed-$U$ changes are not native-program
changes (LSPV-5 report 174). The result does not
assume $P=NP\Rightarrow d=1.802$, nor derive
simultaneous FLMV time-space bounds.
Any guest SAT solver at size $m_i$ retains the full
charged spectrum $\sum_i m_i^K$, with discovery,
access, failures, switches, output and peak space
on both SAT and UNSAT paths through hereditary levels.
No shrinking hereditary ordinary-SAT children are
constructed here.

\paragraph{Cross-domain transfer.}
The rectangle/fooling-set method is a
\textbf{THEOREM TRANSFER} to a sequential single
source-head cut \emph{after} full event-state labels
and local splicing have been proved.
Treating a separately movable proof head, a second
input head, or indexed RAM as the same cut is a
\textbf{FAILED TRANSFER}. An all-UNSAT refutation
grammar with inexpensive \emph{discovery} remains a
\textbf{CANDIDATE} only; easy source-sensitive controls
$U_k$ (ARC196) and the $W_1$ local checker (ARC195)
exhibit cheap correct certification in restricted cases.
