\section{Claim card and preregistered candidates}
The object is $E$, the upward-closed set of pairs simultaneously attained by
\emph{one fixed uniform deterministic total correct ordinary-SAT decider}
on \emph{every ordinary $N$-bit input}, where time
$T(N)\le N^{t+o(1)}$ and counted writable/index space
$S(N)\le N^{s+o(1)}$ are measured in the identical
Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas (FLMV) indexed
read-only-input random-access Turing-machine model.
Under $P=NP$, some integer diagonal $(K,K)\in E$ is achievable.
The strict known forbidden target is
\[
 c_*=\frac{241}{200},\quad
 d_*=\frac{11642479}{23232400}
 =\beta(c_*),\qquad
 \beta(c)=\frac12\left(\frac{c+2}{c^2}-c\right).
\]
The all-input $A$-dependent machine-producing successor
ARC-EDGE-90 is \OPEN. A fixed-depth route may depend on the
\emph{fixed program and $K$}, but never on the varying input length
or an uncharged SAT oracle. Different stages may select different
source-dependent rules, provided every individual new program
is uniform, total, correct on both labels and jointly charged.

Before GitHub/source search, the run fixed four falsifiable candidates:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A finite mixture of eager and lazy stages admits an exact rational
certificate without requiring a common algebraic rule at every stage.
\item One expensive \emph{late} lazy-generation stage can break the
finite strict-box certificate yet a fixed finite number of
later fully charged favorable stages can repair it.
\item Repeating a critical lazy cost $g=1/400$ admits no strict-box
certificate from an initial $K>c_*$, even at arbitrarily large finite depth.
\item Two genuinely executed reductions of ordinary SAT instances
preserve both labels on a disjoint-product promise, but an exact
shared-variable hostile control falsifies independent-status gluing
outside the promise.
\end{enumerate}
Candidates 1--3 are proved \emph{as conditional envelope algebra},
not as machine transformations. Candidate 4 is verified
on its explicit restricted source family; it does not transport to
every ordinary SAT input or a fixed FLMV-hard image.

\section{The exact heterogeneous route-word lemma}
A valid \emph{hypothetical} program transformation of the
current fixed machine $A_{i-1}$ into $A_i$ may be eager or lazy.
Every cost below must already count actual source reads,
changed and unchanged writable-bit operations,
address and index/control arithmetic, discovery and switching,
failed routes, serialization, guest execution, ancestor state,
and the peak simultaneous tapes of the \emph{same} realization.

Write $a_i$ for the fanout exponent, $\lambda_i$ for ordinary
child-input length exponent, $r_i$ for charged noncall work,
$g_i$ for per-child-output-bit access exponent,
and $\eta_i,\kappa_i$ for workspace and retained ancestors.
For \emph{eager readable-child construction}, an admissible
time-envelope phase is
\[
 \widehat t_i=\max\{L_i,u_i+\lambda_i\widehat t_{i-1}\},
 \quad u_i=a_i,\quad
 L_i=\max\{r_i,a_i+\lambda_i+g_i\},
\]
\[
 \widehat s_i=\max\{V_i,\lambda_i\widehat s_{i-1}\},
 \quad V_i=\max\{\lambda_i,\eta_i,\kappa_i\}.
\tag{1}
\]
For \emph{literal lazy repeated probes}, use
$u_i=a_i+g_i$, $L_i=r_i$ and
$V_i=\max\{\eta_i,\kappa_i\}$ instead.
Other channels, nested child-bit generation, shared caches,
extra full-parent calls or variable-length rule choices must first
be folded into valid $u_i,L_i,V_i$, and perhaps different slopes.
Equation (1) is not permission to ignore those terms.
The actual produced machine, if it exists,
has a joint bound dominated by $(\widehat t_i,\widehat s_i)$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact finite route-word certificate]
For any \emph{finite} sequence of phases with nonnegative
$\lambda_i,\nu_i,u_i,L_i,V_i$, define
\[
 F_i(x)=\max\{L_i,u_i+\lambda_i x\},\qquad
 G_i(y)=\max\{V_i,\nu_i y\}.
\]
Let empty products be one and empty sums be zero. Put
\[
 P_{j:d}=\prod_{k=j}^{d}\lambda_k,\qquad
 U_{j:d}=\sum_{k=j}^{d}u_k\prod_{\ell=k+1}^{d}\lambda_\ell,
 \qquad Q_{j:d}=\prod_{k=j}^{d}\nu_k.
\]
Then the \emph{exact iterated envelope}, starting at
$(\widehat t_0,\widehat s_0)=(K,K)$, is
\[
 \boxed{\widehat t_d=
 \max\left\{K P_{1:d}+U_{1:d},
 \max_{1\le j\le d}\left(
 L_jP_{j+1:d}+U_{j+1:d}\right)\right\},}
 \tag{2}
\]
\[
 \boxed{\widehat s_d=
 \max\left\{KQ_{1:d},
 \max_{1\le j\le d}V_jQ_{j+1:d}\right\}.}
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus exact rational strict-box certification for an actual
fully verified machine-producing route reduces to checking
\emph{every} term of (2) against $c_*$ and \emph{every} term
of (3) against $d_*$. This calculation alone creates no
machine and proves no unconditional bound on SAT.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For nonnegative $\lambda_i$,
$\lambda_i\max\{x,y\}=\max\{\lambda_i x,\lambda_i y\}$.
The composed recurrence
$F_i(F_{i-1}(\cdots F_1(K)))$ therefore expands into
the unbroken affine branch from $K$ and one branch for each
latest reset to an $L_j$ floor. Expanding the affine
coefficients yields (2).
The same distributivity with no additive term in $G_i$
gives (3). All identities are exact, for any fixed finite
depth and rational or real coefficients.
\end{proof}
This is a weighted-path/max--plus \emph{algebra} correspondence,
not a transfer of a SAT lower bound from tropical algebra.

\begin{corollary}[Conditional cofinal route implication]
Suppose for every sufficiently large achievable integer diagonal
$(K,K)$ an \emph{actual} finite word of program transformations
produces \emph{one final fixed uniform total exact ordinary-SAT machine}
with fully justified same-machine bounds (1), and (2)--(3) are
strictly inside $(c_*,d_*)$. Then $P\ne NP$ follows from FLMV.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $P=NP$, fix one polynomial-time ordinary-SAT decider $A$.
Some finite $(K_0,K_0)\in E$, and upward closure gives every
integer $K\ge K_0$ in $E$ using the same machine as witness.
Choose a qualifying $K$ and its \emph{finite, fixed} valid
program-transform route. Its final one machine would solve
every ordinary SAT input inside the strict FLMV-forbidden box,
contradiction. The existence of the route is the open hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\section{Quantitative tests of heterogeneous phases}
For the illustrative $(a,\lambda,r)=(3/5,1/2,11/10)$,
an eager generator with $g=1/20$ has
\[
 F_E(t)=\max\{23/20,3/5+t/2\},\qquad
 G_E(s)=\max\{1/2,s/2\}.
\]
A \emph{lazy} generator with $g=1/20$ instead has
$F_L(t)=\max\{11/10,13/20+t/2\}$.
These two fully charged envelopes may be mixed
\emph{if their real semantic machine transformations are proved}.
For $K=10$, eleven eager phases have
$(\widehat t,\widehat s)=(3083/2560,1/2)$, strictly inside
the forbidden box \emph{numerically}.
One lazy stage followed by ten eager stages also enters with
$(4933/4096,1/2)$.
Ten eager stages followed by a lazy stage fail at
$(3211/2560,1/2)$; six further eager stages recover a
strict numerical pair $(196747/163840,1/2)$.
A transient expensive rule is therefore not fatal to a finite
\emph{algebraic} route if its later machine-producing repairs exist.

With the \emph{critical} lazy cost $g=1/400$, the exact time
envelope is $F_C(t)=\max\{11/10,241/400+t/2\}$.
For all $K>c_*$ and all finite $d$,
\[
 F_C^d(K)=c_*+\frac{K-c_*}{2^d}>c_*.
 \tag{4}
\]
Thus repeating \emph{this envelope} never certifies strict
entry, not even if fixed depth depends on $K$.
An actual implementation could be faster than the conservative
envelope; (4) is \emph{not} a lower bound on the real SAT solver.
A one-child eager materialization has the different
$g<21/200$ accounting window already recorded in
\texttt{CACHE-MISS-FRONTIER18}; $g<1/400$
for stateless lazy access was \emph{already known}.
The new contribution is a rule-\emph{heterogeneous},
exact finite-word certificate and its status gate, not rediscovery
of those scalar thresholds.
The space margin is also exact:
$d_*-1/2=26279/23232400$.

\section{Real two-level SAT/UNSAT controls and the eligibility firewall}
The self-contained reproducer constructs a disjoint conjunction
of $q^2$ ordinary 2CNF XOR cycles, each having $q$ distinct
variables, $q=3,4,5,6$.
A cycle expresses $x_i\oplus x_{i+1}=b_i$ and is SAT
if and only if $\bigoplus_i b_i=0$.
The root is serialized and reparsed as an ordinary DIMACS CNF.
Level one consists of $q$ separate group CNFs, each with $q$
disjoint cycles. Level two consists of the $q$ actual serialized
and reparsed individual cycle CNFs within every group.
The same independent deterministic SCC algorithm verifies
both-level child labels and root labels. For this \emph{validated}
disjointness promise, $SAT(F)=\bigwedge_j SAT(G_j)$ and
$SAT(G_j)=\bigwedge_k SAT(H_{jk})$, on both labels.
These are two real ordinary-input recursive levels, not
inferred labels or 1-bit proxy descriptors.

As $q\to\infty$ their ordinary encoded lengths scale
$\Theta(q^3\log q)$, $\Theta(q^2\log q)$,
$\Theta(q\log q)$; they demonstrate proper fractional
child lengths on \emph{this restricted easy family},
but not the proposed all-input ARC fanout or machine bound.
The source is 2SAT/affine, so an SCC/Gaussian-aware solver
can bypass the entire expensive route. The runtime measured
by Python is never represented as FLMV TM bit-work or a
simultaneous writable-space bound.

An explicitly \emph{ineligible} shared-variable control has
$G_1=(x)$ and $G_2=(\neg x)$.
Each is SAT separately, while $G_1\wedge G_2$ is UNSAT.
Thus independence/disjointness is essential: existentially
unrelated witnesses cannot simply be AND-glued.
All recursive promises require true descendant validation.
Malformed DIMACS variable ranges are rejected, not silently
treated as valid source encodings.
