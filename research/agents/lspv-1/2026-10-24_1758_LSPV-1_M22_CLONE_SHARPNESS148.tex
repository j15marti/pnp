\section{Matching Role-5's lower bound}
\begin{theorem}[Role-5 M22-CLONE-EQUALITY-FLOOR147, reproduced]
For $r\ge3$, a proper ternary CNF on exactly $r$ clone variables defining the Boolean relation $\{0^r,1^r\}$ contains at least $2r$ clauses and $6r$ literal occurrences.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both constants must satisfy every clause, so all clauses are mixed-sign. Clauses with exactly one negative literal can reject at most one one-hot word, that of the lone negative variable. Clauses with exactly one positive literal can reject at most one one-cold word. These two clause types are disjoint. Rejecting all $r$ one-hot and $r$ one-cold words requires $r+r=2r$ distinct clauses, each with three slots.
\end{proof}
This exact lower bound is credited to Role-5 and is \emph{not} newly claimed by this report.

\begin{theorem}[M22-CLONE-SHARPNESS148: the floor is sharp]
For every integer $r\ge3$, let indices be modulo $r$ and define
\[
G_r=\bigwedge_{i=0}^{r-1}
[(\neg z_i\vee z_{i+1}\vee z_{i+2})\wedge
 (\neg z_i\vee z_{i+1}\vee\neg z_{i+2})].
\tag{1}
\]
Then $G_r$ has exactly $2r$ distinct proper ternary clauses, model set exactly $\{0^r,1^r\}$, and exactly three positive and three negative appearances of each clone.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $r\ge3$, $i,i+1,i+2$ are pairwise distinct. The clauses with one negative have a unique negative $z_i$, and those with two negatives have a unique positive $z_{i+1}$, showing all $2r$ clauses are different. Let $A_i=\neg z_i\vee z_{i+1}$. The identity $(A_i\vee z_{i+2})\wedge(A_i\vee\neg z_{i+2})\equiv A_i$ gives
\[
G_r\equiv\bigwedge_{i=0}^{r-1}(\neg z_i\vee z_{i+1}).
\tag{2}
\]
A directed implication cycle has exactly two models, all zero and all one. Each clone is twice a negative antecedent, twice a positive consequent, once positive as a third literal and once negative as a third literal. Its signed degree is $(3^+,3^-)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Clausewise-monotone clone-only equality impossibility]
For every $r\ge2$, no clausewise-monotone CNF on only the clones $z_0,\ldots,z_{r-1}$ has exactly $\{0^r,1^r\}$ as its model set, irrespective of width, clause number or signed quotas.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Any nonempty positive OR clause is false on $0^r$; any nonempty negative OR clause is false on $1^r$. A formula containing any clause therefore loses one required constant assignment. The empty conjunction accepts every assignment, including nonconstant ones for $r\ge2$. An empty clause rejects both constants.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Restricted port-normalization firewall]
An independent standalone equality constraint on clones cannot be realized inside the target pure-sign clause grammar without fresh existential auxiliary names or a proved contextual encoding. The sharp mixed-sign construction is \emph{not} a target instance: its clauses are mixed and each clone already has six literal occurrences. If each clone is used once as a source port, only three further literal slots remain under the target exact-(2,2) quota, whereas Role-5's proper clone-only equality needs six on average. Neither result excludes auxiliary-bearing or context-dependent reductions.
\end{corollary}
