\section{Mandatory original-clause S/U targets and falsifiable mechanisms}
For each original clause $C_j$ and signed original-variable occurrence $x_{i,j}$, let $\sigma_{ij}=1$ when positive and zero when negative. The local all-literals-TRUE target $S_j$ fixes $x_i=\sigma_{ij}$; the all-literals-FALSE target $U_j$ fixes $x_i=1-\sigma_{ij}$. Complementing all locally pinned values interchanges $S_j$ and $U_j$. These local targets are \emph{not} a simultaneous assignment of distinct occurrence variables. For two original clauses $C_j,C_k$, SS and UU are compatible precisely if no shared variable has opposite signs; SU and US precisely if no shared variable has equal signs. Both kinds of overlap render all four full-extreme modes incompatible even when both \emph{disjunctions} can be simultaneously SAT. Their true-literal counts can lie in the middle categories 1 or 2.

For globally coherent complete original-variable assignment $a$ and proper width three,
\[
 t_j(\bar a)=3-t_j(a),\quad N_r(\bar a)=N_{3-r}(a),\quad
 F(a)=1\iff N_0(a)=0,
\]
and for an original-variable flip set $H$,
\[
 t_j(a\oplus H)-t_j(a)=|H\cap L_j^0(a)|-|H\cap L_j^1(a)|.
\]
Original-source per-occurrence S/U targets and their inversions, indexed pair compatibility SS/SU/US/UU, verified multi-clause extreme modes, coherent global $0^n,1^n$, intermediate one/multiple flips and complement trails, all four buckets, and proven forbidden cubes are retained in the complete attached reproducibility bundle.

Four mechanisms were tested: (M1) source-parent mixed-extreme pairs yield authenticated short resolvents; (M2) one-step resolvents may exceed source 2SAT closure; (M3) a second resolution step may exceed that closure; (M4) source-indexed generation may lower charged DPLL work on some SAT and UNSAT controls, but increase it elsewhere.

\section{Bounded source resolution construction}
If $C_j=(x\vee A)$ and $C_k=(\neg x\vee B)$ additionally share one same-sign original literal, their SS/SU/US/UU extreme modes are all impossible. Standard resolution derives $R=A\vee B$, a non-tautological clause of width at most three for proper ternary parents. Any original-variable partial cube falsifying $R$ cannot have a satisfying completion: either value of $x$ would falsify one parent. Parent clause IDs, signed pivot and derived literals constitute a replayable proof.
\begin{theorem}
Every proper strict signed exact-(2+,2-) ternary CNF with $n$ original variables admits a complete indexed scan of at most $4n$ opposing-parent original-pivot pairs and at most $4n$ undeduplicated mixed-extreme resolvents of width at most three. Source access, checking unsuccessful pairs, and proof annotation cost $O(N)$ indexed word-RAM operations and words; optional sorting/deduplication costs $O(N\log N)$ operations. This does not decide SAT.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each original variable has exactly two positive and two negative occurrences, yielding four positive-parent/negative-parent pairs. Two ternary parents are checked in constant indexed-word work. The additional equal-sign overlap reduces the post-pivot union of two two-literal sets to at most three distinct literals. Retain original parent indices and pivot.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact one-resolution binary-closure theorem}
For any coherent partial original-variable cube $\rho$, let $B(F\mid\rho)$ consist of the current unsatisfied residual \emph{source} clauses of length at most two. Define $\mathrm{B2Cl}(F,\rho)$ by repeated source unit propagation, exact satisfiability and all unary entailment of $B(F\mid\rho)$ by ordinary implication-graph 2SAT, application of those unary consequences to the \emph{whole} original formula, and iteration to a fixed point or contradiction. This is polynomial-time propagation, not a complete general SAT decider.

\begin{theorem}[One-round original-parent resolution is binary-closure dominated]
For any proper CNF $F$, any family $R$ of non-tautological resolvents individually derived in \emph{exactly one} binary-resolution step from two \emph{original source} clauses, and any coherent cube $\rho$: if UP on $(F\wedge R)\mid\rho$ refutes, then $\mathrm{B2Cl}(F,\rho)$ refutes. Otherwise, every literal assigned by that UP trace is also assigned by the binary closure unless it first refutes. This is a \emph{logical} domination only, not comparative running time.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct over the augmented UP trace. Original source units are handled by source UP. If a derived $R=\mathrm{Res}_x(C,D)$ becomes unit $\ell$, all other non-pivot literals of its \emph{original} parents have already been assigned false. The two current residual original parents contain only the pivot $x,\neg x$ and possibly $\ell$, so both have width at most two. Temporarily setting $\neg\ell$ makes these parents require opposite values of $x$, so their residual source 2CNF entails $\ell$. If $R$ becomes empty, the parents reduce to opposite pivot units (or a source empty clause), already caught by UP. The binary closure iterates after each entailed literal, closing the induction. The argument fails if a resolution parent is itself a derived clause and says nothing about general resolution saturation.
\end{proof}

\section{An explicit strict22 two-step counterexample}
Consider the \emph{original} clauses, with indices from zero:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
C_0=(x_6\vee x_2\vee\neg x_4),&C_1=(x_3\vee x_2\vee\neg x_6),\\
C_2=(x_4\vee\neg x_5\vee\neg x_1),&C_3=(x_5\vee x_4\vee x_3),\\
C_4=(\neg x_3\vee\neg x_6\vee\neg x_1),&C_5=(x_1\vee x_5\vee\neg x_2),\\
C_6=(\neg x_4\vee x_6\vee\neg x_5),&C_7=(\neg x_2\vee\neg x_3\vee x_1).
\end{array}
\]
Each of the six original variables has precisely two positive and two negative source occurrences. Pair $(C_1,C_4)$ shares opposite signed $x_3$ and same-signed $\neg x_6$, so its SS, SU, US, UU target modes all fail. Replayable source-indexed resolution yields
\[
R_1=\mathrm{Res}_{x_3}(C_1,C_4)=(\neg x_1\vee x_2\vee\neg x_6),
\]
\[
R_2=\mathrm{Res}_{x_6}(C_0,R_1)=(\neg x_1\vee x_2\vee\neg x_4).
\]
Under original-variable cube $\rho=\{x_1=1,x_4=1\}$, \emph{source UP}, \emph{source UP with all first-generation mixed-extreme resolvents}, and \emph{iterated exact residual source 2SAT unary closure} all reach consistent fixed points with no newly assigned variables. The second-generation resolvent reduces to $(x_2)$ and thus proves $F\wedge\rho\models x_2$. Exhaustive original-variable enumeration independently finds four satisfying models extending $\rho$, all with $x_2=1$. Ordinary source UP refutes after the further trial $x_2=0$, so established failed-literal probing also finds the implication. This is a strict \emph{finite separation of declared propagation engines}, not a new general SAT method.

A second strict22 SAT control with nine variables has 54 original models. A one-round mixed-extreme derivation refutes a sampled three-pin cube beyond source UP, but complete source residual 2SAT already refutes that cube, as the theorem requires.
