\section{Exact research question and falsifiable candidates}
The active ARC objective is a single fixed uniform deterministic total correct \emph{ordinary SAT} solver $A$ in the indexed-input FLMV model, achieving \emph{simultaneously} $T_A(N)\le N^{K+o(1)}$, $S_A(N)\le N^{s+o(1)}$. Under $P=NP$, one knows some finite unknown polynomial pair, not a $K=1.802$ subpolynomial-space seed. This report tests a narrower necessary condition for source-authenticated adaptive certificates and explicitly refuses a transfer to indexed SAT time. We evaluated four falsifiable mechanisms:
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{H1.} A total exact signed-$(2,2)$ decision algorithm can use arbitrarily long input jumps to avoid the formerly proved \emph{input-direction-reversal versus sequential work-space} cost on the left-gap-right source. \textbf{FALSIFIED} in the precise one-head jump model below.
\item[] \textbf{H2.} The same cost must be paid by every locally accepting \emph{UNSAT} proof path. \textbf{FALSIFIED}: a guessed bad index and a fixed source-authenticated $205$-line Resolution transcript need only a constant number of shore exchanges on the valid-promise image.
\item[] \textbf{H3.} Input jumps reduce the sequential tape travel cost, subject to a fully charged radius-$J$ simulation. \textbf{CONDITIONAL on the independently published HEADMOVE native tape product} for the resulting $t+s+j\ge2$ exponent inequality.
\item[] \textbf{H4.} The reversal-space obstruction directly implies a new FLMV indexed-RAM time-space forbidden region. \textbf{FALSIFIED as a model-transfer inference}: $R$ is not a native indexed-RAM time coordinate and work-tape random access cannot be silently moved onto sequential tapes.
\end{description}

\section{A distinct augmented model with exact resource counters}
Write $M_J$ for a \emph{fixed} total deterministic exact source-bit SAT machine having ONE read-only input head, $k$ fixed finite sequential read/write work tapes, and finite control. The input head may perform one unit movement or one \emph{target-only} signed displacement whose absolute value is at most $J(N)$, with all arithmetic, offset-bit preparation, source-bit reads, unchanged writes, work-head movements and jumps counted as ordinary transitions; jump targets cannot be accessed without moving the single input head. A target symbol may be read on arrival. The input is never writable. Let $T_J(N)$ count all bit-time transitions, $S_J(N)$ the sum of distinct visited work cells, $R_J(N)$ the number of sign changes between consecutive \emph{nonzero signed displacements, including jumps}, and $H_J(N)$ the number of input-head relocation instructions (rather than total skipped physical distance). If the head can leave the $N$-cell input, absolute positions on a $T_J$-step run are bounded by $N+T_JJ$; otherwise they lie in $[1,N]$. A write-only never-reread output has length $L_J$ but no hidden rereadable memory. For SAT decision $L_J=O(1)$.

Neither $M_J$ nor its $R_J$ axis is the FLMV indexed-work/input RAT model. In particular a RAT work-memory access is not a free sequential work-head operation. No multiple input heads are allowed.

\begin{theorem}[Jump-invariant reversal-space lower bound]\label{th:reversal}
For each $m$ and each fixed correct total deterministic $M_J$ on all of the actual encoded $F_m(a,b)$, let $T_m$ and $S_m$ be the maxima of its time and total work cells over those inputs, and $R_m$ the maximum signed-displacement reversal count. There exists one \emph{SAT} source $F_m(a,\neg a)$ for which
\[
\boxed{\bigl(R_J(F_m(a,\neg a))+1\bigr)
 \bigl(S_m+\log_2(N_m+T_mJ(N_m)+2)+1\bigr)=\Omega(m).}\tag{5}
\]
The implicit constant depends only on the fixed finite machine and fixed work-tape count, not on $m$, $J$, its input-dependent strategy or the selected $a$. Under fixed polynomial ceilings $T_m\le N_m^{t+o(1)}$, $S_m\le N_m^{s+o(1)}$, $R_m+1\le N_m^{r+o(1)}$ and $J\le N_m^{j+o(1)}$, (5) implies the \emph{native augmented-model necessary condition}
\[
\boxed{\ r+s\ge1.\ }\tag{6}
\]
Here zero exponent means subpolynomial, including logarithmic work. The bound holds for arbitrarily large polynomial $J$, including direct jumps spanning the entire fixed GAP. It is NOT a RAM bit-time lower bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose one cut $c$ inside the fixed $16m$-clause GAP. Assign all of the left side, including the fixed GAP prefix, to Alice, and the right side to Bob. Their only changing data are $a$ and $b$, respectively. Simulate the single-input-head machine as a deterministic two-party protocol. While the input head lies on one side, its owner simulates all transitions, including changes to any of the \emph{sequential} work tapes. When an input-head motion, including a target-only jump, moves to the opposite side, the owner sends the full work configuration, finite control, all work-head positions, the input-head destination, and the pending jump/read command to the other owner. The receiver performs any destination-side symbol read and continues. These are exact, not guessed, messages; the source bit at the jump target is not silently read by the sender. Since each work-head visits a contiguous interval of at most $S_m$ cells in its own tape, and $k$ and the tape alphabets are fixed, every message has at most
\[
 B_m=C_k\bigl(S_m+\log_2(N_m+T_mJ+2)+1\bigr)
\tag{7}
\]
bits for some machine-specific fixed constant $C_k$. All work traffic and output effects are part of the simulated transitions. Output-only bits cannot be read and do not add a hidden channel.

The $2^m$ pairs $(a,\neg a)$ are all SAT by (3). For any two distinct $a,c$, at least one of the hybrids $(a,\neg c)$ or $(c,\neg a)$ has $a_i\wedge\neg c_i=1$ or $c_i\wedge\neg a_i=1$ at some coordinate, hence is UNSAT. A deterministic protocol's same accepting transcript on two such YES pairs would yield an accepting hybrid by the standard rectangle/splicing property, contradicting correctness. Therefore the $2^m$ YES pairs require $2^m$ distinct complete communication transcripts, including the final acceptance status and halting side. If all those computations exchanged at most $q$ messages, the number of possible transcripts is at most $\sum_{\ell=0}^{q}2^{B_m(\ell+1)}\le2^{O(B_m(q+1))}$. Hence some YES run has $q=\Omega(m/B_m)$ unless $B_m=\Omega(m)$, in which case (5) is automatic.

Across one fixed cut, consecutive side transfers necessarily have alternating directions. Each transfer has a signed head displacement, even when a single jump skips the cut's physical cells. A sequence of $q\ge1$ transfers therefore requires at least $q-1$ sign reversals among the full sequence of nonzero movements; intervening movements cannot eliminate those alternations. Thus $R+1\ge q$. Combining this with the message-count lower bound gives (5). For polynomial bounds the logarithm in (5) is $N_m^{o(1)}$, while $m=N_m^{1-o(1)}$, yielding (6).\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Jump-time interpolation, separate transport]\label{cor:jump}
Suppose a particular $M_J$ run is simulated on one ordinary sequential-input-head, fixed-work-tape machine $M_k$ by replacing each signed jump with at most $J$ unit input-head moves and by charging the preparation and copying of its $O(\log(N+T_JJ))$ address bits. This gives the constructive ceiling
\[
 T_k=O\bigl(T_J(J+\operatorname{polylog}(N+T_JJ+2))\bigr),\qquad
 S_k=O(S_J+\log(N+T_JJ+2)).\tag{8}
\]
\emph{Conditional on} the native, independently governed HEADMOVE-1 sequential-input tape $t_k+s_k\ge2$ theorem on this declared family, the transfer yields $t_J+s_J+j\ge2$ for polynomial bounds. Equation (6) is separately proved \emph{without} this simulation. Neither exponent inequality is asserted for RAT, for a machine with multiple input heads, or for an uncharged output-materializing transducer.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The ordinary head traces the signed jump one cell at a time, and all original computations remain charged. Keeping the original sequential work representations and the bounded offset/counter adds logarithmic work cells; its time is the stated conservative upper bound. The native tape lower bound constrains this \emph{same constructed machine}, not the original jump time directly. Substitution of exponent ceilings gives the conditional inequality.\end{proof}

\section{Sharp restricted controls, two levels and three-model ledger}
On the known canonical image, a deterministic chunked DISJ comparator can retain $B$ Alice bits, jump to and compare the corresponding Bob bits, and repeat for $p=\lceil m/B\rceil$ chunks. On every $(a,\neg a)$ SAT source it visits both shores $p$ times and has $R=2p+O(1)$, $S=O(B+\log N)$, so the reversal-space exponent slope $r+s=1$ is \emph{attained on this EASY promise} for $B=m^{s+o(1)}$, $0\le s\le1$, even if the maximum jump radius equals $N$. Short UNSAT certificates from Lemma~\ref{lem:local} instead guess/identify a single coordinate, and do not inherit the large worst-case SAT reversal count. In a co-nondeterministic SAT construction, the rejecting NO branch cannot replace the required all-branches accepting YES behavior.
