\section{Question, model, three falsifiable mechanisms}
The question is whether an arbitrary uniform deterministic exact SAT decider must construct an expensive rich backward representation, or pay comparably for some alternative symbolic procedure. We do not assume the premise. The fixed NP-complete language is proper, duplicate-free signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF, with each source variable occurring twice positively and twice negatively, so $4n=3m$. Invalid encodings are rejected. The explicit ordered encoding uses a two-word header and four words per ternary clause, at width $w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil$, and $N=(4m+2)w$ bits. An addressed literal-field read costs at least one word operation; parsing, all source reads, validation, construction, failed attempts, recomputation, witness replay, and output are charged separately. There are no uncharged SAT oracles or nonuniform advice strings depending on input length.

The falsifiers are (M1) whether exponential legal sign diversity forces expensive SAT source identification, (M2) whether both SAT and UNSAT labels prevent a compact source-aware root quotient, and (M3) whether an exact sign-field adversary yields more than input-order work when composed into arbitrarily many NP-complete-language blocks. The answers are NO, NO, and NO, respectively. We prove a sharp $2t$ query lower bound \emph{and matching $2t$ promised decision algorithm}, while the source has $2^{199t}$ legal differently signed realizations. This is a source-entropy versus decision-information separation, not a superpolynomial SAT lower bound.

\section{The exact sign gauge and a three-state source quotient}
Let a gauge vector $g\in\{0,1\}^n$ complement \emph{every} signed occurrence of variable $x$ when $g_x=1$. Writing $gF$ for the resulting signed formula,
\[
(gF)(a)=F(a\oplus g).
\]
Hence satisfiability and model counts are invariant, and witnesses transform by $a\mapsto a\oplus g$. The action is free on legal signed exact-$(2,2)$ sources: if $g\ne0$, all four sign fields of some variable change, so $gF\ne F$. Each orbit therefore contains exactly $2^n$ distinct syntactically valid encodings.

\begin{lemma}[Three sign-pair states, PROVED/EXACT]
For a fixed unsigned occurrence structure of a valid exact-$(2,2)$ source, the six possible two-positive sign patterns of each four-occurrence variable split into exactly three complementary pairs. Fixing the first occurrence to be positive gives a unique computable representative of each gauge orbit. Thus the quotient of the complete legal sign fiber has exactly $3^n$ classes, and canonicalization costs $O(n+m)$ word operations with $O(n)$ working words after ordered-source acquisition.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The six choices are $\binom{[4]}2$. Complement maps each chosen two-set to its disjoint complementary two-set, without fixed points. Choosing the representative with its first field positive selects exactly one pattern from each pair. Read each variable's first occurrence, record its sign, and invert that variable's four signs if needed. The resulting representative is unique and the transformation is source coherent; all operations are explicit and polynomial.
\end{proof}
This is an exact symmetry quotient. It does not assert that arbitrary SAT sources have only three semantic states per variable after interactions, nor that quotient computation by itself decides SAT.

\section{Growing two-label family inside the fixed NP-complete language}
For $t\ge1$ form $t$ disjoint variable-renamed source blocks. For $\eta\in\{0,1\}^t$ use $W_0=U$ or $W_1=V$ in block $i$, then independently apply all $198t$ variable gauges $g$. Let $F_{\eta,g}$ denote the resulting source and $\mathcal Q_t$ the family of all such sources.

\begin{theorem}[Two-label orbit product, PROVED/EXACT under seed certificate]
Every $F_{\eta,g}$ is a legal same-shape signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF with
\[
n=198t,\quad m=k=264t,\quad
w_t=\left\lceil\log_2(64(462t+1))\right\rceil,\quad
N_t=(1056t+2)w_t.
\]
All $2^{199t}$ encodings in $\mathcal Q_t$ are distinct, and
\[
\operatorname{SAT}(F_{\eta,g})=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{t}\eta_i.
\]
Exactly $2^{198t}$ sources are SAT and $(2^t-1)2^{198t}$ are UNSAT.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Disjoint variable renaming preserves signed quotas and clause uniqueness. The $2^{198t}$ gauges act freely on each fixed template-vector $\eta$. The orbits for two distinct $\eta$ are disjoint: in a differing block, the four sign fields at $x_3$ change on exactly the two swapped positions, whereas a gauge changes either none or all four. This cannot identify $U$ with $V$. The block conjunction is satisfiable precisely when every block is the SAT template $V$; gauge does not alter the answer. Counting gives the stated formulas.
\end{proof}
The construction gives exponentially many legal sources with both labels \emph{on one identical unsigned scaffold}. This strengthens GLOBALQUOTA80's all-SAT octahedral family as a hostile control, while its computational entropy is still not a SAT runtime lower bound.

\section{Exact two-query per-block amortization}
Consider a deterministic exact algorithm that reads individual signed-literal fields from a source \emph{promised} to lie in $\mathcal Q_t$; the fixed unsigned supports and metadata are public. Each literal occupies one separate source word. The algorithm may query adaptively, reread, cache, and perform arbitrary free internal computation in this \emph{field-query} model. The root answer is one bit. For every prefix transcript $Q$, let $\alpha_i(Q)\in\{0,1,2\}$ be the number of classes among $\{T_i,R_i\}$ from which at least one $x_3$ sign field in block $i$ was queried, and define the independent potential
\[
\Phi(Q)=2t-\sum_{i=1}^t\alpha_i(Q).
\]
A single signed-field query decreases $\Phi$ by at most one, regardless of internal symbolic reasoning.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp all-decider field-query cost, PROVED/EXACT]
On \emph{every} all-$V$ input $F_{1^t,g}$, every total deterministic exact SAT decider for the \emph{full} fixed signed exact-$(2,2)$ language must inspect at least one sign field from $T_i$ \emph{and} one from $R_i$ for each block $i$. Thus at least $2t$ distinct signed-literal word reads are necessary. Conversely, a deterministic promised-family SAT decider uses exactly $2t$ sign queries, and a total uniform full-language decider can recognize the family and answer it in $O(n+m)$ word time. The lower bound is only $\Omega(N_t/\log N_t)$ acquisition, not an unrestricted superlinear time bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix an all-$V$ execution and suppose some block $i$ lacks a query from one of $T_i,R_i$. All queried occurrences of $x_3$ in that block are then from a single type, or none. Replace $V$ with $U$ only in block $i$, and choose its $x_3$ gauge so that the observed signs on that single type match those of the original all-$V$ source. This is possible because the sign difference $U\to V$ is constant within either type. Preserve all other variable gauges and all other blocks. Every source field queried by the deterministic execution has exactly its old value. By transcript induction the execution and one-bit answer are unchanged, while the replacement source is UNSAT. Contradiction. Therefore each of the two types in every block must be queried. This requires at least $2t$ reads and drives $\Phi$ to zero.

For the matching upper bound, read $(3,1)$ from $T_i$ and $(5,0)$ from $R_i$. Their signs are equal in $U$ and unequal in $V$, and simultaneous complementation of all $x_3$ signs preserves equality. Thus their XOR is exactly $\eta_i$. AND these $t$ values. Source headers and supports are a free promise only in the $2t$ query model. Without a promise, a uniform decider scans and validates the complete ordered source, fixes each variable's first-occurrence gauge and checks whether each canonical block is the $U$ or $V$ template. It then applies the same Boolean rule. Outside this recognized family it calls a conventional exact exponential fallback. The recognizer's finite 264-clause templates are fixed machine code, not advice growing with $t$. All source reads, validity checks, caching and output remain charged.
\end{proof}
On an all-$V$ input $\Phi=0$ is necessary for exact acceptance in the declared query model; on an input containing $U$, early rejection may cost only two reads. There is no claim of a compulsory static compatibility table, witness history, DNNF, OBDD, resolution proof, or enumeration of all original assignments. This proof remains valid for arbitrary symbolic methods \emph{only as an addressed input-observation bound}.

\section{Backward Circuit-SAT accounting and all-algorithm coverage}
Backward output TRUE through the AND of 3-literal OR clauses asks for one true literal occurrence per clause with no chosen complementary occurrences of any shared variable. In proper signed exact-$(2,2)$, the opposite-occurrence graph has $n$ disjoint $K_{2,2}$ blocks and $4n=3m$ literal vertices; each clause contributes a triple. This is a compact exact independent-transversal formulation, not a lower bound. The evaluation map $a\mapsto F(a)$ is computable with $n+3m-1$ NOT1/OR2/AND2 gates and $n+6m-2$ input wires under the shared-inverter basis. It is different from the root map $F\mapsto\operatorname{SAT}(F)$, and those evaluation counts are not SAT-solving circuit lower bounds.

All unrestricted methods remain legal: source retention and random reread, SCC and Horn preprocessing, affine elimination, learned clauses and caching, component decomposition, compact symbolic descriptions, and decide-first recognition. In fact, the last two yield the exhibited linear-time algorithm for $\mathcal Q_t$ despite its exponential source and relation diversity. A branch-program or context-complete representation theorem would require a separate extractor with its full construction and evaluation work charged.
