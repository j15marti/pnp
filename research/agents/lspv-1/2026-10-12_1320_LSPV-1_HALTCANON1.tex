The active clean core is the duplicate-free proper signed Monotone
\(3\)SAT-\((2,2)\) family
\[
 \mathcal H_{2,2}^{\pm}
 =\{F:\text{every clause has three distinct variables and one sign,
 and every variable occurs twice in each polarity}\}. \tag{1}
\]
For its canonical indexed encoding,
\(N=|\operatorname{enc}(F)|=\Theta(n\log n)\), and satisfiability restricted
to (1) is NP-complete by D\"ocker's result. Define
\[
 H(F,\alpha)=1\Longleftrightarrow
 \alpha\in\{0,1,\star\}^{n(F)}
 \text{ extends to a satisfying assignment of }F. \tag{2}
\]
This semantic predicate does not assert that a deciding execution retains a
compatibility row, witness, table, or transcript.

The machine model is a deterministic finite program with a read-only
semi-infinite input tape, a fixed finite number \(k\) of semi-infinite work
tapes, left-end markers, a finite alphabet, and one output bit. Every step
and tape move costs one. Let \(T_A(x)\) be time, \(S_A(x)\) the number of
distinct work-tape cells visited, and \(R_A(x)\) the input-dependent bits
remaining in work tapes, head positions, finite control, and output at halt,
after fixed constants are quotiented out.

For unbounded \(g\), the target under attack is
\[
 \forall B\in\PolyDec(K)\;\forall N_0\;\exists F\in\mathcal H_{2,2}^{\pm}:
 |F|\ge N_0\land R_B(F)\ge g(|F|),
 \quad K=\SAT\!\upharpoonright_{\mathcal H_{2,2}^{\pm}}. \tag{3}
\]
When \(\PolyDec(K)\) is empty, (3) is vacuous. A contradiction from \(P=NP\)
would require (3) when that domain is nonempty.

\section{Canonical cleanup theorem}
\begin{theorem}[\texttt{HALTING-CONFIGURATION-CANONICALIZATION1}]
For every total deterministic one-bit decider \(A\) in the declared model,
one can effectively construct a total same-language decider
\(\operatorname{Clean}[A]\) such that, for every input \(x\),
\[
 \operatorname{Clean}[A](x)=A(x),\qquad
 T_{\operatorname{Clean}[A]}(x)\le c_A(T_A(x)+1),\qquad
 S_{\operatorname{Clean}[A]}(x)\le S_A(x)+O_A(1). \tag{4}
\]
At halt all work tapes are blank, all heads are at their left ends, and the
control is one of two fixed states \(q_0,q_1\). Hence
\(R_{\operatorname{Clean}[A]}(x)\le1\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compile \(A\)'s transition table into the wrapper. On each work tape, use a
constant auxiliary alphabet track to mark the rightmost cell ever visited.
Since each tape is semi-infinite from its left end, its visited cells form a
prefix. Crossing the frontier updates the marker with constant overhead.

Simulate \(A(x)\). When it halts, retain its answer \(b\) in finite control.
For each work tape, move to the left end, sweep right while blanking data
until the frontier marker is reached, erase that marker, and return left.
Reset the input and output heads, write \(b\) (or encode it in the halt
state), and enter \(q_b\).

For fixed \(k\), all cleanup sweeps and head resets take \(O(T_A(x)+1)\)
steps: no head can be farther than \(T_A(x)\) cells from its origin, and no
visited prefix is longer than \(T_A(x)+1\). Frontier bits occupy already
visited cells, apart from fixed left-end overhead. Thus (4) holds. Only
\(b\) remains input dependent.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
If \(K\in\mathsf P\), some \(B\in\PolyDec(K)\) satisfies \(R_B(F)\le1\)
for every \(F\); therefore (3) is false for every unbounded \(g\). If the
quantifier ranges over all total exact deciders, the analogous claim is
false unconditionally.
\end{corollary}

This is the exact \(P=NP\) hostile control: a hypothetical polynomial
decider becomes another polynomial decider, not an unconditional
contradiction. Cleanup occurs inside the same execution before halt, so the
counterexample is SAME\_REALIZATION. No runtime is inferred from semantic
state count.

\section{Literature, barriers, and transfer classification}
The two obstacles identified first were separating final retained state from
peak/causally committed state, and matching any survivor to an unconditional
theorem in the same realization. Primary literature was checked before the
claim. Bennett's reversible simulation computes, copies output, and reverses
the computation to remove garbage in a reversible model \cite{Bennett1973}.
That pattern transfers only with its model and overhead; the theorem above
uses simpler irreversible cleanup. FLMV concerns simultaneous time and peak
space, not final garbage \cite{FLMV2005}. Baker--Gill--Solovay and
Aaronson--Wigderson are barrier checks, not positive transfers
\cite{BGS1975,AW2009}.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Directly transferable theorem.} The finite-tape cleanup proof is
model matched and self-contained. Bennett is exact only in its distinct
reversible model; no thermodynamic premise is imported.
\item \textbf{Useful analogy requiring a new transfer theorem.} Reset
circuits, garbage collection, observability, and checkpointing distinguish
live state from garbage. Static SAT still needs a compiler preserving the
cut, time, peak space, rereads, and recomputation.
\item \textbf{Misleading analogy / failed transfer.} VLSI area--time,
dissipative reset cost, communication cuts, proof-system space, and
branching-program width do not lower bound unrestricted-TM SAT without a
model-preserving reduction.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Relativization and algebrization.}
Cleanup treats oracle or algebraic-oracle queries as simulated transitions
and works relative to every such oracle. It crosses neither barrier.

\paragraph{Natural Proofs and uniformity.}
No constructive large circuit property is defined. One uniform finite
wrapper handles every length; unlike a pointwise hardwired minimizer, the
program does not change with \(N\).
