\section{CONSTRUCTION OR PROOF ATTEMPT}
For each $n\ge1$, $a,b\in\{0,1\}^n$, introduce private group variables $x_i,u_i,v_i,y_i,z_i,s_i,t_i$. Let
\[
G(\ell;p,q)=
(\ell\vee p\vee q)\wedge(\ell\vee p\vee\neg q)
\wedge(\ell\vee\neg p\vee q)\wedge(\ell\vee\neg p\vee\neg q).
\]
The four distinct proper ternary clauses force $\ell$. Put $\alpha_i=x_i$ when $a_i=1$ and $\alpha_i=u_i$ otherwise; $\gamma_i=\neg x_i$ when $b_i=1$ and $\gamma_i=v_i$ otherwise, and
\[
F_{a,b}=\bigwedge_{i=1}^nG(\alpha_i;y_i,z_i)\wedge
\bigwedge_{i=1}^nG(\gamma_i;s_i,t_i).
\]
The source has $8n$ proper clauses on $7n$ distinct variable labels, and is SAT iff $\forall i\ \neg(a_i=b_i=1)$: two active indicators force $x_i,\neg x_i$; otherwise private dummies satisfy every group.
\begin{theorem}[Restricted same-machine contractor]
Write a canonical header specifying $n$ and decimal label width, then fixed-width clauses with all Alice groups before Bob groups. This promise has encoded length $N=\Theta(n\log(n+2))$ and can be recognized by bytewise comparison with expected records in $N\operatorname{polylog}N$ time, $O(\log N)$ writable space on a deterministic random-access multitape TM. Let $A$ be a correct all-input SAT decider with simultaneous time $M^{t+o(1)}$, space $M^{s+o(1)}$ on that model. There is a source-recognizing partial controller on the canonical promise satisfying
\[
T_{C[A]}(N)\le N^{\max\{1,(1+t)/2\}+o(1)},\qquad
S_{C[A]}(N)\le N^{\max\{1/2,s/2\}+o(1)}.
\]
It constructs each child explicitly, calls $A$ sequentially, charges source access and all memory, and retains hereditary promise closure. On a nonmember it returns UNKNOWN.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $n\ge4$ divide group indices into $r=\lceil n/g\rceil$ consecutive intervals, $g=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor$. Each child is the locally relabelled canonical formula for the indicator substring, of at most $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ encoded bits. There are at most $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ children. Since indexed group variables are disjoint, the parent SAT bit equals the conjunction of children's SAT bits. Source membership, full byte-address calculations, generated child bytes, exact native SAT encoding and the accumulator cost $N^{1+o(1)}$ total time. The entire sequence of calls to $A$ costs $N^{(1+t)/2+o(1)}$ and a single live child plus $A$ tapes uses $N^{\max\{1/2,s/2\}+o(1)}$ space. Every child has the same grammar and shorter index length, permitting finite iteration with live ancestor buffers included. For $n\le3$ a constant-size base solver terminates. There is no all-input correctness claim.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Cross-boundary counterexample]
For $n=4$ the intervals are $\{1,2\},\{3,4\}$. Put $a_1=a_3=1$ and all other $a_i,b_i=0$. Both generated blocks are SAT. Adding a fresh $z$ and proper clauses
\[
(\neg x_1\vee\neg x_3\vee z),\quad
(\neg x_1\vee\neg x_3\vee\neg z)
\]
makes the parent UNSAT. Thus promoting the block rule to arbitrary SAT without source recognition is FALSIFIED/RETIRED.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The forcing gadgets demand $x_1=x_3=1$; appended clauses then demand $z$ and $\neg z$. The compiler's original blocks contain no doubly-active coordinate. The augmented formula is rejected as outside the canonical grammar.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{Direct easy-family control.} Read every indexed pair $a_i,b_i$ and test for simultaneous ones. This decides the restricted family in $N\operatorname{polylog}N$ time and $O(\log N)$ space without $A$. Hence favorable promise contraction does not imply the FLMV-forbidden general SAT algorithm.
