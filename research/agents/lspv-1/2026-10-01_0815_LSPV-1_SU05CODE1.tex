\paragraph{KNOWN THEOREM: SU05 Lemma 4.16 does not fix a unique code.}
SU05 Definition 4.15 defines a binary code
\[
C:\{0,1\}^{\bar k=\log q}\to\{0,1\}^{\bar n}
\]
to be $(\rho,\ell)$-list-decodable in the usual Hamming sense. Lemma 4.16 takes \emph{any} $(\rho,\rho^{-2})$-list-decodable code $C$ and converts a q-ary extractor
\[

\]
to a binary extractor by adding a codeword-coordinate seed. Thus the theorem hypothesis specifies a code property, not a unique coordinate map.

\paragraph{KNOWN THEOREM: computational analogue and explicit Hadamard permission.}
SU05 Lemma 5.6 is the computational analogue. It assumes $C$ is efficiently encodable and efficiently list-decodable and maps a q-ary generator $G_x$ to
\[
G'_x(y,j)=
C(G_x(y)_1)_j\circ\cdots\circ C(G_x(y)_m)_j.
\]
Immediately after Lemma 5.6, SU05 states that suitable codes can be obtained from general list-decodable constructions, but in their parameter regime they can \emph{even use the simpler Hadamard code, together with the trivial list-decoding algorithm}. Therefore Hadamard is an explicitly source-sanctioned legal SU05 computational binary conversion, but it is not uniquely forced by Lemma 4.16 or Lemma 5.6.
