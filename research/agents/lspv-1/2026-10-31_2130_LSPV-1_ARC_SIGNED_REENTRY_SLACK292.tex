\section{Canonical target and theorem}
Let $F$ be any valid duplicate-free proper monotone 3-SAT-(2,2) source with $n$ variables; malformed encodings reject. A coherent partial assignment pins $t_1$ original variables to 1 and $t_0$ to 0, leaving $f=n-t_1-t_0$ free. Let $u_+$ and $u_-$ be counts of uncovered original clauses of the two signs. Define $u=u_++u_-$, $e=2f-u$, and $\delta=u_+-u_-$. A polarity-faithful clause-retaining re-entry $G$ is another valid canonical formula on $n_G$ variables, retaining a distinct same-sign target clause record for every uncovered original clause. It may add other clauses and variables or rename IDs, but may not merge, delete, or flip source-clause records. This is a SYNTACTIC hypothesis, not a Boolean equivalence claim.

\begin{theorem}[Signed canonical count obstruction]
Every such $G$ obeys
\[
u_+\ge 2n/3-2t_1,\quad u_-\ge 2n/3-2t_0,
\quad
\boxed{n_G\ge3\lceil\max(u_+,u_-)/2\rceil\ge n-3\min(t_0,t_1).}
\]
If all $f$ distinct free original variables are retained and $q=n_G-f$ are new, then
\[
\boxed{q\ge f/2-3e/4+3|\delta|/4.}
\]
The fresh target-root slack satisfies
\[
\boxed{e_G=2n_G/3\ge f-e/2+|\delta|/2.}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The source has exactly $2n/3$ clauses of each polarity. Each pinned 1 satisfies at most its two positive clauses and each pinned 0 at most its two negative clauses. Every valid target has exactly $2n_G/3$ clauses of EACH polarity, so the original uncovered copies force $2n_G/3\ge\max(u_+,u_-)$. Since $n_G$ is divisible by 3, rounding gives the first boxed bound. The remaining statements use $\max(u_+,u_-)=(2f-e+|\delta|)/2$, subtract $f$ or multiply by $2/3$. This gives only count necessities.
\end{proof}
If explicit indexed original and target clause lists have lengths $N=\Theta(n\log n)$ and $M=\Theta(n_G\log n_G)$, then $M\le N^\lambda$ for fixed $\lambda<1$ implies $n_G=o(n)$, hence $t_1,t_0\ge n/3-o(n)$. One-sign-only source pinning never yields a power-short clause-retaining child. If $G$ retains all $u$ uncovered clauses and $f$ free variables and adds $c$ clauses and $q$ variables, then $3c-4q=4f-3u=3e-2f$, generalizing the known one-pivot $3c-4q=2$.
