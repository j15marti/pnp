\section{Precise candidate attacked}
After eliminating $B_Xx=c$, suppose the residual is a square vector-valued
quadratic map $Q:\F^d\to\F^d$.  FIELDTRACEFW1 and FIELDTRACELIFT1 show that a
surjective map with growing full-rank polar pencil may still have an easy
selector.  A plausible repair was:
\begin{quote}
If the projected interface rank is $d\to\infty$, $Q$ is genuinely nonlinear,
and every satisfiable output has at most polynomially many preimages (ideally
one), then finding a preimage must require unavoidable work.
\end{quote}
The universal implication is false.  The counterexample below permits full
source access, polynomial workspace, explicit Gaussian inversion, compact
quadratic circuits, and charged edge-witness reconstruction.

\section{Primary-literature and transfer preflight}

No primary source found converts small fibers, collision entropy, polar rank,

separation.

\section{Quadratic permutation firewall}
For $d\ge3$, define $T_d:\F^d\to\F^d$ by
\[
 (T_d(z))_1=z_1,qquad (T_d(z))_2=z_2,qquad
 (T_d(z))_i=z_i+z_1z_{i-1}\quad(3\le i\le d).            \tag{1}
\]
Every coordinate has degree at most two, and the map is genuinely nonlinear
for $d\ge3$.

\begin{theorem}[bijective quadratic residual firewall]
Let $A,B\in\mathrm{GL}_d(\F)$ and $p,q\in\F^d$.  The map
\[
 Q_{A,B,p,q}(y)=B\,T_d(Ay+p)+q                           \tag{2}
\]
is a quadratic permutation.  For every target $b\in\F^d$, its unique
preimage is computable in polynomial time and space.  If $d=(s-1)^2$ for odd
$s\ge3$, the preimage extends uniquely to a complete witness for every
consistent charge on $K_{s,s}$ in polynomial time.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Given $w=T_d(z)$, recover
\[
 z_1=w_1,quad z_2=w_2,quad
 z_i=w_i+z_1z_{i-1}\quad(3\le i\le d).                  \tag{3}
\]
The recurrence proves simultaneously that $T_d$ is injective, surjective,
and inverted in $O(d)$ Boolean operations.  For target $b$, compute
\[
 w=B^{-1}(b+q),\qquad z=T_d^{-1}(w),\qquad
 y=A^{-1}(z+p).                                         \tag{4}
\]
All three values are unique, so (2) is a permutation.

For the Tseitin embedding, let $X=K_{s,s}$ and use the star-of-stars spanning
tree.  Its cotree has $d=(s-1)^2$ edges.  Set those cotree values to $y$ from
(4), subtract their incidences from the even charge vector, and solve the tree
edges leaf-to-root.  Connectedness makes the last equation consistent.  This
is the unique edge vector with the prescribed cotree projection, and it
satisfies both the affine system and residual target.
\end{proof}

The construction has projected interface rank $\rho=d$.  Explicit $A,B$ cost
$2d^2$ bits, and recognition checks both ranks in $O(d^3)$ bit operations.
Parsing, affine inversion, recurrence (3), graph construction, tree lifting,
complete output, and verification are polynomial in the encoded input length.
No inverse matrix, source witness, or charge solution is advice.

\section{Collision and entropy consequence}
Because (2) is a permutation, every fiber has size exactly one and
\[
 \operatorname{Coll}(Q)=2^d,qquad
 |\operatorname{im}Q|=2^d.                              \tag{5}
\]
The Cauchy--Schwarz image bound is tight.  Hence maximum image size, singleton
fibers, and maximum collision-effective entropy coexist with an explicit
polynomial selector.  Combined with FIELDTRACELIFT1, which has large unequal
fibers and an equally explicit selector, both ends of the fiber spectrum are
easy controls.  Fiber statistics remain useful diagnostics, but no standalone
fiber inequality is an unrestricted runtime theorem.
