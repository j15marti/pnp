\section*{Executive result}

\texttt{CLEANCORE-DEFINITION-SEAM1} is independently adopted as \EXACT.  Two formula families recently called Monotone
$3$SAT-$(2,2)$ are not interchangeable:
\begin{itemize}
\item the all-positive, exact-two-occurrence family has
$m=2n/3$ and every instance is satisfied by the all-one assignment;
\item the active signed family has sign-monochromatic clauses, exactly two
positive and two negative occurrences of each variable, and $m=4n/3$.
Its NP-completeness is a \KNOWN\ result of D\"ocker.
\end{itemize}
Consequently, any universal lower-bound target restricted to the first
family is \RETIRED: a recognize-first exact wrapper returns YES in
polynomial time on every promised input.  This does not refute the
family-independent black-box fork-scale trilemma in LB-0021, but it
invalidates that report's claimed instantiation of the project's hard clean
core.

The result repairs an input-domain seam and reconciles the LSPV-1 specialty; it proves no arbitrary-computation lower bound.
\textbf{P versus NP remains OPEN.}

\section{Exact definitions and theorem}

\begin{definition}[All-positive exact-two family]
$\Hplus$ contains duplicate-free proper $3$-CNFs in which every clause has
three distinct positive literals and every variable occurs in exactly two
clauses.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Signed Monotone $(2,2)$ family]
$\Hsigned$ contains duplicate-free proper $3$-CNFs in which each clause is
sign-monochromatic---all positive or all negative---and every variable
occurs exactly twice positively and exactly twice negatively.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Clean-core definition seam]
For every $F\in\Hplus$ on $n$ variables:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $m=2n/3$;
\item the all-one assignment satisfies $F$;
\item $\SAT|_{\Hplus}$ is the constant-YES promise language.
\end{enumerate}
For every $G\in\Hsigned$ on $n$ variables, $m=4n/3$.  Moreover,
$\SAT|_{\Hsigned}$ is NP-complete.
Hence replacing $\Hsigned$ by $\Hplus$ is not a harmless notation or
encoding change.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In $\Hplus$, summing variable degrees gives $2n$ literal occurrences,
while summing clause widths gives $3m$, so $3m=2n$.  Every literal is
positive; assigning every variable true satisfies every clause.  Thus the
promise answer is always YES.

In $\Hsigned$, every variable has four occurrences, so
$3m=4n$.  D\"ocker's theorem applies exactly to unique monotone
three-clauses with three distinct variables and two unnegated plus two
negated occurrences per variable, and proves NP-completeness.  The sign
balance and doubled incidence count are semantic restrictions, not encoding
conventions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Recognize-first refutation]
Fix any total deterministic exact SAT decider $B$ and define
\[
 W_B(F)=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&F\in\Hplus,\\
  B(F),&F\notin\Hplus.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then $W_B$ is a uniform total exact SAT decider.  On $\Hplus$ it uses
polynomial time and logarithmic working space under a conservative
multi-pass recognizer.  Therefore (1) is false.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The positive branch is correct by the theorem.  Outside the promise,
$W_B$ inherits the correctness of $B$.  A conservative recognizer parses
the canonical input, checks clause width, signs, distinct literals,
duplicate clauses, and occurrence counts by repeated scans.  Even a coarse
$O(N^3)$ time and $O(\log N)$ work-space bound suffices.  Taking any
$d>3$ in (1), no sufficiently large promised source can have
$T_{W_B}(F)>N^d$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Implication for LB-0021.}
The black-box fork-scale trilemma in LB-0021 is algebraically independent
of its chosen clean core and survives this audit.  Its local declaration
of three distinct positive literals, exactly two occurrences, and
$m=2n/3$ is not the active NP-complete family.  Claims that its finite
controls instantiate hard $\Hsigned$ sources must therefore be downgraded.
This is a definition repair, not a reason to retire the trilemma itself.

\section{Primary literature preflight}

D\"ocker defines Monotone $3$Sat-$(2,2)$ using unique monotone
three-clauses and exactly two unnegated plus two negated occurrences of
every variable, and proves the problem NP-complete.  The paper also
constructs an unsatisfiable instance.  This is a \textbf{direct theorem
transfer}: its formula family is exactly $\Hsigned$.  It does not apply to
$\Hplus$.

Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas is a \textbf{direct theorem
transfer} only after one ordinary random-access machine is constructed in
their simultaneous time--space region.  The present easy-island wrapper
does not yield such a machine on the full language and supplies no
contradiction.

Relativization and algebrization remain barriers.  The correction is
syntactic and relativizing; it cannot distinguish oracle worlds and uses no
operation that low-degree extension access invalidates.

\section{Hostile controls and wrappers}

DIRECT, COPY, DEAD, DELAY, INTERP, and RECOMP implementations all returned
YES on every exhausted $\Hplus$ source.  Clause order, three variable
renamings, a textual alternate encoding, independent products, and
redundant-clause padding preserved the all-one witness.

Horn, $2$SAT, bounded-treewidth, affine-as-CNF, and odd-charge Tseitin
UNSAT controls were included as scope guards: the constant-YES shortcut
must not be exported beyond $\Hplus$.  The theorem makes no claim that
those general families are constant.

Compact circuits and branching programs can represent the constant
promised decision with one output gate/state.  This is an algorithmic
exploitation of the structure, not evidence of hardness.  A Schur square,
quadratic relation space, Hilbert function, syzygy, collision distribution,
or source tag computed from $\Hplus$ cannot force more than the promised
decision without a separate full-language transfer.
