\section{Four pre-source falsifiers and exact model}
\begin{description}
\item[] H1 (CANDIDATE): ARC181's one-bit parity promise forces $p$ original input probes on \emph{every accepting nondeterministic UNSAT witness path}, not merely every deterministic deciding computation. We prove this, independent of the witness strategy.
\item[] H2 (CANDIDATE): the same lower bound yields a superlinear lower bound for coNTIME certificate verification. FALSIFIED: it is only $\Omega(N/\log N)$ and a matching near-linear promise checker exists.
\item[] H3 (CANDIDATE): the curved $C(d)=(2d-1)/(d(d-1))$ is the universally sharp van Melkebeek two-algorithm threshold for every $d>1$. FALSIFIED: the theorem has a disjunction; the alternative branch dominates at $d>2$.
\item[] H4 (OPEN): an $A$-dependent construction uniformly produces all-UNSAT sound and complete, cheaply generated certificates with paid input probes, child access, properization, joins, and single-machine resources. The present lower bound neither constructs nor excludes it.
\end{description}
We work on indexed-read-only-input random-access Turing machines, with work/index tapes charged, exactly as in \cite{flmv,vm07}. A verifier for $\UNSAT$ is nondeterministic: an input is UNSAT iff some path accepts. Acceptance on any SAT input is forbidden. Its nondeterministic guesses (equivalently certificate symbols) do not confer true, free source-bit knowledge. Every read, child-bit generation, write, address update, rule switch, and failed attempt is charged. Neither this model nor the proof permits a trustworthy SAT oracle.

\section{Fixed-incidence SAT parity promise, adopted from ARC181}
For $p\ge2$, $b\in\{0,1\}^{p}$, let $F_{p,b}$ denote ARC181's proper signed 3-CNF. Its first $4p$ clauses form $p$ forcing gadgets. The four clauses of the $i$th gadget force an original variable $x_i=b_i$, using two private variables. A chain of $(p-1)$ four-clause XOR gadgets computes the parity of $x_1,\ldots,x_p$. A final four-clause gadget forces odd parity. Every clause has three distinct variables. Exactly
\[
 n_p=4p+1,\qquad m_p=8p,
 \qquad\SAT(F_{p,b})\iff \bigoplus_{i=1}^{p}b_i=1.
\]
This family is ordinary proper \emph{signed} 3-CNF, not exact-$(2,2)$ or clause-monotone. No such transfer is assumed. With fixed-width sign/ID fields of width $w=\lceil\log_2(\max\{n_p,m_p\}+2)\rceil$, its physical encoded length is $N_p=2w+24p(w+1)=\Theta(p\log(p+2))$. All $F_{p,b}$ share the same clause structure, IDs, header, field lengths and incidence. Only the four first-literal sign fields in the $i$th forcing gadget depend on $b_i$. Write $D_i$ for these four original input-bit addresses. The $D_i$ are nonempty, pairwise disjoint, and replacing $b_i$ with $1-b_i$ modifies \emph{exactly} $D_i$.

\section{New accepting-path theorem}
\begin{theorem}[One-sided certificate probe floor, \PROVED]\label{thm:paths}
For every $p\ge2$ and for every nondeterministic machine $V$ sound on every ordinary proper signed three-CNF (or just on the promise family) as an $\UNSAT$ verifier, each accepting computation path of $V$ on the \emph{unsatisfiable} source $F_{p,0^p}$ must read a source bit in every one of the $p$ disjoint groups $D_1,\ldots,D_p$. Therefore any such complete verifier has worst-case accepting-path time
\[
 T_V(N_p)\ge p=\Omega(N_p/\log N_p)=N_p^{1-o(1)}.
\]
The conclusion continues to hold with arbitrary nondeterministic witness guesses, adaptive accesses, arbitrary finite preprocessing, and arbitrary work-space, provided every genuine original-source probe is counted from the initial configuration.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The source $F_{p,0^p}$ is UNSAT by the parity identity. Completeness supplies an accepting path $\pi$. Let $Q(\pi)$ be the physical original-input bit addresses it reads (repetitions allowed). Suppose $Q(\pi)\cap D_i=\varnothing$ for some $i$. Set $b=e_i$, the $i$th unit vector. Then $F_{p,e_i}$ is SAT and differs from $F_{p,0^p}$ \emph{only} at positions in $D_i$. Replay on $F_{p,e_i}$ exactly the nondeterministic choices made along $\pi$. Inductively every state and every observed read value remains identical, since any address chosen depends only on the previous observed values and choices, all of which agree. Thus the replay also accepts, contradicting soundness on the SAT source. Hence $Q(\pi)$ must meet all $p$ pairwise disjoint groups, requiring at least $p$ distinct read instructions. Since $N_p=\Theta(p\log p)$, the displayed bound follows. This proof charges all source reads from the initial state and does not assume a prescribed search order.\qedhere
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Source-probe firewall for certificate simulators, \PROVED]
Any claimed uniform complete all-UNSAT certificate generator/checker, if represented by a sound nondeterministic UNSAT verifier whose individual accepting executions cost $N^{c+o(1)}$ in the source-indexed model, must have $c\ge1$. In particular an uncharged trusted child-SAT answer or a semantic join that secretly reads an omitted group cannot be treated as a free step. This does \emph{not} imply an $N^{1+\epsilon}$ lower bound for any $\epsilon>0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:paths} to infinitely many lengths $N_p$. If $c<1$, the asserted time bound is $o(N_p/\log N_p)$ and contradicts its source-probe floor.\qedhere
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Sharpness on the promise:} Read one signed field from each $D_i$, compute the XOR, and recognize the promised parity label in $O(p\operatorname{polylog}p)=N^{1+o(1)}$ time and $O(\log p)$ work/index space. It is a \emph{promise-only} verifier, not an all-ordinary-input exact UNSAT algorithm. No general circuit-size lower bound follows: ARC181 also gave a $4(p-1)$-NAND promise circuit.

\section{Verified van Melkebeek frontier and numerical gate}
Primary source \cite[Theorem 1.3, p.~2]{vm07} states that for reals $c,d$ satisfying
\[
 (c-1)d<1 \quad \textbf{OR}\quad cd(d-1)-2d+1<0,
\]
there exists $\epsilon>0$ such that SAT cannot have \emph{both} (i) a conondeterministic $N^c$ random-access decider and (ii) a deterministic $N^d$-time, $N^{\epsilon}$-space random-access decider. No single-machine condition binds these \emph{two different} deciders, but a joint $(d,\epsilon)$ bound for the second decider is required. For any fixed $d>1$, the source-exact equivalent threshold is
\[
 \boxed{c<H(d):=\max\left\{F(d),C(d)\right\}},\qquad
 F(d)=1+\frac1d,\quad C(d)=\frac{2d-1}{d(d-1)}.
\]
\begin{lemma}[Exact branch crossover, \PROVED]
For $d>1$,
\[
 C(d)-F(d)=\frac{2-d}{d-1}.
\]
Thus $H(d)=C(d)$ for $1<d\le2$, and $H(d)=F(d)$ for $d\ge2$. In particular $H(d)>1$ for every finite $d>1$. At the \emph{extra} near-boundary seed $d=901/500=1.802$,
\[
 C(d)=\frac{651000}{361301}=1.801821749\ldots,
 \quad \bigl(C(d)-\tfrac12(d-1)\bigr)=1.400821749\ldots.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Bring fractions to the common denominator $d(d-1)$:
$C-F=[(2d-1)-(d+1)(d-1)]/[d(d-1)]=(2-d)/(d-1)$. Strict comparisons follow; exact rational arithmetic gives the displayed example.\qedhere
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Consequences for unknown $K$:} The curved branch alone falls below $1$ once $K\ge (3+\sqrt5)/2$, so it cannot by itself be combined with a sublinear (impossible) all-UNSAT verifier for such seeds. But the theorem's first branch still permits $c<1+1/K$ for any \emph{fixed finite} $K>2$. This is precisely why ARC180's arbitrary-$K$ budget used the first branch. The fact that some $K$ is achievable under $P=NP$ neither provides the required small-space deterministic SAT decider nor yields any cheaply complete verifier.

The weighted adaptive call inequality from ARC179/180 remains valid as \emph{accounting}, not coverage:
\[
 \sum_i m_i^d\le(\max_i m_i)^{d-1}\sum_i m_i,
 \quad c\le\max\{r,u+b+\lambda(d-1)\}.
\]
Here $m_i$ are sizes of actual same-$A$ child calls, $\lambda$ controls the maximum length, $b$ their total-volume exponent, $u$ the paid per-step access overhead, and $r$ all other work. A sound \emph{all-UNSAT} coverage and ancestor/peak-space proof is separately needed. If its claimed $c<1$, Theorem~\ref{thm:paths} independently refutes it, but for the interesting $d=1.802$ route $H(d)\approx1.802>1$, so this theorem alone does not rule out the desired exponent or fill the missing arrow.
