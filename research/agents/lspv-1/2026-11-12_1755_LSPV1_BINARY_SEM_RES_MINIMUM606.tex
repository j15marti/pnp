\section*{Definitions and exact quantifiers}
Let $F$ be \emph{any} finite CNF whose source occurrences are non-tautological signed clauses over finitely many Boolean variables. Sources are free and identified by their original occurrence IDs. Derived clauses are non-tautological, including the empty clause, and paid records form any finite acyclic chronological shared proof DAG with explicit parent identities. Reuse of a derived record is free; each distinct derived record costs one.
Define $S_{\rm RES}(F)$ as the minimum paid inference count of an ordinary oriented binary-Resolution refutation, and $S_{\rm BSEM}(F)$ as the minimum for a different reference calculus that allows a paid clause $C$ from two explicit prior parent clauses $A,B$ iff $A\wedge B\models C$. No ambient source oracle, hidden parent, context, definition, branch, GF(2) or foreign rule is allowed. For SAT inputs both minima are $+\infty$.
\begin{theorem}[Whole-shared-DAG binary-semantic conservative extension]
For every such $F$,
\[
\boxed{S_{\mathrm{BSEM}}(F)=S_{\mathrm{RES}}(F).}
\]
Moreover, any finite BSEM refutation with $q$ distinct paid records can be compiled, in time polynomial in its explicit proof length and input encoding, into an original-source Resolution refutation with at most $q$ distinct paid records, without unfolding shared ancestors or introducing weakening.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every strict Resolution inference is sound from exactly two parent clauses, so $S_{\mathrm{BSEM}}\le S_{\mathrm{RES}}$. For the reverse, topologically traverse an arbitrary BSEM DAG. Associate each source occurrence with its exact source clause. At every BSEM node $i$ with stated conclusion $C_i$, maintain an available \emph{strict Resolution} clause $D_i\subseteq C_i$. The already-translated parents $D_a\subseteq A$, $D_b\subseteq B$ satisfy $D_a\wedge D_b\models C_i$ by monotonicity of entailment. Apply the two-clause implicate characterization (LSPV-1/605, independently obtained in LSPV-2/585): either $D_a\subseteq C_i$ or $D_b\subseteq C_i$, in which case alias the existing parent without paying; or some non-tautological strict resolvent $R_p(D_a,D_b)\subseteq C_i$, in which case append exactly one new strict Resolution record, reusing the two previously available parents. No semantic search oracle is invoked; these alternatives are found by finite signed-clause inspection. Acyclicity and sharing survive because each alias or new inference uses prior nodes only. The final conclusion $C_t=\varnothing$ forces $D_t=\varnothing$. There are at most $q$ new paid Resolution records, and all remaining source leaves retain their original IDs. Hence $S_{\mathrm{RES}}\le S_{\mathrm{BSEM}}$, proving equality. For SAT $F$, soundness prohibits either contradiction.
\end{proof}
The argument is not a proof of checker acceptance if some historical checker mandates exact intermediate clauses, uses branch contexts, forbids source aliasing, or requires every paid identity to survive. No such requirement may be discarded in P2-D1/C3. In particular, this theorem does not transfer to GF(2), AND3, paid DEF, LOCAL-CONVERT, global RUP/RAT, or discharge.
