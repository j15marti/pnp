\section{Source, access, and backward formulation}
Consider valid proper signed 3CNF with exactly three distinct variables per unique clause and every original variable occurring exactly twice positively and twice negatively. Put $n$ for variables, $m$ for clauses, $N$ for the encoded bit length. Then $4n=3m$ and $m$ is a multiple of four. In the two-header/four-word clause encoding with $w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil$ bits per word, $N=(4m+2)w$. The total uniform deterministic word RAM charges parsing, source reads, comparisons, failed trials, all projection/resolution writes, source replay, witness lifting, and peak words.

An evaluator with shared NOT gates, two binary OR gates per clause and an AND tree has $n+3m-1=5n-1$ gates and $n+6m-2=9n-2$ gate-input references. These are evaluator counts, not SAT-solving circuit lower bounds. Going backward from output TRUE gives an exact independent-transversal condition: choose one true literal occurrence per clause without choosing both signs of any shared source variable. In the exact-$(2,2)$ setting the opposite-occurrence graph is a disjoint union of $n$ copies of $K_{2,2}$, with clause triples partitioning $4n=3m$ occurrence vertices. This is an equivalent representation, not a mandatory algorithmic artifact.

\section{Forced pairs and an exact conservation law}
A complementary pair is $(a\vee b\vee x),(a\vee b\vee\neg x)$ with identical signed $a,b$ on distinct variables. It contracts by an assignment-wise equivalence to $(a\vee b)$. Since every signed literal appears exactly twice and source clauses are unique, each clause has at most one complement partner (PAIRRIGID72). Let $p$ be the number of disjoint forced pairs, $\delta=m-2p$ the unmatched-clause defect, $B$ the $p$ binary contractions and $T$ the $\delta$ unmatched ternaries. Exactly $F\equiv B\wedge T$, with no auxiliary variables. Let $U=\operatorname{vars}(T)$.

For a variable $x$, let $t_x$ count pair pivots, $b_x^\pm$ count positive/negative occurrences in $B$, and $u_x^\pm$ their counts in $T$. Because every pivot pair contributes one occurrence of each sign and every common binary literal was doubled in its source pair,
\[
t_x+2b_x^++u_x^+=2,\qquad t_x+2b_x^-+u_x^-=2.\tag{1}
\]
\begin{lemma}[Exact degree bound]
If $x\notin U$, it appears either zero times or exactly once with each sign in $B$. If $x\in U$, it appears at most once in $B$; any such occurrence entails two unmatched occurrences of the opposite sign in $T$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} If both unmatched counts vanish, (1) forces $t_x=0$ or $2$, hence $(b_x^+,b_x^-)=(1,1)$ or $(0,0)$. Otherwise $t_x=0$ allows at most one of $b_x^+,b_x^-$ to be one, while $t_x=1$ forces both to vanish. If there is one binary occurrence, (1) forces two unmatched occurrences with its opposite sign. \end{proof}

\section{PAIRPATH73 projection theorem}
Make one graph vertex per contracted binary clause and connect the two signed occurrences of every $x\notin U$ that survives $B$. The edge labels are the original variables. The remaining clause literal sockets terminate on $U$. Each clause vertex has exactly two sockets. The graph components are paths with two terminal sockets or cycles with none, with parallel edges allowed. The two terminal source variables of a path are distinct.

\begin{theorem}[Exact linear defect kernel; PROVED/EXACT]
There is a uniform $O(m\log(m+2))$-word algorithm producing $K(F)$ with precisely $\delta$ original unmatched ternaries, at most
\[
P\le \left\lfloor\frac{3\delta}{4}\right\rfloor
\]
binary clauses, and at most $3\delta$ source boundary variables, such that
\[
\forall u\in\{0,1\}^{U}:\quad K(F)(u)\iff \exists y\in\{0,1\}^{[n]\setminus U} F(u,y).
\]
The $P$ projected binaries are pairwise variable-disjoint. Therefore $|K(F)|\le\lfloor 7\delta/4\rfloor$, with no fresh variables. A satisfying kernel assignment is lifted to an original source witness in $O(m)$ time with one complete source replay.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For a path, repeated exact existential resolution
\[
\exists x\,[(A\vee x)\wedge(\neg x\vee B)]\equiv(A\vee B)
\]
removes its internal variables and leaves the binary clause on its two boundary endpoint literals. A cycle similarly ends in a tautology and projects to TRUE. The components share no internal variable, so the conjunction of these projections is exact for every fixed assignment to $U$. Each path uses two distinct endpoint variables, and each such variable consumes two unmatched occurrences by the degree lemma. Thus $4P\le 3\delta$. Different paths cannot reuse an endpoint. Source validation, sorting, matching and graph traversal take $O(m\log(m+2))$ word operations. Reconstruction traverses paths/cycles and checks the source.
\end{proof}
The corresponding syntactic residual of this \emph{declared} projection is $d=n-|U|$, $r=n-d=|U|\le3\delta$, and $v=n/r$ only if $r>0$. It does not lower-bound arbitrary decider time.

\section{Falsifiable amortization lemma}
Fix the explicit path-normalization calculus $\mathcal C_{\rm path}$, whose primitive is source-labelled resolution of the two binary clauses containing opposite occurrences of one nonboundary variable. Tautological resolvents are discharged. Let $c$ be the number of cycle components of the contracted binary graph and $P$ its path components. The independently defined potential is
\[
\Phi=p-P-c=\sum_{\text{components }C}(|C|-1).
\]
\begin{theorem}[Exact charged resolution cost; PROVED/EXACT]
Every complete $\mathcal C_{\rm path}$ reduction requires exactly $R=p-P-c$ primitive resolution inferences, at most $R$ emitted resolvent records and one tautology discharge per cycle. Each primitive decreases the live potential by one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A path of $h$ binary clauses needs $h-1$ internal-variable resolutions to leave one boundary binary clause. A cycle of $h\ge2$ binary clauses needs $h-1$ resolutions, ending with the discharge of a tautology. The sum is $p-P-c$. These are explicit charged operations in $\mathcal C_{\rm path}$, not operations demanded of any unrestricted SAT decider.
\end{proof}

\section{Constructive upper bound and tiny-defect criterion}
\begin{theorem}[Defect-parameterized decider; PROVED/EXACT]
Valid signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3CNF can be decided in
\[
O\bigl(m\log(m+2)+2^\delta(\delta+1)+m\bigr)
\]
word operations and $O(n+m)$ reusable words.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First construct the kernel. Select one source variable from each of the $\delta$ residual ternaries and enumerate at most $2^\delta$ assignments to the distinct selections. Every resulting kernel branch is a 2CNF on $O(\delta)$ variables and clauses and is decided by SCC. Charge every failed branch, source validation and representation. If any branch is SAT, lift its witness once via the original binary path/cycle constraints and replay the original source.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Small-defect guaranteed SAT; PROVED/EXACT]
If $\delta+2P<8$, the source is SAT and conditional expectations construct a witness in polynomial time. In particular every valid source with $\delta\in\{0,2\}$ is SAT; every UNSAT source has $\delta\ge4$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A uniform random kernel assignment falsifies a proper ternary with probability $1/8$ and a proper binary with probability $1/4$. Hence its expected number of falsified clauses is $\delta/8+P/4<1$. Successively fix variables without increasing this expectation; the final integer number of false clauses must equal zero. Since $m$ is divisible by four and $\delta=m-2p$, $\delta$ is even. If $\delta=2$, the projection bound gives $P\le1$, so $\delta+2P\le4$.
\end{proof}

\section{Concrete source and computational controls}
An eight-clause, six-variable valid defect-two source is
\[
\begin{split}
&(a\vee b\vee x)(a\vee b\vee\neg x)(\neg b\vee c\vee y)
(\neg b\vee c\vee\neg y)\\
&\quad(\neg c\vee\neg a\vee z)(\neg c\vee\neg a\vee\neg z)
(x\vee y\vee z)(\neg x\vee\neg y\vee\neg z).
\end{split}
\]
Its six paired clauses contract to a binary cycle that projects to TRUE, while the last two unmatched clauses are satisfiable. The separate length-$q$ implication ladder remains UNSAT exactly when all $q$ edge orientations are positive. Every atomic orientation must be read on that input, but SCC decides it in linear time.

The standard-library audit used seed 202610232130 and checked 1,179 balanced sources, 1,103 full existential boundary-projection comparisons, 1,105,248 brute assignment evaluations, 1,082,925 kernel assignment checks, 110 replayed resolution inferences and 222 local resolution identities, plus 240 independent 2SAT and two UNSAT Horn/XOR controls. \section{Uncovered classes and the first missing implication}
All total uniform deterministic exact deciders remain uncovered by the resolution amortization beyond $\mathcal C_{\rm path}$. A solver can use source retention, direct decision, affine elimination, Horn or SCC reasoning, compact symbolic transformations, caching, learning, component decomposition or lazy source replay. It need not emit a DNNF, resolution refutation, complete witness relation, or backward path table. The mandatory one-bit SAT interface has only two output classes. Neither representation width nor a tautological cost ledger implies a time lower bound.

For the fixed NP-complete signed exact-$(2,2)$ language, the universal goal remains
\[
\forall A\;\forall c>0\;\forall N_0\;\exists F_A:
|F_A|\ge N_0\ \land\ T_A(F_A)>|F_A|^c.
\]
This is OPEN. The first missing implication is a cost-preserving, all-decider extraction or an independently proved superpolynomial alternative continuation bound on some NP-complete input family.
