\section{Exact source, objectives, and falsifiable mechanisms}
The fixed NP-complete source language is valid proper, duplicate-free signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF. Each original source variable occurs twice positively and twice negatively, so $4n=3m$. The signed word-RAM encoding has two header words and four words per clause, width $w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil$ and encoded bit length $N=(4m+2)w$. Full algorithms charge parsing, source reads, discovery, preprocessing, validation, failed attempts, construction, branching, replay, output, and peak working space. The separate sign-probe model supplies a fixed unsigned support and its legal exact-occurrence promise, and charges every addressed sign-field read. Its source identification objective is not SAT decision. The three mechanisms: (M1) amplify arbitrary-distance legal sign changes to superlinear acquisition, refuted by a global certificate ceiling; (M2) distinguish rich source or solution relations from the root decision, established exactly below; (M3) retain both SAT/UNSAT labels while multiplying arbitrary sign fibers, conditional on the previously verified Role-4 source pair. No expensive alternative for every exact SAT decider is assumed.
\section{Global arbitrary-perturbation certificate ceiling}
Fix an unsigned clause-support structure. At the four occurrence positions of variable $v$, a valid signed source chooses exactly two positive positions, a member of $K_v=\binom{[4]}2$, with $|K_v|=6$.
\begin{theorem}[Global fixed-input acquisition ceiling, PROVED/EXACT]
For each valid signed exact-$(2,2)$ source $F$, its SAT-label certificate complexity against \emph{all} legal same-support signings, including signings at arbitrary Hamming distance, satisfies
\[
C_{\rm SAT}(F)\le C_{\rm id}(F)\le 2n=\frac{3m}{2}=O(N/\log N).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Query the two occurrence fields at which each variable is positive in $F$. Any other legal signing agreeing on these positions has already exhausted that variable's two-positive quota, forcing the two remaining fields negative. Therefore it agrees with $F$ at all $4n$ source occurrences, and cannot have an opposite SAT label. The $2n$ fields depend on the chosen $F$, so this is a fixed-input certificate bound, not an adaptive algorithm which knows their locations for free.
\end{proof}
This strictly extends the radius-one swap-star input-read bound of SWAP-STAR-CEILING104 to arbitrary legal perturbations. It does not upper-bound full-input validation, root-decision time, or the uniform sign-query decision-tree depth.
\section{An independent product signing family and exact amortization}
For one block use the unsigned octahedral support family
\[
\mathcal O=\{\{x_i,x_j,x_k\}:i\in\{1,2\},\,j\in\{3,4\},\,k\in\{5,6\}\}.
\]
There are eight different proper ternary supports and six named variables, each occurring four times. Independently choose two positive occurrences per variable. For $t$ disjoint blocks,
\[
n=6t,\quad m=8t,\quad|\mathcal F_t|=6^{6t},\quad
N=(32t+2)\lceil\log_2(64(14t+1))\rceil.
\]
Every one of these signings remains a valid unique-clause exact-$(2,2)$ source, since the unsigned supports are all distinct.
\begin{theorem}[Sharp information-service separation, PROVED/EXACT]
On the complete product fiber $\mathcal F_t$, each fixed input has minimum identifying sign certificate size $2n$, while any deterministic uniform sign-query decision tree identifying \emph{all} inputs has worst-case depth exactly $3n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For one variable, one queried field is insufficient; two queried fields with opposite signs leave two allowed completions. Any certificate therefore requires two equal-sign queries, and the two positive fields are sufficient. Independence adds across $n$ variables. A uniform identification tree queries three of the four signs per variable; the last follows from the quota, establishing depth at most $3n$.
For the matching lower bound, an adversary answers the first query for a variable arbitrarily and the second with the opposite polarity. Consistent local candidates then number $6,3,2,1$ after zero, one, two, three distinct queries. The noncircular potential
\[
\Phi(R)=\sum_{v=1}^n\left\lceil\log_2|K_v(R)|\right\rceil
\]
starts at $3n$, must reach zero for complete identification, and decreases by at most one for each paid addressed sign-field read along the adversarial branch. Repeated reads decrease it by zero. The answer sequence always extends to a legal source. Hence $3n$ reads are necessary.
\end{proof}
The potential is an exact \emph{restricted} charged amortization: it lower-bounds only full source identification under that query service. The root SAT label is a different objective.
\section{Constant SAT label, exponentially distinct solution relations}
\begin{theorem}[Octahedral root collapse, PROVED/EXACT]
Every signed octahedral block is satisfiable, irrespective of its signing. Thus every member of $\mathcal F_t$ is satisfiable. Recognition and a witness take $O(N/w)$ word operations and $O(n+m)$ live words. On the signed-fiber promise the SAT-decision sign-query tree has depth zero, although full source identification costs $3n$. At least $2^t$ different satisfying-assignment relations occur in $\mathcal F_t$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A proper width-three clause fails with probability $1/8$ under uniform independent source bits. Among the octahedron faces choose opposite disjoint-variable supports, for example $(x_1,x_3,x_5)$ and $(x_2,x_4,x_6)$. Their failure events are independent and intersect with probability $1/64$. Including this overlap in the union bound on the eight failure events yields
\[
\Pr[\mathrm{any\ clause\ fails}]
\le 8(1/8)-1/64=63/64<1.
\]
Hence at least one assignment satisfies every block. Disjoint blocks can be satisfied independently. A uniform source recognizer checks canonical supports, exhaustively tests the $64$ local assignments per block, emits a local witness, and rereads the original to verify it. Every failed local test and emitted witness bit is charged.
For distinct solution relations, fix a legal signing $\sigma$ and a satisfying assignment $a$. On a selected octahedron face, independently respecify the three incident variables' legal two-positive sign patterns so its literals are all false under $a$, producing another valid signing $\sigma'$. Then $a\in\mathrm{Sol}(\sigma)$ but $a\notin\mathrm{Sol}(\sigma')$; both sets are nonempty. In $t$ disjoint blocks select $\sigma$ or $\sigma'$ independently. The $2^t$ resulting nonempty solution relations differ because their projections to each block recover the differing local relations.
\end{proof}
In contrast, the root SAT function is constant one on $\mathcal F_t$. This is an exact counterexample to inferring compulsory decision work from the number of different source signings or complete satisfying relations.
\section{Both-label hostile control and semantic barriers}
Role 4's TWO-SIGN-SWAP64 gives an imported original proper signed exact-$(2,2)$ UNSAT source $U$ and SAT source $V$, sharing all unsigned supports and differing at just two signs. Its original 198-variable 264-clause UNSAT certificate was checked by Role 2 previously but has not been rerun in this report. On the \emph{promised} disjoint products $U\wedge H,V\wedge H$ with $H\in\mathcal F_t$, one designated differing sign read distinguishes the labels while $H$ ranges over $6^{6t}$ signed variants. No unconditional new UNSAT certificate or general zero-query both-label decider is claimed. Full recognition and validation cost $O(N/w)$; outside the promise a total exact SAT fallback is required.
Backward from TRUE through the AND of 3-literal OR clauses, SAT is equivalent to selecting a nonconflicting true literal occurrence from each clause while preserving shared variable identities. In exact-$(2,2)$ the complementary occurrence graph is a disjoint union of $n$ copies of $K_{2,2}$. This is a correct optional independent-transversal representation, not a mandated output of a SAT decider. In the shared NOT1 / OR2 / AND2 assignment evaluator, the counts are $n+3m-1$ gates and $n+6m-2$ gate-input wires. They are not SAT-solving circuit lower bounds. A one-bit decider can use SCC, Horn, Gaussian elimination, components, learning, caching, source replay, recognition, or direct decision without constructing any full relation.
