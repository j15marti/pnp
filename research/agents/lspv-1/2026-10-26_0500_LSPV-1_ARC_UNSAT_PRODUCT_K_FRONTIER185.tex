\section{State, precise quantifiers and four preregistered falsifiers}
Let $E$ be the simultaneous achievable set of pairs $(d,s)$ for which there exists ONE fixed uniform deterministic total exact ordinary-SAT decider $A$ on every physical $N$-bit ordinary encoding with $T_A(N)\le N^{d+o(1)}$ and writable/index space $S_A(N)\le N^{s+o(1)}$ in the indexed-read-only-input random-access Turing model of Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas (FLMV). The quantifier is $(\exists A)(\forall N)(\forall F)$, not a separate $A$ per input or length. Under $P=NP$ some unknown finite integer diagonal $(K,K)$ is achievable. It need not be $K=901/500$, nor have subpolynomial space. An alternative theorem hypothesis providing $d=901/500$ and negligible space is explicitly EXTRA. The strict FLMV sufficient rectangle remains
\[
t<\frac{241}{200},\qquad s<\frac{11642479}{23232400};\qquad
\beta(c)=\frac12\left(\frac{c+2}{c^2}-c\right).
\]
The earlier universal $t+s<2\cos(\pi/7)$ interpretation is \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}.

\section{Exact primary theorem hypotheses and scope}
Van Melkebeek's survey \cite[Theorem~1.3, p.~2]{vm2007} gives, for constants $c,d$ satisfying
\[
(c-1)d<1\quad\mathbf{OR}\quad cd(d-1)-2d+1<0,
\]
that SAT cannot simultaneously have (i) a co-nondeterministic random-access algorithm in time $n^c$, and (ii) a deterministic random-access algorithm in time $n^d$ and space $n^e$ for the specified positive $e$. The \emph{same deterministic algorithm} achieves both time and space in premise (ii). This is not FLMV's single-deterministic-machine corollary, nor the separate Theorem~6.3 about full-input nonuniform SAT decision circuits and small-space $\Sigma_2\SAT$ \cite{vm2007,flmv2005}. Williams' $2\cos(\pi/7)$ exponent requires subpolynomial space, and no Bathie--Williams formula-depth hypothesis is invoked here. We import no unverified circuit-to-FLMV machine resource identity.
For $K>1$ the two-algorithm time frontier is
\[
H(K)=\max\left\{1+\frac1K,\ \frac{2K-1}{K(K-1)}\right\},
\qquad H(K)=1+\frac1K\quad(K\ge2).
\tag{1}
\]
To apply the theorem from a calculated upper bound $c_0$, one needs some $c$ satisfying $c_0<c<H(K)$, together with the separate small-space deterministic premise. Equality at the frontier is insufficient.

\section{An actual signed exact-$(2,2)$ both-label macro-pair}
Start with the prior project fixed source $W$ with fifteen variables and twenty distinct proper signed ternaries \cite{arc184}. The complete clause list is an immutable literal constant in the reproducer. An independent exhaustive check of all $2^{15}=32768$ assignments gives $\#\SAT(W)=0$. Construct $V$ by switching the sign of variable $1$ in exactly two source locations:
\[
(-x_1\vee x_3\vee\neg x_{14})\mapsto(x_1\vee x_3\vee\neg x_{14}),
\quad
(x_1\vee x_7\vee x_{13})\mapsto(\neg x_1\vee x_7\vee x_{13}).
\]
The two occurrences swapped are one negative and one positive occurrence of $x_1$. Every signed occurrence quota $(2,2)$, every three-distinct-variable clause, the absolute support list, the twenty-clause count, and the physical decimal-identifier serialization length are unchanged. The all-zero assignment satisfies $V$; exhaustive verification counts 216 models. Both $W$ and $V$ are \emph{proper signed} exact-$(2,2)$, not necessarily clause-monotone. A separate clause-monotone six-variable eight-clause $Y$ with twenty models is a positive grammar control. These fixed-pair witness/model claims are \textbf{COMPUTATIONAL}, with a full enumerator and source supplied.

Let $B_0=W$, $B_1=V$, all blocks be independently renamed, and let $b\in\{0,1\}^q$:
\[
P_{q,b}=\bigwedge_{i=0}^{q-1} B_{b_i}(x_{15i+1},\ldots,x_{15i+15}).\tag{2}
\]
Every $P_{q,b}$ is proper signed exact-$(2,2)$, with $n=15q$ and $m=20q$. The same $q$ and variable-identifier schedule gives the same complete physical encoding length for all bit vectors $b$, and
\[
\boxed{\SAT(P_{q,b})\iff\bigwedge_i b_i=1,
\qquad\UNSAT(P_{q,b})\iff\exists i\ (b_i=0).}\tag{3}
\]
The second identity remains true when an arbitrary fixed uniform exact all-input SAT decider $A$ is used to determine the actual SAT status of an arbitrary independently represented block; it is a property of the conjunction and the disjoint variable scopes, not of the chosen $A$.

\begin{theorem}[One-negative-block verifier, exact both-label polarity]\label{th:negative}
For a canonical physical input encoding of a disjoint-block conjunction $F=\bigwedge_{i<q}F_i$, with authenticated, separately encoded original block scopes, a proof record containing one valid $j<q$ is a sound and complete UNSAT certificate \emph{relative to a fixed exact deterministic SAT decider $A$} when verifier $V_A(F,j)$ parses and validates the partition and accepts exactly if $A(F_j)=\UNSAT$. Every malformed record rejects. Consequently,
\[
F\in\UNSAT\iff\exists j<q\;V_A(F,j)=1,
\qquad
F\in\SAT\iff\forall j<q\;V_A(F,j)=0.\tag{4}
\]
The branchwise complement is a sound co-nondeterministic SAT machine for this scoped PRODUCT language, subject to a uniform fixed per-branch clock. No deterministic constructor of $j$ is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Disjoint block scopes make the set of satisfying assignments the Cartesian product of the individual blocks' model sets. That product is nonempty if and only if all sets are nonempty. Therefore if $F$ is UNSAT some block has no model, and that index is accepted by the exact guest. If $F$ is SAT every block has a model, so no index can be accepted. A parser that rejects malformed indices and malformed block delimiters prevents a forged witness. A common polynomial time clock bounds all branches, including rejecting and malformed ones. Complementing each branch yields universal acceptance precisely on the SAT inputs. Output-to-SAT decision circuits, guessed negative answers, and independent certificates for arbitrary overlapping modules are not involved.
\end{proof}

Group each consecutive pair of blocks into a real first-level ordinary CNF child, then extract its two individually renamed ordinary CNF grandchildren. Because the grouping and renaming preserve complete source clauses, all three levels retain the signed exact-$(2,2)$ grammar and both-label identities (3)--(4). This is genuine recursion in the product subclass, but not a guarantee of universal shrinking SAT descendants.

\section{Uniform resource arithmetic at arbitrary polynomial exponent}
\begin{theorem}[Restricted coNTIME witness and all-child frontier]\label{th:k}
Fix one total uniform exact ordinary-SAT decider $A$ with $T_A(m)\le m^{K+o(1)}$ and some jointly achieved writable/index space bound; let $K>1$ be a fixed, initially unknown constant. Suppose an independently valid family of disjoint-block products has complete input length $N$, every block encoded in at most $N^{\lambda+o(1)}$ bits, a total authenticating parser and index policy costing $N^{1+o(1)}$, and a fixed uniform input-service implementation charging at most $N^{u+o(1)}$ per guest bit probe. Then a uniformly clocked co-nondeterministic SAT machine on that \emph{restricted product language} has time
\[
T_{\mathrm{coND}}(N)\le N^{\max\{1,u+\lambda K\}+o(1)}.\tag{5}
\]
If instead one uniform deterministic policy calls $A$ sequentially on $q=N^{1-\lambda+o(1)}$ blocks each of length $N^{\lambda+o(1)}$, with fully charged $N^{1+o(1)}$ other work, its conservative all-child time \emph{upper certificate} is
\[
c_{\mathrm{all}}\le\max\{1,1+\lambda(K-1)\}=1+\lambda(K-1),\tag{6}
\]
provided guest generation overhead is subpolynomial and there is no other larger route cost. For the upper certificate (6) alone to imply the strict necessary inequality $c_{\mathrm{all}}<H(K)$, its exact symbolic requirement is
\[
\boxed{\lambda<\frac{H(K)-1}{K-1},\quad\text{thus for $K\ge2$,}\quad
\lambda<\frac1{K(K-1)}.}\tag{7}
\]
The word ``necessary'' in (7) applies ONLY to the ability to certify strict improvement using the coarse envelope (6); it is not a lower bound on actual run times and not a necessary condition on every adaptive certificate grammar.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A verified UNSAT certificate specifies one child, so a single guest run has at most $N^{\lambda K+o(1)}$ simulated operations. Each step induces at most one virtual input probe, whose source reads, address decoding, index arithmetic and rereads are paid by $N^u$; add the authenticated parser and all fixed record costs. Thus (5). The proof index is $O(\log q)$ bits, and the predetermined uniform clock covers failed guesses and malformed proofs. The complement has the same worst-branch time.
For (6), guest costs sum to $q(N^\lambda)^K=N^{1-\lambda+K\lambda+o(1)}$. This is an upper bound, never a generally matching lower bound: $A$ may halt early, the policy may discover shortcut structure, share work, or avoid most calls. Solving the strict upper-envelope inequality $1+\lambda(K-1)<H(K)$ yields (7). Direct algebra gives the crossover $K=2$ and $H(K)=1+1/K$ for $K\ge2$. The separate deterministic small-space premise of van Melkebeek is neither proved nor inferred by this accounting.
\end{proof}

At the illustrative $K=901/500$, $H(K)=651000/361301\approx1.801821749732$, and the coarse-envelope threshold is $\lambda<(H(K)-1)/(K-1)\approx0.9997777428$. At $K=10$ the coarse threshold is $1/90$, whereas a \emph{sound one-UNSAT-block witness} with $\lambda=1/10$ would have $c_{\mathrm{coND}}=1$ given $u=0$ and charged linear parsing. But the all-block coarse envelope would report $1.9$. This comparison shows that SAT-vs-UNSAT certificate quantifiers can radically change the upper-bound ledger, not that all ordinary UNSAT admits that witness.

For general adaptive children $m_i$, the prior Role-4 inequality \cite{size129} remains
\[
\sum_i m_i^K\le\left(\max_i m_i\right)^{K-1}\sum_i m_i.\tag{8}
\]
It does not establish how the all-UNSAT certificates, signed-degree gadgets, child indices, or shared proof obligations are \emph{acquired}. A claimed small-space second algorithm requires a separate matched FLMV proof. Moreover an all-input near-linear-time ordinary-SAT reduction to a promise where a single small negative block always witnesses root UNSAT would already be an extraordinarily strong missing theorem; neither this easy recognizable family nor the present experiments supply it.
