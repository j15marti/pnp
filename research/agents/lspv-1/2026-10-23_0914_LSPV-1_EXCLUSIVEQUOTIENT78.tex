\section{Target and three falsifiable mechanisms}
We test (M1) whether every signed exact-$(2,2)$ source admits a polynomially computed satisfiability-preserving quotient with no binary clauses and at most two occurrences per literal; (M2) whether any UNSAT source can have fewer than twenty unpaired ternary clauses; and (M3) whether the quotient forces arbitrary exact deciders to materialize a rich relation or perform expensive direct work. M1 is PROVED/EXACT. M2 is falsified by M1 plus Zhang--Peitl--Szeider's minimum-UNSAT result. M3 remains OPEN.

The fixed NP-complete source language is proper, duplicate-free signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF, with each variable occurring twice positively and twice negatively. Thus $4n=3m$. In the two-header, four-word-per-clause encoding, $w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil$ and input bit length $N=(4m+2)w$. A uniform deterministic word RAM pays for source validation, reads, sorting, each failed shortcut, all source-labelled contractions and output writes, branch work, original-source replay and peak space. No mandatory enumeration is assumed.
\section{Established starting calculus}
Contract each unique source complementary pair
\[
(a\vee b\vee x)\wedge(a\vee b\vee\neg x)\equiv(a\vee b).
\]
PAIRRIGID72 proves partner uniqueness in this signed-regular language. If $p$ pairs are contracted, $\delta=m-2p$ original unmatched proper ternaries remain. PAIRPATH73 proves that exact existential projection of the contracted binary paths/cycles leaves
\[
K_F(U)=T(U)\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1}^{P}(\ell_i\vee r_i),\qquad
P\le\lfloor3\delta/4\rfloor,
\]
with \emph{pairwise disjoint} projected binary variables, where $U=\operatorname{vars}(T)$ and $F$ SAT iff $K_F$ SAT. Every endpoint of a projected binary occurs in $T$ only with its \emph{opposite} literal, twice. The source occurrence equations are
\[
t_x+2b_x^++u_x^+=2,\qquad t_x+2b_x^-+u_x^-=2,
\]
for pivot-pair count $t_x$, contracted binary literal counts $b_x^\pm$, and unmatched ternary counts $u_x^\pm$. These statements are existing project results, not new.
\section{A global exclusive-one quotient}
\begin{lemma}[Source-coherent canonicalization]
Every satisfying model of $K_F$ can be transformed to one in which \emph{exactly one} literal of every projected binary $(\ell_i\vee r_i)$ is true.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If both literals are true, flip the source variable of $\ell_i$ so $\ell_i$ becomes false. The other literal keeps the binary true. Every other occurrence of that variable in $T$ is $\neg\ell_i$, so flipping it from true to false can only satisfy more ternaries. Disjointness ensures that no other projected binary changes. Repeat independently.
\end{proof}
For every projected binary introduce one \emph{quotient coordinate} $z_i$ and substitute $\ell_i=z_i$, $r_i=\neg z_i$ throughout $T$. Retain one coordinate for each remaining boundary variable. Delete clauses that become tautologies; retain all other proper ternaries with source provenance. Denote the output by $Q_F$.
\begin{theorem}[Global signed-occurrence quotient; PROVED/EXACT]
For \emph{every} valid original signed exact-$(2,2)$ source $F$, there is a uniform polynomial-time source-only transformation to a proper all-ternary formula $Q_F$ such that
\[
\boxed{F\in\SAT\iff Q_F\in\SAT.}
\]
The quotient has $u\le\delta$ clauses, at most $3\delta/2$ coordinates, and each positive or negative quotient literal occurs at most twice. Its construction plus signed-source mapping costs $O(m\log(m+2))$ word operations and $O(n+m)$ reusable words. A satisfying quotient assignment can be lifted to one satisfying the \emph{original unchanged} formula in polynomial work.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The lemma permits restricting all projected binary pairs to exclusive-one states without changing satisfiability. The substitutions encode precisely those states, and all projected binaries become tautologically true, proving the equivalence. For either endpoint of a projected binary, the signed occurrence identities force exactly two unmatched ternary occurrences with polarity opposite to its binary literal. Paired endpoints map these four occurrences to exactly two positive and two negative occurrences of the shared quotient coordinate. A boundary variable in no binary has signed unmatched counts $(1,1)$ or $(2,2)$, so also respects the bound. Two distinct source coordinates map to one quotient coordinate only if they are the endpoints of one projected binary. If both appear in a ternary, the clause becomes a tautology and is deleted; otherwise the transformed clause still contains three distinct coordinates. Redundant identical clauses can be removed. Every retained coordinate appears at least twice in the original mapped ternaries, hence $2D\le3\delta$ before deletion.

To lift a satisfying quotient assignment, reverse the substitutions to the boundary, recover the original binary path/cycle variables through exact existential projection (or a charged 2-SAT SCC call), choose arbitrary source pivot values, and replay the original CNF. A valid original witness follows. Grouping clauses, traversing incidence graphs, and emitting a quotient take the claimed polynomial work. The transformation preserves \emph{existence of a satisfying assignment}, not necessarily the number of models.
\end{proof}
\section{New twenty-clause necessary UNSAT obstruction}
\begin{theorem}[Twenty-defect barrier; PROVED/EXACT from KNOWN THEOREM]
Every valid signed exact-$(2,2)$ source with $\delta\le18$ is SAT; every UNSAT such source has $\delta\ge20$. Recognition of the low-defect SAT subclass costs $O(m\log(m+2))$ word time after complete validation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Zhang, Peitl and Szeider, SAT 2024, Table~1, establish that an UNSAT proper $(3,2,2)$ formula, where each literal occurs \emph{at most} twice, has at least twenty clauses. Our quotient lies in exactly that class and has $u\le\delta$ clauses. Hence UNSAT $F$ implies UNSAT $Q_F$ and $20\le u\le\delta$. Also $\delta=m-2p$ is even because $m$ is divisible by four, so $\delta<20$ means $\delta\le18$. A SAT-only decision needs merely calculate $\delta$; a model can be reconstructed with the previously certified parameterized matching/SCC continuation and source replay. The published 20 bound is sharp in the \emph{containing} at-most-$(2,2)$ language; its sharpness inside our exact-occurrence quotient image is not proved.
\end{proof}
\section{Restricted amortization and universal-firewall audit}
Declare $\mathcal C_{\rm ex}$ to scan and validate every signed original clause, contract all canonical pairs, project binary paths/cycles, perform the exclusive-one quotient, and emit every distinct non-tautological quotient clause as a separate four-word record. If $u=|Q_F|$ and $j$ required output records are already written, the independently defined potential $\rho_j=u-j$ decreases by at most one per paid emitted record. Thus the \emph{declared explicit-output} task costs at least $m$ original record validations and $u$ quotient record writes, i.e. at least $4m+4u$ record-word input/output operations in this layout. This is a PROVED/EXACT \emph{restricted} amortization lemma, not a lower bound on arbitrary decider time.

The quotient dimension $D$ is not the syntactic residual $r=n-d$: identification of two boundary coordinates is not the same as eliminating an original variable. For PAIRPATH73's prior existential projection, $d=n-|U|$, $r=|U|$, and $v=n/r$ only for $r>0$. Rich relation size, SAT-root decision, and charged machine work remain distinct. Direct deciders, SCC, Horn, Gaussian elimination, caching, source retention, learning, component decomposition and deciding-first are not forced to emit $Q_F$. ROOT-LABEL-QUOTIENT85 proves the SAT root has just two semantic classes.

In backward Circuit-SAT, output TRUE through the AND of OR3 children is equivalent to picking a true literal occurrence per clause without choosing a complementary source pair. Signed exact-$(2,2)$ has independent opposite-occurrence $K_{2,2}$ blocks, but this is an \emph{optional} representation. A source assignment evaluator using shared NOT1/OR2/AND2 has $n+3m-1$ gates and $n+6m-2$ directed gate-input wires, not SAT-solving lower bounds.
