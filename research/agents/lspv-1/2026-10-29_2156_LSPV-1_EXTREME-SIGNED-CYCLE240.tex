\section{PROPOSE, SEARCH, BUILD}
The load-bearing question was whether a graph of opposite requirements among original signed clause occurrences characterizes the minimum number of clauses forced to have one or two true literals. The hypotheses were (1) mixed-sign pairs alone explain every extreme obstruction; (2) global extreme labels are exactly a signed graph parity system; (3) minimum middle clauses equal minimum signed-balance vertex deletion; (4) bounded-resolution preprocessing invariably lowers total search work.
Hypothesis (1) is falsified by an odd cycle, (2) and (3) are exact, and (4) is falsified for the tested DPLL implementation with charged preprocessing.

\section{Exact clause-indexed local extremes}
For a proper original width-three clause \(C_j\), let \(\nu_{ij}=0\) if occurrence \(x_{i,j}\) is positive and \(1\) if negative. The literal truth is \(a_i\oplus\nu_{ij}\). Local \(S_j\) means \emph{all} three literals true, requiring \(a_i=1\oplus\nu_{ij}\) on every occurrence; local \(U_j\) means all three false, requiring \(a_i=\nu_{ij}\). Inverting \(S_j\) gives \(U_j\), and inverting \(U_j\) gives \(S_j\). The occurrence \(x_{i,j}\) is not an independently assignable variable.
Write \(t_j(a)\in\{0,1,2,3\}\) and \(N_r(a)=|\{j:t_j(a)=r\}|\). Then
\[
t_j(\bar a)=3-t_j(a),\quad N_r(\bar a)=N_{3-r}(a),\quad
F(a)=1\Longleftrightarrow N_0(a)=0 .
\]
For flip set \(S\), \(t_j(a\oplus S)=t_j(a)+|S\cap L_j^0(a)|-|S\cap L_j^1(a)|\), where \(L_j^0,L_j^1\) are the originally false and true literal-variable sets. Every original occurrence's \(S,\neg S,U,\neg U\) target and global 0/1/intermediate flip trail is in the full raw artifact.
\begin{theorem}[Clause-indexed four-way extreme matrix]
Let \(I_=\) and \(I_{\ne}\) be the variables shared by original clauses \(C_j,C_k\) with respectively same and opposite literal sign. For local extremes \(S=\) all literals TRUE and \(U=\) all FALSE, \(SS,UU\) are feasible iff \(I_{\ne}=\varnothing\); \(SU,US\) are feasible iff \(I_==\varnothing\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Let \(e_j=1\) for \(S\), \(0\) for \(U\). Every shared variable must satisfy \(a_i=e_j\oplus\nu_{ij}=e_k\oplus\nu_{ik}\). Hence \(e_j\oplus e_k=\nu_{ij}\oplus\nu_{ik}\). The stated cases follow immediately, with all nonshared variables freely extendable. \end{proof}
If a pair shares both kinds of variables, \emph{all four} local extreme combinations are impossible, so for every complete assignment at least one of the two clauses has \(t=1\) or \(2\). Such a pair can still be simultaneously SAT.
\section{Global signed-cycle theorem}
Construct a clause-indexed \emph{multigraph} \(G_F\): for every shared variable \(x_i\) between \(C_j,C_k\), keep an edge with parity \(\delta_{jk}^{(i)}=\nu_{ij}\oplus\nu_{ik}\). Parallel edges are not discarded. A labeled graph is balanced if every cycle has edge-XOR zero (including contradictory two-edge cycles).
\begin{theorem}[Exact multi-clause extrema and middle-state minimum]
For every clause subset \(W\), a single legal complete variable assignment realizes every clause \(j\in W\) in either local \(S\) or \(U\) state iff \(G_F[W]\) is balanced. If \(\tau_{\rm bal}(G_F)\) is the minimum vertex-deletion count that balances this multigraph, then
\[
\boxed{\min_a\bigl(N_1(a)+N_2(a)\bigr)=\tau_{\rm bal}(G_F).}
\]
Let \(H_F\) be the ordinary opposite-polarity clause conflict graph of Kullmann. Then
\[
\boxed{\max_a N_3(a)=\max_a N_0(a)=\alpha(H_F).}
\]
All identities are for proper signed width-three CNF, whether SAT or UNSAT.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For designated extremes, every shared occurrence yields \(e_j\oplus e_k=\delta_{jk}^{(i)}\). These equations have a solution iff all cycle parities vanish. Given a solution, set \(a_i=e_j\oplus\nu_{ij}\) from any incident chosen clause, consistently by the equations, and extend unused variables arbitrarily.
For any \(a\), its set of extreme clauses induces a balanced graph, so its middle count is at least \(\tau_{\rm bal}\). Conversely, delete an attaining set of \(\tau_{\rm bal}\) vertices, reconstruct all remaining clause extremes by the preceding equivalence, and extend globally; at most those deleted clauses can be middle. The two inequalities give equality.
The set of fully true clauses is conflict-independent. Conversely, all literals of any conflict-independent clause set can be made true together since there is no shared variable with opposing signs. Thus its maximum size is \(\alpha(H_F)\). Complementation interchanges \(N_3,N_0\).
\end{proof}
These identities DO NOT decide SAT, bound arbitrary algorithm time, or imply efficient computation of \(\tau_{\rm bal}\) or \(\alpha\). The signed graph result is closely related to known graph balancing and clause conflict work.
\section{Explicit cases and resolution-proven cubes}
Take indexed originals \(C_0=(x_1\vee x_2\vee x_3)\), \(C_1=(x_1\vee x_2\vee\neg x_3)\), \(C_2=(\neg x_1\vee\neg x_2\vee\neg x_3)\).
For pair \((0,1)\), \(I_==\{1,2\}\), \(I_{\ne}=\{3\}\); its \(SS,SU,US,UU\) matrix is entirely infeasible. Nonetheless assignment \((1,0,0)\) satisfies all three, with true literal counts \((1,2,2)\). At \(0^3\) counts are \((0,1,3)\), at \(1^3\) counts are \((3,2,0)\); both endpoints fail. Resolving source \(C_0,C_1\) on \(x_3\) yields non-tautological \((x_1\vee x_2)\), whose false cube \(x_1=x_2=0\) cannot have a satisfying extension. The certificate retains source IDs and pivot, not just a guessed clause.
An independent nonpairwise control is \(C_0=(x_1\vee\neg x_3\vee x_4)\), \(C_1=(\neg x_1\vee x_2\vee x_5)\), \(C_2=(\neg x_2\vee x_3\vee x_6)\). Every pair's \(SU,US\) is feasible; the three opposite-parity overlaps force \(e_0\oplus e_1=e_1\oplus e_2=e_2\oplus e_0=1\), impossible around the cycle. Yet \(0^6\) and \(1^6\) both satisfy the formula, with \(N=(0,3,0,0)\) and \(N=(0,0,3,0)\) respectively. This falsifies pairwise-only detection and any deduction of UNSAT from a signed odd cycle.
