\section*{Conditional general erasure lemma}
\begin{theorem}
Let $D_i=\neg g\lor C_i$, $1\le i\le m$, and $(g)$ be
authentic signed source clauses. Suppose $r$ chronological legal
Resolution inferences derive $(\neg g)$ from the $D_i$,
with no $g$ pivot and each intermediate clause containing
$\neg g$. Introduce $m$ paid port inferences
$Q_i=\operatorname{Res}_{g}((g),D_i)=C_i$.
Rewire each old $D_i$ ancestor to $Q_i$ and delete
the common $\neg g$ literal from every old internal
RES result, preserving all non-$g$ pivots and shared references.
The transformed DAG derives $\bot$ by $m+r$ paid
Resolution steps from the \emph{same original guarded source}.
Paid definition owners, if any, remain separately charged.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\textbf{Proof.} Source authenticity makes each
$Q_i$ legal. Because $p\ne g$ and both premises contain
$\neg g$ but not $g$,
\[
\operatorname{Res}_p(A\vee\neg g,B\vee\neg g)
=\operatorname{Res}_p(A,B)\vee\neg g.
\]
Induction over the chronological DAG preserves exact
signed pivots, parent orientation, resolvents and sharing.
The final $(\neg g)$ becomes the empty clause.
The costs count $m$ new paid ports and $r$ replayed paid
inferences. There is no oracle, weakening, substituted
SOURCE, auxiliary-free computation or hidden branch.
This is a certificate-construction lemma, not a lower bound. \qed
