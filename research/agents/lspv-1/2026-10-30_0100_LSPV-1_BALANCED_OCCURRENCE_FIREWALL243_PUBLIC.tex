\section{Original source indexed extreme assignments}
Let $C_j$ be an original proper width-three clause with sign bit $\nu_{ij}=0$ for positive and $1$ for negative occurrence $x_{i,j}$. Its local \emph{all literal TRUE} target $S_j$ requires $a_i=1\oplus\nu_{ij}$ for every original source occurrence, whereas its all literal FALSE target $U_j$ requires $a_i=\nu_{ij}$. Inversion swaps local $S_j,U_j$. The occurrences of any $x_i$ remain a single global Boolean variable. A disjunction is ordinarily satisfied when $\ge1$ literal is true, not only in $S$.
For original clauses $C_j,C_k$, mode $SS$ or $UU$ is realizable iff they have no opposite-signed common source variable; mode $SU$ or $US$ iff no same-signed common source variable. Sharing both kinds forbids all four modes, ensuring at least one middle state ($t=1$ or $2$) for any full assignment, without proving ordinary CNF UNSAT.
Every coherent complete assignment $a$, complement $\bar a$, and flip mask $M$ obey
\[
t_j(\bar a)=3-t_j(a),\quad N_r(\bar a)=N_{3-r}(a),\qquad
t_j(a\oplus M)=t_j(a)+|M\cap L^0_j(a)|-|M\cap L^1_j(a)|.
\]
The full raw source report separately records every original clause and occurrence local $S$, inverted $S\to U$, $U$, inverted $U\to S$, four indexed pair modes with same/opposite shared variables, actual coherent $0^n,1^n$, single/multiple flips and true-literal vectors. In contrast to hypothetical independent occurrence assignments, all enumerations respect shared variables.
\section{A first-order singleton-count rule, and its exact UP firewall}
For proper signed exact-(2,2)-3CNF on $n$ original variables, each has two positive and two negative occurrences and $m=4n/3$ original width-three clauses. Thus every coherent complete assignment, regardless of answer class, has
\[
\sum_j t_j(a)=2n=3m/2.
\]
Any satisfying assignment has $N_0=0$, so
\[
\boxed{N_1=m/2+N_3,\quad N_2=m/2-2N_3.}\tag{1}
\]
In particular it requires at least $m/2$ original clauses with precisely one true literal.
Fix a globally coherent original-variable partial assignment $\rho$ on $k$ variables. Let $c_j$ be its fixed-TRUE literal count and $u_j$ its unassigned literal count. Define $Z=|\{j:c_j=0\}|$, $H_2=|\{j:c_j\ge2\}|$, $H_3=|\{j:c_j=3\}|$. Each fixed variable has exactly two TRUE occurrences, so
\[
\boxed{H_2+H_3=\sum_j(c_j-1)_+=2k-m+Z.}\tag{2}
\]
Every SAT completion has $N_1\le m-H_2$ and $N_3\ge H_3$. Hence (1) makes $H_2+H_3\le m/2$ necessary, and the strict reverse inequality appears to reject an entire cube.
\begin{theorem}[Unit-propagation dominance, PROVED/EXACT]
If $H_2+H_3>m/2$, some original uncovered source clause has at most one free literal, so an original empty clause or unit clause already exists. After saturated original-source UP without contradiction, the counting condition can never yield any additional excluded partial cube.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If no original uncovered source clause is empty or unit, each of the $Z$ uncovered clauses has at least two unassigned literals. Since the $n-k$ remaining exact22 original variables have precisely four occurrences each, $2Z\le\sum_j u_j=4(n-k)$, whence $Z\le2(n-k)$. Substituting in (2),
$H_2+H_3\le2k-m+2(n-k)=2n-m=m/2$.
Contraposition gives the source-indexed empty/unit witness. An added counting pass following saturated UP cannot eliminate a search node that UP had not already eliminated.
\end{proof}
The same argument generalizes: whenever each original variable has exactly $r$ positive and $r$ negative original occurrences, any completion gives precisely $r$ further TRUE occurrences for each free variable. If $Z$ uncovered clauses exceed $ru$ for $u$ free variables, no completion can satisfy all of them, but absent a source empty/unit clause each uncovered clause contains at least two free occurrences and $2Z\le2ru$. Thus the balanced first-order repair-capacity rule is always dominated by UP, independent of clause width. This is not a lower bound on arbitrary SAT solvers.
\section{Equality gives an exact, but restricted, two-sided SCC cube classifier}
\begin{theorem}[UP-saturated equality, PROVED/EXACT]
For proper strict22 $F$ and coherent partial $\rho$ having neither an empty nor a unit original residual clause, $H_2+H_3=m/2$ implies every uncovered original clause has exactly two free literals, and every free occurrence belongs to an uncovered clause. Therefore $F|_\rho$ is a 2CNF, so the cube is impossible iff for some remaining variable $x$ there are two source-indexed binary implication paths $x\leadsto\neg x$ and $\neg x\leadsto x$ (equivalently, an SCC containing both literals).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Equation (2) at equality implies $Z=2(n-k)$. The $Z$ uncovered source clauses each need at least two free occurrences and together consume at least $2Z=4(n-k)$ free occurrences. This exactly exhausts the $4(n-k)$ total free original occurrences. Each uncovered width-three clause thus has exactly two free variables, and none can lie in any already fixed-TRUE clause. All remaining live clauses are binary. The 2-SAT SCC equivalence is standard. Clause reads, checking $\rho$, implication graph construction, SCC, two-path UNSAT certificate or full SAT witness and final verification are linear in word-RAM input length with bounded word indices; source acquisition and unsuccessful trigger checks must be charged in any larger adaptive solver.
\end{proof}
The converse to equality is false: a strict22 paired-$K_4$ example pinned at $x_3=x_5=0,x_6=1$ leaves a 2CNF but $H_2+H_3=1<m/2=4$.
Independently checked prior LSPV-3 CUBE9 source: pin $x_4=x_5=x_6=0,x_7=x_8=x_9=1$, with $m=12,k=6,Z=6,H_2=4,H_3=2$. No unit or empty clause remains, and the exact six original residual clauses, indexed $C_0,\ldots,C_5$, are
\[
(x_1\vee x_2),(x_2\vee x_3),(x_3\vee x_1),
(\neg x_1\vee\neg x_2),(\neg x_2\vee\neg x_3),(\neg x_3\vee\neg x_1).
\]
They imply both
\[
x_1\xrightarrow{C_3}\neg x_2\xrightarrow{C_1}x_3\xrightarrow{C_5}\neg x_1,\qquad
\neg x_1\xrightarrow{C_0}x_2\xrightarrow{C_4}\neg x_3\xrightarrow{C_2}x_1.
\]
Both endpoints for free variable $x_1$ are impossible, so the entire pinned cube is UNSAT. The source formula itself is SAT. These explicit opposing-demand paths are older 2SAT inference, not a general SAT resolution.
\section{Exact-one pair-compatibility firewall}
\begin{lemma}
For two proper ternary source clauses $C,D$, a coherent assignment with $t_C=t_D=1$ exists unless $C,D$ have identical three-variable support and differ in the signs of an odd number of occurrences. Consequently the pairwise exact-one incompatibility graph is a disjoint union over supports of complete bipartite even-sign/odd-sign graphs, and has independence number at least $\lceil m/2\rceil$. Thus the count necessity $N_1\ge m/2$ combined with root-level \emph{pairwise} exact-one incompatibilities can never prove UNSAT.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On identical support the parity of $t_C-t_D$ equals the number of flipped signs mod two, so two counts of one are possible exactly for even sign-distance; for distance two assign one switched literal TRUE, the other FALSE, the unchanged literal FALSE. Different supports share at most two variables; choose these so both clauses have at most one TRUE shared literal, then independently choose the private literals of each to reach exactly one. Each support block therefore has a complete bipartite conflict graph indexed by sign parity, whose larger part has at least half the vertices. Select larger parts across blocks.
\end{proof}
Pairwise-to-triple extrapolation is false: the original clauses
\[
(\neg x_1\vee\neg x_2\vee\neg x_3),\quad
(\neg x_1\vee\neg x_2\vee\neg x_4),\quad
(\neg x_1\vee x_3\vee x_4)
\]
are each pairwise simultaneously exactly-one-TRUE, but not all three jointly. If $\neg x_1=1$, first two force $x_3=x_4=1$ and third has three TRUE. If $\neg x_1=0$, first two imply $x_3=x_4$ and third $x_3\ne x_4$. Nevertheless their ordinary conjunction is SAT (e.g. $0000$). The separate original pair $(x_1\vee x_2\vee x_3)$, $(x_1\vee x_2\vee\neg x_3)$ yields the non-tautological resolvent $x_1\vee x_2$ and safely forbids the complete cube $x_1=x_2=0$.
