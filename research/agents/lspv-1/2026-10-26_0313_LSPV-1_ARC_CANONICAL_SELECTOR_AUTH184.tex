\section{Exact objects and theorem}
Let $F=C_1\wedge\cdots\wedge C_m$ be an encoded proper signed three-CNF with $n$ distinct active input variables, unique clauses when required, and physical bit length $N$. The signed exact-$(2,2)$ class requires two positive and two negative occurrences of each name; the smaller clause-monotone class also requires every clause to be all-positive or all-negative. No identification of these two classes is permitted. The selector mechanism also extends to arbitrary clause widths with gate count $O(L+m\log(m+2))$, where $L$ is the total number of literal occurrences; the theorem below states the sharper ternary case. Empty-conjunction and empty-clause cases have the evident sentinel conventions.

For an assignment $y\in\{0,1\}^n$, define the violation bit $v_j(y)=[C_j(y)=0]$ and the \emph{canonical first-conflict selector}
\[
J_F(y)=\begin{cases}
\min\{j: v_j(y)=1\},&\text{if a falsified clause exists},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}\tag{1}
\]
Define its local checked obligation
\[
\operatorname{ValidConflict}_F(y,j)=[1\le j\le m\;\wedge\;C_j(y)=0].\tag{2}
\]
Here $0$ is the designated \emph{failure} symbol, not a valid conflict proof. The verifier's proposed global UNSAT statement is
\[
\operatorname{TotalConflict}(F,J_F)
=\forall y\;\operatorname{ValidConflict}_F(y,J_F(y)).\tag{3}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Universal canonical circuit, PROVED/EXACT]\label{th:canonical}
There is a deterministic, syntax-directed, uniform constructor which, for every valid proper signed ternary $F$ of $m\ge1$ clauses, outputs a bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit $J_F$ of $O(m\log(m+2))$ gates and $\lceil\log_2(m+1)\rceil$ output bits. The constructor never calls a SAT decider and does not depend on the answer. For all assignments $y$,
\[
\operatorname{ValidConflict}_F(y,J_F(y))=1-F(y).\tag{4}
\]
Consequently
\[
\boxed{\operatorname{TotalConflict}(F,J_F)\iff F\in\UNSAT},\qquad
\boxed{\exists y\;\neg\operatorname{ValidConflict}_F(y,J_F(y))\iff F\in\SAT}.\tag{5}
\]
Under an indexed conventional encoding, this circuit has polynomially bounded physical description length $O(N\log^2(N+2))$, and its source-dependent construction can be fully charged in $N\operatorname{polylog}N$ time with polynomial writable storage. No FLMV sublinear-space constructor is claimed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compute each $v_j$ by a constant-size conjunction of the complements of the three source literals. Form prefix gates $p_0=0$ and $p_j=p_{j-1}\vee v_j$. Form one-hot first-violation gates $h_j=v_j\wedge\neg p_{j-1}$. For each output index bit $k$, take an OR of all $h_j$ whose binary index $j$ has bit $k$ set. The gates are acyclic, have fan-in at most two, and their total number is $O(m\log(m+2))$; the input literals of $F$ become hardwired connections to the variable-input nodes of $J_F$. If some clause is violated, exactly the smallest violated index has $h_j=1$; if none is violated all $h_j$ are zero, hence the output index is zero. The reported index therefore names a genuinely falsified clause exactly when $F(y)=0$. This proves (4), whose universal and existential quantification gives (5). Constructing the gate records and writing their pointers costs the stated explicit polynomial bounds; the argument does not assume free random access, a free circuit, or a predetermined input label.\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Canonical certificate authentication is decision-contaminated]\label{cor:contamination}
Even when the prover hands the verifier the \emph{correct}, syntactically canonical near-linear-size $J_F$, certifying that it is a total conflict selector is precisely the parent $\UNSAT$ problem. A violation query is the parent $\SAT$ predicate:
\[
\operatorname{Invalid}(F,J_F)
:=\exists y\,\neg\operatorname{ValidConflict}_F(y,J_F(y))
\equiv\exists y\,F(y).\tag{6}
\]
A strategy that authenticates (3) by calling its supplied all-input SAT solver $A$ on this equivalent parent-scale instance has not produced a strict child-size contraction. It incurs one charged parent-scale call with its original worst-case upper bound $N^{d+o(1)}$ and actual $S_A$ storage. The circuit $J_F$ is an \emph{assignment-to-violated-clause} map, neither a SAT decision circuit $C_{A,N}$ over formula encodings nor a Cook--Reckhow proof of $F$'s unsatisfiability \cite{cookreckhow1979}.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Equation (6) is (5) with its quantifiers expanded. A direct parent call simply evaluates $\SAT(F)$, possibly after linear/polylogarithmic encoding normalization. The time upper bound on that call is still the known $d$ exponent; one cannot assert a smaller bound from circuit size or from a guessed output label.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Restricted canonical validation hardness, KNOWN THEOREM/COROLLARY]\label{th:hard}
Let $\mathcal M_{2,2}$ denote D\"ocker's unique proper \emph{clause-monotone} exact-$(2,2)$ three-CNF class. The language
\[
L_{\mathrm{can}}=\{(F,\operatorname{enc}(J_F)):\ F\in\mathcal M_{2,2},\;
 \forall y\operatorname{ValidConflict}_F(y,J_F(y))\}
\tag{7}
\]
with noncanonical or malformed pairs rejected is coNP-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions. The analogous statement holds on the larger proper signed exact-$(2,2)$ class.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Membership in coNP follows because the constructor can be run in polynomial time, canonical gate-list equality is polynomial-time checkable, and an invalidating assignment $y$ is a polynomial-length NP witness for failure of (3). D\"ocker proved that SAT on $\mathcal M_{2,2}$ is NP-complete \cite{doecker2019}. Its complement is coNP-complete. Map each $F\in\mathcal M_{2,2}$ in polynomial time to $(F,J_F)$. By Theorem~\ref{th:canonical}, the image lies in $L_{\mathrm{can}}$ iff $F$ is UNSAT. The signed superset inherits this reduction. This proof establishes coNP-\emph{completeness}, not an unconditional superpolynomial lower bound or $P\ne NP$.\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No discrimination from finite-depth selector syntax]\label{cor:depth}
For any finite depth $h$ and any correctly parsed residual tree produced by assigning original variables (even with adaptive variable selection and both branches retained), every residual node $F_v$ admits its own near-linear gate circuit $J_{F_v}$. This statement is true when the root is SAT and when it is UNSAT. A putative UNSAT verifier that checks only the circuit construction identities at all generated nodes, without authenticating a terminal conflict claim or an equivalent globally valid derivation, accepts some SAT roots and is unsound. In particular two or more actual recursive levels do not remove the universal-totality obligation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every restriction remains a CNF with at most the original clause widths. Apply the explicit first-conflict construction independently at each realized node; neither a root nor a child answer is used. For any satisfiable root, follow any satisfying assignment down its consistent restriction path: its terminal residual remains satisfiable and has a correctly generated selector with zero output on that assignment. Hence the verifier has checked only identities that also hold on a SAT input. A sound verifier must additionally refute or otherwise reject that surviving satisfying path.\end{proof}

\section{Two-level semantics, input type, and quantitative FLMV firewall}
The experiment includes two actual original-variable restrictions on the W20 signed exact-$(2,2)$ UNSAT source and the Y8 clause-monotone exact-$(2,2)$ SAT control. For each two-bit assignment $\alpha$, the genuine residual $F\mid\alpha$ is serialized as a short CNF, its own fresh circuit $J_{F\mid\alpha}$ is built, and every remaining assignment is exhausted. The four W20 residuals are all UNSAT; the four Y8 residuals are all SAT. Both labels and the original-name semantic join are exact. The residuals contain shorter clauses and \emph{do not satisfy exact-$(2,2)$ degree quotas}: this is a negative eligibility firewall, not a claimed two-level ARC contractor. Existing R15/M22 properization schemes must be invoked with full cost if an actual exact-$(2,2)$ child machine is required.

If $d=901/500$ is \emph{separately} hypothesized, the curved two-algorithm frontier in van Melkebeek Theorem 1.3 \cite{vm2007} is
\[
 C(d)=\frac{2d-1}{d(d-1)}=1.8018217497\ldots<d=1.802.\tag{8}
\]
One invocation of $A$ on a parent-scale input has only the original upper bound $N^{d+o(1)}$, and so this strategy cannot \emph{certify} the required strict $c<C(d)$. For arbitrary $K$ from $P=NP$ there is even less basis for exponent shrinkage. Theorem 1.3 requires a co-nondeterministic SAT machine and a \emph{separate deterministic} SAT machine satisfying a simultaneous small-space bound, with $(c-1)d<1$ \emph{or} $cd(d-1)-2d+1<0$. Theorem 6.3 uses full SAT decision circuits and a small-space \emph{$\Sigma_2$SAT} machine, not $J_F$ and ordinary SAT alone. None of the stated hypotheses follows from short $J_F$ syntax. The FLMV paper \cite{flmv2005} supplies a model-matched joint lower bound, but does not convert the present selector into a favorable machine. Likewise, Pippenger--Fischer \cite{pf1979} addresses circuit simulation for particular tape models rather than a free constructor in the indexed-input FLMV model.

The weighted adaptive-query inequality
\[
\sum_i m_i^d\le \bigl(\max_i m_i\bigr)^{d-1}\sum_i m_i
\tag{9}
\]
is still mathematically correct for $d\ge1$, but no short authenticated $\UNSAT$ child list is produced by (1). A full-parent query must be paid at $m_i\ge N^{1-o(1)}$; peak writable/index space, source/child bit traffic including unchanged writes, acquisition, selector-output circuit construction/evaluation, cache identity, ancestor storage and any fallback all remain counted on the \emph{same} machine. Physical bit flips, bit-time and hardware wires/switches are different resources. The circuit here is explicitly built and evaluated; it is not an inexpensive stand-in for the decision circuit of $A$.
