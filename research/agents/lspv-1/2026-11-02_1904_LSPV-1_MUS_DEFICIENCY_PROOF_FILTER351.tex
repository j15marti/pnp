\section*{MUS stability with an explicit Hall proof}
\begin{theorem}[Stable deficiency of every clause-minimally UNSAT core]
If U is clause-minimally UNSAT then every proper source subset H has strictly smaller deficiency:
\[
\boxed{H\subsetneq U\implies\delta(H)<\delta(U),\qquad\delta^*(U)=\delta(U)\ge1.}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose H maximizing $\delta$ over all subsets of U. For every K subset of U minus H, maximality gives
\[
|K|\le|\mathrm{var}(H\cup K)\setminus\mathrm{var}(H)|.
\]
In the bipartite incidence graph with remaining clauses on the left and variables absent from H on the right, the inequality holds for \emph{every} subset of left vertices. Hall's marriage theorem supplies a matching from all remaining clauses to distinct new variables appearing in their respective clauses. If H were satisfiable, fix its satisfying values; assign each matched fresh variable to satisfy its own clause. Each of these assignments is independent of H and makes its designated clause true regardless of other literals. Then all clauses of U would be satisfied, contradiction. Thus H is UNSAT; minimality of U forces H=U. Since the same reasoning applies to any maximum-deficiency H, no proper H can tie the maximum. Finally, positive deficiency follows by choosing H empty in the same argument, or Tarsi's classical lemma.
\end{proof}
\noindent This is an elementary proof of a previously known stability property (also documented for variable-minimally UNSAT formulas). It is \emph{false} without minimality: F equal to $(x)\land(\neg x)\land(y)$ has full deficiency one and a proper contradictory subformula with the same deficiency.

\section*{A combined-calculus polynomial upper bound}
\noindent\textbf{KNOWN PRIMARY THEOREM.} Szeider \cite{Szeider2004} shows that maximum deficiency $k$ is a fixed-parameter SAT parameter, with $2^k\operatorname{poly}(v+m)$ decision time (reported $O(2^k v^3)$ in the declared combinatorial model), and an UNSAT branch that emits a \emph{regular binary Resolution refutation}. The number of printed proof nodes cannot exceed the time to emit them. Converting the syntax to our fixed source-indexed binary-RES format adds at most polynomial overhead. The theorem is restricted to formulas of a given maximum deficiency, not arbitrary SAT at polynomial time.
\begin{theorem}[Minimum-MUS-deficiency proof domination]
There are absolute constants C and a, independent of F and encoded bit length N, so every valid UNSAT F obeys
\[
\boxed{\mu(F)-1\ \le\ W_{R_{\mathrm{mix341}}}(F)\ \le\ C(N+1)^a2^{\kappa(F)},\quad N=|\mathrm{enc}(F)|.}
\]
For a minimally UNSAT U, this specializes to
\[
\boxed{|U|-1\le W_R(U)\le C(N+1)^a2^{|U|-|\mathrm{var}(U)|}.}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pick an MUS U contained in F minimizing $\delta(U)$. By the Hall theorem above its maximum deficiency equals $\delta(U)=\kappa(F)$. Apply Szeider's constructive regular Resolution algorithm to U. Its certificate contains at most $C(N+1)^a2^{\kappa(F)}$ charge-one binary RES nodes after polynomially paid parsing and proof-format conversion; its input has at most N encoded bits by the fixed deletion/renumbering convention. Every resulting inference is permitted by Rmix341 and by source-heredity also refutes F. This establishes the upper even after arbitrary mixed-R strategies are granted; free optimal selection can only improve it. The lower $\mu(F)-1$ is the previously established binary source-ancestry theorem (SOURCE-EXCESS-EULER349). For minimally UNSAT U, $\mu(U)=|U|$ and $\kappa(U)=\delta(U)$.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Necessary superlogarithmic deficiency among worst-case MUSes]
For any fixed c>0, every UNSAT input F with $\kappa(F)\le c\log_2(N+1)$ has a polynomial-in-N Rmix341 refutation:
\[
W_R(F)\le C(N+1)^{a+c}.
\]
Combining with the exact MUS worst-case quotient of LSPV-1 350, if $\overline W_R(N)$ is superpolynomial, then for each c>0 and N0 there exists N at least N0 and a maximizing MUS $U_N$ of encoded length at most N with
\[
\boxed{\delta(U_N)>c\log_2(N+1).}
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first claim substitutes the parameter restriction. Otherwise all sufficiently large maximizing MUSes would have deficiency at most c log N, so the maximum itself would be polynomial, contradicting the superpolynomial worst-case definition. The statement requires hard \emph{subsequences}, not every MUS or a single finite counterexample.
\end{proof}
This strengthens the prior $\mu(F)/\log N$ candidate filter: an arbitrarily large indispensable core of constant deficiency is \emph{always} Resolution-easy in the proof-size sense, even if it looks nonaffine, sign-sensitive or high-treewidth. It does not mean the minimum MUS is efficient to discover. High deficiency is necessary along an extremal sequence, emphatically \emph{not sufficient}; the full 3-variable blocker has deficiency five but a short proof. Odd Tseitin and the newer grid/guarded families also have well-understood short mixed proofs.
