\section{A sufficient constructive SAT certificate}
For a nonempty residual $R=F|_\rho$ with clauses of widths $1$ to $3$ on free original variables, write $U_j(a)$ and $S_j(a)$ for the all-false and all-true indicators, respectively. Define
\[
Z_0=\sum_j U_j,\quad Z_3=\sum_j S_j,\quad
\mu=\mathbb EZ_0=\mathbb EZ_3=\sum_j2^{-|C_j|},\quad
\Gamma=\mathbb E[Z_0Z_3].
\]
For original ordered clause indices $j\ne k$, the intersection $U_jS_k$ is a partial cube on at most six variables and is nonempty iff the pair is US-compatible. Thus
\[
\Gamma=\sum_{j\ne k:U_jS_k\ \mathrm{compatible}}2^{-|D_{jU,kS}|}.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Two-sided exact SAT criterion]
If $\Gamma<\max\{1,2\mu-1\}$, the residual $R$ is SAT. A deterministic indexed-RAM algorithm constructs a source-verified witness using $O(|V|m^2\operatorname{polylog}N+N)$ work and polynomial peak RAM space.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both integer-valued potentials
$G(a)=Z_0(a)Z_3(a)$ and
$H(a)=Z_0(a)Z_3(a)-Z_0(a)-Z_3(a)+2=(Z_0-1)(Z_3-1)+1$
are at least one whenever both $Z_0,Z_3\ge1$. Exact expectations are $\mathbb EG=\Gamma$ and $\mathbb EH=\Gamma-2\mu+2$. If either is below one, repeatedly assign each remaining source variable the value giving the smaller exact conditional expectation of that same potential. By averaging, final integer potential is below one. Therefore either $Z_0=0$ (the chosen assignment satisfies $R$) or $Z_3=0$ (the complement on free residual variables satisfies $R$). Replay every source clause and the retained pin $\rho$. Each conditional expectation is a sum of $O(m^2)$ compatible cubes of arity at most six, calculated by exact dyadic arithmetic. Input scans, both trial values, final reconstruction and witness verification are charged.
\end{proof}
A nine-clause, four-variable example with $0^n$ and $1^n$ both failing as SAT assignments has $\Gamma=72/64$, $\mu=72/64$ and $\mathbb EH=56/64<1$. Its original signed clause list is:
\[
(1,2,4),(1,2,-3),(-1,2,3),(-1,-3,-4),(2,3,-4),
(1,-3,4),(1,3,-4),(-2,3,4),(-1,3,-4).
\]
Here $H$ succeeds while $G$ does not meet its sufficient condition.

\section{Exact source-indexed UU forbidden-cube certificate}
Define second-order Bonferroni term
\[
B_2=\mu-\sum_{j<k:U_jU_k\ \mathrm{compatible}}2^{-|D_{jU,kU}|}
=\mathbb E[Z_0-\tbinom{Z_0}{2}].
\]
\begin{theorem}[Sharp second-order coverage and width cap]
$B_2(R)\le1$, with $B_2(R)=1$ if and only if every extension falsifies exactly one or two active clauses. Hence $B_2(F|_\rho)=1$ proves that the entire original partial source cube $\rho$ is impossible. For nonempty clauses of width at most three, a $B_2=1$ certificate has at most $m=16$ active clauses. This limit is attained by sixteen distinct clauses comprising all eight sign patterns on $(1,2,3)$ and all eight on $(1,2,4)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For every integer $z\ge0$, $\mathbf1_{z\ge1}\ge z-\binom z2$, with equality at $z=0,1,2$ and strict inequality if $z\ge3$. Thus $B_2\le\Pr(Z_0\ge1)\le1$, and equality forces $Z_0\in\{1,2\}$ on every complete extension. This is also sufficient. Consequently $\mu=\mathbb EZ_0\le2$ but every residual clause contributes at least $1/8$ to $\mu$, hence $m\le16$. In the stated sixteen-clause example, each assignment falsifies exactly two clauses.
\end{proof}
Source-parent proof example: original indexed
$C_0=(y\vee a\vee b),C_1=(y\vee a\vee\neg b),
C_2=(y\vee\neg a\vee b),C_3=(y\vee\neg a\vee\neg b)$.
Under $y=0$ the four residual binary clauses have incompatible UU targets with $\mu=B_2=1$, proving the entire $y=0$ cube impossible; saturated original unit propagation alone is silent. Standard three-step authenticated resolution gives $(C_0,C_1;b)\to(y\vee a)$, $(C_2,C_3;b)\to(y\vee\neg a)$, and their resolvent $(y)$. Resolution is known, not newly discovered.

\section{Strict exact-(2,2)-3CNF fixed-order root obstruction}
\begin{theorem}
For a proper signed strict exact-$(2,2)$-3CNF with $n$ original variables and $m=4n/3$ clauses,
\[
\Gamma\ge m(m-4)/64.
\]
For $m\ge16$ the direct unconditioned-root criterion $\Gamma<1$ or $\Gamma<2\mu-1=m/4-1$ cannot hold; for $m>16$, neither can $B_2=1$. This is \emph{not} a SAT hardness or algorithmic time-space lower bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Among $m(m-1)$ ordered distinct clause pairs, a US pair is incompatible only when there is a same-sign shared variable. Each source variable has two positive and two negative occurrences, yielding precisely four \emph{ordered} same-sign occurrence pair witnesses, for a total $4n=3m$. Thus at least $m(m-4)$ distinct ordered pairs are US-compatible. Each intersection fixes at most six source variables and has probability at least $1/64$. For $m\ge16$, $m(m-4)/64\ge\max\{1,m/4-1\}$, proving the claim. Conditional descendants are excluded from the assertion.
\end{proof}
The even-order $2h$ Bonferroni equality would require every assignment falsifies between 1 and $2h$ active clauses, and hence $m\le16h$; source intersections through order $2h$ are polynomial only when $h$ is fixed.
