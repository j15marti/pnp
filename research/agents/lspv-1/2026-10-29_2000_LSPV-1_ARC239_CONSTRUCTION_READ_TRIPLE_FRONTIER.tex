\section{PROPOSE: one missing lemma and four falsifiable routes}
The missing lemma was a simultaneous accounting relation between the cost of \emph{building} a reusable source summary, the number of persistent source-dependent bits, and worst-case subsequent access to the retained original source. ARC238 bounds $L,Q$ with unlimited offline acquisition; that model alone cannot charge preprocessing. The mechanisms audited were:
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{M1 (free offline omission).} A total nonlinear adaptive preprocessor could skip more than $Q$ source coordinates and nevertheless answer every later parity query using at most $Q$ raw probes. \textbf{FALSIFIED} by Theorem~\ref{th:adaptive}.
\item[] \textbf{M2 (fixed-read improvement).} Fixing the $p$ offline-acquired coordinates forces a stronger $L$--$Q$ bound on the $p$ observed source variables. \textbf{PROVED/EXACT} by Theorem~\ref{th:fixed}.
\item[] \textbf{M3 (Hamming continuation composes).} A sharp one-probe nonlinear-storage optimum on $p$ observed coordinates composes with all $b-p$ unobserved coordinates. \textbf{PROVED/EXACT}; the Hamming kernel construction itself precedes this report (ARC237).
\item[] \textbf{M4 (singleton information value is additive and nonnegative).} A target-entropy score assigns uniformly nonnegative realized value and summable initial labels. \textbf{FALSIFIED} by the XOR and rare-event controls in Section~\ref{sec:value}.
\end{description}

\section{General summary model and exact quantifiers}
The general SAT boundary interface is
\[
 F=A(X,Y)\land B(X,Z), \qquad f_A(x)=\exists y\,A(x,y).
\]
A two-stage algorithm receives $A$, performs preprocessing of time $P$ before learning $B$, retains an $L$-bit \emph{source-dependent} summary, and later solves its continuation with online time $R$, further source probes $Q$, peak writable space $S$, communication rounds $M$, physical head crossings $H$, and cumulative symbol changes $D$. The native machine and source-access contract must be declared. The original source may legally remain readable, or may be explicitly unavailable; discarding it cannot be silently assumed. A single joint solver seeing $(A,B)$ simultaneously has a different quantifier order.

We instantiate a source family with $a\in\F^b$ encoded by $b$ authenticated unary clauses $A_a(X)=\bigwedge_i(x_i=a_i)$ in fixed-width records of total physical size $N_A=\Theta(b\log(b+2))$. The future context is the authenticated parity equation $B_{w,t}:w\cdot X=t$; an XOR3-chain has a polynomial-size 3CNF encoding. Thus
\[
 \mathrm{SAT}(A_a\land B_{w,t})\iff w\cdot a=t.
\]
This is a typed unary/parity instance family, \emph{not} strict exact-$(2,2)$ signed 3CNF. The source bit $a_i$ is accessed through its named sign-field coordinate. In the indexed \emph{bit}-RAM, each sign-field probe costs one bit read plus separately charged index arithmetic. Public source-layout arithmetic, programs, and fixed Hamming matrices are independent of $a$. All persistent source-dependent caches, preprocessor transcript encodings, and retained auxiliary state are included in $L$; no free answer bit is supplied.

Let $E$ be any fixed deterministic offline procedure, allowed arbitrary \emph{adaptive} sign-field queries and nonlinear work, but making at most $p$ such reads on every input. It outputs exactly $L$ source-dependent bits. Subsequently the same fixed decoder, for every $a,w,t$, must return the correct parity using at most $Q$ further sign-field reads. Its computation may be nonlinear and its probes adaptive. Offline and online full computation and peak storage are separate charges. The source is unchanged between phases. This restricts only the named raw-sign-bit access primitive, not allowable workspace or algorithms.

\section{A source-acquisition lower bound valid for every adaptive preprocessor}
\begin{theorem}[Adaptive offline read coverage]\label{th:adaptive}
For every deterministic total offline/online pair in the stated model, irrespective of summary encoding and $L$,
\[
 \boxed{p+Q\ge b.}
\]
More precisely, for \emph{each} source $a$, if its actual offline execution reads $r(a)$ distinct sign fields, there exists an adversarial future parity mask $w_a$ such that the subsequent exact execution on that \emph{same} source reads at least $b-r(a)$ previously unread fields. Hence actual cumulative raw-source read count on that paired realization is at least $b$, even if the preprocessing is adaptive and nonlinear.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the complete deterministic offline transcript for a source $a$ and let $R$ be the set of distinct positions queried in that transcript, $|R|=r(a)\le p$. Every source vector agreeing with $a$ on $R$ follows the identical offline transcript and produces the identical retained summary: all future offline address choices and replies are unchanged. Hence the summary fiber contains the entire free subcube on $U=[b]\setminus R$. Choose $w_a$ to be the indicator of $U$. On the source $a$, if the online exact decoder stops without reading some coordinate $j\in U$, flip only that bit. The flipped source has the same offline summary and the same online probe answers, so the deterministic online transcript and returned answer are unchanged, but the requested parity flips. This contradicts exactness. Every coordinate in $U$ must therefore be probed online on that execution. Consequently $Q(a,w_a)\ge |U|=b-r(a)$, proving the same-realization claim and $p+Q\ge b$. Repeated source reads cannot help.\end{proof}
The theorem is independent of any target-value potential, Fourier argument, or assumed SAT difficulty. It is sharp as an acquisition \emph{necessary condition}: offline storing $p$ literal source bits, and later reading the other $b-p$ requested bits, gives $(p,L,Q)=(p,p,b-p)$. It does \emph{not} make every triple satisfying the inequality feasible. Without a declared offline/online separation, the theorem is simply an easy parity-reading property and provides no general SAT time lower bound.

\section{Fixed-read refinement and a three-coordinate frontier}
For the next theorem impose an \emph{additional explicit restriction}: the offline algorithm is source-dependent only through a predetermined, public set $R\subseteq[b]$ of exactly $p$ source coordinates. It may compute an arbitrary nonlinear $L$-bit summary $E(a_R)$, and it may retain the original source for subsequent reads. Let $U=[b]\setminus R$, $u=b-p$, and define
\[
 \V(p,q)=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(p,q)}\binom pj\quad(q\ge0),\qquad \V(p,q)=0\quad(q<0).
\]
\begin{theorem}[Fixed-read nonlinear triple frontier]\label{th:fixed}
Every deterministic exact encoder/decoder in this restricted fixed-read model satisfies
\[
 \boxed{Q\ge b-p},\qquad
 \boxed{2^L\,\V\bigl(p,Q-(b-p)\bigr)\ge2^p}.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \boxed{L\ge p-\left\lfloor\log_2\V\bigl(p,Q-b+p\bigr)\right\rfloor.}
\]
This holds for arbitrary nonlinear storage and adaptive online source probes, but the stronger $L$-bound is \emph{not} asserted for arbitrary adaptive offline choices of $R$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a nonempty summary fiber $F_s=\{a_R:E(a_R)=s\}\subseteq\F^p$. For every mask $v\in\F^p$, select the future full query $w=(v,1_U)$, namely all $u$ unprepared coordinates are queried in its parity. Along every exact online transcript, every coordinate of $U$ must be read, by the same single-bit flip argument as Theorem~\ref{th:adaptive}; the summary is independent of $a_U$. Fix $a_U=0$ and simplify these forced source-bit observations to constants. The remaining decoder is a depth-at-most $q=Q-u$ adaptive coordinate decision tree on $R$, computing the character $(-1)^{v\cdot a_R}$ for all $a_R\in F_s$.

Each depth-$q$ decision-tree output is a real linear combination of indicators of its leaves, each leaf indicator being a product of at most $q$ terms $(1\pm(-1)^{a_i})/2$. Its restricted Fourier expansion therefore uses only characters of Hamming weight at most $q$. Hence every restricted query parity lies in the subspace spanned by at most $\V(p,q)$ such characters. But the full Walsh character matrix restricted to the columns $F_s$ has rank $|F_s|$, since the full Walsh matrix is invertible. Therefore $|F_s|\le\V(p,q)$ for every fiber. Summing over at most $2^L$ summary values gives $2^p\le2^L\V(p,q)$. The first inequality follows already from the forced $U$ reads.\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sharp $q=0$ and $q=1$ endpoints]\label{prop:sharp}
For every $0\le p\le b$, the point $(p,L,Q)=(p,p,b-p)$ is attainable. For every $1\le p\le b$, the minimum persistent length at an online budget $Q=b-p+1$, among \emph{all nonlinear fixed-read summaries}, is exactly
\[
 \boxed{L_\star=p-\lfloor\log_2(p+1)\rfloor.}
\]
A uniform deterministic construction from the classical Hamming family realizes that length after reading precisely the $p$ declared offline coordinates, with at most $b-p+1$ original sign-field probes per query.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first point stores the $p$ literal observations. For the second, put $r=\lfloor\log_2(p+1)\rfloor$ and take a public rank-$r$ matrix $H\in\F^{r\times p}$ whose $p$ columns include all $2^r-1$ nonzero syndromes (possible because $p\ge2^r-1$). Let $C=\ker H$, of dimension $p-r$. Preprocessing reads $a_R$, stores the $p-r$ independent parity values $c_j\cdot a_R$ for a public basis $c_j$ of $C$, and discards the working copy. On future mask $v=w_R$, compute $Hv$. If $Hv=0$, then $v\in C$ and its parity is determined by the summary. Otherwise some column $He_i=Hv$ exists, so $v+e_i\in C$; obtain its parity from the summary and use one charged probe of $a_i$. Independently read all requested $a_j$ for $j\in U$, at most $b-p$ more probes. Theorem~\ref{th:fixed} with $q=1$ yields the matching nonlinear lower bound $L\ge p-\lfloor\log_2(p+1)\rfloor$. The code construction and the standalone one-probe theorem were already proved in ARC237; the constrained acquisition/continuation composition is the present extension.\end{proof}

For a \emph{fixed linear} summary with rowspace $C\subseteq\F^p$, the exact worst-case continuation source probes equal $u+\rho(C)$, where $\rho(C)=\max_{v\in\F^p}\min_{c\in C}\operatorname{wt}(v+c)$. The upper direction computes the codeword $c$ and probes a shortest error mask. For the lower direction, choose a furthest $v$ and the all-one $U$ mask. Every transcript must read $U$, and after observing a set $J\subseteq R$ the parity functional must lie in $C+\mathrm{span}\{e_j:j\in J\}$, or two source vectors with identical summary and observed bits have opposite answers. Therefore $|J|\ge\rho(C)$, even with nonlinear adaptive decoding. This is the linear coset-distance result of LSPV-2's HAMMING235 combined with the forced-$U$ argument, not an independent all-algorithm SAT bound.

\section{Pareto shape, derivatives, and construction/continuation costs}
For the sharp one-extra-probe endpoint with $p\ge1$, define
\[
 L(p)=p-\lfloor\log_2(p+1)\rfloor,\qquad Q(p)=b-p+1.
\]
For fixed nonnegative weights $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ on \emph{raw preparation reads, persistent bits, and worst-case online raw reads}, the illustrative resource objective is
\[
 C(p)=\alpha p+\beta L(p)+\gamma Q(p)
 =\gamma(b+1)+(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)p-\beta\lfloor\log_2(p+1)\rfloor.
\]
Its exact discrete increment is
\[
 C(p+1)-C(p)=\alpha+\beta-\gamma
 -\beta\,{\bf1}_{\{p+2\text{ is a power of }2\}}.
\]
Thus the known-code frontier has genuine integer kinks rather than a smooth universal slope. Removing the floor yields a \emph{continuous proxy}, not a machine-time identity:
\[
 C_{\rm cont}'(p)=\alpha+\beta-\gamma-\frac{\beta}{(p+1)\ln2},\quad
 C_{\rm cont}''(p)=\frac{\beta}{(p+1)^2\ln2}\ge0.
\]
Integer minimization requires checking the actual admissible $p$, not substituting the stationary point without rounding. For $b=15,p=11$, $u=4$, $r=3$: the exact attainable point is
\[
 \boxed{(p,L,Q)=(11,8,5)},\quad
 2^8\V(11,1)=256(12)\ge2^{11}.
\]
The integer storage lower bound is attained even though the unfloored sphere inequality is not an equality. The earlier ARC238 constraint using only $b,L,Q$ is weaker here because it grants unlimited offline acquisition. For $p=\theta b$, $0<\theta<1$, $L=\theta b-O(\log b)$ and $Q=(1-\theta)b+1$; these are \emph{source-access quantities}, not total-Turing-machine exponents.

\noindent\textbf{Native charged upper ledger.} A straightforward indexed bit-RAM implementation reads the $p$ sign fields once; validates source records; constructs $H$ and a systematic kernel basis uniformly; produces the stored parity summary; and answers future queries by syndrome arithmetic and at most $Q$ named original-source probes. Conservative total bit operations are $P=O((N_A+b^2)\log^2(b+2))$ offline and $R=O((N_B+b^2)\log^2(b+2))$ online, with $S=O(b^2)$ peak writable bits including intermediate matrices, and exactly $L$ persistent source-dependent bits. The program can be optimized but this bound is sufficient and charges address formation, parsing, arithmetic, output, and all public code construction. An unchanged read-only source of $N_A$ bits remains externally accessible. The supplied direct construction has no restart or failed strategy branches; any added selector and its failures must be charged. On one-tape or two-tape TMs, head travel, crossings, work changes, and interpreter cost require separate native ledgers; no equal numerical $t,s$ exponents are claimed. For the explicitly canonical $N=\Theta(b\log b)$, these implementations are polynomial, not hardness results.

\noindent\textbf{Composition and caching.} For disjoint source blocks, independently prepared valid schemes can be combined by concatenating their summaries and summing their offline/read/query costs, giving an attainable upper bound. This is \emph{not} a lower-bound direct-sum theorem against joint nonlinear preprocessing. A sequence of $m$ future contexts costs the once-paid preparation plus the sum of complete online expenses, with persistent cache updates, extra stored bits, validation, and failed trials charged. If the online procedure stores source-dependent new information for subsequent contexts, its state is no longer a fixed $L$-bit pre-query summary and must be treated as a dynamic model, not silently inserted into Theorem~\ref{th:fixed}.

\section{Contextual information value: formal elementary lemmas}\label{sec:value}
Fix a declared cost model $\mathcal C$, state $s$, authenticated knowledge $K$, target task $W$, and a separately justified potential $\Phi_W(s,K)$. For datum $I$, use the user's notation $I\mid_{v_\Phi}$, where
\[
 v_\Phi(I\mid s,K)=\Phi_W(s,K)-\Phi_W(U_I(s,K)).
\]
The pipe denotes a \emph{contextual value label}, not conditioning or divisibility. Acquisition, source authentication, score discovery, verification, update, and storage costs are the separate nonnegative ledger $C_s(I)$. For an exact finite feasible-source set $\Omega_K$, a computable semantic potential is $\Phi_W(K)=\log_2|\pi_W(\Omega_K)|$ whenever $\Omega_K\ne\varnothing$. No SAT-oracle cost for constructing $\Omega_K$ is presumed; actually evaluating this potential may itself be computationally expensive.

\begin{lemma}[Redundancy, conditioning, and exact telescoping]
For any state potential and any updates $K_j=K_{j-1}\cup\{I_j\}$, a repeated verified datum has zero marginal score when it changes neither state nor retained cost. Moreover,
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^m v_\Phi(I_j\mid K_{j-1})=\Phi(K_0)-\Phi(K_m).
\]
For two facts, the interaction dividend is
\[
 v_\Phi(I\mid K,J)-v_\Phi(I\mid K)=
 \Phi(K,I)+\Phi(K,J)-\Phi(K)-\Phi(K,I,J),
\]
which may have either sign. Neither initial singleton scores nor independent accounting maxima may be summed as if they were conditional realized values.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}These are substitutions into the definition. The intermediate potentials cancel exactly. The interaction identity follows by expanding the two conditional marginals. The cost of discovering and checking $\Phi$ is not removed by this algebra.\end{proof}

For a uniform two-bit source $(u,v)$ and target $u\oplus v$, the certified facts $(u=0)\mid_{0}$ and $(v=0)\mid_{0}$ separately leave the target binary, but the second fact has value one after the first. A duplicate fact has zero marginal. A realized target \emph{Shannon entropy} drop need not be nonnegative: in eight equiprobable source worlds with target true in one world, prior entropy is $h_2(1/8)\simeq0.54356$ bits. An authenticated event selecting that world and one target-false world makes the posterior target balanced, so the realized contextual drop is $h_2(1/8)-1\simeq-0.45644$. Expected information gain over all possible events remains nonnegative. These different score scales cannot be conflated with bit-RAM time or with each other.

\section{BUILD: independent finite controls and barriers}
The standard-library reproducer, \path{2026-10-29_2000_ARC239_construction_read_frontier.py}, constructed all fixed-read one-extra-probe endpoints for every $1\le b\le8$ and every $0\le p\le b$. Exactly 44 parameter combinations and 757,304 exhaustive (source, mask) pairs were checked with an explicit oracle method that counts each original sign-field read. A separate $b=15,p=11$ control examined 1,081,344 deterministic source/query pairs, attaining $(11,8,5)$.

Exhaustive nonlinear offline summaries on a fixed read set tested all sixteen one-bit maps at $(b,p,L)=(3,2,1)$, with six attaining online $Q=2$ and ten requiring $Q=3$; all 256 one-bit maps at $(4,3,1)$, with two attaining $Q=2$, 60 requiring three, and 194 requiring four. An adaptive offline-address control reads $a_0$ and then $a_1$ or $a_2$ depending on the first bit; it stores their XOR. Exact finite decision-tree analysis gives $Q=2$ with two offline reads on $b=3$. Neither the stronger \emph{fixed-read} theorem nor the universal $p+Q\ge b$ condition was violated. This finite control does not establish a stronger adaptive $L$--$Q$ theorem.

All reported computational checks used deterministic enumeration and exact recursion, no randomness and no online external solver. The code maintains a separate ground-truth checker; the online decoder has only a counted raw-source oracle, the stored summary, and the public query. The SHA-256 manifest and raw JSON permit independent replay. Algebraic/XOR formulas are easy controls, not evidence of general SAT hardness. Our source grammar is not strict exact22; no parameter-preserving reduction to ordinary unrestricted SAT running-time bounds is asserted.

\noindent\textbf{Remaining open lemma (LSPV-1 next run).} Does the sharpened fixed-read inequality $2^L\V(p,Q-b+p)\ge2^p$ continue to hold when the offline algorithm may choose its at-most-$p$ read coordinates \emph{adaptively}, while retaining only $L$ bits? Either prove it by a complete transcript/fiber argument or construct an explicit counterexample, then test storage-versus-probe crossings under fully charged native machines. Without that bridge we retain only the unconditional all-adaptive $p+Q\ge b$ lower bound. No $P=NP$ or $P\ne NP$ conclusion follows.
