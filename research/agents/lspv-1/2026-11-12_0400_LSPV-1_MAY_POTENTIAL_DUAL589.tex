\section*{Exact quantifiers, source semantics and objective}
Fix \emph{one} immutable externally authenticated original CNF input \(F\) and a
\emph{single} fully defined proof calculus \(R\) with nonnegative fixed rule
weights. A concrete configuration carries its entire source-authenticated shared
proof DAG, record and paid-node identities, a globally once-charged set of
definition and inference owners, fresh/private/exported wire scopes,
signed provenance, active and discharged assumptions, branch contexts,
join conditions, and every deferred accepted-byte check. The initial
configuration is \(s_F\). The terminal set \(T_R(F)\) contains only complete
mathematically valid, fully discharged source-bound refutations, not mere
raw acceptance by a potentially unsound legacy parser.

For a finite legal construction trace \(\pi\), pay each distinct native logical
inference and each newly declared paid owner exactly once; zero-cost source,
facet, reference and context transitions must remain represented even if they
use physical execution time or evidence bits. Define
\[
 W_R(\pi)=\sum_{\text{distinct globally paid nodes }u\in \pi} w_R(u),
 \qquad
 \overline W_R(F)=
 \inf_{\pi:\;s_F\longrightarrow T_R(F)} W_R(\pi).
\]
Free optimal selection means this is a mathematical minimum/infimum, \emph{not}
the cost of finding the optimum, executing a SAT solver or parsing a certificate.

A MAY abstraction is a map \(\alpha:S_R(F)\to A\), with concretization
\(\gamma(a)\supseteq\{s:\alpha(s)=a\}\). For every native legal edge
\(s\xrightarrow{w}t\), require a witnessed abstract edge
\[
 \alpha(s)\xrightarrow{c^\#}\alpha(t),\qquad 0\le c^\#\le w.
 \tag{1}
\]
For every completed \(t\in T_R(F)\), require \(\alpha(t)\in G^\#\).
One may conservatively represent as-yet-unknown genuine historical operations
with additional MAY edges, even charging them zero, but may \emph{not}
delete them or substitute CANON1/2/2R for the historical language.
The mathematically sound terminal predicate must be independently
authenticated; raw /341 checker acceptance is not sufficient.

\begin{theorem}[Sound potential lower bound, conditional all-edge transfer]
Let \(h:A\to[0,\infty)\) satisfy
\[
 h(g)=0\quad(g\in G^\#),\qquad
 h(a)\le c^\#+h(b)
 \quad\text{for every legal MAY edge }a\xrightarrow{c^\#}b.
 \tag{2}
\]
If the complete native relation and every genuine terminal satisfy (1), then
\[
 \boxed{h(\alpha(s_F))\le \overline W_R(F).}
 \tag{3}
\]
In a \emph{finite} MAY graph in which each considered state can reach an
abstract goal, the pointwise maximum of all potentials (2) is exactly the
MAY shortest-goal distance \(d^\#(a)\). Hence
\[
 d^\#(\alpha(s_F))\le\overline W_R(F).
 \tag{4}
\]
These assertions hold for directed, one-way free steps, zero cycles and
globally shared paid nodes as long as the concrete state has the whole
charged identity/history and the MAY simulation is valid.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix any valid finite native refutation with states \(s_0,\ldots,s_k\)
and edge charges \(w_i\). Apply (1) to each legal transition, then telescope
(2):
\[
 h(\alpha(s_0))
 \le \sum_{i=0}^{k-1}c_i^\#+h(\alpha(s_k))
 \le \sum_{i=0}^{k-1}w_i=W_R(\pi).
\]
The last abstract goal potential is zero; taking the infimum proves (3).
For the finite all-goal-reachable graph, nonnegative edge lengths imply that
\(d^\#(g)=0\) on goals and \(d^\#(a)\le c^\#+d^\#(b)\) on each edge.
Conversely any \(h\) satisfying (2), telescoped along an arbitrary
abstract path to a goal, obeys \(h(a)\le\text{path cost}\). Minimizing over
paths yields \(h(a)\le d^\#(a)\), proving pointwise maximality.
When some states cannot reach a goal, a finite-valued maximal potential
need not exist on those states; no claim of such maximality is made.
\end{proof}

\section*{Constructive countermodel: a single unknown \emph{positive} shortcut}
\begin{proposition}[The missing-positive-edge firewall]
For every integer \(n\ge2\), there is a finite known-rule fragment with
initial-to-goal minimum \(n\) whose shortest-path lower-bound potential
ceases to be admissible after adding \emph{one} as-yet-unauthenticated
possible rule edge of charge exactly one. The complete MAY minimum is one.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take the directed chain
\(s_0\xrightarrow{1}s_1\xrightarrow{1}\cdots\xrightarrow{1}s_n\),
with \(s_n\) the goal. The fragment's optimal work and potential at \(s_0\)
are \(n\). Add one hypothetical MAY edge
\(s_0\xrightarrow{1}s_n\). The old potential violates the edge
inequality by \(n-1>0\), while the MAY shortest path has work one.
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Epistemic boundary:} The new direct edge is
\emph{not} asserted to be a legally accepted transition of historical
\(R_{\mathrm{mix341}}\). This is a source-independent impossibility test of
any proposed completeness inference from the two \emph{incomplete}
surviving sibling dispatchers. It strengthens the explicit unknown-zero-edge
control in LSPV-1/587 to \emph{positive} missing operations, but
not the classical shortest-path theorem itself.

The second hostile control recovers LSPV-5/462 \emph{as prior art}.
There is an unpaid state \(s_0\), cached state \(s_1\), and goal \(g\);
edges \(s_0\xrightarrow{1}s_1\) and \(s_1\xrightarrow{0}g\).
The true minimum is one. Identifying \(s_0\) and \(s_1\) gives a
spurious abstract initial-to-goal zero-cost edge. The zero-cost
route is safe as an overapproximation but \emph{cannot} prove a
positive lower bound. Retaining certified fresh/paid provenance is
precision-bearing, not an automatic exponential-memory claim.
