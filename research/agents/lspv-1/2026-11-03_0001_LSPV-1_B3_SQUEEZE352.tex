\section*{Setup}
Fix $R$ as in the 2026-11-02 12:04 directive; $\overline W_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},|F|\le N}\min_{\pi}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$, with free choice of optimal $\pi$. B3 is: $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\Rightarrow\overline W_R\le \mathrm{poly}$.

\begin{theorem}[Trivialization of B3 where a lower bound is already known]
Let $R$ be any system with an unconditional proof that $\overline W_R$ is superpolynomial (e.g.\ ordinary Resolution by Haken/Urquhart/Ben-Sasson--Wigderson, or Resolution plus XOR-free cutting-plane fragments with known bounds). Then B3 for $R$ is logically equivalent to $\mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm P$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} B3 reads $A\Rightarrow B$ with $A=$``SAT$\in$P'' and $B=$``$\overline W_R$ polynomial''. $B$ is false by hypothesis, so $A\Rightarrow B$ is equivalent to $\neg A$. \qquad$\square$
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Consequence (rigorous).} For every fixed $R$ whose lower bound is currently provable, the ``weakest sufficient bridge'' is not weaker than the goal: proving it is proving P$\ne$NP. The programme can therefore only gain from B3 for an $R$ whose lower bound is \emph{not yet known}. This is not a refutation; it identifies where B3 has independent content. It also explains the earlier finding that the parity recognizer defeats B1 for Resolution: B1 is false there, and B3 is merely a restatement of the target.

\begin{proposition}[Where B3 can be derived: provable correctness, CONDITIONAL]
Let $A$ be a fixed deterministic polynomial-time SAT decider with running time $T_A(n)\le n^c$, and let $\mathrm{Corr}_A(n)$ be the poly$(n)$-size propositional translation of ``for every $F$ of length $n$ and every assignment $x$: if $A$ rejects $F$ then $F(x)=0$''. Let
\[ H_A:\quad R\text{ has }\mathrm{poly}(n)\text{-size derivations of }\mathrm{Corr}_A(n)\text{ (uniformly in }n).\]
If $H_A$ holds and $R$ simulates Frege-style substitution and cut on circuit-encoded lines (as Extended Frege does), then $\overline W_R(N)\le \mathrm{poly}(N)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Sketch] For UNSAT $F$, write the polynomial-size computation of $A$ on $F$ as extension-variable definitions of the machine configurations; derive ``$A$ rejects $F$'' by propagating the tableau (size $\mathrm{poly}(N)$, no search needed); instantiate $\mathrm{Corr}_A(N)$ at $F$ and cut. The result is $\neg F(x)$ for a free $x$, i.e.\ a refutation. This is the standard argument that a feasibly-provably-correct polynomial algorithm for a coNP problem gives polynomial proofs in Extended Frege (Cook 1975; Krajíček--Pudlák 1989; Krajíček 1995). \qquad$\square$
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Gap.} Proposition 2 needs $H_A$, a \emph{provable correctness} hypothesis on the unknown $A$. SAT$\in$P alone gives only the existence of $A$ and truth, not $R$-provability, of $\mathrm{Corr}_A$. This is the same ``reflection'' gap flagged in the directive; no proof of $H_A$ from SAT$\in$P is known for Extended Frege, and it is a known open problem whether such algorithms are provably correct in weak theories. Hence B3 for Extended Frege is open as an unconditional implication.

A superpolynomial lower bound for a system $R$ strong enough for Prop.~2 would be a major new result (no unconditional Frege or Extended Frege size lower bound is known; see below). Conversely, a weak $R$ makes the bridge vacuous. The realistic programme is thus a \emph{middle system}: strong enough that B3 is plausible via a restricted correctness argument, weak enough that a lower bound is accessible. The existing mixed system $R_{\rm mix341}$ sits there by construction, but neither side is proved.
