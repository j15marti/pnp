\section{Exact optimal antipodal repair}
A target source is called antipodal if all clauses contain at least one positive and one negative literal. Then the assignments $0^n$ and $1^n$ both satisfy the target.
\begin{theorem}[New sharp two-witness upgrade]
Every valid strictly monotone exact-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF $F$ has an antipodal, source-support-preserving, legal exact-$(2,2)$ repair at precisely $m$ original sign flips. This is the minimum possible number, and a polynomial-time integral max-flow algorithm finds the repair.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Balance implies exactly $m/2$ all-positive and $m/2$ all-negative clauses. Build the network $s\to C_+\to V\to C_-\to t$ with unit capacities on outer clause arcs and each original incidence arc. Sending $1/3$ on each incidence arc and one unit on each outer clause arc gives flow value $m/2$: each clause has three incidences, and each source variable has two positive and two negative incidences, so conservation holds. Integer capacities imply an integer max flow of value $m/2$. Flip the selected positive occurrence of each positive clause negative, and the selected negative occurrence of each negative clause positive. Flow conservation at variables preserves exact signed quotas. Each old positive clause ends with two positives; each old negative clause ends with one. Thus both constant assignments satisfy the repaired formula. There are $m$ flips. Any antipodal repair must flip at least one sign in every old monotone clause, so $m$ is optimal. Properness and uniqueness are preserved because original source supports do not change and the two old polarity groups end with different positive counts.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[General nearest antipodal signing]
For every signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF source, the closest source-support-preserving, quota-respecting antipodal signing exists and is computable by integral minimum-cost flow in polynomial time.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $x_{vC}\in\{0,1\}$ denote whether the original incidence $(v,C)$ becomes positive. The exact constraints are
\[
\sum_{C\ni v}x_{vC}=2,\qquad 1\le\sum_{v\in C}x_{vC}\le2.
\]
A bipartite flow has variable supply two, occurrence arcs capacity one, and clause lower and upper outflow bounds one and two. The fractional solution $x_{vC}=1/2$ is feasible, giving an integer feasible flow by network integrality. Cost one on an originally negative incidence selected positive and zero on originally positive incidences yields an optimum. Since both source and target have two positive occurrences per variable, the number of positive-to-negative sign flips equals the number of negative-to-positive flips, so total Hamming distance is twice the flow cost. For monotone sources, fractional $x=2/3$ on positive clauses and $x=1/3$ on negative clauses has sign distance $m$; integrality plus the preceding lower bound proves exact optimum $m$.
\end{proof}
