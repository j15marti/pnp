\section{Both-label deterministic and randomized source-query lower bounds}
\begin{theorem}[restricted classical two-sided direct-sum adversary]
For every $t\ge1$ and every \emph{fixed} total deterministic exact decider $A$ for $L$ on all binary inputs, with $Q_A$ counting actual source-bit reads including repetitions,
\[
Q_A(X_t)\ge t,\qquad \max_{j\in[t]}Q_A(Y_{t,j})\ge t,\qquad
\frac1t\sum_{j=1}^tQ_A(Y_{t,j})\ge\frac{t+1}{2}.
\tag{1}
\]
For every total bounded-error randomized $A$ correct on every binary input with worst-input error $\epsilon<1/2$,
\[
\mathbb E_R Q_A(X_t)\ge (1-2\epsilon)t,\quad
\frac1t\sum_{j=1}^t\mathbb E_R Q_A(Y_{t,j})\ge(1-2\epsilon)\frac{t+1}{2}.
\tag{2}
\]
No witness recovery, syntactic pin key, oracle for SAT labels, or fixed machine-state representation is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If a deterministic run on $X_t$ never reads $D_j$, the whole observation transcript and output are identical on the valid opposite-label input $Y_{t,j}$, a contradiction. Hence all $t$ disjoint sets must be touched. Order them by first touch. On $Y_{t,j}$, the transcript agrees until the first $D_j$ touch, forcing at least its rank in source observations. Summing the ranks $1,\ldots,t$ proves (1).
For randomized $A$, couple executions on $X_t,Y_{t,j}$ with identical coins. Both are correct simultaneously with probability at least $1-2\epsilon$, so on $X_t$ the probability of touching each $D_j$ is at least $1-2\epsilon$. Let $h(R)$ count the distinct $D_j$ touched on the accepting run: $\mathbb E h\ge(1-2\epsilon)t$ and $Q_A(X_t;R)\ge h(R)$. For a fixed random tape, the $h$ hit sets have first-touch ranks $1,\ldots,h$. The $t-h$ unhit counterparts have exactly the same entire trace as $X_t$, with at least $h$ reads. Therefore
\[
\sum_{j=1}^tQ_A(Y_{t,j};R)\ge
\frac{h(h+1)}2+(t-h)h
=th-\frac{h(h-1)}2\ge\frac{t+1}{2}h.
\]
Take expectations and use the coupling bound to prove (2).
\end{proof}
These are genuine original-language both-label restrictions on every correct algorithm, but only $\Omega(t)=\Omega(N_t/\log N_t)$ probes. They do \emph{not} establish a superpolynomial time bound or any nontrivial writable-space bound. On the restricted $t+1$-input promise, a scan of one discriminating bit per block until a U block is found attains all deterministic bounds. A randomized algorithm that scans with probability $1-2\epsilon$, otherwise guessing the two labels with probability $\epsilon$ each, attains the expected floors. Promise-optimal query cost must not be confused with all-binary-input grammar and algorithm time.
