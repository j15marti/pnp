\section*{IDEA, precise assumptions and falsifiers}
Hypothesis: clause indispensability supplies an algorithm-independent raw-input read lower bound, but cannot on its own supply a superpolynomial bound. Predicted: on a padded DIMACS representation of a minimally UNSAT $m$-clause input, every correct adaptive byte-probe SAT solver reads each of $m$ source-clause blocks. Falsifiers: (1) replacement makes an UNSAT rather than SAT neighbor; (2) modified source length/header or another clause block leaks the answer; (3) the solver distinguishes opposite-label streams without reading the changed block; (4) assumed free whole-input hashing, wide reads, or a restrictive promise invalidates byte-probe charging.

\section*{All-algorithm read lemma}
Let $F=\bigwedge_{i=1}^m C_i$ be any clause-minimally UNSAT CNF, $m\ge2$. Encode it as ordinary DIMACS, with header and $m$ clauses as individual lines padded by legal whitespace to equal byte length. Write $x_F$ for its $N$-byte encoding, and $B_i$ for its $i$th disjoint line interval. For each $i$ replace line $i$ by a padded copy of some other existing $C_{j(i)}$ with $j(i)\ne i$, obtaining $x_F^{(i)}$. Repeated clauses are legal in general DIMACS. Exact source length, header and all other lines are unchanged.

\begin{lemma}[Same-length SAT neighbors]
Every $x_F^{(i)}$ encodes a satisfiable CNF differing from $x_F$ only in $B_i$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Minimal UNSAT gives a model of $F\setminus\{C_i\}$. The duplicate $C_{j(i)}$ is already present and true in this model. Padding changes neither semantics nor stream length.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact deterministic byte-read sensitivity]
For every such $F$, there exists one fixed $x_F$ and $m$ legitimate SAT neighbors such that for every uniform total correct deterministic general-CNF SAT decider $A$ with individually charged byte-addressable input reads,
\[
\operatorname{distinctClauseBlockReads}_A(x_F)=m,\qquad T_A(x_F)\ge m.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $A(x_F)$ does not query a byte in $B_i$. Inductively couple its entire adaptive deterministic execution with the run on $x_F^{(i)}$. Every inspected input byte and the input length coincide, so the next configurations and output coincide. The two input labels are opposite, contradicting correctness. Thus every disjoint block is read at least once. These are charged physical operations, not proof-DAG nodes.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded-error randomized version]
If $A$ has error at most $\epsilon<1/2$ on each of the two inputs for every $i$, then
\[
\Pr[A(x_F)\text{ queries }B_i]\ge1-2\epsilon,\qquad
\mathbb E[\operatorname{distinctClauseBlockReads}_A(x_F)]\ge(1-2\epsilon)m.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Couple the same random tape on $x_F$ and $x_F^{(i)}$. By the union bound their outputs differ with probability at least $1-2\epsilon$. A differing output forces a probe of the only differing interval in the original run. Sum indicators over $i$; independence is unnecessary.
\end{proof}
