\begin{theorem}[All-valid-target exact root cofactor]
Let valid source $F$ have $n$ variables and $m=4n/3$ original clauses. For every original variable $x$ and $b\in\{0,1\}$, the ordinary residual $R(F,x=b)$ after dropping satisfied clauses and removing false pivot occurrences has exactly $m-2$ surviving original clauses: $m-4$ width-three and two width-two, with exactly $4n-8$ remaining literal occurrences. Its assignments extend to $F$ precisely when they satisfy the child. No child is a valid canonical source. In the fixed-width explicit indexed clause-list encoding retaining original variable names, source $N$ and raw-child $M$ obey $M=N-O(\log n)=N^{1+o(1)}$, hence $M>N^\lambda$ for every fixed $\lambda<1$ and all sufficiently large $N$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Exactly two distinct clauses contain the pivot literal made TRUE; those two disappear. Exactly two other distinct clauses contain the pivot literal made FALSE; both lose that literal and become binary. No other source clause changes. The remaining literal count is $3m-6-2=4n-8$. Shannon restriction preserves completion truth. Exactly eight literal records and two clause records are deleted; each occupies $O(\log n)$ explicit indexed bits and $N=\Theta(n\log n)$. Arbitrary semantic quotienting, implicit representation and re-encoding are excluded from this restricted theorem.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Two-polarity fixed-depth raw survivor tariff]
If coherent partial assignment $\rho$ fixes $k_1$ variables to one and $k_0$ variables to zero, at most $2k_1$ positive and $2k_0$ negative original clauses can become satisfied. Thus the raw surviving original-clause count is at least $m-2(k_0+k_1)$. \end{corollary}
\begin{proof}Each fixed variable creates exactly two true original signed literal occurrences. Every satisfied source clause consumes at least one. Count separately by sign, then rearrange. \begin{theorem}[Canonical original clause extreme trichotomy]
For any two distinct original canonical clauses, the ordered all-literal-extreme compatibility vector $(SS,SU,US,UU)$ equals $1111$ on disjoint supports, $1001$ for shared same-sign variables, and $0110$ for shared opposite-sign variables. \end{theorem}
\begin{proof}Each original clause is monotone, so all variables shared by a fixed pair have the same relative polarity. SS and UU are compatible iff no shared opposite-sign occurrence exists. SU and US are compatible iff no shared same-sign occurrence exists. Source occurrence targets $S_j,U_j$ invert locally but do not constitute a coherent global assignment across clauses.\end{proof}
