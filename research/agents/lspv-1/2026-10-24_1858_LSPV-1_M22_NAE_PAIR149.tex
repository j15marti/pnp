\section{Exact common NAE clause distribution}
For an unsigned three-name support $S=\{a,b,c\}$, let
\[
 \Omega_S=\{z\in\{0,1\}^{S}:1\le z_a+z_b+z_c\le2\},
 \qquad
 \mu_S(z)=\begin{cases}1/6&z\in\Omega_S,\\0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
All six rows are accepted by \emph{both} the all-positive OR clause on $S$ and the all-negative OR clause on $S$. Unlike the parity-supported four-row local distribution of PARITY-LOCAL142, the same six-row law works on an explicitly complemented clause pair.

\begin{theorem}[Full-pairing-compatible clause-overlap relaxation]
For \emph{every} finite clausewise-monotone proper OR3 source $F$, without any occurrence quota or restriction on fully or partially complemented supports, assign $\mu_C=\mu_{\operatorname{supp}(C)}$ to each signed clause. Every $\mu_C$ is supported entirely on satisfying local assignments. For any pair of clauses $C,D$ and their entire named-variable intersection $I=\operatorname{supp}(C)\cap\operatorname{supp}(D)$, the marginal laws $\mu_C|_I$ and $\mu_D|_I$ agree exactly, including $|I|=3$. Explicitly,
\[
 \Pr(x_i=1)=\tfrac12,\quad
 \Pr(x_i=x_j=1)=\Pr(x_i=x_j=0)=\tfrac16,\quad
 \Pr(x_i\ne x_j)=\tfrac23
\]
whenever $i\ne j$ belong to one common clause support.
Thus the \emph{clause-local marginal consistency} linear relaxation always has a feasible point, including on any UNSAT $F\in\CS$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A nonconstant triple contains at least one $1$ and at least one $0$. It therefore satisfies both signs of the OR3 constraint. The law $\mu_S$ is invariant under all permutations of $S$ and under bit complementation, so its projection to any one or two named variables depends only on the number of coordinates, not on the remaining third name or the clause sign. The one-bit marginal is $(1/2,1/2)$; the two-bit marginal assigns $(00,01,10,11)$ weights $(1,2,2,1)/6$. Two \emph{distinct} unsigned supports intersect in at most two names, giving equality of projected laws. For identical unsigned supports, whether one clause or both polarity clauses are present, the entire three-variable laws coincide by construction. Hence all overlaps, of cardinality zero through three, agree. No global joint distribution is claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp correlation floor on a complementary support]
Let $(X_1,X_2,X_3)$ be distributed on the six NAE assignments to one support, and suppose $\Pr(X_i=1)=1/2$ for each coordinate. Then
\[
 \sum_{1\le i<j\le3}\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)=-\tfrac14,
 \qquad
 \sum_{i<j}\operatorname{Corr}(X_i,X_j)=-1.
\]
Consequently at least one pair has correlation at most $-1/3$, so unbiased pairwise independence is impossible. The common uniform six-row NAE distribution achieves correlation exactly $-1/3$ on \emph{all} three pairs; the $1/3$ maximal absolute-correlation floor is sharp.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $S=X_1+X_2+X_3$. NAE support forces $S\in\{1,2\}$, and unbiased one-variable marginals give $\mathbb E[S]=3/2$, hence $\Pr(S=1)=\Pr(S=2)=1/2$ and $\operatorname{Var}(S)=1/4$. Each Bernoulli marginal has variance $1/4$, so
\[
 \tfrac14=\operatorname{Var}(S)
 =\tfrac34+2\sum_{i<j}\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j).
\]
The covariance sum is therefore $-1/4$. Since each denominator $\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X_i)\operatorname{Var}(X_j)}=1/4$, the correlation sum is $-1$. One of three numbers totaling $-1$ is at most $-1/3$. Under uniform $\mu_S$, each pair has $\Pr(X_i=X_j=1)=1/6$, covariance $1/6-1/4=-1/12$, and correlation $-1/3$, attaining the bound.
\end{proof}
