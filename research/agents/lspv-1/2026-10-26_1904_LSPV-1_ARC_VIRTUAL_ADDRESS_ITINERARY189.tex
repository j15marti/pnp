\section{Source family, source bit addresses, and models}
Use the previously validated 15-variable, 20-clause proper signed exact-$(2,2)$ module $H(a,b)$ obtained by two opposite-sign-preserving literal swaps of $W_1$. Its exact model counts for $(a,b)=(00,01,10,11)$ are $(772,272,272,0)$. Arrange $m$ disjoint renamed copies in the standard fixed-width signed-clause-list encoding with all $2m$ A-controlled clauses first, all $16m$ common fixed clauses in the middle and all $2m$ B-controlled clauses last. The resulting genuine source $F_m(a,b)$ has $15m$ variables, $20m$ clauses and $N=\Theta(m\log(m+2))$ physical bits, with a common gap of $G=\Theta(N)$ bit positions; it satisfies
\[
 \SAT(F_m(a,b))\iff a\wedge b=0^m. \tag{1}
\]
It is signed, NOT clause-monotone. Its particular clause order and physical encoding matter, and this theorem does not import exponent bounds through another encoding without a paid bit-local reduction.

Fix ONE deterministic total exact ordinary-SAT decider $A$ on all inputs of this encoding in FLMV's indexed read-only-input random-access TM model. On a run $F$, enumerate the addressed \emph{source input-bit reads} chronologically as $i_1,\ldots,i_q$, take $i_0=0$, and define
\[
 D_A(F)=\sum_{j=1}^{q}|i_j-i_{j-1}|,\qquad
 V_A(F)=\#\{\text{sign changes among nonzero }i_j-i_{j-1}\}. \tag{2}
\]
These are TRACE-SHAPE quantities. An indexed access writes/uses its binary address at its actual charged index cost, not at cost $|i_j-i_{j-1}|$. Reads and writes of work storage, arithmetic, switching, parsing, failed routes and output remain ordinary charged $T_A,S_A$ resources, but do not artificially increase (2).

Let $B_A(N)$ bound the binary length of a complete mutable configuration, augmented if necessary with $O(\log(N+T_A(N)+2))$ bits for the virtual input position and query target. FLMV Sec. 2.1 counts work/index tapes through their largest accessed positions and explicitly gives configuration size $O(S_A)$; for polynomial-time $A$, therefore,
\[
 B_A(N)=O(S_A(N)+\log N). \tag{3}
\]
For a separate log-word RAM with $W$ touched writable words and polynomially bounded addresses, only $B=O((W+1)\log N)$ follows. Unbounded unit-cost word RAM is NOT covered by that identification.

\section{Complete configuration splicing}
\begin{lemma}[Every-gap-cut transcript injection]\label{lem:inj}
For any bit cut $c$ inside the middle common gap, let $q_c(a)$ count virtual crossings of $c$ by the indexed source-address sequence on the SAT input $F_m(a,\bar a)$. Then the $2^m$ complete oriented configuration-crossing transcripts, including the terminal decision tag, are all different. For a fixed $A$,
\[
 2^{-m}\sum_{a\in\{0,1\}^m}q_c(a)
  =\Omega\!\left(\frac{m}{B_A(N)+1}\right). \tag{4}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put all input bits strictly left of $c$ at Alice and those right of $c$ at Bob. Both know the fixed program, source length, fixed supports and common gap. The party currently holding the virtual input position simulates the deterministic indexed machine's internal operations and reads until the next input query lies across $c$. Before that read, she transmits the complete mutable state and pending input target; the other party continues. The final SAT/UNSAT decision is tagged in one bit. A crossing is a message, with $O(B_A+1)$ bits per message; this is a communication accounting device and assigns NO physical head-motion charge to the indexed machine.

The pairs $(a,\bar a)$ are a size-$2^m$ 1-fooling set for DISJ: each is SAT, and for distinct $a,b$ at least one of $(a,\bar b)$ and $(b,\bar a)$ is UNSAT. The deterministic transcript-rectangle lemma implies that no two SAT fooling pairs can share an entire oriented message transcript. A word of $q$ configuration messages has at most $2^{O((B_A+1)(q+1))}$ representations. Counting short words proves that, if $m/(B_A+1)$ is large, a constant fraction of the $2^m$ transcripts require $\Omega(m/(B_A+1))$ crossings. If the ratio is bounded, at most a constant number of SAT fooling inputs can use an empty crossing transcript, hence the mean number of crossings is $\Omega(1)=\Omega(m/(B_A+1))$. Both cases give (4).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Common-SAT-input virtual path and reversal bound]\label{th:joint}
For all sufficiently large $m$, at least one SAT source $F=F_m(a_*,\bar a_*)$ has SIMULTANEOUSLY
\[
 \boxed{(D_A(F)+1)(B_A(N)+1)=\Omega(N^2/\log N),} \tag{5}
\]
\[
 \boxed{(V_A(F)+1)(B_A(N)+1)=\Omega(N/\log N).} \tag{6}
\]
In the cited FLMV indexed RAT and for a polynomial-time $A$, (3) yields both with $B_A=O(S_A+\log N)$, on the same machine and source input.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are $G=\Theta(N)$ common-gap cuts. Sum (4) over all such $c$ and interchange the finite source and cut sums. By averaging, choose one $a_*$ with
\[
 \sum_{c\text{ in gap}}q_c(a_*)=
 \Omega\!\left(\frac{Gm}{B_A(N)+1}\right).
\]
Any virtual jump between consecutive input-bit addresses crosses every intervening bit cut, so its absolute displacement bounds its central-cut crossings. Summing gives $D_A(F)\ge\sum_cq_c(a_*)$, and hence (5) since $m=\Theta(N/\log N)$. On this SAME SAT run, one central cut is crossed at least $\Omega(m/(B_A+1))$ times. The directed crossings of a fixed one-dimensional cut alternate, so the nonzero address differences reverse direction at least $q_c-1$ times. Adding 1 gives (6). No SAT witness is changed between the two estimates.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exponents are structural, not runtime.}
Writing worst-case ceilings $D=N^{\delta+o(1)}$, $V+1=N^{\rho+o(1)}$, indexed work/index $S=N^{s+o(1)}$ for the same uniform machine, (5)--(6) give
\[
  \delta+s\ge2,\qquad \rho+s\ge1. \tag{7}
\]
Neither $\delta=t$ nor $\rho=t$ follows. Such substitutions would illegally transplant sequential-head lower bounds into a random-access timing theorem.

\section{Matching easy controls, experiment and proof DAG}
A source-indexed $B$-bit block comparator reads the $B$ left data controls, then their matching right data controls, repeats, and decides the PROMISED family. It uses $O(B+\log N)$ work bits, exactly $2m$ indexed source-sign reads, $O(m\operatorname{polylog}N)$ charged indexed bit time, and
\[
 D=O(N\lceil m/B\rceil),\quad V=O(\lceil m/B\rceil).
\]
For $B=m^\alpha$, $0\le\alpha\le1$, the trace axes $\delta=2-\alpha$, $\rho=1-\alpha$, $s=\alpha$ are tight up to polylogs on this easy promise while its runtime exponent is $t=1$. This is a direct falsifier of an alleged $D\le T\operatorname{polylog}N$ bound. The program is NOT an all-input SAT algorithm.
