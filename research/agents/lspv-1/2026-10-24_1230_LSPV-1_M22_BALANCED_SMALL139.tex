\section{Canonical target, old results and falsifiable proposals}
An ordinary CNF belongs to \(\CM\) precisely when each clause has three distinct source-variable names, each is entirely positive or entirely negative, no signed clause is duplicated, and each active source variable occurs exactly twice with each polarity. For an ordinary OR-clause \(C\), \(\bar C\) means flip all signs while retaining OR, \emph{not} Boolean negation of \(C\). Put
\[
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{pair}}=\{F\in\CM:\forall C\in F,\bar C\in F\},
\quad\CS=\CM\setminus\mathcal P_{\mathrm{pair}},
\quad L_\star=\{\operatorname{enc}(F): F\in\CS,\ F\text{ SAT}\}.
\]
Invalid encodings are rejected. Partially paired, disconnected, easy, and non-minimally-UNSAT instances remain allowed. Write \(k=n(F)\), \(m=4k/3\), \(m_+=m_-=2k/3\), and \(N=|\operatorname{enc}(F)|=\Theta(k\log(k+2))\) for the declared dense indexed clause-list code. In particular \(k\ne N\).

D\"ocker's \emph{KNOWN THEOREM} makes \(\CM\)-SAT NP-complete and its fully paired subfamily satisfiable \cite{docker}; the canonical six-variable nonpaired satisfiable fixture \(Y\), with positive supports \(123,145,246,356\) and negative supports \(124,136,235,456\), provides the previously proved NP-completeness of \(L_\star\), not a time lower bound. We considered three falsifiable claims before our new counting argument:

\item[] \textbf{H1, FILLED small class:} fixed-Hamming-weight assignment counting already gives a \emph{symbolic} satisfiability certificate for every \(k=6\) member, without listing all \(75^2\) support pairs.

\end{description}

\section{A symbolic theorem at six and nine variables}
Let \(P\) and \(Q\) be the unsigned supports of the all-positive and all-negative clauses respectively, and let \(V_t(F)\) be the collection of satisfying assignments with exactly \(t\) TRUE source variables.

\begin{theorem}[Small-layer satisfiability; PROVED/EXACT]\label{th:main}
For every \(F\in\CM\) with \(k=6\),
\[
|V_3(F)|\ge12.
\]
For every \(F\in\CM\) with \(k=9\),
\[
\boxed{|V_4(F)|\ge6,\qquad |V_5(F)|\ge6.}
\]
The conclusions hold equally for fully paired, partially paired and pair-free members, independent of connectedness. Therefore \emph{every} valid member of \(\CS\) with \(k=6\) or \(k=9\) is SAT. All UNSAT members of \(\CM\), and hence of \(\CS\), have \(k\ge12\) when active variables and the proper clause grammar are used. This is a finite-size exclusion, not a uniform lower bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(k=6\), \(m_+=m_-=4\) and there are \(\binom63=20\) assignments of TRUE-weight three. A fixed positive triple is false under precisely \(\binom3{3}=1\) of these assignments; a fixed negative triple is false under precisely \(\binom3{0}=1\). The eight clause-failure sets therefore cover at most eight assignments. Thus at least \(20-8=12\) satisfy all clauses.

For \(k=9\), there are six clauses of either sign and \(\binom94=126\) assignments \(T\subseteq[k]\) of TRUE-weight four. Denote by \(v(T)\) the number of violated clauses. A fixed positive triple is violated by \(\binom64=15\) of these \(T\); a negative triple by \(\binom61=6\). Hence
\[
\sum_{|T|=4}v(T)=6\cdot15+6\cdot6=126.
\]
Let \(r(T)\) be the number of violated \emph{positive} clauses. Since the five FALSE variables supply at most ten positive literal occurrences and every positive violated clause consumes three, \(0\le r(T)\le3\).

Every source variable \(x\) lies in two different positive clauses, call them \(A_x,B_x\). A pair of distinct positive triples shares \(j\in\{0,1,2\}\) source names. If \(j=1\), precisely one TRUE-weight-four assignment makes \emph{both} positive clauses false: all five names of their union are set FALSE. If \(j=2\), there are five such assignments: the union contains four names, and one more FALSE name is freely selected. (For \(j=0\) there are none.) Thus the number of simultaneously violating assignments for each distinct pair is at least the number of shared names. Counting over all pairs and then the nine names yields
\[
B:=\sum_{|T|=4}\binom{r(T)}2\ \ge\sum_{x=1}^9 1=9.
\]
For each \(r\in\{0,1,2,3\}\),
\[
\binom r2\le\frac32(r-1)_+.
\]
Consequently \(\sum_T(r(T)-1)_+\ge\frac23B\ge6\). Since \(v(T)\ge r(T)\), also \(\sum_T(v(T)-1)_+\ge6\). The exact incidence equality \(\sum_Tv(T)=|\{T:|T|=4\}|\) implies
\[
|\{T:|T|=4,\ v(T)=0\}|=\sum_{|T|=4}(v(T)-1)_+\ge6.
\]
For TRUE-weight five, interchange positive with negative and FALSE with TRUE. The six negative clause-failure counts are \(\binom62=15\) each, positive counts \(\binom65=6\), again summing to \(126\). Any five TRUE names contain at most ten negative occurrences; every negative source variable occurs twice; each negative pair sharing \(j=1,2\) names has respectively one or five joint violating assignments. The identical argument gives \(|V_5(F)|\ge6\).

In valid nonempty sources \(3m=4k\), so \(3\mid k\); the proper unique signed-clause restriction rules out \(k=3\), because the required two positive clauses would have the identical support \(123\). The next values are \(6,9,12\), establishing the UNSAT size consequence.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope of sharpness.}
The \(k=6\) bound \(|V_3|\ge12\) is attained by members in an independently exhaustive \(75^2\)-pair census. The \(k=9\) lower bound six is certified symbolically but is \emph{not} asserted sharp. The general fixed-weight union estimate is
\[
|V_t(F)|\ \ge\ \binom kt-\frac{2k}{3}
\left(\binom{k-3}{t}+\binom{k-3}{t-3}\right).
\]
The right-hand side is positive for \(k=6,t=3\), zero at \(k=9,t=4,5\), and negative at \(k=12,t=6\), so this argument alone cannot establish SAT beyond \(k=9\). The known \(k=198\) UNSAT original in \cite{docker} is compatible with this theorem. We neither independently exhaust its \(2^{198}\) assignments nor pretend its paper certificate is an enumeration.
