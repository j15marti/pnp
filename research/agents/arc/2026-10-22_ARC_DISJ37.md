## Construction
For nonempty X,Y subsets of [m], m>=3, with shared selectors h_i and visible y, encode L_X=(OR h_i) AND (AND_{i notin X} NOT h_i) and R_Y=(OR h_i) AND (AND_{i notin Y} NOT h_i) in ordinary proper-3CNF using fresh Tseitin auxiliaries and harmless existentially satisfiable clauses mentioning y. Both exact visible projections are total, and both components are SAT. Their conjunction is SAT iff X intersect Y is nonempty.

## Restricted theorem
Any deterministic adaptive two-way protocol in which Alice alone sees L_X and Bob alone sees R_Y and all cross-source information must be communicated requires >=m transmitted bits to decide their exact conjunction on every pair. Proof: the 2^m-2 UNSAT pairs (X, complement X) form a fooling set. If two pairs yield an identical transcript, the rectangle property forces both crossed pairs to be UNSAT, but distinct X,Z guarantee one crossed pair has nonempty intersection, contradiction. Since N=Theta(m log m), the lower bound is Omega(N/log N) detached communication. This excludes N^0.6+o(1) *answer-only* local SAT response transcripts, but NOT source-aware algorithms or mixed cross-input SAT children.

## Exact source-aware escape
On this canonical input family, two cursors scan sorted forbidden-index four-clause proper-3CNF gadgets and detect an index allowed by both sides. Time O(N), modeled space O(log N), with no rereads. Therefore this restricted communication lower bound is not an unrestricted SAT runtime lower bound.
