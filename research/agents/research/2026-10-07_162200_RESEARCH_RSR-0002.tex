\section*{Primary literature and exact transfer}
The primary source was Ordyniak, Schidler, and Szeider, ``Backdoor DNFs,''
IJCAI 2021, pp.~1403--1409, DOI
\href{https://doi.org/10.24963/ijcai.2021/194}{10.24963/ijcai.2021/194}; the
open PDF is
\url{https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2021/0194.pdf}.
The expanded journal version is JCSS 144 (2024), article 103547, DOI
\href{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcss.2024.103547}{10.1016/j.jcss.2024.103547}
\cite{ordyniak2021bdnf}.
The following items were checked in the paper itself.

\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{Definition, p.~1405:} a $\mathcal C$-backdoor DNF is a set
of partial assignments $\mathcal G$ such that every
$F\upharpoonright\tau$ lies in $\mathcal C$ and the associated DNF is a
tautology.  The base class has polynomial membership and SAT algorithms and
is assumed self-reducible.
\item[] \textbf{Observation 1:} the variables used by a backdoor DNF form a
strong $\mathcal C$-backdoor.  Hence the mechanism compresses branching; it
does not evade the existence of strong-backdoor structure.
\item[] \textbf{Observation 2:} a $k$-term backdoor DNF uses at most $k-1$
variables.
\item[] \textbf{Theorem 1:} Horn backdoor DNFs can be arbitrarily smaller
than Horn backdoor trees in the stated measure.
\item[] \textbf{Theorem 3:} detection is FPT in the number of terms/leaves
for 2CNF, Horn, AntiHorn, $2$CNF$\cup$Horn, and
$2$CNF$\cup$AntiHorn.
\item[] \textbf{Theorem 4:} combining Horn and AntiHorn instead is
W[2]-hard for the corresponding detection problems.
\end{description}

The exact transferable identity is
\[
 \SAT(F)=\bigvee_{\tau\in\mathcal G}
 \SAT(F\upharpoonright\tau).
\]
If $F$ is satisfiable, any satisfying full assignment satisfies at least one
term because the DNF is tautological, and its restriction satisfies the
corresponding residual.  Conversely, any satisfying residual assignment
combined with its term satisfies $F$.  This is a direct theorem transfer to
an exact forward OR plan.  What does not transfer is a fixed exponent for
acquisition, a fixed-power encoded residual bound, universal coverage, or a
small live-space implementation.

The recursive-backdoor cross-check was M\"ahlmann, Siebertz, and Vigny,
``Recursive Backdoors for SAT,'' MFCS 2021, Theorem framework at
\url{https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2021.73},
and Dreier, Ordyniak, and Szeider, ``SAT Backdoors: Depth Beats Size,'' ESA
2022, at
\url{https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2022.46}.
Those results justify exact restricted algorithms, not bounded depth or a
general-SAT shrinker.

\section*{Concrete model and proof attempt}
For $s\ge1$, let $r=2^s-s$ and define the Horn-paper family $F_s$ on
$p,a_1,\ldots,a_s,q_1,\ldots,q_r$ by
\[
 (a_i\vee p)\quad(1\le i\le s),\qquad
 (a_1\vee\cdots\vee a_s\vee q_j\vee\neg p)
 \quad(1\le j\le r).
\]
The source paper gives the $s+2$ assignments
\[
 p=0;qquad p=1,\ a_1=\cdots=a_s=0;\qquad
 p=1,\ a_i=1\quad(1\le i\le s).
\]
They form a tautological DNF: if $p=0$ the first term holds; if $p=1$,
either all $a_i$ are zero or some $a_i$ is one.  Every residual is Horn.

\begin{lemma}[BDNF-WRAPPER-GATE1]
In the ordinary globally indexed clause-list encoding, the displayed exact
backdoor DNF has $M_s^{o(1)}$ terms, but one residual has encoded length
$M_s^{1-o(1)}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
There are $\Theta(2^s)$ long clauses, each with $\Theta(s)$ literal
occurrences, and $\Theta(2^s)$ globally indexed variables; hence an index
uses $\Theta(s)$ bits and
\[
 M_s=\Theta(2^s s^2).
\]
The DNF has $s+2=M_s^{o(1)}$ terms.  Under
$p=1,a_1=\cdots=a_s=0$, every binary clause is satisfied and every long
clause becomes the unit clause $(q_j)$.  This residual has length
\[
 L_s=\Theta(2^s s).
\]
Therefore
\[
 \frac{\log L_s}{\log M_s}
 =\frac{s+\log s+O(1)}{s+2\log s+O(1)}\longrightarrow1,
\]
so $L_s=M_s^{1-o(1)}$.  No fixed $b<1$ bounds every explicit residual.
\end{proof}

This falsifies only the raw explicit-residual realization as a fixed-power
shrinker.  Direct Horn solving may return a Boolean value instead; implicit
access may avoid materialization.  Those escapes need separate acquisition,
bit-access, running-time, and workspace proofs.  No machine lower bound is
inferred from the residual length.

\section*{Transfer classification and post-quantum ledger}
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{Directly transferable theorem:} the primary definition and
tautology argument give exact forward OR semantics; Observation~1 gives the
strong-backdoor containment; Theorem~3 gives FPT detection only for the five
listed base classes.
\item[] \textbf{Useful analogy needing a bridge:} viewing terms as a set
cover of the Boolean cube helps formulate coverage and exact finite tests.
It does not make a succinct tautology certificate or favorable acquisition
free; a resource theorem is still required.
\item[] \textbf{Failed transfer:} few terms do not imply fixed-power small
ordinary-CNF residuals, as BDNF-WRAPPER-GATE1 shows.  Nor does FPT in term
count imply a favorable fixed $(c,s)$ when the parameter grows with input.
\end{description}

None of the mandated post-quantum prompts supplies a model-matched theorem
for this barrier.  Schur squares, quadratic relations, Hilbert functions,
and collision entropy concern different algebraic objects.  Black-box
worst-case/average-case extraction remains an analogy without an NP transfer
theorem.  Collision-binding and random-oracle arguments would introduce
inadmissible assumptions.  Status: \textsc{Failed Transfer} for this run,
not a negative claim about their other uses.
