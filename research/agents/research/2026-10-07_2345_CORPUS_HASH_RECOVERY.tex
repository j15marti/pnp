\begin{abstract}
This report reconciles the project's primary artifacts concerning four-, six-
and eight-digit hashes, adaptive fingerprints, collision handling, execution
provenance, random bitstreams, prime labels, precommitment and ratchets.  The
strongest exact conclusion is a separation of roles.  A short fingerprint may
index candidates safely when every reuse is certified by exact replay, but it
does not reduce the number of semantic continuation classes.  If replay is
omitted, collision soundness is itself the missing semantic quotient theorem.
For restriction covers selected only through a short source summary, the exact
unique-witness guard
\[
        B+\log_2 q+\ell\ge n
\]
rules out the desired general-input contraction.  Hashes, bitstreams, primes
and ratchets therefore remain useful infrastructure after a source-dependent
cover theorem is available; none of the recovered artifacts constructs that
cover with the required acquisition cost.  P versus NP remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Status, target and terminology}

The target called \textsc{COVER-SHRINK1} in the current working corpus is an
original-SAT-scale transformation which, on an encoded instance of length
$M$, constructs before any current-scale call to the assumed SAT decider at
most
\[
       q(M)\le M^{1/3+o(1)}
\]
ordinary SAT children, each of encoded length at most
\[
       L(M)\le M^{2/3+o(1)},
\]
with an exact combiner, $M^{1+o(1)}$ construction work and a compatible joint
workspace bound.  Under the previously proved recursive accounting, those
parameters would give time exponent $C=1$, explicit-child space exponent
$\rho=2/3$, and
\[
        C+\rho=5/3<\gammats\approx1.8019377358.
\]
This report does not construct that transformation.

Every recovered artifact using ``four, six or eight digits'' in the hash
experiments means \emph{hexadecimal} digits: respectively 16, 24 and 32 bits.
The older prototype parameter \texttt{digest\_bytes=8} instead means 64 bits,
or sixteen hexadecimal digits.  No primary project artifact was recovered in
which the relevant four/six/eight settings meant decimal digits.  If decimal
characters are intended, $d$ characters carry $d\log_2 10$ bits; none of the
asymptotic conclusions changes.

\paragraph{Global status.}
The exact indexing, counting and recurrence statements below are useful and
source-checkable.  The load-bearing source-dependent semantic selector and its
favorable acquisition bound remain \Open.  Consequently P versus NP remains
open.

\section{Exact boundary theorems}

\subsection{A verified hash indexes classes but does not create them}

Let $X_1,\ldots,X_Q$ be WRAPPER states and let $\equiv$ be the exact semantic
congruence required by every later use of a state.  Let $R$ be the number of
represented $\equiv$-classes and let $H$ be an $h$-bit fingerprint.

\paragraph{HASH-INDEX-NO-SHRINK1 (\Exact).}
Suppose cache reuse is permitted only after a sound verifier proves
$X_i\equiv X_j$.  If the verifier is complete, exact deduplication retains
exactly one representative per class, so
\[
             q_{\rm out}=R.
\]
If the verifier is sound but incomplete, then $q_{\rm out}\ge R$.  Both
statements are independent of $h$.  The proof is immediate: soundness forbids
identifying distinct classes, while completeness identifies every later
member of an existing class.  The fingerprint changes only which pairs are
offered to the verifier.

If equal hashes are merged without verification, correctness requires
\[
       H(x)=H(y)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x\equiv y.       \tag{1}
\]
Consequently $2^h\ge R$.  Constructing a cheap small-range map satisfying
(1) is already a semantic quotient theorem.  It cannot be inferred from hash
collision resistance, finite absence of collisions, equal current SAT bits,
or distinct provenance labels.

\paragraph{ADAPTIVE-EXACT1 (\Exact).}
In a multilevel cache, fingerprints may begin with a short prefix and extend
only colliding buckets.  If every prospective reuse is finally guarded by the
same exact congruence test and a miss invokes the uncached exact computation,
the returned Boolean value is identical to caching-disabled execution for
every hash function, prefix schedule and bucket cap.  Induction over the
solver call graph proves the claim.  Extensions affect time and storage only.

\subsection{Collision and replay ledger}

If $Q$ inequivalent states are assigned to $B=2^h$ buckets and bucket $z$ has
size $m_z$, a chained exact cache performs
\[
       P(H)=\sum_{z=1}^{B}\binom{m_z}{2}
\]
unequal candidate comparisons.  Convexity and Cauchy--Schwarz give the exact
lower bound
\[
       P(H)\ge \max\left\{0,\frac{Q^2/B-Q}{2}\right\}.       \tag{2}
\]
The hostile maximum is $\binom Q2$, attained when all states collide.  This is
a bound for the declared cache design, not for arbitrary SAT algorithms.

For independent uniform $h$-bit fingerprints,
\[
       \mathbb E[P]=\frac{\binom Q2}{2^h}.                   \tag{3}
\]
Under the declared streaming ledger
\[
   Q=M^{u+o(1)},\qquad E(M)=M^{v+o(1)},\qquad
   h=\kappa\log_2 M,
\]
where $E$ is the state-read/replay scale, the expected cost exponent obeys
\[
       C_{\rm exp}\ge
       \max\{u+v,\,2u-\kappa+v\}.                           \tag{4}
\]
At $u=1/3,v=2/3$, a constant-width hash leaves an expected replay exponent
$4/3$.  Choosing $h=(1/3)\log_2M$ reduces expected collision replay to the
linear scan scale; choosing $h=(2/3)\log_2M$ makes the expected number of false
pairs $O(1)$.  A fixed public map still admits the all-collision family, and a
fresh random seed gives an expected randomized bound rather than the required
deterministic all-input bound.

\subsection{Short summaries cannot select a universal cover}

For $x\in\{0,1\}^n$, let $F_x$ be the unit-clause CNF with unique satisfying
assignment $x$.  Suppose a constructor observes only a $B$-bit summary of
$F_x$ and emits at most $q$ partial assignments, each leaving at most $\ell$
variables live.

\paragraph{SUMMARY-COVER-GUARD1 (\Exact).}
If the emitted restrictions cover the unique witness for every $F_x$, then
\[
                \boxed{B+\log_2 q+\ell\ge n}.               \tag{5}
\]
Indeed, some summary fiber contains at least $2^{n-B}$ assignments, while the
same $q$ restrictions are emitted for every member of that fiber and cover at
most $q2^\ell$ assignments.  Thus $q2^\ell\ge2^{n-B}$.

The summary-free special case is OBLIVIOUS-COVER-GUARD1:
\[
                q2^\ell\ge2^n.                              \tag{6}
\]
For the ordinary unit-formula encoding $M=\Theta(n\log n)$, the target
$B=O(\log M)$, $q=M^{1/3+o(1)}$ and $\ell=M^{2/3+o(1)}$ makes the left side of
(5) $o(n)$.  Four, six or eight fixed hexadecimal digits contribute only a
constant to $B$.

The same counting yields a useful random-seed guard.  Within the largest
$B$-summary fiber, if each source-independent seed emits $q$ restrictions,
some unique witness is covered with probability at most
\[
        \min\{1,\,2^Bq2^{\ell-n}\}.                          \tag{7}
\]
Polynomial repetition merely multiplies $q$.  A constructor that reads and
uses detailed source information escapes (5)--(7), but the cost and semantic
correctness of that source-dependent extraction are precisely the open
COVER-SHRINK1 obligation.

\section{Randomness, ratchets and provenance}

\subsection{Random bitstreams}

The Search-G construction fixes a pre-cut source record $X$, retained state
$P(X)$ and side information $Z(X)$, then reveals a fresh block $B$ selecting a
delayed query $f_B(X)$.  Its exact response-signature theorem counts
\[
       \Sigma(X)=(f_B(X))_B
\]
inside each $Z$-fiber.  If no new $X$-dependent information arrives after the
cut, retained state must distinguish the response signatures, yielding the
state lower bound recorded in the matrix.  The theorem is a valid
precommitment/causality statement.  It does not rule out rereading $X$ or
rerunning a deterministic selector after $B$ arrives.

The exact future-guessing control also matters: a nondeterministic machine may
guess $B$ before revelation and retain the branch whose guess matches the
realized block.  Polynomially many future bits do not create exponential
nondeterministic path length.  Under $P=NP$, the recovered REACQ1,
RANDCLOSE1, TRACECLOSE1 and PH-provenance closure results block freshness alone
from becoming a superpolynomial contradiction.  Randomness can still be used
for a Las Vegas cache when every hit is exactly verified, but then its expected
cost, seed search and fallback must be charged.

\subsection{Precommitment and ratchets}

The Library precommit experiment records 480 challenges where an external
token was ambiguous from $F$ plus the SAT bit.  All 960 native existential
queries were satisfiable, and all 480 wrong-token claims passed the native
existential check.  Thus external hiddenness is not native existential
binding.

In the ratchet-link test, an injective commitment accepted the correct joint
continuation in 1,000/1,000 trials and rejected every wrong continuation.  A
nonbinding commitment allowed a wrong continuation in 1,000/1,000 trials.  In
the length-six vector control, injective commitment left zero of 200 records
hidden, while a parity commitment left all 200 hidden.  These are finite
binding/hiding controls, not resource lower bounds.

The dynamic information-transfer and phase-dichotomy audits further show that
freshness need not force algebraic rank progress and that rank stall plus a
compact annihilator need not give the required density.  Their canonical
source-specific phase theorem and the transfer from arbitrary SAT computation
remain open.

\subsection{Prime lineage}

Position-indexed event primes give an exact ordered code
\[
        P(h)=\prod_i p_i^{c(e_i)}
\]
with $O(T\log T)$ bits under polynomial event codes.  An operator-type-only
product loses order.  The CRT construction gives an exact Boolean algebra on
prime coordinates, but plain positive-prime arithmetic is not a full
provenance semiring.  Under $P=NP$, polynomially balanced NP- or fixed-PH
replacement-witness relations are selectable in polynomial time, so such
labels alone do not escape reacquisition closure.  The PMCR audit's exact
validation trilemma is: trust is spoofable; oracle validation relativizes;
oracle-free validation restores source/replay cost.

At macro scale, after a genuine $(\eta,\theta)$ contraction has been proved,
recording the selected child at recursion level $j$ costs at most
$\eta\theta^j\log_2M+o(\log M)$ bits.  Summation gives
\[
        \frac{\eta}{1-\theta}\log_2M+o(\log M).
\]
This is the justified role for prime labels in the Williams spine: compact
post-contraction lineage, not pre-contraction power shrinkage.

\section{Source selectors and the only exact merge found}

The three small selector data files are finite diagnostics.  With four source
bits, SOURCE-SELECTOR records average future-service class counts 14 for 3-SAT
and 5.4 for 2-SAT.  AFFINE-SOURCE-SELECTOR records 11.67 and 4.33 classes.  The
full restriction lattice records 5.125 for 3-SAT, 1.625 for 2-SAT, 8.75 for
Horn and 4 for XOR.  Horn exceeding 3-SAT on this statistic is an explicit
easy-family hostile control.  The JSON files do not contain a general
constructor, acquisition exponent or asymptotic coverage proof.

CLAUSE-DOM-SELECT1 is the strongest recovered source-checkable merge of the
right logical type.  For residual CNFs $R_1,R_2$ on the same remaining
variables, if
\[
       \operatorname{Clauses}(R_2)
       \subseteq \operatorname{Clauses}(R_1),
\]
then $R_1\Rightarrow R_2$ and
\[
       \SAT(R_1)\lor\SAT(R_2)=\SAT(R_2).
\]
It can therefore delete $R_1$ from an OR frontier without a SAT query.
However, at the tested $n=18$ depth the clause-dominance frontiers were 144,
110, 23 and 32 on planted 3-SAT, random 3-SAT, Horn and 3-XOR controls, versus
the nominal target $n^{1/3}\approx2.62$.  The source-only
branch/eliminate/subsume hybrid also failed pigeonhole controls; on PHP$(7,6)$
it produced 107 children against a 6.65 budget and a maximum child of 80
against a 44.2 budget.  The exact rule is reusable, but no recovered theorem
shows that enough comparable pairs exist on every general input.

\section{Strongest justified composition}

The most favorable composition supported by all recovered artifacts is the
following conditional chain:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\boxed{\text{full-source witness-preserving selector/cover}}
\quad\xRightarrow{\Open}\quad
q\le M^{1/3+o(1)},\quad |G_i|\le M^{2/3+o(1)}
\\[2mm]
&\xRightarrow{\text{exact residual certificate/replay}}
\text{sound semantic classes}
\\[2mm]
&\xRightarrow{\text{adaptive }h\asymp(1/3)\log M}
\text{expected linear collision handling on the declared workload}
\\[2mm]
&\xRightarrow{\text{STREAMCOMP1/BATCHSETUP1/ORBITBATCH1}}
\text{fully charged generation, replay and shared setup}
\\[2mm]
&\xRightarrow{\text{MACRO-LINEAGE-SPACE1}}
O(\log M)\text{ realized route lineage}
\\[2mm]
&\xRightarrow{\text{RECURCLOSE1}}
(C,\rho)=(1,2/3)
\xRightarrow{\text{Williams endpoint}}
\bot.
\end{aligned}                                                     \tag{8}
\]

Every arrow after the boxed one has a precise conditional theorem or an
explicit finite implementation.  The boxed arrow is not supplied by the hash,
randomness, prime or ratchet corpus.  A deterministic implementation may use
an exact dictionary rather than a randomized fingerprint; either way, all
canonicalization, comparison, replay and retained-state costs remain charged.

The abstract Witness-Preserving Quantitative Pruning Lemma (WP-QPL) is the
strongest formal consumer of the missing first arrow.  It shows how uniformly
bounded frontier width plus cheap oriented dominance certificates yields a
deterministic recursive algorithm.  Its load-bearing premise remains an
arbitrary exact SAT decider $A$ to a certificate generator whose acquisition
exponent has coefficient below one in $A$'s exponent and whose width is
uniformly favorable.  Neither short image size nor hash collision density
implies that premise.

\section{Duplication, contradiction and barrier audit}

\subsection{Previously solved components that should not be reopened}

The corpus already supplies exact decision semantics for Shannon OR,
component AND and recursive small-leaf construction in the double wrapper;
conditional recurrence closure; implicit-input charging; shared-setup
accounting; exact macro-lineage storage; and collision-safe bounded cache
reuse.  The current gap is not Boolean correctness, the mere existence of
small emitted leaves, or the failure to remember that acquisition must be
charged.  It is a favorable simultaneous general-input bound on construction,
total branching, child length, replay and workspace.

\subsection{Prior statements that do not compose into COVER-SHRINK1}

\begin{itemize}
\item BAD-SEED compresses exponentially many routes into a polynomial circuit;
  it therefore defeats explicit-route multiplicity as a lower-bound resource.
\item Execution hashes and prime labels refine implementation histories, while
  COVER-SHRINK1 needs a small semantic quotient.  Refinement cannot establish
  the missing coarsening.
\item NR-001 constructs a canonical minimum SAT circuit under $P=NP$, but only
  with unspecified polynomial construction and circuit-size exponents.  It
  removes wrapper variance, not the Williams quantitative gap.
\item RESTRICTED-ACCESS-ELIMINATION1 is a necessity theorem inside a declared
  extensional compiler model, not an extractor of a shrinker from arbitrary
  SAT code.
\item The algebraic SLICECOVER results concern spans and slice covers in a
  different object.  No source-matched SAT restriction-cover bridge is proved.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hostile controls and no-go boundaries}

BLACKBOX-MACRO-NOGO1 shows that a Williams-landing shrinker using an assumed
SAT decider only through smaller-instance decision bits would recursively give
the forbidden algorithm directly.  Any successful implication must therefore
use the fixed code of the assumed decider in a load-bearing non-black-box way.
PARTIAL-EVAL-K-CARRY1 shows that naive symbolic specialization materializes
$M^{K+o(1)}$ work.  The code-specialized DAG audits then show that local state
hashing, common-subexpression elimination and exact configuration merging do
not remove $K$ generically; DEAD, COPY, DELAY and RECOMP wrappers expose the
representation dependence, while pairwise-distinct useful transition data
can leave the full $M^K$ skeleton.

Thus the surviving object is a SAT-specific, wrapper-covariant, $K$-neutral
semantic self-improvement theorem.  Generic program compression, fixed-width
hashing, fresh labels, proof-of-history commitments and current-answer merging
do not instantiate it.  The affine/XOR, Horn, 2-SAT, bounded-treewidth,
independent-product, padding, maximal semantic-oracle and algebraic-oracle
controls remain mandatory for any proposed selector.

\paragraph{Relativization and algebrization.}
Exact replay, summary counting, ordinary hashing and the local SAT identities
relativize; they cannot be the final nonrelativizing hinge.  Prime and ratchet
syntax does not establish nonalgebrization.  No large constructive circuit
property is asserted here, so this report introduces no Natural-Proofs claim.

\section{What changed relative to the main manuscripts}

No main manuscript was edited.  The current OPEN conclusion is unchanged.
This reconciliation narrows several phrases that could otherwise be read too
broadly:

\begin{itemize}
\item Collision handling is solved exactly at the cache-correctness level by
  residual replay.
\item Four/six/eight hash settings are finite engineering choices, not missing
  semantic merge rules.
\item Prime lineage and ratchets have a valid post-contraction audit role.
\item The exact general-input missing theorem is the cheap full-source
  witness-preserving selector/cover, not a larger hash or another provenance
  label.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Adopted.}
HASH-INDEX-NO-SHRINK1, ADAPTIVE-EXACT1, SUMMARY-COVER-GUARD1,
OBLIVIOUS-COVER-GUARD1, CLAUSE-DOM-SELECT1, WP-QPL as conditional accounting,
the Search-G response-signature theorem, MACRO-LINEAGE-SPACE1, and the charged
STREAMCOMP1/ORBITBATCH1/BATCHSETUP1 interface.

\paragraph{Rejected as a COVER-SHRINK1 construction.}
Fixed-width hash equality, collision resistance, finite no-collision evidence,
route diversity, current SAT-bit equality, random-seed freshness, raw prime
products, native existential token checks, and generic local DAG hashing.

\paragraph{Retired as generic lower-bound implications.}
Exponential route count forces exponential representation; final SAT
correctness forces lineage retention; random freshness forces costly
reacquisition; binding alone forces computational hardness; and syntactic
provenance multiplicity forces arbitrary solver cost.

\section{Next source-checkable target}

The next theorem should be stated without hashes:

\begin{quote}
For every hypothetical polynomial-time exact SAT decider $A$, construct from
the full source $F$ and the fixed code of $A$, without running $A$ on an
$M^{1-o(1)}$ instance or materializing its $M^K$ computation, a uniformly
checkable family of at most $M^{1/3+o(1)}$ ordinary SAT children of length at
most $M^{2/3+o(1)}$, together with an exact witness-preserving combiner, in
$M^{1+o(1)}$ work and compatible workspace.
\end{quote}

A weaker useful advance would prove that one source-checkable dominance rule,
possibly extending clause inclusion, appears with enough density on every
frontier and can be found below parent-scale semantic cost.  Hashes should then
be used only to index proposed comparable pairs, with exact certificates or
replay resolving every collision.
