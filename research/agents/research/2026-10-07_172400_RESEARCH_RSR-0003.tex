\section*{Primary literature and exact transfer}
Gwynne and Kullmann define $r_1$ as unit-clause propagation, $r_2$ as
complete failed-literal elimination, and $r_{k+1}$ as the look-ahead version
of $r_k$ \cite{gwynne2014nested}.  Their Theorem~5.7 identifies hardness at
most $k$ with derivability of implicates by $k$-times nested input resolution
(equivalently bounded tree-resolution Horton--Strahler number).  The same
paper records Horn clause-sets at level one and 2-CNF at level two.  These are
\textbf{direct theorem transfers}: for $A\in\mathrm{UC}_k$, every semantic
implicate $C$ of $A$ is detected after falsifying $C$ by $r_k$.

The transfer has two exact limitations.  Recognizing membership in
$\mathrm{UC}_k$ is coNP-complete for each fixed $k\geq1$; the selector does
not perform or require that recognition.  More importantly, the hierarchy is
complete only when depth may grow.  Exhaustive failed-literal work also has
conditional fine-grained lower-bound evidence \cite{jarvisalo2014failed};
this is a warning, not an unconditional lower bound and not a component of
the proof.

Proof-complexity nested resolution is therefore an \textbf{EXACT
OBJECT/THEOREM TRANSFER}.  A control-theoretic reading of $r_k$ as a bounded
look-ahead barrier certificate is only an \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}; no
control theorem gives general-CNF implication density.  Collision entropy,
Schur-square, or hash ideas from post-quantum cryptanalysis are a
\textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} here: label collision does not orient semantic
implication and is unnecessary once a nested-resolution certificate exists.

\section*{Exact construction and proof}
For ordinary CNFs $A,B$, write $A\Rightarrow_kB$ when, for every clause
$C\in B$, the reduction
\[
 r_k\!\left(A\wedge\bigwedge_{\ell\in C}\neg\ell\right)=\{\bot\}.
\]
Soundness of every $r_k$ reduction gives $A\Rightarrow_kB\Longrightarrow
A\models B$.  The relation is monotone in $k$, and
$\Rightarrow_1$ is exactly the RUP relation used by WRP-0003.

\begin{theorem}[NESTED-IMPLICATION-SELECT1]
Fix $k\geq1$.  Given explicit CNFs $R_1,\ldots,R_q$ of encoded length at most
$m$, form the directed graph with $i\to j$ iff $R_i\Rightarrow_kR_j$.
Retain one representative from every sink SCC of its condensation.  For the
retained index set $K$,
\[
 \bigvee_{i=1}^{q}\SAT(R_i)=\bigvee_{i\in K}\SAT(R_i).
\]
There are zero SAT-oracle calls.  A direct depth-first implementation takes
$O(q^2m^{2k})$ time and $O(qm+q^2+km)$ live space.  It emits at most $q$
children of length at most $m$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every graph edge is sound semantic implication.  Every vertex of the finite
condensation DAG reaches a sink, so satisfiability of a discarded residual
implies satisfiability of a retained one.  The retained residuals form a
subset of the original frontier, proving the reverse implication.

Let $T_k(m)$ bound one $r_k$ contradiction test.  Occurrence-list unit
propagation gives $T_1(m)=O(m)$.  At level $k+1$, at most $O(m)$ assignments
are committed.  Before each commitment, a scan of at most $O(m)$ candidate
literals invokes an $r_k$ test on a formula of length at most $m$.  Thus
$T_{k+1}(m)=O(m^2T_k(m)+m^2)=O(m^{2k+1})$, whence
$T_k(m)=O(m^{2k-1})$.  One ordered formula pair checks at most $O(m)$ target
clauses, giving $O(m^{2k})$ time per pair and $O(q^2m^{2k})$ total time.
Depth-first recursion stores at most one $O(m)$ reduction state per level;
explicit inputs and the graph contribute $O(qm+q^2)$ space.  SCC computation
is linear in graph size.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Class-completeness corollary.}
If $A\in\mathrm{UC}_k$ and $A\models B$, then $A\Rightarrow_kB$.  It is
enough to apply Theorem~5.7 of \cite{gwynne2014nested} to the prime implicate
subsuming each clause of $B$; falsifying the larger clause also falsifies the
prime implicate.  Consequently depth one is complete for Horn sources and
depth two for 2-CNF sources.  This corollary is conditional on the source
class property; it is not a cheap general-CNF class recognizer.

\section*{Strict level separation and hostile controls}
Let
\begin{align*}
A={}&(x\vee a\vee b)\wedge(x\vee a\vee\neg b)
\wedge(x\vee\neg a\vee b)\\
&\wedge(x\vee\neg a\vee\neg b)\wedge(y),
\qquad B=(x).
\end{align*}
Falsifying $x$ leaves four binary clauses over $a,b$ with no unit clause, so
RUP does not detect the contradiction.  Either failed-literal branch on $a$
creates opposite units on $b$, so $r_2$ does.  Hence $A\Rightarrow_2B$ but
$A\not\Rightarrow_1B$.  The extra unit $(y)$ ensures $B\not\models A$, so on
the OR frontier $[A,B]$ depth two retains only $B$ while depth one retains
both.

The complete-assignment residual family remains a semantic antichain for
every sound depth, and therefore all $2^n$ nodes survive.  Tautological
padding and signed variable renaming do not change the relation.  Independent
products retain their incomparable factors.  Oracle or algebraic replacement
can recognize easy families by other means, but that does not falsify the
selector; it shows why the selector is not a machine lower bound.  No source
program is inspected, and there are no original-scale/current-scale SAT
calls.

\section*{Finite existential iteration versus growing depth}
For every fixed $k$, NESTED-IMPLICATION-SELECT1 names one uniform algorithm
with a fixed polynomial exponent.  A later ARC proof may use finitely many
such independently witnessed algorithms without compiling them into one
universal source transformer.  By contrast, setting $k=k(m)\to\infty$ turns
the displayed bound into $m^{2k(m)}$ and is not a fixed-polynomial uniform
algorithm.  Completeness of the union $\bigcup_k\mathrm{UC}_k$ therefore
cannot be promoted into an ARC closure or WRAPPER realization by choosing
input-dependent depth.
