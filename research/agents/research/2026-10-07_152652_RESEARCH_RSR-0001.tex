\begin{abstract}
This run audits the sparsification lemma as a forward-only disjunctive inner
transformation for the recursive WRAPPER spine.  A later quantitative theorem
repairs the apparent fixed-$\varepsilon$ uniformity gap, but its explicit
dependence on $\varepsilon$ does not certify fixed-power encoded shrinkage when
fanout is forced to be polynomial.  This retires one direct theorem transfer,
not sparsification itself and not arbitrary SAT transformations.  The
SAT-specific inner transformation remains open, and P versus NP remains open.
\end{abstract}

\item[] \textbf{Status:} the cited sparsification statements are
\textsc{Known Theorem}.  Their direct use to prove the present

general-SAT shrinker is \textsc{Open}.
\item[] \textbf{Exact input quantifiers:} fixed clause width $k\ge2$; every
$k$-CNF $F$ with $n$ variables and ordinary encoded length $M$; a chosen
$\varepsilon\in(0,1]$; one uniform sparsification algorithm in the quantitative
formulation.
\item[] \textbf{Required output quantifiers:} constants $a$ and $b<1$,
independent of $F$ and $M$, such that the same realization emits at most
$M^{a+o(1)}$ explicit ordinary-CNF children of length $M^{b+o(1)}$, with
generation, access, combination, and workspace charged.
\item[] \textbf{Same-realization object:} the explicit sequence of CNF
children output by the sparsifier, combined by OR before any child SAT answer
is known.
\item[] \textbf{Weakest useful improvement:} an effective uniform sparsity
rate $C(k,\varepsilon)=\widetilde O(\varepsilon^{-d})$ with $d<k-1$ would
certify power shrink on the maximally dense $n^k$ scale at polynomial fanout.
It would still need a separate mechanism or portfolio theorem for already
sparse formulas.
\end{description}

\section{Primary literature and exact transfer}
Impagliazzo--Paturi--Zane Theorem~1 gives the set-cover form of the
sparsification algorithm.  Corollary~1 states that for every
$\varepsilon>0$ and positive $k$ there is a constant $C$ such that every
$n$-variable $k$-CNF is an exact disjunction of at most
$2^{\varepsilon n}$ $k$-CNFs having at most $Cn$ clauses, computable in
$\operatorname{poly}(n)2^{\varepsilon n}$ time \cite{ipz2001}.

The cross-domain transfer of interest is from SERF reductions, parameterized
by variable count, to the WRAPPER interface, parameterized by encoded length.
This transfer is semantic theorem transfer, not merely analogy: the disjunction
is exact and the children are ordinary $k$-CNFs.  Its quantitative resource
conclusion must nevertheless be re-proved in the new parameter.

Calabro--Impagliazzo--Paturi Lemma~1 supplies the needed uniformity repair:
there exists one algorithm $A$ for all $k\ge2$, all
$\varepsilon\in(0,1]$, and all $k$-CNFs $\varphi$.  It outputs at most
$2^{\varepsilon n}$ children with $n^{O(k)}$ delay, preserves the union of
solution sets, and bounds each child by
\[
 |\varphi_i|=O\!\left((k/\varepsilon)^{3k}n\right)
\]
clauses \cite{calabro2010}.  Thus it is too strong to object merely that the
original corollary was stated for fixed $\varepsilon$.

Primary URLs opened in this run were
\url{https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.2001.1774}, the accessible author copy at
\url{https://www.ccs.neu.edu/home/viola/classes/papers/ImpagliazzoPaturiZane01Which.pdf},
and
\url{https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/CalabroImpagliazzoPaturi_2010_ipec.pdf}.

\section{IPZ-POLYFANOUT-TRANSFER-GATE1}
Let $M$ denote ordinary indexed-CNF bit length and let the desired fanout be
$M^{a+O(1)}$.  If $n\gg\log M$, the theorem's fanout bound forces
\[
 \varepsilon n\le (a+O(1))\log_2M,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon=O(\!\log M/n).
\]
Choosing this input-dependent value is allowed by the uniform quantitative
form.  Substitution into its clause bound yields
\[
 O\!\left(n\left(\frac{kn}{\log M}\right)^{3k}\right).
\]
For duplicate-free fixed-width formulas the maximally dense scale is
$M=n^{k+o(1)}$.  On that scale the displayed guarantee has nominal
$M$-power
\[
 \frac{3k+1}{k}-o(1)=3+\frac1k-o(1),
\]
which is worse than returning the original formula.  Taking the minimum with
that identity return certifies exponent one, not any fixed $b<1$.  On the
sparse scale $M=\Theta(n\log n)$, the input itself already has $O(n)$ clauses,
so a linear-clause conclusion likewise certifies no power shrink.

This proves only a transfer gate: the published upper bound is insufficient
to establish W2 at polynomial fanout.  It does not show that every actual
child is large, does not lower-bound the output of the algorithm, and does not
rule out source-dependent pruning, a different sparsifier, or a portfolio.
For a hypothetical rate $C=\widetilde O(\varepsilon^{-d})$, the dense-scale
child power would be $(d+1)/k$ up to polylogarithms.  Hence $d<k-1$ is the
rate threshold for this calculation, whereas the cited exponent $d=3k$ is
far outside it.

\section{Bounded executable model and hostile controls}
The command
\[
\texttt{timeout 30s python experiments/proof\_spines/
ipz\_transfer\_gate\_test.py}
\]
used seed 20261007 and an internal 25-second budget.  It completed 226 exact
integer/rational checks in 0.002 seconds.  For $k=3$, powers of two from
$n=256$ through $2^{20}$, and fanout target $M^2$, it checked sparse
$m=n$ and dense $m=n^3$ encodings.  Fixed $\varepsilon=1/20$ eventually
exceeded the polynomial fanout budget in both regimes.  The schedule
$\varepsilon=2\lceil\log_2M\rceil/n$ decreased toward zero and made fanout
polynomial.  Applying the optimistic hidden constant one in the published
$(k/\varepsilon)^{3k}n$ bound, then capping by the original formula, certified
child exponent exactly one in all samples.  A 128-case seeded encoding control
and rate degrees $d=0,\ldots,9$ reproduced the threshold $d<2$ for $k=3$.

\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{Easy-family and independent-product controls:} bounded-width
2-SAT, Horn, and independent-clause products may already be sparse.  The
theorem's linear-clause conclusion alone cannot certify their power shrink,
irrespective of their easy decision algorithms.
\item[] \textbf{Source-carry control:} these outputs are explicit CNFs and do
not need a parent pointer.  This is a genuine positive property, but it does
not repair the size exponent.
\item[] \textbf{Current-scale-call control:} the sparsifier is syntactic and
makes no call to the hypothetical SAT decider.  Its failure here is therefore
not the usual hidden-$K$ objection.
\item[] \textbf{Padding control:} padding can make the original formula look
power smaller relative to an inflated $M$.  The audit uses the normalized
explicit encoding and does not credit removable padding.
\item[] \textbf{Oracle/algebraic replacement control:} the exact disjunction
and its parameter arithmetic persist with symbolic or oracle labels.  A SAT
oracle could collapse to the answer bit, but that changes acquisition and
does not turn this syntactic theorem into the required nonrelativizing hinge.
\end{description}

\section{Manuscript reconciliation and open arrow}
Root \texttt{main5.tex} now records the uniformity repair, quantitative gate,
and its precise scope.  Root \texttt{main4.tex} received a dated review entry
with no mathematical status change.  The remaining useful arrow is a
source-dependent exact decomposition with a rate below the dense threshold
and a proved coverage mechanism for sparse inputs, or a different SAT-specific
successor altogether.  Its acquisition, explicit or virtual access, workspace,
and recursive return must belong to the same realization.

Finite existential iteration remains distinct from a growing-depth uniform
algorithm.  If one favorable return is eventually proved, the ARC finite
return-cycle lemma may use different uniform exact algorithms at successive
phases.  Nothing in sparsification supplies that favorable return, and no
growing-depth recursion is asserted.
