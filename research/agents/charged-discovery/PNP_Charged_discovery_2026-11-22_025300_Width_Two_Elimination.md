## Construction and proof

The map between assignments satisfying the exact-one factors and grouped assignments is bijective. For each ternary homogeneous parity check form a local relation on its distinct group identifiers. Conjoin all resulting relations. Each tuple test is exact by the assignment map, in both SAT and UNSAT directions. There is no Gaussian projection that could make the checks dense. Join the vertices of each table scope into a clique. Keep the scopes even for full or empty tables, so the following graph guarantee is independent of shortcuts.

Repeatedly select the lowest-ID current vertex v with at most two distinct neighbors. Form the bucket of every current table containing v. For each tuple on the union of bucket scopes minus v, mark it feasible exactly when some value of v satisfies all tables in the bucket simultaneously. Use the new table to replace the entire bucket and delete v. This is existential projection. Conjoining outside tables commutes with this projection since those tables omit v. The operation uses at most nine output tuples and three trials each. Empty scopes retain a true or false scalar. An isolated unconstrained vertex contributes true. Once all vertices are gone, the conjunction of scalar tables gives the exact decision. If the search for v fails, return internal UNKNOWN and switch to the correct fallback on the retained input.

For completeness of discovery on the stated class, define a width-two tree decomposition as a tree of bags of at most three vertices covering all vertices and edges, with the bags containing each vertex connected. Remove redundant leaf bags contained in their parent. A remaining leaf contains a vertex absent from its parent. This vertex occurs only there and has at most two neighbors. A single remaining bag has the same degree bound. With multiple components this argument applies to their disjoint union by connecting decomposition trees. Filling the neighbors of *any* degree-two vertex and deleting it produces a minor: contract its edge to one neighbor, removing loops and parallel edges. Zero- and one-neighbor elimination just delete vertices. Width at most two survives these operations: delete the discarded label from bags or replace both contracted endpoints by their common new name. For contraction the two bag subtrees meet because the edge had a bag; their union is connected. Thus the chosen greedy pivot always leaves another graph of width at most two. No search over candidate decomposition trees is necessary. Equivalently, an elimination sequence reaching the empty graph certifies the computed result by the projection invariant, without having to verify a separate tree-decomposition theorem.
