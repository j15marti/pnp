## Exact source and reduction

Let q be the number of distinct admitted supports. Form a loopless cubic multigraph G: supports are vertices, and each variable is the edge joining its two groups. Keep distinct variable identities on parallel edges. Thus d=3q/2 and q is even. A group v demands b_v in {1,2}. Source assignments correspond exactly to edge subsets having degree b_v at every v.

For an explicit generator, take any such G with distinct incident edge-ID triples, order vertices and incident IDs, and for each vertex emit the five signed triples excluding the assignments whose weight differs from b_v. A forbidden zero gives a positive literal, a forbidden one a negative literal. Then c=5q, d=3q/2. The general recognizer also admits duplicated clauses and arbitrary clause/literal order; necessarily c>=5q. No table with more than eight rows is materialized per group.

Use Tutte's incidence construction [1]. For each incidence (v,e) make a port. Connect the two ports of each edge e. At v add 3-b_v dummy vertices, each adjacent to all three ports there. The resulting graph H has

    v_H=6q-sum_v b_v <=5q,
    e_H=3q/2+3 sum_v(3-b_v) <=15q/2.

In a perfect matching every dummy consumes one local port. Exactly b_v ports remain for external matching, so the selected original edges satisfy v's demand. Conversely, from a demanded-degree edge subset match its external port pairs; locally biject the remaining ports to dummies. This covers all vertices. Therefore SAT(F) iff H has a perfect matching. In particular failure of the test certifies UNSAT, not UNKNOWN. These two directions are the specialized classical reduction, not a novelty claim.

Each source assignment has exactly product_v (3-b_v)! matching extensions. This identity is only a finite-audit cross-check. No polynomial exact-counting algorithm is asserted, and the Boolean target compiler is not claimed parsimonious. Strict symmetric C, ordered R, and historical S/Q are not substituted for Boolean decision.

K4 with all four demands one is SAT, with three edge-subset witnesses. Replacing one demand by two makes total degree odd and is UNSAT. Both source formulas have c=20,d=6 and the same target length. A stronger negative control is connected and has even total demand: take two K4s, subdivide edge 01 in each, and join the new subdivision vertices. Label the first half 0,...,4 and second 5,...,9. Demands (1,1,1,1,1,2,2,1,1,1) are infeasible. Each half has odd total demand, so its unique bridge must be selected. Vertex 9 then cannot select edges to 5 or 6. Both 5 and 6 must select their edges to 7 and 8, exceeding the latter demands one. This obstruction is a global matching constraint, yet remains polynomially decidable.

## Dynamic-transfer boundary

The rejected inference is that failure of a bipartite-flow representation or the presence of odd cycles makes expensive branching or repeated compatibility evaluation compulsory. It does not. A pays once for general matching and can cache the decision. This is a test of a possible next extension, not an attribution of that false claim to Construction.

For a concrete actual-event calibration, normalize A after paid solving: erase the span except for its Boolean result Y in a fixed origin cell, return the head there in a common READY state, then read and emit the result. This adds O(N) steps. On an equal prior over the two K4 labels, let U_1=Y, B_1 specify the public pair, model and solver, E_1 be this actual READY read, and Pi_1 the bit read. The identity decoder is exact. Conditional code length is always one, so the code is prefix-free; address and length are fixed, h_1=0, b=1, rho=1. Positioning and the real read are paid by A, and decoding the record costs constant time. Thus the shared inequality gives only

    E[T] >= (H(Y|B_1)-0)/(1*1) = 1.

Duplicating this event raises rho; supplying its cache value freely in B_1 sets the entropy to zero. There is no extra demand for all subset inequalities or all local witness choices. As a concrete failure of such a suffix decoder, K4 with all demands one and with all demands two are both SAT. The answer-only suffix is identical, while their demand vectors differ. If those inputs each have probability 1/4 and the odd-total UNSAT companion has probability 1/2, the suffix leaves 1/2 bit uncertainty about which demand vector was supplied. Public indexing cannot secretly encode that choice. Earlier input reads remain charged to the prefix and are not counted a second time.
