Every permutation on F₃ has the unique form i ↦ ai+x, with a in {1,−1}. This gives six distinct bijections, exhausting the possibilities. Write the matching as p(i)=s_e i+t_e and the triangle coloring as f_v(i)=a_v i+x_v. The two affine functions f_u(i) and f_v(p(i)) must differ at all three places. Unequal slopes give exactly one intersection because their difference has an invertible slope. Equal slopes give either zero or three intersections. Consequently the necessary and sufficient edge conditions are

    a_u = a_v s_e,             x_u − x_v ≠ a_v t_e  (mod 3).

Sign propagation detects any inconsistent cycle. Otherwise anchor one root slope at +1 in each component. Composing all palette choices in a component with negation preserves every inequality and reverses every slope, so fixing that anchor loses no solution. Set g_e=a_v t_e. Now satisfiability is exactly the existence of x:V→F₃ avoiding the single prescribed difference g_e on every oriented edge. Reversing an orientation uses p⁻¹ and replaces g_e by −g_e. Conversely, for every oriented Q and every specified g, take p_e(i)=i+g_e. The anchored slopes are then all +1, and exactly the original inequalities are recovered. Anchoring and reconstruction include isolated vertices. This is a decision equivalence for an individual labeling. The universal statement over all labelings of a fixed graph requires a separate theorem.

**Two inputs on the same quotient.** Král’, Pangrác and Voss [1] construct a bipartite planar graph Q of group chromatic number four. By the threshold definition [2], every abelian group of order at least four works for every edge labeling on Q, while the threshold three fails. The group witnessing that failure must have order three, hence is isomorphic to F₃. Select a failing labeling and realize it by rotations as above. This gives UNSAT with every sign positive. On the same Q, the zero labeling gives SAT by assigning the two bipartition classes residues 0 and 1. All dimensions and all matching signs coincide. This settles the existence question left by the previous report. The published construction is used through the authors' statement. An independently transcribed graph and explicit failing edge list are still outstanding.

**A positive guard.** Lai and Li [2, Theorem 1.1] prove group chromatic number at most three for a plane graph of girth at least four whose 4-cycles are pairwise vertex-disjoint and facial and whose 4- and 5-cycles share no vertex. Thus every gain assignment on such a quotient is feasible. A implements the following sufficient recognition test without receiving an embedding. Admit a simple 2-connected planar bipartite quotient with a unique 4-cycle C, provided Q−V(C) is nonempty and connected. No diagonal of C is possible by bipartiteness. In a plane drawing all remaining vertices lie on one side of C by connectedness and the Jordan curve theorem. Every remaining edge stays on that side since there are no chords. Choose an outer face on the occupied side. C now bounds an empty interior, and bipartiteness excludes all 5-cycles. All hypotheses hold. A sign conflict gives UNSAT and consistency gives SAT. Two even cycles of lengths 4 and at least 6 sharing exactly one edge provide an infinite class. This is a sufficient guard and can overlap earlier guards.

## Lower-bound application and the missing theorem

Linial [3] gives an Ω(log* q) deterministic synchronous round lower bound to produce a proper 3-coloring on the q-cycle in the distributed model with distinct identifiers, even with a consistent orientation and unbounded local processing and messages. The tasks differ already on identity cycle bundles. A can recognize a cycle quotient, check the signs and return SAT on every balanced instance by the previously proved degree-two rule. It never computes the vertex offsets on that branch. Removing one sign by flipping a cycle edge to a transposition changes the answer, so the recognized cycle class contains both labels. The local round theorem places no obligation on the suffix of this offline decision execution. A small suffix also gives no upper bound on the elapsed time of distributed coloring.

A transfer would need a theorem extracting a valid distributed coloring, with information at each vertex restricted to a proved radius, from the actual events of **every fixed globally correct offline decider** on the relevant inputs. It must pay for joint preprocessing, location of retained data and any reuse, and relate the resulting rounds to the same execution's work. No such extraction is proved. A witness assembled from multiple calls on perturbed formulas has different executions. The four-cycle counterexample rules out one shortcut, and supplies no compulsory-execution theorem. On unrestricted Q, even zero gains contain ordinary graph three-colorability: precisely x_u ≠ x_v on every edge. The group representation alone gives no all-input polynomial algorithm.
