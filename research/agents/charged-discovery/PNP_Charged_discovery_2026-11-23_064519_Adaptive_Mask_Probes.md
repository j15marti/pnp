## One transfer and its scope

Shigemizu, Suzuki and Usami, Theorem 2, arXiv:1804.06601v1 (2018), treat a multi-branching AND-OR tree of height at most two, at least two leaves, independent leaf values with each zero probability strictly between zero and one, and unit-cost leaf probes. Their specified depth-first directional algorithm minimizes expected probes among **all** algorithms in that query model. The paper permits unequal OR arities and unary nodes. Its ordering compares expected OR cost divided by OR failure probability. We use this one primary theorem. [Paper, sections 2 and 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06601).

With independent fair active bits, piece i succeeds with p_i=1−2^(−k_i). Its expected depth-first cost is 2p_i by the truncated geometric sum. The ordering ratio is 2p_i/(1−p_i)=2(2^k_i−1), increasing with width. Sort the widths in nondecreasing order. Then the minimum over every exact adaptive raw-bit evaluator is

    V(C) = 2 sum_{i=1}^m product_{j=1}^i (1−2^(−k_(j))).

For one leaf the cost is exactly one directly. Every optimum can be compared to this value even if it chooses the next piece after seeing earlier answers. We claim existence of an optimum with the stated order, allowing ties. The bound V(C)≤14(1−(7/8)^m)<14 is sharp at each m by taking all C_i full.

**Worst case on the same interface.** Every deterministic exact evaluator has both a SAT input and an UNSAT input costing at least L distinct active reads. Answer zero to each fresh query in a piece until its last unread bit, then answer one. Rereads are consistent. Until all pieces have supplied a one the partial assignment has both accepting and rejecting completions. Thus the SAT trace reads all L bits. Flipping the last fresh answer leaves the preceding trace unchanged and gives an UNSAT input with all L reads. Reading every active bit gives the matching worst-case upper bound. This adversary chooses the input after fixing the solver and uses no counting task.

**Distribution control.** In each piece independently place exactly one one uniformly among its k_i active positions. The algorithm still owes exact decisions on every assignment. On each of these accepting executions it must read the one in every piece, since otherwise zeroing the unobserved one would preserve its trace and change the verdict. Condition on all other pieces. Before the first one in piece i, its distinct probes follow a fixed order determined by the other answers and an all-zero history in that piece. All k_i positions are queried on the all-zero counterfactual because the other pieces are positive and any unread position could be the unique one. The witness's rank in that order is uniform. Thus expected distinct probes are at least sum_i(k_i+1)/2, attained by scanning each piece up to its first one. For all widths three this is 2m. This control is an accepting-input performance statement about a solver required to reject its negative neighbors. Under a promise that every input has a witness in each piece a constant answer would suffice. A balanced two-label distribution can mix this prior at weight one half with a copy where the first piece is zeroed. This gives at least m expected reads for full widths, without needing a rejecting-side estimate.
