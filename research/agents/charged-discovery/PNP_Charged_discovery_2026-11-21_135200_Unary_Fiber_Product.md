## Why no source-row enumeration is necessary

Fix the anchor frame first. The first five anchors are assigned by a single map z↦s±z mod 5. Express all other colors in the identity frame before applying this map. On any admitted positive input, **every unsplit name is either constant or a unary function of one free x_i**. The proof uses the exact gate order of [1].

* The initial NOT x_0 depends on x_0 only; x_0 OR NOT x_0 is constant even when that bit is free. The selector pairs are functions of this true wire and the fixed masks, hence constant, including the unused outputs.
* U_i=A_i OR x_i is unary. The two operand negations and their OR in U_i AND B_i use at most that same coordinate. On the accepted product domain the resulting V_i is identically zero. Every gate in the final OR fold and the output tail is therefore constant.
* Each Boolean wire's three domain helpers depend only on the wire and the frame. Exactly one helper is allowed per excluded anchor and legal bit. For each OR gadget its first helper is a function only of the first operand (3 for zero, 0 for one); its second helper uses only the second (4 for zero, 2 for one). This includes the helpers of the tautology gate even though its output is constant. NOT introduces no additional gate helper.

All constants and unary color pairs can consequently be recovered by evaluating the all-zero assignment and each permitted single flip, then comparing columns. Single-flip agreement on an arbitrary circuit would be unsound: the function x_1 AND x_2 vanishes on these probes yet is nonconstant. The structural proof above is the admission certificate for this circuit family. For negative inputs no table is needed. For positive inputs the core rows described by independent choices of the free bits are exactly the satisfying rows: all gates are forced by the Boolean inputs in each frame, helpers extend uniquely, and every such evaluation satisfies the pin. This proves the converse as well.

## Full named diagram

Associate to every unsplit name v the complete list of its occurrence copies and all four private names for each consecutive equality link on that list. These inventories partition the split variables. Assign v's inventory to its sole free coordinate if nonconstant, and otherwise to a constant group. Free input x_i occurs itself, so each free group has a selector, the first copy of x_i. Reserve one occurrence copy of each of the five anchors for selecting the frame.

Branch by a decision trie on the 15 bits of the reserved anchor copies; accept just the ten ordered frame tuples as continuations. For a fixed frame, take a decomposable binary AND of the constant group and one factor per free group. Within a free group query the three selector bits once. Dispatch its two legal colors to the two possible group continuations, rejecting other codes. Within each continuation every source name in that group now has a known color p. Enforce this color on every copy by exact three-bit equality, skipping the already tested selectors. For each associated link enforce the two possible private four-color rows (p+1,p+2,p+3,p+4) and (p−1,p−2,p−3,p−4) modulo five by a 12-bit decision trie. Take a decomposable AND of these predicates. The constant group uses the same procedure once, skipping the reserved anchors. Every link has equal ports by the copy tests.

Every accepting traversal therefore gives a frame, a legal choice for every free input, all forced core colors, and a permitted orientation per link. Conversely the projection of any satisfying split assignment has precisely these data [1,3] and follows the corresponding branches. All 3n bits on a successful evaluation are checked; an invalid color code cannot pass. Private names are distinct, selectors are never requeried, and all AND children have disjoint syntactic scopes. This is a decision-DNNF and hence an allowed DLDD. These syntactic conditions are the standard decision/decomposable-AND definition [5].

Within one frame every inventory occurs in at most two continuations. A copy equality needs three tests, a link trie at most 24 tests, and combining each factor costs at most one binary AND per constituent. The free selector trie costs at most six tests per group. The outer trie costs at most 150 tests, plus two shared sinks. For example
    nodes ≤ 152 + 10 [ 8(3m_J) + 50E + 8k + 2 ] = O(n).
Identical nodes may be shared across frames, continuations and calls on the same named bits. This upper bound requires no such savings. In particular the sum over groups replaces the product over their row counts. Representing the rejecting branch costs one FALSE sink.

## Falsifiers, checks and limits

Four falsifiers for applications are a column depending jointly on two free bits, a private name shared by distinct links, an omitted disequality/invalid-code branch, or a fresh mapped output counted as a constant-cost cache hit. The first defeats single-flip certification in general, the second defeats decomposability, the third can create false witnesses, and the fourth defeats the scan-based cumulative bound. Complementing an authenticated Boolean predicate on the declared bit universe can include invalid three-bit codes; relative complement on legal colors requires its validity mask. Affine/Tseitin and private-leaf controls permit compact global scalar evaluators without requiring this representation. A fixed signed partition of normalized wires [4] does not itself describe the frame and private bits. Forced domains are explicitly used here. The decision-witness and matching controls do not force witness construction. The proof uses no deterministic configuration-count lower bound or oracle bypass; natural-proofs and algebrization questions are untouched.
