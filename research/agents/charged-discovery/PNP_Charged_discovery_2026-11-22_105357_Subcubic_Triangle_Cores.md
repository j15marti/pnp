Write p for an edge's matching permutation from u to v. A coloring of each triangle is a bijection f from its three places to the three colors. A valid pair must make f_v p f_u^{-1} fixed-point-free. The only possibilities are the two nonidentity rotations. In particular the vertex signs s satisfy s_v=s_u XOR parity(p). Verify these equations by choosing one root per component, propagating along a spanning forest and checking each chord. Any failed equation implies UNSAT. A global color transposition on a component allows its root sign to be fixed at zero without loss.

With consistent signs each vertex has a list of three bijections of its required sign. For a fixed f_u, the only forbidden f_v in its list is f_u p^{-1}, since the product in the preceding paragraph is even. This correspondence is bijective. Thus the remaining problem is an independent transversal of a correspondence cover with one three-element list per vertex and one matching of forbidden pairs per quotient edge. A transversal is equivalent in both directions to a coloring of the expanded graph, hence to feasibility of the authenticated source.

Consider a connected quotient component with a vertex of degree at most two. Root a spanning tree there and process vertices in decreasing tree distance. Each nonroot has a later neighbor and hence at most two earlier neighbors. The root has at most two neighbors. Each earlier choice forbids at most one of three options, giving a valid transversal. This also handles isolated vertices.

Every remaining component is cubic. By Bernshteyn, Kostochka and Pron, Theorem 9, a connected simple graph fails degree correspondence colorability exactly when each block is a clique or a cycle. Here each list has size equal to the degree. A connected cubic simple graph with this exceptional block structure must be K4. In the single-block case this follows from regularity. Otherwise take a leaf block of the block-cut tree. A vertex in that block other than the attachment vertex has its full degree three within the block. A bridge or cycle cannot supply this degree. A clique block would therefore be K4, whose attachment already has three internal neighbors and also has an external neighbor, violating subcubicity. This proves the assertion even when the cubic graph has cut vertices. Consequently all balanced cubic components other than K4 admit a transversal for every recovered cover. No block search, coloring reconstruction or guessed witness is needed by the decision branch. [1]
