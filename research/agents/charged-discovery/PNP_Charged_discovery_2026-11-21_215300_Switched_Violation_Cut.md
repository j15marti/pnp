## Uniform recognition, including cancellation

Write E as a multilinear polynomial on Boolean bits. For each clause multiply its three falsity indicators (x_i for negative literals, 1−x_i for positive literals) and add the coefficients on identical monomials. Keep the constant. This needs at most eight terms per clause before combination. Work over the integers: recovering the minimum of a modular residue would be unsound.

For pair i,j the discrete derivative D=E11−E10−E01+E00 equals a_ij + sum_h a_ijh x_h. Its exact extreme values are l=a_ij+sum min(0,a_ijh), u=a_ij+sum max(0,a_ijh). The remaining coordinates can be chosen independently to attain both extrema. Under flips f, complementing other coordinates permutes the contexts, and flipping exactly one of i,j negates D. A submodular Boolean function has all D≤0 and conversely: summing the pairwise diminishing increments along a chain gives the full lattice inequality. Hence if l<0<u reject. An identically zero range adds no equation. A nonzero range with u≤0 requires f_i XOR f_j=0; a nonzero range with l≥0 requires parity 1. Solve all such equations by propagating bits along graph components and rejecting contradictory cycles. This proves necessity and sufficiency and avoids searching 2^d gauges. Reexpand the clauses in the recovered coordinates.

## Constructing and solving the cut

For a negative cubic −g xyz (g>0) substitute the minimum, over its own auxiliary bit zeta, of g zeta(2−x−y−z). For a positive cubic g xyz substitute

    g(xy+xz+yz) + min_zeta g zeta(1−x−y−z).

Checking Hamming weights 0,1,2,3 proves both identities. Each pair ij now has coefficient a_ij + sum_h max(0,a_ijh)≤0 in switched coordinates. All auxiliary cross coefficients are nonpositive. Private auxiliaries can be minimized jointly, sharing the original x. Thus the exact switched E is a partial minimum of a quadratic with nonpositive pair coefficients. This explicitly implements the ternary regular-energy theorem [2]; no historical novelty claimed for graph representability. Conversely directed cut capacity is submodular and partial minimization preserves submodularity (apply the lattice inequality to optimizing extensions). Rejection rules out this exact representation in every coordinate gauge.

Let bit 1 denote the source side. Replace −g xy by a capacity-g arc x→y and unary −g x. Unary t x for t≥0 is an arc to the sink; for t<0 use arc source→x of capacity −t and constant t. Keep the final constant even when negative. Augment along residual paths until none remain. Flow is then equal to the capacity of the cut reachable from the source, proving optimality by weak duality. All capacities are nonnegative integers with sum O(c): each source clause contributes bounded coefficient mass and each constant-size gadget costs bounded mass per unit coefficient. At most O(c) augmentations suffice. Return SAT exactly when offset plus cut value equals zero.

A single nonempty proper clause rejects in every gauge. After complementing literals its energy is the indicator of one cube vertex. Its three pair constraints, in coordinates putting that vertex at 111, all require inequality of the flip bits, an inconsistent triangle. This rules out universal direct admission even though the instance is easy SAT. Conversely take all seven clauses forbidding the patterns except 111. Their aggregate is 1−xyz and admits a cut. Each individual clause fails the switch test. Arbitrarily many such blocks, with shared selectors as desired, yield the same polynomial method. Taking all eight forbidden patterns gives constant 1 and UNSAT.
