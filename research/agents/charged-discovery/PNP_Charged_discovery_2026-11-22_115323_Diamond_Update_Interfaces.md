### The transferable theorem and its boundary

**KNOWN THEOREM** [2]. Fix a finite monoid M. Initialize d factors to identity, allow individual assignments, and return the product on request. Operations are deterministic decision-assignment trees. The supplied operation and local computation are free, persistent information is in the probed cells, and the cost is worst-case tree depth. With logarithmic-bit cells, if M has elements s,t with the positive powers of s forming a group with identity u and uts ≠ stu, some operation costs Ω(log d / log log d) probes. Figure 1 also gives the matching upper bound for noncommutative groups. For S3 take s=(01), t=(12), u=e. No restriction to a particular computation DAG or witness output enters this theorem.

**PROVED/EXACT, online Boolean corollary.** For every deterministic structure correct on every persistent assignment/query sequence at length d+1, implement S3-WORD at length d. Reserve the last factor as a scratch tag, initially e. Forward real assignments to their coordinates. To report the original product, for each of the six g in public order set the scratch tag to inverse(g) and query identity. Precisely one test accepts, since P inverse(g)=e iff P=g. Reset the scratch tag to e. This costs at most 13 operations per original query, one per assignment, with only constant finite-control bookkeeping inside the simulated operation. All queries and modifications are legal on that single **interactive** history. A bounded additive change in logarithmic cell width can be simulated with a constant number of cells. Theorem 10 now supplies the lower bound on maximum operation cost. The product structure followed by the identity test supplies the matching upper bound. The reduction never charges six online queries to a static decider. It does not assert an amortized bound, that every operation is expensive, or that every SAT run contains such a history.

### The explicit fixed-source bypass and storage test

**PROVED/EXACT, immutable-source synopsis.** For every x and j, write P=L_j x_j R_j with the factors on each side kept in order. Store the tag

    r_j = inverse(L_j) inverse(R_j).

Multiplying the equation L_j a R_j=e on the left and right by the respective inverses gives exactly a=r_j. A forward scan stores the prefix before each factor, starting with e. A reverse scan maintains the suffix after each factor, writes r_j, then prepends x_j to the suffix. At the end the index stores d tags of three bits each, plus fixed framing. Any number of independent queries against x can share that one table. Accounting already identified the unique repair of one coordinate [1]. The advance here is the exact synopsis requirement, the maintained-update reduction, and the separation of their contracts.

For the lower bound let an arbitrary fixed deterministic preprocessor map each x to an s-bit state, with all sample-dependent information accessible to the decoder included in those s bits. A fixed decoder given that state, d, j and a must answer correctly for **every** query and all original words, with no later source access. Restrict *only the storage argument* to P=e. There are exactly 6^(d−1) such x by unique completion. For every one of them r_j=x_j. Two distinct such words differ at some j, where the replacement equal to the first word's coordinate accepts on the first and rejects on the second. Thus their synopsis states are distinct, so s≥ceil((d−1)log2 6). Both outcomes occur among the required queries. This is a worst-case state-size result for the explicit service. Free retained source, sample-dependent addresses or a variable-length side channel change its hypothesis. Physical state on an actual offline run need never contain this synopsis. Nor do these bits imply that many independent bits per query.

**Concrete falsifiers.** (1) For (e,e,e) the repair at position 2 is e. After persistently writing (01) at position 1 it is (01). The immutable table is stale. (2) Two different identity-product words have identical cached original verdicts and a distinguishing single-deviation query. Verdict-only caching fails the extra service. (3) A paid exact offline decider normalizes directly to the verdict and halts after reading it. Its legal run contains no later substitution. (4) An immutable-source service allowed to retain x can reconstruct a repair by rescanning x. The storage lower bound then concerns *total accessible state*, and cannot be charged solely to an auxiliary cache.

## Actual-event and resource ledger

Fix the canonical-guard solver from [1], with its fused product acquisition and exact fallback. On the recognized material it authenticates the full prescribed graph and ring order, computes P from the extracted tags in that order, places D=[P=e] at a fixed origin-adjacent cell and enters READY, with the head there. A fixed continuation reads that bit and emits terminated output. Retained source and scratch contribute to physical span and cumulative cost even if never read again. The sole writable tape has finite alphabet and one unit-speed read-before-write head. There is no external update channel. Off-family valid inputs receive exhaustive Boolean decision after internal UNKNOWN. Malformed encodings follow the framing convention.

For one obligation use U=D and side information B=(machine, d, fixed layout, READY convention, prior). Give equal mass to the all-identity word and its first-coordinate transposition variant. B supplies neither the sampled word nor its verdict. E is the one actual transition reading the cache at READY. Its old-bit record Pi has fixed length one, conditional prefix freedom, capacity b=1, framing h=0 and reuse rho=1. The decoder returns the read bit. Hence H(U|B)=1, and the conditional code bound gives E[T_suffix]≥(1−0)/(1·1)=1. An alternate proof-mining observer may retain its own history, but if it knows D at entry its conditional demand vanishes. With r duplicate names for that event the reuse becomes r. No E_j exists here for an externally supplied change. Accounting's separate normalized first-read theorem assumes only d hidden tag cells differ across reachable checkpoints. That premise fails for a cache containing D. Both solvers and both checkpoint contracts are explicit. Their costs are never added.

For the direct source, retain the input until the guard finishes. Parse every header and row, check prescribed and missing edges, ordering, shared matching copies and tag agreement. Follow the pinned tariff in [1]: O(N^4) indexed bit operations, O(N²) span, conservatively O(N³) sole-tape transitions per indexed operation including seeks, addresses, scratch and copies. Fused ordered multiplication, retaining six possible products, and terminal framing fit this envelope. Constructing the optional table after paid ordered relocation takes O(d) more single-head transitions and O(d) extra cells with constant-alphabet tracks. A query's constant-probe bound assumes an externally supplied address. On the sole tape a conservative explicit implementation tags rows with binary indices, then scans and compares with a retained binary query by shuttling each requested bit. This costs O(d log d) additional cells if those labels are materialized and O((d log d)^3) transitions for initialization or one lookup, allowing full traversals for each copied bit. These fit the admitted single-query envelope; q real queries must pay for q lookups, supplied query bits and q outputs. They are outside static SAT. Initializing, storing and accessing group tables costs a fixed constant as G is fixed.

The admitted offline path uses T=O(N^7), peak span O(N²), physical cumulative memory O(N^9) and minimum-description cumulative memory O(N^8). The last bound uses a description by input and simulation step, with O(N+log(T+2)) bits per configuration. On fallback the corresponding costs from [1] are O(2^n N^7), O(N²), O(2^n N^9), and O(2^n N^8). The fallback preserves correctness on every valid input, and has no established polynomial worst-case time. Under a fixed cap T≤K N^a, a≥1, the respective general ceilings are span O(N^a), physical CM O(N^(2a)), descriptive CM O(N^(a+1)) and O(N^a log N) for the separate count-map circuit.
