## Claim and setting

For each integer m≥1 there is a fixed simple biconnected quotient graph. Its gains encode two lists of m subsets of the three-element field. Every deterministic preprocessor of the first list that must send a single message sufficient for exact decision against *every subsequently supplied second list*, with no other access to the first, requires exactly 7^m+1 different messages at optimum. Equivalently, the optimal worst-case fixed-width or prefix-free length is ceil(log2(7^m+1)). A single deterministic solver, fixed for all m, decides every admitted complete source in polynomial time and can erase everything except its verdict before output. The input language is Boolean decision for the project's positive exact-one source. A row requires exactly one of its three variables true.

This answers Accounting's question about a single block surviving articulation decomposition [1]. Earlier Discovery work examined subsequent substitutions in a group-product ring. Here arbitrary subsets arise at a separator of two vertices inside one block. The direction of information flow and the availability of the other half are the tested hypotheses.

## Generator and both-label semantics

Use ordered terminals s,t in each diamond with interior u,v. In order (s,u),(s,v),(t,u),(t,v),(u,v), gain g on (a,b) means x_a−x_b ≠ g modulo 3. Translation lets us put x_t=0 and x_s=d. Define M(g) as the set of d admitting an extension. To check the following small certificates, try u,v in {0,1,2} and require all five differences (d−u,d−v,−u,−v,u−v) to differ from their corresponding gains. This enumerates all possible completions for each terminal difference. Mask k represents the set of d with bit d set.

| Mask k | Gain tuple realizing k |
|---:|---|
|1|0,0,0,0,0|
|2|1,1,0,0,0|
|3|2,0,1,0,0|
|4|2,2,0,0,0|
|5|1,2,1,0,0|
|6|0,1,1,0,0|
|7|1,0,0,0,0|

Place two diamonds on the same terminals, with disjoint interiors. Their allowable differences intersect because their completions are independent once both endpoints are fixed. A requested nonempty mask k uses (k,k). The empty mask uses (1,2). This realizes all eight masks on the identical unlabelled graph.

Number s=0 and t_i=1+9i for i from 0 to m−1. The next eight vertices after t_i are the internal pairs of four diamonds, in order. The first two diamonds encode A_i and the last two B_i. For each i<m−1 add fresh w_i=9m+1+i and zero-gain edges from t_i and t_(i+1) to w_i. For any colors of the two neighboring terminals at most two of three colors are excluded at w_i. These paths therefore couple the graph topologically while preserving every feasible combination of terminal colors.

Deleting s leaves the t_i linked by the paths, and every diamond interior reaches its t_i. Deleting a t_i leaves both its interior neighbors in each diamond connected to s and each incident path vertex attached to the surviving neighboring terminal. Deleting one interior vertex leaves the other link from s to its terminal, and deleting one path vertex leaves all terminals linked through s. Thus no vertex disconnects the graph. For m=1 the same proof omits the paths.

The decision function is exactly

    D(A,B) = AND_i [A_i ∩ B_i is nonempty].

Every solution gives the required differences. Conversely, choose any d_i in each intersection, set s=0 and t_i=−d_i, fill the disjoint diamond interiors and then each w_i. An empty intersection at any coordinate proves UNSAT. These are both-label arguments for all lists, with no staged query order.

**Source conversion.** For each quotient vertex make a triangle on its three indexed ports. For a canonical oriented edge (a,b) of gain g connect port j at a to j+g at b, for j in F3. Use the published positive exact-one graph-star compiler [2,3]. Reversing the orientation negates the gain. In a proper coloring every triangle's colors are a permutation j ↦ σj+x with σ=±1. Unequal slopes across an edge force an equal-color matched pair. Thus connectedness imposes one common slope, and a global color negation makes it +1. Each matching now enforces precisely x_a−x_b≠g. Conversely a solution labels every triangle and matching legally. The star equivalence in [2,3] then carries both labels to the source.

There are q=10m vertices and k=22m−2 edges, including the m−1 path vertices and 2(m−1) path edges. The expanded graph has h=3q vertices and e=3q+3k edges. The direct source has n=8h+3e=528m−18 variables and R=4h+3e=408m−18 rows. The unary declaration, gamma(R+1) row count and three incremented variable identifiers of width w=ceil(log2(n+1)) in each row give N=n+2+2 floor(log2(R+1))+3Rw=Θ(m log(m+2)). The explicit generator writes O(m) constant-size graph records with logarithmic-width indices, then the encoded rows. Enumerating or sorting the polynomial-size lists and serializing them fits the acquisition budget below. The one-way split is ownership of gains. There is no claim that the compiler places its two owners on opposite sides of one tape cut.

## One transfer, and precisely where it stops

Fix a deterministic one-way protocol and m before varying the lists. A zero coordinate of A makes its decision row identically zero. Every vector with all coordinates nonempty accepts against the all-full context. For distinct such vectors pick i where they differ and d in their symmetric difference. Choose B_i={d}, all other B_j full. The decisions differ. All 7^m nonempty-coordinate vectors and the one zero row are therefore distinguishable. A shared message would give the same answer against the separating context. Conversely send rank zero for the zero row or one plus the radix-seven rank of the nonempty vector (digit A_i−1). Decode and intersect. This establishes both bounds using the general deterministic one-way message model [4]. The ceiling follows from fixed width or Kraft's inequality, including length and termination. Rank arithmetic on O(m)-bit integers by repeated constant-base operations takes O(m²) bit operations and O(m) extra bits under sequential digit scans. No 7^m-entry advice table is required.

The random access code theorem [5, Thm. 2.3] is a check on this transfer: encoding any n-bit string in k qubits, if every requested bit is recoverable with probability ≥p≥1/2 for each string and index, then k≥(1−H₂(p))n. On the subclass A_i equal to mask 1 or 2, a subsequently chosen singleton at i, all others full, reads any one encoded bit with a fixed convention. Its premise holds for the one-way service. An offline algorithm sees the chosen context before it can be required to encode anything. Our exact count above uses only deterministic distinguishability. No quantum model conversion is needed.

**Actual-event test.** In the one-way channel contract let U be the row class, uniform over its 7^m+1 canonical representatives, with public side information B₀=(m,protocol,codebook,prior). U is specified semantically by its answers to contexts. Each message identifies exactly one class on this support by the separating proof. Take Π to be the self-delimiting message and E its actual serialized bit emissions (one charged event per emitted bit). Framing is included in Π, with h=0 extra, b=1, ρ=1; message lengths, state, timing and addresses provide no supplementary channel. Then log₂(7^m+1)=H(U|B₀)≤E|Π|=E|E|≤E[T] in this bit-emission model. One need never run the hypothetical contexts to incur these sends under that contract. A fixed-width optimal message attains the ceiling. This is a communication charge, without an established injection into an offline trace.

For the offline execution fix the solver below. It computes D once, sweeps its marked interval clean, restores its head and fixed READY state with only D at a public location, reads that bit once and emits the answer and terminator. With B all full, every all-nonempty A has the same post-scrub configuration. Thus no suffix decoder can recover which of the 7^m rows was used. This counterexample applies even when the full computation, including the sweep, is charged. For a separate balanced two-input test, fix B₁=1 and other B_i=7 (coordinates numbered from one here). Choose A₁ equiprobably 1 or 2, others full. U₁=D, B₁-side-information is the public solver, prior, m, and fixed context only; Π₁ is the cached bit; E₁ is the real transition reading it at READY. Read location is fixed, framing h₁=0, capacity b=1, reuse ρ=1. The literal bit decodes D, so E[T]≥1 for this test. Counting the same transition L times raises ρ to L. The all-full example refutes a row decoder; the balanced example charges one genuine decision event.

## Paid offline implementation, comparisons and falsifiers

Use the fixed finite-control, finite-alphabet sole read/write tape and unit movement, retaining the source on tape until authentication or fallback. Parse all fields, authenticate all star rows and private leaves, reconstruct the exact graph and full triangle matchings, and check canonical numbering, the quotient template, rotations and connector gains. Determine m from q, reject a nonintegral or impossible size, and use exact comparisons. On authenticated inputs the solver can calculate M(g) directly for every observed diamond, even for gains outside the witness table. Fold the four masks per group by intersection, then fold the nonemptiness flags by AND. Neutral connectors were checked. A guard miss gives internal UNKNOWN and dispatches the original valid input to exhaustive exact-one decision. Every permitted input receives SAT or UNSAT.

In the symbolic implementation, O(N²) tape span holds the original source, work tables, decoded indices, copies, locations, counters and scratch. The inherited O(N⁴) indexed bit budget [1,2] covers complete acquisition: tables may be scanned for duplicate and missing records, every triple and gain has a fixed expected position or a paid lookup, and at most 27 local assignments are considered per diamond. Even a pairwise scan of O(N) rows with O(N)-bit bookkeeping per comparison fits this budget. Sequential discovery of template roles uses the authenticated numbering. There is no search for a decomposition or graph isomorphism. An indexed bit instruction, including address comparison and travel, can use an O(N³) sweep of that span. Erasure through a right-end marker, return, final read and output cost O(N²) additional transitions. Thus admitted T=O(N⁷), peak span O(N²), physical cumulative memory O(N⁹), and minimum-description cumulative memory O(N⁸). The latter describes each deterministic state by input and time using O(N+log T+log N) bits. All bounds concern the same execution. The general fallback time is O(2^n N⁷), span O(N²), physical CM O(2^n N⁹) and descriptive CM O(2^n N⁸). Source construction is polynomial in the explicit lists; the guard and fallback receive the encoded instance alone. The bounds include initialization, guard failure, switching and output. No native single-tape benchmark is claimed.

Under a cap K N^a for fixed K and a≥1, row information Θ(m)=Θ(N/log N) already fits a linear bit budget. Moreover the one-way emissions have no compulsory offline event mapping. The generic cap ceilings are O(N^a) peak, O(N^(2a)) physical cumulative, O(N^(a+1)) descriptive cumulative, and O(N^a log N) for the handoff's separate count-map circuit simulation. No product of unrelated executions and no counting conclusion enter the argument. The internally reviewed ordered permutation control retains its tight quadratic time and cubic cumulative bounds. No fixed polynomial cap is excluded here.
