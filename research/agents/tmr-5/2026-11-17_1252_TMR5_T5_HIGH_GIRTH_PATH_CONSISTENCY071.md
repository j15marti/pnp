## Primary theorem: exact complexity is NP-complete (KNOWN, not newly proved)

Bang-Jensen, Hell, and MacGillivray, *The Complexity of Colouring by Semicomplete Digraphs*, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 1(3):281-298 (1988), DOI 10.1137/0401029, prove that for a **fixed semicomplete loopless digraph H**, H-coloring is NP-complete when H has more than one directed cycle, and polynomial otherwise. T5 is a tournament (one orientation for every distinct pair), so semicomplete. It contains two distinct directed triangles 0->1->2->0 and 0->3->4->0. Therefore **CSP(T5) is NP-complete**. Membership NP: supply one of five labels per source vertex, verify each arc in time polynomial in the full encoded source length. This theorem supplies NP-hardness without asserting a newly checked gadget reduction, a parsimonious count reduction, ETH-preserving sparsity, or any fixed explicit blowup coefficient.

Independently, Barto, Kozik, and Niven, *The CSP Dichotomy Holds for Digraphs with No Sources and No Sinks*, SIAM J. Comput. 38(5):1782-1802 (2009), DOI 10.1137/070708093, show that the core of a finite smooth digraph is tractable exactly when all its components are directed cycles, and NP-complete otherwise; their algebraic characterization matches the polymorphism dividing line. T5 is smooth (in-/outdegree two). Any endomorphism of a loopless tournament must be injective: merging two adjacent vertices creates a forbidden loop. Thus the core is T5 itself. It is not a directed cycle (10 arcs, degree two each). The smooth theorem independently confirms NP-completeness and rules out a tractable Taylor-polymorphism explanation for this specific core. A 3-cycle template alone is a DIFFERENT tractable template, not this five-vertex tournament.

## New exact local lemma: complete four-step reachability

Let a signed undirected path have edge-orientation signs s_i in {+1,-1}. A homomorphic target labeling along its path changes by s_i*(1+2e_i) modulo 5, e_i in {0,1}. With p positive and q negative steps, path length t=p+q, possible endpoint differences are

    p-q + 2j (mod 5),  -q <= j <= p.

All consecutive integers j in this interval occur as j=(sum positive e_i)-(sum negative e_i). Consequently the path-pair relation contains exactly min(5,t+1) possible target values for EACH first label, and after **any four steps** it is ALL of Sigma x Sigma, independently of edge directions. Length zero is equality; length one has 2 possibilities, two has 3, and three has 4. This is a symbolic identity in Z5, not a claim that all T5 instances are affine-solvable: the binary edge-choice conditions must still agree around cycles.

For any source simple oriented graph of underlying girth >=7 define M_uv by its unique shortest path when undirected distance d(u,v)<=3, including orientation signs, equality on the diagonal, and ALL for d>=4 or disconnected u,v. Each M_uv is nonempty. It is contained in the initial edge/nonedge/diagonal path-consistency relation.

Claim: for every triple (u,w,v), M_uv is contained in M_uw composed with M_wv. When d(u,w)+d(w,v)>=4, composing the two path relations gives ALL, because four signed steps already saturate; if either relation was ALL, the other has nonempty support in every row and column, so the composition is still ALL. When the sum is <=3, concatenate the two unique short paths. Either it is the unique simple u-v path, or it backtracks. A different simple u-v route of total length <=3 would create an undirected cycle of length <=6, forbidden by girth >=7. Each backtrack can be inserted in a valid target path because R(a,b) iff R traversed backwards from b to a on the same source edge, with at least two choices for the intermediate label. Hence every pair in M_uv extends through w and belongs to the composition.

Standard path consistency starts from relations containing M and repeatedly intersects a pair relation with compositions via a third vertex. Monotonicity preserves M under EVERY update. Therefore no pair relation can become empty, even after arbitrary iteration to fixpoint. Arc consistency is weaker and also cannot reject these sources.

Erdos, *Graph Theory and Probability*, Canadian J. Math. 11 (1959):34-38, DOI 10.4153/CJM-1959-003-9, established existence of graphs with arbitrarily high chromatic number and girth. Choose girth at least 7 and chromatic number at least 6; orient every edge arbitrarily (e.g., toward the larger vertex identifier). Such a graph has NO homomorphism into T5 because adjacent source vertices must get distinct target symbols, which would be a proper 5-coloring. Yet the path-consistency invariant above survives. Disjoint copies give infinitely many different sizes with the same property. This is a **PROVED/EXACT scoped existential consequence of a KNOWN theorem**; it does not supply a source-authenticated polynomial-time uniform generator for a high-chromatic base graph. Finding a concrete small witness or efficient generation is OPEN.

Neither the proposed decimal front end nor this estimate explicitly requires n<=N, unary encoding of n, or rejection of enormous declared isolated universes.

The authentic, well-formed 21-byte input "p t5 1000000000000 0\n" declares n=10^12 vertices and m=0 arcs. It has K=1 for the all-unconstrained suffix-domain quotient, but its exact number of homomorphisms is 5^(10^12). Since 5^n > 2^(2n), its ordinary binary output has more than 2n bits. More generally the family "p t5 1" + "0"*d + " 0\n" has N=Theta(d) but exact output length Omega(10^d), not polynomial in N even at K=1. A source that compactly names isolated vertices is not equivalent to a graph encoding that explicitly lists each vertex. This is an unconditional output-length lower bound and a smallest-interface-type counterexample, NOT a SAT lower bound, and it does not challenge the algorithm's cofactor correctness.
