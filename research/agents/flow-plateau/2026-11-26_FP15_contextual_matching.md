## 1. Contextual safety rather than joint phase equality
In the FP13/14 normalized singleton-defect wire network, W is a perfect matching on active ports and four distinct exceptional ends s0,s1,t0,t1. Each disjoint group has exact nonempty table R of attainable perfect pairings of its ports P, with independent original-phase witnesses. Let U be the union of transition edges in R. Define a partial context (i,j,C) by distinct ports i,j and any partial matching C of P minus {i,j}. Define relaxed connectivity iff some partial matching F of P contained in U has i-j connectivity in F union C. Define legal connectivity iff some Pword in R connects i,j in Pword union C. Legal implies relaxed. A group is context-safe iff the converse holds for all such contexts. This is a weaker requirement than extending every disjoint set of transition edges to one table word. It preserves one selected terminal-to-terminal connection, not all boundary pairings or all future cross-direction continuations. Partial contexts include dead-end wires; definition is deliberately conservative, not claimed necessary for exactness on every source chart.

THEOREM: if every group is context-safe, ordinary matching on W union all U gives an exact singleton chart decision/witness. For each of 4 pairs x in {s0,s1}, y in {t0,t1}, test perfect matching after deleting x,y. A legal connection gives matching by toggling W along the alternating x-y path. Conversely, symmetric difference of W and such matching contains a simple alternating x-y path. Process each group once. If path uses no transition of group, choose any table word and retain path. Otherwise remove its path transitions; the external segments of this path define disjoint connections between group ports and two segments reaching x,y, at distinct ports i,j, with intermediate external segments pairing other ports (partial matching C). The old path proves relaxed connectivity. Context safety supplies a legal word connecting i,j through C. Restore the corresponding external segments to obtain a new x-y path, leaving all transitions of other groups among the previously used ones. Equivalently, test each table word for x-y connectivity in W plus outside path transitions plus that word, and retain a simple path. It is alternating since degrees are <=2 and terminals have only wires. Previously processed groups retain their chosen table words, even if path no longer uses them. Induction gives a fully legal path. Choose stored phase witnesses for all groups; reconstruct b and verify exact defect, conservation, degree2 and same cycle of s,t. FP13 parity lemma gives all-even b-factor; color E\B with third color and alternate two colors on B. Thus no joint phase enumeration on safe groups. If relaxed matching fails all four probes, chart NO. Fixed one-word groups are trivially safe. For residual L unsafe original switches, enumerate their <=2 options, charge all branches, apply theorem, O(2^L poly(N)) for fixed bounded-port tests. More general unsafe tables need product of their actual sizes. Arbitrary coupled guards excluded; local pins admitted if tables/certificates recomputed. Disconnected charts handled by same path proof and parity of unmarked circuits.

At six ports, 15 choices of unordered i,j and 10 partial matchings on other four give 150 contexts. Enumerate each by exact small DFS for relaxed alternating simple path (or every partial matching of U); test at most 15 actual table words. A bare hex switch with options 01,23,45 and 05,12,34 is unsafe: i=0,j=2,C={(3,5)}, mixed transitions 05 and 23 connect them, neither phase does. This is a partial-context falsifier, not by itself a complete four-terminal source NO.

## 2. Polynomial certificate at arbitrary port count
Let P=A disjoint union B, |A|=|B|=r, all table words perfect bipartite matchings, union U=K_{r,r}. Then context safety is equivalent to unordered-pair coverage: for every two distinct ports in one side and every two distinct ports in the other, at least one table word pairs the first pair onto the second pair (either order).
Necessity: choose i,j in A and context consisting solely of one edge pq with p,q in B. Relaxed connection always exists. Legal connection must use exactly two transitions mapping i,j onto p,q. Symmetric for endpoints in B.
Sufficiency: if i,j on opposite sides, some table word contains ij. If both in A, any relaxed path uses equally many A and B port vertices (its transition edges cross bipartition and are disjoint). Excluding endpoints, exterior context edges on that path cover two fewer A than B vertices. Hence there is at least one B-B exterior pair. Pair coverage supplies a single legal word joining i,j via that pair. Symmetric for B. This proof does not require retaining every old transition edge; precisely the rerouting freedom above.
Explicit tables tested in O(r^4 |R|) elementary operations (or enumerate covered pairs per word). Thus unbounded port count is not intrinsically hard if such a table is acquired in polynomial size and time. Example: cyclic shifts of 3 ports, three perfect matchings cover K3,3 and all unordered pairs; mixed disjoint edges from different shifts may not extend to any word. More generally affine permutations x -> ux+v over a finite field (u nonzero) cover every ordered pair, giving O(r^2) words, but no claim of source realization or discovery for general r. This is a stronger matching abstraction than joint pair support.

## 4. Infinite family defeating FP14 four-port acquisition yet solvable
For k>=4 use k modules of two hexagons, vertices 12j+0..11, cycles 0..5 and 6..11 in local indices. Internal three wires join (1,11),(3,9),(5,7). External ports (0,2,4) and (6,8,10). Exact module table, in local names:
{(0,10)(2,8)(4,6), (0,6)(2,10)(4,8), (0,8)(2,6)(4,10)}.
Direct path traversal over 4 phase words certifies these three matchings. K3,3 union and unordered-pair coverage; no common transition and joint extendability fails (e.g. 0-6 and 2-8 are legal individually, not together). Each module's first hex external ports connect in order to second hex external ports of next module cyclically. Remove one of those wires (the port0 wire) for module j=0 and j=floor(k/2). Connect the two freed first-hex ports to new vertex s and the two freed second-hex ports to t; add z=st. Source n=12k+2, simple cubic connected. Bipartition: first-hex even ports on one side, second-hex even ports on other; s,t on opposite sides accordingly. Every chart phase gives a spanning factor, so by bipartiteness every such factor is even. a is union of all 2k hexagons, b uses all non-a edges except z and either alternating matching per hexagon, validated. Only s,t are odd components of a, so min_a=1 and singleton chart is valid. There are 2^(2k) exact phases. No self-wires, all initial switches are unsafe six-port hexes; each pair has <=3 joining wires, hence no FP14 four-port union can start. Every internal module pair is a certified edge of the acquisition graph; therefore its maximum matching covers all 2k originals and yields L=0 (any covering matching works, whether or not same modules). Entire family is polynomial by the new method, including acquisition. Family is bipartite and already easy by other algorithms; this is a separation of prescribed absorption criteria, not evidence of unrestricted hardness or a new bipartite-colorability algorithm. Girth exactly6: hexagons are present; no 4-cycle can lie within hexagons or use two wire edges between two hexagons since ports within each wire bundle are nonadjacent; a 4-cycle touching s or t without z would require their neighbors in distinct hexagons to share a neighbor, impossible by port incidence. A 4-cycle using z would require an edge between one freed first port and one freed second port, none exists. No cyclic edge-connectivity promise made. Arbitrarily high girth remains untested.
