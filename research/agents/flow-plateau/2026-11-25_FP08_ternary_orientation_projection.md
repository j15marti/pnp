## 1. Exact alternate projection (Heawood-style orientation variables)
Let G be an explicitly encoded simple cubic graph, n vertices, m edges, c connected components. Validate degree, edges and original binary pair a,b, D a=D b=0, Psi=1 for the repair application. For each v give its three incident edge occurrences distinct local labels ell(v,e) in F3. At good vertices use z_e-1 (colors 1,2,3); at the two defective endpoints choose any bijection. In fact this construction works even without the initial near flow. A proper output coloring of the three local occurrences is uniquely f_v(x)=s_v x+t_v, s_v in {1,2}, t_v in F3, because all six permutations of three symbols are precisely these affine permutations. For edge e=uv agreement is t_v-t_u=s_u ell(u,e)-s_v ell(v,e).
Choose a spanning forest, one root phase 0 per component. Tree paths give linear forms t_v=F_v s. Non-tree edges give homogeneous rows A s=0, at most m-n+c rows. Necessity follows from telescoping tree constraints and subtracting each output's root phase from all colors in its component. Sufficiency: solve the rows, calculate all phases, evaluate both occurrences of every edge and check equality. Nonzero slopes ensure each triple is a permutation. Add 1 to output F3 colors and reinterpret in F2^2 as 1,2,3; conservation and Psi=0 follow and are verified. Thus G is colorable iff ker A has a vector with ALL coordinates nonzero. A solution can recolor arbitrarily many edges atomically. No module recognition or target coloring is used. No inference of polynomial feasibility from mere Gaussian elimination.
Changing local occurrence labels ell_v to alpha_v ell_v+beta_v, alpha_v nonzero, only rescales slope coordinates and adjusts phase; therefore the feasible slope subspace and its dual low-weight support catalogue are invariant up to coordinate signs. The near-flow states of a fixed graph are gauge variations, NOT independent graph samples. Guards on particular output colors require retaining root phases and checking guards; unrestricted terminal here does not certify guarded rejection merely because an unguarded witness fails a guard.

## 2. Exact rank theorem and independent unsigned-incidence representation
Let U be the unsigned vertex-edge incidence matrix over F3. U y=0 means at each vertex its three F3 labels sum to zero, equivalently either all equal or all distinct. A linear map L takes y to slope s_v=y_(local label1)-y_(local label0); since the triple sums to zero, y_(label2)=y_(label0)+2s_v. Hence L(ker U)=ker A. Kernel of L on ker U is precisely the c-dimensional space of edge labelings constant in each component. Left null vectors of U satisfy q_u+q_v=0 on every edge, so dim left kernel is b, number of bipartite connected components. Therefore dim ker A=m-n+b-c and rank A=n/2+c-b for cubic graphs. In particular connected nonbipartite rank n/2+1; connected bipartite rank n/2. The fixed graph rank is linear, not a small global intersection guarantee. The same derivation holds for disconnected inputs and permits componentwise direct processing. Enumerating only free slope coordinates gives an exact 2^(n-rank A) poly(N) terminal, not universal polynomial. This rank formula is the arbitrary-graph spanning-forest analogue of the planar Heawood spin formulation; no priority claim.

## 3. Sparse signed closure, exact polynomial reduction
Let variables s_i be nonzero F3 elements. Every row supported on one coordinate forces zero and rejects. Every valid two-term row a*s_i+b*s_j=0 forces s_i=-a*b*s_j (coefficients 1,2 self-inverse). Every three-term row a*s_i+b*s_j+c*s_k=0 with all coefficients nonzero implies a*s_i=b*s_j=c*s_k: a sum of three +/-1 terms is 0 mod3 only when all three signs match. Hence all these rows are equivalent, on the nonzero domain, to signed equality constraints. Combine by signed union-find; inconsistent product of signs around a cycle rejects, otherwise substitute representative signs into all original linear rows and repeat.
Acquire ALL currently available rows of support <=3 without enumerating all rowspace combinations: let H have rows a basis of ker A. For each coordinate i, q_i=H[:,i]. Row w belongs to row(A) iff sum_i w_i q_i=0. A zero column gives a one-term row. Equal projective signatures q_i~q_j give two-term rows. After merging such duplicates, index normalized q_i; for all i<j and b=1,2, test normalized q_i+b*q_j against the index to obtain a third distinct coordinate k, with exact coefficient found from scaling. This finds every three-term row, including nonlocal combinations of cycles. Verify each equation by direct annihilation of H; an independent verifier may also solve for its row-combination coefficients. Duplicate classes can be handled in a preceding pass, avoiding combinatorial multiplicities. Each accepted nontrivial merge removes at least one variable, so at most n rounds. Substituting signed equalities preserves all feasible sign words via recorded backmaps. If residual row rank zero, assign every representative +1 and lift. If residual dimension small enough, use charged bounded enumeration of free nonzero coordinates; otherwise DECLINE, never reject by stagnation. The complete zero-rank/contradiction terminal is polynomial whenever sparse closure reaches one of these outcomes. It is NOT universal, as below.
A weight-four row with signed nonzero variables is an exact integer balance (two plus, two minus), since among -4,-2,0,2,4 only zero is 0 mod3. This offers a complementary count/matching interface, but arbitrary systems of these rows are not claimed tractable. Pair-sum projective collisions among disjoint pairs enumerate all four-term rows when no lower weight rows exist, in polynomial time. It cannot help when the rowspace has none.

## 4. Graph-theoretic characterization of sparse row support
Fix an edge orientation and write f for an F3 circulation on oriented incidence B. The cycle-to-row linear map is R(f)_v=sum_{e incident v} sigma(v,e)*f_e*ell(v,e), where sigma is the oriented incidence sign. Its image is row(A), since fundamental cycle constraints span the circulation conditions. If R(f)_v=0 then the three signed incident values x_e=sigma(v,e)*f_e have sum zero and label-weighted sum zero. Distinct labels 0,1,2 force x_1=x_2=x_3. Thus for a proposed row support X, on each connected component of G-X these common values alternate signs across internal edges. A nonbipartite component forces zero, a bipartite component has one free ternary amplitude. Assign one parameter per bipartite component and each edge internal to X, enforce ordinary circulation conservation at the vertices of X, and take R(f) restricted to X. This obtains exactly all row vectors supported in X, including zero; each actual circulation and each constructed one correspond. This is an alternate exact support acquisition oracle with graph scan plus small boundary linear algebra (in connected graph with nonempty X, at most 3|X| components and at most 3|X|/2 internal edges). No oracle to SAT. This lemma gives structural interpretation for hidden low-weight row intersections, no numerical test needed for proof. Conditional obstruction: if for every |X|<=q all cyclic components of G-X are nonbipartite and girth(G)>4q, every bipartite component is a tree, its boundary size equals size+2; total such vertices <=3q. Any circulation yielding a row supported in X is zero outside X plus those tree components, so its support has <=4q vertices. A nonzero circulation must contain a cycle, impossible. Thus no nonzero row of weight <=q under this stated graph promise. Existence of broad hard families with the promise and a specified one-zero source not established here.
