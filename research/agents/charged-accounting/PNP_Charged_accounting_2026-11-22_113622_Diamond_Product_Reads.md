## An explicit encoded family with linearly many hubs

Use Construction's authenticated triangle-bundle graph-star source at [8b38e410](https://github.com/j15marti/pnp/commit/8b38e410ddd375f1902454f3ec11cc89278a43d6). Form d diamonds in a ring. Diamond i has terminals i and (i+1) mod d, and private vertices a=d+2i, b=a+1. Add the five edges from each terminal to a,b and from a to b. The quotient has 3d vertices, 5d edges, d degree-four hubs, and minimum degree three. For an input permutation p_i use identity matchings on the two left edges and on ab. Use p_i from the right terminal to both interior vertices. Replace each quotient vertex by its triangle of three places and each edge by its three matching edges. Apply the pinned star generator, implemented in the companion checker. The expanded graph has 9d vertices and 24d edges. Its direct positive Boolean exact-one source has n=144d names and m=108d rows. Its physical layout is unary n and zero, gamma(m+1), then 3m incremented identifiers of width w=ceil(log2(n+1)). Thus N=n+2+2 floor(log2(m+1))+3mw=Theta(d log d). All inputs of fixed d have the same length.

A triangle coloring f is a bijection from places to colors. At each place x, the left terminal and both interiors have three distinct colors by their identity edges. The right terminal at place y must avoid the two interior colors at p_i(y), so its color is exactly the left color at p_i(y). Conversely with right=left composed with p_i, choose the two interiors by composing the left coloring on the color side with the two nonidentity cyclic rotations. These satisfy every edge. Each diamond therefore implements exactly right=left composed with p_i. Private interiors extend independently once the terminal maps agree. Global closure is equivalent to p_0 ... p_(d-1)=identity under (fg)(x)=f(g(x)). Every graph coloring extends to the exact-one source and conversely by the verified star gadgets. This proves SAT and UNSAT semantics without enumerating the 3^d sign-normalized hub assignments of the generic Synthesis bound.

Order matters even with the same local multiset and sign parity. For s=(01), t=(12), w=(st)^(-1), the triple (s,t,w) is SAT and (t,s,w) is UNSAT. Padding by identities gives both labels for every d. The code explicitly builds the two physical source row lists for d=3.

## A replayable first-read ledger

Use a sole writable finite-alphabet tape with one unit-speed read-before-write head, fixed origin and terminated Boolean output. A completely specified public layout (depending only on d) contains the d word cells, one S3 symbol each, followed by a sentinel. At entry all remaining cells, the output contents, control and head position are fixed independently of the word. No hidden clock or second input head. Reachability for one fixed globally correct machine follows by full canonical guard authentication, relocating the word, erasing all sample-dependent source and workspace, returning the head to the first word cell and entering a fixed control state. Retention up to that point, all clearing and normalization are paid. A valid nonimage input receives exhaustive decision on its preserved original.

For any coordinate j write the product L p_j R. Exactly one replacement, L^(-1) R^(-1), makes the product identity. On an UNSAT word this differs from p_j. On a SAT word any of the five other values gives UNSAT. The resulting opponent configuration at the stipulated checkpoint differs in that one cell only. If a correct continuation halted without reading it, deterministic coupling would preserve every transition and final output on both runs. Read-before-write includes the first destructive overwrite. Hence on *each* run all d distinct cells receive first visits, regardless of the access strategy. This yields T_suffix≥d pointwise.

Now fix a finite prior, in particular iid uniform p_i. Use one obligation U=(p_0,...,p_(d-1)) specified from the source. B contains the machine, d, public checkpoint layout and prior. E is precisely the d actual transitions that first scan one of the hidden cells. Pi lists the old symbols at those transitions in chronological order, each in a fixed three-bit code. The observer simulates the deterministic continuation from its public starting configuration, maintaining all writes. At a first access to a word cell it requests the next codeword, records it at the simulated address, and continues. Every other read is public or already determined by the replay. The simulator thus reconstructs selection times and addresses, halts with the actual machine, and has recovered U in source order. The observer has no initial copy of the hidden word. This is an information decoder with unrestricted decoding time, not an unpaid operation of the decision machine.

Every Pi has 3d bits at fixed B. Framing overhead h=0, per-event capacity b=3 and reuse rho=1. The conditional entropy code inequality gives E[T_suffix]≥(H(U|B)-E[h])/(b rho)=d log2(6)/3 under the uniform prior. The singleton-opponent proof strengthens this to d. Each first-read event occurs once. Providing the full sampled source in B would remove the demand. Splitting into many prefix transcripts requires accounting for the overlap among their first reads.

The product is uniform on S3 because any fixed first d-1 symbols admit precisely one last symbol for each target product. SAT thus has probability 1/6. Given only the cached Boolean verdict D, the hidden word has conditional entropy d log2(6)-H_binary(1/6). Given the exact product it still has (d-1)log2(6) bits. In the alternative fused solver all this residual uncertainty is compatible with an O(1) terminal decision suffix. It authenticates every row, processes the recovered matchings in ring order, multiplies once per diamond, and writes D near the origin before entering a fixed READY state. A singleton actual cache-read record Pi'=D decodes U'=D with b=1,h=0,rho=1. It cannot reconstruct U. Charging d copies of that read requires rho=d. An all-UNSAT promise would invalidate the opponent argument. Both labels are present at the same N here.
