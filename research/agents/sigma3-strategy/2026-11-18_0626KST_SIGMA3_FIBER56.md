## EXACT theorem F56-A: affine-hull-of-guard-fiber reduction
Let `Phi = exists x forall y exists z [A z = B_{g(y)} x + C_{g(y)} y + d_{g(y)}]` over GF(2), where g is an arbitrary k-bit Boolean circuit. For every nonempty fiber `Y_a = {y : g(y)=a}`, choose an actual anchor `p_a` and independent differences `v_ai` that span `aff(Y_a)-p_a`. Let L span the left nullspace of A. Then Phi is TRUE iff (i) `L C_a v_ai=0` for every a,i and (ii) ONE common x solves all `L B_a x = L(C_a p_a+d_a)`. Proof: solvability of Az=b iff Lb=0; an affine form vanishes on a fiber iff it vanishes on an affine basis of the fiber. The same x is committed before y.

YES: extract x by Gaussian elimination, and for each fiber solve `A z_a0=B_a x+C_a p_a+d_a`, `A z_ai=C_a v_ai`; let `z(y)=z_a0+sum_i c_i(y) z_ai` for the unique coordinates of y-p_a. This is a uniform polynomial-size per-challenge Skolem circuit when k=O(log N), provided the genuine fiber bases have been obtained.

NO: if `u C_a v_ai=1` for a left-nullspace row u, at least one of `p_a` or `p_a+v_ai` fails for every x. Otherwise, any x violating an anchor equation loses at that anchor. Supply an actual challenge y and algebraic dual `u A=0, u RHS=1`. No uncharged negative-answer witness.

## EXACT theorem F56-B: one computed guard bit can hide coNP-complete certification
For claimed affine basis P of nonempty fiber g^{-1}(1), verifying `aff(P)=aff(g^{-1}(1))` is coNP-complete, even for one guard output bit and claimed singleton `P={0^(n+1)}`. Given CNF F(u), set `g(b,u)=[b=0 and u=0^n] OR [b=1 and F(u)]`. The singleton anchor is exact iff F is UNSAT. Counterexample y with g(y)=1 outside aff(P) is an NP certificate of invalidity. This is a scoped complexity-class classification, NOT a superpolynomial time lower bound.

Naive zero/unit challenge tests fail for `g(y)=AND_j y_j`, n>=2, since guard=0 at zero and every unit but guard=1 at all-ones. A complete deterministic XOR/AND trace may be unique but cannot certify a nonaffine acceptance condition.
