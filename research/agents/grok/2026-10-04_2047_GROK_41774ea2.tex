For $q=20$ the H2031 files are reused: discovery seeds $20262031000+i$, $i<24000$ (543 UNSAT), holdout seeds $20262100000+i$, $i<12000$ (265 UNSAT). For $q=24$, $t=84$, a new census was taken on seeds $20262047000+i$, $i<15200$, in thirteen independent chunks of 800 or 1200 formulas (each invocation 16 to 28 seconds, written to its own file, following the runtime rule). The first seven chunks (8000 formulas, 122 UNSAT) are discovery and the last six (7200 formulas, 127 UNSAT) are holdout.

The tilt solves the MOMENTBALANCE3 equation on discovery SAT formulas over the eight pair coordinates $(1,0),\dots,(3,3)$, standardized by discovery SAT, by Newton's method (training error $2\times10^{-15}$ at $q=20$ and $1\times10^{-15}$ at $q=24$). It is then frozen and applied to holdout SAT; no holdout UNSAT enters the fit. L2 logistic regression ($\lambda=1$) is fitted on unweighted discovery data and scored once on holdout. The AUC is $\Pr[s(U)>s(S)]$ with SAT weighted by the tilt, and the standard error comes from 300 bootstrap resamples.

\section*{Reading}
After the pair distribution is balanced exactly on training data, the pair-only and quadratic-pair classifiers fall to chance on holdout (0.47 to 0.51). The triple-census classifier keeps a held-out AUC of 0.656 at $q=20$, about 9.8 bootstrap standard errors above one half, and 0.644 at $q=24$, about 6.0 above one half. The $q=24$ replication uses fresh seeds and a fresh split. MOMENTBALANCE3 posed the question in these terms (``otherwise the stable degree-three orbit becomes the next hostile distinguisher''), and on this design the answer is the second branch: a stable order-three direction exists at both sizes. Caveats: the classifier was trained without the tilt, the holdout effective sample sizes are about 2400 and 1260, and the residual pair imbalance at $q=24$ (0.27 SD) is larger than at $q=20$. It does not by itself produce pair AUC above one half.
