\section*{Claim audited: SLICEDEPTH2}
With $M=8\binom q3$, $t=6q$ and $S_{q,t}$ the monotone slice extension of clause-presence $\mathrm{UNSAT}_{3,q}$, SLICEDEPTH2 claims that every
monotone DNF for $S_{q,6q}$ has at least $(1-\rho^q)\binom M{6q}$ terms and every monotone CNF has at least $\binom{7M/8}{6q}$ clauses,
$\rho=2(7/8)^6$, both $\exp(\Theta(q\log q))$.

\paragraph{Proof check.} Every UNSAT $t$-set is a minterm and every SAT $t$-set is a maxterm; a monotone DNF (CNF) contains every
minterm as a term (every maxterm complement as a clause). This is the standard prime-implicant fact (\textbf{KNOWN THEOREM}).
The union bound $\#\mathrm{SAT}\le2^q\binom{7M/8}{t}$ and $\binom{7M/8}{t}/\binom Mt\le(7/8)^t$ are correct. I found no error.

\paragraph{Hostile control: the bound does not separate hard from easy (PROVED/EXACT, elementary).}
For \emph{every} slice function $f$ on layer $t$ of $M$ variables, each $t$-set is a minterm if $f=1$ there and a maxterm if $f=0$, so
\[
\mathrm{DNF}(f)+\mathrm{CNF}(f)\ \ge\ \binom Mt .
\]
The easy threshold slice $\mathrm{Th}_{M,t}$ (layer all ones) has exactly $\binom Mt$ minterms at layer $t$, so its monotone DNF is
\emph{larger} than SLICEDEPTH2's DNF bound, while the counting recurrence $c_{i,j}=c_{i-1,j}\vee(x_i\wedge c_{i-1,j-1})$ gives a
monotone circuit with at most $2Mt$ gates. Likewise $\mathrm{Th}_{M,t+1}$ has $\binom Mt$ layer-$t$ maxterms, exceeding the CNF bound.
Concretely: at $q=4$, $\binom{32}{24}=10{,}518{,}300$ DNF terms against at most $1536$ gates; at $q=6$, $\log_2\binom{160}{36}\approx119.34$
against at most $11{,}520$ gates. The recurrence was checked exhaustively for all $M\le12$ and all $t$ ($90{,}114$ inputs, $0$ failures).
So the depth-two theorem is correct but carries no information specific to UNSAT: any argument that uses it to lower-bound
monotone DAG size must fail on $\mathrm{Th}_{M,t}$. This agrees with SLICEDEPTH2's own Wegener caveat and makes it quantitative.
Peer label CANDIDATE left unchanged; this audit is \textbf{COMPUTATIONAL}, no error.
