\section*{2. The claim}
Model, plain encoder (\texttt{regsat\_sb.py}) and tight encoder (\texttt{regsat\_tight.py}, conditions (i) to (v)) are exactly as in H1948. H1948 proved that the two encoders agree at $L=C(f)$. The H1948 next step said a tight encoder is not sound at $L>C$. That is too strong. The tight conditions do not forbid recomputation. They forbid only defects that can be removed from the \emph{program} itself, and removing a defect shortens the program by one step. So the tight encoder is exact at the first length where any plain program exists.

\textbf{Lemma TIGHT-AT-MIN (CANDIDATE).} Let $f$ be neither a constant, nor a literal, nor a negated literal, and let $L=L(f,2)$, so plain $L-1$ is UNSAT. Then every plain program of length $L$ for $f$ is free of defects (a) and (b) of H1948, and after the H1948 moves (c) it satisfies (i) to (v). Hence, whenever plain $L-1$ is UNSAT, plain $L$ is SAT if and only if tight $L$ is SAT.

\emph{Proof sketch.} Each defect is removed at the level of the register program, giving a program of length $L-1$, which contradicts the hypothesis.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(b)] A non-final write that is not read before it is overwritten or before the end can be deleted, since no read changes. If the final step does not write $R_0$, the final step is such a write.
\item[(a1)] A step whose value is a constant or a literal of an input (this includes reading a never-written register, which is $0$) is deleted, and each reader of that value (each read of the written register before its next overwrite) reads the input instead, with any negation or constant absorbed into the reader's operation table. If the step is the final writer of $R_0$, then $f$ is a constant or a literal, which is excluded.
\item[(a2)] A step $R_w:=u$ or $R_w:=\neg u$, where $u$ is the current value of $R_j$. If $j=w$, delete it and absorb the negation into the readers. If $j\ne w$, delete it and send every read of $R_w$ before the next overwrite of $R_w$ or $R_j$ to $R_j$. If that next overwrite is of $R_j$, make that step write $R_w$ instead and swap the names $R_j,R_w$ in all later steps. If the copy survives to the end as the output, relabel the registers globally, and if it was a negated copy, complement the last writer of $u$ and absorb the negation into its readers.
\item[(a3)] Same operand twice, or a degenerate operation, makes the step a constant or a literal of one operand, which is case (a1) or (a2).
\end{itemize}
Moves (c) keep the length and the read and write pattern (H1948). \qed

The case analysis in (a2) is the part that is not yet checked line by line, so the label stays CANDIDATE. TIGHT-AT-C is the special case $L(f,2)=C(f)$.
