\paragraph{Claims under test.} For $F_m=\bigwedge_{i=1}^m(x_i\vee y_i\vee z_i)$ on $3m$ distinct variables: (i) applying CLAUSE-FIRST-COVER1 to $k$ clauses gives exactly $3^k$ nonempty disjoint leaves; (ii) with the published encoding, degree-8 polynomial fanout at $m=1024$ removes only 77 clauses; (iii) fixed-power shrink of every leaf forces $3^{m-o(m)}$ leaves; (iv) in the tagged block, the three first-true residuals are pairwise distinct and clause-set incomparable, so exact-equality merging fails on the tagged product. The repository labels the guard PROVED/EXACT and scopes it to an unmerged tree. It names component decomposition as the escape for the tagged family.

\paragraph{Independent recheck (COMPUTATIONAL).} I wrote \texttt{/workspace/h1818/indep.py} (sha256 prefix \texttt{947ad20e}) and \texttt{iso2.py} (prefix \texttt{db3d5d28}) from the report text. They import no repository code.
\begin{itemize}
\item (i) All 20 cases $1\le m\le5$, $0\le k\le m$, by brute force over $2^{3m}$ assignments: $7^m$ models and exactly $3^k$ nonempty buckets in every case.
\item (ii) The published encoding gives $M=43{,}008$ at $m=1024$. The largest $k$ with $3^k\le M^8$ is 77. The residual is 924{,}804 ppm of the source. Both numbers match the report.
\item (iii) This also holds for nonuniform trees (PROVED/EXACT, short). If every leaf of a clause-first tree on $F_m$ must have processed at least $d$ clauses, then every root-to-leaf path has length at least $d$. Each split has three children, so the tree has at least $3^d$ leaves. An exhaustive check for $m\le8$ agrees. So the converse direction does not depend on uniform depth.
\item (iv) The tagged residuals have 5, 4, and 3 clauses. They are pairwise distinct and incomparable, and for $k\le6$ the $3^k$ leaves of the product are pairwise distinct as clause sets. This confirms the report.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{New hostile control: renaming-canonical merging (PROVED/EXACT, plus COMPUTATIONAL check).} Merge residuals that are equal up to a polarity-preserving renaming of variables. This is sound for deciding SAT, and the LEAD report already lists variable-renaming canonicalization as a RED attack. Then the $3^k$ leaves of the tagged product collapse to exactly $2k+1$ classes.

\emph{Proof.} In block $i$, branch $t\in\{1,2,3\}$ assigns its decision variables. What remains is $t$ positive binary clauses on fresh tag pairs and $3-t$ guard pairs $(\neg w\vee a\vee b),(w\vee c\vee d)$ on a fresh $w$. Blocks share no variables. So a leaf is, up to renaming, a disjoint union of $B=\sum_i t_i$ binary clauses and $3k-B$ guard pairs. Its isomorphism type depends only on $B$, and $B$ takes the $2k+1$ values $k,\dots,3k$.

\emph{Check.} Exact isomorphism tests on labeled clause, literal, and variable incidence graphs give 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13 classes for $k=1,\dots,6$ (729 leaves at $k=6$).

So exact-equality merging fails on the tagged product, but renaming merging makes the tree polynomial. That gives a second bypass next to component decomposition. This does not contradict ARC-0004. Its theorem only concerns unmerged trees, and it claims only that the tagged variant defeats exact equality. Nothing is falsified. The note just records that any future ``hard for merging'' control must also hold up under renaming.
