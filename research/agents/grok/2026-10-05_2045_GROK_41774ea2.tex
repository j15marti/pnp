\section*{2. The claim}
Model as in H1948 and H2013: $n$ read-only inputs, two one-bit registers $R_0,R_1$ starting at $0$, a step is $R_w:=g(a,b)$ with $g$ any of the 16 binary operations and $a,b$ inputs or current registers, and $f$ must be in $R_0$ after the last step. H2013 left Lemma TIGHT-AT-MIN as CANDIDATE because the copy case (a2) of its proof sketch had not been checked line by line. This pass replaces the case analysis by one uniform rewrite. Defects are taken \emph{semantically}, which is weaker as a hypothesis than the syntactic defects of H1948: (a1) the value written is a constant or a literal of an input; (a2) the value written is $u$ or $\neg u$ where $u$ is the current content of some register and is not a constant or literal; (b) the value written is not read before it is overwritten or before the end, and the step is not the last writer of $R_0$.

\textbf{Lemma SHORTEN (PROVED/EXACT).} Let $f$ be neither a constant nor a literal. If a two-register program of length $L$ for $f$ has a semantic defect at step $t$, then there is a two-register program of length $L-1$ for $f$ with operands in strict order $s_a<s_b$ (the plain-encoder form).

\emph{Proof.} Delete step $t$ and replay the remaining steps while keeping, for each original register $R_o$, a descriptor saying where its original value now lives: a physical register together with a negation bit, an input literal, a constant, or ``dead''. At the deleted step the descriptor of $R_w$ becomes the constant or literal in case (a1), the descriptor of the copied register with the negation bit toggled if needed in case (a2), and ``dead'' in case (b). Each surviving step reads its operands through the descriptors, with negations and constants absorbed into its operation table (16 operations are closed under this). It writes to a physical register that the descriptor of the \emph{other} original register does not name; one exists because that descriptor names at most one of the two physical registers. The written register then gets the descriptor (that register, no negation). By induction each original register's value equals what its descriptor says after every step, because a write never overwrites the physical register named by the other descriptor. A ``dead'' descriptor is never read, since in case (b) the value is overwritten before any read, and if it is never overwritten it is not in $R_0$ at the end (otherwise step $t$ would be the last writer of $R_0$). At the end the descriptor of $R_0$ names a physical register $p$, because otherwise $f$ would be a constant or a literal. If its negation bit is set, complement the operation of the last writer of $p$ and absorb the negation into every later read of $p$. If $p=1$, swap the register names in the whole program. Equal operands and reversed operands are put in strict order by a size-preserving rewrite of the operation table (the second operand is an unused input when the step is unary). \qed

\textbf{Consequence (PROVED/EXACT, from SHORTEN and the H1948 moves).} Lemma TIGHT-AT-MIN of H2013 holds: if plain $L-1$ is UNSAT and $L=L(f,2)$, then every plain program of length $L$ has no semantic defect, so in particular none of the syntactic defects of H1948 (a degenerate operation, equal operands, or a read of a never-written register makes the value a constant, a literal, or $\pm$ a current register). The H1948 size-preserving moves then give conditions (i) to (v), and plain $L$ is SAT if and only if tight $L$ is SAT. The case (a2) proof of H2013 is superseded rather than checked; TIGHT-AT-C is the special case $L=C(f)$. The extra content of SHORTEN is that optimal programs also have no \emph{semantic} copy: an exact recomputation of a value that is still held in a register never occurs at $L(f,2)$. A recomputation of a value that has been overwritten is still allowed, and that is exactly the forget-then-recompute that the price measures.
