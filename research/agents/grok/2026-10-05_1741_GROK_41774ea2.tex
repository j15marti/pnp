\section*{1. Claim under test}
Every ``price'' $L(f,2)-C(f)$ in H1617--H1727 takes $C(f)$ from \texttt{exsyn\_n.py}, which counts only the five non-degenerate normal gates and normalises $f$ so that $f(0)=0$ (output negation is free). The register model counts every binary op, degenerate ones included, and must leave $f$ itself in $R_0$. So it is not automatic that $C(f)\le L(f,2)$. A price-one claim needs $L(f,2)\ge C(f)+1$, hence needs this inequality.

\section*{2. Elimination argument (CANDIDATE, not yet independently reviewed)}
Let $P$ be a plain 2-register program of length $L$ that leaves $f$ in $R_0$, with $f$ not a constant and not a literal. Read each step as a gate. A step that reads a never-written register has a constant-0 operand, and a step with a degenerate op computes a constant, a source, or its negation; in every such case the gate can be removed by substituting its value into its readers (whose ops stay binary) or, at the output, by folding a negation into the producing gate's op. Each removal lowers the gate count by one and may make readers degenerate, so iterate. The process stops at a circuit of non-degenerate gates computing $f$ or $\bar f$; it cannot reach a source, because $f$ is not a literal. Pushing negations forward turns every gate normal. So the \texttt{exsyn\_n} count of that circuit is at most $L$, which gives $C(f)\le L(f,2)$.

When $L=C(f)$, no removal can happen (it would give fewer than $C(f)$ gates), so $P$ already satisfies the tight rules (i) no degenerate op, (ii) no read of an unwritten register, and (v) every non-final write is read before overwrite and the last step writes $R_0$ (a dead write is a removable gate). Rules (iii) normal ops before the last step and (iv) $s_a<s_b$ are reached by complementing stored values in step order and swapping operands, which preserves (i), (ii) and (v). That is the tightness lemma. It stays CANDIDATE until someone else checks it; TIGHT-SOUND-AT-C stays COMPUTATIONAL, and the price-one lower bounds that rely on it stay CONDITIONAL.
