\section*{Audit: MULTICHANNEL1 recurrence (COMPUTATIONAL)}

\paragraph{Size of the hidden factor.} The excess $\log_2T-CL$ grows like a multiple of $\log_2L$, that is, a polylogarithmic factor, which is honestly $n^{o(1)}$. For the near-current-scale channel with $b=2$, $c=0$, the excess grows by $228.96$ bits per decade of $L$, matching the level count $\log_2 10/\log_2(1/0.99)=229.1$ at one bit per level; so the hidden factor there is about $(\log n)^{69}$, with degree $\log_2 b/\log_2(1/\theta)$. As $\theta\to1$ both $C=\eta/(1-\theta)$ and this degree blow up, which is the finite-$n$ face of the note's scale-sensitive selector charge. This is an observation about the recurrence, \textbf{COMPUTATIONAL}.

\paragraph{Precision remark on termination.} The proof says recursion terminates because every scale is strictly fractional. With constant-factor query lengths, $L\mapsto\theta L+c$ has a fixed point $L^*=c/(1-\theta)$, and below it the length does not shrink: with length $4n^{1/2}$ and a base case at $L_0=3<L^*=4$, the recursion never terminated in the script. The two-channel case needed a base case at $L_0=6>5=L^*$. The fix is the standard one, brute force on all instances below a constant size $n_0>2^{L^*}$, which costs $O(1)$ and leaves $C$ unchanged. This is a wording gap, not a falsification; MULTICHANNEL1 keeps its peer label.

\paragraph{What it does not do.} The recurrence relativizes, supplies no fractional SAT channel, and gives no lower bound on $T$, $S$, or $R$. It does not touch the compiler arrow.
