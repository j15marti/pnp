\section{Design change from pass 1 (cost model)}
Pass~1 counted AND, OR and NOT gates separately and keyed only cuts with at most 3 inputs. Pass~2 works on And-Inverter Graphs (AIGs), where complemented edges are free and size is the number of AND nodes, the standard model of DAG-aware rewriting (Mishchenko, Chatterjee, Brayton, DAC 2006). With free inverters, minimum AIG size is invariant under NPN transforms. So one library entry per NPN class serves every input permutation, input negation and output negation. That invariance is \textbf{PROVED/EXACT} (inverters are free, and relabelling the inputs does not change the size) and was checked: 0 violations over 10{,}054 truth tables. AIG size and De~Morgan size (counting NOT gates) agree up to a constant factor, so asymptotic questions transfer between the two models.

\section{REWIRE pass-2 engine (own code)}
\texttt{rewire2.py} (SHA256 prefix \texttt{5c000e5cfb3003b1}). Each node gets up to 10 cuts with at most 4 leaves. The gain of a cut is the size of the node's maximum fanout-free cone (MFFC) inside the cut minus the number of new nodes the candidate structure would add under structural hashing (computed in a dry run). The best positive-gain cut is applied, then dead nodes are cleaned up, for up to 6 rounds until the size stops falling. Every output was checked against the original by its full truth table.

Growth control:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Key = NPN class, so the store is bounded by 222 classes. Raw truth-table keys would allow up to 65{,}536.
\item \emph{Pay-for-itself}: an entry is stored only when it gives a strictly positive gain at the cut where it is first met. A lookup that finds nothing does not store anything.
\item Memorized local configurations: when a cut's own cone is smaller than every known structure for its class, that cone becomes the class entry. In \texttt{self\_only} mode this is the only source of entries.
\item Cap of 64 entries, evicting the least-used entry from the older (LRU) half. The cap was never reached.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Readings}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{COMPUTATIONAL.} Library growth is sub-linear in the number of cuts and plateaus near 24--32 entries. NPN keying plus pay-for-itself storage removes the raw-key growth that pass~1 warned about (34 distinct raw keys on 50 small circuits).
\item \textbf{COMPUTATIONAL.} The exact oracle beats self-memorization by about 2$\times$ on large random circuits (10.40 vs 21.05). A purely self-taught library under-simplifies. Seeding from the smallest exhaustive instances (sizes 0--5) earns its keep.
\item \textbf{Caveat.} Many random AIGs collapse to near-trivial functions (mean 0.85 after rewriting at $(4,8)$). Random AIGs are therefore a weak hostility test. Parity is the meaningful control.
\item \textbf{CANDIDATE (not a lower bound).} Plain parity being a fixed point only says that 4-cut rewriting cannot certify anything below $3(n-1)$. It is \emph{not} a proof that parity needs $3(n-1)$ AIG nodes: a local fixed point does not certify a global minimum.
\end{itemize}
