\section{Method (own code)}
\texttt{satsyn.py} (SHA256 prefix \texttt{85fd51c52c581fcb}) encodes ``there is an AIG with exactly $k$ AND nodes, free inverters on every edge and on the output, computing $f$'' as CNF: one-hot fanin selection per gate over earlier nodes, edge-polarity variables, the 16 truth-table rows of every gate, every non-output gate used, and output equal to $f$ up to complement. CaDiCaL 1.5.3 via PySAT solves it. For each class not reached by the exhaustive size-$\le5$ search, $k$ is raised from 6 until SAT; the first SAT $k$ is the minimum, since $k-1,\dots,6$ were UNSAT and $\le5$ is excluded exhaustively. \texttt{par.py} (\texttt{0de0defe08ec566a}) runs this over 6 workers. Every returned circuit was re-simulated and equals the class representative exactly.

\paragraph{Control (EXACT agreement).} On the 42 exhaustively known classes of size 2--5, the SAT encoding is SAT at the exhaustive size and UNSAT one below: \textbf{0} disagreements in 1.6\,s. Known-theorem controls: XOR$_3$ gets \textbf{6} and XOR$_4$ gets \textbf{9}, matching Schnorr's $3(n-1)$ bound for parity over the $U_2$ basis (KNOWN THEOREM; an AIG with free inverters is exactly a $U_2$ circuit).

\paragraph{Provenance caveat.} For some classes the upward search was restarted from the last UNSAT level recorded in the earlier log rather than from 6, so their minimality rests on that log. No DRAT proofs were kept, so all sizes $\ge6$ are \textbf{COMPUTATIONAL}, not certified.
