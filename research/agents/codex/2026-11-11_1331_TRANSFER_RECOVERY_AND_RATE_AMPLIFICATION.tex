\section*{Exact quantifiers}
For a total deterministic SAT decider $A$, let
\[
 T_A(N)=\max_{|F|\le N}\operatorname{time}_A(F).
\]
The polynomial assumption to be contradicted is
\[
 \exists A\,\exists C>0\,\exists d\ge1\,\forall F:
 \quad A(F)=\SAT(F),\quad
 \operatorname{time}_A(F)\le C(|F|+2)^d.
 \tag{P}
\]
A degree-$q$ quasipolynomial assumption replaces the final bound by
$2^{C(\log(|F|+2))^q}$, with fixed $q\ge1$. We use
\[
 \QP_q=\bigcup_{C>0}\mathrm{DTIME}
       \bigl(2^{C(\log(N+2))^q}\bigr),\qquad
 \QP=\bigcup_{q\ge1}\QP_q.
\]
For numerical envelopes, separately write
\[
 \mathcal G_q=\{h:h(N)\le2^{O((\log(N+2))^q)}\},\qquad
 \mathcal G=\bigcup_{q\ge1}\mathcal G_q.
\]
Membership in $\mathcal G_q$ concerns a bound, not the time required to
compute an optimum.
An upper bound belonging to a superpolynomial growth class does not exclude
a better algorithm. Every lower-bound conclusion below quantifies over
\emph{all} fixed correct deciders. Logarithms have base two.

\section{An unconditional computation bridge already in the record}

Fix a polynomial verification allowance $q(N)\ge N+1$. Put
\[
 b(N)=b_0+\lfloor\log(N+2)\rfloor,
\]
where $b_0$ includes an exhaustive witness finder. For a program $p$, let
$t_p(F)$ be its time to halt with a valid satisfying assignment, and
infinity otherwise. On satisfiable formulas define
\[
 H(F)=\min_{|p|\le b(|F|)}
       \{t_p(F)+q(|F|)+1\},\qquad
 \Hbar(N)=\max_{\substack{|F|\le N\\F\ \mathrm{SAT}}}H(F).
 \tag{1}
\]
The selected program can differ by input and selection is free. This
nonuniform choice is controlled by the description budget.

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative witness-work transfer]
There are fixed constants $K,D$, depending only on the machine,
encoding, and fixed verification allowance, such that every fixed correct SAT decider $A$ satisfies
\[
 \Hbar(N)\le C_A\bigl(N+T_A(KN)+2\bigr)^D
 \tag{2}
\]
for all sufficiently large $N$.

Conversely, if $\Hbar(N)\le h(N)$ for a usable explicit clock $h$, then
\[
 \SAT\in\mathrm{TIME}\bigl(\poly(N+h(N))\bigr).
 \tag{3}
\]
In particular,
\[
 \Hbar\text{ is polynomially bounded}\iff\SAT\in\Pclass,
 \qquad
 \Hbar\in\mathcal G\iff\SAT\in\QP.
 \tag{4}
\]
Each fixed log-power degree $q\ge1$ is preserved.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Embed the fixed decider $A$ in one fixed witness finder. For a satisfiable
input, assign one variable at a time, querying whether the current
restriction remains satisfiable. There are at most $N$ queries. Canonical
restriction, renaming, and, if necessary, conversion of short clauses to
proper 3-CNF give queries of length at most $KN$, for a fixed $K$.
Query construction, simulation, and witness verification cost a fixed
polynomial in $N+T_A(KN)$. The program description is constant, so it
eventually fits $b(N)$. Finitely many smaller formulas are absorbed in
$C_A$. Uniform polynomial simulation fixes $D$ independently of $A$.

For the reverse direction, enumerate all descriptions of length at most
$b(N)$. There are at most $2^{b_0+1}(N+2)$ of them. Clock each for $h(N)$
steps and verify each completed output. Accept if one is a valid witness;
otherwise reject. On a satisfiable input, (1) guarantees a successful
candidate. On an unsatisfiable input, no output passes verification.
The total simulation overhead is polynomial in $N+h(N)$.
For polynomial and quasipolynomial bounds, one can choose elementary
explicit clocks; their constants need only exist. This proves (4) and
preservation of each fixed log-power.
\end{proof}

The $T_A$ in (2) is the \emph{global worst-case} envelope. It cannot be
replaced by $\operatorname{time}_A(F)$ for the original formula: the
self-reduction queries different formulas. A constant input dilation
does not alter polynomial or fixed-degree quasipolynomial classifications.

Universal search and its program-length overhead are standard
\cite{trevisan2010}. This report makes no claim that the bridge proves
hardness: proving a superpolynomial lower bound for this $H$ still contains
the central computational difficulty.

\subsection{Description budget is a real resource}
For an integer-valued budget $b(N)$ computable in time polynomial in
$N+b(N)$, a usable explicit clock $h$, and the analogous $H_b$, direct
enumeration gives
\[
 \Hbar_b(N)\le h(N)
 \ \Longrightarrow\
 \SAT\in\mathrm{TIME}\left(
 2^{b(N)+O(1)}\poly(N+h(N)+b(N))\right).
 \tag{5}
\]
The converse solver-to-$H_b$ envelope implication holds when
$b(N)\to\infty$ and a fixed exhaustive finder fits at every length,
or when the budget always includes an appropriate fixed universal witness
finder. More generally finite $H_b$ on every small satisfiable input
suffices to absorb the initial exceptions.
Merely being unbounded without eventually admitting every fixed
description is not the same condition.

A logarithmic budget preserves polynomial time. A
$O((\log N)^r)$ budget can introduce degree-$r$ quasipolynomial enumeration.
A sufficiently large linear budget makes polynomial $H_b$ unconditional:
the chosen program can hardcode a satisfying assignment and print it.
Enumeration still costs exponentially many descriptions. Thus free
instance-dependent selection cannot be ignored.

If a constant budget includes one fixed Levin-style verified universal
witness searcher, constant budget also suffices for the P and QP
equivalences. The slowdown for each competing fixed program is a constant
depending exponentially on its description length, which is harmless for
these fixed-program asymptotic implications. No certification of candidate
programs' global correctness is used: only their actual witnesses are
verified.

\section{Power amplification: a quantified extension of the October note}

Let $F_j$ be satisfiable, $s_j=|F_j|+2\to\infty$, and $H_j=H(F_j)$.
Suppose that eventually
\[
 s_{j+1}\le A s_j^a,\qquad
 H_{j+1}\ge B^{-1}H_j^b s_j^c,
 \tag{6}
\]
for fixed $A,B\ge1$, $a>1$, $c>0$.

\begin{theorem}[Critical and supercritical power amplification]
If $b=a$, then
\[
 \Hbar(N)\ge N^{\Omega(\log\log N)}.
 \tag{7}
\]
If $b>a$ and $p=\log_a b>1$, then
\[
 \Hbar(N)\ge 2^{\Omega((\log N)^p)}.
 \tag{8}
\]
Both hold for every sufficiently large $N$. Through (2), the same
asymptotic lower scales hold for every correct SAT algorithm, after
absorbing the constant input dilation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $b\ge a$, choose
\[
 K_0\ge\max(1,A^{1/(a-1)}),\qquad
 L_0\ge\max(1,(BK_0^c)^{1/(b-1)}).
\]
With $\sigma_j=K_0s_j$ and $\eta_j=H_j/L_0$, substitution gives
\[
 \sigma_{j+1}\le\sigma_j^a,\qquad
 \eta_{j+1}\ge\eta_j^b\sigma_j^c.
\]
After deleting a finite prefix, $\eta_j\ge1$, since the verification
allowance tends to infinity. Set
\[
 u_j=\log\sigma_j,\quad v_j=\log\eta_j,\quad k_j=v_j/u_j.
\]
The numerators are nonnegative, so
\[
 u_{j+1}\le au_j,\quad v_{j+1}\ge bv_j+cu_j,\quad
 k_{j+1}\ge(b/a)k_j+c/a.
 \tag{9}
\]

When $b=a$, (9) gives $k_j\ge k_0+(c/a)j$.
Since $u_j\le a^ju_0$, $j\ge\log_a(u_j/u_0)$, and therefore
\[
 v_j\ge\frac{c}{a\log a}\,u_j\log u_j-O(u_j).
 \tag{10}
\]
This gives the claimed orbit bound.

When $b>a$, put $r=b/a>1$. Then
\[
 k_j\ge r^jk_0+\frac{c}{b-a}(r^j-1).
\]
The inequality $u_j\le a^ju_0$ gives
$r^j\ge(u_j/u_0)^{\log_a r}$. Hence
\[
 v_j\ge C u_j^{1+\log_a r}-O(u_j)
       =C u_j^{\log_a b}-O(u_j)
\]
for a fixed $C>0$.

To obtain all sufficiently large lengths, take the predecessor of the
first index after the fixed initial prefix at which $s_j>N$. It satisfies
\[
 (N/A)^{1/a}<s_{j-1}\le N.
\]
Consequently $\log s_{j-1}=\Theta(\log N)$, and the orbit lower bound
has the same scale in the monotone worst-case envelope $\Hbar(N)$.
The harmless $+2$ convention is absorbed by changing thresholds.
Finally invert (2). Its fixed polynomial exponent and constant input
dilation preserve the scales (7) and (8).
\end{proof}

For the idealized loss-free recurrence with $0\le b<a$, (9) forces only the finite
exponent $c/(a-b)$. Indeed, the idealized sequence
$s_{j+1}=s_j^a$, $H_j=s_j^D$ satisfies the work inequality whenever
$D\ge c/(a-b)$, with constants and integer rounding absorbed if necessary.
This is a limitation of the recurrence's conclusion, not an upper bound
on the actual benchmark.

A fixed $p>1$ in (8) rules out an upper bound in $\QP_q$ when $q<p$.
At $q=p$, constants can coexist. To rule out the union $\QP$, one would
need valid constructions with unbounded $p$, or a stronger lower bound.
The critical bound (7) rules out P but is below every fixed
$2^{(\log N)^{1+\epsilon}}$ scale.

\section{Constant work multipliers versus growing work multipliers}

This distinction applies directly to the earlier sequential
wire-expansion proposal. Suppose
\[
 s_{j+1}\le C s_j,\qquad W_{j+1}\ge\mu W_j
 \quad(C>1,\ \mu>1\text{ fixed},\ W_0>0).
 \tag{11}
\]
The forced lower-bound scale is
\[
 W_j\ge\Omega(s_j^{\log_C\mu}).
 \tag{12}
\]
For example, doubling size and multiplying work by four forces a quadratic
scale. It does not exclude all polynomial algorithms.
The exact sequences $s_j=s_0C^j$, $W_j=W_0\mu^j$ show why no stronger
conclusion follows from (11) alone.

The work multiplier $\mu$ in (11) is not the work power $b$ in (6).
A linear-size bound cannot be replaced by an exponent $a$ tending to one
while dropping its accumulated constant factor.

\begin{theorem}[Size-dependent multiplication under linear growth]
Suppose the \emph{actual witness benchmark} satisfies, eventually,
\[
 s_{j+1}\le C s_j,\qquad
 H_{j+1}\ge B^{-1}H_j s_j^c,
 \quad C>1,\ c>0,\ B\ge1,
 \tag{13}
\]
and $s_j\to\infty$. Then
\[
 \log H_j\ge
 \frac{c}{2\log C}(\log s_j)^2-O(\log s_j).
 \tag{14}
\]
Consequently,
\[
 \Hbar(N)\ge 2^{\Omega((\log N)^2)},\qquad
 \forall A\in\mathrm{DecSAT},\
 T_A(N)\ge2^{\Omega_A((\log N)^2)}
 \tag{15}
\]
for all sufficiently large $N$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $x=\log s_j$ and $d=\log C$. Backward use of the size bound yields
$\log s_{j-k}\ge x-kd$. Choose a fixed $x_0$ at least the logarithm of
the initial tail size and let $m=\lfloor(x-x_0)/d\rfloor$.
The forward bound ensures that the last $m$ stages lie in the valid tail.
Since $H\ge1$, iterating (13) gives
\[
 \log H_j
 \ge c\sum_{k=1}^m(x-kd)-m\log B
 =c\left(mx-\frac{d\,m(m+1)}2\right)-m\log B.
\]
As $m=x/d-O(1)$, this is (14).
The predecessor of the first crossing of $N$ has size in $(N/C,N]$,
so the envelope preserves the squared-log scale. Apply (2) to obtain (15).
\end{proof}

More generally, replacing $s_j^c$ in (13) by
$2^{\kappa(\log s_j)^q}$ for fixed $\kappa,q>0$ yields
\[
 \Hbar(N)\ge 2^{\Omega((\log N)^{q+1})}.
 \tag{16}
\]
For a direct lower estimate, use the last
$\lfloor(\log s_j)/(2\log C)\rfloor$ stages, where every earlier log-size
is at least half the final log-size. The gain is
$\Omega((\log s_j)^{q+1})$, while the fixed loss contributes only
$O(\log s_j)$.

If linear growth were instead combined with a genuine power
$H_{j+1}\ge H_j^b/B$, $b>1$, it would force
$H_j\ge2^{\Omega(s_j^{\log_C b})}$. In particular $b>C$ is impossible for
this benchmark, since exhaustive search gives $H(F)\le2^{O(|F|)}$.
This further demonstrates why multiplier and power cannot be interchanged.

\section{The existing replay obstruction constrains every construction}

\begin{proposition}[Witness replay]
Let $W$ be a fixed polynomial-time formula transformer and $L$ a fixed
polynomial-time witness extender. For one fixed satisfiable seed
$(F_0,a_0)$, suppose
\[
 F_{j+1}=W(F_j),\qquad a_{j+1}=L(F_j,a_j),\qquad F_j(a_j)=1.
\]
If the number of stages and all generation-and-extension work through
stage $j$ are polynomially bounded by $|F_j|$, then
\[
 H(F_j)\le\poly(|F_j|).
 \tag{17}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
One fixed program hardcodes the seed witness and the codes of $W,L$.
On input $F_j$, it regenerates the orbit, transports the witness, and
checks that the generated formula is the input. For strictly increasing
explicit integer lengths, at most $|F_j|$ stages are required, with every
intermediate size bounded by the final size. The assumed more general
generation bound gives the same conclusion. The program eventually
fits $b(|F_j|)$, and finitely many smaller instances are absorbed in
a constant.
\end{proof}

Thus a polynomially replayable orbit cannot satisfy (6) in the critical
or supercritical regime, or (13), indefinitely. This is unconditional.
The original note's acyclic XOR extension is an explicit example:
its variable count squares, but witnesses extend by forward gate
evaluation. A complicated representation does not create minimum witness
work.

The note's separate collision wrapper guarantees another verified search
task, but its oracle collision lower bound is not a lower bound for
explicitly supplied circuits. Nor does adding an independent search
requirement establish multiplicative minimum work. Those implications
remain OPEN.

\section{The recovered fixed-system clock transfer}

For this and the subsequent proof-system sections, use canonical
\emph{general-CNF} encodings and correct general-CNF deciders, as in
the recovered reflection report. Every proof system under comparison
outputs the same UNSAT source language. Here $N$ is its actual encoded
length. Standard polynomial translations between general CNF and 3-CNF
preserve P and each fixed QP log-degree; no linear-size identification
with the earlier $H$ input is asserted.

Fix one externally correct total SAT decider $A$. Define a Cook--Reckhow
UNSAT system $Q_A$ whose proof input is $(F,1^t)$. It outputs $F$ if
the clocked simulation of $A(F)$ returns UNSAT, and otherwise outputs
one fixed trivial contradiction. Parsing and simulation are polynomial
in the proof input because $t$ is unary. External correctness supplies
soundness, and totality supplies completeness. Therefore, pointwise,
\[
 L_{Q_A}(F)\le O_A(|F|+\operatorname{time}_A(F)+1)
 \quad(F\ \mathrm{UNSAT}).
 \tag{18}
\]

Let one fixed target system $R$ satisfy
\[
 w_R(F)\le G_A(|F|+L_{Q_A}(F)+2)
 \tag{19}
\]
for each relevant $A$, with nondecreasing overhead $G_A$. Define
\[
 T_A^-(N)=
 \max_{\substack{|F|\le N\\F\ \mathrm{UNSAT}}}
 \operatorname{time}_A(F),\qquad
 W_R(N)=\max_{\substack{|F|\le N\\F\ \mathrm{UNSAT}}}w_R(F).
\]
Then (18)--(19) give
\[
 \boxed{W_R(N)\le G_A(O_A(N+T_A^-(N)+2)).}
 \tag{20}
\]
This negative-side envelope is stronger than using irrelevant delays on
SAT inputs.

LSPV-1/336 \cite{clock336} obtains (19) from polynomial size optimality of $R$:
\[
 w_R(F)\le(|F|+\operatorname{time}_A(F)+2)^{O_A(1)}.
 \tag{21}
\]
An efficient proof translator is unnecessary for minimum size.
If length rather than cost is simulated, a polynomial cost-versus-length
comparison is required. In the project unit-node setting, node count is
at most serialized proof length.

After logarithms, (21) has the \emph{common} exponent $r=1$:
\[
 \log W_R(N)\le C_A\log(N+T_A^-(N)+2).
 \tag{22}
\]
The simulation degree may depend on $A$; this becomes a coefficient, not
a variable log-power. Size optimality, or the weaker solver-clock
simulation actually needed here, has not been established for the
preselected mixed calculus. The existence and characterization of optimal
systems are active proof-complexity questions \cite{egidy2026}.

Full size optimality plus a superpolynomial proof lower bound would imply
$\NP\ne\mathrm{coNP}$: if these classes were equal, a polynomially bounded
Cook--Reckhow system would exist and be size-simulated by $R$
\cite{cookreckhow1979}. Solver-clock-only simulation need not give this
stronger consequence.

\subsection{A useful consistency check}
If SAT has a degree-$q$ QP algorithm $A$, the $A$-dependent system $Q_A$
QP-simulates every Cook--Reckhow system with the same log-power $q$.
Given a proof $w$ in a system $Q$, compute $F=Q(w)$, run $A(F)$, count its
actual time $t$, and output $(F,1^t)$. Since $|F|\le|w|^{O_Q(1)}$,
the translation costs $2^{O_{A,Q}((\log|w|)^q)}$.
For polynomial $A$, this is a p-optimal system.

This does not establish optimality of the original $R$. Replacing $R$
by $Q_A$ or adding its clocked proofs may eliminate an old lower bound.

\subsection{Completing a one-sided runtime bound}
If a correct $A$ runs within an available clock $g(N)$ on \emph{all}
valid UNSAT inputs, run it for that clock and return SAT on timeout.
Every UNSAT input halts in time; on a SAT input either $A$ answers correctly
or the timeout answer is correct. This gives a global bound polynomial in
$N+g(N)$. Interchanging SAT and UNSAT gives the dual.
It does not apply to a bound known only on a selected family.

\section{How weak a simulation would still give the desired contradiction?}

The generic logarithmic bridge is
\[
 \log W_R(N)\le
 C_A[\log(N+T_A^-(N)+2)]^r,
 \quad r\ge1\text{ fixed independently of }A.
 \tag{23}
\]
If $\log W_R(N)\ge g(N)$, then
\[
 T_A^-(N)\ge
 2^{(g(N)/C_A)^{1/r}}-N-2.
 \tag{24}
\]
Thus a proof lower bound
$W_R(N)\ge2^{\Omega((\log N)^p)}$ contradicts $\QP_q$ time when $p>rq$.
A merely solver-dependent $r_A$ cannot be replaced by one common $r$.

A much larger simulation overhead can suffice if the same system has a
strong enough lower bound.

\begin{proposition}[Weak simulation with a stretched-exponential proof lower bound]
Fix $0<\theta<1$. Suppose every solver clock system has an $R$-simulation
as in (20) with
\[
 G_A(s)\le2^{\,2^{C_A(\log(s+2))^\theta}},
 \tag{25}
\]
and suppose the same fixed $R$ has
\[
 W_R(N)\ge2^{\kappa N^\alpha}
 \tag{26}
\]
on an unbounded set, for fixed $\kappa,\alpha>0$.
Then every correct $A$ obeys, on those lengths,
\[
 T_A^-(N)\ge
 2^{\Omega_A((\log N)^{1/\theta})}.
 \tag{27}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take two logarithms of the upper and lower bounds:
\[
 \alpha\log N+O(1)
 \le C'_A[\log(N+T_A^-(N)+2)]^\theta+O_A(1).
\]
Invert the fixed power. Since $1/\theta>1$, the resulting lower bound on
$N+T_A^-(N)+2$ dominates $N$, yielding (27).
\end{proof}

For $\theta=1/2$, (27) is the squared-log QP lower scale. An assumed upper
bound in $\QP_q$ contradicts (25)--(26) when $q\theta<1$; at equality
constants matter. This pair of hypotheses need not exclude all QP.

More qualitatively, simulation overhead $2^{s^{o_A(1)}}$ and (26)
already exclude polynomial SAT: substituting $s=N^{O_A(1)}$ yields
$W_R(N)\le2^{N^{o(1)}}$, contradicting (26).
The superficially similar $2^{o(s)}$ is insufficient for this argument:
at $s=N^d$ it can be larger than $2^{N^\alpha}$.
Subexponentially optimal systems are defined using the stronger
$2^{s^{o(1)}}$ scale in \cite[Definition 6.7]{ilango2025}.
That terminology supplies no optimality theorem for our $R$.

\section{Universal reflection: what LSPV-1/361 really transfers}

Let $E$ be the explicitly declared strict signed Resolution plus paid
conservative two-input AND-extension fragment. Fix one correct polynomial
SAT algorithm $A$. Its reflection CNF
\[
 \mathcal R_{A,\ell}
 =\operatorname{Valid}_{\ell}(s)\land
   \operatorname{Reject}_{A,\ell}(s)\land
   \operatorname{Eval}_{\ell}(s,y)
 \tag{28}
\]
asserts that a valid formula $s$ is rejected but has a satisfying witness.
It is UNSAT by external correctness and has size at most
$C_A(\ell+1)^{d_A}$.

LSPV-1/361 \cite{reflection361}, with its paid evaluator compilation and polynomial
node-to-evidence normalization, proves
\[
 W_E(N)\le C_A(N+Q_A^*(N)+1)^{k_A},
 \qquad
 Q_A^*(N)\le W_E(C_A(N+1)^{d_A}),
 \tag{29}
\]
where
\[
 Q_A^*(N)=\max_{\ell\le N}w_E(\mathcal R_{A,\ell}).
\]
This is an $E$ theorem. Containment $E\subseteq R$ gives $w_R\le w_E$,
not a reverse lower-bound transfer.

The old polynomial equivalence in (29) extends to every fixed $q\ge1$:
\[
 Q_A^*\in\mathcal G_q\iff W_E\in\mathcal G_q.
 \tag{30}
\]
Indeed, polynomial overhead and polynomial rescaling of $N$ preserve
$(\log N)^q$. This is an exact target family, but its being one family
does not make its missing upper bound free.

For a solver with a larger nondecreasing explicit clock
$t(N)\ge T_A^-(N)$, redefine the formulas to assert rejection within
$t(\ell)$ steps:
\[
 \mathcal R_{A,\ell,t(\ell)},\qquad
 Q_{A,t}^*(N)=\max_{\ell\le N}
 w_E(\mathcal R_{A,\ell,t(\ell)}).
\]
The encoding has size $\poly_A(N+t(N))$, and the correct bound becomes
\[
 W_E(N)\le\poly_A(N+t(N)+Q_{A,t}^*(N)).
 \tag{31}
\]
If $t\in\mathcal G_a$ and $Q_{A,t}^*\in\mathcal G_b$, then
$W_E\in\mathcal G_{\max(a,b)}$.
Conversely, $W_E\in\mathcal G_b$ only gives
$Q_{A,t}^*\in\mathcal G_{ab}$ by direct substitution.
The exact degree-preservation statement (30) requires polynomial-time $A$.

LSPV-1/337's arithmetic alternative similarly must retain the length
of finite negative-soundness proofs. Fast computation does not supply
those proofs automatically. Hard reflection formulas under a hypothetical
fast solver are consistent until an independent reflection upper bound is
proved.
