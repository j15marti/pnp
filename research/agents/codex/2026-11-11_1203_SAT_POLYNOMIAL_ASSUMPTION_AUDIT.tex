\section*{Exact quantifiers and model}
Fix ordinary explicit binary CNF encodings and total deterministic deciders.
Malformed encodings receive a fixed rejecting convention. Let
\[
 T_A(N)=\max_{|F|\le N}\operatorname{time}_A(F).
\]
For contradiction assume
\[
 \exists A\,\exists C>0\,\exists c\ge1\,
 \forall F:\quad A(F)=\SAT(F),\qquad
 \operatorname{time}_A(F)\le C(|F|+1)^c.
 \tag{H}
\]
The algorithm, coefficient, and exponent are fixed independently of $N$.
The exponent is not assumed known, small, optimal, or extractable from code.

Machine-specific bounds are used only on their original models. The
time-hierarchy discussion uses multitape machines; the quoted FLMV result
uses its random-access Turing-machine model. An implementation must pay for
input conversion and cannot combine one machine's time bound with another
machine's space bound. The SAME\_REALIZATION object in each accounting
lemma is the single simulated execution, including construction, queries,
cache work, and output.

For a fixed sound complete calculus $R$, define
\[
 w_R(F)=\min_{\pi:\,R\text{ refutes }F}\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi),\qquad
 W_R(N)=\max_{\substack{|F|\le N\\F\text{ UNSAT}}}w_R(F).
\]
Costs must include all legal rules and count distinct shared paid nodes once.
Encoded proof length and inference-node cost are different measures; a
Cook--Reckhow claim below concerns encoded proof length, or a cost known to
be polynomially bounded by that length. No such comparison is silently
assumed for an unidentified historical calculus.

\section*{Primary literature}
Cook's polynomial nondeterministic hierarchy gives distinct multitape
nondeterministic time classes for distinct real polynomial exponents
\cite{cook1973}. Jahanjou--Miles--Viola describe explicit quasilinear
Cook--Levin formulas and the separate succinct clause-generating
representation \cite{jmv2013}. FLMV establish simultaneous time-space
restrictions on their stated machine model \cite{flmv2005}; these are not

the particular alternation-trading inference system they analyze, rather

$\Pclass^{\NP}$ classification \cite{bozzelli2016}. Cook--Reckhow's

\section{What a quasipolynomial bound would need to mean}
Write
\[
 \QP=\bigcup_{d\ge1}\mathrm{DTIME}\bigl(2^{O((\log N)^d)}\bigr),
\]
with base-two logarithms throughout the report. Polynomial time is contained
in this union. Thus a quasipolynomial SAT \emph{upper} bound does not refute
(H). To refute (H), it suffices, for example, to prove that for every correct
$A$,
\[
 T_A(N)\ge 2^{\Omega_A((\log N)^{1+\varepsilon})}
 \quad\text{for infinitely many }N
 \tag{1}
\]
for some fixed $\varepsilon>0$. Infinitely many sufficiently large violations
already rule out a fixed polynomial upper bound. No fastest algorithm needs
to be postulated.

A quasipolynomial upper bound would imply $\mathsf{EXP}=\mathsf{NEXP}$:
an accepting computation of length $2^{n^a}$ has a SAT encoding of length
$2^{n^{O(1)}}$, on which a quasipolynomial algorithm still takes
$2^{n^{O(1)}}$ time. Under (H), the same reasoning at linear-exponential
scales gives $\mathsf E=\mathsf{NE}$. These are consequences, not hierarchy
contradictions: each is an equality across deterministic and nondeterministic
models at a union of resource scales.

\section{Reduction costs must be smaller than the lower bound}
\begin{lemma}[Reduction-normalized exponent test]
For each fixed index $j$, suppose a language $L_j$ has a deterministic
SAT-oracle reduction which on inputs of length $n$ uses non-oracle work
$n^{h_j+o(1)}$, at most $n^{b_j+o(1)}$ queries, and query lengths at most
$n^{a_j+o(1)}$, where $a_j>0$. Under (H),
\[
 L_j\in\mathrm{DTIME}
 \left(n^{\max\{h_j,b_j+a_jc\}+o(1)}\right).
 \tag{2}
\]
If independently $L_j\notin\mathrm{DTIME}(n^{\lambda_j})$, a contradiction
follows when
\[
 \lambda_j>\max\{h_j,b_j+a_jc\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Therefore a sufficient family-level criterion excluding every fixed $c$ is
\[
 \sup_{\{j:\lambda_j>h_j\}}
 \frac{\lambda_j-b_j}{a_j}=\infty.
 \tag{4}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Substitute the fixed algorithm $A$ for each oracle call. Query evaluation costs
at most $n^{b_j+a_jc+o(1)}$; adding construction and other work proves (2).
A strict exponent gap absorbs the $o(1)$ and contradicts the independent
lower bound. For any fixed $c$, (4) supplies one fixed $j$ satisfying (3).
\end{proof}

Merely making $\lambda_j$ large is insufficient if the reduction's exponents
grow too. For example, a language in
$\mathrm{NTIME}(n^{j+1})\setminus\mathrm{NTIME}(n^j)$ also lies outside
$\mathrm{DTIME}(n^j)$, but a direct quasilinear-in-computation Cook--Levin
encoding has size about $n^{j+1}$. Substitution produces roughly
$n^{(j+1)c}$, and construction itself already costs about $n^{j+1}$.
There is no inversion of the hierarchy.

More generally, for time-constructible $t(n)\ge n$, a uniform encoding of a $t$-time computation with output
$t^{\alpha+o(1)}$ and construction $t^{\beta+o(1)}$ yields, under (H),
\[
 t^{\max\{\beta,\alpha c\}+o(1)}.
 \tag{5}
\]
For a universal simulation to violate a polynomial time hierarchy, the
relevant requirement is $\max\{\beta,\alpha c\}<1$, with all model overheads
included. A universal reduction of arbitrary nondeterministic computations
with both output and construction $t^\alpha$ for fixed $\alpha<1$ is already
excluded by nondeterministic hierarchy, since ordinary SAT is
nondeterministically verifiable in near-linear encoding length.
That impossible universal compression cannot serve as a free missing lemma.

\section{Fixed resource inequalities do not exclude every polynomial}
\begin{lemma}
Let a finite collection of scalar lower bounds have the form
\[
 \prod_{j=1}^{q}R_j(N)^{a_{ij}}\ge N^{b_i},
 \qquad a_{ij}\ge0,\quad \sum_j a_{ij}>0.
\]
Then the polynomial assignments $R_j(N)=N^C$ satisfy all of them whenever
\[
 C\ge\max_i\frac{b_i}{\sum_j a_{ij}}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The exponent on the left becomes $C\sum_j a_{ij}$, which is at least $b_i$
for each row.
\end{proof}

This lemma concerns only these finite scalar inequalities. It does not
limit the power of finite mathematical proofs, exclude induction over an
unbounded parameter, or apply when additional upper constraints prevent
the displayed assignment. It explains why fixed-degree time-space
inequalities alone cannot exclude an unknown polynomial exponent.
A fully charged transformation into a forbidden time-space region could
still create a contradiction; existence and recursive closure of that
transformation remain separate obligations.

The project's conditional recurrence
\[
 t'\le\max\{11/10,3/5+t/2\},\qquad
 s'\le\max\{1/2,s/2\}
\]
would drive every finite starting pair toward $(6/5,1/2)$. Its algebra is
already in the corpus. The required all-input, same-machine, recursively
iterable transformation is not established. Reports /478 and /479 further
explain why answer-only oracle recursion and universal exponent extraction
cannot simply be assumed to implement it.

\section{The recent decomposition under the polynomial SAT hypothesis}
Let $t^h$ bound the polynomial construction factor in the cited
decomposition, and let $2\le b\le t$. Binary indexing gives per-formula bit
length $\widetilde O(t\log b)$. Substituting (H) gives the charged upper bound
\[
 2^{O(t/b)}
 \left[t^h+\bigl(t\log b\,\log(t+2)\bigr)^c\right].
 \tag{6}
\]
At $b=t$ this specializes to quasilinear Cook--Levin and yields
$t^{\max\{1,c\}}\polylog t$, consistent with (H), without the strict
power saving in (5). Choosing an accepting output is not free;
enumeration in this algorithm is not a lower bound for every algorithm.

\section{A complete bounded-incidence representation control}
\begin{proposition}
Every width-at-most-three CNF has an equisatisfiable Boolean circuit-CSP
whose factors have arity at most three and constant circuit size, and whose
variables belong to at most two factors.
If the source has $m$ clauses and $\ell$ literal occurrences, the output has
$O(m+\ell)$ structural size and
$O((m+\ell)\log(\ell+2))$ conventional encoding bits.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Introduce a fresh variable for each literal occurrence, keeping its sign in
its original OR factor. For an original variable with copies $z_1,\ldots,z_r$,
impose equality. There is no equality factor for $r=1$; use
$\mathrm{EQ}_2(z_1,z_2)$ for $r=2$ and
$\mathrm{EQ}_3(z_1,z_2,z_3)$ for $r=3$.
For $r\ge4$, introduce $w_1,\ldots,w_{r-3}$ and impose
\[
 \mathrm{EQ}_3(z_1,z_2,w_1),\qquad
 \mathrm{EQ}_3(w_j,z_{j+2},w_{j+1})\quad(1\le j\le r-4),
\]
\[
 \mathrm{EQ}_3(w_{r-3},z_{r-1},z_r).
\]
Here $\mathrm{EQ}_3(a,b,c)$ means $a=b=c$. Every occurrence copy is in
one OR and at most one equality factor. Every $w_j$ is in two equality
factors. The chain forces all copies to agree. A source assignment lifts by
giving all copies and auxiliaries the original value; any satisfying output
assignment projects to a satisfying source assignment.

There are at most $2\ell$ variables and at most
$m+\ell-n_{\mathrm{occ}}$ factors, where $n_{\mathrm{occ}}$ counts occurring
source variables. Binary identifiers give the stated bit length.
Repeated literals and opposite polarities use distinct copies and cause no
problem. An empty clause may be a false zero-arity factor, or be handled by
the fixed contradictory unary pair $(z),(\neg z)$.
\end{proof}

This proves NP-completeness for this bounded-incidence relation language,
since membership in NP is immediate. It does not prove $\Pclass\ne\NP$.

The distinction from ordinary occurrence-two CNF is exact:
\[
 \mathrm{EQ}_3(a,b,c)\equiv
 (\neg a\lor b)\land(\neg b\lor c)\land(\neg c\lor a).
\]
Each variable appears twice in this expansion. An occurrence copy therefore
appears up to three times after its OR clause, and an auxiliary appears four
times across two equality factors. Each sign occurs at most twice.
Low factor degree has not produced ordinary literal-occurrence degree two.

There is also a source-identical-geometry control. Let
\[
 U=(x\lor y)\land(x\lor\neg y)\land(\neg x\lor y)\land(\neg x\lor\neg y),
\]
\[
 S=(x\lor y)\land(x\lor\neg y)\land(\neg x\lor y)\land(\neg x\lor y).
\]
The first is UNSAT; the second has the unique assignment $x=y=1$.
Their reductions have identical ordered factor scopes and types, with
eight factors and ten variables. Their common unsigned incidence matrix
has rank seven over $\mathbb F_2$. Only a literal polarity differs.
Thus that topology and rank do not determine the answer.
This is an information-loss control, not an algorithmic lower bound.

\section{Sequential nesting and self-reference controls}
\begin{lemma}[Charged query-volume bound]
Under (H), let an acyclic adaptive SAT computation generate distinct
instantiated query strings of lengths $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_M$.
If $G$ pays for all non-oracle work, an evaluator caching identical actual
strings runs in time
\[
 G+C\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\ell_i+1)^c
 \le G+C\left(\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\ell_i+1)\right)^c.
 \tag{7}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Evaluate each actual query once using $A$, and add all charged work.
For $c\ge1$, the sum of nonnegative $c$th powers is bounded by the $c$th
power of the sum.
\end{proof}

Polynomial total query volume and generation work remain polynomial even
with polynomial sequential depth. Template identity does not justify reuse
across different candidates, while recomputing identical queries is not a
valid lower-bound requirement.

For a polynomial-length explicit same-candidate chain
\[
 y_i(u)=\SAT_z F_i(u,y_1(u),\ldots,y_{i-1}(u),z),
\]
fixed-$u$ evaluation is polynomial under (H). An outer existential search for
$u$ is then an NP computation, hence polynomial under (H); the universal
version follows by complementation. Changing-candidate recursive calls with
negation may instead express growing quantifier alternation. A fixed-depth
collapse does not supply a depth-independent exponent for such inputs.

\begin{lemma}[Clocked self-reference size threshold]
Suppose an explicit formula $F_T$ satisfies
\[
 \SAT(F_T)=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 A(F_T)\text{ returns }0\text{ within }T\text{ steps}.
\]
If $A$ is correct, it cannot halt within $T$. Under (H), with harmless
length-convention adjustment,
\[
 |F_T|+1>(T/C)^{1/c}.
 \tag{8}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An early zero makes the equivalence declare the formula satisfiable.
An early one makes its right side false, declaring the formula
unsatisfiable. Either early answer would therefore be wrong.
Combine runtime $>T$ with the assumed upper bound.
\end{proof}

A contradiction would require both the semantic equivalence and a
construction below (8). Available explicit computation encodings do not
supply that saving. A succinct program or a semantic recursion-theorem
fixed point is not automatically a small ordinary SAT formula.
All self-construction work belongs inside the represented clock.

\section{Refining the quasipolynomial proof-to-time calculation}
Fix $r\ge1$ independently of the solver. The proposed, unproved bridge is
\[
 \forall A\text{ correct}\ \exists C_A,N_A\
 \forall N\ge N_A:\quad
 \log W_R(N)\le
 C_A[\log(N+T_A(N)+2)]^r.
 \tag{B}
\]
This is the same global kind of bridge as in /540. Set
\[
 g(N)=\frac{\log W_R(N)}{(\log N)^r}.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Exact scalar growth comparison]
Under (B), for all sufficiently large $N$ with $W_R(N)>0$,
\[
 T_A(N)\ge N^{C_A^{-1/r}g(N)^{1/r}}-N-2.
 \tag{9}
\]
Consequently, $\limsup_{N\to\infty}g(N)=\infty$ excludes every polynomial
SAT algorithm.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take an $r$th root in (B), exponentiate, and subtract $N+2$.
For a hypothetical polynomial exponent $c$, choose an unbounded subsequence
on which the exponent in (9) exceeds $c+2$. This contradicts (H).
\end{proof}

For this scalar comparison alone, bounded $\limsup g$ cannot exclude all
polynomial upper bounds with unrestricted constants. This is not a
necessity theorem for all possible separation arguments.

A particularly mild sufficient lower-bound premise is
\[
 \log W_R(N)\ge
 \kappa(\log N)^r\log\log N
 \quad\text{on an unbounded set}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Equations (9)--(10) yield
\[
 T_A(N)\ge
 N^{\Omega_A((\log\log N)^{1/r})}
 \quad\text{infinitely often}.
 \tag{11}
\]
The subtracted $N+2$ is absorbed since the exponent diverges.
This lower-bound scale is superpolynomial but smaller than
$2^{(\log N)^{1+\varepsilon}}$ for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$.
It neither constructs a SAT upper bound nor proves (10) or (B).

More generally, a lower bound
$\log W_R(N)\ge\kappa(\log N)^\beta$ gives
\[
 T_A(N)\ge 2^{\Omega_A((\log N)^{\beta/r})}
\]
when $\beta>r$. If $\beta=r$, arbitrary algorithm-dependent constants
prevent a superpolynomial conclusion from that comparison.

The bridge must hold on the lengths witnessing the lower bound.
Two unrelated infinitely-often statements need not intersect. If lower-bound
witness lengths $N_j$ satisfy $N_{j+1}\le N_j^D$ for fixed $D$, monotonicity
upgrades a log-power lower bound to all large lengths, replacing its
constant $\kappa$ by $\kappa/D^\beta$. Without a gap hypothesis,
infinitely often remains infinitely often.

\subsection*{Solver-dependent exponents and the user's conjecture}
If (B) instead has an unbounded solver-dependent exponent $r_A$, one fixed
$\beta$ excludes only solvers with $r_A<\beta$.
A sufficient stronger premise is that $\log W_R$ is not bounded by any
fixed power of $\log N$.

However, that stronger resource premise plus an all-decider bridge would
exclude $\SAT\in\QP$ as well. A quasipolynomial solver
$T_A(N)\le2^{O((\log N)^d)}$ would imply
$\log W_R(N)=O_A((\log N)^{dr_A})$.
For example, $\log W_R(N)\ge\kappa N^\alpha$ would force
$T_A(N)\ge2^{\Omega_A(N^{\alpha/r_A})}$ on the witness lengths.
This is stronger than the intended $\QP\setminus\Pclass$ scenario.

\subsection*{Local versus global transfer}
An easy family with exponential $R$ proofs refutes an overly strong
instancewise bridge when an algorithm solves that family quickly.
It does not by itself refute (B): $T_A(N)$ is a maximum over \emph{all}
SAT inputs and may be attained elsewhere. Neither a family-hardest formula
nor a solver-worst input may be substituted for the other without a theorem.
This retains REFEREE295's scope distinction.

\section{A weaker sufficient bridge, and why it remains difficult}
For a preselected fixed $R$, define
\[
 \operatorname{QBound}(R):
 \exists d,K,N_0\ \forall N\ge N_0:
 W_R(N)\le2^{K(\log N)^d}.
\]
The hypothesis-only target
\[
 B_Q(R):\qquad
 \SAT\in\Pclass\Longrightarrow\operatorname{QBound}(R)
 \tag{12}
\]
is enough, together with $\neg\operatorname{QBound}(R)$, to prove
$\Pclass\ne\NP$. Unlike (B), it need not say anything about superpolynomial
deciders, and it does not by itself exclude $\SAT\in\QP\setminus\Pclass$.
No common exponent over hypothetical solvers is required.

A concrete sufficient simulation obligation is this: whenever $A$ is a
correct polynomial-time SAT decider, $R$ has quasipolynomial-size proofs
simulating the solver-induced UNSAT proof system $S_A$. A verifier for $S_A$
can simply run the fixed $A$ on the claimed formula; under the premise it is
polynomial-time, sound and complete, and permits proofs of length $O(N)$.
A quasipolynomial-size simulation into the \emph{same fixed} $R$ would
establish (12) provided its charged native cost satisfies
$\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi)\le (|F|+|\pi|)^{O(1)}$.
Alternatively, require quasipolynomial charged cost directly.
Soundness and completeness of $R$ alone do not establish that simulation.

Cook--Reckhow supplies an existential system under $\NP=\mathsf{coNP}$.
It does not move the short proofs into an arbitrary preselected $R$.
Moreover, once $\neg\operatorname{QBound}(R)$ is proved, (12) is logically
equivalent to the desired separation. Its shorter statement is not evidence
that it is easy. The practical narrowing is that it removes unnecessary
runtime-transfer obligations, not that it resolves the central problem.

\section*{Hostile controls and barrier audit}
Horn, 2-SAT, and affine systems show why a representation-based lower
bound needs an all-algorithm transfer. The finite corpus includes elementary
Horn and 2-CNF sources, repeated literals, tautologies, empty clauses, and
both answer labels. The explicit incidence pair demonstrates loss of
polarity information. Earlier affine/Tseitin shortcuts remain mandatory
controls against importing ordinary Resolution bounds into a stronger
mixed calculus.

DELAY wrappers leave semantic answers unchanged while changing time;
COPY can change writable memory; oracle answer equality does not grant
code-level information. Equations (2), (5), and (7) charge these resources
instead of treating state counts as time.

The basic accounting lemmas relativize in matching models and therefore
cannot alone resolve the oracle barriers to $\Pclass$ versus $\NP$.
No nonrelativizing proof of (B) or (12) is supplied.
No algebraic-oracle or low-degree-extension robustness theorem is asserted.
Natural Proofs is not invoked because no constructive large circuit property
or general circuit lower bound is proved.

The cross-domain factor-graph import is an exact representation theorem.
The inference from low incidence, spectral rank, or a small number of local
modules to a polynomial global algorithm is a \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER}.
The missing connection is to a fully charged global algorithm, or to a
resource unavoidable by every such algorithm.
