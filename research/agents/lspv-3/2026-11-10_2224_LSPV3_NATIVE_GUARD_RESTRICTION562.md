## Theorem 562.1: source restriction and Resolution cost sandwich

For a CNF A whose original clauses are C_1,...,C_m, with fresh guard t, define

$$
G_t(A)=(t)\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}(\neg t\vee C_i).
$$

Then A and G_t(A) are equisatisfiable, with models in bijection via a maps to (a,t=1). Restricting every signed original at t=1 yields precisely A. For ordinary shared-DAG binary Resolution with one fixed cost per distinct resolvent:

$$
W_{\mathrm{Res}}(A)\ \le\ W_{\mathrm{Res}}(G_t(A))\ \le\ W_{\mathrm{Res}}(A)+m.
$$

The upper bound is constructive: resolve the unit t once with each guarded original to obtain each C_i, paying at most m, then replay a freely preselected best Resolution proof of A. The lower bound is the standard restriction-closure induction for Resolution: restrict a proof of G_t(A) to t=1, replace true clauses and steps whose conclusions collapse to a parent by a prior-ancestor alias, and retain only legal resolvents or stronger antecedents. Full shared-DAG ancestry never duplicates a paid node. This is a classical *pure Resolution* cost transport, not a new all-mixed proof lower bound. No discovery cost is added to W.

The guarded original clauses also have width four rather than width three. This strengthens the explicit /549 two-accepted-checker noncomposition observation with a universal source-cardinality witness. It does not rule out a separate source-preserving verified conversion.
