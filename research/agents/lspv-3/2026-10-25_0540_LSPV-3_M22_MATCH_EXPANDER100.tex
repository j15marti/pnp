\section{Source-named incidence graph and polynomial certificate}
Let $P$ consist of the all-positive triples of a valid $F$. Define $G_+(F)$ with one vertex for each positive clause and one named edge $e_x$ for each original variable $x$, joining the two positive clauses in which $x$ occurs. This is a cubic loopless multigraph: every positive clause has three incident variable edges; repetitions between two clause vertices are possible, and source names are never discarded. A negative signed clause corresponds to a triple of distinct named edges.

\begin{theorem}[Source-consistent matching certificate, PROVED/EXACT restricted]
Suppose $G_+(F)$ has a perfect matching $M$ and each negative clause contains at least two named edges incident at one common positive-clause vertex. Then the source assignment
\[
x_e=\begin{cases}1&e\in M,\\0&e\notin M\end{cases}
\]
satisfies $F$. Recognition of the hypotheses and verification of the returned one-bit SAT decision is polynomial in the complete indexed source length. For bipartite $G_+(F)$ a perfect matching can be found using the Hopcroft--Karp algorithm \cite{hopcroft}. No pre-supplied SAT/UNSAT label or free boundary oracle is used.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A matching covers each positive-clause vertex exactly once, hence each positive OR clause has exactly one true literal. A negative clause is false only if all three corresponding source edges are in $M$. Two edges sharing a vertex cannot both be in a matching, so each negative clause has at least one false variable and thus a true negative literal. The indexed source determines all edge endpoints by a scan of the positive occurrences. A bipartiteness test, matching search, negative-pair endpoint comparisons, assignment replay and grammar validation are ordinary polynomial algorithms. In particular a total uniform exact SAT decider can validate this certificate and return YES on its successful cases and run a correct exhaustive fallback on all other ordinary inputs. Its worst-case time is not claimed polynomial.
\end{proof}

\section{Connected cubic bipartite construction with only two unmatched supports}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be any connected \emph{simple} cubic bipartite graph on even $v$ vertices. Name the original variables by its edges, so $k=|E|=3v/2$. For every vertex $u$ let $P_u$ be the three incident source edge names. Start with the $v$ positive clauses on $P_u$ and the $v$ negative clauses on the \emph{same} supports. Pick an edge $e=uw$ and distinguish $a,c\in P_u\setminus\{e\}$ and $b,d\in P_w\setminus\{e\}$. In only the two negative clauses at $u$ and $w$, exchange the source names $a$ and $b$:
\[
P_u=\{e,a,c\},\quad P_w=\{e,b,d\};
\qquad
Q_u=\{e,b,c\},\quad Q_w=\{e,a,d\}.
\]
All other negative supports remain $P_z$. Write $F_G$ for this source.

\begin{theorem}[In-target exactness and polynomial decision, PROVED/EXACT restricted]
For every such $G$, $F_G$ is connected, belongs to $\CS$, and has
\[
k=3v/2,\quad m=2v=4k/3,\quad
|P\cap Q|=v-2,\quad q=2.
\]
Every source variable has exactly two positive and two negative occurrences. All signed clauses are unique, proper and clausewise monotone. Moreover $F_G$ is SAT, with a deterministic polynomially obtainable assignment.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Positive incidence is the simple cubic graph, hence each edge variable appears in precisely its two endpoint positive clauses. Negative occurrence multiplicities are unchanged when $a$ and $b$ are exchanged, and $e,c,d$ retain their original occurrences. Because a simple graph has no second edge between $u,w$, the new three-edge sets $\{e,b,c\}$ and $\{e,a,d\}$ are different from any original star $P_z$ and from each other; all other paired stars remain. The original positive incidence connects all source variables via graph paths, so the signed formula is connected. Every unchanged negative clause has a triple of edges meeting at its original vertex. The two changed negative clauses each contain an adjacent edge pair $(e,c)$ and $(e,d)$ respectively. A regular bipartite graph has a perfect matching by Hall's theorem. The preceding certificate therefore applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All-clause-order named-source frontier, PROVED/EXACT restricted]
Let $B(G)=\min_{|U|=v/2}|\delta_G(U)|$ denote edge-bisection width. For \emph{every} permutation of all $2v$ signed original clauses of $F_G$, there is a prefix/suffix cut exposing at least $B(G)$ live original source names that occur on both sides. \[
w(F_G)\ge h_0v/2=\Omega(k)=\Omega(N/\log N).
\]
Infinite simple cubic bipartite expander families exist; the Ramanujan constructions of Marcus--Spielman--Srivastava \cite{mss} provide a primary existence anchor. The theorem constrains \emph{unchanged source-name clause scans}, not arbitrary adaptive deciders, affine/semantic rewrites or work-tape memory.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In any clause permutation stop immediately after the $(v/2)$th \emph{positive} clause has appeared. Let $U$ be those $v/2$ processed positive graph vertices. Every edge $e=uw$ crossing $\delta_G(U)$ occurs in a positive clause belonging to $U$ and in another positive clause belonging to $V\setminus U$, thus its original source name occurs on both sides regardless of where the negative clauses were placed. At least $B(G)$ names are live at that cut. Apply edge expansion and $N=\Theta(v\log v)$.
\end{proof}
