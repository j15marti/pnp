\section{Source model, falsifiers, and exact theorem}
The selected language is the full duplicate-free proper clausewise-monotone $3$-SAT-$(2,2)$ language of D\"ocker: every original clause contains three distinct variables of one polarity, and every active variable occurs twice per polarity. The decider must reject malformed inputs. For $n$ variables, $m=4n/3$ clauses and ordinary explicit indexed record encoding of $N=\Theta(n\log n)$ bits, consider either one separately addressable sign bit per literal or one per monotone clause.

Four falsifiers were checked: (F1) a successful accepting computation can skip source sign positions (false in the total-language model); (F2) the same argument holds under a valid-input promise (not established, since mutants leave the promise); (F3) a batched frequency/duplicate validator fits the strict FLMV time-space box (false for that architecture alone); and (F4) logical/physical XOR alone predicts satisfiability or computational work (false for matched original SAT/UNSAT sources).

\begin{theorem}[Total-language acceptance authentication]
For every valid \emph{satisfiable} canonical M22 input, every accepting computation of every deterministic total exact decision algorithm rejecting malformed strings reads all $3m$ sign positions of the literal-sign representation, respectively all $m$ sign positions in the one-sign-per-clause representation. This forces only $\Omega(N/\log N)$ separate bit-input probes, not a superlinear time lower bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the accepting transcript and an unread sign position. Flipping one literal sign converts the affected monotone three-clause into a mixed-sign clause, hence the mutated string is malformed and must be rejected. With one sign per whole clause, flipping the clause sign changes each of three original variables from quota $(2,2)$ to $(1,3)$ or $(3,1)$, again malformed. The unread-bit mutation produces exactly the original sequence of source replies, deterministic states and accepting output, contradiction. Repeat for all sign positions. The technique is the known sensitive-coordinate/certificate adversary, already present in the general project SIGN-SENSITIVITY-WORD-FIREWALL171. On a valid UNSAT source, malformed mutants are also NO, so this proof supplies no corresponding all-sign UNSAT lower bound.
\end{proof}
\section{A fully specified but restricted batch-acquisition gate}
Given batch width $B\in[1,n]$, first scan the original source for local syntax. For every batch of at most $B$ variable names, scan all $m$ clauses and count positive/negative occurrences; require exactly two each. For each batch of at most $B$ source clauses, load and sort its signed three-literal keys, reject within-batch duplicates, then rescan all $m$ clauses to reject earlier duplicate keys. The fully validated input can then be passed to any complete both-label decision procedure, such as UP-first DPLL. On valid sources the exact number of \emph{source clause-record fetches} of this declared procedure is
\[
R(n,m,B)=2m+m\lceil n/B\rceil+m\lceil m/B\rceil.
\]
The explicit fixed-width counter-and-key workspace is $O(B\log n+\log n)$ bits. With $B=n^{\beta+o(1)}$ the designated source rereads give the architecture-specific exponent pair $t=2-\beta+o(1)$, $s=\beta+o(1)$. Consequently $t+s\ge2-o(1)$ for this particular batch method, which cannot meet the strict numerical FLMV box $t<241/200$, $s<11642479/23232400$. This is \emph{not} a lower bound for alternative source-implicit grammar checking, arbitrary target deciders or SAT itself.
