\section{An exact graph lemma}
\begin{lemma}[Defect-push weak edge coloring]
Let $G$ be any finite loopless multigraph all of whose vertices have degree two or three. Assign an arbitrary forbidden bit $b_v\in\{0,1\}$ to each degree-two vertex. Then edges admit a binary coloring such that every degree-three vertex sees both colors and at each degree-two vertex its two incident edges are \emph{not both} colored $b_v$. Such a coloring is constructible in polynomial bit work using at most $|V(G)|(|V(G)|+1)$ \emph{logical edge-color toggles}. All actual addressing, access, control, and writes remain paid.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Initialize edge bits arbitrarily. A vertex is defective if its specified rule fails. Thus a defective degree-three vertex is monochromatic, and a defective degree-two vertex has both incident edges equal to $b_v$. Pick one defective vertex $v$. Toggle an incident edge. This heals $v$. If the opposite endpoint stays valid, stop. Otherwise the defect moves to that endpoint. It was valid before the toggle, and the toggled edge was pivotal: at a degree-three vertex, it was its unique minority-colored edge; at a degree-two vertex, it was its sole edge colored opposite $b_v$. Toggle a \emph{different} incident edge there, and continue.

Before any repeated vertex, the defect walk visits distinct vertices. Every departed vertex is healed. Upon the first revisit, flipping the arrival edge cannot make the revisited vertex defective. If that vertex is the initial vertex, its original monochromatic edges have already had a \emph{different} edge flipped, so flipping the new incident edge still leaves it valid. If it is a previously traversed degree-three vertex, its pivotal minority edge was changed to the majority color on arrival and one other edge was changed to the minority color on departure; the returning third edge can be flipped without restoring monochromaticity. A previously traversed degree-two vertex has no unused third edge and cannot be the first nontrivial revisit. Parallel edges do not affect these arguments because edges carry distinct identities; loops are excluded.

Thus the walk stops within $|V(G)|+1$ toggles, strictly reducing the number of defective vertices and creating no surviving new defect. Repeating at most $|V(G)|$ times gives the claimed bound. Local condition tests have constant edge degree, but every edge probe, index computation, visited-set update, unchanged-value write, and traversal choice carries ordinary bit cost.
\end{proof}
