Let $F_b$ be their conjunction, and $X_b(a)=N_0(a)$ the number of original clauses with zero true literals under a \emph{globally coherent} complete edge assignment $a$.
\begin{theorem}
For even and odd total vertex-charge parity respectively, the resulting formulas have identical unsigned clause supports and opposite SAT labels. For every integer $0\le r<q$,
\[
\mathbb E[X_{\rm even}^r]=\mathbb E[X_{\rm odd}^r],\qquad
\mathbb E[(X)_r]=\frac{(q)_r}{2^r},
\]
where $(z)_r=z(z-1)\cdots(z-r+1)$. At order $q$ the factorial moments differ by $q!/2^{q-1}$. The even-charge source has exactly $2^{n-q+1}$ models, the odd-charge source none. Thus for every fixed $r$ there are a connected proper-3CNF SAT/UNSAT pair whose first $r$ exact $N_0$ moments coincide.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The $\mathbb F_2$ vertex-edge incidence matrix $B$ of a connected graph has rank $q-1$ and image the even-parity vectors. Therefore $Ba$ for uniform $a$ is uniform on this image, with $2^{n-q+1}$ preimages per syndrome. The violated-equation vector $Y=Ba\oplus b$ is uniform on the charge-parity class, so every proper subset of its coordinates is jointly independent fair bits. Since a satisfied parity block falsifies no original clause and a violated block falsifies exactly one, $X_b=\sum_vY_v$. Hence for $r<q$ the ordered-distinct falling moments are $(q)_r/2^r$, and the triangular Stirling transform gives raw moment equality. The exact probability generating function is $2^{-q}[(1+z)^q+(-1)^\varepsilon(1-z)^q]$. Differentiating at $z=1$ proves the $q$th-order separation. Zero syndrome is present only for even charge; the rank gives its witness count.
\end{proof}
