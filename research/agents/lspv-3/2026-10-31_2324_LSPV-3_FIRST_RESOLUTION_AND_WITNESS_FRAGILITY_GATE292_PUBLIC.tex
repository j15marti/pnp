\begin{theorem}[Original-original one-round width]
Every valid $n$-variable target has exactly $4n$ signed opposite-parent pivot attempts (counting original clause-index pairs and pivots). Each attempt yields either a tautology or a non-tautological width-four clause containing two positive and two negative literals on distinct variables. No original-original single resolvent is unit, Krom or width three.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}Each variable occurs twice positive and twice negative, giving four pivot pairs. Removing one opposite pivot from two same-sign triples leaves two positive and two negative literals. Any additional shared source variable occurs with opposite signs and makes the result tautological; otherwise all four variables are distinct.\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Fragile-neighbor bound, sharp]
For every satisfying assignment $a$, let $N_r(a)$ count original clauses with exactly $r$ true literals, and let $Q(a)$ be the set of variables uniquely satisfying at least one original clause. Then
\[
N_1(a)=m/2+N_3(a),\qquad |Q(a)|\ge\lceil N_1(a)/2\rceil\ge n/3.
\]
A one-bit flip of $a$ fails SAT if and only if it flips a variable in $Q(a)$. The $n/3$ bound is attained for infinitely many canonical formulas.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}Each source variable contributes exactly two true signed occurrences whatever its Boolean value, hence $\sum_jt_j=2n=3m/2$, so $N_1=N_2+3(N_3-N_0)$. A witness has $N_0=0$, giving $N_1=m/2+N_3$. Each variable uniquely satisfies at most its two true occurrences. Flipping a uniquely satisfied literal falsifies that clause; without any uniquely satisfied literal of the variable, a flip cannot falsify a clause. Tightness: positive supports $123,124,356,456$ and their identical negative supports form a canonical six-variable source. Set $x_3=x_4=1$, others zero. Exactly $3,4$ are critical. Take disjoint signed-indexed copies.\end{proof}
