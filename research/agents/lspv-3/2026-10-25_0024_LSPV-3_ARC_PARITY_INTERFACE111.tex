\section{Matched opposite-label proper 3CNFs}
For integer $b\ge3$, take the same boundary names $x_1,\ldots,x_b$ in two modules. Define $M_d(x)$ on private prefix names $y_1,\ldots,y_{b-1}$ and private helpers $p,q$ by
\[
 y_1=x_1\oplus x_2,\quad
 y_j=y_{j-1}\oplus x_{j+1}\ (2\le j\le b-1),\quad
 y_{b-1}=d.
\]
Each three-distinct-variable XOR relation is encoded by its FOUR forbidden rows as four proper ternary CNF clauses. Replace its final unit condition by the four clauses
\[
 (\ell_d\vee p\vee q),\
 (\ell_d\vee p\vee\neg q),\
 (\ell_d\vee\neg p\vee q),\
 (\ell_d\vee\neg p\vee\neg q),
 \qquad \ell_d=\begin{cases}y_{b-1}&d=1,\\\neg y_{b-1}&d=0.\end{cases}
\]
These force the unit while leaving $p,q$ free. Thus each module has exactly $4b$ distinct proper ternary clauses and $2b+1$ active names. Use disjoint private namespaces in $M'_d$ while retaining exactly the $b$ source boundary names:
\[
 F_b^{YES}=M_0(x)\wedge M'_0(x),\qquad
 F_b^{NO}=M_0(x)\wedge M'_1(x).
\]
Each full source has $3b+2$ distinct active names, $8b$ unique proper ternary clauses and dense indexed length $N=\Theta(b\log(b+2))$. Changing YES to NO affects four final pin sign fields only. These are valid ORDINARY SAT instances, not generally M22; the relevant target is all ordinary inputs.

\begin{theorem}[Exact full join and strict-marginal indistinguishability]\label{th:join}
For every $b\ge3$,
\[
 \SAT(F_b^{YES})=1,\quad \#F_b^{YES}=2^{b+3},\qquad
 \SAT(F_b^{NO})=0.
\]
Each isolated module is SAT. For each strict $S\subsetneq[b]$ and each $a\in\{0,1\}^{S}$, the number of accepted boundary completions of $M_d$ is $2^{b-|S|-1}$, independent of $d$; including the private helpers gives exactly $4\cdot 2^{b-|S|-1}$ complete extensions. Hence every proper-coordinate projection, including its exact projected assignment counts, is identical for $M_0$ and $M_1$. A deterministic adaptive source-blind controller observing only such per-module projections, without original sign fields or full boundary predicates, receives the same transcript on both parents and cannot decide both labels.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $x$, each prefix XOR uniquely fixes its output; the final pin accepts iff $\oplus_i x_i=d$, with precisely four independent helper choices. The YES pair has $2^{b-1}$ compatible boundary assignments, each admitting 16 helper choices, and the NO pair has none. If $S$ is strict, at least one variable is free, and for either parity exactly half of the $2^{b-|S|}$ completions pass. The observation transcript is therefore independent of the right module's $d$, so adaptation cannot distinguish the labels. Original source inspection or a linear-time parity computation does distinguish them. Thus this is NOT an arbitrary-SAT time lower bound.
\end{proof}

\section{Known parity encoding lower bound and a source-named child fence}
A CNF that describes parity directly on only the $b$ boundary names needs exactly $2^{b-1}$ clauses, each involving ALL $b$ names. Omitting one $x_i$ makes a clause false under two adjacent assignments of opposite parity; a width-$b$ clause excludes one assignment, and $2^{b-1}$ assignments must be excluded. This is a KNOWN result \cite{ekms}. The primary theorem of Emdin--Kulikov--Mihajlin--Slezkin further proves for a parity CNF with $u$ existential auxiliary variables and $m$ clauses:
\[
 m\ge\Omega\bigl((u+1)\,2^{b/(u+1)}/b\bigr),\qquad m\ge3b-9.
\]
A polynomial clause budget $m\le N^C$ requires $u=\Omega(b/\log N)$ whenever $b\le\mathrm{poly}(N)$, subject to the paper's usual asymptotic constants. This bounds an EXPLICIT REPRESENTATION, not runtime of a one-bit decider. Our $M_d$ uses $b+1$ private names and $4b$ clauses, demonstrating the compact affine/Tseitin escape.

\begin{proposition}[Name-faithful length floor]\label{prop:floor}
In a standard dense indexed clause-list encoding, every ordinary child that explicitly retains all $b$ distinct shared source names in its OWN clause body needs $\Omega(b\log b)$ bits. Since the parent above has $N=\Theta(b\log b)$ bits, such a child has length $\Omega(N)$ and cannot be $N^{\lambda+o(1)}$ for fixed $\lambda<1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each distinct retained name must occur in at least one literal. Renumbering names densely requires $\Omega(\log b)$ bits per literal identifier, even ignoring sign and header fields. The complete parent has $8b$ constant-width clauses with identifiers of $O(\log b)$ bits. The conclusion is restricted to explicit full-name retention: source-aware elimination, symbolic parity, or a charged virtual accessor can alter the representation and are NOT excluded.
\end{proof}
