\section{Filter and charged closure}
\begin{theorem}
In a UP-saturated residual without an empty clause, every literal $p$ whose assignment causes a UP conflict must falsify a literal in a residual binary clause. Recompute this candidate set after every committed inference. Testing only those candidates, in the same relative order, yields the same full failed-literal closure as testing all literals.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are no remaining unsatisfied units. An assignment not falsifying a binary literal leaves every shortened unsatisfied clause of length at least two. No unit or empty clause is created, so no propagation chain starts. Such a probe cannot change the state. Induct on the successful probes after removing these inert tests from the order.
\end{proof}
At most $n$ probes are committed, and at most $2n(n+1)$ candidates are tried. With maximum width $k$, literal-occurrence count $L$, physical bit length $N$, and an indexed occurrence propagator, discovery costs conservatively $O(N+n^2(kL+n+\log(n+2)))$ word operations. Source-unit reason certificates and working state occupy $O((L+n^2)\log(N+2))$ bits. Every failed attempt, residual scan, candidate construction and verification is charged. This is per closure; the complete DPLL tree may be exponential. Sequential-tape and fixed-UTM implementations require additional access/simulation costs; no native lower bound is asserted.
