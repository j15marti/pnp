\begin{theorem}[Exact structure]
\[
n=3r,\quad m=3r(r-1),\quad\delta=\kappa=2r-2,\quad
\operatorname{tw}\ge2r-2,\quad
\operatorname{vc}=2r,\quad f=m-n+1=3r^2-6r+1.
\]
All $3r$ open neighborhoods are distinct, every ternary edge relation is nonpermutation, all vertices remain three-choice under unpinned BINARYPROJ1 projection, and there are exactly six proper three-colorings.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each vertex has $2r-2$ neighbors, and its nonneighbors other than itself are the $r-1$ remaining vertices of its part plus the two foreign vertices of its index. This distinguishes all open neighborhoods. An independent set intersecting two parts must use a single index, so has size at most three. For $r\ge4$ the only maximum independent sets, all of size $r$, are the three full parts. Thus $\operatorname{vc}=2r$. Three color classes covering $3r$ vertices are forced to be exactly these parts, with six palette permutations; all three colors are locally possible at every vertex. Treewidth at least minimum degree follows from degeneracy.

For connectivity, the complement $H=K_3\square K_r$ is a rook graph. Any nonempty disjoint $X,Y$ with all cross edges in $H$ obey $|X|+|Y|\le r+2$. If either is a singleton, this is its degree $r+1$ plus one. Otherwise, choose two vertices of $X$: if they share a row, their common neighbors lie in that row, forcing both sets into a row and total at most $r$; if same column in two distinct rows, they have only one common neighbor, impossible; if different rows and columns, they have just two opposite rectangle corners as common neighbors, which reciprocally have only those two as common neighbors, giving total at most four. Disconnecting $\Gamma_r$ by deleting $S$ implies such a complete cross-biclique in $H$ on all $3r-|S|$ remaining vertices, so $|S|\ge2r-2$. Deleting all neighbors of one vertex isolates it, so equality holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact odd-cycle hitting]
There are $r(r-1)(r-2)$ triangles, $r-2$ per edge and $(r-1)(r-2)$ per vertex. The exact minimum edge and vertex bipartization sizes are respectively $r(r-1)$ and $r$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each triangle chooses three distinct indices and one vertex per part. To hit them all requires at least total triangles divided by the incidence per edge or vertex, respectively. Deleting every $A$--$B$ edge, or all $A$ vertices, achieves the two bounds and leaves a bipartite graph.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial source-aware recovery]

It uses conservative simultaneous time $O((N+2)^5)$ and space $O((N+2)^3)$, including source acquisition and output.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compute the complement $H$. Retain its edge $uv$ if $u,v$ have at least two common neighbors in $H$. Within-part pairs have $r-2\ge2$ common neighbors; same-index cross-part pairs have exactly one. Thus the retained graph has three clique components of size $r$. Recover these parts, check that their cross-part nonedges are aligned perfect matchings, and verify every source adjacency. Only if all tests pass, color the three components differently and independently verify the result. No favorable coordinates are supplied. Finite bit loops are polynomial.
\end{proof}
Adding one same-part edge makes the source unsatisfiable and exhibits a $K_4$: use the two endpoints and two foreign-part vertices of distinct indices outside the endpoint indices.
