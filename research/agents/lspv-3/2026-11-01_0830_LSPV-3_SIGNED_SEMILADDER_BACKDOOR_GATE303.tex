\section*{Exact obstruction and falsifiers}
The missing universal claim is a polynomial bound on total strategy acquisition, switching and descendant computation for every valid source of the canonical duplicate-free proper monotone 3-SAT-(2,2) language. We falsify the narrower hypothesis that uniformly bounded signed semi-ladder height guarantees a small weak backdoor into bounded incidence-treewidth.
\begin{description}
\item[] F1. The signed semi-ladder promise is restrictive for this grammar: FALSIFIED (maximum degrees 3 and 4).
\item[] F2. Every connected easy canonical source has $o(n)$ weak $(k,t)$ backdoors for fixed $t$: FALSIFIED by the family below.
\item[] F3. The absence of a small weak backdoor implies UNSAT: FALSIFIED since this family is SAT with a polynomially acquired witness.
\item[] F4. Finite adaptive terminals plus exact DPLL fallback imply polynomial all-input total work: OPEN.
\end{description}
\begin{theorem}[Restricted family]
For every even $r\ge4$, the previously authenticated connected GATE302 canonical source $F_r$ has $n=6r^2$ variables and $m=8r^2$ clauses. The signed vertex incidence graph has maximum degree 3 and semi-ladder height at most 4; signed edge incidence has maximum degree 4 and semi-ladder height at most 5. The original source has a polynomially acquired checked SAT witness and its complement, and a separate four-pin UNSAT residual with opposite source-derived unit clauses. Nevertheless for every integer $t\ge0$ and every original-variable partial assignment $p$,
\[
|p|<\tfrac12\left\lfloor\frac r{t+3}\right\rfloor^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{tw}(I(F_r|_p))>t .
\]
Thus every weak or strong $(k,t)$ backdoor requires
\[
\boxed{k\ge\left\lceil\tfrac12\left\lfloor\frac r{t+3}\right\rfloor^2\right\rceil.}
\]
In particular $k=\Omega(n)$ for fixed $t$, and $k=\Omega(n/\log^2 n)$ for $t=O(\log n)$ in the nontrivial range.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In GATE302, each cell of an $r\times r$ toroidal grid is replaced by a four-cycle of cubic graph ports N,E,S,W. The resulting $H_r$ has $4r^2$ vertices and $6r^2$ edges. Every graph edge is one Boolean source variable; each cubic vertex supplies a positive and a negative clause on its incident triple. Selected positive external north occurrences are exchanged in paired columns, preserving all exact source quotas and uniqueness. Negative clauses are unchanged. Reconstruct $H_r$ from those negative clauses, check the four-cycle partition and positive two-internal-edge scope, and set alternating internal matching edges TRUE. This source-authenticated polynomial recognizer verifies both this SAT witness and its complement against every original clause. A four-pin assignment on an unchanged horizontal external edge produces opposing original-source units $(x_e)$ and $(\neg x_e)$ with no pre-existing empty clause.

The signed vertex graph has one node per positive/negative literal, with two original clause incidences plus one opposite-literal edge, and each original clause has degree three. The signed edge graph has original-variable degree four and original-clause degree three. In any simple graph of maximum degree $\Delta$, the first vertex of a semi-ladder of height $h$ has $h-1$ distinct later neighbors, hence $h\le\Delta+1$.

The negative-clause plus variable incidence subgraph is a subdivision of $H_r$: graph edge $e=uv$ corresponds to $C_u^--x_e-C_v^-$. Put $s=t+3$, $q=\lfloor r/s\rfloor$, and choose $q^2$ pairwise vertex-disjoint $s\times s$ grid-cell patches. In each patch retain only its original negative-clause vertices and the original variable vertices whose two negative-clause endpoints are in the patch. Contracting four-cycles in a patch gives an $s\times s$ planar-grid minor, of treewidth at least $s-1=t+2>t$.

Pinning $x_e$ removes its original variable vertex; pinning it FALSE additionally satisfies and removes its two negative-clause endpoints. Because the patches are pairwise vertex-disjoint and an original variable has only two negative-clause endpoints, each pin can disturb at most two patches, including its own variable vertex when both endpoints are in the same patch. If $2|p|<q^2$, one entire large-width negative-incidence patch survives in $I(F_r|_p)$, proving the claim for SAT and UNSAT cubes alike. The SAT witness itself is polynomially recognizable, so no hardness of arbitrary algorithms follows.
\end{proof}
