\section{Claim card and exact target}
The fixed language is
\[
 \Ltwo=\{\operatorname{enc}(F):F\text{ is nonempty, proper,
 distinct-clause signed exact-}(2,2)\text{ 3-CNF and satisfiable}\},
\]
with malformed or out-of-domain strings rejected.  The desired endpoint is
\[
 \forall A\,\forall k\in\mathbb N\,\forall C>0\,\forall N_0\,
 \exists G:\quad |\operatorname{enc}G|\ge N_0
 \ \land\ T_A(G)>C|\operatorname{enc}G|^k ,
\]
where $A$ ranges over total deterministic uniform deciders of this one
language.  BLOCKPROBE-TEST1 instead gives a restricted byte-query baseline on
an explicit compiler-image family.  This run audits the conversion of that
baseline to total encoded length.

\section{The exact padding firewall}
Let $P=U((x_1\vee x_2\vee x_3))$, where $U$ is the actual repository
exact-$(2,2)$ compiler.  Then $P$ is a fixed satisfiable, proper,
distinct-clause exact-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF.  For variable-disjoint formulas write
$F\sqcup G$ for clause concatenation after offsetting variables of $G$.

Let $X_r$ and $X_{r,i}$ be the YES and one-bad-block NO instances from
BLOCKPROBE-TEST1.  In its compact ordered JSON encoding, $D_i$ is the set of
byte positions on which $X_r$ and $X_{r,i}$ differ.  Define
\[
 X_{r,s}=X_r\sqcup P^{\sqcup s},\qquad
 X_{r,i,s}=X_{r,i}\sqcup P^{\sqcup s}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{LEGAL-PADDING-PROBE-FIREWALL1}]
For all $r\ge1$ and $s\ge0$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item $X_{r,s}$ and $X_{r,i,s}$ are valid \(\Ltwo\)-domain encodings,
with $X_{r,s}$ satisfiable and $X_{r,i,s}$ unsatisfiable;
\item the differing-byte set of the padded pair is exactly $D_i$;
\item conditional on the BLOCKPROBE disjointness premise, the same transcript
argument forces one query in each $D_i$ on $X_{r,s}$, hence $r$ byte probes;
\item $N_{r,s}=|\operatorname{enc}X_{r,s}|\to\infty$ as $s\to\infty$
for fixed $r$. With $s=2^r$, the certificate supplies only
$r=\Theta(\log N_{r,2^r})$ probes.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, the $D_i$ certificate alone cannot establish a lower bound
$g(N)$ with $g(N)/r\to\infty$ uniformly over the legal padding closure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every variable in a disjoint union retains its two positive and two negative
occurrences; properness, clause distinctness and density are preserved by
offset renaming. SAT of a disjoint union is the conjunction of component SAT
values. Thus adjoining satisfiable $P$ preserves the label.

The good and bad bases have equal variable and clause counts. Appending
identical offset copies of $P$ appends identical encoded data. Earlier byte
positions agree or differ exactly as before; the common replacement of the
old closing bracket by a comma creates no pairwise difference. If a
deterministic byte-query algorithm does not query $D_i$ on the padded YES
realization, its transcript is identical on the padded NO alternative.
Pairwise disjointness yields $r$ hits. Each padding component adds clauses and
variables, and offsets use $O(\log(r+s))$ digits, so
$N_{r,s}=\Theta((r+s)\log(r+s))$. For $s=2^r$,
$N_{r,s}=\Theta(2^r r)$ and $r=\Theta(\log N_{r,s})$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope.}
The theorem does not upper-bound true query complexity, refute
BLOCKPROBE-TEST1 on $X_r$, or decide all of \(\Ltwo\). It retires only the
inference from the exhibited $D_i$ sets to a padding-robust total-length
bound. A different adversary could use padding bytes.

\section{SAME\_REALIZATION, costs and representation}
The transcript is the actual execution on padded YES string $X_{r,s}$; the
$X_{r,i,s}$ are counterfactual witnesses. The firewall charges the entire
padded string in $N$ and grants no free core discovery. The model is adaptive
byte query, not RAM, circuit, branching program, arithmetic circuit, PC/PCR,
Resolution, DPLL or whole-machine time. Since the output is one bit, no
compatibility object, proof or assignment is mandatory. Visible independent

warning, not an algorithm for general \(\Ltwo\).

\section{Primary-literature and barrier-transfer preflight}
The obstacles are direct-sum/query sensitivity versus whole-machine time and
robustness of length under legal semantic padding.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Directly transferable theorem/model.} Nisan's decision-tree
framework and Jain--Klauck--Santha direct-sum theorems control explicit query
models. The transcript proof is stated directly in that model.
\item \textbf{Useful analogy requiring a new transfer theorem.} VLSI
area--time and communication direct-sum intuitions motivate charging
independent blocks, but supply no theorem converting these $D_i$ hits into
superpolynomial time for unrestricted uniform \(\Ltwo\) deciders.
\item \textbf{Misleading analogy / failed transfer.} Fortnow--van Melkebeek
SAT time--space bounds require declared joint resource restrictions and do
not cover every polynomial-time algorithm with arbitrary polynomial space.
\item \textbf{Barrier.} The firewall and block theorem relativize and are
representation-level. They supply no nonrelativizing or nonalgebrizing hinge;
Aaronson--Wigderson remains an active warning.
\end{itemize}
