\section{The smallest balanced, nonpaired named-join construction}

                  (\neg a\lor\neg b\lor c).}

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each of the six names occurs twice in the displayed positive family and twice in the negative family. The named supports overlap and connect the incidence graph, and the two unmatched support pairs prevent full pairing. With only three active variables, $m=4$ and two distinct all-positive ternaries would be needed on the sole available triple; uniqueness makes this impossible. Thus six is the smallest nonempty valid $\CM$ source size.

For each fixed boundary word, setting all $H$ bits to one satisfies $F_+$ and setting them all to zero satisfies $F_-$. For boundary $001$, positive clauses $abu,abv$ force $u=v=1$, contradicting negative clause $cuv$. For boundary $110$, negative clauses $abu,abw$ force $u=w=0$, contradicting positive clause $cuw$. Every other word has a \emph{joint} witness: $000\mapsto(1,1,0)$, $111\mapsto(0,1,0)$; if $a\ne b$, choose $(1,1,0)$ for $c=0$, or $(0,0,0)$ for $c=1$. Substitution checks all eight clauses. The displayed two mixed-sign clauses exclude precisely the two forbidden words. Every ordered pair of boundary bits takes all four values among the six allowed words. An affine set has power-of-two cardinality; six is not affine. Any proper affine subspace of $\mathbb F_2^3$ contains at most four words, giving full affine hull. The two resulting mixed-sign projected clauses are \emph{outside} $\CM$ and $\CS$ as ordinary target syntax.
\end{proof}

The semantic non-distributivity $\exists H(F_+\land F_-)\not\equiv(\exists H F_+)\land(\exists H F_-)$ is elementary.
