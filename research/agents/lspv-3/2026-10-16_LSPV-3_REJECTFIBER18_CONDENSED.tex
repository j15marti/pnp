\section{CONSTRUCTION OR PROOF ATTEMPT}
Let $C:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$, $Z_C=C^{-1}(0)$, and any $h_C:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}^m$, even chosen with full source access.
\begin{theorem}
Every $h_C$ has a distinct rejecting pair $x,x'$ with $h_C(x)=h_C(x')$ whenever $|Z_C|>2^m$. This bound is set-theoretically sharp.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pigeonhole applied to $h_C|_{Z_C}$ proves necessity. For sharpness, when $|Z_C|\le2^m$ assign distinct output labels to its elements; this does not imply efficient evaluation.
\end{proof}
If $C$ has exactly one satisfying assignment and $n\ge2$, then $|Z_C|=2^n-1>2^m$ for every $m<n$. Thus compressed collisions need not reveal a SAT witness. For the special map $h_C=C$, every collision contains a satisfying endpoint iff $|Z_C|\le1$, in which case testing any two distinct inputs finds a witness. An arbitrary source-aware reconstruction after seeing collision endpoints is NOT excluded; all its computation must be charged, including circuit/source parsing, map construction, verification, and any Cook-Levin or Tseitin compilation.
