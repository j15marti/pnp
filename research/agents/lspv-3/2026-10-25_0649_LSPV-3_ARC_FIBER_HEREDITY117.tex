\section{Exact source-name fiber saturation}
Let $B=\{0,1\}^{Y}$ be the boundary assignments to an ordered
tuple $Y$ of \emph{identical original source names}. Let $P(Y,U)$
and $Q(Y,V)$ be ordinary proper signed ternary CNF modules
with disjoint private-name sets $U,V$.
Write
\[
S_P=\{z\in B:\exists u\,P(z,u)\},\qquad
S_Q=\{z\in B:\exists v\,Q(z,v)\}.
\]
Exact witness gluing is $\SAT(P\wedge Q)$ iff
$S_P\cap S_Q\ne\varnothing$. For a fixed map $h:B\to H$,
a coarse join would instead accept when
$h(S_P)\cap h(S_Q)\ne\varnothing$.

\begin{theorem}[Universal-suffix quotient criterion]\label{th:fiber}
For every fixed ordinary proper-3CNF prefix $P$ and map $h$,
the coarse join is correct for \emph{both SAT and UNSAT labels
under every compatible ordinary proper-3CNF suffix $Q$}
if and only if $S_P$ is a union of complete $h$-fibers:
\[
                  h^{-1}(h(S_P))=S_P.                 \tag{1}
\]
No promise of small $|Y|$ or free semantic labels is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If (1) holds and $h(p)=h(q)$ with $p\in S_P$, $q\in S_Q$,
then $q\in h^{-1}(h(S_P))=S_P$, so the original named join
is also SAT. An exact join always implies a coarse join.

Conversely, if (1) fails, choose $p\in S_P$, $q\notin S_P$
with $h(p)=h(q)$. Give $Q$ exactly the singleton support $\{q\}$:
pin each common original name to its bit of $q$. Every pin literal
$\ell$ is made an ordinary proper ternary CNF using two fresh,
private distinct names $a,b$:
\[
\mathrm{PIN}(\ell)=
\bigwedge_{\epsilon,\delta\in\{-1,1\}}
 (\ell\vee\epsilon a\vee\delta b).                   \tag{2}
\]
The fresh names are disjoint from $U$, and the formula is satisfiable
exactly when $\ell$ is true. The true join is UNSAT because
$q\notin S_P$, while the coarse join is SAT because $h(q)=h(p)$.
This contradicts universal correctness.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[One-bit semantic minimum]\label{cor:one}
For every fixed support $S_P$, the map
$h_P(z)=\mathbf 1[z\in S_P]$ is universally suffix-correct.
An interface with only one class (zero bits) is universally correct
iff $S_P=\varnothing$ or $S_P=B$. A proper nonempty support
requires at least two classes, hence one bit.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$S_P$ is an exact union of the $h_P$-fibers.
For a constant map, (1) holds precisely for empty/full $S_P$;
otherwise its fiber merges feasible and infeasible rows.
\end{proof}
This is a new \emph{extensional} generalization of
Role-3 SEMANTIC-CYLINDER116 from deleting one Boolean source name
to \emph{arbitrary quotient fibers}. A semantically optimal
one-bit code is \emph{not} thereby a cheaply computable code,
a reusable ordinary child, or an all-input contractor.

\begin{corollary}[Recognition complexity from earlier CYLINDER116]
If $h$ is represented by a polynomial-time evaluable circuit,
recognizing (1) for proper-3CNF $P$ is $\Pi_2^P$-complete,
already for the special projection $h(x,y)=y$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Its negation is
$\exists p,q,u\,\forall u':
[h(p)=h(q)\wedge P(p,u)\wedge\neg P(q,u')]$,
a $\Sigma_2^P$ predicate. For hardness choose the one-coordinate
projection: condition (1) is precisely the existential
prefix-cylinder recognition theorem proved in
LSPV-3 SEMANTIC-CYLINDER116, which reduced quantified
proper 3SAT to this problem with an explicit two-clause gadget.
This is a \emph{derived corollary}, not an independently
new complexity-hardness theorem. Under $P=NP$ the polynomial
hierarchy collapses, so no SAT runtime lower bound follows.
\end{proof}

\section{SAT-hidden semantic bit and charged fixed-solver ledger}
Let $F=\bigwedge_{C=(a\vee b\vee c)}C$ be any proper ternary
SAT instance. Allocate a new boundary bit $z$ and
a fresh helper $g_C$ for each original clause:
\[
G_F=\bigwedge_{C=(a\vee b\vee c)}
  [(\neg z\vee a\vee g_C)\wedge(\neg g_C\vee b\vee c)].\tag{3}
\]
Every clause has three distinct signed names. Existentially
projecting the helpers gives
\[
 [\exists X,g\,G_F(0,X,g)]=1,\qquad
 [\exists X,g\,G_F(1,X,g)]=\SAT(F).                 \tag{4}
\]
This is a uniform polynomial-ordinary-encoding and linear-clause-count
transformation, and the unpinned $G_F$ is always easy SAT.
Nevertheless the bit $h_{G_F}(1)$ is exactly the \emph{arbitrary
original SAT decision}. The two-clause gate itself was already
proved in SEMANTIC-CYLINDER116; it is reused, not rebranded.

For one fixed uniform total exact SAT machine $A$ of \emph{joint}
profile $(t,s)$, the literal deterministic certificate
constructs a complete ordinary pinned prefix and calls
that SAME $A$. Its guest length is $N^{1+o(1)}$, not
$N^{\lambda}$ with $\lambda<1$.
For $N^{b+o(1)}$ boundary invocations,
per-emitted-bit charge $N^{g+o(1)}$, other work exponent $r$,
generator/index state exponent $\eta$ and concurrent
parent/ancestor state exponent $\kappa$, the conservative
eager \emph{implementation upper certificate} is
\[
t'\le\max\{r,b+1+g,b+t\},\quad
s'\le\max\{1,s,\eta,\kappa\};                       \tag{5}
\]
literal fully virtual per-request regeneration gives
\[
t'\le\max\{r,b+t+g\},\quad
s'\le\max\{s,\eta,\kappa\}.                         \tag{6}
\]
The $b+t$ contribution is \emph{not a universal lower bound
against different solvers}; a cheap specialized predictor
could replace this exact literal implementation.
More generally $N^b$ calls at guest length $N^\rho$
contribute $N^{b+\rho t}$ with paid input construction and
replay, not a free SAT predicate.
A parent-scale lookahead $A(F)$ or off-promise fallback
does not certify favorable exponent contraction.

\section{Two actual ordinary levels; recertification is necessary}
Let original named variables be $x=1$, $y=2$, and
private witness $u=3$. A proper ternary realization of
$x\leftrightarrow u$, with fresh $p,q$, is
\[
(\neg x\vee u\vee p)\wedge(\neg x\vee u\vee\neg p)
\wedge(x\vee\neg u\vee q)\wedge(x\vee\neg u\vee\neg q).
\]
Define
\[
P(x,y,u)=\mathrm{EQ}(x,u)\wedge\mathrm{PIN}(\neg y),
\quad M(x)=\mathrm{PIN}(\neg x),\quad
Q_+(x)=\mathrm{PIN}(x),\quad
Q_-(x)=\mathrm{PIN}(\neg x).
\]
The existential prefix support of $P$ is exactly
$R_P(x,y)=[y=0]$, independent of $x$.
Thus the \emph{first} universal-suffix source-name erasure is
certified: rename $x$ inside $P$ only to fresh $x_L$,
leaving $M$ and $Q$ on common original $x$:
\[
F_0=P(x,y,u)\wedge M(x)\wedge Q_\pm(x),\quad
F_1=P(x_L,y,u)\wedge M(x)\wedge Q_\pm(x).          \tag{7}
\]
The new, actual prefix $P(x_L,y,u)\wedge M(x)$ has
projected support $[y=0]\wedge[x=0]$ and is \emph{not}
an $x$-cylinder. An incorrect reuse of the OLD certificate
to erase $x$ from $M$, by renaming to distinct private $x_M$,
produces a second fully ordinary proper ternary formula
\[
F_2=P(x_L,y,u)\wedge M(x_M)\wedge Q_\pm(x).       \tag{8}
\]
But for $Q_+$, the fully computed labels are
\[
(\SAT(F_0),\SAT(F_1),\SAT(F_2))=(0,0,1).
\]
For $Q_-$ they are $(1,1,1)$.
The first edge preserves both labels under \emph{every} suffix;
the second is \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED as a legal edge},
not an example of valid recursion. The exactly serialized
parent/child/grandchild lengths are $307/307/307$ bits
and $16/16/16$ proper clauses on both controls.
Original source $x$ remains identifiable in suffix $Q$;
$x_L=12$, $x_M=13$ are fresh, and compatibility is
rechecked at the second prefix. The previously valid
certificate is \emph{not hereditary} after prefix mutation.
Fresh recertification would reject the second erasure,
and its complete SAT/UNSAT acquisition queries are charged.
This counterexample refutes a particular unsafe two-level
strategy, \emph{not} every possible recertifying $A$-contractor.
