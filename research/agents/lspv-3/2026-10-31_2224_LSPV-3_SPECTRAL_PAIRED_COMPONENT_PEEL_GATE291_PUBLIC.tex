\paragraph{Exact representation.} Let $p_F(T),q_F(T)\in\{0,1\}$ mark positive and negative original clauses on an unordered distinct triple $T$. Write $z_i=(-1)^{x_i}$ and $V_F(x)$ for the number of falsified original clauses. With $m=4n/3$, exact signed quotas cancel all linear Fourier coefficients, leaving
\[
8V_F(z)=m+\sum_{i<j}c_F(i,j)z_iz_j+
\sum_{|T|=3}d_F(T)\prod_{i\in T}z_i,
\]
where $c_F(i,j)=\sum_{T\supset\{i,j\}}(p_F(T)+q_F(T))$ and $d_F(T)=p_F(T)-q_F(T)$.

\begin{theorem}[Full-profile collision confinement]
For valid aligned canonical $F,G$ with $V_F(x)=V_G(x)$ for \emph{every} assignment, every changed signed original clause lies in a whole incidence component of $F$ composed exclusively of paired positive/negative triples. In particular, a connected $F$ with an unpaired clause is uniquely determined by its complete Walsh fingerprint $(c_F,d_F)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Walsh orthogonality gives equality of every $c,d$ coefficient. If $d_F(T)=\pm1$, the signed occurrence is unique and unchanged. For $d_F(T)=0$, the only options are the paired set $(p,q)=(1,1)$ or no clause $(0,0)$. Every changed triple is thus a paired add/remove. Write $e(T)=p_G(T)-p_F(T)=q_G(T)-q_F(T)$. For each pair $\{i,j\}$, equality of $c$ gives $\sum_{T\supset\{i,j\}}e(T)=0$. Consider a removed $T=\{u,v,w\}$. The pairs $uv$ and $uw$ each demand a different added triple, since the only triple containing both is $T$, which was removed. Therefore $u$ appears in at least two added positive triples. Exact two-positive quota entails precisely two removed positive triples and no unchanged positive clause at $u$. Because all changes are paired, its two negative clauses are also removed. The same reasoning holds for each vertex in a removed triple and propagates across original incidence adjacency. Hence changes are full paired-only components, not partial modifications of a component containing any unpaired clause.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Constructive sparse peeling]
Given exact sparse $(c_F,d_F)$ for a \emph{validated} canonical source, one can recover every original signed clause in every component containing an unpaired original clause. A hash-dictionary RAM implementation requires expected $O(n+m)$ word operations and $O(n+m)$ words; deterministic ordered dictionaries use $O((n+m)\log(n+m))$ operations. These are not native FLMV machine bounds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Recover every $d_F(T)=+1$ triple as a positive-only clause and $d_F(T)=-1$ as negative-only. Subtract each recovered unpaired clause's three pair incidences from $c$. All remaining pair counts are even; divide by two to obtain the pair-incidence multiset $R$ of the unknown paired triples. If $a_v$ is the number of recovered positive-only clauses containing $v$, let $h_v=2-a_v$; canonical signed balance ensures the same number of negative-only clauses and $h_v\in\{0,1,2\}$. For every $h_v=1$, exactly two nonzero $R$ neighbors identify its sole paired triple. Remove that triple, subtract its three pairs, decrement its three residual degrees, and enqueue newly degree-one vertices. This is correct by induction. Any paired clause connected to a recovered unpaired clause is eventually reached: a boundary vertex incident on one unpaired and one paired clause has residual degree one, and the same reasoning propagates. Unrecovered residual degree-two vertices form entire paired-only components, detached from the recovered components. Each triple is removed at most once, and all source-incidence bookkeeping is linear in the sparse input size.
\end{proof}
