\section{Explicit proper-3CNF source pairs}
A unit $a=b\in\{0,1\}$ uses fresh $z,w$ and the four distinct proper ternaries $(\ell\vee \pm z\vee\pm w)$ for all sign combinations, with $\ell=a$ for $b=1$ and $\ell=\neg a$ for $b=0$. These four clauses exactly enforce $\ell$, without precomputing the SAT answer.

\paragraph{Equality family.}
For each $i=1,\ldots,h$, introduce $u_i,v_i,z_i,w_i$ and the four public clauses
\[
(\neg u_i\vee v_i\vee z_i),\quad
(\neg u_i\vee v_i\vee\neg z_i),\quad
(u_i\vee\neg v_i\vee w_i),\quad
(u_i\vee\neg v_i\vee\neg w_i).
\]
They enforce $u_i=v_i$. Add a public guard $g$ pinned TRUE by the four-clause unit gadget (two new auxiliaries), and $(g\vee u_i\vee u_{i+1})$ for $1\le i<h$, connecting the incidence graph without affecting satisfiability. Pin all $u_i=x_i$ in the $X$ block, followed by all $v_i=y_i$ in the $Y$ block. The resulting connected proper CNF $G^=_{h,x,y}$ has
\[
 n_{\rm eq}=8h+3,\qquad m_{\rm eq}=13h+3,\qquad
 \SAT(G^=_{h,x,y})=[x=y].
\tag{2}
\]
The unsigned source supports and serialized length do not depend on either orientation string.

\paragraph{Parity family.}
Introduce data $u_i,v_i$, running XOR variables $r_0,\ldots,r_{2h}$, public unit pins $r_0=r_{2h}=0$, and for $j=1,\ldots,2h$ the four three-literal clauses excluding the wrong truth-table rows of $r_j=r_{j-1}\oplus d_j$, with $(d_1,\ldots,d_{2h})=(u_1,\ldots,u_h,v_1,\ldots,v_h)$. Append the same $X$ then $Y$ pin groups. The connected proper formula has
\[
 n_{\oplus}=8h+5,\qquad m_{\oplus}=16h+8,\qquad
 \SAT(G^\oplus_{h,x,y})=
 [\bigoplus_i x_i=\bigoplus_i y_i].
\tag{3}
\]
Both families have $N=\Theta(h\log(h+2))$, matched both-label sign data and independently checkable local-gadget truth tables.

\section{Contextual quotient and one-way source work}
Let $P_x$ contain the public preamble and all signed $X$ pins. Let $C_y$ be the allowed $Y$ continuation, including its exact source bits. Define independently of runtime
\[
 P_x\equiv_{\mathcal C}P_{x'}\ \iff\
 \forall y\in\{0,1\}^h:
 \SAT(P_xC_y)=\SAT(P_{x'}C_y).
\tag{4}
\]
This is not the two-class semantic quotient of a \emph{fully given} root formula. The future suffix is not yet available to a one-way reader. The quotient is an analysis-only structural parameter, not a cost-free algorithmic table.

\begin{theorem}[Exact root-SAT continuation quotient; PROVED/EXACT restricted]
The equality-prefix quotient has $Q_{\rm eq}=2^h$ classes; the parity-prefix quotient has $Q_\oplus=2$. Any deterministic one-way exact source-stream SAT decider on every $G^=_{h,x,y}$ must have at least $2^h$ distinguishable complete states after the $X$ group, thus at least $h-O(1)$ live writable bits on some input. The parity family has a deterministic exact stream decider retaining one parity bit plus $O(\log(h+2))$ control bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $x\ne x'$, choose common continuation $y=x$. Equality is SAT for $x$ and UNSAT for $x'$, so their states at the cut cannot be identical. There are $2^h$ prefixes. In the parity family the response row depends exactly on $\oplus_i x_i$, giving two distinct rows. Accumulating parity across both signed blocks is a uniform exact procedure, with no label oracle and charged source signs.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Same-execution SAT-only bit-time; PROVED/EXACT restricted]
For each accepting diagonal formula $G^=_{h,u,u}$, let $B_j(u)$ be the number of complete live mutable bits after the whole $X$ block and the first $j$ $Y$ pins, $0\le j<h$. With $u$ uniform,
\[
\mathbb E_u\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}B_j(u)\ \ge\ \frac{(h-1)(h-2)}{2}.
\tag{5}
\]
Hence some SAT source yields $\Omega(h^2)$ cumulative bit-time and $(U+1)S\ge(h-1)(h-2)/2$ for the same run, where $U$ charges elementary bit operations and $S$ is its peak writable-bit count.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix the first $j$ bits $p$ of the $Y$ group. For every remaining suffix $q\in\{0,1\}^{h-j}$ consider the accepting source $x=pq$, $y=pq$. Two different $q$ cannot give the same live state after seeing the common $Y$ prefix $p$: appending $Y$ suffix $q$ would then incorrectly accept the mismatched $x=pq'$. Thus $2^{h-j}$ states are distinguishable for this conditional distribution. Even variable-length binary state encodings have average length at least $\max(0,h-j-2)$. Sum over $j$. The bit-time of an execution includes these checkpoints, and $BT\le(U+1)S$. This proof uses only the final SAT bit, not witness verification or UNSAT proof output.
\end{proof}

\section{The exact bounded-pass tradeoff}
A declared $p$-pass sequential machine scans the whole public/$X$/$Y$ stream from beginning to end on exactly $p$ passes; every rewind is charged, no indexed source reads or hidden side memories are available. Let $S$ be its maximum complete writable-state bits.

\begin{theorem}[Full-pass deterministic equality barrier; PROVED/EXACT restricted]
For every exact such decider and each $p\ge1$,
\[
 (2p-1)(S+O(1))\ge h.
\tag{6}
\]
There is a uniform exact $p$-pass algorithm with $O(\lceil h/p\rceil+\log(h+2))$ writable bits and $O(pN\operatorname{polylog}N)$ primitive bit work. Under the full-traversal contract, its worst-case time and space bounds obey $TS=\Omega(Nh)$ up to finite-controller conventions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Treat the public preamble as shared. Alice holds the $X$ source-sign bits; Bob holds $Y$. On each full pass Alice transmits the complete mutable configuration when the head moves from the $X$ block to the $Y$ block, and Bob returns it to Alice before the next full pass. There are $2p-1$ transmissions, each using $S+O(1)$ bits. This is a deterministic two-party protocol for equality. Its $2^h$ accepting pairs $(x,x)$ form a fooling set: distinct accepting diagonal pairs cannot share a transcript, since then a crossed pair $(x,x')$ would be erroneously accepted. Therefore the total communication is at least $h$ bits. For the upper bound, partition indices into $p$ groups, retain only the current group of $X$ bits and compare with the matching $Y$ bits on the same full pass. Reject any mismatch, otherwise accept after all groups. Each pass traverses $N$ source bits, proving the charged product statement. Partial scans, free rewinds, and random-addressed inputs require a different proof.
\end{proof}

\section{Adaptive representation escape and ARC accounting}
A source-aware indexed random-access algorithm uses $O(\log N)$ live writable bits and $N\operatorname{polylog}N$ bit work on either recognized family: after paid grammar validation, reread the signed $X_i$ and $Y_i$ fields in pairs and compare them, or use a Gaussian parity accumulator in the second family. It may use SCC/2-CNF quotient recognition on the equality public constraints. The full-language machine can retain exact correctness by invoking an arbitrary total exact fallback when recognition fails, but its worst-case resource bound is then the fallback's. Source-reordering, cache reuse and child-bit regeneration are paid, not prohibited.

 (U,R_{\rm source},R_{\rm workspace},W_{\rm workspace},
 \Delta_{\rm workspace},\Delta_{\rm total},
 S_{\rm peak},BT,|{\cal P}|).
\tag{7}
\]
Here $U$ counts primitive operations, including unchanged writes, source and work reads, parser/head/address control, failed choices and switching; $\Delta$ counts actual state-bit changes rather than writes. A compact representation's existence is separate from charged acquisition, membership, reconstruction and query processing. \paragraph{ARC scale calibration.}
The one-way source floor for an ordinary child of bit length $L=N^{1/2+o(1)}$ is only $h=\Theta(L/\log L)$ writable bits, which still fits the intended $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ parent-workspace allowance. Different children can reuse this memory sequentially; their widths may not be multiplied without a proof. The protected model is also sequential input, whereas FLMV permits indexed random access. Therefore neither this source-stream bound nor its bit-time consequence establishes a favorable all-input ARC successor or an unrestricted deterministic SAT-time lower bound.
