\section{Uniform source-implicit separator theorem}

Define the undirected primal graph $G(F)$ by sharing a source clause. For a pivot $x$, let $V_1,\ldots,V_r$ be the connected components of $G(F)-x$. A clause not confined to $x$ has all its other variables in one component; any remaining boundary-only clauses constitute $B_x$. Let $F_{i,b}$ be source clauses assigned to component $i$ simplified after $x=b$.

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The adjacency predicate $E_F(u,v)$ is computed by scanning source clauses using logarithmic counters. Delete $x$ by defining it as an isolated vertex. Reingold's deterministic undirected $s$-$t$ connectivity procedure runs in logarithmic space over this \emph{virtual} adjacency representation; logspace closure under logspace composition gives a source-implicit connectivity predicate with polynomial running time. For each $r\ne x$, it is a component representative exactly when no smaller $u\ne x$ connects to $r$. Enumerate representatives with nested logarithmic counters, repeated connectivity queries, and no stored graph. Every source clause with a non-$x$ literal belongs to the component of its first such variable, because all variables from one clause are adjacent. To produce cofactor $F_{i,b}$, stream original clauses in source order, omit those satisfied by $x=b$, delete falsified $x$ occurrences, and retain only clauses assigned to $i$. A preliminary source scan counts clause lengths and output bits; another scan locates the requested bit with an $O(\log N)$ position counter.
