\begin{abstract}
The verified TESTS-0016 report proves an exact $m-1$ locator depth for a
one-survivor proper-$3$CNF family in a declared no-preprocessing,
arbitrary-block-order record interface.  This run preserves that theorem and
attacks its possible promotion to a representation-invariant source-pass or
SAT cost.  A mixed YES/NO family with the same one-live-block geometry has
three encodings that differ only by a clause permutation.  Block order has
exact locator depth $m-1$; sign-grouped order places the live block at a known
suffix and needs no locator and one clause-record probe for the answer.  A
single linear scan converts the former to the latter while buffering four
clauses.  The semantic quotient $(t,b)\mapsto b$ has image two and collision
count $2m^2$, so a one-bit decider need not retain the source location.
Therefore a physical locator floor is not a representation-invariant
all-algorithm obligation.  The missing arrow remains a source-matched,
order-robust BIND/transfer theorem on a fixed full-language encoding.
P versus NP remains \OPEN.
\end{abstract}

\section{The mixed-answer proper-$3$CNF family}

Let $x,p,q$ be shared variables and include
\begin{equation}
 (\neg x\vee p\vee q)\wedge(\neg x\vee p\vee\neg q)\wedge
 (\neg x\vee\neg p\vee q)\wedge(\neg x\vee\neg p\vee\neg q).
                                                               \tag{1}
\end{equation}
The conjunction (1) is equivalent to $\neg x$.  For each
$i\in[m]$, use fresh $a_i,b_i,c_i$.  The residual YES and NO gadgets are
\begin{align}
Y_i&=(a_i\vee b_i)\wedge(a_i\vee\neg b_i)\wedge
     (\neg a_i\vee b_i)\wedge(a_i\vee c_i),                    \tag{2}\\
U_i&=(a_i\vee b_i)\wedge(a_i\vee\neg b_i)\wedge
     (\neg a_i\vee b_i)\wedge(\neg a_i\vee\neg b_i).          \tag{3}
\end{align}
$Y_i$ is satisfiable and $U_i$ is not.  Given source
$(t,\beta)\in[m]\times\{0,1\}$, gate every inactive block by $\neg x$ and
the unique active block by $x$: each pair $(u\vee v)$ becomes
$(\neg x\vee u\vee v)$ when $i\ne t$, while (2) or (3) becomes
$(x\vee u\vee v)$ at $i=t$ according as $\beta=1$ or $0$.
Call the resulting conjunction $F_{m,t,\beta}$.

All clauses have three distinct literals, there are $3+3m$ variables and
$4+4m$ duplicate-free clauses, and the ordinary indexed encoding length is
\begin{equation}
 N=\Theta(m\log m).                                             \tag{4}
\end{equation}
Because (1) fixes $x=0$, every inactive clause is satisfied and the active
block reduces exactly to (2) or (3).  Hence
\begin{equation}
 F_{m,t,\beta}\in\SAT\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\beta=1.      \tag{5}
\end{equation}
This is deliberately an easy residual family; it is a hostile control for a
universal locator inference, not a claim of SAT hardness.

\section{Exact order-collapse theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{CLAUSE-ORDER-LOCALITY-COLLAPSE1} --- \EXACT]
Fix the family above and a clause-record interface that exposes the sign of
the $x$-literal in a queried record.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em]
\item In block order, with no preprocessing index, exact worst-case locator
depth is $m-1$ block probes.
\item Permuting exactly the same clauses so that all $\neg x$-gated blocks
precede the unique $x$-gated block gives locator depth zero.  One probe to the
last clause record distinguishes (2) from (3), hence decides (5).
\item The block-order encoding can be converted to the grouped encoding in
$O(N)$ bit time, $O(\log N)$ work space, and one streaming scan, by emitting
inactive blocks and buffering the four-clause active block until the end.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, no representation-invariant lower bound for arbitrary SAT
deciders follows from the physical locator depth alone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In block order a probe either finds the unique positive $x$ gate or rules out
one candidate.  An adversary answers ``inactive'' while two candidates
remain.  Thus $m-1$ probes are necessary, and sequential inspection attains
the bound.  In grouped order the active block is, by definition, the final
four records.  The last residual clause is $(a_t\vee c_t)$ in (2) and
$(\neg a_t\vee\neg b_t)$ in (3), after adjoining the same positive $x$ gate;
one record distinguishes them.  Both layouts contain the identical multiset
of clauses and have identical length.  The streaming transducer reads each
four-clause block, emits it immediately if its gate is negative, otherwise
retains those four clauses and emits them after the scan.  Four literals per
clause and variable identifiers of $O(\log m)$ bits give the stated space.
Equation (5) proves semantic preservation.  Therefore the locator charge is
a property of the declared physical layout, not of the Boolean function or
clause multiset. \qed
\end{proof}

The scan has local ledger exponents $r=1$ and $p=0$ (polylogarithmic space)
in (4).  Thus even the adversarial layout supplies a charged linear-time
upper bound.  Counting $m-1=\Theta(N/\log N)$ possible block locations does
not yield a superlinear, superpolynomial, or arbitrary-algorithm lower bound.

\section{One-bit quotient, wrappers, and SAME\_REALIZATION}

Let $L(t,\beta)=\beta$.  Its domain has size $2m$, each of its two fibers has
size $m$, and
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Coll}(L)=m^2+m^2=2m^2,
 \qquad
 |\operatorname{im}L|\ge\frac{(2m)^2}{2m^2}=2.                 \tag{6}
\end{equation}
The Cauchy--Schwarz bound is exact.  Location $t$ is pure collision entropy
for decision.  DIRECT, COPY, DEAD, DELAY, INTERP, and RECOMP one-bit wrappers
all preserve $L$ while erasing $t$.  The suffix decoder is uniform: it parses
the length and addresses the final record; no nonuniform advice is used.

For a total full-language algorithm, a recognize-first wrapper may scan the
entire input, use the suffix rule only on this island, and otherwise call an
assumed polynomial SAT algorithm.  That wrapper is not a proof that SAT is in
P; under the counterfactual assumption it merely preserves polynomiality and
exhibits why an island-specific access cost cannot constrain the same
realization on arbitrary inputs.  Conversely, charging the streaming
normalizer to one machine preserves SAME\_REALIZATION and still costs only
linear time and logarithmic work space.

The smallest viable repair is not ``choose block order.''  It is an \OPEN
source-matched theorem on one fixed full-language encoding:
\begin{quote}
Every favorable hereditary source pass produced from an arbitrary SAT
decider must either retain an order-covariant source certificate or pay a
model-matched cost that survives clause permutations, semantic quotienting,
and equivalent one-bit wrappers; the certificate must be required by an
exact BIND/reconstruction theorem in the same realization.
\end{quote}
No current report proves this statement.  Without it, compatibility
extraction, state exposure, memory, time, and communication are not universal
obligations.

\subsection*{Hostile controls}
The active residual is 2SAT and is solved directly; Horn, affine/Tseitin
Gaussian elimination, and bounded-treewidth dynamic programming remain
semantic-shortcut controls.  Independent products multiply records but keep
the componentwise reorder and quotient.  Padding with inactive or redundant
blocks increases physical search while leaving (5) unchanged.  Compact
circuits and branching programs can compute $L$ without materializing $t$.
Alternate clause order, variable renaming, and the six wrappers therefore
attack exactly the attempted universal promotion.  None is extrapolated to a
general SAT algorithm.

\section{Barrier and transfer audit}

Golynski--Orlandi--Raman--Rao and Raman--Raman--Rao give primary theorems for
rank/select indexes over read-only or succinct bit vectors
\cite{GORR11,RRR07}.  They are directly applicable to their own bit-vector
models, and structurally reinforce the point that access cost depends on the
representation and its index.  Importing their tradeoffs as SAT lower bounds
would require a new reduction preserving preprocessing, word size, probes,
and the full-language quantifiers; that transfer is \OPEN.

Schur squares, quadratic-relation ranks, Hilbert functions, syzygies, and
special-soundness reconstruction are \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} here: no code or
opening object is forced by a one-bit SAT decider.  The collision calculation
is an \textbf{EXACT OBJECT/THEOREM TRANSFER}.  Worst-case/average-case
architecture is an \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE needing a new bridge}.  Hashes,
random oracles, and cryptographic binding remain inadmissible assumptions.

The argument and its wrapper survive oracle access, so they provide no
nonrelativizing hinge; Baker--Gill--Solovay remains a barrier
\cite{BGS75}.  No low-degree extension is attacked, so algebraization is
unaudited rather than overcome \cite{AW09}.  No constructive property against
large circuits is supplied, so Natural Proofs is neither invoked nor evaded
\cite{RR97}.  The result is uniform and representation-level; allowing
nonuniform advice cannot repair the universal claim.
