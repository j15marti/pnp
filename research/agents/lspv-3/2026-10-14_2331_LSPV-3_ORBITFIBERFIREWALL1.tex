\section{CONSTRUCTION OR PROOF ATTEMPT}
For $m\ge1$, let $G_m$ have a base triangle with vertices $b_0,b_1,b_2$ and $m-1$ other vertices in each of three classes. Each class-$i$ vertex is adjacent precisely to the two base vertices of classes other than $i$. Hence $t=3m$, $|E|=6m-3$, and exactly six ordinary proper 3-colorings exist, indexed by a permutation $\sigma\in S_3$.

For each vertex independently choose a local permutation $\pi_v\in S_3$, and construct an explicit three-choice binary CSP $P_\pi$ whose domain at every vertex is $\{0,1,2\}$ and whose edge relation is
\[
R^\pi_{uv}=\{(a,b):\pi_u^{-1}(a)\ne\pi_v^{-1}(b)\}.
\]
The six satisfying assignments are $w_\sigma(v)=\pi_v(\sigma(i(v)))$. For every full exclusion mask $e\in\{0,1,2\}^t$ let $f_\pi(e)$ be the satisfiability indicator after deleting $e_v$ from each variable's domain. Such a supplied fiber is 2-SAT decidable with polynomial input access.

\begin{theorem}[Exact orbit density]
For each fixed $\pi$, exactly
\[
g_m=6\cdot8^m-9\cdot2^m-6
\]
of the $27^m$ exclusion masks are successful. For uniformly independent $\pi_v$ and \emph{every fixed} mask $e$,
\[
\Pr_\pi[f_\pi(e)=1]=p_m=\frac{6\cdot8^m-9\cdot2^m-6}{27^m}
=6(8/27)^m(1+o(1)).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each of the six global color permutations permits $2^{3m}=8^m$ excluded masks. Of the fifteen unordered pairs of distinct permutations, nine transposition pairs share $2^m$ masks and six 3-cycle pairs share one. Three distinct permutations cannot share any excluded mask at all: at some class their colors exhaust the palette. Inclusion--exclusion proves $g_m$. Coordinatewise maps $e_v\mapsto\pi_v^{-1}(e_v)$ are bijective. For uniformly independent permutations a fixed input mask maps to a uniform mask, proving the probability.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive fiber-only lower bound]
An adaptive deterministic algorithm that knows the family and $m$ but \emph{cannot read the explicit source or permutations}, and that has access only to full-fiber yes/no queries $f_\pi(e)$, requires at least
\[
Q\ge\lceil1/p_m\rceil=\Omega((3/2)^{3m})
\]
attempts in the worst case to output a successful mask for every $\pi$. A randomized algorithm making at most $Q$ attempts and succeeding with probability at least $2/3$ on every $\pi$ requires $Q\ge(2/3)/p_m$. Uniform random independent masks find a successful mask in geometric mean $1/p_m$ queries for each fixed $\pi$. Count an unqueried final proposed mask as an attempt.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the hypothetical all-NO transcript a deterministic algorithm generates a fixed sequence of at most $Q$ masks. To succeed on a particular $\pi$, some mask on this fixed sequence must be successful. Each has hit probability $p_m$ under random $\pi$; the union bound gives $\Pr_\pi(\mathrm{success})\le Qp_m$. For randomized algorithms condition on the coins, apply the same inequality, then average. The uniform-random upper bound is a geometric trial because every fixed $\pi$ has exactly $g_m$ good masks.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Partial-tag source-peek bound]
If the algorithm may additionally learn the entire $\pi_v$ for at most $k$ adaptively chosen vertices, interleaved with its $Q$ fiber attempts, then
\[
\Pr_{\pi,\mathrm{coins}}(\mathrm{success})
\le\min\{1,6Q(2/3)^{t-k}\}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Follow the virtual all-NO transcript while answering tag peeks truthfully. Every unpeeked local palette remains independently uniform conditional on revealed tags. A fixed attempted mask can avoid the labels of any one global witness permutation on unpeeked coordinates with conditional probability $(2/3)^{t-k'}$ for $k'\le k$. Union bound over six global permutations and $Q$ attempts. Every real success must occur somewhere on this virtual path prior to its first positive, avoiding the fallacy of conditioning on prior negative results.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Unrestricted transfer counterexample]
There is a deterministic polynomial-bit-time, source-aware algorithm finding a satisfying assignment to \emph{every} explicitly represented $P_\pi$, without making any full-fiber queries.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Try at most $3^3=27$ assignments to the three named base vertices. For each choice satisfying the three base constraints, each remaining vertex is constrained to one unique label by its two incident base-edge relation rows. Assign that label and finally verify the explicit source. Some base tuple always succeeds. There are only $6m-3=O(t)$ edges and each relation is of constant size; input parsing and indexing are polynomial bit operations.
\end{proof}

\noindent Hence the exponential query lower bound does not imply a single hard instance for arbitrary input-reading solvers. It is a precise counterexample to the proposed route from rare successful fibers to the universal witness insufficiency bound
\[
H(F)=\min_{|p|\le b_0+\lfloor\log_2(|F|+2)\rfloor}(t_p(F)+q(|F|)+1).
\]
It also does not prove a lower bound on the fixed total two-tree compatibility language $L_{\rm TT}$. The twisted binary relations need not be literal canonical PINCORE/BINARYPROJ images, and compiler/source visibility is not an unavoidable restricted oracle.
