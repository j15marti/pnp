\begin{theorem}[Unbounded, polynomially recognizable strict22 tree]
For every $k\ge1$, every tree $T$ on $k$ vertices of maximum degree at most four, and any choice of $G$ or $U$ at each vertex, allocate distinct occurrences of the local port $p$ to incident edges, with equal literal sign at both endpoints. Swap the two selected literal occurrences across each edge, preserving their signs. Use consecutive 15-variable blocks and twenty ordered source-clause positions as the \emph{canonical encoded grammar}. The resulting CNF has $15k$ variables and $20k$ proper ternary clauses, is connected and strict signed exact-$(2,2)$, and is deterministically decidable, with SAT witnesses and complete original-source authenticated negative tables, in polynomial time by \emph{one} uniform program. Its grammar and tree are reconstructible in polynomial time from the encoded CNF itself, without given answer class or decomposition.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each exchange preserves every signed occurrence count and every clause's number of literals. Each non-port variable belongs to just one source block; no new duplicate variable enters its clause. By checking the fixed source's non-port incidence support and following port links along the tree one verifies global connectedness. The recognizer tests each twenty-clause block against the two fixed source signatures, permitting only substituted same-sign port literals. It checks each source-clause position, reciprocal occurrence swaps, per-slot uniqueness, the tree and signed degrees. Thus decomposition recognition and all failed tests are polynomial in the entire explicit input length.

For a vertex $v$, let $P_v$ be its 15 variables and $N_T(v)$ its neighbors. The bag $V_v=P_v\cup\{p_u:u\in N_T(v)\}$ has at most 19 variables; its \emph{complete} shared interface $B_v=\{p_v\}\cup\{p_u:u\in N_T(v)\}$ has at most five. Adjacent bags intersect in $\{p_u,p_v\}$ and satisfy running intersection. Each source clause belongs to one bag. Enumerating all $2^{15}$ private candidate assignments and all $2^{|B_v|}\le32$ interface signatures gives the exact existential local table
\[
 R_v(p_v,(p_u)_{u\in N_T(v)})=
 \exists(P_v\setminus\{p_v\})\bigwedge_{C\in C_v} C.
\]
Rooting the tree, compute $2$-by-$2$ parent-edge messages by joining child tables with every local signature. A subtree-induction proves exactness. On SAT, store local witness pointers and reconstruct and verify all $20k$ clauses. On UNSAT, retain and independently replay all negative local rows and the empty root message; the verifier re-enumerates every fixed-source row, so no negative inference is accepted as an oracle answer. Input authentication, all failed candidates, joins, intermediate storage, output and certificate acquisition cost $O(kC_0)+\operatorname{poly}(N)$ for a fixed $C_0$, and the explicit encoding has $N=\Theta(k\log k)$.
\end{proof}

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

\section{Growing SAT and UNSAT controls}
Every all-$G$ tree is SAT by choosing the source witness with $p=0$ at every vertex. For every $k\ge3$, attach two $U$ leaves to a central $G$ using opposite-sign port slots, then attach other $G$ modules as a tree. It is UNSAT: since a standalone $U=R\land(\ell\lor q)$ is inconsistent, any assignment satisfying $R$ falsifies both $\ell$ and $q$. When the leaf's literal $\ell$ is replaced by the central signed port literal, its clause forces that new literal true. Two opposite-sign leaves therefore force $p_{\rm center}$ and $\neg p_{\rm center}$ simultaneously. Both families remain connected strict22 for unbounded $k$.
