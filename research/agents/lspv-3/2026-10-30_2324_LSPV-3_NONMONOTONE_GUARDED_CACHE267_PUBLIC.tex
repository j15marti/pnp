\section{Restricted support guard and both-label theorem}
Let $F=\bigwedge_{j=1}^m C_j$ be a fixed source CNF with immutable signed
original-clause IDs, and $D$ a coherent decision cube. For every decision
$d\in D$, assign transitive support $G(d)=\{d\}$. When original clause
$C_j=(\ell\vee\ell_1\vee\cdots\vee\ell_s)$ becomes unit and forces $\ell$
because all $\neg\ell_i$ are already assigned, assign
\[
G(\ell)=\bigcup_i G(\neg\ell_i),\qquad
P(\ell)=\{j\}\cup\bigcup_i P(\neg\ell_i).
\]
The $P$ set records authentic original-source proof dependencies.
For an empty source clause under UP, take the corresponding unions
$G(\bot),P(\bot)$ over all falsifying literals and that source clause.

\begin{theorem}[Nonmonotone guarded unit consequence]
For every such complete unit trace, source $F$ entails
\[
 F\models\left(\bigvee_{d\in G(\ell)}\neg d\right)\vee\ell
 \quad\text{and, on conflict,}\quad
 F\models\bigvee_{d\in G(\bot)}\neg d.
\]
Every displayed clause is RUP from the original source. Consequently
the learned consequence is valid under any later coherent decision cube
$E\supseteq G$, whether or not $E$ contains the old decision cube $D$.
Source-clause additions preserve it. Deletions preserve its \emph{given
proof} if every source clause in its recorded $P$ remains available; a
different proof may independently justify the consequence after deletion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct on the source-indexed unit derivation. Each decision holds when its
singleton guard is assumed. Each falsifying literal $\ell_i$ of a later
reason clause is true-negated whenever every assumption in
$G(\neg\ell_i)$ holds. Therefore any model of the source and all decisions
in $G(\ell)$ must satisfy the remaining literal $\ell$. The same
reasoning on an empty clause proves the contradiction guard. Unit
propagation with the negation of either derived clause replays the
source-indexed unit reasons and reaches conflict, proving RUP. Assumption
inclusion rather than chronological search ancestry is the exact reuse
criterion. Adding clauses preserves entailment. A deletion may destroy
a used premise; retaining all authentic premises preserves this proof.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact falsifiers and charged experiment}
Take $F=(\neg a\vee b)\wedge(\neg b\vee c)$ and a separate decision
variable $d$. Under decisions $(a,\neg d)$, original UP derives $c$
with minimal transitive support $\{a\}$, hence $F\models\neg a\vee c$.
The incomparable new branch $(a,d)$ may safely reuse it, while
$(\neg a,d)$ cannot: $a=b=c=0$ is an original-source model.

the claimed consequence, while adding source clauses preserves it.
