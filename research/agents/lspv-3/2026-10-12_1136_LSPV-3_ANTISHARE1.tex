\begin{abstract}
REPDIRSUM1 correctly retires a repeated-component direct-sum obligation, but
its proposed repair begins by excluding detectable repetition, isomorphism,
padding, and reusable redundancy.  The first three syntactic exclusions are
insufficient.  The corpus already contains a stronger counterfamily: connected
simple cubic-bipartite paired-incidence formulas are proper duplicate-free
Monotone 3SAT-(2,2), are polynomially recognizable, and are all satisfiable by
a perfect matching.  They need not contain repeated components.  This run
formalizes the consequence for the new anti-sharing repair and adds an exact
same-size nonisomorphism regression: five connected clean cores with identical
numbers of variables and clauses are pairwise nonisomorphic and have five
different model counts, yet the one-bit decision remains a recognizable YES
shortcut.  The mathematics is explicitly corpus-derived from LB-0008,
RT-0012, and TESTS-0012; it is not relabeled as a novel matching theorem.
P versus NP remains \OPEN.
\end{abstract}

\section{Primary-literature and barrier preflight}

The two obstacles are (i) syntactic diversity does not imply semantic
difficulty, and (ii) a pointwise easy island does not refute an all-decider
worst-case statement.  Döcker proves the full Monotone 3SAT-(2,2) language
NP-complete but also identifies the paired Not-All-Equal special case as
always satisfiable\footnote{J. Döcker, ``Monotone 3-Sat-(2,2) is
NP-complete,'' arXiv:1912.08032, 2019,
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08032}.}.  Thus global hardness of the syntax
cannot be inherited by every structural subclass.  Regular bipartite perfect
matching is algorithmic, not merely existential; Goel, Kapralov, and Khanna
place it within efficient matching algorithms and recall the general
Hopcroft--Karp bound\footnote{A. Goel, M. Kapralov, and S. Khanna, ``Perfect
Matchings in \(\widetilde O(n^{1.5})\) Time in Regular Bipartite Graphs,''
STOC 2010 / arXiv:0902.1617,
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.1617}.}.  Brinkmann, Goedgebeur, and McKay give
isomorph-free exhaustive generation for connected cubic graphs, including
restricted bipartite classes\footnote{G. Brinkmann, J. Goedgebeur, and
B. McKay, ``Generation of Cubic Graphs,'' DMTCS 13:2 (2011), 69--80.}.

These sources transfer exactly only to graph recognition, matching, and
isomorphism control.  They do not prove an unrestricted SAT lower bound.
VLSI routing, communication cuts, proof-system width, control observability,
and coding-theoretic rank remain \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} candidates unless a
same-machine compiler forces those resources on every SAT execution.

\section{Corpus-derived exact theorem}

For a finite simple cubic bipartite graph \(G=(L\sqcup R,E)\), place one
Boolean variable \(x_e\) on each edge and define
\[
 F(G)=\bigwedge_{v\in L\sqcup R}
 \left(\bigvee_{e\ni v}x_e\right)
 \wedge
 \left(\bigvee_{e\ni v}\neg x_e\right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[CUBIC-BIPARTITE-CLEAN-CORE-BYPASS1]
For every connected finite simple cubic bipartite graph \(G\), \(F(G)\) is a
connected, proper, duplicate-free Monotone 3SAT-(2,2) formula.  It is
satisfiable.  The class of such paired-incidence encodings is polynomially
recognizable, and a satisfying assignment is polynomially constructible.
For every total exact deterministic SAT decider \(A\), there is a total exact
wrapper \(B_A\) that performs the recognition, outputs one immediately when
the test succeeds, and otherwise invokes \(A\) on the original input.
Therefore, on the recognized class, \(B_A\) makes zero calls to \(A\), zero
compatibility-extractor calls, zero witness-reconstructor calls, and emits one
decision bit after polynomial preprocessing.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each vertex has three distinct incident edges, so each clause is proper and
has width three.  Every edge has two endpoints, hence its variable appears in
exactly two positive and two negative clauses.  Simplicity and connectedness
exclude equal vertex stars and make the incidence formula connected.  Thus
\(F(G)\in\Htwo\).

A regular bipartite graph has a perfect matching \(M\).  Assign \(x_e=1\) for
\(e\in M\) and zero otherwise.  Every vertex sees exactly one true and two
false incident variables, so both its positive and negative clauses are true.
The paired clauses and their incidence graph can be checked by sorting literal
triples and counting occurrences; bipartiteness, cubicity, connectedness, and
a matching are polynomially decidable/constructible.  Define \(B_A\) by this
recognized YES branch and exact fallback.  Both branches are correct, proving
the resource ledger.  This is the theorem already present in substance in
LB-0008/TESTS-0012, now applied to the anti-sharing repair. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[SYNTACTIC-ANTI-SHARING-SUFFICIENCY1]
No pointwise universal obligation follows merely from connectedness, absence
of duplicate clauses, exact (2,2) occurrence, absence of repeated connected
components, pairwise nonisomorphism, or equal encoded size.  Any proposed
anti-sharing premise accepting the family above needs an additional semantic
condition.  Defining that condition as ``has no polynomial shortcut'' would
assume the computational burden to be proved.
\end{corollary}

\section{Hostile controls and barriers}

The regression includes Horn, 2SAT, affine/Tseitin, and bounded-treewidth
one-bit controls under all six wrappers.  An independent product has two
components and is rejected by the one-component filter; redundant-clause
padding violates the clean-core check.  The counterfamily therefore does not
depend on either control.  Compact branching-program or circuit
representations make the shortcut easier, not harder: after recognition the
decision branch is constant one.  A maximal semantic oracle and an algebraic
oracle likewise preserve the negative conclusion.

The construction relativizes and algebrizes; it retires a route rather than
escaping either barrier.  Natural Proofs is not directly invoked because no
large constructive circuit property yields a lower bound.  Nonuniform advice
is unnecessary: recognition and matching are uniform.  If the fallback
decider has polynomial exponent \(K\), the wrapper retains exponent \(K\) off
the island and fixed polynomial preprocessing on it.  Thus no unknown exponent
is erased and no full-language polynomial SAT algorithm is obtained.
