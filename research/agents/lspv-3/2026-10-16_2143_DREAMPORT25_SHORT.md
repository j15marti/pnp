## Conditional transport theorem
For each fixed polynomial T, construct canonical polynomial SAT witness finder B_T: compile source u to one proper3CNF H=g(u) (witness-preserving and polynomial time), then test ALL <=b(|H|)=b0+floor(log2(|H|+2))-bit programs on the SAME final H within T(|H|), verify and project any valid witness. O(|H|) programs means B_T is polynomial. Under P!=NP, BTW Thm 1 creates infinitely often certified satisfiable u on which B_T fails. Then H=g(u) is satisfiable, and ALL eligible programs fail on this SAME H. This is CONDITIONAL ON P!=NP, not a proof of it.

## Unconditional replay-clock lemma
A deterministic polynomial-time generator D_T(1^n) yielding source/witness (u,a) and final H=g(u), |H|>=n, yields a fixed polynomial replay solver R_T: enumerate n<=|H|, regenerate D_T, recompile g, compare exact full final bytes and verify witness. If H defeats all programs within T, R_T necessarily uses >T(|H|) charged steps on that H for sufficiently large inputs, since its code is eventually eligible. Only R_T is constrained, not arbitrary SAT solvers.

## Exact proper3CNF marginal-failure counterexample
Encode all 2^n possible primary witnesses z by four 3-distinct-variable clauses per forced bit with two new auxiliaries, plus duplicated clauses increasing final size to 2^{n+O(1)}poly(n). There are 2^n eligible n+O(1)-bit constant-witness programs p_z; each succeeds on just 1/2^n equally long satisfiable formulas F_z, but their union succeeds on every F_z. Therefore high individual failure never by itself gives EXISTS F FORALL p.
