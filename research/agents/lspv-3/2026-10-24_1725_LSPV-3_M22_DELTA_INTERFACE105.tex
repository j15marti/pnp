\section{Original literature and an exact boundary closure theorem}
A nonempty family $M\subseteq\{0,1\}^B$ is a \emph{delta-matroid} when, for all $x,y\in M$ and each $e\in x\triangle y$, some $f\in x\triangle y$, \emph{possibly $f=e$}, satisfies
\[
x\triangle\{e,f\}\in M.
\]
When $e=f$ the convention is to toggle $e$ \emph{once}, not zero times. Kazda--Kolmogorov--Rol\'inek, Proposition 6 of \emph{Even Delta-Matroids and the Complexity of Planar Boolean CSPs} (arXiv:1602.03124v6, printed p.~4), give exact closure under composition of delta-matroid relations: agree on shared names, existentially eliminate the common names, and retain the others. Their main polynomial edge-CSP theorem concerns \emph{even} delta-matroids with appropriate explicit relation access, so it must not be invoked as a polynomial algorithm for arbitrary succinct non-even delta-matroids. The present paired NAE network has a \emph{different, already established} factor algorithm from LSPV-1 INTERVAL-FACTOR134 and LSPV-2 DEFECT-ADAPTER131/ONE-SIDED-FACTOR133.

\begin{lemma}[Local non-even delta relation]
$\NAE_3=\{0,1\}^3\setminus\{000,111\}$ is a delta-matroid, but not an even delta-matroid.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $x,y\in\NAE_3$ and $e\in x\triangle y$, flipping $e$ suffices unless it produces $000$ or $111$. In the first exceptional case $x$ is a singleton $\{e\}$ and a second differing coordinate occurring in the nonempty $y$ produces a feasible two-coordinate flip. In the second case $x$ has two ones and adding its sole missing coordinate produces $111$; since $y\ne x$ has at most two ones, some differing coordinate can be removed to restore a feasible weight-two word. Both parities occur among the six tuples.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Paired-core projected boundary, PROVED/EXACT restricted]
For every $F\in\CM$, let
\[
B_H=\left\{u\in\{0,1\}^{D}:
\exists z\in\{0,1\}^{V\setminus D}\ 
\bigwedge_{S\in H}\NAE_S(u,z)\right\}.
\]
Then $B_H$ is a nonempty delta-matroid on its \emph{original named} coordinates $D$. It has a source-readable NAE-network representation containing $|H|$ constant-arity relations and $O(N)$ bits of original indexed records. Membership can be decided with polynomial charged work and writable space using the \emph{preexisting signed degree-two interval-factor service}; the theorem does not materialize the full relation or give a free semantic oracle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The signed degree-two quotas imply $\deg_H(v)=2$ for $v\notin D$ and $\deg_H(v)\le1$ for $v\in D$. Thus every bound name occurs in exactly two NAE constraints and every output name in at most one. Take the product of the local six-tuple delta-matroid relations and identify/eliminate each internal name using the published composition closure. An output name of zero NAE degree is carried as an independent free unary relation $\{0,1\}$. No internal name is independently renamed or duplicated. Closure by finite composition and direct products proves the exchange axiom for the resulting nonempty relation.

Nonemptiness is separately checked: construct the loop-free multigraph with one degree-three vertex per NAE triple, one edge for each name appearing twice, and a new degree-one leaf for each name occurring once. Its maximum degree is three. By Shannon's multigraph edge-coloring bound, four edge colors suffice. Collapse any two colors to 0 and the remaining two to 1. At each degree-three constraint vertex, its three edge colors are distinct, so its three source bits cannot all coincide. This supplies a feasible NAE assignment, with arbitrary values on zero-degree names. Alternatively the already established paired-factor service supplies feasibility. A parser discovers signed supports, $H,D$, shared names, and the named factor instance in polynomial bit work; generated node and edge descriptions and repeated lookups are paid. The statement is an \emph{upper} representation and membership service, not an arbitrary SAT-machine lower bound.
\end{proof}

The result separates four often conflated objects: NAE sign-cycle information, affine/XOR equation rank, projected feasible-boundary affine dimension, and the exact nonaffine symmetric-exchange property. No one scalar is substituted for the other three. The theorem is not a claim that the joined full source is a delta-matroid.

\begin{proof}

\[

\[
X\triangle\{a\}=\{c\},\quad
X\triangle\{a,c\}=\varnothing,\quad
X\triangle\{a,u\}=\{c,u\},\quad
X\triangle\{a,v\}=\{c,v\}.
\]
