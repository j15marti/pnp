\textbf{Theorem 610.1 (K4 prefix family and exact model spectrum).}
Fix graph edges $(01),(02),(03),(12),(13),(23)$ and charges
$(\mathrm{true},0,0,0)$, where the last three zeros are numeric. Let the last three vertices use the uniquely permitted four \emph{odd} forbidden patterns, and let $S$ be an arbitrary four-element forbidden-pattern set at vertex zero. Every one of the 70 resulting distinct 16-clause signed SOURCE records passes all original /341 \emph{source-prefix} conditions. If $k=|\{s\in S:p(s)=0\}|$, the number of satisfying assignments of the resulting six-variable signed CNF equals
\[
N_{\rm SAT}(S)=2(4-k).
\]
The spectrum by $k=0,1,2,3,4$ has respectively $(1,16,36,16,1)$ clause-set instances, carrying $(8,6,4,2,0)$ source models each. Therefore exactly \textbf{69 of 70} SOURCE-passing variants are SAT. Replacing only the Boolean charge \texttt{true} by numeric 1 leaves the global XOR unchanged but makes only the unique $k=4$ forbidden family SOURCE-admissible; that family is UNSAT.

\textbf{Proof.} The strict comparison and source uniqueness checks give the 70 choices. At vertices 1,2,3 each local four-clause block enforces even incident-edge parity. For K4 these three independent parity constraints force even parity at vertex 0, because every edge occurs at two vertices. Their solution space has dimension $6-3=3$, hence eight assignments. The four possible even patterns at vertex 0 each have precisely two extensions: the linear map from that three-dimensional space to the two-dimensional even-parity vertex-0 pattern space is surjective with one-dimensional kernel. The block $S$ excludes exactly $k$ of those four even patterns, so $2(4-k)$ models remain. The number of sets with exactly $k$ even patterns is $\binom4k\binom4{4-k}$, giving the stated multiplicities. For numeric charge 1, only even forbidden patterns are permitted and all four must occur, giving $k=4$ and zero models. \hfill$\square$
