\section{One target and exact scope correction}
The target language $L_{M22}$ consists of fixed-encoding strings for duplicate-free proper clause-monotone 3CNFs $F$ with dense variables $1,\dots,n$: every clause has three distinct variables and all literals of the same sign; each variable appears positively exactly twice and negatively exactly twice. Invalid encodings are rejected. Thus $n\equiv0\pmod3$, $m=4n/3$, $m_+=m_-=2n/3$. This is the \emph{complete} language established NP-complete by D\"ocker \cite{docker}, not the easy all-positive promise nor the larger signed exact-$(2,2)$ language. The reviewed 2026-10-31 11:00 user scope correction supersedes the earlier general-SAT target coverage obligation. A polynomial total decider for every valid $L_{M22}$ source would imply P=NP. A bound on this language cannot inherit the precise ordinary-SAT FLMV curve merely from NP-completeness.

\paragraph{Exact obstruction.} The ARC policy must discover a usable, small-workspace, hereditary target-language computation without knowing the answer and without using a cheaper structural classifier as free advice. Here the concrete obstruction is the soundness of a matchings-based policy on all $M22$ sources and the inability to equate a failed sufficient structure test with UNSAT.

\section{An exact same-source two-cubic-graph representation}
For each sign $s\in\{+,-\}$ construct a multigraph $G_s$ whose vertices are the \emph{original clause indices} of sign $s$. Each original variable $x_i$ defines a labelled edge $e_i^s$ between the two distinct $s$-clauses containing it. All these edges are loops-free. Every graph vertex has degree three and both graphs have the \emph{same edge label set} $E=[n]$, with $2n/3$ vertices and $n$ edges each.

\begin{lemma}[Two coupled edge covers; PROVED/EXACT]\label{dual}
For every valid $F\in L_{M22}$, let $T\subseteq[n]$ contain precisely the variables set TRUE. Then
\[
 F(T)=\mathrm{TRUE}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 T\text{ is an edge cover of }G_+\quad\land\quad
 [n]\setminus T\text{ is an edge cover of }G_-.
\]
The construction and reconstruction preserve every original clause and source variable; they introduce no independently assignable indexed literal occurrence.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A positive original clause is satisfied exactly when some incident variable is TRUE, i.e. it meets $T$. A negative clause is satisfied exactly when some incident variable is FALSE, i.e. it meets $[n]\setminus T$. These are the two stated vertex-covering conditions on edges. Both implications are pointwise for each assignment.\end{proof}

\section{Certified three-factor sufficiency and an exact hostile control}
Call a negative clause $D=\neg x_a\vee\neg x_b\vee\neg x_c$ \emph{dangerous} if no pair in $\{a,b,c\}$ shares a positive clause, equivalently the three positive-graph edges $e_a^+,e_b^+,e_c^+$ are vertex-disjoint. Let $q(F)$ count these clauses. This is a computable structural predicate and not a hardness parameter or equity score.

\begin{theorem}[Uniformly acquired sufficient SAT policy; PROVED/EXACT restricted]\label{three}
For every valid $F\in L_{M22}$ whose positive graph $G_+$ is bipartite, a polynomial-time algorithm can construct three pairwise edge-disjoint perfect matchings $M_0,M_1,M_2$ partitioning $[n]$. If $q(F)\le2$, then at least one complete assignment $T=M_i$ satisfies \emph{every} original source clause. The selected assignment is verified against the entire source before acceptance. The algorithm decides SAT for every instance with these hypotheses, and \emph{never} uses failed matching tests to declare UNSAT.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$G_+$ is a 3-regular loopless bipartite multigraph, so Hall's theorem supplies a perfect matching. Removing it leaves a 2-regular bipartite multigraph, then a 1-regular one. Repeating polynomial augmenting-path matching constructs three disjoint matchings. Each has exactly one edge incident with each positive clause, hence its characteristic assignment satisfies all positive clauses. If a negative clause is not dangerous, it contains two variables whose positive edges share a positive vertex; no matching contains both, so it cannot be falsified under any matching assignment. A dangerous clause is falsified by $M_i$ only if all three of its distinct edge labels belong to $M_i$. Since the matchings are disjoint, one dangerous clause falsifies at most one of the three candidate assignments. At most two such clauses cannot block all three. Testing all original source clauses validates the resulting witness.\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Counterexample to completeness of matchings alone.} Let the six positive clauses be
\[
\begin{split}
& (1\vee2\vee3),\ (4\vee5\vee6),\ (7\vee8\vee9),\\
& (1\vee4\vee7),\ (2\vee5\vee8),\ (3\vee6\vee9).
\end{split}
\]
They form $G_+=K_{3,3}$ with edges labelled $1,\dots,9$. Take all six of its perfect-matching triples as the \emph{negative} clauses:
\[
\begin{split}
&(\neg1\vee\neg5\vee\neg9),\ (\neg1\vee\neg6\vee\neg8),\\
&(\neg2\vee\neg4\vee\neg9),\ (\neg2\vee\neg6\vee\neg7),\\
&(\neg3\vee\neg4\vee\neg8),\ (\neg3\vee\neg5\vee\neg7).
\end{split}
\]
This is a valid duplicate-free proper monotone exact-$(2,2)$ 12-clause input. Every positive perfect matching is one of these six triples, and hence falsifies its matching-indexed negative clause. There is \emph{no} safe positive perfect matching. Nevertheless, independent exhaustive evaluation of $2^9=512$ complete assignments finds exactly \textbf{18 SAT models}; e.g. $T=\{1,3,5,8\}$ satisfies all 12 clauses. Hence a negative answer from the sufficient policy is neither UNSAT nor a small certificate for the complement.

\section{Fully charged status and FLMV boundary}
For $n$ variables and $N=\Theta(n\log n)$ fixed-width original-source bits, the current source parser reads all $m=4n/3$ clauses and signed occurrences, allocates $O(n+m)$ Python objects, and checks signed counts and uniqueness with dictionary memory. The \texttt{dual} routine scans $O(nm)$ original occurrence fields to discover both per-sign endpoint pairs, and repeats these scans when called for bipartition and for matching factorization. The danger predicate, including all failed tests, scans $O(m^2)$ clause-pair incidences. A three-round simple augmenting-path factorization uses polynomial (at most $O(n^3)$ for the implemented broad upper bound) adjacency/augment operations, with up to three complete witness tests at $O(m)$ literal checks each. Source recognition, policy selection, rejected trials, final witness verification and fallback are all inside the controller. Unbounded DPLL may use $2^{\Theta(n)}$ search nodes and $O(n)$ recursion frames plus clause and cache storage. Each baseline run scans and counts original clause/literal accesses and records Python \texttt{tracemalloc} peak bytes, not TM tape cells. As tracking starts after fixture allocation, those bytes omit the preexisting original source and cannot be interpreted as full simultaneous machine space; the analytical ledger explicitly includes source, graph, recursion or stack, child buffers, proof material, and input indices. The current explicit source-plus-graph representation takes $\Theta(N)$ retained bits or more, far above the desired $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ workspace, and its $\Theta(n^2)$ discovery time likewise misses the proposed $N^{11/10+o(1)}$ ARC envelope. Repeated source rereads could reduce retained memory but have an unproved time tariff.

A source clause cofactor after pinning loses width/occurrence balance, so it generally exits $L_{M22}$. This was proved in prior project \texttt{PIN-ERASURE147}; neither matching nor unbounded DPLL currently emits a hereditary strictly smaller target-language child. For the seven small sources the baseline UP-first DPLL used respectively 4, 4, 6, 6, 6, 5, 4 visited nodes; the factored policy avoided DPLL on four $K_{3,3}$ cases but still paid grammar, graph, factorization, and witness tests; the all-six counterexample paid the full fallback plus failed matching attempts. Python-node counts are not directly comparable with source-query bit costs. The project-prescribed hypothetical ARC recurrences

therefore remain \textbf{CONDITIONAL/OPEN}, and the fixed point $(6/5,1/2)$ is not an achieved resource pair.
