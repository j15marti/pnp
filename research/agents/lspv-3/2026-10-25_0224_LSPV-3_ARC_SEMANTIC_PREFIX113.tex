\section{Exact proper-3CNF generator and source-name identity}
Fix $b\ge2$ source variables $x_1,\ldots,x_b$ with the \emph{same original
names} in two modules, and use disjoint auxiliary sets. For any three
distinct variables $a,c,z$ define $\operatorname{XOR}(a,c,z)$ as the four
ternary clauses that forbid precisely the tuples $z\ne a\oplus c$.
For any literal $\ell$ define
\[
 \operatorname{PIN}(\ell)
 =\!\!\bigwedge_{(\epsilon,\delta)\in\{\pm1\}^2}
    (\ell\vee \epsilon p\vee\delta q);
 \quad
 \operatorname{OR2}(u,v)
 =(u\vee v\vee h)\wedge(u\vee v\vee\neg h),
\]
where the temporary variables $p,q,h$ are fresh for each gadget. Each
clause contains three distinct names. Eliminating the temporary variables
gives exactly $\ell$ and $u\vee v$, respectively.

For $R_b$ introduce fresh $z_0,\ldots,z_b$, pin $z_0=z_b=0$, and impose
$\operatorname{XOR}(z_{i-1},x_i,z_i)$ for every $i$. Its shared-name feasibility
relation is
\[
 R_b(x)=[\bigoplus_{i=1}^b x_i=0].
\]
For any target word $u\in\{0,1\}^b$, build $Q_{b,u}$ with a separate
parity chain $w_0,\ldots,w_b$, pin $w_0=0$, introduce selector $q$, and
impose
\[
 \operatorname{OR2}(q,w_b),\qquad
 \bigwedge_{i=1}^b \operatorname{OR2}(\neg q,\ell_i(u)),
 \quad \ell_i(u)=
 \begin{cases}x_i,&u_i=1,\\\neg x_i,&u_i=0.\end{cases}
\]
The selector is existential. If $q=0$, parity must be odd; if $q=1$,
every $x_i$ equals $u_i$. Thus, \emph{for every shared assignment},
\[
 Q_{b,u}(x)=[\bigoplus_i x_i=1]\ \vee\ [x=u].
 \tag{1}
\]
All private variables remain disjoint across $R_b,Q_{b,u}$. The parent
$F_{b,u}=R_b\wedge Q_{b,u}$ is an \emph{ordinary proper signed 3CNF},
not a detached Boolean table. Its source identifiers for $x_i$ are
literally $1,\ldots,b$ in \emph{both} modules, before and after extraction
and dense local renumbering. The two independently pinned descendants
carry the inverse source-name maps and may be glued only at equal
original-name assignments.

The $R_b$ module has $4b+8$ clauses and $b+5$ private variables;
$Q_{b,u}$ has $6b+6$ clauses and $2b+5$ private variables. Therefore
\[
 m(F_{b,u})=10b+14,\qquad n(F_{b,u})=4b+10.
 \tag{2}
\]
For the matched \emph{YES} target $u=0^b$ and \emph{NO} target
$v=0^{b-1}1$, the entire ordered clause-support list, headers, variable
count and clause count are identical; exactly \emph{two literal-sign bits}
change inside the duplicated $\operatorname{OR2}(\neg q,\ell_b)$ gadget.
The source-sign difference is public to a source-readable decider and
must not be concealed when judging general SAT hardness.

\begin{theorem}[Matched opposite-label and exact original-name join]
For every $b\ge2$, $F_{b,u}$ is satisfiable if and only if $u$ has even
parity. In fact the set of feasible shared assignments of $F_{b,u}$
is $\{u\}$ if $u$ is even and empty if $u$ is odd. For every shared
prefix $p\in\{0,1\}^k$ with $0\le k<b$,
\[
 \SAT(R_b\wedge\operatorname{PIN}(p))=
 \SAT(Q_{b,u}\wedge\operatorname{PIN}(p))=1
 \quad\text{for every }u.
 \tag{3}
\]
At full depth, for each shared word $x$,
\[
 \SAT(R_b\mid x)=[|x|\equiv0\pmod2],\qquad
 \SAT(Q_{b,u}\mid x)=[|x|\equiv1\pmod2]\vee[x=u],
\]
and the \emph{correct} parent join is
\[
 \SAT(F_{b,u})
 =\bigvee_{x\in\{0,1\}^b}
  \bigl(\SAT(R_b\mid x)\wedge\SAT(Q_{b,u}\mid x)\bigr).
 \tag{4}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
XOR constraints uniquely determine each parity-chain bit for any assigned
shared variables. The initial/final parity pins give $R_b$; the existential
selector implications give equation (1). Their conjunction has a feasible
shared word exactly when $u$ is even, then only $x=u$ survives.
For every proper prefix, at least one unassigned shared coordinate remains:
there is both an even-parity and an odd-parity completion. This proves
both claims in (3), independently of the target. All gadgets use fresh
private variables, so their existential witnesses may be combined if and
only if they agree on \emph{each original} $x_i$, which proves (4).
Equation (2) follows by counting four clauses per XOR or PIN and two
per OR2; source supports do not depend on target signs.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Detached adaptive prefix-query floor; exact scoped transfer]
Suppose a deterministic join controller may query \emph{only separate
module} SAT-status oracles of the form
$\SAT(R_b\mid p)$ or $\SAT(Q_{b,u}\mid p)$, where $p$ is a leading
shared-variable prefix, without reading the modules' literal signs or
asking SAT on a \emph{mixed} formula containing both modules. To
distinguish the baseline NO source $F_{b,0^{b-1}1}$ from \emph{every}
YES source $F_{b,u}$ with $u$ even, its exact execution on the baseline
must query at least $2^{b-1}$ distinct complete \emph{even} assignments
to the $Q$ module.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All $R$ answers are independent of $u$. All proper-prefix $Q$
answers are $1$ by (3). On a complete even assignment $x$, the NO
$Q$ answer is zero, whereas for the YES target $u$ it changes only
when $x=u$. Any even word not queried on the NO transcript can be
chosen as $u$ without changing any answer seen by the controller;
the deterministic transcript and final NO output would be identical
on the YES instance. Therefore all $2^{b-1}$ even words must be
queried. This is an information restriction on the declared
detached answer channel, \emph{not} a lower bound on SAT decision.
\end{proof}
The abstract prefix-adversary argument is \emph{already present} in
project MAIN \texttt{INTERFACE-ADAPTIVITY-BARRIER73}, Lemma 73B
(22 October 2026). The new contribution is its matched metadata/sign-only
proper-3CNF source realization, explicit same-name two-level children,
and the size/charge firewall; no new unconditional decision-tree
technique is claimed.

\section{Two real ordinary descendant levels and charged failure to shrink}
The reproducer uses the complete source-bit grammar
\[
 h=\operatorname{bitlength}(2\max(n,m)+1),\quad
 N=1+(3+3m)h:
 \quad 1^h0\;\Vert\;\mathrm{bin}_h(n)\Vert\mathrm{bin}_h(m)
 \Vert\!\!\big\Vert_{\ell\text{ in clause order}}
 \mathrm{bin}_h(2(|\ell|-1)+[\ell<0]).
 \tag{5}
\]
The parent YES/NO encodings have identical $N$. A pinned child
copies \emph{every} module clause with its exact sign and source name,
then adds four real ternary clauses plus two private variables.
At the second level a \emph{different original} name is pinned, and
the complete body/header is independently serialized, parsed and
decided again. The full \emph{mixed-parent} child is also tested through
two levels, so false source-anonymous gluing is not substituted for
correct root inheritance. In each path, the source-name map fixes
$x_i\mapsto i$, and private names are newly allocated and charged.

For $b>2$, every separated-module depth-two prefix query returns SAT
on both the YES and NO parent. In contrast, complete mixed-parent
children of the NO root are UNSAT, and the YES root's branch matching
the first two bits of $u$ remains SAT. The latter mixed child retains
the entire parent body and is a \emph{parent-scale SAT lookahead}, not a
certified fractional-power guest. This explicitly exhibits the
difference between (i) correct both-label conjunction semantics and
(ii) an information-poor separated-subquery channel.

\[

The ordinary module descendants have four or eight extra PIN clauses.
Their sizes at each \emph{fixed} depth remain
$\Theta(b\log(b+2))=\Theta(N_{\rm parent})$, since the copied modules

two-level child schedule, \textbf{no} uniform $\lambda<1$ satisfies

syntactic size floor, \emph{not} a floor for every conceivable

Every bit of the fixed header, every copied clause or virtual

 \begin{aligned}

 \end{aligned}
 \tag{6}

None gives an ARC-EDGE-90 successor on these copied $\lambda=1$

\section{Reproducibility, hostile controls and adaptive escape}
A deterministic standard-library Python implementation constructs
each full CNF, performs an \emph{independent generic unit-propagating
DPLL} on re-parsed source/children, and checks the semantic predicate
separately. For every $b=2,\ldots,6$, both opposite labels have
identical ordered supports and encoding length, and differ by exactly
two sign bits. The executable performed \textbf{1,188
assertion-counted checks}, \textbf{25,110} recursive DPLL invocations,
10 ordinary parent decisions, 40 first-level separated-module
children, 80 second-level separated-module grandchildren, and 40
second-level \emph{mixed-parent} grandchildren, with zero failures.
It also enumerated 248 full-target module predicates and every proper
prefix predicate at the tested sizes; controls included SAT/UNSAT,
a proper OR3 clause, a proper Horn clause, the eight signed-triplet
UNSAT core and a malformed truncated encoding rejection. All
lengths include the actual header and body. The audit is
\textbf{COMPUTATIONAL} and does not measure FLMV physical time/space.

\emph{Adaptive-source escape (important hostile control).} For these
known-matched source templates, one paid scan verifies the chain,
selector and pin syntax and reads the signed target literals
$\ell_i(u)$. Computing $\bigoplus_i u_i$ then decides the \emph{root}
both ways in $O(N\,\mathrm{polylog}\,N)$ elementary bit work with
$O(\log N)$ index/work bits under an appropriate streaming
implementation. Recognition failure on any arbitrary source must
invoke a genuine exact SAT fallback if all-input totality is claimed;
its cost cannot disappear. Horn/SCC/affine, graph decomposition/DP,
branching, learning, caching or direct decision are likewise allowed
state-dependent choices. This construction therefore \emph{refutes
an oracle-channel inference}, not polynomial-time SAT.
