\section{Claim card}

\paragraph{Hard source.}
The project source remains the signed, duplicate-free, proper,
sign-monochromatic Monotone $3$SAT-$(2,2)$ family $H^{\pm}_{2,2}$.  Every
variable occurs exactly twice positively and twice negatively, every clause
has three distinct variables, no clause is duplicated, and $3m=4n$.  Its
NP-completeness is a \KNOWN\ dependency.  Let $N=|\operatorname{enc}(F)|$ for
the canonical indexed encoding.  The theorem below is language-generic and
therefore includes this source without exploiting its semantics.

\paragraph{Machine and costs.}
Fix a deterministic multi-tape Turing-machine model with read-only input,
work tapes, and one-bit output.  For a total exact decider $A$ and input $x$,
let $T_A(x)$ be the actual transition count and let $S_A(x)$ be the maximum of
$2$ and the number of work-tape cells simultaneously holding live nonblank
symbols.  Define exact worst-case functions
\[
 \tau_A(N)=\max_{|x|=N}T_A(x),\qquad
 \sigma_A(N)=\max_{|x|=N}S_A(x).
\]
Simulation, counters, touching, allocation, retained markers, output, wrapper
recognition, extraction and reconstruction are charged.  No supplied clock and
no semantic-state count is used.

\paragraph{Candidate under test.}
\texttt{EXACT-JOINT-POWER-GAUGE-AS-OBSTRUCTION1} says that an implication of
the form
\[
 \forall A\ \exists B=\mathcal C(A)\ \exists\alpha,\gamma\in(0,1):
 \tau_B(N)\le\tau_A(N)^{\alpha+o(1)},\quad
 \sigma_B(N)\le\sigma_A(N)^{\gamma+o(1)}
\]
must reflect genuine solver progress.  We actively falsify this interpretation
by constructing the favorable inequalities without changing the decided
language or solving any new SAT instances.

\section{Exact symbolic counterexample}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{JOINT-RESOURCE-PADDING-GAUGE1}; \EXACT]
For every total exact deterministic decider $A$ and fixed integers $q,r\ge2$,
there is a uniformly constructible, syntax-tagged total exact decider
$J_{q,r}(A)$ such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $L(J_{q,r}(A))=L(A)$;
\item polynomial time and workspace of $A$ imply polynomial time and workspace
      of $J_{q,r}(A)$;
\item for every $N$,
\[
 \tau_{J_{q,r}(A)}(N)\ge\tau_A(N)^q,
 \qquad
 \sigma_{J_{q,r}(A)}(N)\ge\sigma_A(N)^r;
\]
\item a fixed linear-time parser on the tagged source of $J_{q,r}(A)$ returns
      the source of $A$.
\end{enumerate}
Hence, for rational $0<\alpha,\gamma<1$ with $q\alpha\ge1$ and
$r\gamma\ge1$,
\[
 \tau_A(N)\le\tau_{J_{q,r}(A)}(N)^\alpha,
 \qquad
 \sigma_A(N)\le\sigma_{J_{q,r}(A)}(N)^\gamma
\]
for every $N$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Compile an instrumented interpreter for $A$.  On $x$, it simulates $A(x)$,
records elapsed transitions $L$ and the maximum number $s\ge2$ of live
simulated work cells, and stores the answer bit.  It then executes at least
$L^q$ real dummy transitions, writes and retains at least $s^r$ marked work
cells, and finally returns the bit.  Every touch is charged in time; the marked
cells remain live until output and are charged in peak workspace.  Instrumented
simulation and counters can only increase the two lower bounds.  On the fixed
machine model, $s=O(L+1)$, so fixed $q,r$ preserve polynomiality.  The wrapper
is total and language-preserving because padding begins after the answer has
been stored and is finite.

For each length, take an input attaining $\tau_A(N)$ and possibly a different
input attaining $\sigma_A(N)$.  The corresponding padded runs establish the
two maximum inequalities.  The final power inequalities follow from
$q\alpha,r\gamma\ge1$.  A fixed parser strips the explicit wrapper tag; it does
not inspect SAT semantics, enumerate witnesses, or reconstruct compatibility.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Scoped falsification; \EXACT]
A simultaneous strict power contraction relative to a source implementation's
exact worst-case time and exact peak workspace is not by itself an intrinsic
algorithmic obstruction.  It can be manufactured by padding the source and
stripping its tag.  Thus
\texttt{EXACT-JOINT-POWER-GAUGE-AS-OBSTRUCTION1} is \RETIRED\ in this
presentation-relative form.
\end{corollary}

The theorem does not refute space hierarchy, FLMV, or a genuinely absolute
compiler theorem.  The favorable ratios compare two different realized
programs and do not place the stripped output in any forbidden absolute region.
