\section{Exact new parameter and theorem}
A negative original clause $D=(\neg x_a\vee\neg x_b\vee\neg x_c)$ is dangerous for $G_+$ if its three labelled positive edges have disjoint endpoints. It is \emph{extendible dangerous} iff some perfect matching contains all three edges. Let $\mathcal E$ denote these source-indexed clauses and $q_{\mathrm{ext}}=|\mathcal E|$.
\begin{lemma}[Extension test and exact matching-class semantics]
For any dangerous $D$, let $U_D$ be its six distinct endpoints. Then $D$ is extendible iff $G_+-U_D$ has a perfect matching. A positive perfect matching $M$ satisfies all original negative clauses under the original-name assignment $x_i=[e_i\in M]$ iff no $D\in\mathcal E$ has all its edges in $M$. Thus matching-based certificate existence is decidable by at most $q+3^{q_{\mathrm{ext}}}$ polynomial-time matching calls. Failed matching-only search means UNKNOWN, not UNSAT.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A matching containing the three edges decomposes as exactly those edges and a matching on remaining vertices. A nondangerous triple has two incident edges and cannot be contained in any matching. An unextendible dangerous triple is also excluded from every matching. For remaining triples choose an omitted edge in each. Conversely a matching avoiding one chosen edge from every extendible dangerous clause satisfies every original negative clause. Enumerate the at most $3^{q_{\mathrm{ext}}}$ source-labelled choices; preserve parallel edge IDs.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Polynomial SAT terminal beyond the two-edge-transversal guard]
For every valid source $F$, if $G_+(F)$ is bridgeless and $q_{\mathrm{ext}}\le2$, a uniform polynomial-time procedure produces an original-source-verified complete SAT witness. The symmetric $G_-$ rule holds with global sign inversion and assignment complementation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Check all $q$ extension tests. Choose one edge from each of the at most two extendible dangerous clauses, forming $Q$, $|Q|\le2$. By Edmonds' perfect-matching polytope, the all-$1/3$ edge vector in a bridgeless cubic multigraph is a convex combination of perfect matchings. Their expected intersection with $Q$ is $|Q|/3<1$, so some perfect matching avoids all of $Q$. Find it by maximum matching on $G_+-Q$. All positive source clauses then have exactly one TRUE occurrence, and all negative source clauses have at least one FALSE original variable by the previous lemma. Replay the witness against every original clause. Rejected guards imply nothing about UNSAT.
\end{proof}
