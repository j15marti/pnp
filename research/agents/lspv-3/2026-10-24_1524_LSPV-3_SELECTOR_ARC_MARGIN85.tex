\section{Mathematical model and reference forbidden boundary}
Let $N$ be full ordinary encoded source bit length. Let $E$ be simultaneous exponent pairs attained by \emph{one} fixed total uniform exact all-input SAT decider in FLMV's random-access Turing model. Every policy can switch between arbitrary computable procedures and representations during execution; the following per-child portfolio is an \emph{implementation class}, not a claim of coverage of all possible policies.

The two-level FLMV curve is
\[
\beta_2(c)=\frac12\left(\frac{c+2}{c^2}-c\right),
\qquad {\cal B}_2=\{(t,s):\exists c>\max\{1,t\},\ s<\beta_2(c)\}.
\]
The published indexed Theorem~5.7 yields other certified endpoints, but at the particular space exponent $s=1/2$ this report uses the exact, already adequate $\beta_2$ curve. The historical claimed all-space product endpoint $t+s<2\cos(\pi/7)$ is retired.

\begin{lemma}[The exact half-space curve margin, PROVED/EXACT]
Let $c_{1/2}$ be the unique root in $(6/5,121/100)$ of
\[
p(c)=c^3+c^2-c-2=0.
\]
For $t>1$, the two-level curve forbids $(t,1/2)$ precisely when $t<c_{1/2}$. In particular
\[
1.2055<c_{1/2}<1.2056,\qquad
c_{1/2}\simeq1.2055694304005904.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The strict condition $1/2<\beta_2(c)$ is equivalent after multiplying by $c^2>0$ to $p(c)<0$. Since $p'(c)=3c^2+2c-1>0$ for $c>1$, $p$ is strictly increasing there. $p(6/5)<0$, $p(121/100)>0$ and the rational tests $p(12055/10000)<0<p(12056/10000)$ give the bracket. Because the FLMV witness must have $c>t$, existence of a witness with $\beta_2(c)>1/2$ is equivalent to $t<c_{1/2}$. Strict margins absorb the polylogarithmic SAT transfer.
\end{proof}

\section{A conditional per-child portfolio contraction and its exact margin}
Assume a source-correct all-input semantic ARC step, applicable at every iterated level, and \emph{charge its implementation} as follows. From a predecessor machine with simultaneous $(t,s)$, at most $N^{3/5+o(1)}$ ordinary self-contained SAT child decisions of length at most $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ are made. Each child uses at most $N^{\mu+o(1)}$ \emph{sequential} sound candidate attempts; their aggregate charged time per attempt is bounded by $N^{t/2+o(1)}$. The semantic correctness and child cover must already be established, including correct treatment of UNSAT. Total nonattempt work, such as source/parameter discovery, candidate description generation, input reads, source-to-child addressing, compatibility and switching, is at most $N^{11/10+o(1)}$. Work/index peak, including candidate regeneration, all live ancestors and a full ordinary child buffer when used, is at most $N^{\max\{1/2,s/2\}+o(1)}$. Candidate descriptions are no larger than this charged allowance. All exponents are uniform over inputs and candidates.

These stringent premises are \emph{OPEN}, not supplied by $P=NP$. They specify one fixed implementation rule. The established sequential selector permits a different successful candidate on different children, without a label oracle.

\begin{theorem}[Selector-cost threshold for this ARC recurrence, PROVED/EXACT conditional]
Under the above charged \emph{semantic and coverage premises}, the resulting one-machine exponent envelope is
\[
t_{j+1}\le\max\left\{\frac{11}{10},\,\frac35+\mu+\frac{t_j}{2}\right\},
\qquad
s_{j+1}\le\max\left\{\frac12,\frac{s_j}{2}\right\}.
\]
For any seed $(K,K)$ with $K\ge2$, iterating these envelopes forces finite entry into the two-level FLMV forbidden region if
\[
\boxed{\mu<\mu_{\mathrm{crit}}:=\frac12\left(c_{1/2}-\frac65\right)
\simeq0.0027847152002952.}
\]
If $\mu\ge\mu_{\mathrm{crit}}$, this \emph{particular envelope calculation} does not force entry, irrespective of route depth. No general impossibility of a better algorithm or a different route follows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Multiplying the number of child calls by the number of charged sequential attempts gives exponent $3/5+\mu+t_j/2$, with independent local overhead $11/10$. Per-trial memory is recycled and not multiplied by candidate count, but the charged whole-machine simultaneous peak must hold. Put $u_*=6/5+2\mu\ge11/10$. From a seed $K\ge2\ge u_*$ for the small $\mu$ relevant to finite entry, the equality-envelope iterates satisfy
\[
u_j=u_*+(K-u_*)2^{-j}.
\]
More generally, if $u_*>K$, the maximum-rule envelope is already at least $u_*$ after finitely many steps and still cannot force a smaller limit. The space envelope reaches exponent $1/2$ after finitely many iterations. A strict $(c,\beta_2(c))$ witness exists at the limiting point precisely when $u_*<c_{1/2}$ by the lemma. Strict continuity then implies a finite iterate is inside the forbidden region for every sufficiently large diagonal seed. Conversely $u_*\ge c_{1/2}$ makes the equality-envelope iterates stay outside the required time threshold when begun above $u_*$, so the displayed bounds alone do not imply a forbidden landing. This proves a fact about a \emph{sufficient majorizing recurrence}, not a lower bound on actual optimal SAT computation.
\end{proof}

The fixed-box witness $c=241/200$ is a more conservative convenient route: it suffices to have $\mu<1/400$ and wait for $u_j<241/200$. The exact curved test also admits $\mu=1/400$ with a slightly larger fixed $c$. At the other side $\mu=1/256$ gives $u_*=773/640=1.2078125>c_{1/2}$ and therefore cannot be certified by this envelope. An $N^{1/100}$-size per-child selector yields $u_*=1.22$ and loses the two-level landing. A polylogarithmic number of trials means $\mu=0$ and preserves the old fixed point, provided trial \emph{generation and verification} remain fully charged.

\paragraph{Child-length-relative portfolio size.}
If each child of length $M=N^{1/2+o(1)}$ instead has at most $M^{\delta+o(1)}$ candidates, the parent exponent is $\mu=\delta/2$. Hence the $\beta_2$ sufficient threshold becomes $\delta<2\mu_{\mathrm{crit}}\simeq0.00556943$. An $N^{1/100}$ portfolio is \emph{not} harmless solely because each candidate is individually small.

\section{Selector coverage, SAT/UNSAT semantics, and storage firewall}
The previous MOVINGCOVER1 exact theorem states that if a uniform generator lists $M(N)$ descriptions and a single bounded runner returns a sound bit or UNKNOWN in time $t(N)$ and peak workspace $s(N)$, resetting after each attempt, then the aggregate time includes $M(N)[t(N)+s(N)+b(N)]$ and peak space includes the one live runner, the iterator and description. Input-specific winning indices are permitted. Full coverage yields a total exact decider. This does \emph{not} establish the favorable $N^{t/2}$ trial bound required above, nor the all-input semantic child decomposition.

\paragraph{SAT-only exactness.}
One may allow only a verified satisfying witness or UNKNOWN. If \emph{every SAT child} has some accepted bounded witness generator, exhausting all candidates without a witness establishes UNSAT. This needs a proved uniform SAT-side cover; timeout by itself is not evidence of UNSAT. A full witness occupies potentially $\Omega(n)$ bits, so the claimed half-power workspace may fail unless witness generation/verification is genuinely streamed, recomputed and charged. Under $P=NP$, a fixed polynomial-time SAT machine exists, but this alone does not give any specified fixed-time/space portfolio cover inside FLMV.

\paragraph{Three concrete negative transports.}
(1) A general exact fallback at the original source length preserves correctness but can inherit exponent $t$, defeating contraction. (2) A cached $N$-bit source or materialized child strings impose their actual work-space footprint; write-only output is not free readable memory. (3) LSPV-5 WRITE-SHADOW-ENDPOINT118's low-write RAM simulation yields an FLMV space exponent from cumulative charged writes only when writes are small; sequentially reusing $N^{\sigma}$ cells for $N^\mu$ trials can require $N^{\mu+\sigma}$ \emph{writes} even though peak cells remain $N^\sigma$. The write bound is then weaker than direct peak-space accounting.
