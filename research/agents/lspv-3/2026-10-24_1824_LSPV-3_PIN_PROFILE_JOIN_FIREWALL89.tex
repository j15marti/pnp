\section{Target and falsifiers}
The shared target is a favorable achievable-pair edge or a finite cofinal route to
a Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas (FLMV) forbidden region. One simultaneous
pair $(t,s)$ is witnessed by ONE total, uniform, all-input exact SAT machine in
the matched read-only indexed random-access Turing model. The ARC endpoint is
correct, but the all-input semantic contraction is still \textbf{OPEN}.

Three falsifiable mechanisms were selected before testing:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[] \textbf{M1:} Source-independent pinned query schedules cannot see all
original clause signs. This is \textbf{FALSIFIED}: eight fixed pins recover
all three signs of any proper clause.
\item[] \textbf{M2:} The complete source-anonymized local SAT truth tables,
including their entire eight-bit answer vectors, suffice to decide the

proper 3CNFs with identical $(n,m,N)$.
\item[] \textbf{M3:} A two-level hereditary family of tiny ordinary pinned
children fills ARC's charged semantic gluing gap. This is
\textbf{FALSIFIED as a sufficient inference}: source-name incidence and
cross-clause consistency remain unavailable from those anonymous profiles.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Model and fixed-width ordinary encoding}
A proper source clause has exactly three distinct, noncomplementary variable
identifiers. The source is an ordered proper 3CNF with $m$ clauses and
$n$ declared variable IDs, encoded by two header words $(n,m)$ and four
words per clause $(3,\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)$. A signed literal uses code
$2|\ell|+\mathbf1_{\ell<0}$; the word width and bit length are
\[
 w(n,m)=\left\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\right\rceil,\qquad
 N=(4m+2)w(n,m).
\]
There is no free original-source address table. Original source reads,
indices, control, child headers, changed/unchanged writes, and all writable
workspace are charged at elementary-bit granularity.

For a literal $\ell$ and fresh $a,b$ put
\[
 U(\ell;a,b)=\bigwedge_{\epsilon,\delta\in\{\pm1\}}
     (\ell\vee\epsilon a\vee\delta b).
\]
Each block has four proper ternary clauses and is equivalent to unit $\ell$.
For an isolated clause $C=(\ell_1\vee\ell_2\vee\ell_3)$,
canonically rename its three positions to $u_1,u_2,u_3$, preserving only
their polarities. For each \emph{source-independent} assignment
$b\in\{0,1\}^3$, define its ordinary pin child
\[
 H(C,b)=\widehat C\;\wedge\;
 \bigwedge_{i=1}^3 U(u_i^{b_i};a_i,d_i),\quad
 u_i^{1}=u_i,\ u_i^{0}=\neg u_i.
 \tag{1}
\]
Here the six $a_i,d_i$ are fresh variables. There are exactly thirteen
proper ternary clauses, nine declared variables and
\[
 L=(4\cdot13+2)w(9,13)=594 \text{ bits}
 \tag{2}
\]
under the above fixed-width encoding, independent of source $N$.
The generator must nevertheless read the source clause's literal
codes to copy their signs and compute its source index. Renaming is charged;
(1) is not an oracle for unread input bits.

\section{Exact semantic bit recovery and two levels}
\begin{theorem}[Eight-pin clause recovery; PROVED/EXACT restricted]
For every proper signed ternary clause $C$, the fixed ordered vector
\[
 {\cal P}(C)=(\operatorname{SAT}(H(C,b)))_{b\in\{0,1\}^3}
 \tag{3}
\]
has exactly one zero. Its zero is at the unique assignment $b^*(C)$
falsifying $C$, and it recovers all three ordered literal polarities:
$\ell_i$ is negative if and only if $b_i^*(C)=1$.
Hence $8m$ fixed-schedule, constant-size ordinary child SAT labels
recover every polarity in an ordered $m$-clause source whose incidence
triples are given separately.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each four-clause unit gadget requires its named variable to have the
pinned value: if the unit is false, one clause is falsified for every
setting of its two auxiliaries; if true, all four hold. Thus the child
is satisfiable exactly when $C(b)=1$. A disjunction of three proper
literals has one and only one falsifying assignment, with the stated
sign convention. The eight-query schedule depends only on the clause
index and $b$, never on its hidden SAT label or original sign fields.
\end{proof}

For the \emph{status-only information model} where the controller sees
public variable incidence and one-bit oracle answers but no source
polarity fields, at least $3m$ answers are necessary in the worst case
to reconstruct all $3m$ polarities: take $m$ disjoint triples and their
$2^{3m}$ possible sign patterns; a binary decision tree with fewer than
$3m$ questions has too few leaves. This is \textbf{NOT} a lower bound
on ordinary SAT decision, or on a machine that directly reads the
signed source words. The latter can bypass all queries.

The construction is recursively syntactically valid. Each first-level
child has 13 clauses. One can recover the signs of each of those clauses
from eight additional pinned grandchildren (104 calls per first-level
child), then evaluate its reconstructed constant-active-variable
formula, and finally recover the root clause signs. The maximal
second-level ordinary child is again the canonical 594-bit instance.
A literal two-level service uses at most $832m$ grandchild SAT calls,
plus fully charged source-address generation, local reconstruction,
and verification. The two-level source bit-provider reads the parent
source; the grandchildren are \emph{not} self-sufficient when that
provider is withheld. Direct evaluation, content caching, or source
rereads are legal and usually cheaper.

\section{Connected opposite-label semantic-fiber collision}
\begin{theorem}[Full local-profile join failure; PROVED/EXACT]
There are connected, duplicate-free, proper 3CNFs $F,G$ with exactly
$n=51$ declared occurring variables, $m=36$ clauses, and
$N=(4\cdot36+2)w(51,36)=1898$ encoded bits such that
\[
 \operatorname{SAT}(F)=0,\qquad \operatorname{SAT}(G)=1,
 \qquad {\cal P}(F_j)={\cal P}(G_j)
 \quad\text{for every ordered clause }j\le36.
 \tag{4}
\]
The profiles in (4) are source-\emph{anonymized} profiles: each
isolated clause renames its three identifiers in position order.
The originals' incidence labels are not assumed equal or free.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $v_1,\ldots,v_{24}$ be the original variables and let
$C_\eta$ run through the eight sign combinations on a named
triple. The first eight clauses of $F$ are all eight
$C_\eta(v_1,v_2,v_3)$, so $F$ is UNSAT. The first eight clauses of
$G$ are the respective $C_\eta(v_{3i+1},v_{3i+2},v_{3i+3})$
for $i=0,\ldots,7$: each uses a disjoint triple and all eight
constraints are simultaneously satisfiable.

Introduce a guard variable $g=v_{25}$, two unit-gadget
auxiliaries $v_{26},v_{27}$, and $24$ distinct
bridge auxiliaries $v_{28},\ldots,v_{51}$.
Append to BOTH formulas the 24 bridge clauses
\[
 (g\vee v_i\vee v_{27+i})\quad (1\le i\le24)
\]
and the four clauses of $U(g;v_{26},v_{27})$.
The guard is forced true, so bridges add no restrictions.
Every variable occurs, and the incidence graph is connected
through $g$. Every clause contains three distinct variable IDs;
within either formula all 36 clauses are distinct.

The first eight clauses have identical ordered sign triples
between $F$ and $G$, as do the common 28 other clauses.
Theorem 1 says each anonymous local pinned profile is determined
by this sign triple alone, proving the equality in (4).
The first eight $F$ clauses are contradictory; all $G$ core
clauses can be simultaneously satisfied and extended with $g=1$.
All displayed counts follow directly.
\end{proof}

The identity of shared variables is therefore an indispensable
\emph{additional channel} for any exact join that uses local
source-anonymized profiles. If an implementation instead carries
the original variable-ID triples, $F$ and $G$ become distinguishable,
but those identifiers, their acquisition, lookup, equality tests,
and their compatibility work must be charged. The theorem does NOT
say that the full original sources are computationally hard: both
family grammars admit a linear source-aware recognizer.

\section{Same-realization and adaptive-state ledger}
The actual tested policy begins with a read-only source and paid
headers; $m,n$ and clause addresses are acquired by bit operations,
not by a SAT-label oracle. Its rule switches on observed metadata:
it may apply direct Horn/SCC/affine methods, or a source-preserving
index service, or canonical eight-pin source-anonymized profiles,
or cached constant-local truth tables, or an exact fallback.
The concrete experiment selects profiles to test the proposed
gluing contract on both labels. After each query it retains its
current source index, eight-bit local profile, current child
bit-provider state and resource budget. Exactness at this step is
the literal equivalence $\mathrm{SAT}(H(C,b))=C(b)$, not a claim
that the parent SAT bit has been decided.

Record $(U,R_{\mathrm{source}},R_{\mathrm{work}},
W_{\mathrm{work}},\Delta_{\mathrm{work}},
\Delta_{\mathrm{head}},\Delta_{\mathrm{addr}},
\Delta_{\mathrm{control}},S_{\mathrm{peak}},BT,
|\mathrm{program/rep}|)$. Every bit write counts even if unchanged;
flips count changes only; $BT$ sums actual live writable bits over
actual steps. Generating one canonical 594-bit child, source field
reads, parsing, calls, failed strategies, caching and any
fallback are charged. A virtual bit-provider may avoid holding 594
child bits at once but must answer repeated indexed bits from the
original source. One explicit depth-two path has $O(\log N)$
scratch plus the constant-active-variable child solver; the
full local-profile scan costs $O(m\operatorname{polylog}N)$
bit work on the canonical source format. These are
\emph{upper bounds for restricted transcription}, not a
model-matched favorable all-input SAT decider.
The fallback exact SAT decider may be exponential and cannot be
silently dropped from worst-case accounting.

For the disjoint-triple hostile control, all $2^{3m}$ sign strings
lead to satisfiable parents, although the transcript-only
\emph{reconstruction} problem needs $3m$ bits of information.
The connected opposite-label pair in Theorem 2 has identical
full local profiles. These two controls reject any inference from
local information amount to necessary root decision cost.
