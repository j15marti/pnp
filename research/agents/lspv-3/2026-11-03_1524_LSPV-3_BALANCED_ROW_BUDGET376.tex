\section*{Published hardness and model}
Urquhart (1987) and Ben-Sasson--Wigderson (2001) establish $2^{\Omega(h)}$ lines in every ordinary unrestricted DAG-Resolution refutation of odd Tseitin CNFs on a uniform constant-degree expander family with $h$ graph vertices. In the cubic case, $n=3h/2$ edge variables, $m=4h$ source clauses, and signed ASCII DIMACS input length $N=\Theta(h\log h)$. This is a Resolution proof-system lower bound, NOT a time lower bound for all correct SAT algorithms. LSPV-2 report 341, blob c87acd11, independently proves $W_{R_{\mathrm{mix341}}}=4h-1$ for connected bridgeless cubic sources. We keep the identical fixed unit-cost binary-premise calculus: original clauses free; RES, local AND3, GF(2) XOR, conservative two-input DEF, and checked LOCAL-CONVERT paid. No new rule is introduced.

\begin{theorem}[A low-total-width explicit mixed refutation]
For every even $h\ge4$, every connected bridgeless simple cubic graph $G$ with $h$ vertices, and every odd charge, there is a fully explicit, same-source legal $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ refutation with exactly $4h-1$ charged nodes whose XOR outputs, represented by sorted original-edge identifiers, satisfy
\[
\max_u\operatorname{width}(u)\le3h/2,\qquad
\sum_{u:\mathrm{XOR}}\operatorname{width}(u)\le3h\lceil\log_2h\rceil.
\]
The full certificate can be serialized in $O(h\log^2h)$ bits and emitted and checked by a deterministic bit-cost construction in $O(h\log^2h)$ bit operations and $O(h\log h)$ bits of simultaneous work space, given the labelled CNF/graph source.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conjoin the four authentic signed 3-clauses at each vertex by three legal AND3 nodes and verify their eight-row factor equals the local affine equation $R_v$. There are $3h$ such nodes. Construct a balanced binary tree whose leaves are the $h$ equations; each internal node applies one legal XOR. There are $h-1$ such nodes and the final equation is $0=1$ since each edge occurs at two endpoints and the total charge is odd.
An internal node for leaf subset $S$ has variable set $\delta(S)$, so its row width is at most $3|S|$. At every tree depth the subtrees are disjoint and their leaf counts total at most $h$; therefore the sum of row widths at that depth is at most $3h$. The tree has at most $\lceil\log_2 h\rceil$ internal depths. Every edge label and parent identifier costs $O(\log h)$ bits. An XOR on two sorted explicit masks is a streaming symmetric-difference merge; the total number of row items read and written is $O(h\log h)$ and the bit cost is $O(h\log^2h)$. A postorder depth-first schedule retains only disjoint completed sibling rows, altogether at most $3h$ edge identifiers, plus $O(h)$ input/incidence bookkeeping. Local source tests cost constant work per vertex after parsing; reordering source records, when needed, has polynomial bit-sorting overhead of the same order. Hence the stated evidence, time and peak-space upper bounds.
\end{proof}

\section*{Falsification and representation firewall}
LSPV-3 report 375 proves that on constant expanders EVERY pure-XOR DAG must have a row of width at least $\beta(G)h/3=\Omega(h)$. The theorem above, for the SAME authenticated source, shows that a compulsory $\Omega(h)$ maximum row width does NOT force exponential total explicit row evidence, verification time, or memory. This counterexample needs no lazy circuits. Its existential upper bound is not an intrinsic lower bound, and it does not extend the pure-XOR width theorem to all mixed rules. A uniform polynomial-time recognizer for authenticated Tseitin instances plus exhaustive fallback is correct on SAT/UNSAT/malformed strings but has exponential global worst-case; thus the pointwise family control does not establish or refute B2/B3.
