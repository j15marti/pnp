\section{BUILD: source and theorem}
Let G denote the explicit connected proper 15-variable, 20-clause strict signed exact-(2,2)-3CNF source encoded in the companion independent JavaScript checker and local complete Python reproducer. Its 272 satisfying assignments have exposed bits $(a,b,c)=(x_1,x_2,x_{13})$ and hidden bit $p=x_4$. Write $t=a+2b+4c$.
\begin{lemma}[Four-port relation and two-puncture rigidity]
The exact existential relation is
\[
Q_0(t,p)\iff(t\ne3)\land((t\ne1)\lor(p=0)).
\]
Complementing all four occurrences of the hidden variable gives a second balanced source with exactly the same exposed relation but
\[
Q_1(t,p)\iff(t\ne3)\land((t\ne1)\lor(p=1)).
\]
In the original source, negative hidden-port slots are clauses 2 and 3 (zero-indexed); after flipping the hidden port, they are slots 15 and 16. On removing both negative slots simultaneously, every assignment satisfying the other 18 clauses and having physical $p=0$ forces \emph{both} removed clause feet false.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Full enumeration of all $2^{15}=32768$ source assignments yields the four-port nonzero cofactor multiplicities $32,29,16,10,8,44,37,36,32,10,8,5,5$ for $(t,p)=(00),(01),(10),(20),(21),(40),(41),(50),(51),(60),(61),(70),(71)$, respectively, and zero for all others. The mode-zero and mode-one double-puncture $p=0$ rows contain only foot pattern $00$, with 153 and 119 witnesses. The two independent source implementations assert these complete tables. This finite lemma awaits external kernel verification.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[A three-port summary is not a congruence]
Take a disjoint copy of G in mode zero and a copy in mode $f\in\{0,1\}$. Swap one negative hidden-port occurrence from each source. The connected proper strict exact-(2,2) formula has 30 variables, 40 clauses, and exposed relation
\[
\exists p\;Q_0(t_A,p)\land Q_f(t_B,p).
\]
For $(t_A,t_B)=(1,1)$, the formula is SAT when $f=0$ but UNSAT when $f=1$, although both original modules have identical three-port projections. The 64 pinned tuple pairs contain 49 SAT entries for mode 00 and 48 for mode 01.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If the physical hidden bits differ, the module with $p=0$ receives a false exchanged negative literal and its foot is forced false by the simultaneous puncture lemma, yielding a contradiction. Equal hidden bits make every swapped literal semantically identical to its original occurrence, so full source witnesses compose. The displayed existential relation follows. Tuple 1 requires $p=f$, and all other permitted tuples allow either bit. This proves the distinction.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[All-size one-bit repair]
For $k\ge2$ and modes $f_0,\ldots,f_{k-1}$, use $k$ variable-disjoint mode-flipped copies of G and cross-swap the second negative hidden port slot of each left module with the first negative slot of its right neighbor. The resulting connected strict exact-(2,2) formula has $15k$ variables and $20k$ clauses, and exact root relation
\[
\exists p\in\{0,1\}\;\bigwedge_i Q_{f_i}(t_i,p).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\left(\bigwedge_i t_i\ne3\right)\land
\neg\left[(\bigvee_{i:f_i=0}t_i=1)\land(\bigvee_{j:f_j=1}t_j=1)\right].
\]
It has polynomial uniform deterministic SAT witness and authenticated exact relational UNSAT certificate acquisition on this recognized grammar.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each intermediate module uses two distinct negative slots, so double-puncture rigidity forces all physical hidden bits to agree. Under agreement all original module constraints hold and independent witnesses compose. The one-bit product formula and its elimination follow. A uniform checker validates each source clause, occurrence swap and mode, recomputes both fixed 15-variable profiles, intersects allowed shared hidden-bit values, and outputs either a full original-clause witness or the authenticated empty-intersection trace.
\end{proof}
