\section{A stronger ALL-YES theorem on the cyclic clause scopes}
Fix $n=3r\ge3$, with cyclic subscripts, and label the four groups of clause records as
\begin{equation}
 C_i\text{ on }(x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2})\quad(0\le i<n),
 \qquad Q_j\text{ on }(x_{3j},x_{3j+1},x_{3j+2})\quad(0\le j<r).
\end{equation}
Every clause has three literals with independent signs. The source encoding lists all $C_i$ and then all $Q_j$, and retains authentic clause IDs. Unlike the single explicit signing in the archive, our statement quantifies over \emph{every} balanced signing.

\begin{theorem}[Blockwise witness for all balanced cycle signings]\label{thm:all-yes}
For every $r\ge1$, every signing of these $4r$ proper ternary clauses in which each $c_j=x_{3j+2}$ occurs exactly twice positively and twice negatively is satisfiable. The other variables need not be signed-balanced. A satisfying assignment can be constructed in $O(n)$ Boolean operations after canonical source parsing by independently checking at most eight assignments within each block. In particular, \emph{every} strict signed exact-$(2,2)$ signing on these scopes is SAT.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix block $(a,b,c)=(x_{3j},x_{3j+1},x_{3j+2})$. Its two internal clauses are $C_{3j}$ and $Q_j$. The cross clauses $C_{3j+1}$ and $C_{3j+2}$ contain, respectively, the two \emph{left} literals on $(b,c)$ and the single \emph{left} literal on $c$. We seek an assignment to $(a,b,c)$ satisfying both internal clauses and the left-side disjunction of each cross clause. Such choices for separate blocks concatenate to a satisfying assignment of the whole cyclic formula, including at the wraparound.

Choose $c$ to make its literal in $C_{3j+2}$ true. If its literal in $C_{3j+1}$ is also true, both cross clauses are already satisfied from this block. The two internal clauses, after fixing $c$, each forbid at most one of the four assignments to $(a,b)$, so choose any pair satisfying both.

Otherwise the two cross-clause occurrences of $c$ have opposite signs. By the two-positive/two-negative quota, the remaining occurrences of $c$ in $C_{3j}$ and $Q_j$ also have opposite signs. Therefore the selected value of $c$ satisfies exactly one internal clause. Set $b$ to satisfy its literal in $C_{3j+1}$; this satisfies the other cross clause. There is at most one unsatisfied internal clause left, and it contains a literal on $a$, so set $a$ to satisfy that clause. Both cases choose a valid block assignment using only the block's four source records. Apply the construction independently for every $j$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:all-yes} is a \emph{falsifier of using this particular exact-$(2,2)$ cyclic family as a both-answer-class adversary}. The earlier positive moving-frontier DP theorem remains true, but its strict balanced-sign subfamily has no UNSAT inputs. Its contracted fixed YES/NO child is therefore just a constant YES on that promise and says nothing about finding UNSAT certificates there. Promise recognition (including source format and signed quota) must still be charged if the procedure is used outside the declared promise.

\section{Source-ID-authenticated frontier transcripts: a separate proof system}
For any ordinary proper 3CNF $F$ with authentic source rows and a chosen order $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, let $E_j$ be \emph{all and only} source clause IDs whose latest variable is $x_j$. Let $B_j$ be the already processed variables still occurring in a clause with unprocessed variables. Define $S_0=\{()\}$ and
\[
 S_j=\left\{\alpha|_{B_j}:\begin{array}{l}
 \alpha\text{ extends some }\beta\in S_{j-1}\text{ by a choice of }x_j,\\
 \text{and satisfies every original source clause in }E_j
 \end{array}\right\}.
\]
Here the extension preserves the complete predecessor boundary assignment. All forgotten variables occur in no future clause. A transcript lists $(E_j,B_j,S_j)$ for each layer; its checker independently computes each transition from the previous row, exact original clauses and source IDs, rejecting any omitted ID, changed state, malformed record or incorrect final polarity.

\begin{proposition}[Fully charged bounded-order DP certification]\label{prop:dp}
For every input $F$, both labels are determined exactly by $S_n\ne\varnothing$ versus $S_n=\varnothing$. If $w=\max_j|B_j|$ for the \emph{specified and authenticated} order, there are at most $2^w$ boundary states per layer and $2(n+m)2^w$ candidate local extension/clause checks after indexed bucketing. The acquisition and authenticated replay costs are $2^{O(w)}\operatorname{poly}(N)$, with polynomial-size source parsing and bucketing charged. For $w=O(\log N)$ this is polynomial, and for $w\le4$ it is linear in the number of local DP tests. SAT witnesses can be obtained with charged predecessor pointers. On UNSAT inputs the full transcript of $n$ exact projections is a sound, uniformly constructible refutation in \emph{this augmented DP grammar}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Induct on $j$. Every predecessor boundary tuple represents at least one assignment satisfying all completed clauses. A new bit that satisfies exactly all newly completed clauses extends such an assignment. Conversely every satisfying assignment through layer $j$ restricts at layer $j-1$ to a predecessor tuple and must pass those exact tests. Projection removes only names absent from future clauses, hence it preserves all continuation possibilities. At $j=n$, the boundary is empty. Existence of the empty tuple is equivalent to satisfiability. The state and transition counts follow from $|B_j|\le w$, and bucketing source clauses by their last variable takes polynomial source preprocessing. Every update and unsuccessful branch is charged. Pointer reconstruction is a separate $O(n2^w)$-entry cost, not a free SAT witness.
\end{proof}
