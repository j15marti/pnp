\section{General one-clause source-splice lemma}
Let $F_i=A_i\wedge C_i$ ($i$ modulo $k\ge2$) be minimally UNSAT CNFs on pairwise disjoint sets of original variable names, with $C_i=\ell_i\vee D_i$. Here $D_i$ is the disjunction of the other literals in $C_i$, and the chosen $\ell_i$ in different components have the same sign. Set
\[
H_i=A_i\wedge(\ell_{i-1}\vee D_i),\qquad H=\bigwedge_{i=0}^{k-1}H_i.
\tag{1}
\]
These are ordinary clauses with \emph{actual cross-component original names}, not semantic placeholder edges.
\begin{lemma}[One-clause puncturing]\label{lem:puncture}
If $A\wedge(\ell\vee D)$ is UNSAT, then $A\models\neg\ell$ and $A\models\neg D$. If it is minimally UNSAT, $A$ is SAT. In (1), every $H_i$ individually is SAT when only its listed clauses are retained and its borrowed name remains unconstrained, but $H$ is UNSAT. More strongly,
\[
\boxed{\ A_{i-1}\wedge A_i\wedge(\ell_{i-1}\vee D_i)\quad\text{is UNSAT for each }i.\ }
\tag{2}
\]
A cyclic exchange of equal-polarity literal occurrences preserves each variable's complete signed occurrence count.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Any assignment satisfying $A$ cannot satisfy $C$, or it would satisfy the UNSAT conjunction $A\wedge C$. Thus it falsifies every literal of $C$, yielding $A\models\neg\ell\wedge\neg D$. Minimal unsatisfiability gives satisfiability after removal of $C$. In an isolated altered $H_i$, use such a witness for $A_i$ and freely set the external original variable of $\ell_{i-1}$ to make the borrowed literal true. In the joint conjunction, $A_{i-1}$ forces $\ell_{i-1}$ false, and $A_i$ forces $D_i$ false, contradicting the cross-clause. This is already a contradiction in (2); all other modules may be discarded. Each original occurrence of $\ell_i$ moves to exactly one recipient clause with the same sign, so its positive and negative occurrence multiplicities stay unchanged.\end{proof}
