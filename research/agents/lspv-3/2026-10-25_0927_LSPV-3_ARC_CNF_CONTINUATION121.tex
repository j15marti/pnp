\section{Exact universal common-CNF-filter compatibility}
Let $Y=(y_1,\ldots,y_k)$ be an ordered vector of precisely the
\emph{same original source variable names} on ordinary CNF modules
$D(Y,U)$ and $R(Y,V)$, with $U\cap V=\varnothing$ and no private name
equal to a source name. Let
\[
 X=\{0,1\}^k,\quad
 P=\{u\in X:\exists U\ D(u,U)\},\quad
 Q=\{v\in X:\exists V\ R(v,V)\}.
\]
These projected feasibility sets are mathematical objects, \emph{not}
a computationally free source representation. Let $h:X\to H$ be
a \emph{fixed} quotient. For every consistent \emph{common}
continuation $C(Y,W)$ with fresh private $W$ disjoint from $U,V$,
write $S_C=\{w:\exists W\ C(w,W)\}$. The true and coarsened
joins respectively accept when
\[
 J(C):=[P\cap Q\cap S_C\ne\varnothing],\qquad
 \widetilde J_h(C):=
 [h(P\cap S_C)\cap h(Q\cap S_C)\ne\varnothing].
\]
This is a \emph{common} Boolean filter on both modules, not an arbitrary
replacement of just the second module $R$.

\begin{theorem}[Universal common-CNF-continuation criterion]\label{th:iff}
For finite $P,Q\subseteq X$ and a fixed $h:X\to H$, the following
are equivalent:
\[
\begin{split}
& \forall\text{ ordinary proper-3CNF common continuations }C(Y,W):
          \quad J(C)=\widetilde J_h(C), \tag{A}\\
& \forall u\in P\setminus Q\quad
  \forall v\in Q\setminus P:\quad h(u)\ne h(v). \tag{B}
\end{split}
\]
The same equivalence holds if (A) quantifies over \emph{all}
subsets $S\subseteq X$, or only over continuations of the elementary
two-word-selector form in the proof.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For any $S$, an accepting exact join necessarily yields an accepting
quotient join. Suppose (B) and the quotient accepts on $S$.
There are $u\in P\cap S$, $v\in Q\cap S$ with $h(u)=h(v)$.
If $u\notin Q$ and $v\notin P$, they form a prohibited
cross-exclusive collision; thus $u\in Q$ or $v\in P$.
That word lies in $P\cap Q\cap S$, proving exactness for both labels.

Conversely suppose (B) fails for $u\in P\setminus Q$ and
$v\in Q\setminus P$ with $h(u)=h(v)$. Such $u,v$ are distinct.
Choose $S=\{u,v\}$. Because $u\notin Q$ and $v\notin P$,
$P\cap Q\cap S=\varnothing$, while $u\in P\cap S$ and
$v\in Q\cap S$ have identical label. This produces a false SAT.
To realize the \emph{same} $S$ by a legitimate ordinary proper-3CNF,
introduce one fresh selector $b$ and, for each source $y_i$, write
\[
(b\lor L_i(u))\land(\neg b\lor L_i(v)),
\qquad
L_i(z)=
\begin{cases}y_i&z_i=1,\\ \neg y_i&z_i=0.\end{cases}
\]
When $b=0$ all $u$ literals are forced; when $b=1$ all
$v$ literals are forced. Replace each binary clause $(\ell\lor m)$
by the \emph{two} proper ternary clauses
$(\ell\lor m\lor a)\land(\ell\lor m\lor\neg a)$
with its own fresh auxiliary $a$. Each ternary clause has
three distinct variables, with no private/source alias.
The resulting source-compatible $C_{u,v}$ has $4k$
proper-3CNF clauses, $2k+1$ new private variable names,
and projected support exactly $\{u,v\}$.
Full decimal headers, identifiers, delimiters and clause bodies
have $O(k\log(k+2))$ encoding size for consecutive names
in the standalone selector; embedding against large external
source IDs requires the \emph{actual} identifier lengths to be paid.
Hence (A) forces (B).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Source-independent reuse cannot collapse words universally]
If the \emph{same} quotient $h$ satisfies Theorem~\ref{th:iff}(A)
for every pair of source-named module supports $P,Q$ on $X$,
then $h$ is injective and has at least $2^k$ labels.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For distinct $u,v$, take $P=\{u\}$ and $Q=\{v\}$.
Theorem~\ref{th:iff}(B) demands $h(u)\ne h(v)$.
\end{proof}
This does \emph{not} prohibit a source-dependent, state-dependent or
$A$-dependent quotient, nor imply a runtime lower bound for arbitrary SAT.

\paragraph{Relationship to inherited semantic theorems.}
Earlier ARC-PIN-HEREDITY120 proved the sharp criterion only for
\emph{consistent coordinate pins} applied to both fixed modules:
a collision $h(u)=h(v)$ must admit some joint word in the
cylinder fixing equal coordinates of $u,v$. It is \emph{weaker}
than (B), because a general clause can exclude that joint word
while retaining both colliding exclusive words. Earlier
ARC-FIBER-HEREDITY117 handled a different quantifier:
\emph{one fixed prefix against all possible opposing suffix modules};
fiber saturation is required there. Theorem~\ref{th:iff}
instead fixes \emph{both} modules and quantifies over a
\emph{common appended} ordinary CNF filter. Thus it is not a
rebranding of either previous iff.

\section{Source-named two-level counterexample in real encoded SAT}
Let the three \emph{original} common boundary names be
$x=y_1$, $y=y_2$, and $z=y_3$, with $x$ as the low bit
in decimal boundary words. Define
\[
D(x,y,z)=(\neg x)\land(\neg y\lor\neg z),\qquad
R(x,y,z)=(\neg y)\land(\neg x\lor\neg z).
\]
Use the explicit four-ternary-clause UNIT expansion for each
unit literal $\ell$,
\[
(\ell\lor a\lor b)\land(\ell\lor a\lor\neg b)
\land(\ell\lor\neg a\lor b)\land(\ell\lor\neg a\lor\neg b),
\]
and the two-clause BINARY expansion described above.
Private auxiliary names of $D$ and $R$ are disjoint,
and the three boundary names are \emph{not} renamed.
Their exact projected supports are
\[
P=\{000,010,001\}=\{0,2,4\},\qquad
Q=\{000,100,001\}=\{0,1,4\}.
\]
Here binary vectors are displayed in $(x,y,z)$-coordinate order;
decimal labels use $x$ as the $2^0$ coordinate.
The true joint supports are $\{0,4\}$.
Define the reusable $h$ by $h(2)=h(1)=0$;
give each of the other six boundary vectors a distinct label,
different from $0$.

The \emph{only} nontrivial quotient collision is
$u=2=(0,1,0)$ and $v=1=(1,0,0)$.
They agree only on $z=0$, and the genuine joint
witness $0=(0,0,0)$ agrees with both on that coordinate.
Therefore ARC-PIN-HEREDITY120's condition holds for
\emph{all} 27 possible coordinate partial pins, not just
the selected path. But Theorem~\ref{th:iff}(B) fails.

Build real source-preserving levels, appending clauses on the
\emph{same} names and fresh noncolliding auxiliaries:
\[
\begin{array}{ccl}
F_0&=&D\land R,\\
F_1&=&D\land R\land\operatorname{PIN}(\neg z),\\
F_2&=&D\land R\land\operatorname{PIN}(\neg z)
        \land\operatorname{BINARY}(x\lor y).
\end{array}
\]
All original clauses and PIN/BINARY expansions have \emph{three
distinct literals on distinct variable names}; all are
fully ordinary proper-3CNF with accurate complete headers.
The SAT/UNSAT truth and the \emph{incorrectly reused} quotient
labels are
\[
\begin{array}{lccccc}
\toprule
 &\text{variables}&\text{clauses}&
 \text{serialized ASCII bytes}&\SAT(F_i)&\widetilde J_h(F_i)\\
\midrule
 F_0 &9 &12&133&1&1\\
 F_1 &11&16&182&1&1\\
 F_2 &12&18&201&0&1\\
\bottomrule
\end{array}
\]
The genuine joint word $0$ survives $F_1$ but is excluded by
$x\lor y$ in $F_2$. The exclusive pair $(2,1)$ survives both
restrictions and has a colliding quotient label. This is a
\textbf{second-level false SAT} with \emph{no} source-name
reinterpretation and \emph{no} free input or oracle labels.
Both descendant strings are \emph{larger}, not $N^\lambda$
power-short with $\lambda<1$. Consequently this construction
only falsifies a semantic-inheritance shortcut; it does
\emph{not} instantiate an ARC contractor.

\section{Acquisition firewall and fully charged machine ledger}
Even the tiny criterion (B) is extensional, not a free computable
object. Detecting one unsafe collision for \emph{given ordinary CNF
modules} is SAT-hard by a direct reduction: given arbitrary ordinary
proper-3CNF $F(U)$ use $k=1$ and
$D(y,U)=F(U)\land\operatorname{PIN}(\neg y)$,
$R(y)=\operatorname{PIN}(y)$, with a constant quotient.
If $F$ is satisfiable, $P=\{0\},Q=\{1\}$ create a
cross-exclusive collision; if $F$ is unsatisfiable, $P$
is empty and no violation exists. Ordinary proper ternary
PIN expansion and distinct variable-name rebasing are explicit
and have polynomial size. This is a recognition reduction,
\emph{not} an unconditional machine-time lower bound.

An arbitrary all-input solver $A$ may use Horn/SCC, affine
elimination, graph factorization, low-width DP, branches,
learning, caches, or direct decisions. A proposed fixed-$A$
certificate selector making $N^b$ calls to $A$ on queries
of real length $N^\rho$ pays at least the implementation's
corresponding \emph{upper-envelope} term
$N^{b+\rho t+o(1)}$ for worst-case guest-call budgeting,
plus actual query generation, source rereads, indexing,
control, failed attempts, changed and unchanged writes,
reconstruction and simultaneous ancestor/index/cache peaks.
For $\rho=1$ a parent-scale pinned SAT lookahead budgets
$N^{b+t+o(1)}$, which does \emph{not} certify the favorable
$\lambda t$ contraction term.

For an \emph{already proved exact} all-input policy, the
standard eager conditional accounting is
\[
t'\le\max\{r,a+\lambda+g,a+\lambda t\},\qquad
s'\le\max\{\lambda,\lambda s,\eta,\kappa\};
\]
the literal fully virtual repeated-probe simulation has
\[
t'\le\max\{r,a+\lambda t+g\},\qquad
s'\le\max\{\lambda s,\eta,\kappa\}.
\]
Actual construction time, accessors for \emph{two} levels,
ancestors, policy state and available preprocessed summaries
must be accounted for in one realized deterministic machine.
These display equations are conditional \emph{upper bounds}
for specified simulations, not universal lower bounds.
Logical temporal flips, physical circuit hardware switches,
and bit-time are distinct resources from total bit steps and
FLMV peak counted space; this finite semantic test measures
none of those physical resources. The support certificate
does not yield a model-preserving FLMV contradiction.
