\section{The two-Hall theorem}

(\forall S\in H)\ |S\cap D|=1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
(\forall d\in D)\ \deg_H(d)=1.
\]
Then for every $u\in\{0,1\}^{D}$ the pinned paired NAE network has a source-consistent extension to $Z$:
\[
\operatorname{proj}_D\SAT(H_{\rm NAE})=\{0,1\}^D.
\]
Furthermore $F$ is SAT, and two bipartite matchings produce a named satisfying assignment in uniform polynomial bit work and polynomial writable/index space. Necessarily
\[
|D|=|H|=|Z|=3q,\quad k=6q,\quad m=8q.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every $d\in D$ has one positive and one negative paired occurrence by (G), hence one positive unmatched and one negative unmatched occurrence. Form the bipartite incidence graph between the $2q$ \emph{signed, distinct} clauses $P'\sqcup Q'$ and the source names $D$. Each clause has degree three and each $d$ has degree two. For every subset $A$ of these clauses, $3|A|\le 2|N(A)|$. Hall's theorem therefore supplies a distinct representative $d_C$ for every clause $C$. Set that original bit TRUE when $C$ is positive and FALSE when $C$ is negative. No conflicting demands occur because representatives are distinct. Fill unselected $D$ bits arbitrarily. All unmatched clauses are satisfied.

Now take \emph{any} fixed boundary assignment, not necessarily the preceding one. By (G) every paired support $S\in H$ has precisely two internal names in $Z$. Every $z\in Z$ occurs in precisely two paired supports because it occurs in no unmatched clause and has exactly two occurrences per polarity. In the incidence graph of supports $H$ versus $Z$, each left node has degree two and each right node degree two. Every left subset $B$ satisfies $2|B|\le2|N(B)|$, so a matching selects one distinct internal name $z_S$ per support. If $d_S$ is the unique exposed name in $S$, assign $z_S=1-u(d_S)$; fill unselected $Z$ names arbitrarily. Each paired triple contains the opposite-valued pair $(d_S,z_S)$, so its positive and negative clauses both hold. Since matches are injective, original-name identity is respected and no bit is assigned inconsistently. This proves both full-cube extension and compatibility with the unmatched witness above.

Each of the $q$ positive unmatched triples uses three distinct boundary names, each of positive unmatched degree one, so $|D|=3q$. The two sides of (G) count exactly one exposed name per paired support and one paired support per exposed name, giving $|H|=|D|$. Two internal incidences per paired support versus two paired incidences per internal name give $|Z|=|H|$. Thus $k=6q$, $m=2(|H|+q)=8q$. Standard augmenting-path or Hopcroft--Karp matching is constructive in polynomial time; all grammar validation, supports, graph construction, failed augmentations, original-name assignments, and a final full-clause replay are ordinary paid operations.
\end{proof}
