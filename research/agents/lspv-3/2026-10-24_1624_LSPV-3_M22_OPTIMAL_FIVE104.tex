\section{Exact scope, sources, and falsifiable questions}

\section{Degree-two four-triple completion}
Let $V=\{1,\ldots,6\}$ and $\mathfrak B$ be the set of four-element families of distinct three-subsets of $V$ in which every vertex occurs exactly twice. A triple $T$ denotes the TRUE-name set of a weight-three assignment. For a triple family $H$, put $H^\perp=\{V\setminus T:T\in H\}$. Since each of four triples has three names, $H\in\mathfrak B$ implies $H^\perp\in\mathfrak B$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact pair extension; PROVED/EXACT]\label{lem:pair}
For any two distinct triples $U,W\subseteq V$, write $r=|U\cap W|\in\{0,1,2\}$. Exactly $9$, $3$, or $1$ members of $\mathfrak B$ contain both $U$ and $W$ when $r=0,1,2$, respectively. In the $r=2$ case the unique completion is invariant under $\perp$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
When $r=2$, relabel $U=abc$, $W=abd$, with two unused vertices $e,f$. Vertices $a,b$ already have degree two, $c,d$ degree one, and $e,f$ degree zero. The only possible remaining triples are $cef$ and $def$. Thus the unique completion is $\{abc,abd,cef,def\}$, whose triples form two complementary pairs.
When $r=1$, write $U=abc$, $W=ade$, with unused $f$. The remaining triples must both contain $f$, and their remaining four positions partition $\{b,c,d,e\}$ into two unordered pairs. There are three such partitions.
When $r=0$, the first two triples partition all six names. The other two must be a different unordered partition of $V$ into complementary triples, to avoid duplicated clauses. There are $\binom63/2-1=9$ choices. Each construction meets the degree-two condition; there are no additional completions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Optimal fixed balanced-witness number, PROVED/EXACT]\label{th:five}
For any specified variable $v\in V$ and specified bit $b\in\{0,1\}$, \emph{every} set of five distinct weight-three assignments with $x_v=b$ contains a satisfying assignment of \emph{every} $F\in\CM$ on six active names. Conversely, for \emph{every} set $W$ of at most four distinct weight-three assignments, there exists a valid $F_W\in\CS$ on six active names such that \emph{all} assignments in $W$ falsify $F_W$. Thus the minimum size of a universal \emph{source-independent, nonadaptive, balanced} candidate list is exactly five, even when the adversary is restricted to $\CS$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $P,Q\in\mathfrak B$ for the positive and negative unsigned clause supports. A balanced assignment $T$ violates a positive clause precisely if $T\in P^\perp$, and violates a negative clause precisely if $T\in Q$. Every vertex occurs twice in each of $P^\perp$ and $Q$. The same is true for the number of triples \emph{omitting} any fixed vertex, because each family has four triples. Hence each of the two forbidden families contains exactly two weight-three words with $x_v=b$. Their union contains at most four such words. Every selection of five therefore contains a satisfying word.

For the converse extend an arbitrary list with fewer than four distinct candidates to four arbitrary distinct weight-three words $T_1,T_2,T_3,T_4$. If some pair has intersection at most one, partition the four words into two pairs so that the second pair has intersection at most one. Choose any balanced four-triple family $A$ extending the first pair. By Lemma~\ref{lem:pair}, at least three distinct balanced families extend the second pair; choose one $B$ with $B\ne A^\perp$. Set positive supports $P=A^\perp$ and negative supports $Q=B$. Every $T_i$ belongs to either $A=P^\perp$ or $B=Q$, so all four fail the resulting formula. Each sign support family is balanced, signed clauses are unique, and $P\ne Q$, so the formula is in $\CS$.

The remaining case has $|T_i\cap T_j|=2$ for \emph{all} six unordered pairs. Split the four words into any two pairs and let $A,B$ be their unique balanced completions. Lemma~\ref{lem:pair} gives $A^\perp=A$ and $B^\perp=B$. They cannot be identical: if $A=B$, all four $T_i$ would be the four edges of one balanced degree-two family, whose sum of six pairwise intersections is
\[
\sum_{\{E,E'\}\subseteq A}|E\cap E'|
=\sum_{v\in V}\binom{\deg_A(v)}2=6,
\]
but by hypothesis it is $6\cdot2=12$. Thus $A\ne B=A^\perp$; the same choice $P=A^\perp$, $Q=B$ is valid and nonpaired, and defeats the four words.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Five source-oblivious tests per fixed bit]
For each source name $v$, each value $b$, and any five distinct balanced words realizing $x_v=b$, a valid six-name source has at least one satisfying word on the selected list. For instance $\{123,124,125,126,134\}$ is a universal five-test family, now with a symbolic proof. A four-test family cannot work uniformly even with the canonical \emph{nonpaired} promise.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from Theorem~\ref{th:five}.
\end{proof}
