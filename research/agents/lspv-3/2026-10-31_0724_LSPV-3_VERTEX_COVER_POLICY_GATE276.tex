\section*{Exact theorem: trivial decision and NP-hard optimal selection}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be any simple undirected graph with $q=|V|$, and let $r=q+1$. For each graph edge $e=\{u,v\}$ and each $j\in\{1,\ldots,r\}$ introduce a private Boolean $z_{e,j}$ and the positive proper clause
\[
 C_{e,j}=(x_u\lor x_v\lor z_{e,j}).
\]
Add three entirely fresh variables $a,b,c$. Define $P_G$ as the conjunction of all $C_{e,j}$. The two original-source formulas are
\[
 F_G^+=P_G\land(a\lor b\lor c),\qquad
 F_G^-=P_G\land\bigwedge_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^3}
 (a^{\epsilon_1}\lor b^{\epsilon_2}\lor c^{\epsilon_3}),
\]
where $v^0=v$ and $v^1=\neg v$. Both are proper 3CNF with $n=q+r|E|+3$ variables; they have $r|E|+1$ or $r|E|+8$ clauses respectively. Let $\tau(G)$ be the minimum size of a graph vertex cover.

\begin{theorem}[Matched-label optimal strong-Krom acquisition, restricted exact]
For every $G$,
\[
\operatorname{SAT}(F_G^+)=\mathrm{YES},\quad
\operatorname{SAT}(F_G^-)=\mathrm{NO},\quad
\beta_{\rm Krom}(F_G^+)=\beta_{\rm Krom}(F_G^-)=\tau(G)+1.
\]
Consequently deciding whether the minimum strong Krom backdoor of an \emph{all-positive proper 3CNF} is at most $k$ is NP-complete, even though SAT on that input class has the all-TRUE witness. The same optimal-backdoor NP-hardness is present under either promised polarity of the authenticated guard, although the two labels have simple direct certificates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The assignment $1^n$ satisfies $F_G^+$. Every assignment to $(a,b,c)$ falsifies exactly one of the eight signed guard clauses of $F_G^-$, so it is UNSAT. Original-source Resolution combines each pair of guard clauses differing only in $c$ into four two-clauses, then in $b$ into two units, and finally in $a$ into the empty clause: exactly seven source-indexed resolution steps, independent of $G$.

Take a minimum vertex cover $U\subseteq V$. The set $B=\{x_v:v\in U\}\cup\{a\}$ intersects every source three-clause in either formula, and hence is a strong Krom backdoor; $\beta\le\tau(G)+1\le q+1$. Conversely let $B$ be a minimum strong Krom backdoor, $|B|\le q+1$. Its guard clauses force it to include at least one of $\{a,b,c\}$. Suppose for some edge $e=\{u,v\}$ neither $x_u$ nor $x_v$ belongs to $B$. Because the $r=q+1$ private variables $z_{e,1},\ldots,z_{e,r}$ occur in distinct clauses, hitting all $r$ clauses requires selecting all $r$, in addition to at least one guard variable. This entails $|B|\ge q+2$, a contradiction. Thus $\{v:x_v\in B\}$ is a vertex cover and $|B|\ge\tau(G)+1$. Equality follows.

The reduction from \textsc{Vertex Cover} maps $(G,t)$ to the all-positive $F_G^+$ with threshold $t+1$, with $O(q|E|)$ variables and clauses. A strong Krom backdoor is verifiable in polynomial time using the clausewise hitting criterion. Since \textsc{Vertex Cover} is NP-complete \cite{karp1972}, the acquisition decision problem is NP-complete. The NP-hardness is only of \emph{optimal backdoor acquisition}, not of solving these SAT or UNSAT formulas. \qed
\end{proof}
