\section{Source construction and channel contract}
For $n\ge1$, form the Boolean variables $x_i,y_i,z_i,w_i$ for $1\le i\le n$, with four proper ternary clauses per coordinate:
\[
 (\neg x_i\vee y_i\vee z_i),\quad
 (\neg x_i\vee y_i\vee\neg z_i),\quad
 (x_i\vee\neg y_i\vee w_i),\quad
 (x_i\vee\neg y_i\vee\neg w_i).
\]
Their conjunction $E_n$ has $4n$ distinct variables and $4n$ proper clauses. It is satisfied exactly when $x_i=y_i$ for all $i$, independently of $z_i,w_i$. Its explicit fixed-record bit length is $\Theta(n\log(n+2))$.

A connected modification $E_n^{\rm conn}$ adds a fresh guard $g$, forced TRUE by the four clauses $(g\vee\pm a\vee\pm b)$ with two private auxiliaries. It also adds $(g\vee x_1\vee y_1)$ and $(g\vee x_i\vee x_{i+1})$ for $1\le i<n$. The guard makes each connector semantically vacuous but makes the genuine clause-incidence graph connected. The connected unpinned family has $4n+3$ variables and $5n+4$ clauses.

For matched labels add the unit constraints $x_1=0$ and $y_1=b$, where $b=0$ or $1$, using four proper ternaries and two fresh auxiliaries per unit. Both connected labeled variants have $4n+7$ variables and $5n+12$ clauses with the same unsigned supports and fixed-record length. The $b=0$ member is SAT and has $2^{n-1}$ allowed $(x,y)$ pairs; the $b=1$ member is UNSAT.

\paragraph{ONE-WAY COMPLETE-VERIFICATION CONTRACT.}
A fixed deterministic validator sees the source formula as fixed/read-only metadata and receives an \emph{arbitrary} candidate assignment via one nonrewindable transcript. The bit order is
\[
 x_1,\ldots,x_n,\ y_1,\ldots,y_n,\ z_1,\ldots,z_n,\ w_1,\ldots,w_n,
\]
followed by the bounded guard/pin auxiliaries when present. It must output ACCEPT if and only if the complete proposed assignment satisfies the fixed source. It has no independent random-access copy, no source/witness advice indexed by the candidate, and may not restart the transcript. Every mutable controller, head, address, saved bit, and state descriptor is counted. This \emph{complete} verifier contract is deliberately stronger than the sound partial-witness acceptance needed by MOVINGCOVER1.

\section{Exact streaming-space and cumulative-bit-time theorem}
For a particular execution let $B_j$ be the number of complete live writable-state bits just after all $x$ bits and the first $j$ $y$ bits have arrived, $0\le j<n$. The state includes controller and address metadata and has an injective finite binary encoding. No uncharged side channel stores the prefix.

\begin{theorem}[One-way exact-verification firewall, PROVED/EXACT in its declared channel]
On the unpinned $E_n$ or $E_n^{\rm conn}$, every deterministic exact one-pass complete witness validator has some input with peak live writable space at least $n$ bits. Moreover, averaged uniformly over the $2^n$ accepting transcripts with $x=y=u$ and fixed valid auxiliary bits,
\[
 {\mathbb E}_u\left[\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}B_j\right]\ \ge\
 \sum_{h=1}^{n}\max\{0,h-2\}
 =\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}2.
\]
Consequently some SAT-valid candidate has cumulative bit-time $\Omega(n^2)$, even if all other processing between checkpoints is omitted from that metric. These are not all-input SAT decision lower bounds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After the $x$ prefix $u$ is read, distinct $u,v\in\{0,1\}^n$ must yield distinguishable complete states: append the same $y=u$ and fixed valid auxiliary suffix. The transcript with initial $x=u$ must be accepted and that with initial $x=v\ne u$ rejected. There are $2^n$ distinct prefix states; even permitting variable-length binary state encodings, at least one needs $n$ bits.

For bit-time fix $j$, a common $j$-bit prefix $p$, and the continuation family $x=pq,\ y$-prefix $p$ where $q$ ranges over $h=n-j$ free suffixes. If two distinct $q,q'$ led to the same post-prefix complete state, appending $y$-suffix $q$ would make the complete validator accept $pq,pq$ and reject $pq',pq$, contradiction. Thus $2^h$ suffixes give $2^h$ distinct state encodings. Among all finite binary strings fewer than $2^k$ have length strictly below $k$. For $h\ge3$ an injection of $2^h$ equidistributed suffixes into complete-state strings therefore has mean encoded length at least $h-2$ (indeed at least $h-2+(h+1)/2^h$). This bound holds for every fixed $p$ and hence after averaging over uniformly random $u$. Sum over $j$ and use linearity of expectation. The cumulative bit-time includes at least these designated checkpoints. The connected guard has fixed satisfying auxiliary values and does not alter any indistinguishability argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Same-execution time--space consequence, PROVED/EXACT restricted]
Let $U(u)$ be the total charged elementary work on a complete accepting witness and $S(u)$ its peak live writable-bit count, in the same declared one-way verifier. For some accepting diagonal $u$,
\[
 (U(u)+1)S(u)\ \ge\ BT(u)\ \ge\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}2.
\]
This is a same-input product bound for \emph{complete witness verification}, not a bound for an arbitrary SAT decider. It follows directly from $BT=\sum_tB_t\le(U+1)\max_tB_t$ and the preceding theorem. For explicit source length $N=\Theta(n\log n)$ it gives only this restricted model's $\Omega(N^2/\log^2N)$ work--space product.
\end{lemma}

For the SAT-pinned member $b=0$, fixing the common first coordinate reduces the prefix fooling set to $2^{n-1}$, giving the analogous peak lower bound $n-1$ bits. The UNSAT-pinned member is recognizable from two inconsistent pins and needs no candidate verification. This both-label control explicitly blocks a false inference of universally costly UNSAT decisions.

\begin{lemma}[Online reordering is not free, PROVED/EXACT for the fixed channel]
An exact deterministic one-way transducer receiving grouped $x_1\cdots x_n y_1\cdots y_n$ and emitting the interleaved stream $x_1y_1x_2y_2\cdots x_ny_n$ in order, with no rereads or source-dependent side information, requires at least $n-1$ writable state bits for some source, even if its input/output heads and local arithmetic are otherwise unrestricted.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Before the first $y$ bit is received, the output cannot have passed the first $x_1$ (the next output bit is $y_1$). Thus it has emitted at most $x_1$, and must be capable of faithfully emitting all the remaining $x_2,\ldots,x_n$ after seeing the $y$ suffix. For fixed $x_1$, the $2^{n-1}$ possible remaining prefixes must induce distinct retained states, by appending the same $y$ suffix. The canonical state-counting argument requires at least $n-1$ bits at some cut.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Adaptive escape and sharp model boundary.}
If the generator itself emits $x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2,\ldots$, an exact equality checker uses only one saved data bit plus $O(\log n)$ controller bits. If the generator instead allows cheap random-access recomputation of any witness bit, it can compare the two bits for each coordinate in $O(\log n)$ live bits, paying the generator for every requested bit. If the source is directly recognizable, SCC or the equality quotient decides the fixed 2-CNF/proper-3CNF family in polynomial time without validating an arbitrary witness at all. An arbitrary sound selector may accept only selected cheaply verifiable witnesses rather than implementing complete verification on every candidate. Consequently neither theorem is a universal obstruction against arbitrary state-adaptive SAT deciders.

\section{BIT-RESOURCE1 accounting and ARC/FLMV transport}
Let $N=\Theta(n\log(n+2))$ be full encoded source length. In this one-way verification channel, the proven peak floor is $\Omega(N/\log N)$ writable bits, and cumulative bit-time is $\Omega(N^2/\log^2 N)$ on some SAT witness. Each transcript bit read contributes charged primitive work; writes, even unchanged-value writes, are charged. Actual complete-state flips are separately counted and may differ from the number of writes: the all-zero candidate can produce zero \emph{data-cell} flips, but the complete machine's addressing/control flips cannot be ignored.

The fixed-channel constraints do not contradict FLMV: the linear-space validator lies outside its small-workspace region, while the alternative direct SAT decision is efficient because the input family is restricted. Neither cumulative bit-time nor temporal flip count is an FLMV time or peak-space coordinate. A conversion of this witness verifier into an all-input small-space selector would require a new source-, policy-, and representation-preserving construction and a correct witness-order/bit-access contract, including all read-only-input and output-buffer distinctions. \paragraph{DUPLICATION / CONTRADICTION AUDIT.}
The state-counting method is elementary deterministic one-way equality communication, not newly invented; MOVINGCOVER1 already gives pointwise trial scheduling and BIT-LEDGER-CEILING120 already separates complete-state flips from work and establishes the $LB$ global Lipschitz ceiling. The exact novelty in this project run is a fixed explicit \emph{proper 3CNF} source, connected matching SAT/UNSAT variants, a complete one-way witness-output channel, an averaged quadratic \emph{bit-time} ledger, and the resource cost of changing output order. This does not refute prior source-aware upper algorithms or supply mandatory full-witness extraction.
