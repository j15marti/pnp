\section{Uniform state and genuine residual semantics}
A residual state is a source-linked tuple
\[
z=(F,\rho,\text{source index},\text{derived clauses and reasons},\text{representation},
\text{memo},\text{branch stack},\text{policy history},\text{work ledger}).
\]
Its measured, possibly incomplete, parameter vector is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa(z)=(&N_z,n_z,m_z,w_z,\mathrm{Horn},\mathrm{dualHorn},\mathrm{Krom},\mathrm{XOR},\mathrm{strict22},q_z,\\
&\text{discovered interfaces},\text{failed trials},\text{source reads},\text{live bits}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $q_z=\bot$ outside the actual monotone exact-(2,2) grammar; it is not silently set to zero. A recognizer's runtime and storage are not included for free. The controller tests endpoint witnesses, Horn and dual-Horn closure, 2SAT implication SCC, syntactically complete three-variable XOR blocks with GF(2) elimination, one source-verified canonical signed22 witness grammar, and ordinary UP-DPLL with several branching rules. The fallback is complete even when every advertised structure fails. SAT is accepted only after checking the full source witness. UNSAT comes from exact restricted deciders or complete UP-DPLL refutation trees; a failed repair attempt alone has no UNSAT authority.

For fixed partial assignment $\rho$, forward restriction and backward Boolean evaluation have the exact recurrence
\[
V(F\mid\rho)=\begin{cases}
1&\text{all residual clauses are satisfied},\\
0&\text{an original clause is falsified},\\
V(F\mid\rho,x=0)\vee V(F\mid\rho,x=1)&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
A SAT branch can terminate after one YES child; an UNSAT branch must establish both NO children. Canonical \emph{syntactic} residual memoization is safe for decision, but discovering all its states costs the actual forward restrictions and backward joins. The resulting memo count is recorded \emph{after the fact}, never provided to the selector as an oracle.

\section{An exact policy-acquisition lower bound in a narrow model}
For a fixed instance $F$, define $H_{B,\mathcal{P}}(F)$ to mean that none of the $m$ listed certified partial methods outputs a verified decision within $B$ charged steps. This is a portfolio-relative, budget-relative \emph{operational} residual predicate, not ordinary SAT hardness.

\begin{theorem}[Opaque finite-policy minimax, PROVED/EXACT restricted]\label{thm:opaque}
Consider $m$ opaque procedures with no visible program text or source-derived shortcut. Before its $B$-th allocated primitive step, a procedure produces only an identical silent transcript. The interface permits a procedure either to produce a checked terminal answer on its $B$-th step or to remain silent past that step. Any deterministic scheduler that must distinguish $H_{B,\mathcal P}$ from its negation in every admissible world needs at least $mB$ allocated procedure steps in the all-silent world. Likewise, a scheduler guaranteed to discover the sole successful index in the worst case needs at least $mB$ steps. Round-robin achieves this bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If one index $i$ receives fewer than $B$ steps, the all-silent transcript is identical to an admissible world in which precisely $i$ answers at its $B$-th step. Thus no correct decision about $H_{B,\mathcal P}$ is possible. Every index must receive $B$ steps, totalling $mB$. For the unique-winner promise, choose the index whose $B$-th allocated step is last; an adversarial but fixed hidden winner yields the same bound. Round-robin assigns $B$ steps to all $m$ procedures in $mB$ total steps.
\end{proof}
\noindent\emph{Scope firewall.} A solver that reads the original formula, candidate code or semantic proof of a good method may bypass this interface. The theorem is \textbf{not} a time lower bound for SAT, a universal parameter discovery lower bound, or a restriction on arbitrary $A$. It also does not presume access to a free SAT/UNSAT bit: for an instantiated SAT source, a terminal procedure must compute its source-correct answer and pay its own verification cost. Universal search and provably correct program scheduling are classical; Hutter's proof-search scheduler has its own proof-acquisition costs, program-dependent constants and no automatic FLMV low-space transfer.

\begin{theorem}[Charged geometric finite portfolio, restricted constructive upper bound]\label{thm:budget}
Let $m$ public, \emph{sound} partial decision methods execute under an indexed word-RAM step simulator with fixed read-only input. Each either returns a source-authenticated result or \texttt{UNRESOLVED}. For a declared writable-bit cap $H$, an exact finite geometric restart scheduler tests each method at budgets $1,2,4,\ldots,2^J$, aborting an over-budget or over-space run and discarding its state. If some method finishes with checked output within $t\le 2^J$ steps and at most $H$ bits, a result is returned within $4mt+O(m\log t)+G+V$ charged steps, with $H+S_G+O(\log(mt))$ writable bits plus simulator overhead. Here $G$ is explicit method enumeration/generation cost and $V$ any output checking not already charged in $t$. After an unsuccessful final stage, calling a complete SAT fallback gives an exact all-input decider but \emph{no claimed global $H$-space or polynomial-time guarantee}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $j=\lceil\log_2 t\rceil$. The successful method finishes by stage $j$, after at most $m\sum_{a=0}^j2^a<m2^{j+1}\le4mt$ simulated steps. Every failed attempt, source reinitialization and replay is included in the bounded run's charged steps. One candidate is resident at a time, so no sum of candidate workspaces is claimed. The method generator, checking and scheduler counters are additional explicit costs. A sound returned result is correct, and after the bounded trial period the total exact fallback establishes both SAT and UNSAT correctness. The limit on $H$ is a \emph{real bit-cap condition of the abstract theorem}; the Python benchmark below implements time slicing only, not a FLMV machine bit-cap.
\end{proof}

\section{Precisely conditional ARC/FLMV consequence}
Let $A$ be an arbitrary fixed uniform total exact ordinary-SAT decider in the FLMV indexed read-only-input random-access Turing model, with all-input simultaneous exponents $(K,L)$. The unconstructed ARC contract seeks a fully source-authenticated $\Phi$ with
\[
K'\le\max(\alpha,\gamma+\lambda K),\qquad
L'\le\max(\sigma,\lambda L),
\]
including all child descriptions, source queries, per-child policy discovery, retries, retention and output. It is \textbf{OPEN} whether such a transformer exists, especially with $\lambda<1$ on every ordinary SAT input. The finite selector theorem above does not construct it.

\paragraph{Conditional portfolio tax.}
If a future correct all-input $\Phi$ uses $N^\gamma$ children, each of physical length at most $N^\lambda$, and each child requires an opaque $m(N)=N^{\eta+o(1)}$-candidate geometric selector whose fastest child continuation has time $(N^\lambda)^{K+o(1)}$, with native simulation overhead already absorbed and no larger discovery term, then this \emph{particular architecture} has the amended time recurrence
\[
K'\le\max\bigl(\alpha,\gamma+\eta+\lambda K\bigr).
\]
For $(\alpha,\gamma,\lambda,\sigma)=(11/10,3/5,1/2,1/2)$ the formal fixed-point pair becomes
\[
(K_*,L_*)=(6/5+2\eta,\;1/2)\quad(\eta\ge0).
\]
FLMV Theorem 5.7 (the \emph{actual} forbidden curve, not a $t+s$ substitute) has, at $c=241/200$, certified optimum $k=2$ and
\[
d_{\rm FLMV}(c)=\frac{11642479}{23232400},\qquad
 d_{\rm FLMV}(c)-\tfrac12=\frac{26279}{23232400}>0.
\]
Strict interior for this formally fixed box requires $6/5+2\eta<241/200$, hence
\[
\boxed{\eta<1/400.}
\]
At equality the needed strict time margin disappears. Recomputing the rational FLMV recurrence and certifying the infinite $k$-tail is done by the provided script. The bound on $\eta$ is inherited from the already-verified project contract; the gain in this run is its connection to an explicit opaque-selector lower bound and a finite uniformly correct controller. This is \textbf{CONDITIONAL}, not a proof that any ARC exists or that every selector must incur an $N^\eta$ penalty. Native FLMV tape simulation, arbitrary-program access, child bit-addressability, full-parent $A(F)$ escapes, and retained space are still missing obligations.

\section{Adversarial auditing, independent checks and source fidelity}
The original-clause adversarial control stores, for every signed occurrence $x_{i,j}$, its local all-literals-true $S_j$ value, local all-literals-false $U_j$ value and both inversions, with original clause index. Four compatibility modes $SS,SU,US,UU$ are derived by exact shared-variable sign consistency, not mistaken for ordinary disjunctive SAT. Full coherent $0^n,1^n$, complements and progressive flips store $(N_0,N_1,N_2,N_3)$. The independent verifier rechecks pair feasibility by full enumeration on small sources, and for each clause verifies $t_j(\overline a)=|C_j|-t_j(a)$, specializing to $3-t_j(a)$ on proper three-clauses. No local $S_j$ targets are combined as if their occurrences were distinct variables.

The separate verifier completed 132 primary/random case checks, 1,452 individual policy executions, 10,612 exhaustively checked original-clause pair modes, and 1,968,500 original-clause complement identities. All tested SAT/UNSAT answers agreed with complete truth tables for $n\le15$; all larger reported SAT cases have verified source assignments. The independently recomputed FLMV rational and semantic forward/reverse tables agreed. Seeds and full primary source clauses are in the executable Python file, with raw per-method traces, source parameters, certificates, time and peak storage in JSON. The verification code does \emph{not} implement a proof kernel for the arbitrary-decider ARC hypothesis.
