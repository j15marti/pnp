\begin{abstract}
P versus NP remains \OPEN.  This run attacks a natural repair after terminal,
causal, and information-flow traces failed: charge every SAT decider for the
logical erasures or entropy production needed to compress a large clean-core
compatibility object to one bit.  The repair is false in the logical
computation model.  Every acyclic Boolean computation with $G$ gates has an
explicit compute--copy--uncompute realization with $G$ clean ancillas,
$2G+1$ reversible gates, the same one-bit answer, restored input and
ancillas, and no noninjective internal transition.  Bennett's reversible
simulation theorem supplies the corresponding uniform machine-level result.
Thus a hypothetical uniform polynomial-time SAT decider can be wrapped as a
uniform polynomial-time logically reversible SAT decider without exposing
$H(F)$ or contradicting any unconditional bound.  Landauer's physical heat
principle does not repair the transfer: initialization, output reset, noise,
and physical realization are extra model assumptions, and zero logical
erasure is not a claim of zero physical energy.  A bounded audit passed
293,813 assertions over eight control families, six wrappers, four encodings,
and sizes $1\le n\le8$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Barrier-transfer literature preflight}
The two load-bearing obstacles are (i) forcing every extensionally correct
uniform SAT decider to incur a representation-independent internal charge,
and (ii) transferring that charge to an unconditional lower bound in the
same computational realization.  Primary literature was checked before the
new construction.

Bennett proves that every standard one-tape Turing machine has a reversible
deterministic three-tape simulator which leaves input and output intact
\cite{Bennett1973}.  This is a \emph{directly transferable theorem}: the
objects are deterministic computations and reversible deterministic
computations.  Lange--McKenzie--Tapp later prove reversible space equals
deterministic space \cite{LMT2000}; this is also direct at the complexity-class
level, but is not needed for the simple polynomial-overhead conclusion here.
Landauer relates logical irreversibility to physical heat generation
\cite{Landauer1961}.  That is a \emph{useful structural analogy requiring a
new transfer theorem} for this project: a physical device, thermodynamic
protocol, temperature, error tolerance, initialization, and reset policy are
not part of an arbitrary uniform Turing decider.  Importing a VLSI,
communication, or thermodynamic lower bound without that correspondence is a
\emph{misleading analogy / failed transfer}.

The familiar relativization \cite{BGS1975}, Natural Proofs \cite{RR1997}, and
algebrization \cite{AW2009} barriers remain relevant: a generic black-box
wrapper supplies no nonrelativizing or nonalgebrizing hinge and no circuit
lower bound.

\section{Exact object, quantifiers, encoding, and attacked target}
Let $x=\langle F,\alpha\rangle\in\{0,1\}^N$ be a self-delimiting encoding of
a 3-CNF $F$ and a partial assignment $\alpha$, and define
\[
 H(F,\alpha)=\{\beta:\alpha\cup\beta\models F\},\qquad
 h(F,\alpha)={\bf1}[H(F,\alpha)\ne\varnothing].
\]
The clean-core class includes well-formed hard instances and the required
Horn, 2SAT, affine/Tseitin, bounded-treewidth, independent-product, padded,
redundant-clause, and alternate-encoding controls.  Fix an arbitrary uniform
total deterministic decider $A$ for the one-bit predicate $h$.  If
$T_A(N)\le N^K$ for some fixed but unknown $K$, all preprocessing,
simulation, copying, uncomputation, input preservation, ancillas, and output
are charged in $N$.

The attacked universal implication is
\[
 \forall A\;[A(x)=h(x)]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists x\;[\text{$A$ must merge logical states or erase information in a
 way that certifies work specific to $H(x)$}].
\]
Its conclusion is implementation-sensitive.  Extensional correctness gives
only one answer bit and does not select a history or representation of $H$.

\section{Exact reversible construction}

\begin{theorem}[\claim{REVERSIBLE-COMPUTE-COPY-UNCOMPUTE1}]
Let $C:\{0,1\}^N\to\{0,1\}$ be an acyclic Boolean circuit with $G$ gates
from a fixed finite basis.  There is a circuit $R_C$ acting on the input,
$G$ zero ancillas, and one zero output bit such that
\[
 R_C(x,0^G,0)=(x,0^G,C(x)).
\]
Every gate of $R_C$ is a bijection on its full state space, $R_C$ has exactly
$2G+1$ reversible gates, and the transformation $C\mapsto R_C$ is uniform.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Topologically order the gates of $C$.  For gate $i$ computing the Boolean
function $g_i$ of already available source wires, allocate a fresh target
$z_i=0$ and apply
\[
 U_i:(s,z_i)\longmapsto (s,z_i\oplus g_i(s)).
\]
For each fixed $s$, $U_i$ is its own inverse, hence a bijection.  After
$U_1\cdots U_G$, the designated $z_G$ holds $C(x)$.  Apply one controlled-NOT
from $z_G$ to the fresh answer bit, then apply
$U_G\cdots U_1$.  The second pass restores all $z_i$ to zero while the copied
answer remains.  The input was never overwritten.  There are $G+1+G$ gates
and $G$ ancillas.  A streaming compiler enumerates the forward gates, the
copy, and the reverse list, so the transformation is uniform.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Counterfactual $P=NP$]
If a uniform deterministic SAT decider $A$ runs in polynomial time, then a
uniform deterministic logically reversible SAT decider $R[A]$ also runs in
polynomial time and maps initialized configurations as
$(x,0^m,0)\mapsto(x,0^m,A(x))$.  In a direct $G(N)$-gate unrolling the cost is
$2G(N)+1$ time and $G(N)$ ancillas; Bennett's theorem gives the corresponding
machine-level reversible simulation.  No logical-erasure lower bound can
therefore contradict the assumed decider.
\end{corollary}

This corollary does not suppress the unknown exponent.  If $G(N)=N^{K+O(1)}$
then $2G(N)+1=N^{K+O(1)}$.  Nor is the $G$-ancilla construction a space
optimality claim.  It is enough to falsify necessity of logical erasure while
accounting for all resources.

\section{SAME\_REALIZATION and one-bit output audit}
The construction is not a transcript from a different run.  The copied bit
is produced during the same realized forward computation whose inverse is
then executed.  After uncomputation, the realized state contains precisely
the unchanged input, clean work bits, and the one-bit answer.  Thus it meets
SAME\_REALIZATION while displaying no $H(F)$, satisfying assignment,
compatibility table, proof object, or canonical witness.

The restriction to initialized ancillas does not make an internal gate
irreversible: each $U_i$ is a permutation on \emph{all} bit strings.  Loading
an input, supplying clean ancillas, reading the answer, resetting a device for
reuse, and maintaining reliability may have physical cost.  Those costs are
external boundary conditions, not solver-independent evidence that the run
materialized $H(F)$.  Landauer therefore supplies no model-matched SAT lower
bound here.

\section{Hostile controls and attempted repairs}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.8cm,style=nextline]
\item[Horn, 2SAT, affine/Tseitin.] Their semantic shortcuts are compatible
with the theorem.  Gaussian elimination or implication propagation can be
embedded reversibly just like any other Boolean computation.
\item[Bounded treewidth and products.] Compact dynamic programming and
componentwise decisions do not force erasure; their gates also compute,
copy, and uncompute.
\item[Padding and redundant clauses.] They may enlarge $N$ or semantic state
descriptions without enlarging the decision information.  Reversibility
survives all such encodings and gives no lower bound from state count.
\item[Wrappers and alternate representations.] DEAD, COPY, DELAY, INTERP,
and RECOMP change histories and charged constants but not the reversible
compiler.  Hence the claim fails under extensionally equivalent programs.
\item[Compact circuits/branching programs.] A lower bound restricted to a
representation class remains a structural lower bound.  It does not transfer
to every uniform decider without an additional compiler theorem preserving
the charged resource.
\item[Physical dissipation.] A theorem charging device reset or noisy
operation needs a fixed physical model and a lower bound in that model.  It
would still have to imply an ordinary uniform-computation contradiction.
This arrow is absent.
\end{description}

\section{Barrier audit}
The proof is syntactic and relativizes: an oracle call can be included as a
reversible query gate that preserves the query while XORing the reply into a
target.  It therefore provides no route around BGS.  It neither arithmetizes
the SAT computation nor proves a lower bound, so it supplies no response to
algebrization.  It defines no large constructive property of Boolean
functions and supplies no Natural-Proofs evasion.  Uniformity is preserved;
nonuniform circuit existence is not promoted to a uniform algorithm.

The arbitrary exponent $K$ remains on every simulated path.  The
transformation multiplies time by a polynomially bounded overhead but never
contracts $K$ to a model-independent forbidden exponent.  Time--space,
communication, VLSI, branching-program, and proof-system lower bounds apply
only after a theorem maps the same realized decider to the restricted model
with all costs preserved.  No such theorem is proved.

\section{Handoff and single next target}
Seek an operational quantity $Q$ with all four properties:
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
 (1)& Q\text{ is defined for every uniform deterministic SAT realization};\\
 (2)& Q\text{ is invariant under compute--copy--uncompute and wrapper equivalence};\\
 (3)& Q\text{ is provably $H$-specific rather than source or answer transport};\\

 \end{array}
\]
At present (2)--(4) are jointly \OPEN.  Until they are proved, the universal
clean-core transfer is missing and P versus NP remains open.
