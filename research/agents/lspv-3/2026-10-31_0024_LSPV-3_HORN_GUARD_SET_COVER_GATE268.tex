\section{Exact source-level guarded Horn optimization theorem}
A \emph{definite Horn 3CNF} contains clauses of length at most three with exactly one positive head and zero or more negative premises. Let $H$ be such a formula, $D$ a set of positive unit assumptions, and $t$ a designated positive literal. The \emph{minimum-support acquisition problem} is, under the promise $H\wedge\bigwedge D\models t$, to determine whether some $G\subseteq D$ with $|G|\le k$ satisfies $H\wedge\bigwedge G\models t$. Neither $H$ nor $D$ is an oracle; they are explicit source clauses/assumption names. All source clauses are retained with their original indices. Horn implication is tested by UP on $H\wedge\bigwedge G\wedge\neg t$.
\begin{theorem}[Restricted minimum Horn guard is NP-complete]
The minimum-support acquisition decision problem is NP-complete, even when $H$ is satisfiable definite Horn 3CNF, each assumption variable occurs only as a negative premise in $H$, all $D$ together force $t$, and one valid (not necessarily shortest) support is immediately supplied by $D$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Membership in NP holds: guess the indices of $G$ and check $H\land G\land\neg t$ by polynomial-time Horn unit propagation. Reduce a Set Cover instance $(U=\{1,\ldots,m\},S_1,\ldots,S_s,k)$, assuming $m\ge2$ and $\bigcup_i S_i=U$ (uncovered elements can be detected in polynomial time and mapped to a fixed NO-instance satisfying the promise). Introduce assumptions $D=\{d_1,\ldots,d_s\}$, element variables $e_1,\ldots,e_m$, output $t$, and an AND chain of fresh $z$ variables. For every $u\in S_i$, include the original binary Horn clause $(\neg d_i\vee e_u)$. To force $t$ exactly when all $e_u$ are forced, include $(\neg e_1\vee\neg e_2\vee z_2)$, then $(\neg z_{j-1}\vee\neg e_j\vee z_j)$ for $3\le j<m$, and $(\neg z_{m-1}\vee\neg e_m\vee t)$; when $m=2$ use $(\neg e_1\vee\neg e_2\vee t)$. Every clause has at most three literals and exactly one positive head. The least Horn model of $H\land G$ forces $e_u$ exactly when some selected $d_i\in G$ covers $u$. It forces $t$ exactly when every element is covered. By completeness of the least-model method for definite Horn, $H\land G\models t$ iff the chosen sets cover $U$. Thus a guard of size at most $k$ exists iff the Set Cover instance has a cover of size at most $k$. The construction has $O(\sum_i|S_i|+m)$ original literal occurrences and is computable in polynomial time. $H$ is satisfiable by setting all variables false; $D$ forces $t$ because the full collection covers $U$. QED.
\end{proof}
\noindent The theorem concerns \emph{optimal acquisition}, not the existence, validation or use of an arbitrary sound guard. The common Set-Cover/abduction underlying reduction is classical (Karp 1972; Eiter--Gottlob 1995). A Horn SAT decider never needs to solve this optimization problem. Under a hypothetical $P=NP$, this NP-complete optimization would itself be polynomial, so it cannot be used to refute $P=NP$.
