\section{CONSTRUCTION OR PROOF ATTEMPT}
Ordinary Tseitin variables encode values of acyclic circuit gates. A global status variable denotes the genuine satisfiability of a closed formula and must not be conflated with an existential wire in that formula.
For fixed machine D, source F, and an explicit guaranteed time bound T, a functional circuit/tableau encoding of D(F) has size and compilation cost polynomial in |D|+|F|+T. If D is a hypothetical polynomial-time SAT decider this reifies SAT and UNSAT status in polynomial source size. Search-to-decision recovers a witness in at most n+1 decider calls, charging all restrictions and verification.
Define J_D(F) as the bounded computation formula forcing D(F)=0. For a correct D,
\[
\operatorname{SAT}(J_D(F))=1-\operatorname{SAT}(F),\quad
\operatorname{SAT}(J_D(J_D(F)))=\operatorname{SAT}(F).
\]
These are different closed formulas, not a contradiction or a fixed point.
\begin{theorem}
There is no total computable translation E from indices e of arbitrary programs to closed finite CNFs such that SAT(E(e)) equals the computed constant Boolean output of every total constant-output program e.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose E exists. The function g(e)=1-SAT(E(e)) is total computable because SAT of finite formulas is decidable. Produce effectively an index h(e) for a program that ignores its input and outputs g(e). Kleene's second recursion theorem yields e with computed function equal to that of h(e). Hence e computes a total constant function, so E must satisfy SAT(E(e))=g(e)=1-SAT(E(e)), contradiction.
\end{proof}
The theorem rejects the hypothetical unclocked universal quotation, not P=NP. An explicitly clocked compiler does exist; the recursion fixed point is not guaranteed to meet the chosen clock. A source-status map with no fixed point cannot be a realized family of genuine closed formulas with exactly those statuses, unless an independent realization theorem is supplied.
For the UWI quantifier audit, all 256 clause-subset CNFs over two variables (eight possible non-tautological clauses) include 95 satisfiable formulas. Every one of four fixed assignment programs fails on exactly 63 satisfiable inputs; the four-program portfolio succeeds on all 95. Thus individual failures do not produce a common failed input.
