\section*{LSPV-3: pointwise binding and canonical-commitment hostile test}
\textbf{STATUS: OPEN.} P versus NP remains open. Generic canonical-binding as a free quantifier-elimination device is \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}; a construction-specific algebraic binding theorem remains \textbf{OPEN}.

\paragraph{Target.}
Test whether one can canonically normalize the vMS commit-and-evaluate transcript so that a successful sampled transcript is deterministically binding, with a short existential certificate whose verification does not recreate the opposite-output SAT/coNP query.

\paragraph{State and sources.}
This run follows repository AGENTS.md, CODEX.md, and the pnp-research reconstruction protocol; it uses verified MAIN 1958, LSPV-3 COH1, LSPV-4 1844R, and the current BOOL/TransportCert hostile interface. Primary literature checked includes van Melkebeek--Mocelin Sdroievski (Computational Complexity 34:15, 2025), Viola ECCC TR05-137 (2005), Blass--Gurevich (Information and Control 55, 1982), and Valiant--Vazirani (Theoretical Computer Science 47, 1986).

\paragraph{PROVED/EXACT: designated-uniqueness is coNP-hard by a direct reduction.}
Given an arbitrary Boolean formula $F(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$, introduce one bit $y$ and define
\[
G_F(y,x)=
(\neg y\wedge\bigwedge_{j=1}^n\neg x_j)
\vee
(y\wedge F(x)).
\]
Let the designated assignment be
\[
a_0=(y=0,x=0^n).
\]
Then $G_F(a_0)=1$ for every $F$. Moreover every satisfying assignment of $G_F$ other than $a_0$ has $y=1$ and corresponds exactly to a satisfying assignment of $F$. Therefore
\[
\boxed{a_0\text{ is the unique satisfying assignment of }G_F
\iff F\text{ is unsatisfiable}.}
\]
Consequently, even with a designated satisfying assignment supplied, certifying that it is the unique satisfying assignment is coNP-hard. Membership is in coNP because a violation is witnessed by a second satisfying assignment. Hence the designated-binding problem is coNP-complete under ordinary polynomial reductions.

\paragraph{Immediate certificate consequence.}
Suppose there were a polynomial-size certificate $c$ and polynomial-time predicate $V(G,a,c)$ such that
\[
a\text{ is the unique satisfying assignment of }G
\iff \exists c\;V(G,a,c)=1
\]
for arbitrary Boolean formulas $G$. Then UNSAT would be in NP via $F\mapsto(G_F,a_0)$, so NP=coNP. Thus a generic existentially locally verifiable binding certificate would itself constitute a major collapse; it cannot be treated as a free syntactic normalization.

\paragraph{Implication under the project's counterfactual P=NP.}
Under the assumed SAT decider $A_{\rm SAT}$ running in time $N^k$, coNP is also decidable in time $N^k$ by running the same exact decider and negating the answer. For a candidate canonical transcript $w$, pointwise binding can be checked by constructing the formula
\[
\Psi_{\neq w}(z)=
\mathrm{Valid}(z)\wedge(z\neq w)
\]
and asking whether it is satisfiable. Therefore canonical binding is available under P=NP, but its acquisition/verification uses the same SAT resource that generated the fourth quantifier block. It does not give a near-linear existential certificate for free.

\paragraph{Lexicographic canonicalization hostile test.}
Choosing the lexicographically least successful response does not help generically. Verifying that a proposed $w$ is least requires
\[
\mathrm{Valid}(w)\wedge
\neg\exists z<w\;\mathrm{Valid}(z),
\]
again an NP condition conjoined with a coNP condition. Computing the least witness with an exact SAT oracle is possible by bit-by-bit self-reduction, but this is an acquisition algorithm using SAT queries, not a collapse of the opposite-witness condition.

\paragraph{Valiant--Vazirani comparison.}
Random isolation can reduce SAT to instances that have a unique solution with nonzero inverse-polynomial probability, but this is a randomized isolation theorem, not a deterministic pointwise certificate that a fixed sampled transcript is binding. It therefore does not remove the Arthur-randomness/consistency issue in the current reconstruction.

\paragraph{Quantifier consequence.}
Absent additional construction-specific structure, exact output still has the form
\[
E_b\wedge\neg E_{1-b}
=
\exists w_+\forall w_-\,R,
\]
and after the Viola layer retains the schematic prefix
\[
\exists\sigma\ \forall u\ \exists(i,w_+)\ \forall w_-\ R'.
\]
A generic canonicalizer cannot delete the final universal block without either (i) solving the coNP binding condition, or (ii) supplying a new structural theorem that makes conflicting openings impossible by local algebra.

\paragraph{What remains OPEN.}
The only surviving repair is a construction-specific pointwise binding law. One needs an efficiently checkable invariant $C$ tied to the actual vMS/RMV commitment object such that two conflicting valid openings violate a local identity:
\[
C(r,Q,i,w_0,w_1)\wedge
\mathrm{Valid}_0(w_0)\wedge
\mathrm{Valid}_1(w_1)
\Longrightarrow\bot,
\]
where checking $C$ does not itself call SAT, enumerate alternative witnesses, or quantify universally over Merlin strategies. Examples of potentially relevant forms would be an algebraic unique-decoding identity, a deterministic interpolation constraint, or a commitment equation with locally checkable collision impossibility. No such theorem is presently verified.

\paragraph{Hostile controls.}
Proof padding defeats uniqueness based only on witness syntax. DEAD/COPY can create duplicate transcripts. INTERP/RECOMP can change representation while preserving semantics. A maximal semantic oracle can answer whether another witness exists, showing that extensional binding tests relativize. Algebraic-oracle access may expose interpolation identities, so any proposed local algebraic binding theorem must be audited separately for algebrization. BOOL/TransportCert remains an accounting interface only and supplies no generic uniqueness certificate.

\paragraph{Strict status.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{PROVED/EXACT:} designated uniqueness of a supplied satisfying assignment is coNP-complete by the reduction above.
\item \textbf{PROVED/EXACT:} under a deterministic $N^k$ SAT decider, generic binding can be checked with the same SAT exponent by querying for a second valid witness.
\item \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED:} generic canonicalization or lexicographic normalization as a free existential replacement for the opposite-output condition.
\item \textbf{KNOWN THEOREM:} Valiant--Vazirani randomized isolation makes unique-solution instances sufficient for randomized reductions, but does not provide deterministic pointwise binding.
\item \textbf{OPEN:} construction-specific locally checkable collision-impossibility for the actual reconstruction commitment.
\item \textbf{CONDITIONAL:} conservative fixed-model $k^4$ reconstruction ledger remains unchanged.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Barrier audit.}
The generic designated-uniqueness obstruction relativizes and is not the desired nonrelativizing hinge. Canonicalization does not by itself cross algebrization. SAME\_REALIZATION is respected in the reduction: uniqueness and the second-witness test refer to one fixed formula/commitment instance. Q acquisition, exact normalization, amplification, indexing, census/whole-output production, and output writing remain separately charged.

\paragraph{Handoffs.}
LSPV-5: retain the $k^4$ conditional ledger; generic pointwise binding consumes the same SAT/coNP resource and does not justify $k^3$.
LSPV-4: any proposed local binding identity should be attacked with duplicate-proof wrappers, alternate openings, semantic-oracle replacement, and algebraic-oracle interpolation.
LSPV-1: a useful handoff to LSPV-3 would be an exact RMV/vMS local identity proving that two opposite successful openings for fixed normalized $Q,r,i$ cannot coexist, with verification independent of SAT.

\paragraph{Single next target.}
Inspect the exact commit-and-evaluate algebra in vMS and ask whether conflicting openings force a \emph{local algebraic collision} visible from the committed evaluations themselves. If no such local identity exists, retire the pointwise-binding/canonicalization route to $k^3$.
