\section{Intrinsic polar-output kernel}
For a quadratic map $F:V\to W$, $V=W=\F^d$, define
\[
 \Phi_F:W^*\to\operatorname{Alt}(V),\qquad
 \Phi_F(\lambda)(x,y)=\lambda\cdot
 (F(x+y)+F(x)+F(y)+F(0)).                              \tag{1}
\]
The space $K_F=\ker\Phi_F$ is obtained by Gaussian elimination on the
quadratic coefficient vectors.  Exactly the combinations
$\lambda\cdot F$, $\lambda\in K_F$, are affine.

\begin{theorem}[polar-kernel recovery]
Let $d=m+n$, let $P=(P_1,\ldots,P_n):\F^m\to\F^n$ have linearly independent
polar forms, set $S_P(u,v)=(u,v+P(u))$, and let
\[
 F(y)=B S_P(Ay+p)+q                                      \tag{2}
\]
for $A,B\in\mathrm{GL}_d(\F)$.  From the expanded ANF of $F$ alone one can
compute $K_F$ with $\dim K_F=m$, compute an $m$-dimensional affine quotient
whose value fixes $\pi_u(Ay+p)$, and recover the unique preimage of every
target by Gaussian elimination in time polynomial in the ANF length.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $\lambda\in W^*$ put $c=B^{\mathsf T}\lambda=(c_u,c_v)$.  Translation
changes only affine terms, while invertible input substitution acts on polar
forms by congruence.  Hence
\[
 \Phi_F(\lambda)=A^{\mathsf T}
 \left(\sum_{j=1}^n(c_v)_j\Phi_{P_j}\right)A.            \tag{3}
\]
Independence gives
\[
 K_F=B^{-\mathsf T}(\F^m\times\{0\}),\qquad \dim K_F=m. \tag{4}
\]
For $\lambda\in K_F$, $\lambda\cdot F(y)$ is an affine combination of the
first $m$ coordinates of $Ay+p$.  A basis of these affine functions has
linear rank $m$, because $B^{\mathsf T}$ maps $K_F$ isomorphically onto
$\F^m\times\{0\}$ and $A$ is invertible.  Equating them to
$\lambda\cdot b$ fixes $u=\pi_u(Ay+p)$ and leaves an $n$-dimensional affine
fiber.  On that fiber $P(u)$ is constant, so $F$ is affine in the remaining
$v$ coordinates.  Its rank is $n$, or else the permutation (2) would collide.
A second Gaussian solve recovers the point, and direct evaluation verifies it.
Every matrix used is read from the same ANF.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Recognition and off-image behavior.}
The implementation computes $K_F$ without assuming (2).  It checks
$0<\dim K_F<d$, full rank of the affine quotient, vanishing of every polar
form on pairs of quotient-fiber directions, and full column rank of the
restricted affine map.  It solves and verifies accepted targets; otherwise it
rejects this structured promise and hands the input to a fallback.  Rejection
is not a claim about arbitrary MQ.  For an expanded ANF of
$N=\Theta(d^3)$ bits, parsing, discovery, solves, reconstruction, and
verification are polynomial in $N$.

\section{Hostile controls, barriers, and surviving repair}

phases.  DEAD/COPY/DELAY/INTERP/RECOMP wrappers cannot erase the ANF kernel
when recognition and replay are charged.  The algorithm uses polynomial
workspace and emits no DNNF, PC/PCR proof, Resolution proof, pebbling, or
semantic-state table.  Program length is not confused with workspace.

The theorem does not make arbitrary MQ easy.  A repair must at least defeat
$K_F$, but $K_F=0$ is not sufficient: FIELDTRACEFW1 has injective
output-to-polar map and a uniform selector through large fibers.  Singleton
fibers are not sufficient: QUADSHEAR1 is a permutation.  The smallest current
structural corner is therefore the joint regime
\[
 K_F=0,\qquad |F^{-1}(b)|\le\operatorname{poly}(d)
 \quad\text{(ideally one)},                              \tag{5}
\]
plus exclusion of every other polynomial selector.  Even (5) is only a
candidate filter, not a lower bound.

The inverter relativizes and algebrizes; no barrier-crossing hinge is found.
Natural Proofs is not invoked.  ETH, cryptographic hardness, and random
oracles are absent.
