\begin{lemma}[Strict signed ER fragment]
For every proper UNSAT CNF \(F\) and every strict ER refutation of \(F\) whose paid operations are \(d\) binary signed extension definitions and \(r\) binary RES steps, there is a same-source Rmix341 refutation with at most \(d+r\) paid nodes. If an ER normal form explicitly copies extension slots as separately charged nodes, a fixed constant-factor overhead suffices. More general ER syntax can first be normalized by polynomial local expansion; that cost is \emph{not} assumed zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For every fresh binary AND definition \(z\leftrightarrow(a\wedge b)\), introduce exactly one paid R-DEF with defining clauses \((\neg z\vee a)\), \((\neg z\vee b)\), \((z\vee\neg a\vee\neg b)\). Signed literals \(a,b\) may themselves contain negation or earlier extension variables. An OR definition \(z\leftrightarrow(a\vee b)\) can be represented by a fresh AND gate \(u\leftrightarrow(\neg a\wedge\neg b)\) with all subsequent \(z\)-literals globally replaced by complementary \(u\)-literals. This is a consistent literal renaming and preserves every defining clause. Induct over the original shared-DAG proof: each strict RES inference remains the same valid pivot inference after the signed translation. Original source clauses are unchanged. No extra source, semantic oracle or unit-cost multi-premise rule is introduced. The proof has \(d\) definition nodes and \(r\) Resolution nodes. For general ER proof presentations, use their standard polynomial-size strict extension/Resolution normal form before applying this simulation.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Polynomial DPR-to-fixed-R domination]

\[
\boxed{\overline W_{R_{\mathrm{mix341}}}(N)\le p(N+\overline B_{\mathrm{DPR}}(N)+1).}
\]
Therefore superpolynomial \(\overline W_{R_{\mathrm{mix341}}}\) would force superpolynomial \(\overline B_{\mathrm{DPR}}\). A superpolynomial DPR lower bound \emph{alone} would not establish mixed hardness.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

The result is existential and does not require any fastest-proof discovery algorithm. Maximizing the pointwise inequality over finitely many valid UNSAT encoded inputs of bit length at most \(N\) yields the worst-case inequality. Contraposition proves the hardness-necessity statement. \emph{No inequality is reversed.}
