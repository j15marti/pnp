It is either satisfied, contradictory, or a proper residual clause D' of width r.

(1) When r >= 4, every Boolean predicate Q on at most three FREE original variables that follows from Gamma AND D AND DEFINITIONS is already implied by Gamma. Hence no new nontrivial original-three-local factor can be exported from this one clause and its definitions.

(2) When r >= 2, no nontrivial original-coordinate affine GF(2) equation follows beyond Gamma. The projected satisfying assignments of D' have full affine hull over the remaining free original-variable coordinates.

(3) When r = 1, the residual unit and its one-variable affine equation do follow. When D is satisfied by Gamma, it imposes no new condition; when D is contradicted by Gamma, the branch is inconsistent and must be discharged by a legal proof, not treated as a no-consequence case.

**Proof.** Every assignment of original variables uniquely extends across acyclic conservative gate definitions. Therefore the original-variable projection of the model set is unchanged. For (1), fix arbitrary values of any set S of <=3 free original variables. Since r > |S|, there is an unassigned residual literal outside S. Choose it true, obtaining a satisfying extension of every assignment on S. The projection is the full Boolean cube, so any entailed predicate on S is tautological modulo Gamma. For (2), translate each signed literal to its 0/1 truth value. The satisfying assignments of a proper OR of r >= 2 variables contain e_1,...,e_r and e_1+e_2. These r+1 points are affinely independent over GF(2): relative to e_1, the r difference vectors e_i+e_1 (i>1) and e_2 are independent. Remaining free variables are unconstrained. Therefore the affine hull is full, giving no nonzero affine equation. (3) follows by restriction. QED.
