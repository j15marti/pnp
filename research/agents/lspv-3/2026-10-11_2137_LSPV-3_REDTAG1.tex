\begin{abstract}
LB-0018 proves that every deterministic total exact SAT decider must probe at
least one differing position in each of $k$ equal-size clean-core blocks on an
all-YES input.  This run identifies the exact scope of that theorem.  Two
equal-size satisfiable Monotone $3$SAT-$(2,2)$ components can encode the AND of
the $k$ block selectors as a semantically neutral answer tag.  On the
consistent tagged promise, one tag probe decides satisfiability.  On the same
all-YES encoded input, every \emph{total} exact SAT decider still needs $k$
block discriminations, because leaving the YES tag fixed while changing one
main block to the fixed UNSAT block gives a valid off-promise $3$CNF with the
opposite answer.  Thus the probe floor is a total-language robustness theorem,
not intrinsic promise complexity, compatibility extraction, state exposure,
or NP-hardness.  LB-0018 survives; its unqualified semantic interpretation is
retired.  P versus NP remains \OPEN.
\end{abstract}

\section{Concrete equal-size clean-core tags}

For a simple cubic graph $G=(V,E)$, introduce one Boolean variable $x_e$ for
each edge.  For every vertex $v$, add the paired clauses
\[
 C_v^+=\bigvee_{e\ni v}x_e,
 \qquad
 C_v^-=\bigvee_{e\ni v}\neg x_e.                  \tag{1}
\]
If $G$ has a perfect matching, setting matching edges to one and all other
edges to zero satisfies (1).  Each edge variable occurs exactly twice
positively and twice negatively.  For a simple cubic graph with distinct
vertex neighborhoods, (1) is duplicate-free, proper, and monotone.

Let $T_0$ be (1) for $K_{3,3}$ and $T_1$ be (1) for the triangular prism.
Both have exactly nine variables, six positive clauses, six negative clauses,
and twelve clauses total; both are satisfiable, and their indexed encodings
differ.  These are ordinary clean-core components, not trusted non-CNF
annotations.

Let $Y,U$ be the equal-size satisfiable and unsatisfiable components of
LB-0018.  For $s\in\{0,1\}^k$, with disjoint variable sets, define the tagged
formula
\[
 G_{k,s}=T_{\AND(s)}\wedge B_{s_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge B_{s_k},
 \qquad B_1=Y,\quad B_0=U.                         \tag{2}
\]
Use the tag-first block-concatenated indexed encoding.  Every formula in (2)
is in $\Htwo$, its encoded length is $N=\Theta(k\log k)$, and
\[
 G_{k,s}\in\SAT\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \AND(s)=1. \tag{3}
\]
The tag is semantically neutral because both $T_0,T_1$ are satisfiable; it
only exposes the generator's already explicit selector AND.

\section{Exact totality-seam theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{TOTALITY-SEAM-TAG1} --- \EXACT]
For every $k\ge1$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item There is a deterministic decider exact on the promise
$\mathcal G_k=\{G_{k,s}:s\in\{0,1\}^k\}$ that makes one bit probe.
\item Every deterministic decider exact on the total language of all valid
explicitly encoded $3$CNFs makes at least $k$ bit probes on $G_{k,1^k}$.
\end{enumerate}
Both statements concern the same tag-first encoding and the same all-YES
input.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose one indexed-encoding position $d(k)$ at which $T_0$ and $T_1$ differ;
the position is computable from $k$ and the global literal width.  On the
consistent promise, query $d(k)$ and output one exactly when it contains the
$T_1$ bit.  By (2)--(3), this is exact and uses one probe.

For totality, fix the all-YES input
\[
 X_k=T_1\wedge Y_1\wedge\cdots\wedge Y_k.
\]
For each $i\in[k]$, let
\[
 X_{k,i}=T_1\wedge Y_1\wedge\cdots\wedge U_i
              \wedge\cdots\wedge Y_k.             \tag{4}
\]
Formula (4) is a valid ordinary clean-core $3$CNF and is unsatisfiable.  It is
not in the consistent tagged promise, because its tag was deliberately left
unchanged.  Let $D_i$ be the positions where the equal-size encodings of
$Y_i$ and $U_i$ differ.  If a deterministic total exact decider probes no
position in $D_i$ while running on $X_k$, its complete adaptive transcript is
unchanged on $X_{k,i}$, so it outputs YES on an unsatisfiable input.  Therefore
it probes every $D_i$.  The block sets are disjoint, giving at least $k$
probes.  The tag probe cannot discharge any of these obligations because the
tag is identical in (4).  \qed
\end{proof}

The Boolean skeleton is the code
\[
 \mathcal C_k=\{(\AND(s),s):s\in\{0,1\}^k\}.
\]
Its promise decision reads the first bit; the total extension that returns
$\AND(s)$ on every pair $(t,s)$ has all-ones decision-tree complexity $k$.
The clean-core construction is an exact formula-level realization of this
elementary seam.

\section{Costs, wrappers, and smallest repair}

The promise decoder has zero preprocessing, one probe, $O(\log N)$ address
workspace, one output bit, and zero compatibility extraction or
reconstruction.  The family generator computes $\AND(s)$ in $O(k)$ time and
one accumulator bit before choosing the tag; that work is explicitly charged.

Assuming a polynomial total SAT decider $A$, a total recognize-first wrapper
can scan the complete input, verify every $Y/U$ block and tag consistency,
then use the tag; otherwise it invokes $A$.  Recognition costs $\Theta(N)$
input inspection and $O(\log N)$ workspace.  It does not inherit the
one-query promise cost.  This is another form of the already verified
recognizable-island barrier, not a new polynomial algorithm for total SAT.

DIRECT, COPY, DEAD, DELAY, INTERP, and RECOMP implementations preserve (3)
while changing traces.  Tag-first and tag-last encodings both permit one
promise query when the declared locator is computable.  Removing the tag
restores the original blockwise promise adversary.  Arbitrary permutation
without a charged locator destroys the one-query interface.  Padding and
redundancy enlarge syntax without changing selector information unless they
are answer-dependent.

The weakest repair is exact: state probe theorems for total SAT on a fixed
explicit encoding, and retain the valid off-promise mutations used by the
adversary.  A promise-only theorem must instead require an answer-oblivious,
block-local encoding and prove its adversary wholly inside that promise.
Neither restriction yields a superpolynomial SAT lower bound.

\subsection*{Hostile controls}
The same tag/totality seam was checked for explicit truth values produced by
2SAT/SCC, Horn forward chaining, affine/Tseitin Gaussian elimination,
width-one dynamic programming, and independent-product AND aggregation.
These controls show that the seam is generic and not evidence of NP-hardness.

aggregation; they do not remove the total exact decider's obligation to handle
inconsistent ordinary inputs.

\[
 \operatorname{Coll}(L)=(2^k-1)^2+1,
 \qquad

Encoding $L(s)$ as the tag makes this extreme lossiness directly exploitable
by the promise algorithm.  This is an \textbf{EXACT OBJECT/THEOREM TRANSFER}

Schur squares, quadratic relation ranks, Hilbert functions, syzygies, and
special-soundness extraction are \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} for this seam: no

\textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE needing a new bridge}; it must externally recover a

Hashes and random oracles remain inadmissible assumptions.

The theorem and counterexample relativize.  Algebraic oracle access does not
repair the totality-to-time gap; the tag is already a degree-$k$ AND but is
explicitly supplied on the promise.  No constructive circuit property is
used, so Natural Proofs is not the active barrier.  The total lower bound is
uniform and uses no advice; nonuniform advice containing the selector answer
would change the input model.  Solver-to-protocol transfer remains \OPEN.

\section{Primary-literature preflight}

Jain--Klauck--Santha prove direct sums for deterministic and randomized
decision trees in the query model \cite{JKS}; this transfers to LB-0018's
declared query measure, not to arbitrary time, space, or promise encodings.
Locally decodable code results formalize recovery of message coordinates from
few codeword probes \cite{KdW}; they are an analogy for the explicit tag but
do not lower-bound total SAT.  D\"ocker supplies NP-completeness of the exact
clean-core language \cite{Doecker}; NP-hardness of the language does not make
every easy subfamily hard.  FLMV supplies a model-specific SAT time--space
endpoint \cite{FLMV}; the $k$-probe floor does not reach it.
