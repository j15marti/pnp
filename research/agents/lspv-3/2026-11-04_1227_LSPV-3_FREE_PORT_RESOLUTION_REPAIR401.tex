\begin{theorem}
For every integer $k\ge0$, every set $\Gamma$ of $s$ distinct, previously justified, non-tautological clauses on $k$ named Boolean ports, and every clause $C$ on the same ports: if $\Gamma\models C$ under all independent valuations of the ports, a clause $D\subseteq C$ has a strictly source-authenticated Resolution derivation from $\Gamma$ with at most $3^k-s$ additional paid inference nodes. Ordinary RES remains valid when a named port is an already-paid conservative circuit output. The bound does not include justifying $\Gamma$ or its definitions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are $3^k$ proper clauses on $k$ ports, including the empty clause. Saturate distinct non-tautological resolvents to obtain at most $3^k-s$ charged nodes. Eliminate every variable absent from $C$ by Davis--Putnam Resolution; this computes the existential projection of $\Gamma$. As $\Gamma$ implies $C$, the assignment falsifying $C$ falsifies some projected clause $D$. Only variables of $C$ remain, so $D\subseteq C$. The resolution closure already contains $D$, and each inference retains the original ancestry.
\end{proof}
For $\Gamma=\{a\lor y,b\lor\neg y\}$, exactly one RES node yields $a\lor b$ even if $y$ represents nonlinear inner product, provided both antecedents are independently justified. In contrast $\{y\}$ does not imply $x$ on independent ports, although $\{y,y\leftrightarrow x\}$ does: no free-port compiler for definition-dependent claims is established.
