\section{One target and four falsifiers}
The target is a necessary certificate-coverage condition for an adaptive parameterized controller whose successor stage generates only original-source direct cofactors $F\mid\rho$ while attempting to keep both the number and physical size of guest SAT calls subpower. Four falsifiers were tested:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The conjecture that a polynomial number of small direct cofactors can cover all remaining branches without large certified pruning. \emph{Falsified as stated in this restricted tree model by the theorem below.}
\item The proposed shortcut ``unit propagation is free depth reduction.'' \emph{Falsified: every forced value omits the opposite Boolean cylinder unless that opposite cube is explicitly accounted as certified rejection.}
\item The claim that small cofactor counts automatically meet the ARC physical-child bound. \emph{Falsified by actual encoded child sizes, including the W1/SWAP6 controls.}
\item The claim that large local residuals or adverse overlap force every SAT algorithm to be expensive. \emph{Falsified by the connected strict22 saturation orbit's easily verified canonical witness and existing bounded-degree isomorphism recognition.}
\end{enumerate}
We compared the complete certified partition, which preserves every assignment, with a decision-only short-circuit implementation and with an identical UP-first DPLL backend. None realizes the all-input FLMV contractor.

\section{Formal model and theorem}
Let $F$ be a proper strict signed exact-$(2+,2-)$-3CNF on $n$ variables and $m=4n/3$ original clauses. A finite \emph{certified cofactor partition} is a complete binary tree. Every internal node sets exactly one previously unassigned original variable, and hence branches into two disjoint partial-assignment cubes. Leaves have one of three labels:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[P.] a partial cube proved incompatible with $F$ by an authenticated source-conflict, derived clause, or genuine checked UNSAT certificate;
\item[S.] a partial cube making every original clause true, so all its completions are SAT; or
\item[E.] an explicit residual ordinary clause-list child exactly equal to $F\mid\rho$, formed only by deleting satisfied original clauses and false original literals, with at most $r$ surviving original clauses.
\end{enumerate}
Source-unit propagation may be used, but its opposite assignment is an explicit P sibling certified by the clause that became unit. A complete decision search may stop early upon finding a valid SAT witness, but then its explored prefix is not claimed to be a complete assignment partition. The theorem concerns a complete partition needed for a two-sided disjunctive collection of E children and source certificates.

Put $t_\ell=|\rho_\ell|$, let $P,S,E$ be the corresponding leaf sets, and $\mu(X)=\sum_{\ell\in X}2^{-t_\ell}$. For each partial assignment $\rho$, let $s_j(\rho)$ be the number of original literals already TRUE in $C_j$ and define $\Omega(\rho)=\sum_j\max(0,s_j(\rho)-1)$. Let $q=|E|$.

\begin{theorem}[Source-certified cofactor coverage gate]
For every such formula and partition,
\[
D(\rho)=2|\rho|-\Omega(\rho),\qquad m_{\rm residual}(\rho)=m-2|\rho|+\Omega(\rho),
\]
where $D$ counts removed satisfied original clauses. With $t_* =\max\{0,\lceil(m-r)/2\rceil\}$,
\[
\boxed{\mu(P)+\mu(S)+\mu(E)=1,\qquad
  \mu(E)\le q2^{-t_*},\qquad
  \mu(P)+\mu(S)\ge 1-q2^{-t_*}.}
\]
On an UNSAT input $S=\varnothing$, hence $\mu(P)\ge1-q2^{-t_*}$. If explicit ordinary clause-list child encodings have at least one bit per surviving clause and length at most $N^\lambda$, with fixed $\lambda<1$, then $r\le N^\lambda$. On a dense strict22 source with $N=\Theta(n\log n)$ and any $q\le N^\gamma$ for fixed $\gamma$, one has $t_*=\Theta(n)$ and therefore $\mu(E)\le2^{-\Theta(n)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An assigned variable has exactly two original occurrences whose signed literals become true. Thus the number of true assigned literal occurrences is exactly $2t=\sum_j s_j(\rho)$. Each removed clause contributes one initial true occurrence to $D$, while all further true occurrences in that clause contribute to $\Omega$, so $2t=D+\Omega$. In particular $m_{\rm residual}=m-D\ge m-2t$. Each E leaf with at most $r$ source clauses consequently has depth $t\ge t_*$. A complete binary decision tree partitions $\{0,1\}^n$ into disjoint cylinders; every depth-$t$ leaf has exactly $2^{n-t}$ completions. Summing their measures gives $\mu(P)+\mu(S)+\mu(E)=1$. At most $q$ E leaves each contribute at most $2^{-t_*}$, giving the inequalities. On UNSAT, no S leaf is possible. For a fixed-width explicit indexed clause-list source $N=\Theta(n\log n)$ and $m=4n/3$. When $r\le N^\lambda=o(n)$, $t_*\ge(m-o(n))/2=\Theta(n)$; a polynomial $q$ is $2^{o(n)}$, giving $q2^{-t_*}=2^{-\Theta(n)}$. All inequalities concern the declared restricted architecture; no arbitrary-machine runtime statement follows.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Necessary nuance.} The result does \emph{not} imply an exponential number of proof leaves. A short powerful UNSAT proof can certify a shallow cube (even the root), and a globally recognized SAT witness can end the decision without constructing a full partition. Nor does it rule out resolution, factoring, shared circuits, compressed source access, equivalence compilation, affine Gaussian elimination, formula-specific recognizers, or adaptive algorithms beyond direct cofactor trees. The quantity $\mu$ is a rigorous cube measure, not an equity score or a difficulty potential.

\section{Source-authenticated finite controls}
The full producer preserves original signed occurrences and source indices, local all-TRUE $S_j$ targets and inverted $U_j$ targets, the reverse local targets and inversions, all $SS,SU,US,UU$ indexed pair modes, coherent $0^n/1^n$ statuses, and flip/complement trails. Source-exact P sibling checks, the residual child list, and every assigned cube are written to the raw archive. The independent verifier replays all leaf cubes, checks disjointness, checks complete coverage, verifies false-source-clause P certificates, checks each residual child by re-evaluating source clauses, and compares complete assignment truth tables. For the 21-variable orbit, it instead verifies disjointness and total mass symbolically plus the canonical witness; it does not claim to enumerate all $2^{21}$ assignments.

\paragraph{Hardness falsifier.} The 21-variable saturation source has 28 strict22 clauses and a globally known witness $A_i=1$, $B_i=C_i=0$. Its first-pivot direct-cofactor tree at $r=24$ emits four children (490 bits each), while a source-grammar recognizer may solve it directly. These facts do not imply that cofactor size or search-node count lower-bounds arbitrary SAT.
