\begin{theorem}[Exact central-layer expectation]
For every valid canonical formula on $n$ variables, $n\in3\mathbb N$, $n\ge6$, $m=4n/3$, and exactly $2n/3$ original clauses of each sign. Let $V(a)$ count falsified original clauses. On the uniform Hamming-weight-$k$ layer,
\[
\mathbb E[V\mid |a|=k]
=\frac{2n}{3\binom nk}
\left[\binom{n-3}{k}+\binom{n-3}{k-3}\right].
\]
Its minimum over $k$ is $n(n-4)/(6(n-1))$ for even $n$ and $(n-3)/6$ for odd $n$. It is $2/5$ at $n=6$, exactly one at $n=9$, and strictly above one for every permitted $n\ge12$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each positive triple is false on $\binom{n-3}k$ assignments, each negative triple on $\binom{n-3}{k-3}$, independent of incidence structure. Sum exactly $2n/3$ indicators of each type. In falling-factorial form the numerator is $(n-k)_3+(k)_3$, a symmetric quadratic minimized at the middle layer. Direct substitution gives the two formulas.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Six satisfying central-layer witnesses of each weight at $n=9$]
For every valid duplicate-free monotone 3-SAT-(2,2) source with $n=9$, there exist at least six distinct SAT assignments of weight four and at least six of weight five. If $a_2^+$ counts pairs of positive original clauses with two shared variable names and $a_2^-$ the negative analog, the bounds improve to $6+2a_2^+$ and $6+2a_2^-$ respectively.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each polarity contains six distinct triples, each variable appearing in two of them. Let $a_1^+,a_2^+$ count positive-clause pairs with one or two shared variables. Counting two positive occurrences of each of nine variables gives $a_1^++2a_2^+=9$. On a weight-four assignment, five variables are zero. A pair of positive clauses sharing one variable is simultaneously falsified on exactly one such assignment (union size five); a pair sharing two is jointly false on exactly five (union size four). Writing $P(a)$ for the number of false positive clauses,
\[
D=\sum_{|a|=4}\binom{P(a)}2=a_1^++5a_2^+=9+3a_2^+.
\]
There are at most ten positive occurrences among the five zero variables. Every false positive clause consumes three, so $P(a)\le3$. Hence
$\binom{P(a)}2\le\frac32(V(a)-1)_+$.
The expectation identity gives $\sum_{|a|=4}V(a)=\binom94=126$, and therefore $\sum(V(a)-1)_+$ equals exactly the number $W_4$ of zero-violation assignments in the layer. Thus $W_4\ge(2/3)D=6+2a_2^+$. Swap the literal signs and interchange zeros and ones to obtain the weight-five bound from negative-clause pairs. The two sets of witnesses are disjoint.
\end{proof}
