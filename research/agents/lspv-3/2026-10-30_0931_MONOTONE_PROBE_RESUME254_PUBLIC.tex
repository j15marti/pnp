\section{Exact identity and acquisition bound}
Let $U_F(\alpha)$ be the complete unit-propagation closure, including assumptions, or $\bot$. Union of contradictory assignments is $\bot$.
\begin{theorem}
For fixed CNF $F$, base assignments $\alpha\subseteq\beta$ and probe $p$, if $U_F(\alpha\cup\{p\})=\tau\ne\bot$, then
\[
U_F(\beta\cup\{p\})=U_F(\tau\cup\beta).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each source-unit derivation under the smaller assumptions persists under the larger assumptions unless a conflict occurs first. Its conclusion is still unit, already true, or opposed by a base fact, making its reason clause false. Hence every cached consequence is already in the new least fixed point. Conversely the cached assignment and new base contain all new assumptions. Monotonicity gives equality, including conflict outcomes.
\end{proof}
Enqueue newly assigned variables and inspect every original clause containing them; propagate newly unit source clauses. Only such clauses can change status from the former fixed point, so queue exhaustion gives the exact new fixed point. Do not reuse this state after backtracking, source rewriting or added clauses. The implementation discards all sessions before another DPLL node is processed. Conditional consequences are never committed unconditionally.

\begin{theorem}
On an indexed word RAM, let $N$ be source bits, $n$ occurring variables, $\ell$ literal occurrences and $w$ maximum width. One full failed-literal closure can be implemented with at most $2n$ monotone probe sessions in
\[
O(N+nw\ell+n^2\log(n+2))
\]
word operations and $O(N+(\ell+n^2)\log(N+2))$ stored bits, including unsuccessful probes, discovery, base updates and independently replayable source-unit certificates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Within each session a variable is assigned and enqueued at most once. Each clause is inspected at most its width times, costing at most its squared width; all clauses therefore cost at most $w\ell$ per session. Each session consumes at most $n$ new base-log records. There are at most $n$ committed failed literals and $n+1$ candidate scans; candidate sorting costs $O(n^2\log(n+2))$. On every conflict the implementation regenerates a fresh source-reason trace from current assumptions, charging $O(w\ell+n)$ each time. Base propagation and checking have the same bound. At most $2n$ assignments of size $n$, and $n$ proof traces of length at most $n$, use $O(n^2)$ records. Source and occurrence index are also charged.
\end{proof}
For strict22, $\ell=4n,w=3$, giving a conservative $O(N+n^2\log(n+2))$ per-closure upper bound. The complete DPLL tree can still be exponential. No RAM bound is silently transferred to tape-head or crossing counts. A native tape implementation needs separately charged access and simulation.

\section{Connected repeated-probe family}
In numerical variable order $p_1,\ldots,p_k,z,y_1,u_1,\ldots,y_k,u_k$, use
\[
F_k=\bigwedge_i(\neg p_i\vee z)\land\bigwedge_i[(\neg y_i\vee u_i)\land(\neg y_i\vee\neg u_i)\land(\neg y_i\vee z)].
\]
This connected 2CNF has $3k+1$ variables and $4k$ clauses. With the specified ascending probe policy, each $p_i$ is revisited before every next failed $y_j$. Fresh propagation rescans all previous base facts, giving $\Theta(k^3)$ selected events; resumption consumes each new fact once per session, giving $\Theta(k^2)$. A counting-scan candidate selector realizes the quadratic word-RAM bound; generic comparison sorting adds the stated logarithmic upper factor. The Python benchmark counts selected events, not all sort comparisons. Live assignment records grow from $\Theta(k)$ fresh to $\Theta(k^2)$ cached on this family.

This is not a hard SAT family: all-zero is a witness and 2SAT is legal. It measures one closure procedure, not fastest SAT decision.
