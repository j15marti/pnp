\section*{Exact general one-cycle decomposition}
\begin{lemma}[Unique shared node and duplicated-subtree test]
Let $F$ be clause-minimally UNSAT with $m$ source leaves. Suppose a pruned sound proof in the fixed system has exactly $q=m$ paid unit-weight nodes. All $m$ original clauses occur. The graph excess identity yields
\[
q-(m-1)=\beta+u+2d_0=1,
\]
where $\beta$ is the undirected proof-dependency cycle rank, $u$ the number of one-premise nodes, and $d_0$ the number of source-independent zero-premise nodes. Thus exactly one of the following occurs: $(\beta,u,d_0)=(0,1,0)$ or $(1,0,0)$.

In the first case a semantics-preserving unary conversion can be erased under free exact representation aliases, leaving an $(m-1)$-node source-once binary proof tree. In the second case exactly one source or derived proof node $v$ has two uses as a premise; every other nonroot node has one use. Its ancestor subproof is a source-once binary tree over a nonempty source set $S$, and replacing the two uses of $v$ by two independent \emph{leaves carrying the same computed function} leaves a source-once binary outer tree on two copies of $v$ plus the $m-|S|$ original leaves outside $S$. This construction, with any nonempty $S$, exhausts the one-cycle class. The inner tree uses $|S|-1$ nodes and the outer tree uses $m-|S|+1$, totaling $m$. No assumption that intermediate representations shrink is used.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All original clauses are indispensable by deletion satisfiability. Connectedness and the exact graph Euler identity give the displayed equality. In the all-binary case there are $m+q$ graph vertices and $2q$ premise edges. Of the $m+q-1$ nonroot vertices each has at least one outgoing use, so there is exactly one surplus use, i.e., exactly one vertex with outdegree two. Removing one of its two uses breaks the unique undirected cycle. As there are no other duplicated nodes, its entire ancestor subproof is a source-once tree; every remaining source appears in the other part of the tree. Duplicating the semantic result of $v$ only as two outer leaves gives the claimed exhaustive decomposition. A semantics-preserving unary step contributes no new semantic function if representation aliases are free. Independent VARDEF consumes two excess units and is impossible at $q=m$.
\end{proof}

\section*{A deliberately stronger finite lower-bound relaxation}
For a formula with $n$ original variables, encode each Boolean function by its exact $2^n$-row truth table. For every nonempty source subset $S$, compute a set $\mathcal T(S)$ of outputs obtainable by \emph{source-once} binary trees under these relaxed operations: exact Resolution of freely reinterpreted clause functions; GF$(2)$ addition whenever both inputs are freely reinterpretable as affine rows; and exact conjunction whenever the \emph{output} function has essential support at most three (regardless of the inputs' original support). Clause and affine representation changes are free. This is an \emph{over-approximation}, not a new rule for $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$. In particular the relaxed AND3 may be stronger than legal bounded-input local AND3.

The same subset-partition recurrence computes all possible outer source-once outputs on the leaf multiset
\[
[\;f,\ f,\ \{C_j:j\notin S\}\;],\qquad f\in\mathcal T(S).
\]
If the constant-false table is absent for every nonempty $S$ and every $f\in\mathcal T(S)$, then no $m$-node legal $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ proof exists. Likewise absence from $\mathcal T([m])$ excludes the floor $m-1$. Exact semantic aliases eliminate charged conversion advantages for the unary case. This search covers \emph{all adaptive orders}, all permitted source-once internal shapes and all single shared-node placements; it does not cover proofs with $q>m$, two reuse cycles, or source-bearing DEF rule versions.
