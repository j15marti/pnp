### Proof of grammar lower bound
The concatenation through the end of the k-bit words has length T_k=sum_{j=1}^k j2^j=(k-1)2^{k+1}+2. Choose k=n-ceil(log2 n)-3>=2. Then T_k<=N. Hence all 2^k different k-bit words appear as k-factors within S_n. Each distinct k-factor of any binary SLP-generated word has an occurrence crossing a child boundary at its *lowest containing node*; one binary concatenation rule introduces at most k-1 different crossing k-factors. If B is the number of binary rules, B(k-1)>=2^k. Since 2^k=N/[8*2^{ceil(log2 n)}]>N/(16n) and k-1<n, B>N/(16n^2). This is a standard grammar factor-count argument (Charikar et al., 2005), not a new general SLP lower bound.
