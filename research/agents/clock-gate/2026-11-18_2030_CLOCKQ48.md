## Exact theorem: Q graph representation and counting
Use Z5 with R(a,b) iff (b-a) mod 5 is in {1,3}, R(a,a)=false.
Q(h,u,v)=R(h,u) XOR R(h,v), old P(h,u,v)=R(h,u) AND R(h,v).
Of 125 ordered triples, exactly 60 satisfy Q, 20 satisfy P; these sets are disjoint. Q is not a relaxation of P. Variable names in clauses are distinct, but assigned values may coincide.

For endpoint colors a,b, direct five-head enumeration gives
w(a,b)=|{h:Q(h,a,b)}|=0 if a=b, 4 if (b-a) mod5 in {1,4}, and 2 if in {2,3}.
The 5x5 matrix is:
```
0 4 2 2 4
4 0 4 2 2
2 4 0 4 2
2 2 4 0 4
4 2 2 4 0
```
Thus EXISTS h Q(h,a,b) iff a != b. A tie is a non-win; do not replace NOT R(h,a) by R(a,h) without strict disequality.

For any graph G=(V,E), create an independent fresh head h_e for every edge e={u,v}, and put Q(h_e,u,v) into the source. The *whole authentic source formula* is SAT iff G has a proper five-coloring. Exact full original-source assignment count:
```
#Q_G = SUM_{proper c:V->[5]} PRODUCT_{{u,v} in E} w(c(u),c(v)).
```
The proof fixes endpoint colors and independently counts all legal head extensions. Consequently this fresh-head Q-CSP is NP-complete through the classical fixed-5-coloring reduction. The reduction is not parsimonious: a one-edge graph has 20 colorings but 60 full Q-source assignments. T3 has an analogous graph-three-coloring reduction, so T5 is not the first hard alphabet.
