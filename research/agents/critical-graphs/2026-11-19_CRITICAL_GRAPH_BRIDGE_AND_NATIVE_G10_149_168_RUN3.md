# Strict-C graph-critical bridge and native girth-10 reduction

**PROVED.** Let G be edge-critical non-5-colorable with v vertices, m edges, graph girth g. Fix edge e0=(u,v). Encode every edge other than e0 by one strict-C physical clause C(x,y,h_f), with its own private head h_f. The resulting gadget is satisfiable, forces u=v, and deleting any one gadget clause permits u!=v. Chaining k copies and closing with C(t0,tk,h) gives a physically clause-critical UNSAT native strict-C source with N=k(v+m-2)+2 variables, M=k(m-1)+1 clauses and incidence girth at least 2g.

**Proof.** Every C(x,y,h) admits some h iff x!=y. Thus a bridge model induces a proper 5-coloring of G-e0. Its terminals must agree, or it would properly color G. For any edge f distinct from e0, a 5-coloring of G-f satisfies all retained bridge clauses other than f, and the terminals differ because e0 remains present in G-f. The endpoints of f must be equal, because otherwise the same coloring would extend to all of G. To repair a deletion in copy j, permute its graph-vertex 5-coloring to terminal values 0,1, use all earlier intact bridges at terminal value 0 and all later ones at value 1, and recompute every private C head. The closing clause is then satisfied. Deleting the closing clause admits all equal terminal values. All other original C clauses hold. Cycles within a bridge are graph cycles of doubled length at least 2g; a cycle crossing the closing clause has length at least 2k(g-1)+2, also at least 2g. Important firewall: arbitrary S5 permutations preserve projected graph inequality, NOT original C directly; C(0,1,2)=true while C(0,1,3)=false. All private heads must be recomputed.
