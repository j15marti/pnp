\section*{Fixed-depth parent-scale firewall}
LSPV-4's declared growing family has
\[
 (k_0,m_0)=(198p,264p),
\]
and after its first and second fixed block projections, before unit
simplification,
\[
 (k_1,m_1)=(198p-197,264(p-1)+2),
\quad
 (k_2,m_2)=(198p-394,264(p-2)+4).
\]
For the declared dense indexed encoding put
\[
 D(k,m)=3\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil+2
       +3m\bigl(\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil+1\bigr).
\]

\paragraph{Lemma (\EXACT).}
For every fixed projection depth $d$ that removes only $O(d)$ constant-size
blocks and adds $O(d)$ interface clauses,
\[
 \frac{D(k_d,m_d)}{D(k_0,m_0)}\longrightarrow 1,
 \qquad
 \frac{\log D(k_d,m_d)}{\log D(k_0,m_0)}\longrightarrow 1.
\]
Consequently its ARC solver-call length exponent is $\lambda=1$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Here $D(k_0,m_0)=\Theta(p\log p)$.  At fixed $d$, the changed clause and
variable records occupy only $O(d\log p)$ bits; word-width discontinuities add
at most another $O(p)$ bits at isolated powers of two, still
$o(p\log p)$.  Hence $D(k_d,m_d)=D(k_0,m_0)(1-o(1))$.  Taking logarithms gives
the second limit.  The explicit $d=1,2$ formulas above are special cases.
\hfill$\square$

At $p=2^{20}$, the audit obtains parent length $24{,}083{,}693{,}654$,
first-child length $24{,}083{,}670{,}860$, and second pre-simplification length
$24{,}083{,}648{,}066$.  Their finite logarithmic exponents are approximately
$0.9999999604$ and $0.9999999208$; the table illustrates, but is not used to
prove, the limit.

\section*{Same-machine resource ledger}
Consider the total uniform wrapper $B_A$: parse and recognize the explicit
family, execute its proved direct shortcut when recognized, and otherwise call
the original all-input machine $A$ on the untouched ordinary input.
The charged categories are disjoint as follows.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{0.25\linewidth}p{0.60\linewidth}}
\toprule
Category & Required charge and worst-case consequence\\
\midrule
Source traffic & Every source bit read, reread, index update, and unchanged
write used by parsing/recognition; polynomial $N^{1+o(1)}$ work.\\
Control/selection & Template tests, arithmetic, branch choice, failed tests,
and mutations; all paid, with no semantic SAT bit for free.\\
Children & Headers, bodies, interface clauses, compatibility, and two actual
levels.  Eager materialization has length exponent $1$; lazy probing retains
parent-scale $A$ calls.\\
Workspace & Indices, recognizer state, buffers, live ancestors, cache keys and
$A$'s workspace are simultaneous; no time/space borrowing across machines.\\
Fallback & An unrecognized ordinary CNF is submitted to the same $A$ at length
$N$, contributing time $N^{t+o(1)}$ and space $N^{s+o(1)}$.\\
Other metrics & Actual complete-state bit flips, physical switches,
gates/wires, cumulative bit-time, code and representation size remain separate
from elementary bit work and peak writable bits.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Thus for $t\ge 1$ the certified pair is
\[
 (t_B,s_B)=(\max\{1,t\},s)=(t,s),
\]
not the illustrative favorable successor.  A constant number of $A$ calls on
children with $\lambda=1$ likewise costs $N^{t+o(1)}$.  Special-family early
termination does not alter the ordinary all-input worst case.

\section*{Barrier-transfer and post-quantum preflight}
The directly transferable ingredients are only exact encoding arithmetic,
same-machine exponent composition, and the published FLMV model/endpoint.
VLSI routing and fixed-layout locality are a misleading transfer here: a
constant number of removed blocks does not imply a power shrink of a growing
software input.  Communication cuts and amortized potentials are useful
analogies but require a new theorem covering arbitrary adaptive SAT deciders.
Collision entropy, Schur squares, quadratic relations, and hash/binding ideas
do not presently correspond to the child-length/fallback obstruction; importing
them would be a failed transfer.  Any hidden algebraic structure must also be
tested as an algorithmic shortcut rather than presumed hard.
