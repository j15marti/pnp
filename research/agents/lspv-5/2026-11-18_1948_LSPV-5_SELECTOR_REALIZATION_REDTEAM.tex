\section{Target, quantifiers, and frozen falsifiers}

\textbf{Target claim.}  Test whether the existence of a row-dependent refutable block, together with a large or ``complex'' selector $f:z\mapsto j(z)$, yields a hard one-sided specialist or a square-root-space lower bound.  The relevant quantifiers are
\[
 \forall F\in\UNSAT\;\forall z\in Z(F)\;\exists j\le B(F)
 \quad F_j(z,\cdot)\in\UNSAT,
\]
while the desired specialist must be one fixed deterministic uniform program, receive no answer advice, and pay source parsing, candidate acquisition, every failed child of either polarity, verification, replay, materialization, output, and peak live workspace.

Before searching, the claim was tested against four precise falsifiers:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a source that realizes every selector $f$ but remains easy to solve from its syntax;
  \item a selector in which every shared bit is relevant and each flip changes many output bits, yet $f$ and $f^{-1}$ are algebraically cheap;
  \item a complete selector table of size $o(n^{1/2})$ in the advertised exponent regime; and
  \item an inconsistency between the parameter $n$ and the physical size of all $RB$ maximum-length children.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Arbitrary selector realization}

Let $h\ge1$, $Z=\{0,1\}^h$, and let $f:Z\to[B]$ be arbitrary.  For $s\in Z$ define the full-sign clause
\[
 C_s=\bigvee_{i=1}^{h}\ell_i(s),\qquad
 \ell_i(s)=\begin{cases}x_i,&s_i=0,\\ \neg x_i,&s_i=1.\end{cases}
\]
Thus $C_s$ is false at exactly the row $s$.  Put $C_s$ in block $f(s)$ and let
\[
 H_f=\bigwedge_{s\in Z}C_s.
\]

\begin{theorem}[arbitrary selector, critical deletion, and unique witness]
For every $h\ge1$, every $B\ge1$, and every function $f:Z\to[B]$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $H_f$ is UNSAT;
  \item under row $z=s$, exactly block $f(s)$ is UNSAT and every other block is SAT;
  \item deleting $C_t$ makes the source SAT with the unique satisfying assignment $t$;
  \item all private variable sets are empty and hence pairwise disjoint; and
  \item for $h\ge2$ the primal graph is connected and every original clause is indispensable.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At row $s$, clause $C_s$ is false and every $C_t$ for $t\ne s$ is true.  Hence exactly the block containing $C_s$, namely $f(s)$, is false.  Every row falsifies one clause, so $H_f$ is UNSAT.  If $C_t$ is deleted, row $t$ satisfies every remaining clause.  Any other row $s$ still falsifies the present clause $C_s$, proving uniqueness and indispensability.  Every full-sign clause contains every shared variable, so the primal graph is complete when $h\ge2$.
\end{proof}

The same selector can be realized with explicit disjoint private variables: introduce fresh $u_s$ and place
\[
 (C_s\lor u_s)\wedge(C_s\lor\neg u_s)
\]
in block $f(s)$.  At $z=s$ this is $u_s\wedge\neg u_s$; at every other row it is true independently of $u_s$.  This variant independently checks that the phenomenon is not an artifact of empty private sets.

There are $B^{2^h}$ possible selectors.  Nevertheless the full-sign source is easy.  A parser extracts the unique falsifying mask of each valid clause.  Completeness of the mask set certifies the full-cover UNSAT case.  On the one-clause-deletion promise, the missing mask is recovered by XORing all masks in $Z$ with the masks present in the source, then checked against every clause.  This is a source-aware upper algorithm, not a lower bound.

\section{Dense affine and table-size hostile controls}

For even $h$, define over $\mathbb F_2$
\[
 f(z)=(J+I)z,
\]
where $J$ is the all-ones matrix.  Each input-bit flip changes exactly $h-1$ output bits; every input bit is globally relevant.  Since $h$ is even,
\[
 (J+I)^2=J^2+2J+I=hJ+I=I,
\]
so $f$ is a self-inverse permutation and is evaluated by parity in $O(h^2)$ elementary bit operations (or faster with word operations).  If one target block is deleted in the bijective selector source, its unique satisfying row is $f^{-1}(t)=f(t)$.  Thus dense dependence and bijectivity do not imply hard acquisition.

At the advertised values $R\le n^{.195+o(1)}$ and $B\le n^{.65+o(1)}$, a literal selector table uses
\[
 R\lceil\log_2 B\rceil=n^{.195+o(1)}\log n=o(n^{1/2}).
\]
It may be too large for logarithmic space, but it cannot alone prove a square-root-space lower bound.  Its information content also does not prove that a solver must materialize it; the affine control computes entries on demand.

These results \textbf{FALSIFY/RETIRE} the following standalone mechanisms: selector range, selector-table entropy, full input support, a fixed bad block, or a unique deletion witness as an unrestricted time or space lower bound.  They do not construct a general arbitrary-CNF specialist and do not rule out a more sophisticated model-matched invariant.

\section{The reproduced charged recurrence and a new encoding caveat}

For an authenticated supplied layout
\[
 F(z,y_1,\ldots,y_B)=\bigwedge_{j=1}^B F_j(z,y_j)
\]
with disjoint private sets, $R\le n^{.195+o(1)}$, $B\le n^{.65+o(1)}$, and child length $L\le n^{.35+o(1)}$, a sequential deterministic scan using one fixed total exact child SAT decider $A$ obeys
\begin{align*}
 T'(n)&\le n^{1.195+o(1)}+n^{.845+o(1)}T_A(n^{.35+o(1)}),\\
 S'(n)&\le n^{.35+o(1)}+S_A(n^{.35+o(1)}),
\end{align*}
when only one streamed child is live and all indexing fits the stated budget.  Hence a one-step $n^{1.33+o(1)}$ time bound requires child exponent $t\le97/70$, and $n^{.5+o(1)}$ space requires $s\le10/7$.  A generic polynomial SAT seed supplies neither numerical gate.  The hereditary fixed point $(1.30,.35)$ remains conditional on an all-input, solver-dependent, recursively closed compiler.

There is an additional source-size consistency obligation.  If all $RB$ maximum-length children are independent and physically explicit, then their aggregate size is
\[
 RBL=n^{.195+.65+.35+o(1)}=n^{1.195+o(1)}.
\]
Thus $n$ cannot simultaneously be ordinary encoded input length and the parameter governing all independent maximum-size children.  One must either (i) remeasure exponents in the true expanded input length, or (ii) provide a compressed source with charged random access, virtual child generation, replay, and materialization.  This is a model-consistency firewall, not a proof that every compressed layout is hard.

\section{Correct paths, polarity, and wrong-path soundness}

\paragraph{SAT output.}
A sound successful path outputs one common row $z$ plus a private witness for every block; the verifier checks every original-source constraint.  Wrong or malformed paths return $\UNKNOWN$, never UNSAT.  Candidate acquisition and witness output are charged.

\paragraph{UNSAT output.}
A sound successful path supplies, for every row $z$, a block index and either a source-bound local refutation or a replay of a total exact child decider.  Every universal row is checked.  Indices alone are not arbitrary-CNF refutations.  Wrong paths return $\UNKNOWN$, never SAT.

\paragraph{Free polarity.}
A free bit that identifies the correct side is answer advice.  Two uniformly sound partial specialists become a decision algorithm only after all-input completeness and a common worst-case clock are proved.  Timeout cannot be interpreted as the opposite label without such a theorem.

\paragraph{$k$ verifiable bits.}
If a total verifier is sound on every path, complete for one polarity, and costs at most $V(n)$ time and $W(n)$ writable space on every capped replay, deterministic enumeration costs
\[
 2^{k(n)}V(n)\operatorname{polylog}n
 \quad\text{time},\qquad
 W(n)+O(k(n)+\log V(n))
 \quad\text{space}.
\]
At verifier time $n^{1.33+o(1)}$, space $n^{.5+o(1)}$, and $k=\alpha\log_2 n+o(\log n)$, Buss--Williams' $1.34070$ point excludes all fixed $\alpha<.01070$; $\alpha\le.0106$ is a safe rounded statement.  The full selector table has $k=n^{.195+o(1)}\log n$, far outside this logarithmic boundary.

\paragraph{Adaptive and alternating strategies.}
A supplied favorable root-to-leaf path is not the whole strategy.  A depth-$k$ adaptive strategy may have $2^k$ leaves.  Any universal branch must be visited or represented by a sound globally verified policy.  ``Correct path'' cannot erase the $\forall z$ or $\forall y$ quantifiers.

\paragraph{Certificate access.}
An external random-access read-only certificate tape is a verifier/advice model distinct from ordinary writable workspace.  Its length, address computation, access time, and replay are charged.  A standard one-way nondeterministic guess is not freely reread unless stored.
