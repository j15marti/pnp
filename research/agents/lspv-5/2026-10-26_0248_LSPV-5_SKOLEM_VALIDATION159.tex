\section*{Executive finding}
The circuit-size/coNTIME/ARC--FLMV spine remains incomplete.  Even when an
adaptive UNSAT certificate supplies an explicit circuit-valued extension for
every source boundary, checking that the extension works for \emph{all}
feasible boundaries is coNP-complete on a regular exact-$(2,2)$ source family.
The hardness persists with a proper 3-CNF child, literal retention of every
source name, fan-in-two witness circuits, and a genuine second recursive level.

This does not show that every proof format pays a coNP-complete validation
step: a locally checkable derivation can avoid generic semantic validation.
It shows that one cannot count only guest calls $\sum_iT_A(m_i)$ while treating
a supplied global Skolem circuit as self-authenticating.  A sound local proof
system or the validation exponent must be charged.  Under
$\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$, coNP also equals P qualitatively, but this yields
neither $d=1.802$ nor subpolynomial writable space.  P versus NP remains
\status{OPEN}.

\section*{Falsifiers fixed before search}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A recursive NO certificate may contain a cycle or nondecreasing semantic
      obligation rather than a well-founded proof.
\item The weighted leaf inequality may omit topology, source indexing,
      witness-circuit evaluation, retained ancestors, and universal validation.
\item A coNTIME verifier may accept invalid certificates or timeouts, reversing
      the complement quantifiers needed for UNSAT.
\item A local reduction may preserve syntax at one level but fail child grammar,
      named-index access, or retained-space accounting at level two.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Exact semantic obligation}
For a proper 3-CNF source $F(X)$, proper child $H(X,E)$, and explicitly
supplied fan-in-two circuits $W=(W_e)_{e\in E}$, define
\[
 \operatorname{SkValid}(F,H,W)\iff
 \forall\alpha\in\{0,1\}^X\bigl(F(\alpha)=1\Rightarrow
 H(\alpha,W(\alpha))=1\bigr).
\]
This is weaker than discovering a Skolem circuit and stronger than checking it
on one guessed boundary.

\section*{Regular exact-$(2,2)$ hardness}
\begin{theorem}[SKOLEM-VALIDATION159]
$\operatorname{SkValid}$ is coNP-complete even when: (i) $F$ is a proper
3-CNF and every variable occurs exactly twice positively and twice negatively;
(ii) every word $u\in\{0,1\}^q$ extends to a model of $F$; (iii) $H$ is a
proper 3-CNF containing every named source variable literally; (iv) every
component of $W$ is an explicit fan-in-two circuit; and (v) there is a second
child $H^+=H\wedge(r\vee s\vee t)$ with fixed extension $r=1,s=t=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Membership in coNP is immediate: a rejecting certificate is one assignment
$\alpha$ with $F(\alpha)=1$ and $H(\alpha,W(\alpha))=0$.

Reduce from \textsc{Circuit-Tautology}.  For each input $u_i$ create
\[
\begin{split}
G_i={}&(u_i\vee a_i\vee b_i)\wedge
(u_i\vee\neg a_i\vee\neg b_i)\\
&\wedge(\neg u_i\vee a_i\vee\neg b_i)\wedge
(\neg u_i\vee\neg a_i\vee b_i).
\end{split}
\]
Every variable occurs twice with each sign, all clauses are proper and
distinct, and either value of $u_i$ has two extensions to $(a_i,b_i)$.
Thus every word $u$ occurs among the models of $F_C=\bigwedge_iG_i$.

Compile circuit $C$ gate-by-gate with Tseitin constraints.  Replace every
binary clause $(p\vee q)$ by
\[(p\vee q\vee z)\wedge(p\vee q\vee\neg z)\]
with fresh $z$.  Force output $o$ true with
\[
(o\vee c\vee d)\wedge(o\vee c\vee\neg d)\wedge
(o\vee\neg c\vee d)\wedge(o\vee\neg c\vee\neg d).
\]
For each absent source name $x$, append the neutral pad
\[T(x)=(x\vee p\vee r)\wedge(\neg x\vee p\vee r)\]
and set $p=1,r=0$.  Let $W_C$ evaluate all circuit gates and choose all
Tseitin, widening, and pad auxiliaries.  Then
\[H_C(\alpha,W_C(\alpha))=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C(u(\alpha))=1.
\]
Every $u$ has a source extension, so SkValid holds exactly when $C$ is a
tautology.  The construction is polynomial and fan-in two.  Appending
$(r\vee s\vee t)$ and the fixed extension preserves the equivalence at an
actual second level.
\end{proof}

The theorem rules out only treating a global Skolem circuit as free.  A local
proof format may avoid generic validation, but must prove bounded coverage of
every UNSAT input and charge construction, checking, topology, and retained
state.

\section*{Restricted black-box lemma}
\begin{lemma}
If a deterministic validator accesses only
$R_W(u)=H(u,W(u))$ on $u\in\{0,1\}^q$, distinguishing the all-one function
from every function with exactly one zero needs $2^q$ worst-case queries.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
After fewer than $2^q$ queries choose unqueried $u^\star$.  The all-one oracle
and the oracle zero only at $u^\star$ give identical transcripts.
\end{proof}
Circuit inspection, algebra, SAT, or a local proof may beat the black-box bound,
but their real costs must then appear in the ledger.

\section*{Repaired weighted-leaf ledger}
For leaf lengths $m_i$ and one fixed same-machine decider $A$,
\[
\begin{split}
T_{\rm cert}={}&C_{\rm topology}+C_{\rm source}+C_{\rm policy}\\
&+\sum_i(C_{{\rm build},i}+V_i+T_A(m_i)),
\end{split}
\]
where $V_i$ includes syntax, source/child index access, witness evaluation,
compatibility, and any universal validation.  If the spectrum gives guest
exponent $h_d=\max_j(a_j+d\lambda_j)$, the scoped bound is
\[t'\le\max\{r,u_{\rm agg},h_d,v\},\]
unless validation exponent $v$ is proved absorbed.  If validation makes
$N^b$ calls to $A$ on length $N^\rho$, the same-solver contribution is
$N^{b+\rho d+o(1)}$.  A parent-size query ($\rho=1$) does not contract.
Materializing the query costs space exponent $\rho$; lazy access requires a
charged indexer and charges every probe.

\section*{Decisive next lemma}
For every ordinary UNSAT input and the same fixed decider $A$, construct a
well-founded locally checkable certificate DAG with bit-local source/child
indexing, two-level eligibility, sound invalid-record and timeout behavior,
and full construction, validation, solver-call, replay, and peak-space
exponents inside the strict FLMV budget.  Without this uniform theorem,
neither $d=1.802$ arithmetic nor the weighted leaf inequality reaches the
all-input same-machine endpoint.
