\section*{Executive finding}
This run corrects a material error in the preceding LSPV-5/root synthesis.  A
Boolean decision has only two final labels, but it can have exponentially many
query transcripts.  The LSPV-1 parity promise gives an exact proper-3-CNF
example: every deterministic exact decider starting before acquisition must
read at least one bit in each of $p$ disjoint sensitive groups, while the final
answer is one bit.  Thus the old inference ``two labels, hence at most two
transcripts'' is \status{FALSIFIED}.  It is replaced below by the standard
sensitivity lower bound on queries.

The same example also blocks the opposite overreach: many transcripts do not
imply large writable space or large circuits.  Parity streams through one
accumulator bit plus an index and has a linear-size logarithmic-depth NAND
circuit.  Hence this is a restricted input-read lower bound, not a simultaneous
FLMV time--space lower bound.

This run additionally proves a compatibility-deficit theorem.  Even if every
one- and two-channel projection is full and uniform, the joint realizable
image can have only $k+1$ states rather than $2^k$.  Summing local channel
supports therefore needs an independently proved global compatibility or
direct-sum theorem and a proof that the target computation must materialize
that joint object.  No such unrestricted theorem is currently present.
Accordingly P versus NP, the all-UNSAT certificate arrow, the circuit lower
bound arrow, and ARC--FLMV remain \status{OPEN}.

        the space of another or confuse transcript diversity with circuit size.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Lemma 1: sensitivity forces reads, not large final state}
Let $f:\{0,1\}^p\to\{0,1\}$ and let a deterministic bit-query algorithm be
correct on every input.  Write
\[
  S_x=\{i:f(x)\ne f(x\oplus e_i)\},\qquad s(f,x)=|S_x|.
\]
Then the algorithm queries every coordinate in $S_x$ on input $x$, and hence
its query count $q(x)$ satisfies
\[
  q(x)\ge s(f,x).
\]
Indeed, if it does not query $i\in S_x$, the executions on $x$ and
$x\oplus e_i$ have the same answers to every query, so determinism gives the
same final output, contradicting sensitivity.  This proof charges the actual
read and makes no claim about writable state after the read.

For parity, $s(f,x)=p$ for every $x$.  Thus every initial-checkpoint exact
decision tree reads all $p$ bits and the family contains $2^p$ distinct complete
answer transcripts.  Yet a streaming implementation stores only parity plus an
$O(\log p)$ address, and the restricted parity function has a linear-size,
$O(\log p)$-depth NAND circuit.  Consequently:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{0.95\linewidth}{@{}lXl@{}}
\toprule
claim & exact scope & status\\
\midrule
two labels cap transcripts & arbitrary deterministic exact decision & \status{FALSIFIED}\\
$q(x)\ge s(f,x)$ & deterministic bit-query model & \status{PROVED}\\
parity promise needs $p$ initial reads & LSPV-1 fixed-template promise & \status{PROVED/RESTRICTED}\\
many transcripts force large peak space or circuit size & unrestricted algorithms & \status{FALSIFIED}\\
parity promise yields FLMV-forbidden SAT pair & ordinary all-input SAT & \status{OPEN}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
At a later checkpoint after the input has been scanned, a single cached parity
bit can suffice.  The scan cannot be charged again, but it also cannot be
erased from the total execution ledger.

\section*{Theorem 2: pairwise-full projections can have a tiny joint image}
For a map $L=(L_1,\ldots,L_k):X\to\prod_iY_i$, define the compatibility deficit
\[
  \Delta(L)=\sum_{i=1}^k\log_2|\operatorname{im}L_i|
     -\log_2|\operatorname{im}L|\ge0.
\]
If a computation is required to materialize the full joint vector $L(x)$, the
information supplied by its possible values is exactly
\[
  \log_2|\operatorname{im}L|
  =\sum_i\log_2|\operatorname{im}L_i|-\Delta(L),
\]
not the uncorrected sum of local support logarithms.  A computation required
only to output one bit $g(L(x))$ need not materialize even this joint vector.

The deficit can be almost $k$ even when every pair projection is full and
uniform.  Fix $r\ge2$, let $k=2^r-1$, index coordinates by nonzero vectors
$v\in\mathbb F_2^r$, and for each seed $z\in\mathbb F_2^r$ define
\[
  L(z)_v=\langle v,z\rangle\pmod2.
\]
Every coordinate takes both values.  Any two distinct nonzero vectors in
$\mathbb F_2^r$ are linearly independent, so the corresponding map
$z\mapsto(\langle v,z\rangle,\langle w,z\rangle)$ is onto
$\mathbb F_2^2$, with every pair value attained $2^{r-2}$ times.  Nevertheless
$L$ is injective because the nonzero coordinate vectors include a basis.
Therefore
\[
 |\operatorname{im}L|=2^r=k+1,
 \qquad \Delta(L)=k-r.
\]
Thus full singleton and pair signatures coexist with an exponential gap
between the local product $2^k$ and the joint image $k+1$.  This is the binary
simplex/Hadamard evaluation code, and its linear-algebraic recognizer makes it
an easy family rather than a hardness witness.

\section*{Circuit, coNTIME, and ARC--FLMV scope}
Nothing here proves that P=NP yields a particular $d=1.802$
subpolynomial-space SAT algorithm.  P=NP supplies some finite polynomial
exponent; the stronger near-boundary pair is independent.  Refuting that
stronger pair would not separate P from NP.

The van Melkebeek circuit lower-bound endpoint applies to full decision
circuits under its exact coNTIME hypotheses.  Per-instance relations,
certificate fragments, or a collection of separately feasible terminal
outputs do not automatically give such a circuit.  Invalid certificates and
timeouts must be included on the correct complement side.  The
Bathie--Williams depth statement remains restricted to its strong-uniformity
and circuit model.  The signed exact-$(2,2)$ compiler currently supplies only
the explicitly verified local-access statements; ordinary-SAT transport,
two recursive levels, retained space, and exact SAT/UNSAT equivalence remain
\status{CONDITIONAL/OPEN}.  No time exponent from one algorithm is paired here
with a space exponent from another.

The weighted leaf-volume inequality
\[
  \sum_i m_i^d\le M^{d-1}\sum_i m_i\qquad(0\le m_i\le M, d\ge1)
\]
is arithmetically \status{PROVED}, but it becomes useful only after all leaf
lengths, certificate and verification work, source-index/gate access, failed
strategies, reconstruction, and both SAT and UNSAT soundness have been charged.
It does not provide all-UNSAT certificate coverage by itself.

for every legal adaptive all-UNSAT computation, either (i) a large joint image

caching, affine/Horn/2SAT/graph-factor recognition, source rereads, and cheaper
representations.  Sensitivity supplies reads, but a superlinear or genuine
time--space consequence still needs a new argument.
