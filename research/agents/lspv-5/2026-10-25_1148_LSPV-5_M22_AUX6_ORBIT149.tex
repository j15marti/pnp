\section{Scope and falsifiable links}
A formula in $\CM$ has unique proper three-variable clauses, every clause all positive or all negative, and every active variable occurring exactly twice per sign.  The canonical target $\CS$ excludes only formulas in which every clause has its sign-complement partner.  Partial partners, easy instances, and disconnected inputs remain.  D\"ocker's theorem establishes NP-completeness of $\CM$-SAT and satisfiability of the fully paired subfamily; it does not establish a decision-time lower bound \cite{docker}.

The preceding AUX-PORT-CONGRUENCE148 theorem considered a standalone pure-sign completion block with $r$ source ports, $p$ pre-used positive source occurrences and $a$ private auxiliaries.  Exact final signed degree $(2,2)$ forces
\[
 a\equiv0\pmod3,\qquad p\equiv2r\pmod3.
\]
The minimum $a=3$ cases were exhaustively negative.  This run tests the first open congruence-allowed point, $r=3,a=6,p=3$, with one pre-used positive occurrence on each port.

Three falsifiable proposals were separated.  (H1) A standalone uniform-sign six-auxiliary equality block exists.  This is \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED computationally at the declared finite parameter}.  (H2) simultaneous auxiliary renaming reduces the two-witness search to finitely many complete cases.  This is \textbf{PROVED/EXACT}: there are 84 witness-pair orbits.  (H3) failure of the gadget implies compulsory SAT work.  This inference is \textbf{FALSIFIED}: the design search is a fixed offline computation and supplies no all-decider lower bound.

\section{Exact finite feasibility model}
Let the ports be $x_0,x_1,x_2$ and the auxiliaries $u_0,\ldots,u_5$.  For every three-element support $e\subseteq[9]$, binary variables $P_e,N_e$ choose the positive or negative clause on $e$.  There are $\binom93=84$ candidate supports per sign.  Signed-degree equations are
\[
 \sum_{e\ni x_i}P_e=1,\quad \sum_{e\ni x_i}N_e=2,
 \qquad
 \sum_{e\ni u_j}P_e=\sum_{e\ni u_j}N_e=2.
\]
Thus exactly five positive and six negative clauses are selected.

For every nonconstant $z\in\{0,1\}^3$ and every auxiliary word $y\in\{0,1\}^6$, at least one selected clause must be false:
\[
 \sum_{e:\,(z,y)|_e=000}P_e+
 \sum_{e:\,(z,y)|_e=111}N_e\ge1.
\]
There are $6\cdot64=384$ such constraints.  Two six-bit witness vectors encode extensions of $000$ and $111$ using ordinary conditional linear inequalities.  All variables are binary; no semantic oracle or fractional relaxation is accepted.

The controls are essential.  The 18 degree equations alone are feasible.  The degree equations plus extension of both constant port words are also feasible; a returned formula has 22 witnesses for $000$, 9 for $111$, and extends all eight port words.  Only after the 384 nonconstant-rejection constraints are added does the 180-variable, 610-constraint model become infeasible.

\section{Independent symmetry-complete formulation}
To remove any dependence on the aggregate witness linearization, fix a witness pair $(y^0,y^1)\in(\{0,1\}^6)^2$.  Simultaneous renaming of the six auxiliaries preserves every support, degree, satisfaction and projection requirement.  Hence its orbit is determined exactly by
\[
 (n_{00},n_{01},n_{10},n_{11}),\qquad
 n_{00}+n_{01}+n_{10}+n_{11}=6.
\]
The number of orbits is the number of weak compositions,
\[
 \binom{6+4-1}{4-1}=\binom93=84.
\]
For each canonical representative, constant-witness satisfaction is imposed by forbidding every clause false under that fixed full assignment; no witness variables remain.  All 84 independent 168-binary-variable models returned infeasible, with zero feasible, timeout, or unknown cases.

This exhausts all witness pairs modulo a proved symmetry.  Nevertheless, the final exclusion is labeled \textbf{COMPUTATIONAL}, because the HiGHS infeasibility decisions are not accompanied here by independently checkable integer or Farkas certificates.  It excludes exactly the declared $a=6$ standalone proper pure-sign model.  It does not exclude $a\ge9$, shared auxiliaries or clauses across source variables, contextual ports, mixed-sign clauses, repeated literals, larger occurrence budgets, or a different semantic reduction.
