\section{Four falsifiers fixed before source search}
\begin{description}
\item[F1.] From ``each cut has some long computation'' one may sum the cutwise maxima and infer one common hard input.  \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}: the quantifiers do not commute.
\item[F2.] Distinct crossing sequences by themselves force an average length $\Omega(m/B)$, where one complete crossing record has at most $B$ bits.  \textbf{PROVED/EXACT} for nonempty sequences by Lemma~\ref{lem:code}.
\item[F3.] The per-cut averages can be combined without changing the input.  \textbf{PROVED/EXACT}: double counting gives one common YES input, Theorem~\ref{th:product}.
\item[F4.] The resulting $t+s\ge2$ stripe transfers to indexed RAT/RAM or to unrestricted ordinary SAT.  \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED AS A TRANSFER}: indexed comparison solves this easy promise in near-linear bit time and logarithmic work space; the family does not decide malformed or arbitrary SAT inputs.
\end{description}

\section{Model and exact hypotheses}
Fix one deterministic total machine $M$ with one two-way \emph{sequential} read-only input head and a fixed finite number $k$ of sequential work tapes.  Its input head starts at the declared left end.  Let $T(N)$ be its worst-case transitions and $S(N)$ the maximum number of simultaneously visited work-tape cells, excluding the input.  The tape alphabets and $k$ are fixed.

For every $m\ge1$, assume the published canonical family $E_m(a,b)$, $a,b\in\{0,1\}^m$, has physical length $N_m=\Theta(m\log(m+2))$, layout
\[
       \operatorname{LEFT}(a)\;\operatorname{GAP}\;\operatorname{RIGHT}(b),
\]
with $D_m=\Theta(N_m)$ adjacent input-cell boundaries in the fixed gap, identical names and lengths for fixed $m$, and
\[
                  E_m(a,b)\in\SAT\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a=b. \tag{1}
\]
Every differing right bit changes a source position beyond every gap cut.  These are the locality hypotheses of EXACT22-TAPE-TRANSPORT1; the generator scripts named there were not published at that path, so this report does not relabel the concrete construction as independently reconstructed.

At one fixed gap boundary $g$, record after every input-head crossing: finite control, crossing direction, the contents of every visited work-tape interval, the endpoints of those intervals, and all work-head positions.  A complete record has
\[
 B_m\le c_k\bigl(S(N_m)+\log(T(N_m)+N_m)+1\bigr) \tag{2}
\]
bits for a fixed constant $c_k$.  Work \emph{contents alone} are insufficient; head positions and control are load-bearing.  Crossings are already transitions and are not added again to physical time.

\section{The average-code repair}
\begin{lemma}[Distinct nonempty sequence average]\label{lem:code}
Let $K=2^m$ distinct nonempty words be drawn from an alphabet of size at most $2^B$, with $B\ge1$.  If their lengths are $q_1,\ldots,q_K$, then
\[
                       \frac1K\sum_{i=1}^Kq_i\ \ge\ \frac{m}{4B}. \tag{3}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Enlarging the alphabet can only decrease the minimum possible total length, so take alphabet size $A\le2^B$ and write $y=\log_A K\ge m/B$.  If $y\le4$, nonemptiness gives average length at least one, hence at least $y/4\ge m/(4B)$.  If $y>4$, put $L=\lfloor y\rfloor-1\ge y/2$.  There are fewer than
\[
       \sum_{j=1}^{L-1}A^j < \frac{A^L}{A-1}\le\frac{K}{A(A-1)}\le\frac K2
\]
words of length below $L$.  At least half of the $K$ distinct words therefore have length at least $L$, so their average is at least $L/2\ge y/4\ge m/(4B)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Common-input crossing product]\label{th:product}
Under (1)--(2), every correct $M$ satisfies, for infinitely many canonical lengths $N_m$,
\[
 T(N_m)\bigl(S(N_m)+\log(T(N_m)+N_m)+1\bigr)
        =\Omega\!\left(\frac{N_m^2}{\log N_m}\right). \tag{4}
\]
This is a native one-input-head tape theorem for the declared promise family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each $a$, correctness makes $M$ accept $E_m(a,a)$.  Its crossing sequence at every gap cut is nonempty: otherwise the accepting run fails to inspect a right-dependent source bit beyond that cut; changing that bit creates some $E_m(a,b)$ with $b\ne a$ following the same run, contradicting (1) and total correctness on the promise.

Fix a cut $g$.  The $2^m$ complete crossing sequences for $E_m(a,a)$ are pairwise distinct.  If the sequences for $a\ne b$ were equal, the standard cut-and-paste induction, now using the full work configurations, control and directions in each record, would splice the left part of the accepting run on $E_m(a,a)$ to the right part of the accepting run on $E_m(b,b)$.  It would accept the inconsistent hybrid $E_m(a,b)$, contradicting (1).  Lemma~\ref{lem:code} therefore gives
\[
              2^{-m}\sum_a q_g(a)\ge \frac{m}{4B_m}. \tag{5}
\]
Sum (5) over the $D_m=\Theta(N_m)$ gap cuts and interchange the finite sums:
\[
 2^{-m}\sum_a\sum_gq_g(a)\ge\frac{D_m m}{4B_m}. \tag{6}
\]
Hence one \emph{common} $a_*$ has $\sum_gq_g(a_*)\ge D_m m/(4B_m)$.  One transition crosses at most one adjacent input boundary, so
\[
 T(N_m)\ge\sum_gq_g(a_*)\ge\frac{D_m m}{4B_m}.
\]
Use $D_m=\Theta(N_m)$, $m=\Theta(N_m/\log N_m)$ and (2) to obtain (4).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Polynomial exponent corollary.}
If this same sequential-input machine has polynomial ceilings $T(N)=N^{t+o(1)}$ and $S(N)=N^{s+o(1)}$, $s\ge0$, then (4) implies $t+s\ge2$.  This is not the FLMV indexed model.  It cannot be combined with the time coordinate of one RAM implementation and the space coordinate of another.

\section{Explicit quantifier countercontrol}
The pure inference
\[
        \forall g\ \exists a:q_g(a)\ge L
        \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
        \exists a:\sum_gq_g(a)\ge |G|L \tag{7}
\]
is false.  For $|G|=|A|=D$, set $q_g(a)=H$ when $a=g$ and $q_g(a)=1$ otherwise.  Every cut has maximum $H$, but every input has total $H+D-1$, not $DH$.  The finite control used $D=H=32$: the sum of cutwise maxima is $1024$ while the largest single-input total is $63$.  Theorem~\ref{th:product} avoids (7) by proving a per-cut \emph{average} and then applying the exact double sum (6).

The zero-dependency reproducer exhaustively compared the closed-form shortest distinct-word total with brute enumeration on seven small alphabets/counts, checked (3) for $1008$ parameter pairs, checked the double-average inequality in $240$ cyclic adversarial assignments, and checked the max-only countercontrol: $1738$ assertion groups, zero failures, no randomness.  This is \textbf{COMPUTATIONAL} support for the separate proof, not measured TM time.
