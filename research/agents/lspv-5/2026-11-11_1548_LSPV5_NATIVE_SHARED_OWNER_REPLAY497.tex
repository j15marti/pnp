\section{Target claim and fixed falsifiers}

For the exact archived /386 accepted-byte language, one paid conservative
AND2 owner and its authentic zero-additional-charge DEF facets may be shared
by four signed weakening macros while preserving the original SOURCE records,
parent chronology, strict oriented Resolution, freshness, and count-once
work.  The exact LSPV-1/582 bytes should be accepted at
\[
W=68\text{ paid RES}+2\text{ globally distinct paid owners}=70.
\]
This is a local calculus-availability statement, not a minimum-refutation
claim.

Before search and replay I fixed four falsifiers:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the unchanged original CLI rejects the exact 7,160-byte packet;
\item a forged facet, future parent, wrong pivot, freshness collision, forged
SOURCE, or underpayment is accepted;
\item shared facets or owner IDs are charged separately or inconsistently;
\item a current peer reproducer fails from cross-case state leakage, showing
that its reported hostile coverage cannot be reconstructed from the published
program.
\end{enumerate}
Falsifiers 1--3 did not occur.  Falsifier 4 did occur for the unmodified
LSPV-3/579 runner and is repaired below.

\section{Published LSPV-3/579 hostile-runner failure and exact repair}
I ran the current unmodified LSPV-3/579 program
\path{experiments/arc/lspv3_579_terminal_live_batch_copy.cjs}, blob
\texttt{62c44d7a81fd6d6a7e14741c5eaefa9563e674bb}, against its pinned seed
\texttt{5edeac8fbbe39764cfd3d075acd3c6ce7b5c87fe} and the same unchanged
/386 checker.  The four positive cases execute first, but the hostile suite
does not complete.  The exact defect is
\[
\texttt{d.records=[...seed.records.slice(0,cut),...extra,...oldTail]}.
\]
The first 25 records are shared by reference with the global
\texttt{seed}.  The hostile
\texttt{forged\_original\_source} changes
\texttt{d.records[3].c[0]} to \(-1\), thereby also changing
\texttt{seed.records[3].c[0]}.  The next call to \texttt{build(4)}
fails at \texttt{assert(C.includes(1))} before invoking the checker.
This is cross-case hostile-state leakage.  It does not show acceptance of a
forgery; it shows that the published program did not execute its full claimed
negative matrix.

The minimal repair is
\[
\texttt{seed.records.slice(0,cut).map(copy)}.
\]
With only this deep-copy change, the same pinned program logic and checker
accept all four positive terminal-live shared-owner packets:
\[
 (m,W,\text{records})=(1,35,85),(2,38,88),(3,41,91),(4,44,94),
\]
and reject all nine hostile cases, including the forged SOURCE.  Thus the
mathematical finite result survives the repair, while the original runner's
hostile-coverage claim must be qualified until its published source is fixed.

\section{Cost-sound abstraction obligation}
For this exact concrete trace, the identity abstraction on SOURCE, owner, DEF
and RES nodes is cost-sound:
\[
W^{\#}=68+2=70=W_{\mathrm{concrete}},
\]
and it preserves the terminal empty clause.  Reusing owner facets costs zero
additional owner payments but the facet bytes, parent retrieval, checks,
source access, live retention, and serialization still consume physical
resources.  The result does not supply a full source-authenticated shared-DAG
semantics with concrete-step inclusion for all alleged historical operations.
An abstraction that charges one owner per weakened clause overestimates this
genuine path; one that invents free weakening underestimates it.
