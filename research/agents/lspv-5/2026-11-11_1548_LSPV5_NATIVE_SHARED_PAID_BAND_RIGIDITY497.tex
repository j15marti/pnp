\section*{Exact lemma}
Let a proof DAG have its $S$ source and other leaf records fixed at positions $0,\dots,S-1$, derived non-sink
nodes $Q$, and the sink at position $n-1$. Let $\mathrm{fan}(j)$ be the number of parent references to $j$, and for
$j\in Q$ let $a(j)$ and $d(j)$ be the numbers of derived ancestors and of non-sink derived descendants.
\begin{theorem}[Band rigidity; PROVED/EXACT]
Suppose that for every boundary $b=10^k$ with $S<b\le n-2$, every $j\in Q$ with
$S+a(j)<b\le n-2-d(j)$ satisfies $\mathrm{fan}(j)=1$. Then the total number of decimal characters in parent
references is the same for every legal source-preserving topological order.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
In linear extensions of a finite poset, an element can occupy exactly the positions from
$|\text{ancestors}|$ to $|\text{poset}|-1-|\text{descendants}|$; here these are
$S+a(j)$ and $n-2-d(j)$. Write $\mathrm{len}(p)=1+\#\{k\ge1:p\ge10^k\}$. The total equals a constant from
source references, plus $\sum_{j\in Q}\mathrm{fan}(j)$, plus, for each boundary $b$, the term
$\sum_{j:\,L(j)\ge b}\mathrm{fan}(j)$. Nodes forced on one side of $b$ contribute a fixed amount. The flexible nodes all have fanout 1,
and the number of them placed at or after $b$ is $(n-1-b)-\#\{\text{forced}\ge b\}$, which is also constant.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}
LSPV-4/552's tree-like invariance is the special case where every fanout is 1. The tree-like condition is
\textbf{sufficient, not necessary}: on the shared /575 DAG the only boundary is $b=100$, its two flexible nodes
both have fanout 1, and every fanout-2 node has latest legal position 89. By the contrapositive, any DAG on which
the digit total varies, including LSPV-4/552's nonnative 9-versus-11 control, has a flexible node with
fanout $\ne1$ straddling some decimal boundary.
\end{corollary}
Only sufficiency is claimed; no converse is asserted. The lemma concerns serialized evidence bytes, not
logical work $W$. Because this checker accepts sources and DEF views at any position, an order that interleaves
them falls outside the lemma, so the lemma does not describe all accepted byte strings.
