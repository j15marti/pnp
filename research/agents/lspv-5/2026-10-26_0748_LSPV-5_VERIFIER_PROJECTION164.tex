\section{Exact projection theorem}
Let $V_N(F,w)$ be the standard polynomial-size uniform verifier circuit for
SAT on $N$-bit formula encodings and $q(N)$ witness bits.  Invalid encodings
are rejected.  Define
\[
 D_N(F)=\bigvee_{w\in\{0,1\}^{q(N)}}V_N(F,w).                       \tag{2}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Verifier projection firewall]
The family $V_N$ exists unconditionally.  Equation~(2) computes the full SAT
decision function.  The following implications are exact in the standard
Boolean-circuit model.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If a deterministic polynomial-time constructor on $1^N$ outputs a
polynomial-size $D_N$ satisfying (2), then $P=NP$.
\item Polynomial-size $D_N$ satisfying (2) exist nonuniformly for every $N$
if and only if $\SAT\in P/\mathrm{poly}$; because SAT is NP-complete, this is
equivalent to $NP\subseteq P/\mathrm{poly}$.
\item From $V_N$ alone one always obtains the explicit upper bound
\[
 |D_N|\le 2^{q(N)}|V_N|+2^{q(N)}-1,                                \tag{3}
\]
by hardwiring every witness and ORing the copies.  Equation~(3) is not a
lower bound and cannot exclude a smaller decision circuit.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The verifier identity is the definition of SAT membership and the
Cook--Levin verifier characterization \cite{cook1971}.  A uniform constructor
followed by polynomial-time circuit evaluation decides SAT in polynomial
time, proving item 1.  Item 2 is the definition of $P/\mathrm{poly}$ applied
to SAT plus polynomial many-one closure for NP.  For item 3, take one copy of
$V_N$ for every witness string, hardwire that string, and connect the
$2^{q(N)}$ outputs with a fan-in-two OR tree.
\end{proof}

Thus a claimed generic polynomial elimination step would settle the uniform
$P$ versus $NP$ question, while mere nonuniform existence would imply the
Karp--Lipton circuit premise and its polynomial-hierarchy collapse
consequence \cite{karplipton1980}.  This is a barrier classification, not a
proof that polynomial decision circuits do not exist.

\begin{corollary}[Dual canonical-selector projection]
For Role~1's syntax-directed first-conflict circuit $J_F$, let
$R(F,y)=\operatorname{ValidConflict}_F(y,J_F(y))$.  Its proved pointwise
identity is $R(F,y)=1-F(y)$.  Therefore
\[
 \forall y\ R(F,y)=1\iff F\in\UNSAT,
 \qquad
 \exists y\ \neg R(F,y)=1\iff F\in\SAT.                           \tag{4}
\]
Producing $J_F$ and checking its local wiring does not authenticate the
universal quantifier.  A parent-scale SAT call used for (4) is the original
decision problem and certifies no contraction.
\end{corollary}
