\section{Exact fixed-syndrome theorem}
Let $B(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ be a 2-CNF with $m_2$ clauses.  Let
$A\in\mathbb F_2^{\ell\times n}$ and $b\in\mathbb F_2^\ell$ be explicit.
Paid Gaussian elimination either detects inconsistency or reduces the system
to rank $\rho\le\ell$; hence assume $A$ has independent rows.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-syndrome dispatch]\label{thm:fixed}
There is a uniform deterministic exact algorithm for
\[
B(x)\wedge(Ax=b)
\]
whose time is $(n+m_2)^{O(\rho)}$ plus polynomial parsing, rank reduction,
reconstruction and source replay.  In particular, every fixed-$\rho$ rung is
in P; $\rho=O(\log N)$ gives at most quasipolynomial
$N^{O(\log N)}$ time.  This is an XP-style upper bound in $\rho$, not a claim
that every solver is fixed-parameter tractable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $G=\mathbb F_2^\rho$.  For column $j$ of $A$, define
\[
g_j(0)=0,\qquad g_j(1)=A_{*j}\in G.
\]
Then, for every Boolean assignment $x$,
\[
\sum_{j=1}^n g_j(x_j)=Ax.
\]
Consequently $B(x)\wedge Ax=b$ is exactly the corresponding weighted
2-SAT-MOD$_G$ instance, with the same satisfying assignments and no
auxiliary-variable or witness-multiplicity issue.  Apply the
$G=\mathbb F_2^\rho$ specialization following Proposition~5.2 of
Brakensiek--Gopi--Guruswami~\cite{BGG}.  On SAT, retain the assignment supplied
by that algorithm, reverse the paid row operations and replay every original
2-CNF and affine record.  UNSAT needs only the exact decision bit; no
particular proof system is imposed.  All representation and conversion work
is polynomial in the explicit source length.
\end{proof}

Negated affine literals merely add a known constant to $b$.  Multiple parity
conditions are not tested separately: their entire joint syndrome is the
single group element in $G$.  Thus Role~4's marginal-feasibility and affine-
hull counterexamples do not defeat the theorem; they defeat only weaker
summaries of the joint syndrome.

\rho\text{ unrestricted}&\text{contains an NP-complete language}&\text{all-decider LB OPEN}
\end{array}                                             \tag{5}
\]

$(\ell_1\vee\ell_2\vee\ell_3)$ introduce fresh selectors
$s_1,s_2,s_3$, one row $s_1\oplus s_2\oplus s_3=1$, three 2-CNF
