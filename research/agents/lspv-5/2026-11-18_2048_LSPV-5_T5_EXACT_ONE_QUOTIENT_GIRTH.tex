\section{Exact quotient-template theorem}
For a Q-instance $F$ on variable set $V$, an \emph{admissible quotient template} consists of (i) an equality partition $\pi$ of $V$ into $q\le5$ blocks and (ii) a tournament $D$ on the blocks such that every vertex of $D$ has in-degree and out-degree at most two.  Put $d(A,A)=0$ and $d(A,B)=1$ iff $A\to B$ in $D$.

\paragraph{Theorem (PROVED/EXACT).}
$F$ is T5-satisfiable if and only if it has an admissible quotient template for which every clause satisfies
\[
d(\pi(x),\pi(y))\oplus d(\pi(x),\pi(z))=1.
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
Given a T5 assignment, quotient variables by equal color.  The distinct colors induce a subtournament of T5, hence the capacity bounds, and the displayed identity is exactly Q.  Conversely, every tournament $D$ on $q\le5$ vertices with both degrees at most two embeds in T5.  For $q\le2$ this is immediate; for $q=3$ both tournament isomorphism types occur in T5.  At $q=4$ the bounds force score sequence $(1,1,2,2)$, the unique strong four-tournament, which is T5 with one vertex deleted.  At $q=5$ the sum of out-degrees is ten, so all out-degrees are two; the unique regular five-tournament is T5.  Assign each quotient block its embedded color.  The displayed identities then give all clauses. \hfill$\square$

This theorem handles ties correctly: $d(A,A)=0$.  It never substitutes $\neg R(x,y)$ by $R(y,x)$ when values may tie.  It is an exact finite-alphabet semantic characterization, not a polynomial algorithm: naive equality-partition search has Bell-number growth, followed by up to $2^{q(q-1)/2}$ orientations.

\paragraph{Private-head projection.}
For fixed $u\ne v$, exactly two or four head colors satisfy $Q(h,u,v)$; for $u=v$ none do.  Therefore a fresh head per graph edge projects decision exactly to graph 5-coloring, but it does not preserve model counts.  One common head projects to bipartiteness of its follower graph.  Neither statement makes T5 the first hard alphabet: the analogous existential-head projection over T3 gives graph 3-coloring.

\section{Critical high-girth existence and hardness firewall}
\paragraph{Theorem (PROVED/EXACT existence).}
For every integer $g$, there is a clause-minimal UNSAT Q-instance with a fresh private head per clause, sound per-head parity, incidence girth at least $2g$, and a SAT witness after deleting any designated clause.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Erd\H{o}s' theorem supplies a graph of girth at least $g$ and chromatic number greater than five.  Choose an inclusion-minimal edge subgraph $H$ that is not 5-colorable and discard irrelevant components.  It is connected and each $H-e$ is 5-colorable.  Replace every edge $uv$ by $Q(h_e,u,v)$ with fresh $h_e$.  The private-head projection makes the Q-instance SAT iff $H$ is 5-colorable.  Every deletion coloring extends to a full Q witness by choosing one of the two or four valid head colors.  Private heads are leaves in the incidence graph, and each graph edge becomes a two-step vertex--clause--vertex path, so every incidence cycle is exactly a doubled graph cycle. \hfill$\square$

Kami\'nski--Lozin Theorem 2.3 proves that for every fixed $k,g\ge3$, vertex $k$-colorability is NP-hard on graphs of girth at least $g$.  With $k=5$, the private-head mapping gives NP-hard Q decision at every \emph{fixed} incidence-girth threshold.  It does not give a polynomial reduction whose girth grows with input length, nor critical-promise hardness: extracting an inclusion-minimal non-5-colorable subgraph is not supplied as a polynomial operation.  Existence, fixed-threshold NP-hardness, designated-deletion witness verification, and uniform witness acquisition are four distinct quantifier statements.

This theorem falsifies any claim that a bounded catalogue of small parity/capacity cores decides all instances: critical controls exist beyond every fixed incidence-girth radius.  It does not prove that finding their deletion colorings is superpolynomial.

\section{Matched row--block and correct-path accounting}
On private-head graph instances, a SAT output is a 5-coloring of graph vertices plus one valid head color per edge; it is verified in $O(|V|+|E|)$ word operations after source access.  On an UNSAT row, ``one refutable block'' must identify a non-5-colorable residual block or present a declared proof-system certificate.  Per-head parity cannot do so for the five-clause and $K_6$ controls.  Absence of all quotient templates is a coNP statement, not a compact certificate proved here.

For the supplied layout $R\le n^{.195}$, $B\le n^{.65}$, and child length $m\le n^{.35}$, the independently reproduced fully charged scan remains
\[
T(n)=n^{.845+.35t+o(1)},\qquad
S(n)=n^{\max\{.195,.35s\}+o(1)}
\]
when every failed child and verification is charged and only one materialized child is live.  Hence a one-step $n^{1.33+o(1)}$ bound requires $t\le97/70$, while square-root space requires $s\le10/7$.  ``A polynomial SAT seed'' supplies neither numerical inequality nor the hereditary compiler for arbitrary CNFs.  T5 projection supplies concrete SAT/UNSAT blocks, not that compiler.
