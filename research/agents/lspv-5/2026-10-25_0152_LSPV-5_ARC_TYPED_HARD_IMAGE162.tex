\section{Executive result}
The newest LSPV-1 endpoint refinement is sound and materially weakens the
required ARC landing.  A final machine need not be correct or fast on every
ordinary SAT string.  It is enough to halt, be exact and have favorable joint
bounds on the image of one fixed locally indexable FLMV reduction from one
fixed hard language.  Intermediate machines may likewise carry explicit
typed promises, provided every parent in its type produces only children in
the preceding type.  This does not create a pair in the original all-input set
$E$ and must not be described that way.

The resulting highest-priority gap is \emph{hard-image/promise alignment}:
the fixed FLMV-hard image must lie inside the final contractor domain (or be
transported there by a separately paid local reduction), and every generated
child must inherit the next type.  Nonempty overlap, NP-completeness of a
separate promise, or an uncharged fallback is insufficient.

\section{Independent hard-image theorem}
\begin{theorem}[One fixed hard image is sufficient]
There are a fixed language
\[
 L^\dagger\in\NTIME[n]\setminus
 \DTISP[n^{c_\star},o(n^{d_\star})]
\]
and a fixed locally indexed SAT reduction $R^\dagger$ such that, for
\[
 \mathcal H^\dagger=\{R^\dagger(x):x\in\{0,1\}^*\},
\]
no fixed uniform deterministic program $B$ can halt and return the exact SAT
bit on every $y\in\mathcal H^\dagger$ while simultaneously satisfying there
\[
 T_B(y)\le |y|^{t+o(1)},\qquad
 S_B(y)\le |y|^{s+o(1)}
\]
for constants $t<c_\star$ and $s<d_\star$.  No correctness, termination or
recognition obligation is imposed on $B$ outside $\mathcal H^\dagger$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $L^\dagger$ witnessing (1), fix its nondeterministic machine, and use
the associated local reduction $R^\dagger$.  On every source $x$, simulate
$B$ on the virtual string $R^\dagger(x)$.  Each guest input-bit request is
answered by the fixed local generator with polylogarithmic time and logarithmic
workspace overhead.  The guest is always in $\mathcal H^\dagger$, so its
promise is honored and the simulator halts correctly on every source.  Since
$|R^\dagger(x)|=n^{1+o(1)}$, its joint bounds are
\[
 n^{t+o(1)},\qquad n^{s+o(1)}+O(\log n)=o(n^{d_\star}).
\]
Strict $t<c_\star$ and $s<d_\star$ contradict the choice of $L^\dagger$.
\end{proof}

This is a \textbf{KNOWN THEOREM CONSEQUENCE}, not a new lower bound.  It
does not prove $(t,s)\in E$; define a separate promise-achievable set
$E_{\mathcal H^\dagger}$ if desired.

\section{Finite typed-route lemma}
Let $\mathcal H_0,\ldots,\mathcal H_d$ be fixed domains of ordinary binary
encodings.  A program is exact on a domain if it halts with the correct SAT bit
on every member; behavior elsewhere is irrelevant.

\begin{lemma}[Typed correctness and joint resource composition]
Suppose $B_0$ is exact on $\mathcal H_0$.  At level $j$, one fixed uniform
contractor is exact for every parent in $\mathcal H_j$, generates only children
proved to lie in $\mathcal H_{j-1}$, and reconstructs the parent SAT bit from
their exact bits.  If it makes $N^{a_j+o(1)}$ sequential child calls of length
$N^{\lambda_j+o(1)}$, uses local time exponent $r_j$, and has nonchild peak
space exponent $h_j$, then at fixed finite depth
\[
 t_j=\max\{r_j,a_j+\lambda_j t_{j-1}\},qquad
 s_j=\max\{h_j,\lambda_j s_{j-1}\},
\tag{2}
\]
subject to separately including eager output, virtual-bit generation, retained
ancestors, caches and adapters in $r_j,h_j$.  The composed program is uniform
and exact on $\mathcal H_d$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on levels.  Every actual child satisfies the preceding promise, so its
bit is correct and its declared resource bound applies.  The exact join then
settles both SAT and UNSAT parents.  Sequential child work gives the affine
time term; depth-first reuse gives the peak-space maximum.  A fixed finite
composition of fixed programs is uniform and contains no length-dependent
advice.
\end{proof}

Taking $\mathcal H_0$ to be all ordinary strings under the initial polynomial
SAT machine, and $\mathcal H_d=\mathcal H^\dagger$, would suffice if a finite
typed route made the final exponents strict below $(c_\star,d_\star)$.  This is
weaker than an all-input successor at each level but remains \OPEN{} because no
such typed contractors and eligibility proofs are complete.

\section{Promise-alignment gate}
\begin{theorem}[Containment, not overlap]
Let a program $C$ have favorable bounds only on a promise $\mathcal P$.  The
hard-image contradiction follows directly only if
$\mathcal H^\dagger\subseteq\mathcal P$, or if there is a fixed exact local
transport from every member of $\mathcal H^\dagger$ into $\mathcal P$ whose
length, bit access, time and simultaneous workspace preserve the strict box.
Nonempty or infinite $\mathcal H^\dagger\cap\mathcal P$ is insufficient.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The source simulator must decide $L^\dagger$ on every source $x$.  Its guest is
$R^\dagger(x)$, so the favorable promise must apply to every such guest.  An
overlap omits some source images and supplies no bound or answer there.  A
transport is a valid substitute only when it is exact for both labels and all
its resource costs are included.
\end{proof}

Consequently, LSPV-3's scoped-width contractor is a genuine restricted
positive result, but no reviewed source proves that one fixed FLMV-hard image
has its validated scoped syntax and logarithmic-width promise, nor provides a
strict-box-preserving local normalization into that promise.  The two results
cannot yet be connected.

\section{Two-level independent countercontrol}
For a bit string $x_1\cdots x_n$, construct ordinary 2CNF on
$u_0,\ldots,u_n$ with units $\neg u_0,\neg u_n$ and two clauses encoding
$u_i=u_{i+1}$ when $x_{i+1}=0$, or $u_i\ne u_{i+1}$ when $x_{i+1}=1$.
It is satisfiable exactly when $x$ has even parity and is locally indexable.

The standard-library audit generated all 1,022 images for source lengths one
through nine: exactly 511 SAT and 511 UNSAT.  It executed two actual Shannon
levels and independently brute-forced every node.  All 2,044 first children and
all 4,088 grandchildren left the original image, even though every SAT/UNSAT
join remained exact.  It additionally checked malformed/off-image rejection
and 363 heterogeneous exponent routes through depth five.  Total: 6,866
assertions, zero failures, under ten seconds.  This is
\textbf{COMPUTATIONAL}; the parity family is deliberately easy and is not the
existential hard image.

\section{Charged ledger and hostile controls}
Each typed edge must pay validation or state why validation is unnecessary on
the promised call path; source/workspace reads; changed and unchanged writes;
address arithmetic; policy discovery and switching; failed trials; guest
instructions and clocks; child headers/bodies or every virtual probe; source
name maps; compatibility and reconstruction; cache keys and replay; retained
ancestors and buffers; malformed-input behavior where totality is claimed; and
program/representation size.  Actual state flips, physical switches,
gates/wires and cumulative bit-time remain separate metrics.

Controls include SAT and UNSAT image members, off-image descendants, Horn,
SCC/2-SAT, affine parity, direct recognizers, cache reuse, one-bit mutations,
parent-scale fallback and decide-first behavior.  A promise machine need not
recognize its promise, but the proof must guarantee that every actual call lies
inside it.

\section{Barrier-transfer and post-quantum preflight}
Promise-preserving reductions and local decoding are direct structural
analogies, but only FLMV's actual local SAT reduction transfers as a theorem.
Cryptographic lossiness, collision entropy, Schur squares, binding and hash
ideas do not establish hard-image containment or hereditary child typing and
are rejected as transfers here.  Distributed cut or VLSI locality language is
also insufficient without a matched bit-access and workspace theorem.  Hidden
algebraic structure may instead yield a fast promise algorithm, as parity does.
