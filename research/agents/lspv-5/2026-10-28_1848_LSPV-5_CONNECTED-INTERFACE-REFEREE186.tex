\section{BUILD I: exact audit of the static theorem}
Let $F$ be a connected proper strict signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3CNF on $n$
variables and $m$ clauses.  Let $Y$ be a supplied boundary of size $b$ and
let all private variables be assigned to nonempty pairwise-disjoint modules
$Z_1,\ldots,Z_k$.  No clause may contain private variables from distinct
modules.  For each module, $B_i\subseteq Z_i$, $|B_i|\le q$, and every module
clause meets $Y\cup B_i$.

\begin{theorem}[Static-interface theorem, independently checked]
If $b>0$, then
\[
 k\le4b,\qquad
 n\le3\left(b+\sum_i|B_i|\right)\le3b+12bq,
 \qquad b+q\ge\sqrt{n/3}-\tfrac14.
\]
If $b=0$, connectedness forces $k=1$ and $q\ge n/3$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each private module must occur in a clause touching $Y$, or it is a separate
incidence component.  Such a clause can witness only one module because
cross-module private names are forbidden.  Boundary variables have exactly
four incidences, hence there are at most $4b$ such clauses and $k\le4b$.
Boundary-only clauses meet $Y$; every other clause is a module clause and
meets $Y\cup B_i$.  Thus $H=Y\cup\bigcup_iB_i$ hits every clause.  Since each
variable occurs four times, $m\le4|H|$, while exact occurrence gives
$3m=4n$.  Therefore
$n\le3(b+\sum_i|B_i|)\le3b+3kq\le3b+12bq$.
Equivalently $b(q+1/4)\ge n/12$; AM--GM yields the displayed lower bound.
For $b=0$, two nonempty modules would disconnect the incidence graph, and
the same hitting-set count gives $q\ge n/3$.
\end{proof}

The theorem survived falsifiers 1 and 2 because the full partition and
no-cross-module condition are load-bearing.  The following smallest control
shows that this condition cannot be silently dropped.

\begin{proposition}[Cross-module hypothesis control]
Let $Y=\{y\}$ and declare eight private singleton labels
$Z_i=\{z_i\}$.  The four clauses
\[
 (y,z_1,z_2),(y,z_3,z_4),(y,z_5,z_6),(y,z_7,z_8)
\]
form a connected incidence hypergraph but give $k=8>4|Y|$.
Every clause contains private names from two declared modules.
\end{proposition}
This is not a counterexample to the theorem: it is an explicit counterexample
to any version omitting clause isolation.  Exact-$(2,2)$ is not asserted for
this control.

\section{BUILD II: local discovery, cyclic polarity, and grammar closure}
For a supplied $Y$ and authenticated module partition, the clauses in a
module that avoid $Y$ form a rank-three hitting-set instance.  Branching on
one of the three variables of any uncovered triple has at most
$(3^{q+1}-1)/2$ nodes.  Thus finding $B_i$ or certifying its absence costs
$O(3^q\operatorname{poly}(N))$.  Enumerating boundary and backdoor assignments
and solving the residual 2-CNFs costs
$O(k(3^q+2^{b+q})\operatorname{poly}(N))$.  This is
\textbf{PROVED/EXACT (restricted)} and removes free \emph{local} discovery;
it does not find $Y$ or the partition.

The archive's cyclic scopes have clauses on
$(x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2})$ and on each block
$(x_{3j},x_{3j+1},x_{3j+2})$.  Processing variables in order retains only the
last two names plus wrap names $x_0,x_1$, so the exact frontier relation has
at most four names and sixteen assignments for arbitrary signs.  The
wraparound accounting is sound.

There is, however, a stronger polarity correction.
\begin{theorem}[All-YES strict exact-$(2,2)$ cyclic promise]
Every strict signed exact-$(2,2)$ signing on those cyclic scopes is
satisfiable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write block $j$ as $(a,b,c)=(x_{3j},x_{3j+1},x_{3j+2})$.  Choose $c$ to make
its literal in the outgoing clause $C_{3j+2}$ true.  If its literal in
$C_{3j+1}$ is also true, the two internal clauses each forbid at most one of
the four choices for $(a,b)$, so some pair works.  Otherwise those two
occurrences of $c$ have opposite signs.  The two-positive/two-negative quota
forces the two internal occurrences of $c$ also to have opposite signs.
Choose $b$ to satisfy its outgoing literal in $C_{3j+1}$, then choose $a$ to
satisfy the at most one remaining internal clause.  Blocks concatenate,
including wraparound, because each outgoing clause is already satisfied by
its left block.
\end{proof}

Hence the exact-$(2,2)$ promise needs only a fixed YES terminal after charged
promise recognition.  The arbitrary-sign scope class may use both YES and NO
terminals, but it is a larger class.  The conventional eight-clause UNSAT
terminal on three variables has four positive and four negative occurrences
of each name, not exact-$(2,2)$.  A correct closure statement must therefore
either (i) use the all-YES exact-$(2,2)$ class plus a fixed YES terminal, or
(ii) explicitly enlarge the arbitrary-sign class by both terminal encodings.
This repairs the grammar; it supplies no general SAT shrinker.

\section{BUILD III: moving interfaces do not give a universal shortcut}
The role-4 grid construction has variables $A_{r,c},B_{r,c},C_{r,c}$ and four
clauses per cell:
\begin{align*}
 &(A_{r,c}\vee B_{r,c}\vee C_{r,c}),
 (A_{r,c}\vee B_{r+1,c}\vee C_{r,c}),\\
 &(\neg A_{r,c}\vee\neg B_{r,c}\vee\neg C_{r,c+1}),
 (\neg A_{r,c}\vee\neg B_{r+1,c}\vee\neg C_{r,c+1}).
\end{align*}
Independent incidence counting confirms exact two-positive/two-negative
occurrence.  Its primal graph contains a subdivided $L\times L$ grid.  The
row--column crosses form a bramble of order at least $L$, so every tree
decomposition has a bag of at least $L$ names and every variable order has a
frontier of at least $L-1$.  A row-major order has $O(L)$ frontier.  Thus a
universal logarithmic \emph{syntactic} moving/hierarchical interface is false
even under connected exact-$(2,2)$.

This is not a separation result: $A=1,B=C=0$ satisfies every displayed grid
instance, so the decision answer has a constant representation.  The example
simultaneously falsifies ``bounded occurrence implies small syntactic
separator'' and ``large syntactic separator implies hard SAT.''  A valid
constructive route must bound the size, discovery, updates, and total
accumulation of an exact \emph{semantic} summary on both answer classes.

\section{Same-program and native-model ledgers}
The exact-$(2,2)$ cyclic promise has no UNSAT side, so it cannot establish
worst-case all-UNSAT coverage.  SAT/UNSAT resource measures elsewhere must be
restrictions of one fixed correct program; no answer-class bit is supplied.

On indexed RAM, charge canonical parsing, source authentication, boundary or
order discovery, every failed branch, relation tables, and physical child
serialization.  On a fixed-$k$ multitape machine with sequential read-only
input, charge all rescans and work-tape cells; random indexed access is not
free.  On a single writable tape the original $N$ cells count in footprint,
and all head travel and crossings are time.  On two fully writable tapes keep
input-tape changes, auxiliary changes, head positions, and visited auxiliary
cells separate.  On a fixed universal machine charge source/program parsing
and simulation overhead; the fixed program prefix contains no advice table.
The conservative multitape-to-one-tape construction is an upper simulation,
not a lower bound, and no tape inequality is imported to RAM or FLMV.

The structural theorems relativize against a semantic decision oracle: such
an oracle immediately emits a constant answer terminal.  Thus they cannot by
themselves prove runtime hardness.  Padding, dead variables, renaming, and
source-preserving wrappers likewise do not create an all-input semantic
summary theorem.  Compact circuits may represent a projection even when an
auxiliary-free CNF expands; representation, acquisition, evaluation, and
recursive closure remain distinct costs.
