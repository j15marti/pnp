\section{Claim card and four falsifiers proposed before search}
\textbf{Claim ID:} SHORE-ADVICE-FACTOR176.  \textbf{Mode:} red-team.
The target is the multiplicity term in a crossing-splice portfolio bound.
The exact quantifiers are: fix one deterministic machine $U$, one source
cut $c$, a sound selected program family $\mathcal P$, and common ceilings
on the complete crossing labels; then bound the number of accepted diagonal
fooling inputs, allowing the selected $p\in\mathcal P$ to depend on the
input with free hindsight.  Time, visited work cells, actual changes,
reversals, jumps, labels and soundness refer to that one run of $U$.

Before retrieving the sources, four falsifiers were fixed.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A different inline prefix may invalidate cross-program splicing.
\emph{Falsified} when the entire prefix is on one source shore: the prefix
rides with that shore and the crossed run is still an execution of one of
the two original sound programs.
\item Arbitrarily split program bits also cost no factor.
\emph{Falsified} by Counterexample~\ref{c:split} unless soundness is closed
under shore recombination.
\item A separate proof tape behaves like inline source advice.
\emph{Falsified}: it is global and unsplit; future-readable contents must
match or be placed in the interface record.
\item ARC197's ``long at every cut'' conclusion may interchange a union and
an intersection.  \emph{Not falsified}: the union bound is in the correct
direction once cross-program injectivity is established at each fixed cut.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The exact shore-factor theorem}
Let a source at cut $c$ decompose as $X=L\mid R$.  A selected program has
representation $p=(p_L,p_R)$, where $p_L$ is physically confined to the
left shore and $p_R$ to the right shore.  Let $\mathcal P$ be a set of
complete programs, each globally sound: no $p\in\mathcal P$ accepts any
NO source.  For an accepting run, let $\tau$ be a \emph{complete} crossing
record: control state, direction, and every future-readable work-tape
content/head position needed by the standard splice induction.  In a
bounded-jump machine it additionally contains the source and landing
offsets, symbols, and complete pre/post work configurations of each jump.
Let $B$ bound the number of possible short records at the fixed cut.
Write
\[
 P_L=|\{p_L:p\in\mathcal P\}|,\qquad
 P_R=|\{p_R:p\in\mathcal P\}|.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Selected-sound shore factor]\label{t:shore}
For a fooling diagonal family in which every two distinct accepted
diagonals have at least one crossed NO source, the number of diagonals
accepted with one of at most $B$ records satisfies
\[
 |\mathcal A|\le \min\{P_L,P_R\}\,B.                 \tag{1}
\]
If every program prefix is wholly left of the cut, $P_R=1$ and hence
$|\mathcal A|\le B$, independent of portfolio cardinality.  The symmetric
statement holds for a wholly right-shore encoding.  If every shorewise
recombination $(p_L,p_R')$ is itself globally sound, the multiplicity again
collapses to one even for split encodings.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Partition accepted runs by their right fragment $p_R$.  Within one class,
suppose distinct diagonal runs under programs $(u,v)$ and $(u',v)$ share
the same complete record.  Splicing the left subrun of the first with the
right subrun of the second yields a run under $(u,v)$, one of the original
sound programs; the reverse splice runs under $(u',v)$.  At least one
crossed source is NO, contradicting soundness.  Thus records are injective
inside every right-fragment class, so $|\mathcal A|\le P_RB$.  Partitioning
instead by $p_L$ gives $|\mathcal A|\le P_LB$.  Taking the smaller bound
proves (1).  If every recombination is sound, no grouping is required:
an arbitrary collision yields two sound hybrid programs and the same
contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{counterexample}[Split selected programs need a factor]\label{c:split}
For $m\ge1$, let the source predicate be
$\operatorname{DISJ}(x,y)=[x\wedge y=0]$.  Give a two-shore program
$p=(u,v)$ the acceptance relation
\[
 A_{u,v}(x,y)=[x=u]\wedge[y=\overline v].            \tag{2}
\]
Select only $\mathcal P=\{(a,a):a\in\{0,1\}^m\}$.  Every selected program
is globally sound and accepts exactly the diagonal YES source
$(a,\bar a)$; all selected runs may have one common central record after
the local comparisons are erased.  For $a\ne a'$, at least one crossed
source $(a,\bar a')$ or $(a',\bar a)$ is NO.  It is nevertheless accepted
by the hybrid $(a,a')$ or $(a',a)$, which is not a selected program and need
not be sound.  Therefore arbitrary cross-program splicing does not prove
injectivity.  Here $P_L=P_R=2^m$, and the factor in (1) is tight at the
logical splice interface.  A sequential implementation can compare each
shore locally using charged work, erase it, and cross in a fixed state; this
is a model-boundary control, not an efficient ordinary-SAT algorithm.
\end{counterexample}

\paragraph{Separate proof/advice tape.}
A reusable read-only proof tape is not divided by the source cut.  A splice
between runs with different proof strings can subsequently read different
global cells, so equality of source-cut records alone is insufficient.
Grouping by the entire proof string gives the safe full multiplicity, such
as $2^{L+1}-1$ for strings of length at most $L$.  This independently
upholds LSPV-3's UWI-PROOF-TAPE-CROSS141 theorem and distinguishes it from
inline source advice.

\section{Independent referee decisions on the newest peer claims}
\begin{lemma}[ARC197 all-cut tail, independently upheld]
At one fixed cut $c$, let $S_c(h)$ be diagonals for which some sound inline
left-prefix program accepts with a record of length at most $h$.  With label
alphabet size $A$,
\[
 |S_c(h)|\le B_A(h):=\sum_{j=0}^{h}A^j.
\]
For $G$ fixed central cuts, at least
\[
 2^m-G B_A(h)                                            \tag{3}
\]
diagonals require more than $h$ crossings at \emph{every} cut for every
eligible sound program.  If $GB_A(h)\le2^{m-2}$, the fraction is at least
$3/4$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{t:shore} with $P_R=1$ gives the fixed-cut bound even when the
chosen prefix varies with the input.  The set failing the all-cut property
is $\bigcup_c S_c(h)$, whose size is at most $\sum_c|S_c(h)|$.  Taking its
complement proves (3).  Unequal prefix lengths merely translate the formula
shore; the standard induction is relative to the same formula cut and must
match all global work state.  Formula record lengths and the right-endmarker
offset are common across the diagonal family.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{UTM-JUMP-CHANGE150 correction.}
Its endpoint-authenticated labels and finite cap
\[
 L_J\le\min\{R+1,(D+1)V_J+D\}
\]
survive review.  Under its inline-left prefix hypothesis, however, the two
packing inequalities strengthen from
\[
2^m\le2^{b+1}A_J^{L_J+1},\quad
2^m\le2^{b+1}G A_J^{1+\min\{L_J,\lfloor TJ/G\rfloor\}}
\]
to
\[
\boxed{2^m\le A_J^{L_J+1}},\qquad
\boxed{2^m\le G A_J^{1+\min\{L_J,\lfloor TJ/G\rfloor\}}}. \tag{4}
\]
Thus its exponent faces remain valid and no assumption $b\le\eta m$ is
needed for the finite counting step.  If program length is included in
physical input length or source travel, that cost must still be charged;
(4) is not a free nonuniform-advice theorem.
