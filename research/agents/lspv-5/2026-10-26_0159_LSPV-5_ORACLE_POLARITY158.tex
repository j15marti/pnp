\section*{Executive finding}
The current adaptive-certificate spine remains incomplete, but this run
isolates a precise missing condition.  An accepting transcript of a SAT-oracle
computation cannot be certified merely by supplying witnesses for the claimed
SAT answers.  Claimed UNSAT answers have the opposite polarity and must be
validated by a sound recursive UNSAT certificate, a charged deterministic
decision, or another independently proved mechanism.

The new theorem below gives an exact necessary-and-sufficient condition for a
positive-witness-only transcript verifier: \emph{negative-answer robustness}.
For a fixed nonadaptive query vector this condition is exactly monotonicity of
the machine's accepting predicate in the oracle-answer bits.  The canonical
one-query UNSAT decider is antimonotone, so it fails at depth one.  A proper
3-CNF equivalent to a literal supplies the smallest explicit SAT
counterexample: the verifier can falsely claim the sole oracle answer is NO,
has no positive witness to check, and accepts the SAT input as UNSAT.

This does not refute the well-typed LSPV-3 recursive certificate schema, which
already requires sound leaves and admits exhaustive branching.  It shows why
the schema's bounded all-UNSAT coverage and well-founded recursive NO-answer
closure are load-bearing premises rather than bookkeeping.  The weighted leaf
inequality remains correct only after these obligations and their costs have
been included.  P versus NP remains \status{OPEN}.

\section*{Exact model and polarity}
Let $M^{\SAT}$ be a deterministic adaptive oracle machine.  On input $F$, a
claimed transcript
\[
 \tau=((Q_1,a_1),\ldots,(Q_q,a_q)),\qquad a_j\in\{0,1\},
\]
is \emph{path-consistent} if each $Q_j$ is exactly the query generated by $M$
from $F$ and the earlier claimed answers.  It is
\emph{positive-certified} if every $j$ with $a_j=1$ carries an assignment
satisfying $Q_j$.  No condition is imposed yet on $a_j=0$.

For a co-nondeterministic certificate verifier for UNSAT, the required
polarity is
\[
 F\in\UNSAT\Rightarrow\exists\pi\;V(F,\pi)=1,
 \qquad
 F\in\SAT\Rightarrow\forall\pi\;V(F,\pi)=0.                 \tag{1}
\]
Malformed proofs and timeouts reject.  A transcript accepted solely because
its positive claims verify must still satisfy the universal SAT-side clause of
(1).

\begin{theorem}[Negative-answer robustness, \status{PROVED}]
Fix $M$ and let $V_+$ accept exactly the path-consistent transcripts on which
all claimed YES answers have valid satisfying assignments and the simulated
$M$ ends in its designated UNSAT-accepting state.  Then $V_+$ is sound on SAT
inputs if and only if
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \forall F\in\SAT\ \forall\tau:\
 \bigl[\tau\text{ path-consistent and every claimed YES is true}\bigr]
 \Longrightarrow M(F;\tau)\text{ rejects}.
 \quad}                                                     \tag{NAR}
\]
Here claimed NO answers may be false.  Condition (NAR) is therefore precisely
robustness of rejection against replacing any true oracle answer by an
unchecked claimed NO answer along the resulting adaptive path.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If (NAR) holds, every transcript satisfying $V_+$'s checks rejects on every SAT
input, so (1) is sound.  Conversely, if (NAR) fails, the witnessing SAT input,
transcript, and satisfying assignments for its YES claims form an accepting
certificate for $V_+$ on a SAT input, contradicting soundness.  No complexity
assumption is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-query specialization, \status{PROVED}]
Suppose $M$ asks a fixed nonadaptive vector of $k$ SAT queries and accepts
according to $g:\{0,1\}^k\to\{0,1\}$.  Positive-only transcript verification
is sound relative to the true answer vector exactly when $g$ is monotone
increasing:
\[
                 a\le x\text{ and }g(a)=1\Longrightarrow g(x)=1.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A claimed vector $a$ passes all positive checks against a true answer vector
$x$ exactly when $a\le x$ coordinatewise.  Substitution in (NAR) gives the
displayed monotonicity condition.
\end{proof}

\section*{Proper-3-CNF depth-one counterexample}
For a literal $\ell$ and fresh $a,b$, define
\[
 U(\ell;a,b)=\bigwedge_{\sigma,\rho\in\{+1,-1\}}
   (\ell\vee\sigma a\vee\rho b).
\]
Every clause has three distinct variables and $U(\ell;a,b)$ is equivalent to
$\ell$.  Hence
\[
 F_{\rm sat}=U(x;a,b)
 \quad\text{has four models},
 \qquad
 F_{\rm unsat}=U(x;a,b)\wedge U(\neg x;c,d)
 \quad\text{has none}.
\]
Consider the exact one-query oracle decider: query $\SAT(F)$ and accept UNSAT
iff the answer is NO.  Its acceptance predicate is $g(z)=1-z$, the unique
nonmonotone Boolean function of one bit.  On $F_{\rm sat}$ a forged claimed-NO
transcript contains no claimed YES answer and therefore no witness to check;
$V_+$ accepts it.  This violates (1).  A full verifier rejects the forgery only
by establishing that the NO claim is false---equivalently, by finding a SAT
witness or otherwise deciding the query.

This counterexample is an ordinary proper signed 3-CNF control.  It is not
exact-$(2,2)$ or clause-monotone, and no transfer to either grammar is claimed
or needed for the generic oracle-polarity failure.

\section*{Charged repair: oracle-polarity closure}
A sound recursive adaptive certificate must account for each actual query:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item reproduce and serialize, or virtually expose, the exact ordinary query
        $Q_j$ with every source/index probe charged;
  \item for $a_j=1$, supply and verify a satisfying assignment;
  \item for $a_j=0$, supply a sound UNSAT subcertificate or execute another
        charged exact decision method;
  \item prove well-foundedness, for example by an independently verified rank
        $\mu(Q_j)<\mu(F)$ on every recursive NO edge;
  \item include parsing, malformed-proof rejection, timeouts, joining, ancestor
        state, caches, and reconstruction in the same execution ledger.
\end{enumerate}
Call these conditions \emph{oracle-polarity closure}.  If the proof is an
explicit recursive tree, its work recurrence has the form
\[
 C(F,\pi)=C_{\rm local}+\sum_{j:a_j=1}C_{\rm yes}(Q_j,w_j)
                         +\sum_{j:a_j=0}C(Q_j,\pi_j).       \tag{2}
\]
If repeated subqueries are represented by a DAG, (2) must be evaluated on the
actual cached execution rather than an unfolded tree.  Table construction,
keys, collision-safe identity, lookup, stored subproofs or replay, and all live
ancestors are charged.  Sharing can reduce work and is permitted; it cannot be
counted once as a tree lower bound and again as cached computation.

The inequality
\[
 \sum_i m_i^d\le M^{d-1}\sum_i m_i,
 \qquad M=\max_i m_i,\ d\ge1,                              \tag{3}
\]
remains \status{PROVED}.  It bounds only the declared terminal guest calls.
Equation (3) gives a certificate-runtime upper bound only after (2), proof
bits, query construction/access, and peak live storage also fit the stated
budget.  It supplies neither soundness nor all-UNSAT coverage.

\section*{Decisive next lemma}
The next load-bearing statement must construct, not assume, a uniform
well-founded all-UNSAT certificate system satisfying oracle-polarity closure.
It must bound the true recurrence (2) and peak live storage, prove exact SAT
and UNSAT semantics through two real ordinary-input levels, and prevent a
same-size cycle such as ``certify UNSAT($F$) by querying UNSAT($F$).''  A
candidate may exploit shared DAGs, caching, algebraic terminals, Horn/2SAT
recognizers, or direct decisions; their discovery and execution costs must be
included.  The LSPV-1 read floor is a valid firewall but is only
$N^{1-o(1)}$, so it neither supplies nor refutes the required $c<H(K)$ system.
