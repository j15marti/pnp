\section{Preregistered falsifiers and exact model}
Before the targeted source search, four attacks were fixed.
\begin{description}
\item[F1.] The $2^{b+1}$ factor may be an artifact of grouping histories by program even though the program prefix lies on the left source shore.  \textbf{CONFIRMED}; Theorem~\ref{th:inline}.
\item[F2.] Removing that factor may incorrectly remove an external reusable proof tape.  \textbf{FALSIFIED as a generalization}; Theorem~\ref{th:proof}.
\item[F3.] Different program lengths or absolute cut positions may obstruct splicing.  \textbf{FALSIFIED} for translation-invariant tapes with relative formula cuts and fixed-length formula suffixes; the right shore is translated, not altered.
\item[F4.] The newest connected minimally-UNSAT source may turn source authentication into a superlinear SAT lower bound.  \textbf{FALSIFIED as an inference}; it has an exact linear Resolution proof and a cheap globally sound syntactic recognizer.
\end{description}

Fix one deterministic finite-control machine $U$.  Its single sequential input has the physical form
\[
       p\#H_m\,A_m(a)\,X_m\,B_m(b),                 \tag{1}
\]
where $p$ is an arbitrary inline program prefix, $H_m$ is the source header, $X_m$ is the fixed central body with $G=\Theta(N)$ legal cuts, and $A_m,B_m$ are the two sign-controlled shores of the verified signed exact-$(2,2)$ DISJ family.  For every $p$, acceptance must be sound on every declared NO source.  Formula length and every suffix length from a fixed relative cut are independent of $p,a,b$.  The program may depend on the input with free hindsight and be incomplete on YES.

The same argument applies to one read-only program/source input head plus fixed work tapes if each crossing record contains the complete work contents and every work-head coordinate.  It does not apply unchanged when the advice lives on a separate tape accessible from both shores.

\section{Cross-program splice theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Inline-prefix portfolio elimination]\label{th:inline}
For every $a\in\{0,1\}^m$, select any program $p_a$ such that
$U(p_a,F_m(a,\bar a))$ accepts soundly.  Programs may have arbitrary and unequal lengths.  If each accepting run has time at most $T$, and $U$ is a one-shared-tape machine with $q$ states, then
\[
 \boxed{2^m\le G(2q)^{\lfloor T/G\rfloor+1}.}       \tag{2}
\]
There is no program-count factor.

For one read-only inline program/source head and fixed work tapes, let $A$ bound complete crossing labels and let
$L$ bound transcript length by reversals, actual-change epochs, or any other valid same-run cap.  Then
\[
 \boxed{2^m\le A^{L+1}},\qquad
 \boxed{2^m\le G A^{\min\{L,\lfloor T/G\rfloor\}+1}.} \tag{3}
\]
Thus the UTM-CROSS-WRITE1 inequalities remain true but their displayed factor $2^{b+1}$ is redundant in the declared inline-prefix model.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each accepted $a$, sum crossings over the $G$ relative cuts in $X_m$ and choose a cut $c_a$ having at most $\lfloor T/G\rfloor$ crossings.  Suppose distinct $a,a'$ choose the same relative cut and have the same complete crossing transcript, even though $p_a$ and $p_{a'}$ may differ.

Standard deterministic shore splicing uses the complete left half of the first initial tape and a translated copy of the complete right half of the second.  The result is precisely
\[
  p_a\#F_m(a,\bar a').
\]
The common crossing transcript makes the stitched accepting computation valid.  Reversing the shores similarly gives an accepting computation of
$p_{a'}\#F_m(a',\bar a)$.  Different prefix lengths merely translate the second right shore: its distance from the relative cut to every formula symbol and right endmarker is unchanged.  The machine has no absolute cell addresses.

At least one crossed formula is UNSAT, so at least one of the globally sound programs would accept a NO source, contradiction.  Therefore the map
\[
          a\longmapsto(c_a,\text{complete crossing transcript})
\]
is injective across \emph{all} selected programs.  Counting cut choices and histories proves (2).  With work tapes, equality of complete labels allows the identical induction on successive crossings and gives (3).  The argument uses no bound on the number or length of prefixes.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why grouping by program was safe but non-tight.}
The governance proof first fixed $p$, obtained injectivity inside its accepted subset, and then union-bounded over programs.  That proves a valid weaker inequality.  The strengthening observes that soundness is per program but the splice contradiction also is: the left hybrid retains $p_a$, and the opposite hybrid retains $p_{a'}$.  Hence cross-program collisions are forbidden too.

\paragraph{Consequences.}
For fixed $q$ and $G=\Theta(N)$, (2) still yields
$T=\Omega(N^2/\log N)$ on one common diagonal SAT source, now without $b=O(\log N)$ or $b\le\eta m$.  This remains fixed-$U$ time in the sequential inline-prefix model, not native program time.  Program length and travel to the formula are already paid inside $T$.  With the sparse-label bound $A(S,D,I_0)$ from report 174, all fixed-parameter reversal/write/time faces survive without a portfolio-size premise.  Actual-change noninvariance and the $D+I_0$ repair remain unchanged.

\section{Separate proof/advice tape firewall}
\begin{theorem}[External contents must match]\label{th:proof}
Suppose a verifier has an additional two-way read-only proof tape containing $\pi_a$ of length at most $L$.  A collision of source-cut crossing labels for $(a,\pi_a)$ and $(a',\pi_{a'})$ licenses splicing only when $\pi_a=\pi_{a'}$, unless the complete proof contents are included in every event label.  Consequently the safe count contains
\[
        P_L=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}2^\ell=2^{L+1}-1.       \tag{4}
\]
This is exactly the factor in LSPV-3 UWI-PROOFTAPE-CROSS141 and cannot be deleted by Theorem~\ref{th:inline}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A source-shore splice leaves the external proof tape global and unsplit.  If its contents differ, the right portion of one computation may read symbols from $\pi_{a'}$ while the proposed hybrid physically supplies $\pi_a$; equality of source-boundary state, work contents and proof-head position does not repair the differing future symbols.  Group by the full proof contents, splice only within a group, and sum over $P_L$ possible strings.  If the proof is instead serialized into the source prefix left of every cut, it falls under Theorem~\ref{th:inline}; if copied into work storage, its cells and acquisition are charged there.
\end{proof}

This distinction independently upholds Role 3's theorem
\[
2^m\le(2^{L+1}-1)\sum_{j=0}^{R+1}Q^j.
\]
Its saturation control $L=m$, $\pi=a$ is legitimate in the separate-tape model.  It is not a counterexample to the inline theorem because moving those $m$ bits into the source prefix changes which shore owns them and forces their effect through the source cut.

LSPV-2 ZERO-WRITE-PORTFOLIO176 gives a compatible stronger special case: on entirely blank unmarked work tapes with $D=0$, even an unbounded inline program family cannot cover all diagonals soundly, since after the delimiter only finitely many residual two-way-finite-automaton states remain.  Its one-change marker and free-$I_0=1$ variants correctly lie outside that zero-change submodel.

with $M=2k+8$ proper ternary clauses.  The report's four main arrows survive independent review.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Eliminating PIN auxiliaries forces $x_1$ and $\neg x_{k+1}$; each LINK forces $\neg x_i\vee x_{i+1}$, so $U_k$ is UNSAT and connected.

\item The explicit proof uses three inferences for the first unit, two per link, three for the last negative unit and one final inference: $3+2k+3+1=2k+7=M-1$.
\item Minimality forces every source clause into the ancestral binary-Resolution DAG.  Its connected underlying simple graph has at most $2I$ premise edges, so $2I\ge M+I-1$ and $I\ge M-1$.
