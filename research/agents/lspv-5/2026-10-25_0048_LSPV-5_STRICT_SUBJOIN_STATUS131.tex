This run tests three precise proposals linking MULTIBIT-ENDPOINT129's
power-short children to EXACTONE-JOIN-CYCLE95's global compatibility.
\begin{description}
\item[M1.] SAT bits of all proper module subjoins determine the full named
join.  This is \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED} in the declared interface.
\item[M2.] A fixed partition can be repaired by asking SAT of larger unions
of blocks, while never asking the whole parent.  This is also

\item[M3.] The collision forces expensive unrestricted computation.
This is \textbf{REJECTED}: one source sign field distinguishes the pair, exact

\end{description}

\section{Exact matched pair}
For Boolean \(a,b,c,d\), let \(E(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3)\) mean that exactly one
of the three literal values is true.  Define four named modules
\[
M_1=E(a,b,c),\quad M_2=E(a,b,d),\quad
M_3=E(a,c,d)
\]
and two choices for the fourth:
\[
M_4^-=E(b,c,d),\qquad M_4^+=E(\neg b,c,d).
\]
Put
\[
U=M_1\wedge M_2\wedge M_3\wedge M_4^-,
\qquad
S=M_1\wedge M_2\wedge M_3\wedge M_4^+.
\]
They differ in exactly one literal-sign field and have equal unsigned
incidence, metadata and encoded length.  The source-named proper-3CNF
compiler of EXACTONE-JOIN-CYCLE95 gives both
\[
n'=32,\qquad m'=56,\qquad N'=2938
\]
under its fixed-width record encoding.

\begin{theorem}[Strict-subjoin status collision; PROVED/EXACT restricted]
For every proper \(I\subsetneq[4]\),
\[
\SAT\!\left(\bigwedge_{i\in I}M_i^-\right)=
\SAT\!\left(\bigwedge_{i\in I}M_i^+\right)=1,
\]
where only module four differs between the signs.  But
\[
\SAT(U)=0,\qquad \SAT(S)=1.
\]
Consequently the complete 15-bit vector of proper-subjoin SAT statuses is
\(1^{15}\) on both inputs.  Every deterministic adaptive controller whose
only source-dependent answers are such strict-subjoin SAT bits has the same
transcript on \(U\) and \(S\), regardless of query order, repetitions,
caching, restarts or policy switching.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Summing the four positive exact-one equations in \(U\) over the integers gives
\[
3(a+b+c+d)=4,
\]
which is impossible for Boolean variables.  If one positive module is
deleted, let \(v\) be the unique variable omitted by that module.  Set
\(v=1\) and the other three variables to zero.  Each of the three retained
triples contains \(v\), so every retained exact-one equation holds.  Thus
every three-module subjoin of \(U\) is SAT, and every smaller subjoin is SAT
by deletion.  The assignment \((a,b,c,d)=(1,0,0,0)\) satisfies \(S\), hence
all its subjoins are SAT.  The transcript statement follows inductively:
after any common history the deterministic policy chooses the same next
proper subset, and both oracle answers are YES.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Partition-status failure]
For every partition of the four modules into at least two nonempty blocks,
the vector of block-SAT bits is all YES on both \(U\) and \(S\).  This covers
all 14 nontrivial set partitions, including every two-level partition.
\end{corollary}

The theorem survives source-name permutations and module reordering.  It
also survives ordinary proper-3CNF compilation of each named subjoin:
the compiler is equisatisfiable and its occurrence-copy equalities enforce
the original names.  It does not license dropping those equalities.

\section{What richer channels recover}
The theorem is deliberately about one-bit subjoin status, not every possible
interface.  For the partition \((M_1,M_2)\mid(M_3,M_4)\), let each half
export its exact relation on \((a,b,c,d)\).  Both half relations have size
three.  The intersections satisfy
\[
|P_{12}\cap P_{34}^-|=0,\qquad
|P_{12}\cap P_{34}^+|=1.
\]
Named relation gluing therefore recovers the parent label.  It must be
charged for discovery, representation, compatibility, replay and peak live
space.

Exact model counts are also strictly richer here.  For example the subjoin
\(\{M_1,M_4\}\) has three assignments in \(U\) and two in \(S\).  Thus this
pair is \emph{not} a count-oracle collision.  LSPV-4's
PARITY-MARGINAL-COUNT92 supplies a different family where every strict
partial-pin count is orientation-blind; its children remain
\(\Theta(N)\)-length and its source acquisition is matched by a linear
affine recognizer.  A witness-returning interface can likewise expose more
than one SAT bit.

Within the declared interface, any exact wrapper must therefore do at least
one of the following: acquire a source distinction or derived syntax; query
the whole parent or another non-strict child; or transmit richer
compatibility information than a SAT bit.  This is a semantic trichotomy,
not a work lower bound.  An arbitrary transformed short child may encode
compatibility in its SAT label and is not ruled out.

\section{Adaptive state and bit-resource ledger}
The tested policy receives no truth label.
\begin{description}
\item[State 0.] Read-only unknown source, headers and names not yet trusted.
Analysis knows the candidate pair; a real solver must pay recognition.
Alternatives are SCC/Horn/affine tests, degree-two matching, decomposition,
learning, direct sign recognition, or exact fallback.
\item[State 1.] A restricted policy chooses any proper module subset from
the observed transcript.  The answer is YES on both matched inputs.
Selection, child construction or virtual source dereferences, headers,
solver work, cache updates and failed trials are charged.
\item[State 2.] A source-aware policy may switch after reading the differing
literal-sign field.  On this canonical promise it can decide the pair
directly; on an unsupported input it must use an exact fallback.  This cheap
bypass is why no unrestricted lower bound follows.
\item[State 3.] A compatibility-preserving policy exports the two half
relations and intersects them.  SAT needs the one common assignment;
UNSAT observes empty intersection.  The relations, names and join work are
all live resources, not free semantic transitions.
\end{description}
The accounting vector is primitive bit work; source/work reads; all writes,
including unchanged writes; actual complete-state flips; peak live writable
bits; cumulative live-bit-time; and program/representation size.  The
experiment counts logical assertions only, not FLMV-machine operations.
