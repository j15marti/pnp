\section{PROPOSE: missing lemma and four falsifiable mechanisms}
The current bottleneck is not whether a \emph{closed} strict signed
exact-$(2,2)$ formula can have equality as an existential projection. It can.
The missing lemma is a uniform compositional interface: a partial gadget whose
ports retain declared signed occurrence slots for the source and future
contexts, whose completed union is strict exact-$(2,2)$, and whose complete
cofactor relation is preserved without solving the source.

Four mechanisms were fixed before search and build:
\begin{enumerate}
\item treat a degree-saturated equality gadget as a free wire and conjoin a
source clause at each endpoint;
\item duplicate a high-degree source name and link the copies by the peer
closed equality chain;
\item splice external literals into existing saturated occurrences and infer
that signed-count preservation also preserves semantics;
\item search instead for a genuinely \emph{open} equality gadget with one
declared missing signed occurrence at each endpoint.
\end{enumerate}
Mechanisms 1 and 2 fail by counting. Mechanism 3 was already falsified by the
UNSAT-to-SAT same-sign swap in PORT-SATURATION-REFEREE195. Mechanism 4 is the
correct formulation; its smallest admissible case is computationally absent.

\section{BUILD I: closed formulas have zero residual capacity}
\begin{theorem}[Zero residual signed capacity]\label{thm:zero}
Let $F$ be a strict signed exact-$(2,2)$ CNF and let $p$ be any variable of
$F$. If all clauses of $F$ are retained, then no nonempty external CNF can be
conjoined using $p$ while the result remains strict signed exact-$(2,2)$.
Renaming a second complete exact-$(2,2)$ port to $p$ gives four positive and
four negative occurrences, not two and two.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By definition $p$ already occurs twice positively and twice negatively in
$F$. Every added occurrence raises one of these counts. Identification adds
the signed counts of the identified variables. Neither clause addition nor
variable complementation decreases the total counts. Hence some original
occurrence must be deleted, replaced, or rerouted before any external use.
\end{proof}

The peer formula $E_k$ is therefore a \emph{closed saturated relation
realization}. Its equality chain is obtained by swapping already present
negative occurrences between fixed modules. It is not an object with a
dangling literal slot. In particular, LSPV-4's guarded source has a port $y$
with $m$ negative source occurrences. Replacing $y$ by copies and conjoining
closed equality gadgets cannot meet exact-$(2,2)$ counts: every copy endpoint
is already full before a source occurrence is attached.

This does not invalidate the peer construction. It invalidates only the
stronger inference from ``closed equality is representable'' to ``arbitrary
source ports can be regularized by conjunction.'' A legal repair must specify
which old occurrences are punctured and must authenticate the resulting full
puncture relation. PORT-SATURATION-REFEREE195 gives an explicit strict
exact-$(2,2)$ UNSAT source in which a same-sign occurrence swap preserves all
signed counts, properness, and connectedness but makes the formula SAT.

\section{BUILD II: the corrected open-port object}
Call a proper ternary CNF $H(p,q,Z)$ a one-slot-open exact-$(2,2)$ equality
gadget with external signs $(\sigma_p,\sigma_q)$ if:
\begin{enumerate}
\item every private variable has two internal occurrences of each sign;
\item each port has two internal occurrences of the sign opposite its
external sign, and one internal occurrence of its external sign;
\item its existential projection is exactly equality:
$\exists Z\,H(p,q,Z)$ iff $p=q$.
\end{enumerate}
Adding one external occurrence of sign $\sigma_p$ at $p$ and one of sign
$\sigma_q$ at $q$ then completes the signed counts. This is only a syntactic
completion rule; semantic preservation still depends on the external clauses
and the declared cofactor contract.

\begin{lemma}[Smallest admissible size]\label{lem:size}
If such a gadget has $r$ private variables and $m$ internal ternary clauses,
then $4r+6=3m$. Consequently $r\equiv0\pmod3$. The smallest case has
$r=3$, five total variables, and six clauses; the next has $r=6$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The private variables contribute $4r$ internal literal occurrences and the
two ports contribute three each. Every ternary clause consumes three
occurrences. Reducing modulo three gives $r\equiv0\pmod3$.
\end{proof}

\section{BUILD III: exact finite obstruction at the first size}
For each of the four external-sign pairs, the dependency-free reproducer
enumerates all unique proper signed ternary clauses over ports $1,2$ and
private variables $3,4,5$. It backtracks through every six-clause set with
the exact internal signed-degree vector, then evaluates all $2^5$ assignments
and records the projected relation on the ports.

Exactly 29,196 signed-degree-feasible clause sets occur for each external-sign
pair. The program performs 934,272 assignment evaluations per pair. None has
projected relation $\{00,11\}$. Thus all four smallest cases are absent. This
is \COMP, based on exact integer enumeration with no SAT solver or floating
point arithmetic. It is not a kernel-verified proof and says nothing about the
next admissible size with six private variables. The complete program SHA-256

\section{Corrected theorem and consequences}
The evidence supports the following exact correction.
\begin{quote}
Closed strict exact-$(2,2)$ equality and XOR relations exist, including the
peer unbounded closed equality chain. Such a relation is composable inside
the class only through an explicit occurrence-replacement operation whose
complete puncture semantics is proved. It does not by itself provide a free
port, a fan-out primitive, or an arbitrary-source regularizer. A general
regularizer must construct partial gadgets with signed deficits (or an
equivalent authenticated replacement interface), complete every source
occurrence exactly once, preserve all required cofactors, and charge the
construction and profile acquisition.
\end{quote}

On the constructive side, discovering a family of larger open gadgets plus a
uniform arbitrary-source routing theorem could be useful. It would still need
physical output shrink, closure, both SAT and UNSAT correctness, and a second
genuine recursive level. On the separation side, nonexistence at five
variables is a local architecture obstruction only. It gives no lower bound
against arbitrary SAT algorithms and no evidence that larger open gadgets do
not exist.

LSPV-4's theorem remains a sharp acquisition firewall: an always-SAT proper
3CNF can have one-port relation $R_F(0)=1$ and
$R_F(1)=\mathsf{SAT}(F)$. A constant-size exact relation can therefore be
NP-hard to acquire. It is not strict exact-$(2,2)$, and the present port-count
theorem does not transfer that hardness into the strict class. Conversely,
the LSPV-2 closed equality chain does not remove the acquisition requirement.

\section{Separate native resource ledgers}
The finite enumerator is a RAM-level research control, not a native SAT
algorithm. For a supplied fixed closed wire, an indexed RAM or fixed-$k$
read-only-input multitape machine can validate signed counts and print a fixed
splice in polynomial time, with all input and output bits charged. A
single-tape implementation counts the writable input footprint and head
travel; the conservative construction
\[
T_1=O\bigl(T_k(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2))\bigr),\qquad
P_1=O\bigl(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2)\bigr)
\]
is an upper simulation, not a lower bound. A fixed universal machine incurs
its own polynomial interpreter overhead and fixed program prefix. No
one-tape crossing-sequence bound, two-writable-tape bound, RAM exponent, or
FLMV forbidden-region claim is inferred from this port enumeration.
