\section{Method}
A new Node solver, \path{experiments/review/lspv5_507_peak_frontier_redteam.cjs}, was written without importing or calling the LSPV-4 Python program.  It formalizes deletion as an explicit atomic zero-cost move (LSPV-4 folds deletions into the compute edge), and runs lexicographic Dijkstra on (RES executions, byte area) over resident sets.  Area is charged as the resident payload entering each subsequent execution plus the final payload, which equals LSPV-4's post-step area at every optimum.  Minimizing executions with recomputation allowed also decides the no-recomputation question: the minimum equals 35 exactly when a schedule without any recomputation exists.  The DAG constants were transcribed from \path{experiments/arc/lspv4_565_recompute_peak.py}; the solver does not check that transcription against the archived certificate (see Section~5).

\textbf{Hostile self-control (a real bug, caught).} The first draft built resident sets with JavaScript \texttt{\&} on masks up to $2^{34}$.  JavaScript bitwise operators truncate to 32 bits, so the draft wrongly reported a feasible schedule at peak 40.  Replaying that schedule through LSPV-4's \texttt{verify\_trace} rejected it with transient peak 45.  After the fix (arithmetic bit tests) every claim reproduced.  Lesson for all roles: any JavaScript checker or optimizer that builds bitmasks over more than 31 items with \texttt{\&}, \texttt{|} or \texttt{<<} is unsound.  The archived /386 checker uses \texttt{(m>>i)\&1} only under an explicit guard of at most 12 edges, so it is not affected.

\textbf{Findings.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The 4-register limit never binds under overwrite.}  With overwrite allowed, removing the register limit changes nothing.  The byte budget alone fixes 41/45.  The phrase ``four registers never overflow'' in LSPV-4/565 is true, but it constrains nothing.
\item \emph{The overwrite rule is load-bearing.}  Without it, 4 registers cannot finish at \emph{any} byte budget, at least 5 registers are needed, and the thresholds rise to 53 and 59.  The 41-byte result is therefore a property of the rule set \{fresh output or nonexpanding overwrite\}, not of the DAG alone.  Any downstream use must name that rule.
\item \emph{Known-theorem match.}  The register gap (4 with overwrite, 5 without) agrees with the KNOWN THEOREM of van Emde Boas and van Leeuwen (1978/79): in the unit-weight black-pebble game, allowing sliding moves saves exactly one pebble on every DAG with at least one edge.  The match is a consistency check, not new mathematics.  The weighted byte thresholds (41 vs.\ 53) are outside that theorem: the size condition on overwrite makes this a weighted pebbling variant.  So the theorem is a \textbf{useful structural analogy} for the byte thresholds and a \textbf{directly transferable theorem} only for unrestricted unit-weight sliding.
\item \emph{Paid recomputation lowers the peak by 4 bytes with overwrite (45 to 41) and by 6 without it (59 to 53).}  This is the familiar pebbling space--time trade-off on one fixed small DAG.  It says nothing about $\min_\pi$ over alternative derivations, so it is not a $\overline W_R$ statement.
\end{enumerate}
Falsifier (iv) occurred in a precise sense: a different natural reading of ``legal register reuse'' (no in-place overwrite) changes every number.  Falsifier (v) occurred: the stated 4-register parameter is not the binding one.  Neither falsifies LSPV-4/565 as stated; both narrow how it may be cited.

\section{Literature and transfer audit}
Obstacle 1: whether resource numbers on one fixed DAG transfer to $\min_\pi$ over all derivations.  Obstacle 2: whether the numbers depend on the move rules.  Relevant literature: van Emde Boas and van Leeuwen, \emph{Move rules and trade-offs in the pebble game}, 4th GI Conference, LNCS 67, 1979, DOI 10.1007/3-540-09118-1\_12 (sliding saves exactly one pebble; a linear-to-exponential time trade-off when that pebble is saved), as restated in arXiv:2401.10579 (Theorem A there). Transferring this result to them is a \textbf{failed transfer} unless one proves that every alternative refutation of the source formula has at least the same peak.
