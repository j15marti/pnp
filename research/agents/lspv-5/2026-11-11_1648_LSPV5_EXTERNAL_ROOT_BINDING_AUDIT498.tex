\section{Target claim, quantifiers and predeclared falsifiers}
The single target was the following source-interface statement.
For every designated canonical source $E$ and candidate packet $\pi$, a
source-view composition verifier may soundly accept unequal component views
only if (i) the packet root is exactly $E$, (ii) every accepted SOURCE record
is an occurrence-indexed exact view of that root, and (iii) every internal and
cross-component edge is checked by the same sound typed transition relation.
The verifier must have the signature $V(E,\pi)$, not merely $V(\pi)$.

The fixed falsifiers were: (F1) change the caller source while keeping the
packet source fixed and obtain acceptance; (F2) obtain equal view labels with
different decoded clauses; (F3) retain a paid owner, facet or derived parent
across a source mismatch; (F4) obtain a cheap equal-view path with divergent
semantics.  F1 succeeds against the \emph{naive wrapper}; F2--F4 do not
succeed against the fully specified SOURCE/strict-RES core tested here.

The SAME\_REALIZATION objects are the designated caller root $E$, the packet
root $E_\pi$, the SOURCE occurrences and the single chronological proof DAG.
The weakest useful repair is the boundary equality below; no claim about a
native mixed-rule lower bound is made.

\section{Exact counterexample and repaired lemma}
Let the caller ask about the satisfiable formula
\[
 E_{\rm sat}=(x),
\]
which has one model.  Give the LSPV-1/583-style embedded verifier a packet
whose self-declared root is
\[
 E_\pi=(x\lor y)\wedge(\neg y)\wedge(\neg x).
\]
The packet contains component views $\{s_0,s_1\}$ and $\{s_2\}$ and the exact
two paid strict-Resolution steps
\[
 (x\lor y),\neg y\vdash x,
 \qquad x,\neg x\vdash\bot.
\]
The published embedded semantics correctly accepts this packet and correctly
proves $E_\pi$ UNSAT.  A wrapper that returns ``$E_{\rm sat}$ is UNSAT'' after
calling only $V(\pi)$ is nevertheless wrong.  The executable's in-band source
map is not an authentication of an absent out-of-band argument.

\paragraph{Repaired source-bound composition lemma (PROVED/RESTRICTED).}
Let $\operatorname{enc}$ be one total injective canonical encoding of
occurrence-indexed CNF sources.  Define
\[
 V^*(E,\pi)=
 [\operatorname{enc}(E)=\operatorname{enc}(E_\pi)]\wedge V_{\rm emb}(\pi).
\]
Suppose every SOURCE leaf in $\pi$ equals its indexed occurrence in $E_\pi$,
every component rule and every cross-component rule is locally sound, all
assumptions are discharged at the terminal empty clause, and all dependencies
are chronological.  Then $V^*(E,\pi)=1$ implies that $E$ is unsatisfiable.
The proof is immediate induction over the shared DAG: each leaf is entailed by
$E$ after the boundary equality, each checked inference preserves entailment,
and the sink is empty.  Component source views need not be equal to one
another or contain all of $E$; exact occurrence inclusion suffices.  Equality
of a digest is interchangeable with equality of canonical bytes only under an
explicit collision-free mathematical encoding theorem; no cryptographic
assumption is used here.

\section{Primary literature and transfer classification}
Cook--Reckhow's original proof-system definition makes the proof relation a
polynomial-time relation for the formula being proved; it does not authorize
attributing a proof of one formula to another.  See S. Cook and R. Reckhow,
``The Relative Efficiency of Propositional Proof Systems,'' \emph{JSL} 44
(1979), \url{https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~sacook/homepage/cook_reckhow.pdf}.
The LRAT paper likewise validates a clausal derivation relative to the input
CNF, and FRAT uses unique clause identifiers for proof references; neither
paper supplies the missing historical mixed $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ semantics.
See L. Cruz-Filipe et al., ``Efficient Certified RAT Verification,''
\url{https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mheule/publications/lrat.pdf}, and S. Baek,
M. Carneiro and M. Heule, ``A Flexible Proof Format for SAT Solver--Elaborator
Communication,'' \emph{LMCS} 18(2), 2022,
\url{https://lmcs.episciences.org/9357}.

Clarke et al.'s CEGAR discipline requires replay of an abstract
counterexample before refinement; this transfers exactly as a methodology,
not as a calculus-authentication theorem.  See ``Counterexample-guided
Abstraction Refinement,'' \url{https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs357/cegar.pdf}.
Thus input binding and concrete replay are \textbf{THEOREM/METHOD TRANSFERS};
the idea that generic certified SAT formats authenticate the historical mixed
grammar is a \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER}.
