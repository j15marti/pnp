\section{Claim card and falsifiable mechanisms}
\noindent\textbf{Claim ID:} PINNED-CHILD-SEPARATOR124.  \textbf{Status before
run:} pure strict-deletion child statuses are identical on the archived
MINIMAL-UNSAT-PROJECTION20 pair; whether ordinary pinned children separate
that pair was open.  \textbf{Desired conclusion:} find the first cyclic
child/query whose SAT status differs, or prove none exists.

\noindent\textbf{Quantifiers and model.}  For every power of two
$M=2^h\ge16$ and the fixed Role-4 cyclic schedule satisfying the conditions
below, there is one metadata-computable pinning rule and one indexed child
that distinguishes the two named ordinary proper-3CNFs.  This is a promise
statement about a matched pair, not a transformation correct on every SAT
input.  Children are self-contained fixed-width proper-3CNFs; all headers,
pin gadgets, source reads, addressing, construction and solver work are
charged.  The base solver is one fixed uniform exact random-access machine.

\noindent\textbf{SAME\_REALIZATION IDs:} parent source, cyclic selector,
ordinary pin-augmented child and child-solver simulation all belong to the
same declared execution.  No SAT label, witness or source pointer is free.

Three mechanisms were fixed before the test.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Pure deletion statuses separate the pair.}
\textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED} by MINIMAL-UNSAT-PROJECTION20: every strict

\item \emph{A source-independent fixed pin set can separate it.}

\section{Exact pair and ordinary encoding}
For a literal $\ell$ and fresh variables $a,b$, let
\[
 U(\ell;a,b)=\bigwedge_{\epsilon,\delta\in\{+1,-1\}}
 (\ell\vee \epsilon a\vee\delta b),
\]
an ordinary proper-3CNF block equivalent to the unit $\ell$.  For $m\ge2$
put
\[
 P_i=(\neg x_i\vee x_{i+1}\vee c_i)\wedge
     (\neg x_i\vee x_{i+1}\vee\neg c_i)
\]
and
\[
 F_m=U(x_1;a_0,b_0)\wedge\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m-1}P_i
      \wedge U(\neg x_m;a_m,b_m).
\tag{1}
\]
The SAT parent $G_m$ replaces the first final-block clause
\[
 D_F=(\neg x_m\vee a_m\vee b_m)
 \quad\hbox{by}\quad
 D_G=(\neg x_m\vee a_m\vee x_1).
\tag{2}
\]
Both have $M=2m+6$ clauses and $n=2m+3=M-3$ variables.  The archived proof
shows that $F_m$ is minimally UNSAT and $G_m$ is SAT, while every strict
clause subformula of either is SAT.

Use Role~4's fixed-width encoding: two header words $(n,M)$ and four words
per clause, with signed literal code $2|\ell|+\mathbf1_{\ell<0}$ and width
\[
 w(n,M)=\left\lceil\log_2(64(n+M+1))\right\rceil,
 \qquad N=(4M+2)w(n,M).
\tag{3}
\]
Choose odd $K=\Theta(\sqrt N/\log N)$ with $2K\le M$ and set
$Q=\min\{M,\lfloor N^{3/5}\rfloor\}$.  The $j$th cyclic child selects
\[
 I_j=\{(jK+i)\bmod M:0\le i<K\}.
\tag{4}
\]
The altered clause has zero-based index $d=M-4$ in the declared ordering.

\section{The first pinned separator}
For every $j$, augment the selected source clauses by the three ordinary
pin blocks
\[
 U(x_m;\alpha_1,\alpha_2),\quad
 U(\neg a_m;\alpha_3,\alpha_4),\quad
 U(\neg b_m;\alpha_5,\alpha_6).
\tag{5}
\]
These are twelve explicit proper ternary clauses on six fresh variables.
The query is source-independent within the declared family: its indices and
variable names are computed from $(M,K)$ and the public identifier layout,
not from a source literal or a hidden label.

\begin{theorem}[First differing cyclic pinned child; PROVED/EXACT]
Let
\[
 j_*=\left\lfloor\frac{M-4}{K}\right\rfloor.
\tag{6}
\]
For every $j<j_*$, the complete ordinary pinned-child encodings obtained
from $F_m$ and $G_m$ are identical and hence have identical SAT status.  At
$j_*$,
\[
 \SAT(\widehat C_{j_*}(F_m))=0,
 \qquad
 \SAT(\widehat C_{j_*}(G_m))=1.
\tag{7}
\]
For the Role-4 asymptotic parameters $j_*<Q$ for all sufficiently large
$M$; it also holds in every finite row audited below.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $j<j_*$, the nonwrapping interval $jK,\ldots,jK+K-1$ ends before
$d=M-4$.  The two parents differ only at $d$, so the selected records and
the identical pin blocks are byte-for-byte equal.  By (6), $d\in I_{j_*}$.
The pins make $x_m=1,a_m=b_m=0$, which falsifies $D_F$; hence the $F_m$
child is UNSAT.  The full $G_m$ has a satisfying assignment with all
$x_i=1$ and $a_m=b_m=0$.  Its restriction satisfies every selected source
clause, and the six fresh auxiliaries extend the true unit literals in (5).
Thus the $G_m$ child is SAT.  Finally, $N=\Theta(M\log M)$ and
$M/K=\Theta(\sqrt N)$, whereas $Q=N^{3/5+o(1)}$, proving eventual
$j_*<Q$.
\end{proof}

The ordinary pinned child has exactly $K+12$ clauses, $n+6$ named
variables and bit length
\[
 L_{\rm pin}=(4(K+12)+2)w(n+6,K+12)=N^{1/2+o(1)}.
\tag{8}
\]
If windows are queried in order, the first difference appears after
$j_*+1=\Theta(\sqrt N)$ calls.  This is not compulsory: the controller can
compute $j_*$ and jump directly to the one distinguishing child.  Under the
promise it can do even less---after recognizing the canonical schema, read
the one altered literal field in (2), which costs only $O(\log N)$ source
bits.  Full schema recognition costs $N^{1+o(1)}$ bit work and permits direct
decision.  The pair therefore tests semantic separation; it supplies no
acquisition or continuation lower bound.

\section{Why this is not parent gluing}
The theorem resolves the exact next test in CYCLIC-VIRTUAL-SELECTOR82 but
does not repair ARC.  A SAT parent $U(\neg x;u,v)$ becomes UNSAT after the
incompatible pin $U(x;r,s)$.  Conversely, the UNSAT parent
$U(x;u,v)\wedge U(\neg x;r,s)$ has a SAT strict child containing only its
first block and the compatible pin $U(x;p,q)$.  Therefore neither
\[
 \SAT(F)\Rightarrow\SAT(\text{pinned child})
 \quad\hbox{nor}\quad
 \SAT(\text{pinned child})\Rightarrow\SAT(F)
\]
holds on unrestricted inputs.

Decide-first normalization is also a valid bypass: after paying to decide a
source, one may emit a constant SAT or constant-false UNSAT representation.
The output size does not charge the earlier decision.  The SAT witness of
$G_m$ is source-readable and reconstructible in linear time; $F_m$ needs no
enumeration or particular refutation.  Source rereads, representation
switching and direct decision are therefore retained as hostile controls.

\section{Adaptive-state and BIT-RESOURCE1 ledger}
At state $S_0$ only the ordinary read-only source pointer and headers are
available; $m,K,Q,j_*$ are obtained by charged arithmetic.  The policy may
(i) use deletion-only labels, already known to collide; (ii) validate the
canonical family and directly inspect (2); (iii) issue (5) at $j_*$; or
(iv) abandon the promise attempt and select Horn, SCC, affine/Gaussian or
exact fallback.  No true label chooses the branch.

After family validation, direct field inspection is the least expensive
restricted rule.  For the requested semantic experiment, the policy instead
selects (iii), generates each requested ordinary child bit from a source
record or one of twelve fixed gadget clauses, and simulates one fixed child
decider.  State $S_1$ contains the input index, child index, gadget counter,
solver state and remaining budget.  Correctness of returning the child bit as
the parent bit holds only under the explicit $\{F_m,G_m\}$ promise; outside
it the policy must retain an exact fallback, whose worst-case exponent is not
improved.

The resource vector is
\[
(U,R_s,R_w,W_w,\Delta_{\rm work},\Delta_{\rm total},S_{\rm peak},BT,
 |\mathcal P|).
\tag{9}
\]
$U$ charges source/workspace reads, unchanged writes, control, addressing,
selection, switching, failed recognizers, gadget generation and solver
steps.  Complete-state flips include head/index/control flips in addition to
workspace changes.  Peak live bits include the simultaneous generator,
solver, parent state and live ancestors; cumulative bit-time uses actual
live bits at actual steps.  A materialized child writes $L_{\rm pin}$ bits;
a virtual service charges every regenerated probe and keeps the child
solver's space.  An arbitrary semantic transition or correct output bit is
never a free primitive.

\paragraph{Restricted adaptive controls.}
The executable runs both labels through five observed-evidence rules:
canonical direct-field recognition, Horn forward chaining, SCC 2-SAT,
affine Gaussian elimination and exact DPLL fallback.  These ten finite cases
are a substantive switching control, not coverage of every uniform policy.
SAT and UNSAT have different terminal invariants: SAT needs one extendible
assignment only when requested, whereas UNSAT may stop on a conflict and is
not required to emit a proof.
