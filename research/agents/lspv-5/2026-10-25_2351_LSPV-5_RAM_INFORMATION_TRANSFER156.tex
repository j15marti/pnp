\section*{Executive verdict}
P versus NP remains open.  The strongest new exact statement is a checkpoint
transcript bound.  If an indexed read-only-input deterministic computation has
$S$ writable/index bits at a checkpoint, then makes at most $q$ probes returning
$w$ input bits each, the number $R$ of post-checkpoint behaviours it can distinguish
satisfies
\[
             R\le 2^{S+qw},\qquad S+qw\ge\log_2 R.                 \tag{1}
\]
This is useful accounting, but it exposes rather than closes the central barrier:
ordinary SAT decision requires only two output classes.  Without an independently
proved online, multi-output, witness, or all-UNSAT certificate obligation, (1) gives
only $S+qw\ge1$.  Counting models, proof leaves, or internal representations does not
force an arbitrary decider to preserve them.

The literature provides strong memory--time formulae for sorting, dynamic data
structures, streaming, fixed computation DAGs, circuit evaluation, and restricted
branching programs.  None presently transfers to unrestricted ordinary SAT without
a new object- and model-matched compiler.  In particular, the current formula
\(\mathrm{TIME}[t]\subseteq\mathrm{SPACE}[O(\sqrt{t\log t})]\) for multitape
Turing machines is a space simulation theorem; it does not license pairing the
original machine's time with the simulator's space.  Thus $P=NP$ still supplies only
an unknown finite polynomial exponent, not the project-specific pair
$(1.802,o(1))$ or the FLMV pair.

\section*{Pre-search falsifiers}
Before searching the literature, this run fixed four hostile tests.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item A state-only cut bound should fail when read-only source bits can be reread after
the cut.
\item Entropy/counting should collapse for Boolean decision unless a many-output
obligation is independently justified.
\item Pebbling, VLSI, and circuit-evaluation tradeoffs should constrain a fixed DAG or
representation, not every algorithm for the represented Boolean function.
\item A low-space simulation of a fast machine should not be combined with the fast
machine's time unless one and the same simulator achieves both bounds.
\end{enumerate}
All four tests survive review; the first two receive an exact theorem and finite
counterexample below.

\section*{Checkpoint transcript theorem}
Fix a deterministic indexed read-only-input machine and a checkpoint.  Its complete
writable/index state has at most $S$ bits.  After the checkpoint it makes at most $q$
adaptive probes.  A probe returns at most $w$ input bits.  Addresses, control choices,
and the next probe are deterministic functions of the checkpoint state and earlier
answers; they do not contribute fresh source information.

For each of at most $2^S$ starting states there are at most $(2^w)^q$ answer
transcripts.  Hence at most $2^{S+qw}$ behaviours can be distinguished.  If correctness
requires $R$ pairwise distinct post-checkpoint behaviours, (1) follows.  This proof is
\proved.  Variable-length runs are padded by a stop symbol or bounded by the same full
tree.  Randomized, quantum, nondeterministic, or word-RAM variants require their own
state and success conventions.

For a Boolean decider, correctness itself names only the classes SAT and UNSAT, so
$R\le2$.  A lower bound using $R=2^{\Omega(N)}$ must prove that the algorithm must
emit or preserve that many different objects.  Witnesses, proof trees, contexts,
models, and internal clause states are not automatically required outputs.  The
decisive missing lemma is therefore:
\begin{quote}
Construct, for every exact ordinary-SAT decider, an all-input transformation into a
model with $R=2^{\Omega(N^\alpha)}$ required post-checkpoint distinctions, while
preserving correctness, uniformity, encoded length, source access, and a synchronized
time--space pair, and while charging the transformation itself.
\end{quote}
No reviewed theorem supplies this bridge.

\section*{Concrete reread counterexample}
Define the proper 3-CNF gadget
\[
U(\ell;a,b)=\bigwedge_{\sigma,\tau\in\{+1,-1\}}
  (\ell\vee \sigma a\vee \tau b).
\]
It is exactly equivalent to the literal $\ell$.  Consider
\[
F_1=U(x;a,b)\wedge U(x;c,d),\qquad
F_0=U(x;a,b)\wedge U(\neg x;c,d).
\]
$F_1$ is SAT with $16$ assignments and $F_0$ is UNSAT.  Before reading the second
block, a machine may have identical (even zero-source-information) persistent state on
both instances.  Reading the four sign bits of $x$ in the late block distinguishes the
two cases.  Thus a state-only cut bound is false; a bound charging $q=4$, $w=1$ probes
is intact.

The reproducer exhaustively verified properness, all $2^5$ assignments, four late sign
differences, and 75 finite transcript identities.  Status: \comp{} plus the symbolic
\proved{} theorem.  This is an easy family and does not refute a worst-case SAT lower
bound; it refutes only a universal no-reread/state-only claim.

\section*{SAT upper and lower-bound calibration}
Let $n$ be variable count and $N$ full encoding length.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item A deterministic exhaustive solver uses time $O(2^nN)$ and writable/index space
$O(n+\log N)$ by streaming assignments and rescanning the read-only formula.  This
already demonstrates exponential time with small memory; large time does not imply
large peak space.
\item As of July 2026, Jiang--Cai report randomized worst-case general-3-SAT time
$O^*(1.307031578^n)$ for repeated PPSZ, with Unique-3-SAT
$O^*(1.306969598^n)$; see \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10697}.
This is a randomized variable-count upper bound, not a deterministic $N$-exponent or
an unconditional lower bound.
\item ETH and SETH are conditional hypotheses: ETH excludes $2^{o(n)}$ 3-SAT and SETH
excludes $(2-\varepsilon)^n$ algorithms uniformly over sufficiently large clause
width.  They are not proofs of P$\ne$NP and do not provide the current ARC arrow.
\item Van Melkebeek's coNTIME/small-space SAT theorem and FLMV give unconditional
incompatibilities only when their simultaneous machine premises hold.  The present
project's strict FLMV target is
\[
 t<\frac{241}{200}=1.205,
 \qquad s<\frac{11642479}{23232400}=0.5011311358\ldots .
\]
A promised-family solver, a full-circuit bound from another algorithm, or a separate
space simulation does not instantiate that same ordinary-SAT machine.
\item Bathie--Williams' strongly uniform NAND-depth result and ACC/circuit lower-bound
results are genuine circuit-model theorems.  They do not imply an arbitrary RAM SAT
time lower bound without a model-matched compiler.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Why the $d=1.802$ claim still fails}
Assuming $P=NP$ gives some fixed uniform polynomial SAT decider with an unknown finite
exponent $K$.  Williams then gives a language-equivalent square-root-space simulation
in its multitape model, but no theorem reviewed here forces $K=1.802$, subpolynomial
space, or a same-machine pair in the strict FLMV box.  The independent stronger
assumption ``SAT is in deterministic $N^{1.802}$ time and $N^{o(1)}$ space'' may be
studied conditionally.  Falsifying it alone is not P$\ne$NP.  Status of the implication
from $P=NP$ to that pair: \retired.

\section*{Circuit/certificate project impact}
The weighted leaf inequality
\[
\sum_i m_i^d\le M^{d-1}\sum_i m_i
\]
remains \proved arithmetic after a sound complete all-UNSAT leaf family is supplied.
Information transfer does not supply that family.  A deterministic decider may
recognize an affine, Horn, 2SAT, graph-factor, cached, or direct case and output one bit
without materializing the proposed leaves.  Therefore all-UNSAT coverage remains
\openstatus, independently of the leaf-volume arithmetic.

Likewise, a circuit representing one trace or a witness relation is not the full
length-$N$ SAT decision circuit required by circuit lower-bound premises.  Uniform
circuit generation, named source indices, gate access, and two recursive levels remain
charged obligations.  The 15/19 versus 348/463 gadget discrepancy is unaffected by
this run; the unavailable 348/463 source cannot be inferred from information theory.

\section*{Post-quantum technique ledger}
The checkpoint transcript map is an exact setting for collision accounting.  For a
map $L:A\to B$,
\[
 \operatorname{Coll}(L)=|\{(a,a'):L(a)=L(a')\}|,
 \qquad |\operatorname{im}L|\ge |A|^2/\operatorname{Coll}(L)
\]
is an \textbf{EXACT OBJECT/THEOREM TRANSFER} by Cauchy--Schwarz.  However, a SAT
decider is allowed to collapse every satisfiable input to output 1 and every
unsatisfiable input to 0.  Without a proof that collisions correspond to conflicting
valid openings or wrong future answers, substituting input entropy for required image
size is a \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER}.  Schur squares, quadratic relations, syzygies,
hashes, and collapse-binding remain \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE} only; hidden algebraic
structure must also be tested as a solver shortcut.

\section*{Single decisive next lemma}
Prove or falsify a \emph{decision-to-many-output compiler}: from every fixed uniform
total exact ordinary-SAT decider $A$, construct on every input length an online task or
complete all-UNSAT certificate relation with exponentially many required compatible
answers, while simulating $A$ with fully charged random access, retries, caches,
strategy choices, and no parent-size hidden call.  The compiler must preserve one
machine and the strict time--space pair.  Without this arrow, known information-
transfer formulae remain restricted-model controls rather than a P versus NP proof.
