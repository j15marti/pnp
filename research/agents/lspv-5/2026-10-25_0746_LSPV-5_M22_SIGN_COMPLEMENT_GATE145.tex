\section{Exact global-complementation criterion}
For a literal $\ell$ let $v(\ell)$ be its variable and let $\sigma(\ell)\in\{0,1\}$ be zero for a negative literal and one for a positive literal.  A global complementation is a vector $f:V\to\{0,1\}$; the transformed sign of $\ell$ is $\sigma(\ell)\oplus f(v(\ell))$.  Variable complementation is a bijection on assignments and therefore preserves the exact model count.

Construct a parity constraint graph $G_F$.  For every clause $C$ and every two literals $\ell,ell'\in C$, impose
\[
 f(v(\ell))\oplus f(v(\ell'))
   =\sigma(\ell)\oplus\sigma(\ell').                 \tag{1}
\]
It is enough to use two constraints from one anchor literal per ternary clause.

\begin{theorem}[SIGN-CONSISTENCY1]
For every CNF $F$, a global variable complementation makes every clause of $F$ all-positive or all-negative if and only if the XOR system~\emph{(1)} is consistent.  Consistency can be decided by a parity disjoint-set structure or by graph traversal.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If the transformed clause is monotone, all transformed signs in that clause are equal; cancelling them pairwise gives (1).  Conversely, if (1) holds, every pair of transformed signs in a clause is equal, so that clause is all-positive or all-negative.  The same $f$ is used at every occurrence, exactly enforcing the global quantifier.  XOR consistency is the standard parity-connectivity problem.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Proper padding obstruction]
For any two distinct variables $r,s$ and fresh $p$, the proper clauses
\[
 (r\vee s\vee p),\qquad(r\vee s\vee\neg p)           \tag{2}
\]
cannot simultaneously be made monotone by any global variable complementation.  The conjunction is nevertheless satisfiable and is equivalent, after existentially forgetting $p$, to $(r\vee s)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first clause requires $f(r)\oplus f(p)=0$, while the second requires $f(r)\oplus f(p)=1$.  Satisfiability and the projection claim follow because one of $p,\neg p$ satisfies its padding clause whenever $r\vee s$ is false only if the other fails; together the pair is exactly $r\vee s$.
\end{proof}

The Horn control $(\neg r\vee\neg s\vee p)\wedge(\neg r\vee\neg s\vee\neg p)$ has the same obstruction and is equivalent to the easy Horn clause $\neg r\vee\neg s$.  Standard proper padding of the two binary clauses in
\[
 z\leftrightarrow(x\wedge y):quad
 (\neg x\vee\neg y\vee z)\wedge(x\vee\neg z)\wedge(y\vee\neg z)
\]
therefore creates the obstruction twice.  If a Tseitin encoding instead permits repeated literals or width below three, it already misses the target's proper-three-distinct-variable grammar.  Thus an exact finite gate/padding library and a genuine semantic monotone gadget compiler are indispensable; tags alone do not fill the gap.

This result does \emph{not} say that a semantic equisatisfiable monotone gadget is impossible.  Darmann--D\"ocker explicitly construct such gadgets for a different source promise.  The receiving mismatch is locality, exact source grammar, occurrence quotas, output access, and inverse-parent access---not mere polynomiality.
