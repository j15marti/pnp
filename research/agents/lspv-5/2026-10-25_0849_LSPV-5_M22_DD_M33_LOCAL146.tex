\section{Exact templates}
For each source variable \(x_i\), create \(x_{i,1},x_{i,2}\).  Replace its
two negative occurrences by positive \(x_{i,1}\), its two positive
occurrences by positive \(x_{i,2}\), and erase signs.  Add
\((x_{i,1}\vee x_{i,2})\) and the following ten-clause gadget on private
\(a,b,c,d\):
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
(\bar a\vee\bar b\vee\bar x_{i,1})&
(\bar a\vee\bar c\vee\bar x_{i,1})&
(\bar a\vee\bar d\vee\bar x_{i,1})\\
(\bar b\vee\bar c\vee\bar x_{i,2})&
(\bar b\vee\bar d\vee\bar x_{i,2})&
(\bar c\vee\bar d\vee\bar x_{i,2}).
\end{array}
\]
The remaining four clauses are all positive triples on \(\{a,b,c,d\}\).
This block extends iff at least one copy is false.  Together with the positive
pair it forces exactly one copy true.

Because \(4n=3m\), \(n\) is divisible by three.  Batch the \(n\) positive
pairs in source-variable order.  For each batch create \(y,a,b,c,d,e,f\),
pad the three pairs by \(y\), and add the literalwise complement of
Darmann--D\"ocker's thirteen-clause \(\mathcal S\).  Its seven negative
clauses are
\[
\begin{gathered}
(\bar y\vee\bar a\vee\bar b),(\bar y\vee\bar c\vee\bar d),
(\bar y\vee\bar e\vee\bar f),\\
(\bar a\vee\bar c\vee\bar f),(\bar a\vee\bar d\vee\bar e),
(\bar b\vee\bar c\vee\bar e),(\bar b\vee\bar d\vee\bar f),
\end{gathered}
\]
and its six positive clauses are
\[
(a\vee c\vee f),(a\vee d\vee e),(a\vee e\vee f),
(b\vee c\vee d),(b\vee c\vee e),(b\vee d\vee f).
\]
It extends iff \(y=0\), so padding preserves the pairs.

\begin{lemma}[Size, degrees, syntax]
The output has \(25n/3\) variables and \(50n/3\) clauses.  Every variable
has three occurrences of each sign.  Properness and clause uniqueness are
preserved.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The variables are \(2n\) copies, \(4n\) first-gadget auxiliaries, and
\(7n/3\) batch variables.  Clause counts are
\[
\frac{4n}{3}+10n+n+\frac{13n}{3}=\frac{50n}{3}.
\]
A copy has its two transformed source positions plus its padded pair
positively, and its three gadget ports negatively.  Each auxiliary has three
occurrences of each sign.  A batch \(y\) has three padded positive
occurrences and three negative ports.  The literal-to-copy map is injective;
private namespaces separate all gadget blocks.  A transformed clause uses
three distinct base variables, whereas a padded pair uses both copies of one
base variable, so the record types cannot collide.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Equisatisfiability]
\(F\) is satisfiable if and only if \(R(F)\) is satisfiable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Given a source assignment set \(x_{i,2}=x_i\),
\(x_{i,1}=\neg x_i\), extend every first gadget, set each \(y=0\), and
extend each complemented \(\mathcal S\) block.  Conversely, the positive
pair plus its first gadget forces \(x_{i,1}=\neg x_{i,2}\).  Projecting
\(x_i=x_{i,2}\) then satisfies exactly the original clauses.
\end{proof}

\section{Fixed-block locality and composition}
Order output records as transformed source clauses, \(n\) ten-clause blocks,
\(n\) padded pairs, and \(n/3\) thirteen-clause blocks.  Use consecutive
identifier intervals for copies, four private auxiliaries per source name,
and seven names per batch.  Division and remainder identify the block and
constant template.  A transformed record reads only the three source
identifier/sign fields.  For either copy, the two transformed occurrences
retain ranks \(1,2\), the padded pair is positive rank \(3\), and its three
first-gadget ports are negative ranks \(1,2,3\).  Auxiliary and \(y\) tags
are template positions.  Thus forward records and inverse
\emph{occurrence/incidence} parents are local.  This does not assert constant
inverse dependence for every global header or malformed-input validity bit.

M22-LOCAL-CHAIN140 maps a tagged Monotone 3-Sat-$(3,3)$ source with \(q\)
variables to \(\mathcal C_\star\) with
\((1995q+6,2660q+8)\).  Substitution of \(q=25n/3\) gives the displayed
\((n_\star,m_\star)\).  Four fixed local representation layers preserve
polylogarithmic addressed work and logarithmic accessor space.  The exact
remaining theorem is
\[
\texttt{LOCAL-COOK-TAGGED-SIGNED22-1}:
\quad\text{ordinary indexed SAT}\longrightarrow
\text{valid tagged signed 3-Sat-}(2,2).
\]
It must pay occurrence-rank discovery, malformed rejection, output access,
inverse ports if used, length, and simultaneous workspace.

\section{Direct post-acquisition red team}
The proposed potential \emph{number of unresolved copy pairs} is \RETIRED as
a universal lower-bound potential.  It starts at only \(n\), and a solver may
decide directly on the signed source, recognize affine/Horn/2SAT factors,
cache a source decision, reorder clauses, or never instantiate any copy.
If the potential is forced to zero only on the output write, that transition
drops all remaining \(n\), so \(\Gamma(N)\ge n\) and telescoping gives only a
constant bound.  If it is not forced to zero, the terminal condition fails.
This does not count ADAPTIVE-COVER75 acquisition again and does not rule out a
different independently initialized structural potential.
