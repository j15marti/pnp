\section*{Status and one result}

P versus NP, ARC-EDGE-90 and the direct post-acquisition lower-bound spine
remain \OPEN.  This run proves one restricted no-go theorem.  If a source
variable with $r$ literal occurrences is replaced by $r$ clones, with every
clone used once in the transformed source clauses, then a clone-only proper
ternary CNF enforcing equality of all clones needs at least $2r$ clauses.
Exact $(2^+,2^-)$ target quotas leave only $3r$ clone slots, hence only $r$
proper ternary clauses.  Therefore the quota-tight, auxiliary-free,
one-clause-per-occurrence normalization is impossible.  Repeated literals,
fresh existential auxiliaries, larger port budgets and different reductions
are not excluded.

\section{Exact boundary-separation theorem}

For $i\in[r]$, let $e_i$ be the word with only coordinate $i$ equal to
one, and let $\bar e_i$ have only coordinate $i$ equal to zero.

\begin{theorem}[Proper equality clause floor]
Let $G$ be a CNF over $z_1,\ldots,z_r$ in which every clause contains
exactly three distinct variables.  Suppose
\[
   \operatorname{Mod}(G)=\{0^r,1^r\}.
\]
Then $G$ contains at least $2r$ clauses and at least $6r$ literal
occurrences.  Uniqueness of clauses is not needed for the lower bound.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every clause is satisfied by both $0^r$ and $1^r$, so it contains at least
one positive and at least one negative literal.  A proper ternary clause has
therefore either one negative and two positive literals, or two negative and
one positive literal.

Consider $e_i$.  A clause can be false on $e_i$ only if its sole negative
literal is $\neg z_i$ and its two positive variables are different from
$z_i$.  Thus such a clause falsifies exactly one of $e_1,\ldots,e_r$.
Symmetrically, a clause can be false on $\bar e_i$ only if its sole positive
literal is $z_i$; it then falsifies exactly one of
$\bar e_1,\ldots,\bar e_r$.  No proper ternary clause belongs to both sign
types.  Since $G$ must reject all $2r$ boundary words, at least $2r$ clauses
are required.  Three literals per clause give at least $6r$ occurrences.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Quota-tight clone-only normalizer does not exist]
Split a source variable with $r$ occurrences into clones $z_1,\ldots,z_r$,
and use each clone in exactly one transformed source literal.  There is no
auxiliary-free proper ternary equality gadget on only those clones that both
enforces $z_1=\cdots=z_r$ and leaves every clone with exactly two positive
and two negative target occurrences.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each clone already occupies one source-clause slot and therefore has exactly
three remaining occurrence slots under the exact-$(2,2)$ quota.  The gadget
has $3r$ literal slots.  Because it is proper ternary, it has exactly $r$
clauses, contradicting the theorem's $2r$ lower bound.
\end{proof}

The result applies regardless of the signs of the $r$ original occurrences:
the source sign changes which polarity is missing but not the three-slot
budget per clone.  It is stronger than merely rejecting a directed cycle of
implications, but deliberately weaker than rejecting all bounded-occurrence
normalizers.

\section{Primary-literature and archive reconciliation}

Jahanjou--Miles--Viola reduce nondeterministic time $T$ to a quasilinear-size
3SAT instance with a local clause accessor.  Their theorem supplies the
correct source-local starting point, but not proper unique exact signed
$(2,2)$ quotas.  Berman--Karpinski--Scott establish bounded-occurrence
hardness, including exact four occurrences in the relevant later
formulations, but a qualitative NP-hardness theorem does not itself state the
indexed output length, addressed target-bit cost, inverse occurrence ports,
malformed-input path or same-machine workspace needed by FLMV.

The explicit $(2/2/3)$ construction of Dehghan et al. is a useful near miss:
its occurrence-splitting clauses contain a repeated padding literal
$(x_i\vee y_i\vee y_i)$ or its signed variants, and its problem definition
requires only two distinct variables per clause.  The paper explicitly leaves
the three-distinct-variable version as an open question.  It therefore does
not instantiate the present proper source interface.  Darmann--D\"ocker and
D\"ocker supply the semantic bounded-occurrence targets already used in
M22-DD-M33-LOCAL146; they do not remove this first-leg port obligation.

\section{Contraction and bit-resource ledger}

For any proposed replacement, the same uniform indexed machine must charge:
source parsing and validity; occurrence-number or native-tag acquisition;
block and template selection; address arithmetic; every source/work read;
every changed or unchanged write; emitted clause headers and literal IDs;
duplicate/properness checks when required; failed template trials; witness
transport if used; representation switches; cache traffic; live ancestors;
and peak target-bit/accessor buffers.  Literal flips, logical bit work,
physical switching, hardware size and cumulative bit-time remain distinct.

The theorem shows that the clone-only quota ledger is infeasible before time
or space is optimized.  It does not show that auxiliaries are expensive.
Indeed, a binary implication cycle enforces clone equality with $r$ easy
constraints, and SCC or parity union--find decides it in $O(r)$ work.  Turning
those binary clauses into the required proper exact-$(2,2)$ target is the
unfilled step.  A polynomial emitter is insufficient for the FLMV endpoint:
the required first leg still needs $N^{1+o(1)}$ output length,
$N^{o(1)}$ addressed-bit overhead and compatible low workspace, or another
fully charged route with strict endpoint slack.

Two actual recursive levels cannot be tested for the rejected compiler
because the first level has no legal output.  This is a construction
infeasibility, not a favorable zero-cost ledger.  Dehghan's repeated-literal
construction and Berman--Karpinski--Scott's qualitative reduction remain
candidate sources only after a separate proper localizer and two-level cost
audit.

\section{Direct-spine and hostile controls}

The $2r$ separating clauses are not a post-acquisition lower bound for an
arbitrary decider.  The source predicate ``all clones agree'' is recognized
directly in linear work and logarithmic address space; the SAT control has
models $0^r,1^r$, while opposite pinned endpoints give an immediate UNSAT
control.  A 2-SAT implication cycle is solved by SCC, and the equivalent
affine equations $z_i\oplus z_{i+1}=0$ are solved by parity union--find or
Gaussian elimination.  Caching the recognized relation also bypasses clause
materialization.  Horn, padded 2-SAT, affine and bounded-treewidth
representations therefore refute any promotion from the syntactic clause
floor to mandatory decision work.

The result relativizes and algebrizes: a semantic or algebraic oracle can
answer the equality relation directly.  No Natural-Proofs claim is involved.
No candidate complete-state potential is proposed.  ADAPTIVE-COVER75's
input-read hitting number is not reused after acquisition.
