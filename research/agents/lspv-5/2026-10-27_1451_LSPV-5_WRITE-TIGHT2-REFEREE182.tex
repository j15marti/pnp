\section*{Executive status}
\textbf{PROVED/EXACT (restricted model).}  I independently rederived the two
WRITE-TIGHT2 inequalities in the declared deterministic model: one two-way
\emph{sequential} read-only input head, a fixed finite number $k$ of initially
blank sequential work tapes, fixed finite state and tape alphabets, and no
other memory-bearing head.  The sharper bound
\[
 (D+1)(S+2)^k\log(S+2)=\Omega(N/\log N)
 \tag{1}
\]
and the sparse-change consequence
\[
 D\leq N^{1-\varepsilon},\quad T\leq N^K
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 R=\Omega(N/\log^2N),\quad H_{\rm in},T=\Omega(N^2/\log^2N)
 \tag{2}
\]
are sound for the stated easy exact22/DISJ promise and sound accepting
portfolio.

\textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED transfers.}  Equations (1)--(2) are false after
silently adding either (i) a second sequential input head or (ii) an uncharged
two-way output head whose position is usable.  Both give explicit
$O(N)$ or zero-work-change controls.  Indexed RAM is a third escape.  These are
model controls, not counterexamples to (1)--(2).

\textbf{OPEN.}  No theorem here applies to unrestricted ordinary SAT, FLMV's
random-access model, or arbitrary RAM.  The interior upper frontier for
$0<s<1$ remains open.  Consequently $P$ versus $NP$ and ARC-EDGE-90 remain
\textbf{OPEN}.

\section{Claim card and exact quantifiers}
For every fixed deterministic machine $U$ with fixed $k$, state set and
alphabets, for every sufficiently large module count $m$, let
$\mathcal{Y}_m=\{F_m(a,\bar a):a\in\{0,1\}^m\}$.  Programs $p$ have length at
most $b(N)=O(\log N)$ and are placed in the same read-only prefix.  Programs
may be chosen with perfect input-specific hindsight and may reject or diverge
on other satisfiable formulas.  The only semantic requirement is
\[
 \forall p\ \forall F\in\NO:\quad U(p,F)\ \hbox{does not accept}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Coverage asserts
\[
 \forall a\in\{0,1\}^m\ \exists p, |p|\le b(N):
 U(p,F_m(a,\bar a))\ \hbox{accepts within declared ceilings}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Thus the theorem permits separate programs but derives a common-input defeat
by contraposition when the counting inequality fails.  It does not assume one
program accepts all $\mathcal{Y}_m$.

The resources belong to the same accepting run: $T$ transitions,
$H_{\rm in}$ one-cell input-head moves, $R$ changes of nonzero input direction,
$S$ total distinct visited work cells (blank included), and $D$ total
\emph{actual work-cell symbol changes}; restoring blank counts and a formal
same-symbol write does not.  Soundness (3), rather than unchecked witness
printing, is the polarity needed for splicing.

\section{Falsifiers proposed before source search}
The four predeclared attacks were:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a blank work-head coordinate could carry uncharged information when
  $D=0$;
\item an output-head coordinate could provide hidden memory;
\item one transition could change several tapes, invalidating an epoch count
  based on transition count rather than actual changes;
\item (2) might have been inferred from (1) without a separate movement/cut
  inequality.
\end{enumerate}
Attack 1 is absorbed because every visited blank cell is in $S$ and every work
coordinate is in each crossing label.  Attack 2 succeeds if such a head is
allowed uncharged, so its exclusion is load-bearing.  Attack 3 is repaired by
canonically ordering same-transition changes and counting every changed cell.
Attack 4 fails against the actual proof: Theorem~B below is an independent
cut-selection argument and supplies the missing movement inequality.

\section{Independent proof of the restricted theorem}
\subsection{The event chronicle}
Fix $p$, an input cut $c$ in the invariant middle gap, and an accepting run.
Write an event sequence containing:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{WRITE}(j,x,\sigma)$ for every actual change of work-tape $j$,
  cell $x$, to symbol $\sigma$;
\item $\mathrm{CROSS}(\delta,q,x_1,\ldots,x_k)$ for every crossing of $c$,
  recording direction, control state, and all work-head coordinates.
\end{itemize}
For events in one transition, order WRITE events by tape number and place them
before CROSS.  Because the initial work tapes are blank, replaying all prior
WRITE events reconstructs the complete work contents at every CROSS.  Together
with the recorded control and head coordinates this is the complete boundary
configuration needed for deterministic splicing.

\begin{lemma}[epoch bound]
In an interval containing no actual work-symbol change, a halting run crosses
the fixed input cut at most $C_U(S+2)^k$ times.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Work contents are fixed throughout the interval.  At a crossing, direction and
finite control have $O_U(1)$ possibilities.  If tape $j$ visits $s_j$ cells,
its head has at most $s_j+1$ relevant coordinates, and
$\prod_j(s_j+1)\le (S+2)^k$ up to a $k$-dependent constant.  Repetition of the
same crossing direction, state, and all work-head coordinates repeats the
entire deterministic configuration.  The later computation then loops, in
contradiction with halting.  This argument also covers formal same-symbol
writes: they leave the configuration unchanged.
\end{proof}

There are at most $D+1$ no-change epochs.  Hence the number of CROSS events is
$O_U((D+1)(S+2)^k)$.  Every WRITE or CROSS event takes
$O_U(\log(S+2))$ bits, and the number of WRITE events $D$ is already at most a
constant times $(D+1)(S+2)^k$.  Therefore
\[
 |E_{p,c,a}|\le c_U(D+1)(S+2)^k\log(S+2).
 \tag{5}
\]

For a fixed $p,c$, two different accepted $a,a'$ cannot have equal event
codes.  Equality reconstructs equal complete crossing sequences.  Splicing
the left part of one run to the right part of the other accepts one crossed
formula, while the exact22 construction makes at least one crossed formula
unsatisfiable.  This contradicts (3).  There are fewer than $2^{b+1}$ programs,
so (4), (5), and counting yield
\[
 m\le b+c_U(D+1)(S+2)^k\log(S+2)+O(1).
 \tag{6}
\]
The physical encoding has $N=\Theta(m\log m)$ and $b=O(\log N)$, giving (1)
and, under power-law ceilings, $d+ks\ge 1$.

\subsection{Why simultaneous changes do not break the proof}
On a conventional fixed-$k$ multitape transition, at most one scanned cell per
work tape can change.  If $\widetilde D$ counts transitions containing any
change while $D$ counts changed cells, then
\[
 \widetilde D\le D\le k\widetilde D.
 \tag{7}
\]
Canonical within-transition tape order reconstructs the same post-transition
contents.  Fixed $k$ therefore changes only constants.  A statement that lets
one primitive rewrite an unbounded block is a different RAM/string-operation
model and does not inherit (6).

\subsection{Independent movement inequality}
At a fixed cut, crossing directions alternate, so the number of crossings is
at most $R+1$.  Logging all $D$ changes and all crossings gives
\[
 m\le b+c_U(D+R+2)\log(S+2)+O(1).
 \tag{8}
\]
For every accepted input, among the $G=\Theta(N)$ fixed middle-gap cuts one has
at most $H_{\rm in}/G$ crossings.  Include that cut's index in the code and
count only inputs using the same $(p,c,E)$:
\[
 m\le b+\lceil\log G\rceil
      +c_U(D+H_{\rm in}/G+1)\log(S+2)+O(1).
 \tag{9}
\]
The input-dependent cut is legitimate because its identity is encoded before
collision/splicing.

If $T\le N^K$ for one fixed finite $K$, then
$S\le k(T+1)$ and $\log(S+2)=O(\log N)$.  With
$D\le N^{1-\varepsilon}$, the $D\log(S+2)$ contribution is
$o(N/\log N)$, while $m=\Theta(N/\log N)$.  Equations (8)--(9) imply
$R=\Omega(N/\log^2N)$ and
$H_{\rm in}=\Omega(GN/\log^2N)=\Omega(N^2/\log^2N)$; finally
$T\ge H_{\rm in}$.  This proves (2).  The polynomial-time ceiling is used only
to control $\log S$ and cannot be dropped silently.

\section{Finite reproducible controls}
The Python reproducer exhaustively checked all $87{,}380$ ordered $(a,b)$ pairs
for $1\le m\le8$ with four comparators: legal zero-change unary work-head,
illegal uncharged output-head counter, two-input-head comparator, and legal
full buffer.  It also ran 20,000 seeded simultaneous-change chronicles for up
to six work tapes and 51,005 exponent-grid checks.  In total 1,072,185
assertions passed.  The finite experiment checks implementations and
bookkeeping only; the asymptotic status comes from the proof above.

At $m=8$, the two-input-head control used 86 abstract transitions and no work
cells or changes; the hidden-output-head control used 1,928 transitions, no
declared work cells, and 9 auxiliary head positions; the legal zero-write
endpoint had the same transition count but charged 9 work cells; the buffer
used 87 transitions, 8 cells, and 8 changes.  These are canonical-layout
abstractions, not general 3SAT solvers.

\section{Primary-source and model-transfer audit}
Hennie's one-tape off-line work and Hartmanis's one-tape analysis establish the
crossing-sequence setting for a single tape/head; they do not state (1) for a
fixed-$k$ machine with a read-only input and separate work tapes.  Gajser's
one-tape verification results explicitly separate one-tape and multitape
questions.  Pighizzini also distinguishes accept and weak nondeterministic time
measures.  These papers are background, not substitutes for the exact proof
above:
\begin{itemize}
\item F.~C. Hennie, ``One-tape, off-line Turing machine computations,''
Information and Control 8 (1965), DOI
\href{https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(65)90399-2}{10.1016/S0019-9958(65)90399-2}.
\item J.~Hartmanis, ``Computational complexity of one-tape Turing machine
computations,'' JACM 15 (1968),
\url{https://cse.iitkgp.ac.in/~goutam/toc/readingMaterial/computational-complexity-one-tape-Hart.pdf}.
\item D.~Gajser, ``Verifying Time Complexity of Deterministic Turing Machines,''
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.3648}.
\item G.~Pighizzini, ``Nondeterministic one-tape off-line Turing machines and
their time complexity,'' \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.1271}.
\end{itemize}
Hennie--Stearns' fast \emph{two-tape} simulation is not a one-tape theorem and
supplies no reverse lower-bound transfer.  Likewise the conservative
$T_1=O(T_k(N+S_k+\log(T_k+2)))$ construction is an upper simulation bound, not
a lower bound on $T_k$.
