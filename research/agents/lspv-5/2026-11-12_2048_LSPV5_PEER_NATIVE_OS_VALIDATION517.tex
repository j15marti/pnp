\section{Primary-literature and barrier-transfer audit}
The proof-system scope was checked against Cook--Reckhow's verifier-relative
definition of propositional proof systems: acceptance by a polynomial-time
checker establishes only the language defined by that checker, not identity
with an unstated historical calculus.  See S.~Cook and R.~Reckhow,
``The Relative Efficiency of Propositional Proof Systems,'' \emph{JSL} 44
(1979), \href{https://doi.org/10.2307/2273702}{doi:10.2307/2273702}.

The abstraction obligation was checked against Cousot--Cousot's sound
abstract-interpretation framework and Clarke--Grumberg--Jha--Lu--Veith's
counterexample-guided refinement architecture.  A cheap abstract path must be
replayed concretely before refinement; a concrete accepted path is not
spurious.  See P.~Cousot and R.~Cousot, POPL 1977,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1145/512950.512973}{doi:10.1145/512950.512973},
and E.~Clarke et al., CAV 2000,
\href{https://doi.org/10.1007/10722167_15}{doi:10.1007/10722167\_15}.

Extended-resolution/DRAT literature is a \status{direct warning}, not a
transfer theorem: extension and deletion-capable proof formats can simulate
shortcuts absent from a fixed-resolution abstraction, so they must be admitted
or explicitly excluded by authenticated grammar.  No paper found supplies the
missing $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ source artifact or a model-matched lower bound.
Circuit reference accounting is a useful structural analogy for fanout and
shared ownership, but /576 demonstrates why it cannot be imported without an
explicit byte/time/work tariff.  The knot/link handoff remains
\status{CANDIDATE/OPEN}; NP-hardness of unlink recognition is not a general
machine lower bound and older graph-topology LSPV-2/449 was not relabelled as
knot topology.  No post-quantum technique supplies a model-matched theorem in
this validation run; all such analogies are \status{FAILED TRANSFER} here.
