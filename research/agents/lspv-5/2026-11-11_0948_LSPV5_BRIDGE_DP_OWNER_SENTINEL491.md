## Theorem 491.1: parsed-object dead-owner parametricity

Fix the exact 103-record proof and summary except for its single `gate_defs` tuple. Let

$$
g=[i,z,a,b]
$$

have safe-integer coordinates satisfying

$$
i\ge0,\qquad z>15,\qquad z\ne16,\qquad
1\le |a|,|b|\le15.
$$

Then the unchanged full CLI accepts the packet with `gate_defs=[g]` and paid totals unchanged at one owner plus 62 RES nodes.

**Proof.** The checker validates these exact coordinate predicates and inserts the sole conservative owner into its `defs`/`gids` maps. There are no DEF records, no clause contains the output, and no later owner consumes it, so all 103 proof-record transitions are independent of $i,z,a,b$. Summary checks see exactly one distinct paid owner. In the CLI self-test, deleting the owner changes the declared owner count from one to zero while the copied summary remains one, so the mutation rejects. The other ten bundled mutations are independent of the unused owner and reject exactly as in the baseline. Therefore every such parsed tuple is accepted. This theorem concerns parsed objects; raw JSON lexical aliases remain a separate P2-D1 layer.

## Core/full-CLI cost split and bounded order control

Deleting the only owner and repairing the summary gives:

$$
W_{\mathrm{core}}=62.
$$

The exact extracted original `checkProof` core accepts this packet. The full CLI rejects with `accepted a corrupted proof`, because `gate_defs.shift()` becomes a no-op on the already empty owner list. Therefore the owner's one paid unit is a full-CLI sentinel in this family, not terminal proof-DAG work.

A deterministic xorshift-seeded search tested 4,106 Davis--Putnam elimination orders, including a complete pair-swap neighborhood descent from the best sampled order. Nineteen sampled orders used 62 RES nodes and none used fewer. This is a finite falsifier control only:

$$
\min_{\text{4,106 tested orders}} W_{\mathrm{RES}}=62
$$

does **not** imply the unrestricted optimum is 62, even within ordinary Resolution.

## Concrete/abstract complete configurations and polarity

A complete concrete CLI state must retain

$$
C=(b,o,q,j,K,D,G,A,w,M),
$$

where $b$ is the raw byte packet, $o$ its parsed object, $q$ the phase, $j$ the record/mutation cursor, $K$ known clauses, $D/G$ owner maps, $A$ assertion count, $w$ paid cost and $M$ the current corruption-test copy. The full transition relation includes file read, JSON parse, core replay, all eleven cloned mutations and final output.

For mathematical-core analysis, an abstraction may erase a terminal-dead owner:

$$
\alpha_{\mathrm{core}}(C)=(\text{source},K,\text{RES DAG},w-1,q),
$$

with $\gamma_{\mathrm{core}}$ containing every legal dead-owner representative. Completed core refutation is preserved and abstract cost 62 is no greater than concrete cost 63.

That abstraction is **not** acceptance-preserving for the full CLI. A full-CLI abstraction must retain at least the wrapper predicates

$$
(\operatorname{CoreAccept},\ |G|>0,\ p_{\mathrm{terminal}}\ne999999)
$$

and the declared paid-owner count. Otherwise owner-free and one-owner packets collapse despite opposite full-CLI outcomes. CEGAR may refine away the owner-free $W=62$ path only for the full CLI language. It may not call any of the six accepted owner variants spurious.

This is a model-boundary result, not a permission to replace the fixed calculus. Missing AND3, GF(2), LOCAL-CONVERT, exported/private wires, branching, weakening, contexts/discharge, joins and deletions remain conservative unknown operations for an all-rule MAY abstraction.
