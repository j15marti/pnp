\section{Definitions}
A \emph{proof system} $R$ is a poly-time verifier $V_R(F,\pi)$ with $\exists\pi\,V_R(F,\pi)\iff F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}$ (Cook--Reckhow). Multisign DAG hypergraph: vertices = clause/derived-node
objects, hyperedges = inference steps, signs on incidences = literal polarity; this is a DAG-like proof object, so $\mathrm{cost}_R$ (size, or size$\times$space) is a verifier-checkable quantity and the setting is a Cook--Reckhow system. Define
$\overline W_R(N)=\max_{|F|\le N,F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}}\min_\pi \mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$. Free choice of $\pi$ is exactly the nondeterminism of $\exists\pi$; charging nothing for search is therefore \emph{built in}, not an extra concession.

\section{Results (all PROVED/EXACT, elementary)}
\textbf{Prop.\ 1 (Cook--Reckhow, KNOWN THEOREM).} $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$ iff some $R$ has $\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$.
\textbf{Corollary.} A superpolynomial bound for \emph{every} sound system would prove $\mathrm{NP}\ne\mathrm{coNP}$, hence $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$. A bound for one fixed $R$ proves nothing about P vs NP without a bridge.

\textbf{Prop.\ 2 (the bridge is vacuous-or-circular).}
(a) If $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$, then ``$\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\Rightarrow\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$'' holds trivially for every $R$. So proving the bridge as a bare implication is at least as hard as proving $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$ \emph{or} establishing a genuine simulation; only a \emph{constructive/uniform} bridge is informative.
(b) Take $R_{\mathcal M}$: derivations are accepting transcripts of poly-time machine $\mathcal M$. If $\mathcal M$ decides SAT then $\overline W_{R_{\mathcal M}}\le\mathrm{poly}$, but $R_{\mathcal M}$ is sound only if $\mathcal M$ is correct, and a superpoly lower bound for it is equivalent to ``no correct $\mathcal M$ of that time'' --- i.e.\ the lower bound \emph{is} P$\ne$NP-type. Circular.
(c) For a fixed natural $R$ (resolution, Frege, EF): the bridge for EF follows if the correctness of the polynomial algorithm is provable in the theory corresponding to EF (PV/$S^1_2$ $\leftrightarrow$ EF). Hence a nonconstructive $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P$ with no feasible proof of correctness gives no EF proofs.
\textit{Proof sketch of (c)} (KNOWN, Cook 1975; Krajíček--Pudlák): PV-provable $\forall x\,(\mathrm{Sat}\text{-claim}(x)\to\ldots)$ translates to poly-size EF proofs of the propositional correctness statements for each length; instantiating at a UNSAT $F$ gives a poly EF refutation.

\textbf{Consequence (CONDITIONAL).} The weakest sufficient bridge is therefore: \emph{(B*) for every poly-time $\mathcal M$ that decides SAT, EF (or chosen $R$) has poly-size proofs of $\mathcal M$'s correctness on length-$n$ inputs.} (B*) is a statement about \emph{provability of correctness}, not about SAT$\in$P alone; it is not implied by SAT$\in$P in any known way. Combined with an EF lower bound it would yield P$\ne$NP: this is the Pich--Santhanam program.

\section{Hard-family audit (what is known)}
Superpolynomial lower bounds are known for Resolution (Haken: PHP; Urquhart/Chv\'atal--Szemer\'edi: random/expander), bounded-depth Frege (Ajtai, Beame et al.), cutting planes (Pudl\'ak), polynomial calculus (degree/size, Razborov). \emph{None} survive ``circuit compression + shared DAGs + algebraic reasoning'' simultaneously: PHP has poly-size EF/Frege proofs (Buss), Tseitin has poly Frege proofs over bounded-degree graphs with log-depth/EF, so any hard family for the combined system must defeat EF. No such family is known; this is the open core (Frege/EF lower bounds). Our combined system is at least as strong as Frege-like DAG reasoning, so the worst-case target is as hard as EF lower bounds, strictly harder than the P vs NP gap it was meant to bypass.
