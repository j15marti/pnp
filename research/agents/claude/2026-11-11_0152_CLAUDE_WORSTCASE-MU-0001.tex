\section{Target}
The shared target is
\[
\overline W_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},\,|\langle F\rangle|\le N}\;
\min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi),
\]
with the best derivation chosen for free and the discovery of that derivation
not charged. This run studies the outer $\max$: which sources can be worst cases at all.
This is a filter on candidate hard instances. It is not a lower bound.

\section{Main obstacles and literature preflight}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Obstacle 1 (B1 for a mixed Resolution+GF(2) calculus).} $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$
contains Resolution and GF(2) linear reasoning. If it p-simulates DAG-like
Res($\oplus$), then B1 would imply a superpolynomial Res($\oplus$) lower bound,
and that is a recognised open frontier. Current literature: Efremenko--Garl\'ik--Itsykson (STOC 2024) prove
superpolynomial bounds only for \emph{regular} Res($\oplus$). Bhattacharya--Chattopadhyay
(arXiv:2507.23008) and Shekhovtsov (ECCC TR26-018) prove bounds only for
\emph{depth-restricted} Res($\oplus$). General DAG-like Res($\oplus$) remains open
(ECCC TR25-116, TR26-018; ITCS 2026 LIPIcs vol.~362 paper 81).
\emph{Classification:} DIRECTLY TRANSFERABLE OBSTRUCTION. Any argument for B1 that
works for a calculus containing unrestricted Res($\oplus$) would settle that open
problem, so B1 should be targeted at the provably weaker historical fragment.
The corpus already records restricted Res(XOR) transfers ([P2-F3], [P2-F9], LSPV-1/481).
\item \emph{Obstacle 2 (choosing the worst source).} Hard-instance builders
propose families without a structural filter. Literature: Fleischner--Kullmann--Szeider
(TCS 2002) and Kullmann (2000) show that minimally unsatisfiable (MU) formulas of
fixed deficiency $k=m-n$ have short Resolution refutations that can be found in polynomial time.
Szeider (ECCC TR03-002, 2003) gives an FPT algorithm, parameterised by maximum
deficiency, whose UNSAT certificate is a regular Resolution refutation.
\emph{Classification:} DIRECTLY TRANSFERABLE theorem for every $R$ that
polynomially simulates Resolution with $O(1)$ paid nodes per resolution step.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Results}
\textbf{(PM) Premise monotonicity.} If $F\subseteq F'$ as clause sets over the same
variable namespace, then every $R$-refutation of $F$ that cites only source clauses of $F$
is also an $R$-refutation of $F'$, with the same paid cost.
For an encoding that charges source authentication, the cost may instead rise by an additive $O(|\langle F'\rangle|)$.
Resolution and the declared source-authenticated SOURCE/AND3/XOR completions satisfy PM, because source
premises are checked by membership only. Encodings are \emph{subset-monotone}: deleting clauses never increases $|\langle F\rangle|$.

\begin{lemma}[MU reduction; PROVED/EXACT under PM]
For every $N$,
$\overline W_R(N)=\max\{W_R(F_0): F_0 \text{ minimally unsatisfiable},\ |\langle F_0\rangle|\le N\}$.
Under the additive-charge variant of PM the two sides differ by at most $O(N)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every UNSAT $F$ contains an MU subformula $F_0$. By PM, $W_R(F)\le W_R(F_0)$ (plus $O(N)$ in the additive-charge variant).
By subset-monotonicity, $|\langle F_0\rangle|\le|\langle F\rangle|\le N$. So the maximum over UNSAT sources is at most the maximum over MU sources.
The reverse inequality holds because MU formulas are themselves UNSAT.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[CONDITIONAL on the cited certificate theorem]
Assume $R$ simulates Resolution with $O(1)$ paid nodes per step. Assume also, following Szeider 2003, that
UNSAT formulas of maximum deficiency $\delta^*(F)=\max_{F'\subseteq F}(|F'|-|\mathrm{var}(F')|)$
have Resolution refutations of size $f(\delta^*)\cdot|F|^{c}$ for a computable $f$ and an absolute $c$.
Then every family $F_h$ with $W_R(F_h)$ superpolynomial in $|\langle F_h\rangle|$ satisfies
$f(\delta^*(F_h))\ge |\langle F_h\rangle|^{\omega(1)}$.
In particular $\delta^*(F_h)\to\infty$, and the deficiency of the MU cores that witness the worst case must also be unbounded.
\end{corollary}

\noindent\emph{Consequences for the five roles.}
(i) The Hypergraph/Certificates role should certify MU cores, or at least their deficiencies, for proposed hard families.
Low-deficiency gadgets are refuted in polynomial size by Resolution alone.
(ii) The standard anchors pass the filter: odd-charge cubic Tseitin ($m=4h$, $n=3h/2$, deficiency $5h/2$)
and random 3-CNF at density $\Delta>1$ (deficiency $\ge(\Delta-1)n$).
The filter is necessary, not sufficient: Tseitin collapses under GF(2) reasoning.
(iii) The Red Team role may restrict counterexample search to MU sources without loss of generality.
(iv) None of this touches B3.
