\section{Why this matters}
The five-role directive asks for a superpolynomial $\overline W_R$ for a \emph{fixed combined} calculus in which the optimal derivation is free. LSPV-2/341 fixed $R_{\rm mix341}$ with a unit-cost conservative two-input DEF rule. A rule of that kind is the Tseitin extension rule, so the lower-bound target for this $R$ may be far stronger than the exact linear optima computed so far suggest.

\section{Extended Resolution inside $R_{\rm mix341}$}
Let ER be Resolution plus the extension rule: for fresh $z$ and literals $a,b$ add the clauses $(\neg z\vee a),(\neg z\vee b),(z\vee\neg a\vee\neg b)$. Let $S_{ER}(F)$ be the minimum number of resolvents plus extension steps in an ER refutation of $F$ (source clauses free).
\begin{theorem}\label{sim}
For every UNSAT CNF $F$, $W_{R_{\rm mix341}}(F)\le 4\,S_{ER}(F)$. Hence $\overline W_{R_{\rm mix341}}(N)\le 4\,\overline S_{ER}(N)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take a minimum ER refutation. Translate each resolution step into a RES node (cost 1). Translate each extension step into one DEF node introducing $z=a\wedge b$ (the two-input gate; the case $z=\neg(\cdot)$ and $z=a\vee b$ are the same gate up to literal polarity), followed by three LOCAL-CONVERT nodes, one extracting each defining clause by a constant-support truth-table check (cost 3). Total cost $\le 4$ per ER step. Freshness and conservativeness of $z$ are inherited from ER. Shared derived clauses are charged once, as in ER. \qedhere
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[KNOWN THEOREM inputs: ER $\equiv_p$ EF, Cook--Reckhow 1979, Tseitin 1968, Kullmann 1999]\label{ef}
$R_{\rm mix341}$ p-simulates EF. Consequently a superpolynomial lower bound $\overline W_{R_{\rm mix341}}$ would imply a superpolynomial lower bound for Extended Frege, a well-known open problem for which, to my knowledge, no technique is currently available (the best known unrestricted Frege lower bounds are only polynomial of low degree; not re-verified this run).
\end{corollary}
\noindent The exact linear optima of LSPV-2/341 ($4h-1$ for odd cubic Tseitin) and LSPV-3/342 are compatible with this: they concern families that are easy for XOR/AND3 \emph{and} for ER.

\paragraph{Reverse direction (CANDIDATE).} Each $R_{\rm mix341}$ rule is sound and locally checkable; RES, AND3 and LOCAL-CONVERT are constant-support, and XOR on unbounded-width rows is simulable in Frege (hence in EF) with polynomial overhead via polynomial-size formulas for parity. So $R_{\rm mix341}\equiv_p$ EF is expected, but the width-unbounded XOR simulation was not written out here.

\section{Circularity and trade-off structure of B3}
Write $LB(R)$: $\overline W_R$ superpolynomial. Write $B3(R)$: SAT$\in$P $\Rightarrow$ $\overline W_R$ polynomial. Write $R\le_p R'$ if $R'$ p-simulates $R$.
\begin{lemma}\label{mono}
(a) If $R\le_p R'$ then $B3(R)\Rightarrow B3(R')$ and $LB(R')\Rightarrow LB(R)$.
(b) If $LB(R)$ is a theorem, then $B3(R)\iff$ SAT$\notin$P (classically; the conclusion is false, so the implication is equivalent to the negation of its hypothesis).
(c) If $LB(R)$ is proved, $LB(R)\wedge B3(R)$ is equivalent to $LB(R)\wedge$ SAT$\notin$P; $B3$ adds no independent content beyond the target.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) $W_{R'}\le \mathrm{poly}(W_R)$ pointwise by simulation. (b) Classical logic: if $\overline W_R$ is superpolynomial, $B3(R)$ reads ``SAT$\in$P $\Rightarrow$ false''. (c) from (b). \qedhere
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Tier structure]\label{tier}
\emph{Tier 1}: systems with an unconditional superpolynomial lower bound (Resolution, bounded-depth Frege over AC$^0$, Cutting Planes, restricted Res($\oplus$) fragments). For these, B3 \emph{is} the claim P$\neq$NP; a proof of B3 here cannot be a ``weaker sufficient bridge''. \emph{Tier 2}: systems with open lower bound (dag-like Res($\oplus$), AC$^0[p]$-Frege, Frege, EF, $R_{\rm mix341}$). Only here is $LB\wedge B3$ non-circular. Moving a candidate upward makes B3 easier (Lemma~\ref{mono}a) and $LB$ harder; moving downward does the reverse. The two obligations cannot both be eased at once.
\end{corollary}
\begin{corollary}[Optimality is the only known route to B3 at the top]\label{opt}
For $R\equiv_p$ EF: $B3(R)$ follows from p-optimality of EF (LSPV-1/336), and EF polynomially bounded would give NP$=$coNP. Whether SAT$\in$P alone implies EF polynomially bounded is not known to me and not claimed; it would require a feasible (PV/$S^1_2$-style) correctness proof of the polynomial SAT algorithm, which Levin-type universal search does not supply on the UNSAT side.
\end{corollary}
