\section{Objective and exact quantifiers}
For a fixed Cook--Reckhow refutation system $R$ (sound, complete, verifier
deterministic polynomial time in $|F|+|\pi|$), with $\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi)$
at least the encoded length $|\pi|$,
\[
 w_R(F)=\min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi),\qquad
 \Wbar_R(N)=\max_{F\in\UNSAT,\ |\langle F\rangle|\le N} w_R(F).
\]
Minimizing over all refutations is a legitimate \emph{lower-bound} quantity: it
charges no search for the best derivation, so any lower bound on $w_R$ is a lower
bound on \emph{every} procedure whose execution trace is (or cheaply converts to)
an $R$-refutation. The whole difficulty is the last clause. The weakest sufficient
bridge of the directive is
\[
 \mathrm{B}(R):\qquad \SAT\in\mathsf P \Longrightarrow \Wbar_R(N)\le\poly(N).
\]
With $\neg$(\,$\Wbar_R\le\poly$\,), $\mathrm B(R)$ yields $\mathsf P\neq\mathsf{NP}$.

\section{The reflection system $R_T$}
\begin{definition}\label{def:RT}
Fix an r.e.\ theory $T$ (e.g.\ PV, $S^1_2$, PA, ZFC) whose proofs are checkable
in polynomial time and which is $\Pi^0_1$-sound. For a deterministic machine $M$,
let $\mathrm{Snd}(M)$ be the $\Pi^0_1$ sentence
\[
 \forall G\,\forall t\,\forall \alpha:\ \bigl[M(G)\text{ halts within $t$ steps with
 output UNSAT}\bigr]\rightarrow \alpha\not\models G .
\]
An $R_T$-refutation of a CNF $F$ is either (i) a resolution refutation of $F$, or
(ii) a tuple $(\ulcorner M\urcorner,\ p,\ 1^t)$ where $p$ is a $T$-proof of
$\mathrm{Snd}(M)$ and $M(F)$ outputs UNSAT within $t$ steps. Cost $=$ encoded
length.
\end{definition}
\noindent
$M$ need not be correct on satisfiable inputs, need not be polynomial-time, and
no time bound appears in $\mathrm{Snd}(M)$; only one-sided (UNSAT-answer)
soundness is required.

\begin{theorem}[$R_T$ is a Cook--Reckhow system]\label{thm:proofsystem}
$R_T$ is sound, complete, and verifiable in time polynomial in $|F|+|\pi|$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Completeness: clause (i). Verification: check resolution, or check $p$ (polynomial
by hypothesis on $T$) and run $M$ on $F$ for at most $t$ steps (a fixed universal
simulator has polynomial overhead in $|\ulcorner M\urcorner|+t+|F|$). Soundness:
if (ii) is accepted, $T\vdash\mathrm{Snd}(M)$; $\Pi^0_1$-soundness of $T$ makes
$\mathrm{Snd}(M)$ true, and the clocked run witnesses its antecedent for $F$, so
$F$ is unsatisfiable.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pointwise transfer to provably sound algorithms]\label{thm:pointwise}
For every machine $M$ with $T\vdash\mathrm{Snd}(M)$ there is a constant $K_M$
(the length of $\ulcorner M\urcorner$ and one fixed proof $p_M$) such that for
every UNSAT $F$ on which $M$ outputs UNSAT in $t_M(F)$ steps,
\[
 w_{R_T}(F)\ \le\ K_M + O\bigl(t_M(F)+\log|F|\bigr).
\]
Equivalently, $t_M(F)\ge \Omega\bigl(w_{R_T}(F)\bigr)-K_M$: a lower bound on minimum
$R_T$-derivation cost is, instance by instance, a running-time lower bound for every
$T$-provably-sound algorithm, with free choice of the optimal derivation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The tuple $(\ulcorner M\urcorner,p_M,1^{t_M(F)})$ is an accepted refutation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Provable bridge]\label{cor:bridge}
If some polynomial-time decider $A$ for $\SAT$ satisfies $T\vdash\mathrm{Snd}(A)$,
then $\Wbar_{R_T}(N)\le\poly(N)$. Hence
\[
 \mathrm{B}^{\vdash}(T):\quad \SAT\in\mathsf P\Longrightarrow
 \exists A\ \bigl(A\text{ poly-time SAT decider}\wedge T\vdash\mathrm{Snd}(A)\bigr)
\]
implies $\mathrm B(R_T)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[What an $R_T$ lower bound proves unconditionally]\label{thm:lb-consequence}
If $\Wbar_{R_T}(N)$ is not polynomially bounded, then no polynomial-time SAT decider
has $T$-provable UNSAT-soundness. Moreover, for every Cook--Reckhow system $Q$ with
$T\vdash\mathrm{Snd}(\mathrm{Ver}_Q\text{-search})$ (in particular resolution,
cutting planes, Frege and Extended Frege when $T\supseteq\mathrm{PV}$), $R_T$
p-simulates $Q$, so the lower bound for $R_T$ implies one for $Q$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First claim: contrapositive of Corollary~\ref{cor:bridge}. Second: take $M_Q$ that
reads a $Q$-proof appended in a fixed field of $1^t$ padding and runs $\mathrm{Ver}_Q$;
the standard formalization of $Q$'s soundness gives $T\vdash\mathrm{Snd}(M_Q)$ and
Theorem~\ref{thm:pointwise} gives $w_{R_T}(F)\le K_Q+\poly(|F|+|\pi_Q|)$. (Encoding
detail: the $Q$-proof is carried inside the tuple; $M_Q$ is given it as auxiliary
input, and $\mathrm{Snd}$ is stated for the auxiliary-input version.)
\end{proof}

\section{The single remaining arrow and why it is hard}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:bridge}, the directive's bridge for the fixed system $R_T$ is
reduced to the \textbf{provable-soundness gap} $\mathrm{B}^{\vdash}(T)$ (\textbf{OPEN}):
if a polynomial-time SAT algorithm exists, does \emph{some} such algorithm have a
$T$-proof of its UNSAT-soundness? Observations (all \textbf{PROVED/EXACT}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{SAT answers are free, UNSAT answers are not.} Self-reducibility lets any
 decider be made provably correct on its SAT answers (search and check the
 assignment). Nothing analogous certifies UNSAT answers; $\mathrm{Snd}$ is exactly
 the unverifiable half. This is the precise location of NP versus coNP inside the
 bridge.
\item \emph{No collapse by universal search.} Levin-style dovetailing over $R_T$
 proofs is $T$-provably sound only if $T$ proves $\mathrm{Snd}$ of $R_T$'s own
 verifier, i.e.\ local reflection for $T$; by G\"odel's second theorem one must
 pass to $T+\mathrm{Rfn}(T)$, giving an $\omega$-chain $R_{T_0}\preceq R_{T_1}
 \preceq\cdots$ with no fixed top. A bridge for a fixed $R$ must therefore commit to
 a fixed $T$ before seeing the hypothetical algorithm.
\item \emph{Lower bounds already in hand make the bridge equivalent to P$\neq$NP}
 (corpus result, codex 2026-11-11, eq.~(12); reconfirmed here): for resolution
 (Haken), cutting planes (Pudl\'ak), bounded-depth Frege, $\mathrm B(R)$ is literally
 equivalent to $\mathsf P\neq\mathsf{NP}$. For $R_T$ with $T\supseteq\mathrm{PV}$ no
 superpolynomial lower bound is known, since it would imply one for Extended Frege
 (Theorem~\ref{thm:lb-consequence}).
\item \emph{Known calibration.} Cook (1975): if PV proves that a polynomial-time
 algorithm decides SAT, then EF is polynomially bounded; this is
 Corollary~\ref{cor:bridge} for $T=\mathrm{PV}$ with EF in place of $R_T$.
 Atserias--M\"uller: resolution is not automatable unless P$=$NP; P$=$NP makes every
 system automatable, which is the \emph{search} side, not the size side, of
 $\mathrm B(R)$.
\end{enumerate}
So the decomposition ``easy bridge $+$ hard lower bound'' is unavailable in both
directions: for weak $R$ the bridge is the whole problem; for strong $R=R_T$ the
bridge is reduced to a metamathematical provability statement and the lower bound
is at least as hard as Extended Frege lower bounds.

\section{Hypergraph/DAG reading of the user's program}
The multisign DAG hypergraph of a 3-CNF with minimal reduced logical state $\bot$
and minimal work $w_R(F)$ is exactly $w_R$ for the rule set $R$ that the hypergraph
reductions implement. Theorem~\ref{thm:pointwise} says: \emph{choose the rule set to
contain reflection}, and then minimal work bounds the running time of every
provably sound algorithm pointwise; but choosing such a rule set makes hard
families collapse (next section), so the lower-bound side gets harder exactly as
the transfer side gets easier.
