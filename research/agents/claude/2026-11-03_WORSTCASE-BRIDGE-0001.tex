\noindent\textbf{Status.} P versus NP remains OPEN. Additive report on the worst-case derivation-cost directive
$\overline W_R(N)=\max_{F\in\UNSAT,|F|\le N}\min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$.
No lower bound for any strong $R$ is proved here; this report classifies which choices of $R$ make the
program non-vacuous and what the proposed bridge is equivalent to.

\section{Free strategy choice collapses unless $R$ is Cook--Reckhow}
\begin{proposition}[Hardcoding collapse]\status{PROVED/EXACT}
Let $R$ be any ``system'' whose derivations of $\bot$ from $F$ are pairs $(M,\text{run})$ with $M$ a Turing machine
(or circuit family index) and cost $=$ running time of $M$ on $F$, with correctness of $M$ on \emph{other} inputs
required or not. Then $\overline W_R(N)=O(N)$.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Proof.} Fix $F$. Let $M_F$ hardcode the answer ``unsat'' on $F$ and run brute force otherwise; $M_F$ is a correct
decider, runs in time $O(|F|)$ on $F$. Minimum over $M$ is $\le O(|F|)$. \qed

Since the directive grants the optimal sequence for free, the minimum ranges over exactly such per-instance
specialisations. Hence a nontrivial $\overline W_R$ requires that every derivation be \emph{locally checkable
without trusting a global property of a program}, i.e.\ $R$ is a Cook--Reckhow system: derivations checkable in time
$\mathrm{poly}(|\pi|)$, sound, complete.

\begin{proposition}[Cook--Reckhow equivalence]\status{KNOWN THEOREM}
For Cook--Reckhow $R$ with $\mathrm{cost}=|\pi|$: $\overline W_R$ is polynomially bounded iff $R$ is polynomially
bounded on $\UNSAT$; if some Cook--Reckhow $R$ is, then $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$. Conversely
$\overline W_R$ superpolynomial for \emph{every} Cook--Reckhow $R$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{NP}\ne\mathrm{coNP}$,
which is strictly stronger-or-equal to the target direction ($\mathrm{NP}\neq\mathrm{coNP}\Rightarrow \mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$).
\end{proposition}
So the program, taken over all $R$, is the NP vs coNP problem; for one fixed $R$ it is a (hard, but weaker) statement.

\section{The weakest bridge is equivalent to the target for every $R$ with a known lower bound}
Let $\mathrm{Bridge}_R:\ \SAT\in\mathrm P\Rightarrow\overline W_R(N)\le\mathrm{poly}(N)$.
\begin{proposition}\status{PROVED/EXACT}
(i) If $R$ is Cook--Reckhow and $\overline W_R$ is known superpolynomial (resolution, cutting planes, bounded-depth
Frege, polynomial calculus: Haken, Pudl\'ak, Ajtai/Kraj\'i\v cek--Pudl\'ak--Woods/Pitassi--Beame--Impagliazzo, Razborov--Smolensky)
then $\mathrm{Bridge}_R\iff \SAT\notin\mathrm P$, i.e.\ $\mathrm{P}\ne\mathrm{NP}$. Proving the bridge there \emph{is} proving the theorem.\\
(ii) For $R$ with unknown status (Frege, Extended Frege) $\mathrm{Bridge}_R$ is not known to follow from $\SAT\in\mathrm P$:
a decider $M$ yields a polynomial refutation only if $R$ proves, with polynomial-size uniform proofs, the soundness statement
``$M(F)$ rejects $\Rightarrow F$ unsat'' for the unknown $M$. No such uniform reflection is known for EF.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Proof of (i).} $\mathrm{Bridge}_R$ has a false conclusion, so it is equivalent to its premise's negation. \qed

\begin{proposition}[No fixed system absorbs all deciders]\status{PROVED/EXACT}
Let $R_\infty$ add, for every machine $M$ with $\mathrm{Sound}(M)$ true, the axiom $\mathrm{Sound}(M)$, with cost
$|M|+\mathrm{time}_M(F)$. Then membership of axioms is not decidable (``$M$ is a correct SAT decider'' is $\Pi_2$-hard, indeed
undecidable), so $R_\infty$ is not Cook--Reckhow; admitting unverified axioms re-enters the hardcoding collapse of Prop.~1.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Sketch.} Correctness of $M$ on all CNFs encodes totality plus agreement with $\SAT$; totality alone is $\Pi_2$-complete. Admitting
a decidable subset (e.g.\ clocked machines with a bounded-test filter) lets a machine that errs only beyond the tested range pass.\qed

\paragraph{Consequence for the five roles (CANDIDATE guidance, not a claim).}
(1) Lower-Bound Lead: target an $R$ with \emph{unknown} status and aim at the structural theorem ``$R$ polynomially bounded
$\Rightarrow$ [circuit/NP-coNP consequence]'' rather than a bridge from $\SAT\in\mathrm P$; the bridge is only meaningful for such $R$ and then
requires uniform reflection (Part 1.(ii)). (2) Reduction Builder: any ``combined inference system'' must be demonstrably
polynomially verifiable. (3) Certificates: avoid resolution/PC/CP-hard families as evidence for the bridge, since by 2.(i) they are
unconditionally consistent with it only if $\SAT\notin\mathrm P$. (4) Resource Auditor: charge verification time of each rule application.
(5) Red Team: first test is hardcoding collapse (Prop.~1) on any definition where cost counts running time of an unverified object.
Hypergraph-DAG ``minimal reduced state'' definitions must be checked against Prop.~1: if state-reduction cost is per-instance and rules
are not locally checkable, the minimum is $O(N)$.
