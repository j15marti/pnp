\section{Setup}
Fix a proof/derivation system $R$ with polynomial-time checkable derivations (a Cook--Reckhow system, sound and complete for UNSAT CNF). Let $\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$ be derivation size (DAG nodes/steps, charged by bit-length) and
\[\Wb_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},|F|\le N}\ \min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi).\]
Define the bridge $B(R):\ \mathrm{SAT}\in P\Rightarrow \Wb_R(N)\le\mathrm{poly}(N)$.

\begin{proposition}[free choice of the optimal derivation]\status{PROVED/EXACT}
$\Wb_R$ is a purely combinatorial quantity; the cost of \emph{finding} the minimizing $\pi$ is irrelevant to it. A lower bound on $\Wb_R$ is a lower bound for any procedure that outputs an $R$-derivation, however found. (Immediate from the definition.)
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Cook--Reckhow]\status{KNOWN THEOREM}
$\exists R$ with $\Wb_R\le\mathrm{poly}$ iff $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$. Hence ``$\Wb_R$ superpolynomial for \emph{every} $R$'' is equivalent to $\mathrm{NP}\neq\mathrm{coNP}$, which implies $\mathrm{P}\ne\mathrm{NP}$ but is formally stronger. A fixed $R$ with a superpolynomial bound proves nothing about NP vs.\ coNP unless $B(R)$ is established.
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[no free lunch for the bridge]\status{PROVED/EXACT}
If $\Wb_R$ is \emph{unconditionally} known superpolynomial (e.g.\ $R$ = resolution, Haken; cutting planes, Pudl\'ak; bounded-depth Frege, Ajtai/Krajicek--Pudlak--Woods; Polynomial Calculus on suitable families), then $B(R)\iff \mathrm{P}\ne\mathrm{NP}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$B(R)$ is $\neg\mathrm{SAT}\!\in\!P\ \vee\ (\Wb_R\le\mathrm{poly})$; the second disjunct is false by hypothesis, so $B(R)\iff \mathrm{SAT}\notin P$. $\square$
\end{proof}
\noindent\emph{Consequence.} For every system whose lower bound is already known, the ``weakest sufficient bridge'' \emph{is} P$\ne$NP; any proof of it must use $\mathrm{SAT}\in P$ in an essential way. The bridge is informative only for $R$ whose $\Wb_R$ is still open (Frege, Extended Frege $\mathrm{EF}$, and stronger).

\begin{lemma}[two-sided witnessing axioms trivialize the bridge]\status{PROVED/EXACT}
Let $A$ be a candidate poly-time SAT algorithm and $R_A=R+\{\,A(F)=0\Rightarrow\neg F\,\}$ for all $F$. If $A$ is correct, $\Wb_{R_A}=O(\mathrm{time}_A)$; so a lower bound for $R_A$ ``for all $A$'' is literally the statement that no correct $A$ exists. The soundness axiom must be \emph{derived}, not assumed, for the cost to mean anything.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[provable-correctness case]\status{KNOWN THEOREM (Cook; Krajicek--Pudlak), stated from memory}
If some poly-time $A$ decides SAT and $\mathrm{PV}\vdash$ ``$A$ is sound on UNSAT'', then $\mathrm{EF}$ p-simulates the system $Q_A$ (whose proofs are empty and verifier runs $A$), so $\Wb_{\mathrm{EF}}\le\mathrm{poly}$.
\end{theorem}
\noindent Contrapositive: an $\mathrm{EF}$ superpolynomial lower bound rules out \emph{PV-provably correct} polynomial SAT algorithms, but not P$=$NP via an algorithm whose correctness is unprovable. Levin's universal algorithm does not help: it certifies only YES answers.

\begin{proposition}[the exact missing arrow]\status{PROVED/EXACT reformulation}
For $R=\mathrm{EF}$: $B(\mathrm{EF})$ holds iff P$=$NP implies EF has poly-size refutations; it is implied by ``P$=$NP$\Rightarrow$ some correct algorithm has EF-provable soundness''. Neither is known (and unprovability results of Pich--Santhanam type suggest feasible provability of lower bounds is itself restricted). STATUS: \status{OPEN}.
\end{proposition}

\section{Test of candidate hard families (directive item 4)}
Standard literature facts (recalled, \status{CANDIDATE} until re-verified against sources): PHP is poly for EF and CP, exponential for resolution; Tseitin on expanders is exponential for resolution and for PC over fields of characteristic $\neq2$ but \emph{easy over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$} (Gaussian elimination) and easy for EF; random 3-CNF at constant density is exponentially hard for resolution and PC, open for Frege/EF. Hence any family claimed to ``survive alternative reasoning'' must beat EF, i.e.\ is itself a Frege/EF lower bound. No such family is known. Shared DAGs/circuit compression correspond to moving from Frege to EF, which already absorbs the families above.

\section{Literature clues (this run)}
\begin{itemize}
\item Cook--Reckhow equivalence and Krajicek--Pudlak: $\mathrm{NE}=\mathrm{coNE}\Rightarrow$ optimal proof systems exist; $E=NE\Rightarrow$ p-optimal; nonexistence implies separations. Existence of optimal systems is open; recent oracle work: \url{https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02294v1}, \url{https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.07408}, \url{https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.14702}.
\item Pich--Santhanam, \emph{Towards P$\ne$NP from Extended Frege lower bounds}: a super-polynomial lower bound for EF augmented with witnessing axioms (when those are tautologies) yields superpolynomial circuits for SAT; the natural-proofs-style analogue. \url{https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2312.08163}. This is the closest published form of our desired bridge, in the non-uniform setting. Follow-up (Arteche--Khaniki--Pich--Santhanam, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02232}) notes that formalizing such a connection would imply $\mathrm{NEXP}\not\subseteq P/\mathrm{poly}$, a warning about the strength required.
\item Pich--Santhanam, STOC 2021: PV cannot prove strong co-nondeterministic lower bounds for NP; relevant to proving the bridge inside feasible arithmetic.
\end{itemize}

\section{Weakest sufficient transfer theorem (current best statement)}
Not proved. The right target, in light of the above, is a uniform theorem $T(R)$ of the form: \emph{for $R\supseteq\mathrm{EF}$ augmented with one-sided witnessing axioms $\mathrm{Wit}$, every algorithm $A$ running in time $N^c$ yields $\mathrm{poly}(N)$-size $R+\mathrm{Wit}$ refutations of all UNSAT $F$ for which $A$'s correctness holds on the relevant slice}, together with a lower bound for $R+\mathrm{Wit}$. The obstruction is Lemma~2 (two-sided axioms are circular) and the one-sided axioms, which constrain only satisfiable inputs and cannot by themselves refute a fixed $F$. The missing theorem: a one-sided-axiom system whose refutations of a fixed UNSAT $F$ are short whenever SAT is in P.
