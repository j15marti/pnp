\section{Target and conventions (unchanged from the 2026-11-02 directive)}
Fix one sound, complete, effectively specified refutation system $R$ with
nonnegative node weights. For an UNSAT CNF $F$ let
$W_R(F)=\min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi)$, and let
\[
  \Wb_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},\ |\langle F\rangle|\le N} W_R(F),
\]
with $N$ the encoded \emph{bit} length. By definition $\Wb_R$ is
nondecreasing in $N$. The inner minimum grants free choice of the best
derivation. For a fixed uniform total correct decider $A$ let
$T_A^{wc}(N)=\max_{|x|\le N}T_A(x)$. The bridges are:
\begin{itemize}
\item[B1] $\forall A\ \exists p_A\ \forall F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}:\ W_R(F)\le p_A(|F|+T_A(F))$;
\item[B2] $\forall A\ \exists p_A\ \forall N:\ \Wb_R(N)\le p_A(N+T_A^{wc}(N))$;
\item[B3] $\mathrm{SAT}\in P\Rightarrow \exists C,k\ \forall N:\ \Wb_R(N)\le C(N+1)^k$.
\end{itemize}
B1$\Rightarrow$B2$\Rightarrow$B3 is established in the corpus. The directive
calls B3 the \emph{weakest sufficient} bridge. This report measures what that
weakness actually buys.

\section{Results}

\begin{theorem}[collapse where B3 has content]\label{thm:collapse}
Assume $\mathrm{SAT}\in P$. Then for every fixed $R$:
\[
 \mathrm{B1}(R)\iff\mathrm{B2}(R)\iff\mathrm{B3}(R)\iff \Wb_R(N)=(N+1)^{O(1)}.
\]
Unconditionally, $\Wb_R(N)=(N+1)^{O(1)}$ implies B1$(R)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The forward chain is known. Under $\mathrm{SAT}\in P$ the hypothesis of B3
holds, so B3$(R)$ is equivalent to its consequent. Now suppose
$\Wb_R(N)\le C(N+1)^k$, with no assumption on SAT. For every $A$ and every
UNSAT $F$,
$W_R(F)\le\Wb_R(|F|)\le C(|F|+1)^k\le C(|F|+T_A(F)+1)^k$, because
$T_A\ge 0$. This is B1 with $p_A(x)=C(x+1)^k$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Reading.} B3 is ``weaker'' than B1/B2 only in worlds with
$\mathrm{SAT}\notin P$, where it is vacuous. In the one world in which B3
says anything, all three bridges are the single statement ``$R$ is
polynomially bounded.'' So any proof of B3$(R)$ either proves
$\mathrm{SAT}\notin P$ outright, or shows how a polynomial-time SAT algorithm
forces short $R$-proofs. That second kind of argument is exactly a proof of
$\mathrm{SAT}\in P\Rightarrow\mathrm{B1}(R)$.

\begin{theorem}[strength transfer of B2]\label{thm:transfer}
If B2$(R)$ holds, then for every correct decider $A$ there is a constant
$c_A\ge1$ such that for all $N$
\[
 T_A^{wc}(N)\ \ge\ \bigl(\Wb_R(N)/c_A\bigr)^{1/c_A}-N-1 .
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Enlarge $p_A$ to $c_A(x+1)^{c_A}$ for some $c_A\ge1$. Then B2 gives
$\Wb_R(N)\le c_A(N+T_A^{wc}(N)+1)^{c_A}$. Solve for $T_A^{wc}(N)$.
\end{proof}

So B2 for $R$ turns \emph{every} proved lower bound on $\Wb_R$ into a lower
bound on the worst-case running time of \emph{every} SAT algorithm, losing
only a polynomial.

\begin{corollary}[B2 for Resolution implies a length-measured ETH-type bound]\label{cor:eth}
Let $\mathrm{Res}$ be ordinary DAG-like resolution with unit cost per derived
clause. If B2$(\mathrm{Res})$ holds, then for every correct CNF-SAT decider
$A$ there are $\gamma_A>0$ and $N_A$ such that
$T_A^{wc}(N)\ge 2^{\gamma_A N/\log N}$ for all $N\ge N_A$. In particular
$\mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm{DTIME}(2^{o(N/\log N)})$, which is far stronger
than $\mathrm{SAT}\notin P$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{KNOWN THEOREM} (Urquhart, JACM 1987; also Ben-Sasson--Wigderson,
JACM 2001, through width and expansion): fix an explicit $d$-regular expander
family $G_n$ with $d$ constant, and with vertex counts $n$ that have bounded
ratio gaps. An example is the Margulis--Gabber--Galil graphs on
$\mathbb Z_m^2$, where $d=8$ and $n=m^2$; multi-edges and loops must be
handled as in the cited sources. Odd-charge Tseitin formulas $T(G_n)$ then
require resolution refutations of size $2^{\alpha n}$ for some $\alpha>0$.
The DIMACS encoding has $dn/2$ variables and $2^{d-1}n$ clauses of width
$d$, so its bit length is $N_n=\Theta(n\log n)$. This is checked exactly
below, and it does not depend on graph structure. Given $N$, pick the
largest admissible $n$ with $N_n\le N$. Then $n=\Omega(N/\log N)$, and since
$\Wb$ is nondecreasing, $\Wb_{\mathrm{Res}}(N)\ge 2^{\alpha' N/\log N}$ for all
large $N$. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}. The polynomial loss changes only
the constant in the exponent.
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Calibration.} This bound is tight up to the constant in the
exponent. A length-$N$ CNF has at most $n=O(N/\log N)$ distinct variables,
because distinct indices need $\sum\log i=\Theta(n\log n)$ bits, so exhaustive
search runs in $2^{O(N/\log N)}$. The known length-parameterized algorithms
(Wahlstr\"om 2005, $O^*(1.0663^L)$; Peng--Xiao, $O^*(1.0638^L)$; $L$ is the
number of literal occurrences) do not beat $2^{\Theta(N/\log N)}$ in bit
length. So B2(Res) is \emph{not} falsified by known algorithms. Under ETH
with sparsification, sparse 3-CNF already has bit length $\Theta(n\log n)$
and needs time $2^{\delta n}$, so B2(Res) is consistent with standard
conjectures. Still, proving B2(Res) means proving a length-ETH lower bound
for every algorithm. That is strictly harder than P$\ne$NP. With the
directive's own hard-instance baseline, Haken's PHP, the same argument gives
only $2^{\Omega((N/\log N)^{1/3})}$.

\begin{theorem}[monotonicity tension]\label{thm:mono}
Suppose $R'$ cost-faithfully polynomially simulates $R$, meaning
$W_{R'}(F)\le q(|F|+W_R(F))$ for a fixed polynomial $q$. Then
(i) B3$(R)\Rightarrow$B3$(R')$ and B2$(R)\Rightarrow$B2$(R')$; and
(ii) a superpolynomial lower bound on $\Wb_{R'}$ implies one on $\Wb_R$.
Hence the systems with a proved bridge form an up-set, and the systems with
a proved lower bound form a down-set. A system in both would yield
P$\ne$NP.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\Wb_{R'}(N)\le\max_{|F|\le N}q(N+W_R(F))=q(N+\Wb_R(N))$ for nondecreasing
$q$. Compose this with the bounds on $\Wb_R$ for (i), and take the
contrapositive for (ii).
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Consequence.} Every system with a currently proved
superpolynomial lower bound (Resolution, Cutting Planes, bounded-depth Frege,
and others) has B3 \emph{equivalent} to $\mathrm{SAT}\notin P$. The corpus
already credits this for Resolution and for the class schema (LSPV-5 status
correction; ARC226 M2 qualification). B2 for such systems is even stronger,
by Corollary~\ref{cor:eth}. For systems at the top of the order, such as
Extended Frege and $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}\supseteq\mathrm{ER}_2$, no lower bound
is known. Moreover ``$P=NP\Rightarrow$ EF polynomially bounded'' is itself a
standard open problem; this run's literature search found no theorem proving
it. Cook--Reckhow ties polynomial boundedness of \emph{some} system to
NP$=$coNP, not to the preselected one. The known sufficient routes are the
$S^1_2$/PV reflection route (LSPV-1/357) and the Pich--Santhanam antichecker
route (LSPV-1/406). Both are CONDITIONAL. So the program
``$\Wb_R$ lower bound + B3$(R)$'' does not break P vs NP into an easier pair
for \emph{any} currently analyzable $R$. Its two halves sit at opposite ends
of the simulation order, and a single $R$ must satisfy both.

\begin{lemma}[the free-optimal-sequence convention is sound]\label{lem:free}
Let $M$ be any procedure that, on UNSAT input $F$, \emph{outputs} an
$R$-refutation $\pi$. Suppose every node of weight $w$ needs at least $w$
output symbols (true for $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$'s unit weights). Then
$\mathrm{time}_M(F)\ge|\pi|\ge W_R(F)$, however $M$ found $\pi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Writing $\pi$ takes at least $|\pi|$ steps, and $|\pi|\ge\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi)\ge W_R(F)$.
\end{proof}

\noindent This confirms the user's clarification: not charging strategy
discovery can only weaken the lower bound, never invalidate it. It also
locates the gap precisely. A general SAT decider is not required to output an
$R$-refutation, and Theorems~1--4 show that closing that gap (B1/B2/B3) is at
least as hard as the separation itself for every system whose lower bound is
known.

\section{Stale or overstated claims found}
Wording in the directive, ``prioritize the weakest sufficient bridge,'' can
be misread as saying B3 is easier to \emph{prove}. By Theorem~1 it is

correction of a verified theorem.

\section{Barrier-transfer and PQ ledger}
\textbf{Obstacles:} (1) no superpolynomial lower bound for EF-strength
systems; (2) no derivation of reflection or correctness from SAT$\in P$ for
any fixed $R$. \textbf{Transfer domains checked:} proof complexity and logic
(Cook--Reckhow; Krajíček--Pudlák optimality; $S^1_2$/PV translation) apply
directly as known theorems. Fine-grained complexity (ETH and sparsification)
is an exact object transfer for Corollary~3. Control-theory barrier
certificates would be a misleading analogy: a certificate is a proof object,
so asking a decider to produce one reintroduces B1. \textbf{PQ techniques:}
Schur squares, Hilbert functions and collision entropy are FAILED TRANSFER
here, since these results are about quantifier structure and have no code
object. The LWE worst-case/average-case lesson is an ANALOGY only: B2 is
already worst-case on both sides, and no average-case reduction is proposed.
\textbf{Relativization:} Theorems 1--4 are quantifier manipulations and
relativize trivially. Corollary~3 uses only a proof-complexity lower bound.
No claim here gets past relativization, natural proofs or algebrization.
