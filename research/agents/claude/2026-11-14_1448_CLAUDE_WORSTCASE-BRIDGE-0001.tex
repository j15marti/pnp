\section{Target, as assigned}
For a fixed sound, complete proof system $R$ with cost measure $\operatorname{cost}_R$,
\[
\overline W_R(N)=\max_{\substack{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}\\|\langle F\rangle|\le N}}\;
\min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi).
\]
Taking the minimum over $\pi$ grants free knowledge of the best reduction
sequence. That is valid for a lower bound \emph{on any procedure that
executes an $R$-derivation}: searching can only add work. It does not by
itself bind an arbitrary algorithm, which need not execute any
$R$-derivation. That gap is the bridge
\[
\mathrm{B}(R):\qquad \mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\;\Longrightarrow\;\overline W_R(N)\le N^{O(1)}.
\]
The multisign DAG hypergraph ``minimal reduced logical state'' is the empty
clause (UNSAT) at the sink of a shared derivation DAG; its ``minimal work'' is
exactly $\min_\pi\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi)$ with shared nodes counted once.

\section{The bridge is a disjunction}
\begin{proposition}[PROVED/EXACT]
For every fixed $R$,
\[
\mathrm B(R)\iff\bigl(\mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm P\bigr)\;\lor\;\bigl(\overline W_R(N)\le N^{O(1)}\bigr).
\]
Consequently: (a) $\mathrm B(R)$ is implied by $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$ for every $R$;
(b) if $\overline W_R$ is known to be superpolynomial, then $\mathrm B(R)$ is
logically equivalent to $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$;
(c) a proof of $\mathrm B(R)$ that is not itself a proof of $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$
must establish the disjunction constructively: from any hypothetical
polynomial-time SAT decider $A$ it must build short $R$-refutations of every UNSAT $F$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$X\Rightarrow Y$ is $\lnot X\lor Y$. (b): the second disjunct is false. (c): the
only non-vacuous route is to derive $R$-proofs from $A$.
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Duplication.} (b) for Resolution is already in root
\path{main.tex} (line $\approx$4464, ``equivalent to $\mathrm{SAT}\notin P$'').
The disjunctive form and item (c) make the requirement on the bridge explicit for
the new directive: the bridge must \emph{compile an arbitrary SAT algorithm into
$R$-proofs}. Searching only over hard instances cannot produce it.

\section{Literature check for this run (KNOWN THEOREMS)}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Constructive bridge routes (strong $R$).} Cook (STOC 1975): if PV proves
the UNSAT-soundness of a polynomial-time $A$, then Extended Frege has
polynomial-size refutations of all UNSAT CNFs, which gives $\mathrm B(\mathrm{EF})$
for such $A$. Krajíček--Pudlák (JSL 1989): a p-optimal or optimal proof system
$R^\ast$ satisfies $\mathrm B(R^\ast)$ unconditionally, but whether such a system
exists is OPEN. Both routes are already in the corpus:
\path{research/agents/lspv-1/2026-11-03_0032_LSPV-1_GODEL_REFLECTION_B3_FIREWALL357.tex}
and \path{research/main/2026-11-02_1348_MAIN_BLACK-BOX-RELATIVIZATION-FIREWALL-REFEREE296.md}
(which also gives the relativization barrier for $\mathrm B(R)$).
This run's search found no source showing that $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$ alone
implies that EF is polynomially bounded. Recent work studies the conjecture
that PV cannot prove ``$\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$'' or ``$\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$
via a fixed $f$'' (Li, ECCC TR25-187; Pich--Santhanam line; arXiv:1905.12935, appendix).
That is the exact missing piece for the EF route: \emph{unprovability} of correctness.
\item \emph{Exact shortest Resolution proofs.} Peitl--Szeider, ``Finding the
hardest formulas for resolution'' (CP 2020; JAIR 72, 2021) compute the first ten
resolution hardness numbers with a SAT encoding of ``proof of length $\le L$''
(after Mencía et al.). It is a finite version of $\overline W_{\mathrm{Res}}$
indexed by clause count. The corpus already uses their \texttt{smu} data (REFEREE301).
\item \emph{Survival tests (assigned priority 4).} Tseitin on expanders: exponential
for Resolution (Urquhart 1987), but polynomial for Gaussian elimination,
PC over $\mathbb F_2$, and Res(lin). PHP: exponential for Resolution (Haken 1985),
but polynomial for Cutting Planes and Extended Resolution (Cook 1976).
Random 3-CNF at constant density: exponential for Resolution (Chvátal--Szemerédi 1988)
and Polynomial Calculus (Ben-Sasson--Impagliazzo; Alekhnovich--Razborov). For Cutting
Planes, the known lower bounds are for random $\Theta(\log n)$-CNFs
(Fleming--Pankratov--Pitassi--Robere 2017; Hrubeš--Pudlák 2017). No superpolynomial
Frege or EF lower bound is known for any family.
\end{itemize}

\section{Computational: certified minima and brackets for Resolution}
Fixed $R=$ DAG Resolution, with cost equal to the number of distinct derived clauses.
Axioms are free, and the best proof is chosen freely.
\begin{lemma}[PROVED/EXACT]
If $F$ is minimally unsatisfiable with $m$ clauses, then $W_{\mathrm{Res}}(F)\ge m-1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every axiom must be an ancestor of $\bot$; otherwise a proper subformula would be
refuted. The ancestor DAG of $\bot$ is connected (every node reaches $\bot$).
It has $m$ axioms and $d$ derived nodes, each with two premise edges, so it has
$2d$ edges on $m+d$ nodes. Connectivity needs $2d\ge m+d-1$, so $d\ge m-1$.
\end{proof}
Upper bounds come from explicit Davis--Putnam elimination refutations, pruned to
the ancestors of $\bot$ and replayed by an independent checker. Orders were
exhaustive for $n\le 6$ and sampled within a 15\,s budget otherwise.
SAT-encoding queries were each capped at 25\,s (the 30\,s rule).
One CaDiCaL query ($\mathrm{PHP}^3_2$, $L=9$) ignored the interrupt and finished
UNSAT in 90\,s. That breaks the runtime rule, but the result is a complete solver answer.

\noindent\textbf{Red-team reading.} The Tseitin rows are the Resolution-hard
type (Urquhart), but a uniform GF(2) solver decides every Tseitin instance in
$O(n^3)$ time. A growing Resolution lower bound on them therefore says nothing
about algorithms, as Proposition~1(b) predicts. Finite minima at these sizes cannot
distinguish polynomial from superpolynomial growth. They are controls, not evidence.
The generic encoding stalls near $L\approx m$, which matches Peitl--Szeider's
need for MU-specific encodings and symmetry breaking.
