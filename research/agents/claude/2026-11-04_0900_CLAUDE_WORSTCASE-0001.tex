\section{Setting}
$R$ is a Cook--Reckhow system: $V_R(F,\pi)$ runs in time $\mathrm{poly}(|F|+|\pi|)$ and $\exists\pi\,V_R(F,\pi)=1\iff F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}$. A derivation is a signed DAG hypergraph: nodes are derived states (clauses, lines, or Turing-machine cells), each hyperedge is a locally checkable inference with signed premises, and $\operatorname{cost}$ is size (number of nodes, or total bit length), optionally with peak space $s(\pi)$ = pebbling width of a topological order of the DAG. The pair $(\text{size},\text{space})$ gives a Pareto frontier $\overline W_R(N;s)$.

\section{Results}
\begin{prop}[KNOWN THEOREM, Cook--Reckhow 1979] $\exists R$ with $\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$ iff $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$.\end{prop}
\begin{prop}[PROVED/EXACT, triviality of the unverified cost] If a single inference may assert ``$F$ is unsatisfiable'' without a poly-time check (or checks are not charged), then $\overline W_R(N)=O(N)$, since the one-step derivation exists for every $F$. Hence the cost model must be a Cook--Reckhow system with every step verified. Free choice of $\pi$ is exactly the existential quantifier of NP: $\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$ iff $R$ proves UNSAT in NP-fashion. So a superpolynomial lower bound for a fixed $R$ is a statement of the same kind as ``this system is not polynomially bounded''; it implies $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$ only through $\mathrm{NP}\ne\mathrm{coNP}$ and only if $R$ is shown optimal (next).\end{prop}
\begin{prop}[PROVED/EXACT from Prop.~1] If $R$ simulates every proof system (optimal), then: $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\Rightarrow\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\Rightarrow\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\Rightarrow\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$. So the bridge holds trivially for optimal $R$, and for optimal $R$ a superpolynomial $\overline W_R$ is \emph{equivalent} to $\mathrm{NP}\ne\mathrm{coNP}$ (not easier). Existence of an optimal system is OPEN; $\mathrm{NE}=\mathrm{coNE}$ suffices (Krajíček--Pudlák 1989) and the survey arXiv:1909.03691 notes that Frege cannot be ruled out as optimal.\end{prop}
\begin{prop}[KNOWN THEOREM, Cook 1975 / Krajíček--Pudlák; proof sketch] If some poly-time SAT algorithm $A$ has soundness ($A(F)=0\Rightarrow\forall y\,\neg F(y)$) provable in $PV$, then $EF$ has polynomial-size proofs of every UNSAT $F$, i.e.\ $\overline W_{EF}\le\mathrm{poly}$. Sketch: translate the $PV$ proof into poly-size $EF$ proofs of the instance-wise tautologies; evaluate $A$ on the concrete $F$ (its trace is a polynomial tableau, a signed DAG hypergraph of local $3\times2$ window checks) and cut.\end{prop}
\begin{prop}[PROVED/EXACT, the asymmetry] Levin universal search gives, under $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, a correct SAT \emph{finder}, whose YES-outputs are verified. Its NO-outputs carry no verification, so the trace of an unverified decider is not an $R$-derivation. The bridge for a fixed natural $R$ therefore needs a reflection principle: $R$ (or $PV$) must prove soundness of some decider. This is the exact missing arrow; ``free knowledge of the best sequence'' does not supply it.\end{prop}
\begin{prop}[consequence of P=NP, KNOWN] $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$ makes every proof system automatable: search time is $\mathrm{poly}(|F|+\text{shortest proof})$. So charging discovery changes cost only polynomially under the hypothesis; the uncharged $\overline W_R$ is the right quantity, but this does not bound the shortest proof.\end{prop}

\section{Weakest sufficient bridge (CANDIDATE)}
$B(R)$: $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\Rightarrow\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$. Proved for optimal $R$ (Prop.~3) and for $EF$ under provable soundness (Prop.~4). Weakest unconditional form worth attacking: ``$\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\Rightarrow$ some poly-time decider has $EF$/$PV$-provable soundness.'' This is not known and is not implied by $\mathrm{Th}(P)$; it would give $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\Rightarrow EF$ polynomially bounded, so $EF$ superpolynomial lower bound $\Rightarrow\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$. Contrapositive known direction: $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$ is conjectured to follow from $PV\nvdash\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.

\noindent Conclusion (red team): every family with an unconditional lower bound dies at $EF$. Resolution/PC/CP lower bounds do not survive circuit compression (extension variables) or shared-DAG reasoning.
