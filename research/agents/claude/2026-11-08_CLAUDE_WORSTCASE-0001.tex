\noindent\textbf{Status: P versus NP remains OPEN.} Nothing below proves a lower bound.

\section*{Setup}
Let $R$ be a fixed derivation system whose steps are checkable in polynomial time, which is sound, and which is complete for UNSAT 3-CNF.
Take $\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi)=|\pi|$ in bits, with shared DAGs allowed.
Then $R$ is a Cook--Reckhow proof system, and
$\overline W_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},|F|\le N}\min_\pi|\pi|$ is the optimal proof length.
Free choice of $\pi$ is exactly the $\min$; no search cost is charged.

\section*{Prop.~1 (bridge equivalence) --- PROVED/EXACT, elementary}
The bridge $B_R$: $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathsf P\Rightarrow\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$ is logically the disjunction
$\mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathsf P\ \vee\ R\text{ is polynomially bounded}$.
So $B_R$ is not a ``weak'' hypothesis: it is a theorem about $R$ that must be proved on its own.
If $\overline W_R$ is superpolynomial, then $B_R$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathsf P$.
Hence proving $B_R$ and the lower bound together is exactly proving $\mathsf P\neq\mathsf{NP}$, and the bridge carries no saving.

\section*{Prop.~2 (where the bridge is true) --- PROVED/EXACT given Cook--Reckhow}
(a) If $R$ is optimal (every proof system is polynomially simulated by $R$), then $B_R$ holds.
Reason: $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}\Rightarrow\mathsf{NP}=\mathsf{coNP}\Rightarrow$ some system is polynomially bounded $\Rightarrow R$ is.
In that case $\overline W_R$ superpolynomial is equivalent to $\mathsf{NP}\ne\mathsf{coNP}$, which is strictly stronger than what is needed.
Existence of an optimal system is open; it follows from $\mathsf{NE}=\mathsf{coNE}$ (Krajíček--Pudlák).

(b) For $R=\mathrm{EF}$: if the correctness of some polynomial-time SAT algorithm is provable in $\mathrm{PV}$ (or $S^1_2$), then EF is polynomially bounded (Cook's translation). KNOWN THEOREM, quoted from memory; verify before load-bearing use.
So $B_{\mathrm{EF}}$ is provable by this route only for provably correct algorithms.
Whether $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$ forces EF to be polynomially bounded when correctness is unprovable is OPEN.

\section*{Consequence for the five-agent plan}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Lower-Bound Lead.} Weakest sufficient bridge: choose $R\supseteq\mathrm{EF}$ and prove $B_R$ via (b), restricting to the case where an algorithm with provable correctness exists, or via an optimality hypothesis. Either leaves a proof-complexity lower bound for EF-strength systems as the sole hard arrow: OPEN, with no known technique.
\item \textbf{Hard families.} The classical families (pigeonhole, Tseitin on expanders, clique-coloring) have polynomial-size EF/Frege proofs, or are hard only for weaker fragments. They are therefore killed by ``adaptive strategy switching''. KNOWN, from memory.
\item \textbf{Barrier.} Feasible interpolation fails for EF under standard cryptographic assumptions (Krajíček--Pudlák, Bonet--Pitassi--Raz), so interpolation-based lower bounds do not extend to it. KNOWN, from memory.
\item \textbf{Resource Auditor.} Work with $|\pi|$ in bits. Peak memory is a separate measure (space for proofs) and does not enter $\overline W_R$.
\item \textbf{Red Team.} Target: a candidate $R$ that is not a Cook--Reckhow system, because its steps are not poly-time checkable. Such an $R$ is not an inference system, and $B_R$ then loses its meaning.
\end{itemize}
