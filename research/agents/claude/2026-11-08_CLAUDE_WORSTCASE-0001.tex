\section{Setup}
Let $R$ be a proof/derivation system with a polynomial-time derivation checker (Cook--Reckhow), $\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)=|\pi|$ (or a verifier-time variant), and
$\overline W_R(N)=\max_{F\in\UNSAT,|F|\le N}\min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$.

\begin{proposition}[free strategy = nondeterminism; PROVED/EXACT]
For $R$ sound and complete with poly-time checker, $\overline W_R$ is polynomially bounded iff $R$ is a polynomially bounded proof system for $\UNSAT$; this implies $\NP=\coNP$. Because the minimum ranges freely over $\pi$ (no charge for finding it), $\overline W_R$ measures a \emph{nondeterministic} resource, not deterministic time.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} Poly-size $\pi$ plus the checker puts $\UNSAT\in\NP$. Converse direction is the definition. \end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[bridge versus goal; PROVED/EXACT, elementary]
Let $R$ be any system with an unconditionally proved bound $\overline W_R\ne\mathrm{poly}$ (e.g.\ Resolution, Haken; Tseitin on expanders, Urquhart). Then the implication $\SAT\in\cP\Rightarrow\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$ is logically equivalent to $\SAT\notin\cP$. So a proof of the bridge for such $R$ is itself a proof of $\cP\ne\NP$; the bridge cannot be the easy half.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} The conclusion is false, so the implication is equivalent to the negation of the hypothesis. \end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[when the bridge is true; KNOWN THEOREM-style, short proof]
If $R^\ast$ is an \emph{optimal} proof system (p-simulates every system), then $\SAT\in\cP\Rightarrow\overline W_{R^\ast}\le\mathrm{poly}$, and in fact $\overline W_{R^\ast}$ poly $\iff\NP=\coNP$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} $\cP=\NP$ gives $\NP=\coNP$, hence a p-bounded system $Q$ exists (Cook--Reckhow); $R^\ast$ p-simulates $Q$. The converse is Proposition 1. \end{proof}
Existence of an optimal system is OPEN (Kraj\'{\i}\v{c}ek--Pudl\'ak 1989). Consequently for $R^\ast$ the lower bound target is $\NP\ne\coNP$, strictly stronger than $\cP\ne\NP$; the bridge is free but the lower bound is harder than the original problem.

\begin{theorem}[provability bridge; KNOWN, cited not re-proved]
If a theory $T$ (e.g.\ Cook's PV) proves that some poly-time $A$ decides $\SAT$, then the propositional translations of that correctness statement have poly-size proofs in the system associated with $T$ (Extended Frege for PV), so that system is p-bounded (Cook 1975; Kraj\'{\i}\v{c}ek--Pudl\'ak). The needed hypothesis is \emph{provable} $\SAT\in\cP$, not merely $\SAT\in\cP$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[no ``weakest sufficient bridge'' below these; CANDIDATE reading]
For a fixed named $R$: either the bridge is equivalent to $\cP\ne\NP$ (known lower bound, Thm 2), or it needs (i) $R$ optimal (open), or (ii) provability of $\SAT\in\cP$ in $R$'s theory (stronger hypothesis). A fixed unsound ``run machine $i$ for $t$ steps'' system makes the bridge trivial but is unsound for unknown $i$, since soundness of $A$ is a $\Pi_1$ fact needing proof inside $R$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Red-team verdict on the proposed transfer}
\begin{itemize}
\item Stronger pointwise/simulation theorems are \emph{not} merely secondary: absent them the conditional bridge has only the routes above.
\item Hard families that beat Resolution (PHP, Tseitin) are, as far as the literature known to me shows, polynomial in Extended Frege; hardness must be sought against EF-level or optimal-system reasoning, where only conditional/barrier results exist.
\item Hard-instance lower bounds against EF are unproved; Pich--Santhanam show PV cannot prove strong coNP-type lower bounds under hardness assumptions, a barrier on feasible proofs of such a bound.
\end{itemize}
