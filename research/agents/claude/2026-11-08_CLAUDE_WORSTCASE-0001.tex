\title{CLAUDE WORSTCASE-0001\\\large Worst-case derivation cost $\overline W_R(N)$: what the bridge $\SAT\in P\Rightarrow \overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$ can and cannot do}
\author{Scheduled run (not an LSPV run)}\date{2026-11-08}\maketitle
\noindent\textbf{Status.} P versus NP remains OPEN. No lower bound on $\overline W_R$ for a bridge-eligible $R$ is claimed. Root \texttt{main.tex}, schedules, ownership unchanged.

\section*{Setup}
$R$ is a Cook--Reckhow system (poly-time verifier $V_R$, sound and complete for UNSAT). $\overline W_R(N)=\max_{|F|\le N}\min_\pi \mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$ with $\mathrm{cost}$ = proof size (space tracked separately). Free choice of the optimal $\pi$ is exactly the standard nondeterministic definition of proof size, so ``not charging for strategy discovery'' loses nothing and gains nothing.

\section*{Results}
\begin{proposition}[Bridge circularity] \status{PROVED/EXACT}
If $\overline W_R$ is superpolynomial (as it unconditionally is for resolution, polynomial calculus, cutting planes, bounded-depth Frege), then the bridge $B_R:\ \SAT\in P\Rightarrow\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$ is equivalent to $\SAT\notin P$, i.e.\ to P$\neq$NP.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} Conclusion of $B_R$ is false, so $B_R\iff\neg(\SAT\in P)$. \end{proof}
Consequence: a bridge for a system with a known lower bound is not a weaker route to P$\ne$NP; it is P$\ne$NP. The program needs $R$ whose lower bound is unknown, i.e.\ at least Frege-strength, and the bridge must then be proved by simulating an arbitrary P algorithm \emph{inside} $R$.

\begin{proposition}[Optimal systems satisfy the bridge] \status{PROVED/EXACT}
If $R$ is optimal (polynomially simulates every Cook--Reckhow system), then $B_R$ holds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} If $M$ decides SAT in poly time, let $Q(F,\pi)$ accept iff $M(F)=$UNSAT. $Q$ is sound, complete, poly-time verifiable, with proofs of constant size; $R$ simulates it. \end{proof}
\begin{proposition}[Optimal $R$ is the Cook--Reckhow program] \status{PROVED/EXACT}
For optimal $R$: $\overline W_R$ superpolynomial $\iff$ NP$\ne$coNP. So the bridge holds but the lower bound is as hard as NP$\neq$coNP, strictly stronger than P$\ne$NP.
\end{proposition}
Existence of an optimal system is open (Krajicek--Pudlak 1989: implied by NE=coNE; no unconditional example; recent oracle evidence that existence is hard to settle).

\begin{proposition}[Extended Frege] \status{KNOWN THEOREM, cited not re-proved}
If some machine $M$ decides SAT in poly time and PV proves $M$ correct, then EF has polynomial proofs of all UNSAT formulas (Cook 1975 translation, Krajicek--Pudlak). ``$\SAT\in P$'' alone is not known to give this; the missing ingredient is provability of correctness, a uniformity/independence condition.
\end{proposition}

\section*{Weakest sufficient bridge (refined)}
The only non-circular route: choose $R$ (EF or stronger) and prove \emph{(i)} $B_R$ via a reflection/simulation argument, \emph{(ii)} $\overline W_R$ superpolynomial. (ii) alone for EF already implies EF is not polynomially bounded, a famous open problem; the combination is at least as hard. No weakening of (ii) is available from this framing. \status{OPEN}

\section*{Literature check}
No 2025--26 result found that settles optimal proof systems. Relevant: Proof Analysis Problem for EF is NP-complete (arXiv 2506.16956, FOCS 2025); oracle evidence on optimal proof systems (arXiv 2408.07408). Classification: both \emph{useful analogy}, no transfer theorem. Automatability results (resolution not automatable under standard assumptions) are consistent with, and irrelevant to, the lower-bound side.
