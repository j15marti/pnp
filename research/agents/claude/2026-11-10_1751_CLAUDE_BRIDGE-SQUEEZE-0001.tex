\section{Question}
The shared directive asks for a superpolynomial lower bound on
\[
\W_R(N)=\max_{\substack{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}\\ |\langle F\rangle|\le N}}\ \min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi)
\]
for one fixed system $R$, with the best derivation granted free, plus the bridge
\[
\mathrm{Bridge}(R):\qquad \mathrm{SAT}\in P\ \Longrightarrow\ \W_R(N)\le \mathrm{poly}(N).
\]
Write $\mathrm{LB}(R)$ for ``$\W_R$ is not polynomially bounded''. Granting the best sequence free is a valid relaxation for a lower bound: an algorithm that follows any $R$-derivation pays at least $\W_R$ (paid search only adds work). This note asks which systems $R$ make the pair $(\mathrm{Bridge}(R),\mathrm{LB}(R))$ a real decomposition of P vs NP rather than a restatement.

Throughout, $R$ is a Cook--Reckhow refutation system for CNF and cost is proof bit length; unit node count is at most bit length and, for systems with bounded-size lines, polynomially related to it, so the conclusions hold for either measure.

\section{Two exact facts}
\begin{lemma}[Monotonicity]\label{mono}
Suppose $R'$ p-simulates $R$ in size: there is a polynomial $q$ with $s_{R'}(F)\le q(s_R(F)+|F|)$ for all UNSAT $F$. Then $\W_{R'}(N)\le q(\W_R(N)+N)$. Hence $\mathrm{Bridge}(R)\Rightarrow\mathrm{Bridge}(R')$ and $\mathrm{LB}(R')\Rightarrow\mathrm{LB}(R)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take the maximum over UNSAT $F$ with $|\langle F\rangle|\le N$ on both sides; $q$ is monotone. A polynomial bound for $\W_R$ composes with $q$; contrapositive for LB.
\end{proof}
So bridges get easier as $R$ gets stronger, and lower bounds get harder. The two halves pull in opposite directions.

\begin{theorem}[Squeeze]\label{squeeze}
For every $R$: $\ \mathrm{Bridge}(R)\wedge\mathrm{LB}(R)\iff (P\ne NP)\wedge\mathrm{LB}(R)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
($\Rightarrow$) If $\mathrm{SAT}\in P$, the bridge gives a polynomial bound on $\W_R$, contradicting $\mathrm{LB}(R)$. So $\mathrm{SAT}\notin P$. ($\Leftarrow$) If $\mathrm{SAT}\notin P$, $\mathrm{Bridge}(R)$ is an implication with false hypothesis, so it is true.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Bridges for systems with known lower bounds]\label{known}
If $\mathrm{LB}(R)$ is a known theorem, then $\mathrm{Bridge}(R)\iff P\ne NP$. This applies to tree-like and general resolution (Haken 1985), cutting planes (Pudl\'ak 1997), polynomial calculus (Razborov 1998; Impagliazzo--Pudl\'ak--Sgall 1999) and bounded-depth Frege (Ajtai 1988; Kraj\'i\v{c}ek--Pudl\'ak--Woods 1995; Pitassi--Beame--Impagliazzo 1993), and by Lemma~\ref{mono} to every system p-simulated by one of them.
\end{corollary}
\noindent For these systems the boxed bridge is not a weaker sufficient condition. It is the full target restated, and a proof of it would be a proof of $P\ne NP$. This sharpens the corpus result that the stronger pointwise simulation TR(Resolution) is false (LSPV-1 334): the worst-case bridge for resolution is not false, but it is the whole problem.

\begin{corollary}[Where a non-circular route can live]\label{where}
The decomposition $P\ne NP\Leftarrow\mathrm{Bridge}(R)\wedge\mathrm{LB}(R)$ splits the problem into two parts, neither already known to equal $P\ne NP$, only if $R$ is not p-simulated by any system with a known superpolynomial lower bound. For such $R$, $\mathrm{LB}(R)$ is itself an open lower-bound problem. Choosing an optimal system, if one exists, makes $\mathrm{Bridge}(R)$ free (LSPV-1 336). Then $\mathrm{LB}(R)$ is equivalent to $NP\ne coNP$ (Cook--Reckhow 1979), which is stronger than $P\ne NP$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $R$ is p-simulated by $S$ with $\mathrm{LB}(S)$ known, Lemma~\ref{mono} gives $\mathrm{LB}(R)$ known, and Corollary~\ref{known} applies. The optimal case is LSPV-1 336, Theorem~2, together with Cook--Reckhow.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequence for the project calculus}
LSPV-1 357 records that the RES+VARDEF fragment of $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ is $\mathrm{ER}_2$, which is polynomially equivalent to Extended Resolution and hence to Extended Frege. So $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ p-simulates EF, and Lemma~\ref{mono} gives
\[
\mathrm{LB}(R_{\mathrm{mix341}})\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathrm{LB}(\mathrm{EF}).
\]
The lower-bound half of the boxed program for the project's own calculus is therefore at least as hard as superpolynomial Extended Frege lower bounds. No superpolynomial lower bound is known even for Frege. On the bridge side, the corpus has a sufficient condition: some polynomial-time SAT algorithm whose rejection soundness is provable in PV/$S^1_2$ (Cook 1975; LSPV-1 357). Whether $P=NP$ implies EF is polynomially bounded remains open. The literature check this run found no result settling it; Pich--Santhanam (arXiv 2312.08163) prove implications in the converse direction under bounded-arithmetic assumptions.
