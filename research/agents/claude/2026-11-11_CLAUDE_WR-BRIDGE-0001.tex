\section*{Status summary}
P versus NP remains \textbf{OPEN}. This note proves short \textbf{PROVED/EXACT} logical reductions about the shared
worst-case directive target
\[
\overline W_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},\ |\langle F\rangle|\le N}\ \min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi),
\]
and the proposed ``weakest sufficient'' bridge
\[
\mathbf{Bridge}(R):\qquad \mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\ \Longrightarrow\ \overline W_R(N)\le\mathrm{poly}(N).
\]
The finding is negative for the strategy's framing: the bridge is \emph{not} weaker than P$\ne$NP for any system
whose lower bound is already known, and for strong systems it is a known open problem in proof complexity.

\section{Setup}
$R$ is a Cook--Reckhow propositional proof system: a polynomial-time verifier, sound and complete for UNSAT.
$\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi)=|\pi|$ (bit size; line count changes nothing below up to polynomial factors).
The multisign DAG-hypergraph reduction calculus of the directive is one such $R$ once its rule set is fixed and
each rule is polynomial-time checkable. Taking the minimum over $\pi$ is exactly the free-strategy convention:
the search for $\pi$ is not charged. $R$ is \emph{p-bounded} when $\overline W_R(N)\le\mathrm{poly}(N)$.

\section{Exact reductions}
\begin{proposition}[PROVED/EXACT; restatement]\label{p:restate}
$\mathbf{Bridge}(R)\iff(\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\Rightarrow R\text{ is p-bounded})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}Definition of p-bounded; $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\iff\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT: collapse for systems with known lower bounds]\label{t:collapse}
If $R$ has an unconditional superpolynomial lower bound $\overline W_R(N)\ne N^{O(1)}$, then
$\mathbf{Bridge}(R)\iff \mathrm P\neq\mathrm{NP}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}($\Rightarrow$) Contrapositive of the bridge with the true premise ``$R$ not p-bounded''.
($\Leftarrow$) If $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$ the bridge's premise is false, so it holds vacuously.\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[KNOWN THEOREM inputs]
For $R\in\{$Resolution (Haken 1985; Ben-Sasson--Wigderson 2001), Polynomial Calculus (Razborov 1998),
Cutting Planes (Pudlák 1997), bounded-depth Frege (Ajtai 1988; Krajíček--Pudlák--Woods, Pitassi--Beame--Impagliazzo 1993),
Res$(\lim)$/SoS degree bounds (Grigoriev 2001; Schoenebeck 2008)$\}$, proving $\mathbf{Bridge}(R)$ \emph{is} proving
P$\ne$NP. For these $R$ the bridge is not a ``weaker sufficient'' target: it is equivalent to the whole problem,
and all the difficulty sits in it, none in the lower bound.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[PROVED/EXACT: the bridge is monotone the wrong way]\label{p:mono}
If $R'$ p-simulates $R$, then $\mathbf{Bridge}(R)\Rightarrow\mathbf{Bridge}(R')$; the lower bound, by contrast, moves from
$R'$ to $R$. So the bridge gets easier, and the lower bound harder, as $R$ grows stronger. The two requirements meet only at
systems for which neither is known: at present Frege, Extended Frege (EF) and anything stronger.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}If $R$ is p-bounded and $R'$ p-simulates $R$, then $R'$ is p-bounded.\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[KNOWN THEOREM, Cook 1975 / Krajíček--Pudlák 1989, stated without new proof]\label{p:pv}
If $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$ is witnessed by a polynomial-time algorithm $A$ whose soundness
($\forall F\,[A(F)=\mathrm{UNSAT}\to F\text{ unsatisfiable}]$) is provable in Cook's theory PV, then EF is p-bounded.
Without such provability, whether $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$ implies EF is p-bounded is \textbf{OPEN}; Krajíček (2010, ECCC TR10-054)
shows related statements are consistent with PV and with true universal $T_{\mathrm{PV}}$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\emph{Consequence.} $\mathbf{Bridge}(\mathrm{EF})$ is equivalent to ``every polynomial-time SAT algorithm (if one exists)
has PV-provable soundness, up to replacing it by another one that does''. That is a statement about the provability of
algorithm correctness, not about the cost of executing a derivation. The free-strategy convention does not help, because the missing
step is converting \emph{any} algorithm's run into an $R$-proof, not finding the best $R$-proof.

\section{Falsification-first check of the ``hard families'' (directive item 4)}
A family that is hard for a fixed $R$ supports a SAT lower bound only if no polynomial-time algorithm refutes it.
For the classical families the opposite holds:
\begin{itemize}
\item Tseitin on expanders: exponential for Resolution and polynomial calculus over $\mathbb F_{p\ne2}$, yet solved in polynomial time by
Gaussian elimination over $\mathbb F_2$ (KNOWN THEOREM; in the companion script, GF(2) elimination returns UNSAT immediately).
\item PHP$^{n+1}_n$: exponential for Resolution and bounded-depth Frege, solved by counting (polynomial time; polynomial-size in Frege
and in Cutting Planes, Buss 1987; Cook--Coullard--Turán 1987).
\item Random 3-CNF at constant clause density $\Delta$ above threshold: exponential for Resolution (Chvátal--Szemerédi 1988),
polynomial calculus and SoS degree $\Omega(n)$ (Grigoriev 2001; Schoenebeck 2008). Cutting Planes is known only for $\Theta(\log n)$-CNF
(Sokolov 2023); bounded-depth Frege only reaches $\Omega(n^{1+\epsilon_k})$ steps (Gryaznov--Talebanfard 2024, arXiv:2403.02275).
No polynomial-time refuter is known for constant $\Delta$ (Feige's hypothesis); dense formulas with $\Delta\gtrsim n^{1/2}$ have
polynomial-size refutations (Müller--Tzameret, arXiv:1101.3970).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Recommendation (CANDIDATE):} the Hypergraph \& Proof Certificates role should treat random 3-CNF at constant density as the main
hard-instance family and Tseitin/PHP only as negative controls (they show that $R$-hardness alone transfers no algorithmic hardness).
Any multisign-hypergraph lower bound has to survive the GF(2) and counting controls in the companion script.

\section{Computational illustration (COMPUTATIONAL)}
\texttt{research/agents/claude/wr-bridge/width\_vs\_algorithm.py} computes the exact minimum resolution width by width-bounded
saturation and runs the polynomial-time decision procedure on the same instance. Each family/size runs as an independent
chunk capped at about 30\,s (directive runtime rule); chunks that hit the cap are recorded as timeouts, not results. Raw output is in
\texttt{width\_vs\_algorithm\_results.jsonl}. These are tiny instances: they illustrate the controls and do not, on their own,
show any asymptotic behaviour.

Observed: Tseitin prism $k=3$ (9 variables) has minimum width 4 and GF(2) returns UNSAT; PHP$^{3}_2$ and PHP$^{4}_3$ have width 2 and 3,
and PHP$^{5}_4$ times out at width 4; random 3-CNF at $\Delta=6$ with $n=10,14,18$ has width 3. At these sizes small $n$ dominates, so no
growth is visible. Tseitin $k\ge4$ ran past the cap and has no recorded result. The naive saturation is $O(|S|^2)$ per round, and a faster
implementation is needed before any scaling claim.
