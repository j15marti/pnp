\section*{Target}
All five roles study
\[
\Wb_R(N)=\max_{\substack{F\in\UNSAT\\ |\langle F\rangle|\le N}}\ \min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi),
\qquad
\textsc{Bridge}(R):\ \SAT\in\mathrm P\implies \Wb_R(N)\le\poly(N),
\]
\[
\textsc{LB}(R):\ \Wb_R(N)\ \text{is not polynomially bounded}.
\]
$R$ is fixed before any instance, and the best derivation is supplied for free.
$\textsc{Bridge}(R)\wedge\textsc{LB}(R)$ gives $\SAT\notin\mathrm P$ by modus tollens.
This run asks which half of the conjunction carries the difficulty, as a function of $R$.

\section{Exact identification of the user's construction}
\status{PROVED/EXACT} Take the signed multisign DAG hypergraph of a 3-CNF, with
hyperedges as clauses and signs as literal polarities. A \emph{valid reduction sequence}
uses a fixed finite set of sound local moves, for example signed hyperedge merging on a
complementary pair. The reduction reaches the minimal reduced state $\bot$, and its work
counts the moves, with shared intermediate states counted once. The
minimum work over all valid sequences is then exactly $w_R(F)=\min_\pi\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi)$
for the proof system $R$ whose inferences are those moves. Granting free knowledge of the best
sequence is exactly \emph{nondeterminism}, the Cook--Reckhow notion of proof length. The
user's remark that ``finding the sequence only adds work'' is correct. It is also exactly why
$w_R$ lower-bounds only computations whose run \emph{is} an $R$-derivation. DPLL and CDCL runs
are Resolution derivations; an arbitrary algorithm's run is not an $R$-derivation. So the
transfer to arbitrary algorithms is precisely $\textsc{Bridge}(R)$. If the merge move is
signed binary resolution, $R$ is Resolution.

\section{The sandwich theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Difficulty sandwich]\label{thm:sandwich}
\status{PROVED/EXACT, elementary from KNOWN THEOREMS} For every fixed Cook--Reckhow refutation
system $R$ whose cost is polynomially related to encoded proof length:
\[
\mathrm{NP}\ne\mathrm{coNP}\ \Longrightarrow\ \bigl[\textsc{Bridge}(R)\wedge\textsc{LB}(R)\bigr]
\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}.
\]
Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}\Rightarrow\textsc{Bridge}(R)$, because the implication is then vacuous.
\item $\mathrm{NP}\ne\mathrm{coNP}\Rightarrow\textsc{LB}(R)$ \cite{cookreckhow1979}.
\item If $\textsc{LB}(R)$ is a known theorem, then $\textsc{Bridge}(R)\iff\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$.
\item Suppose $R'$ p-simulates $R$, meaning $w_{R'}(F)\le p(w_R(F)+|F|)$ for a fixed polynomial
$p$. Then $\textsc{Bridge}(R)\Rightarrow\textsc{Bridge}(R')$ and $\textsc{LB}(R')\Rightarrow\textsc{LB}(R)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(1) is propositional. (2) is Cook--Reckhow: a polynomially bounded system exists iff
$\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$. Then (1), (2) and $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}\Leftarrow\mathrm{NP}\ne\mathrm{coNP}$ give
the left arrow. The right arrow is modus tollens. For (3), if $\textsc{LB}(R)$ holds then the
consequent of $\textsc{Bridge}(R)$ is false, so $\textsc{Bridge}(R)\iff\neg(\SAT\in\mathrm P)$.
For (4), polynomial bounds on $w_R$ give polynomial bounds on $w_{R'}$ by composing
with $p$. The lower-bound statement is the contrapositive.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Consequence: anti-monotone difficulty.} Item (4) says the bridge gets easier
as $R$ gets stronger, while the lower bound gets harder. Item (3) shows the cost of picking a weak $R$.
Resolution, Cutting Planes, Polynomial Calculus, bounded-depth Frege, and (for its DNF family)
AC$^0[p]$-Frege all have known superpolynomial lower bounds. For each of them, proving
$\textsc{Bridge}(R)$ is \emph{literally equivalent} to proving $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$, so it is
a reformulation and not progress. This applies the world-ledger classification rule in
\texttt{AGENTS.md}. The vacuous-truth observation for B3 was already recorded in REFEREE343
(\path{research/main/2026-11-04_1248_MAIN_REFLECTION-SANDWICH-POLY-BALANCE-REFEREE343.md}).
Items (3)--(4) and the two-sided sandwich are the additions here.

\section{The Extended Frege floor for the project's fixed calculus}
The corpus's fixed calculus $\Rmix$ is specified in
\path{research/agents/lspv-2/2026-11-02_1216_LSPV-2_MIXED_TSEITIN_MIN_DAG341.tex}. Its rules are
binary RES, local AND3 with GF(2) annotation, binary XOR, unary CONVERT, and paid conservative
fresh two-input DEF. LSPV-1/357 already observed that the RES+VARDEF fragment is
$\mathrm{ER}_2$. It used this for the \emph{upper-bound} (bridge) direction.

\begin{theorem}[Lower-bound floor]\label{thm:floor}
\status{PROVED/EXACT, given KNOWN THEOREMS and the /341 reading that DEF admits every
two-input Boolean gate over existing literals} Let $(F_n)$ be any family of 3-CNFs with
$w_{\Rmix}(F_n)\ge s(n)$. Then every Extended Resolution refutation of $F_n$ has at least
$s(n)/2$ lines. Every Extended Frege refutation of $F_n$ has size at least $s(n)^{\Omega(1)}$.
In particular, $\textsc{LB}(\Rmix)$ implies a superpolynomial Extended Frege lower bound for an
explicit-encoded 3-CNF family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An ER refutation is a sequence of extension definitions $z\leftrightarrow(a\wedge b)$ over literals,
together with binary resolution steps. Two-input AND extensions over literals already make ER
p-equivalent to EF \cite{cookreckhow1979,krajicek1995}. Map each extension to one paid DEF node,
whose three definitional clauses are attributed to that node. Map each resolution step to one
paid RES node. Source clauses are free in $\Rmix$. This gives
$w_{\Rmix}(F)\le 2\,\lvert\pi_{\mathrm{ER}}\rvert$. Composing with the polynomial EF-to-ER
simulation \cite{krajicek1995} yields the EF statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\status{KNOWN THEOREM, literature check this run} No superpolynomial lower bound is known even
for Frege. The newest results this run found are restricted: tree-like semantic Frege with bounded
line size and tree-like threshold systems \cite{drengr2026}, AC$^0[p]$-Frege for a DNF family
\cite{lst2025}, and Res(log) formalising bounded-depth Frege lower bounds \cite{davisrobere2026}.
2026 surveys state explicitly that Frege lower bounds remain missing.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Where $\Rmix$ sits]
\status{PROVED/EXACT, from Theorems~\ref{thm:sandwich}--\ref{thm:floor}} For the historical calculus:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\textsc{LB}(\Rmix)$ is at least as hard as an Extended Frege lower bound, which is beyond
current technique.
\item $\textsc{Bridge}(\Rmix)$ follows from FS-PV: \emph{if $\SAT\in\mathrm P$, some polynomial-time
SAT decider has PV/$S^1_2$-provable rejection soundness} (LSPV-1/357, via Cook's translation).
FS-PV is OPEN. LSPV-1/357 and /415 show that it cannot hold for \emph{all} correct deciders, by
Gödel and $\Pi^0_1$ arguments. Whether it holds for \emph{some} decider is open.
\end{itemize}
So with $R=\Rmix$, the program trades a bridge that is plausibly reachable conditionally for a
lower bound at least as hard as the central open problem of proof complexity. With $R$ =
Resolution or another weak system, it trades a known lower bound for a bridge equivalent to
$\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$. No choice of $R$ along the p-simulation order makes both halves
easier than their extremes. This is a diagnosis, not an impossibility theorem: a single
non-relativizing argument proving both halves at once is not excluded.
\end{corollary}

\section{Role-by-role findings}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{General SAT lower-bound lead.} The weakest sufficient bridge is $\textsc{Bridge}(R)$ for
the \emph{strongest} $R$ one is willing to lower-bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:sandwich}(4)). For
$\Rmix$, the cleanest sufficient hypothesis is FS-PV. Next target: an \emph{unconditional} theorem
of the form ``$\SAT\in\mathrm P\Rightarrow$ FS-PV for some decider''. Equivalent formulations to
compare against: Krajíček--Pudlák (E=NE $\Rightarrow$ p-optimal systems exist) and the
Köbler--Messner--Torán refinements \cite{kp1989,kmt2003}. Under $\SAT\in\mathrm P$, E=NE holds, so
a p-optimal system exists. The open question is whether $\Rmix$ p-simulates it. This is
\status{OPEN}.
\item \textbf{SAT--circuit reduction builder.} Theorem~\ref{thm:floor} needs only RES+DEF. The
GF(2)/AND3/CONVERT rules matter only for the \emph{upper-bound controls} (Tseitin). Certifying
shortest derivation DAGs is possible only at toy size; see the timeouts above.
\item \textbf{Hypergraph \& proof certificates.} Above, PHP and Tseitin are retired as $\Rmix$-hard
families. Random 3-CNF in the window $n\ll m\ll n^{3/2}$ is the sole CANDIDATE. Its $\Rmix$ hardness
is an EF-strength conjecture.
\item \textbf{Time--space auditor.} The cost model matters only up to polynomial factors in
Theorems~\ref{thm:sandwich}--\ref{thm:floor}. The factor $2$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:floor} is exact
under unit DEF and RES charges with free sources. Bit-length encoding adds an
$O(\log)$ factor per node.
\item \textbf{Red team.} Item (3) of Theorem~\ref{thm:sandwich} is the main hostile finding. Any
proposed bridge for Resolution, CP, PC or bounded-depth Frege is P$\ne$NP restated. Any proposed
lower bound for $\Rmix$ must be checked against Theorem~\ref{thm:floor}: if it would not also
prove an EF lower bound, it contains an error.
\end{enumerate}
