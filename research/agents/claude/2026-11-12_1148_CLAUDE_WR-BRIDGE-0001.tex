\section{Objects}
For a Cook--Reckhow proof system $R$ with cost measure $\mathrm{cost}_R$ (size, unless
stated otherwise),
\[
\overline W_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},\,|\langle F\rangle|\le N}\ \min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi).
\]
The inner minimum ranges over \emph{all} derivations, so the optimal sequence is free. That
makes $\overline W_R$ a valid lower bound on the cost of \emph{any} $R$-derivation of $F$.
This principle is correct and is adopted. The load-bearing question is different: does an
arbitrary polynomial-time SAT algorithm produce, or get simulated by, an $R$-derivation?
\[
(\mathrm B_R)\qquad \mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\ \Longrightarrow\ \overline W_R(N)\le N^{O(1)}.
\]
In the 3-SAT setting, the DAG hypergraph and reduced-state formulation is a choice of $R$
together with $\mathrm{cost}_R$. Everything below applies to it once $R$ is fixed and
polynomially verifiable.

\section{Results}
\begin{theorem}[EXACT: collapse of the bridge for systems with known lower bounds]\label{thm:collapse}
Suppose $R$ satisfies $\overline W_R(N)\ne N^{O(1)}$ as a KNOWN THEOREM. Examples are
tree-like and general resolution (Haken 1985 for PHP; Urquhart, Ben-Sasson--Wigderson for
Tseitin and random 3-CNF), cutting planes (Pudl\'ak 1997), bounded-depth Frege (Ajtai;
Beame--Impagliazzo--Pitassi; H\aa stad--Risse), and polynomial calculus in degree or size.
Then $\mathrm B_R\iff \mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm P$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\mathrm B_R$ has the form $A\Rightarrow C$, where $C$ is known to be false. Hence
$\mathrm B_R\iff\neg A$.
\end{proof}
So for every system where hard UNSAT families are actually known, the ``weakest sufficient
bridge'' is \emph{literally equivalent} to $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$. It is a
reformulation, in the world-ledger sense (row category ``equivalent of P$\neq$NP''), and not
a weaker intermediate target. This includes 3-SAT encodings of these families with
extension variables of constant width. Any new hard family that work packages 3--5 certify
for such an $R$ therefore cannot lower the difficulty of $\mathrm B_R$.

\begin{proposition}[EXACT: family-locality barrier]\label{prop:local}
Let $\{F_n\}$ be any UNSAT family whose members are recognisable in polynomial time
(PHP$^{n+1}_n$, Tseitin on an explicit expander, or any explicit construction from this
corpus). If $A$ is a polynomial-time SAT decider, then so is
$A'(F)=$``if $F\in\{F_n\}$ output UNSAT, else $A(F)$''. On $F_n$, $A'$ runs in linear time
and executes no inference steps at all.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$A'$ is correct because every $F_n$ is UNSAT. Its running time is at most the
recognition time plus the running time of $A$.
\end{proof}
Consequence: no proof of $\mathrm B_R$ can lower-bound an algorithm's running time by
inspecting its behaviour on the hard family. The bridge must use $A$'s correctness on
\emph{all} inputs. Concretely, it must produce short $R$-proofs of $\neg F_n$ from a short
proof that $A$ is sound. This is a \emph{reflection} statement, and it is exactly the form
of the known positive result:

\begin{theorem}[KNOWN THEOREM: Cook 1975; Krajíček--Pudlák 1990]\label{thm:pv}
Suppose $A$ is polynomial-time and $\mathrm{PV}$ (equivalently $S^1_2$ for the
$\forall\Sigma^b_1$ consequences used here) proves
$\mathrm{Sound}(A)\equiv\forall F\,(A(F)=\mathrm{UNSAT}\to F\text{ unsatisfiable})$. Then
extended Frege has refutations of size $|F|^{O(1)}$ for every $F$ that $A$ rejects. In
particular, if $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P$ via such an $A$, then
$\overline W_{\mathrm{EF}}(N)\le N^{O(1)}$.
\end{theorem}
So $\mathrm B_{\mathrm{EF}}$ holds \textbf{CONDITIONALLY}, restricted to SAT algorithms
whose soundness is PV-provable. The precise remaining gap is:

\begin{definition}[OPEN target GAP-PV]
Show that if $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P$, then some polynomial-time SAT algorithm has
PV-provable soundness. (Correctness on satisfiable inputs is free, because it can be
certified by self-reduction. Only the UNSAT direction matters.)
\end{definition}
GAP-PV together with Theorem~\ref{thm:pv} gives $\mathrm B_{\mathrm{EF}}$. That would leave
the superpolynomial EF lower bound, which is OPEN with no unrestricted Frege or EF bound
known as of this search, as the sole open arrow. GAP-PV is unprovability-flavoured: it says
PV cannot be ``too weak'' about a true $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$. It is CANDIDATE as a
research direction, not a claim. Natural relativising arguments cannot settle it, since
oracle worlds separate provability from truth.

\begin{remark}[Proof-system choice is forced into a dilemma]
Pick $R$ to make $\mathrm B_R$ easy, for example by adding a rule ``apply any PV-verified
poly-time refuter''. Then $R$ p-simulates EF, and $\overline W_R$ lower bounds are at least
as hard as EF lower bounds. Pick $R$ weak, so that lower bounds are known. Then
Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} applies. No fixed multisign-hypergraph calculus escapes this
dilemma unless it comes with a new argument of reflection type.
FAILED TRANSFER: ``free optimal sequence $\Rightarrow$ algorithm lower bound'' is valid only
after a p-simulation of the algorithm by $R$. That simulation is the entire content of the
bridge.
\end{remark}
