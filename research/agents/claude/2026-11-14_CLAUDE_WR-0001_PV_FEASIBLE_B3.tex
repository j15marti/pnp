\section{Target}
The shared directive (\path{research/coordination/2026-11-02_1204_WORST_CASE_MINIMUM_DERIVATION_FIVE_ROLE_REFOCUS.md})
fixes a sound complete calculus $R$ and studies
\[
 \Wbar_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},\ |\langle F\rangle|\le N}\
 \min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi),
\]
with free choice of the optimal derivation. P versus NP would follow from
\textbf{B1} ($\Wbar_R$ superpolynomial) together with
\textbf{B3} (SAT$\in$P $\Rightarrow$ $\Wbar_R(N)\le\mathrm{poly}(N)$).

The corpus already records (REFEREE365; LSPV-1/592) that once $R$ contains
Resolution and paid fresh AND2 definitions with constant weights, $W_R$ is
polynomially related to Extended Frege (EF) size, so that B1 is the
superpolynomial-EF-lower-bound problem and B3 is the open implication
$\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\Rightarrow\mathrm{EF}$ p-bounded. This run asks a
narrower question: \emph{what does each half buy on its own}, and how close is
B3 to a known theorem?

\section{Results}

Let $\psi_{\mathrm{SAT}}(x,y)$ be the PV formula ``$y$ satisfies the CNF
encoded by $x$'', and for a PV function symbol $f$ let
\[
 \varphi_{\mathrm{P=NP}}(f)\;:=\;\forall x\,\forall y\,
 [\psi_{\mathrm{SAT}}(x,y)\rightarrow\psi_{\mathrm{SAT}}(x,f(x))]
\]
(the formalization in Bydžovský--Krajíček--Oliveira 2020, App.~A, eq.~(A.1)).

\begin{theorem}[KNOWN THEOREM: Cook 1975 translation, search form]
If $\mathrm{PV}\vdash\varphi_{\mathrm{P=NP}}(f)$ for some PV symbol $f$, then
there are a polynomial $q$ and a polynomial-time map $G$ such that for every
UNSAT CNF $F$ with $|\langle F\rangle|=N$, $G(F)$ is an EF refutation of $F$
of size at most $q(N)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$\varphi_{\mathrm{P=NP}}(f)$ is a universal PV sentence, so Cook's translation
gives EF proofs of its propositional translations
$\|\varphi\|_{n}$ of size $\mathrm{poly}(n)$, constructible in time
$\mathrm{poly}(n)$; extension atoms name the gates of the circuit computing $f$
on length-$n$ inputs. Substitute the constant bits of $\langle F\rangle$ for $x$
(substitution and constant simplification are polynomial in EF). The
circuit for $f$ on a constant input is evaluated gate by gate, giving an EF
proof that $f(\langle F\rangle)$ equals a concrete string $a$; since $F$ is
UNSAT, EF proves $\neg\psi_{\mathrm{SAT}}(\langle F\rangle,a)$ by evaluation.
Modus ponens yields $\neg\psi_{\mathrm{SAT}}(\langle F\rangle,y)$, and with
the constant $x$ this formula is provably equivalent, in polynomial size, to
$\neg\bigwedge_{C\in F}C(y)$. Every step is uniform in $F$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CONDITIONAL composition]
Let $R$ satisfy the LSPV-1/592 hypotheses (arbitrary authenticated CNF
sources, fresh paid AND2 definitions with their three facets, unrestricted
binary Resolution, fixed positive constant weights, shared-DAG payment). If
$\mathrm{PV}\vdash\varphi_{\mathrm{P=NP}}(f)$ for some $f$, then there is a
polynomial $r$ such that $\Wbar_R(N)\le r(N)$ for all $N$, and an optimal-up-to-polynomial
$R$-refutation is produced by a deterministic polynomial-time generator.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~1 gives EF refutations of size $q(N)$ in polynomial time. EF and
Extended Resolution p-simulate each other (Cook--Reckhow 1979), and extension
formulas reduce to fan-in-two AND/NOT gates with polynomial overhead. LSPV-1/592,
Theorem~592.1, compiles an AND2-normalized ER refutation into an $R$-refutation
with $W_R=w_Dg+w_Rr$, polynomial in its size. Composition of polynomial-time
maps is polynomial time.
\end{proof}

The generator form matches the B3-equivalent of LSPV-1/466 (deterministic
same-$R$ proof generator), so under the PV hypothesis B3 holds in its strongest,
constructive form.

\begin{corollary}[B1 alone is not empty]
If B1 is proved for any $R$ satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem~2, then for no
PV symbol $f$ does PV prove $\varphi_{\mathrm{P=NP}}(f)$. That is,
Conjecture~A.1 of Bydžovský--Krajíček--Oliveira holds: P$\neq$NP is consistent
with PV, and by the conservation results they cite, with $S^1_2$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Contrapositive of Theorem~2: a PV proof of $\varphi_{\mathrm{P=NP}}(f)$ would
make $\Wbar_R$ polynomially bounded, contradicting B1.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where exactly B3 sits]
For EF-equivalent $R$, B3 is the implication P$=$NP $\Rightarrow$ EF p-bounded.
Theorem~1 proves it when ``P$=$NP'' is replaced by ``PV proves
$\varphi_{\mathrm{P=NP}}(f)$ for some $f$''. The residual B3 gap is therefore
exactly the gap between \emph{truth} of $\varphi_{\mathrm{P=NP}}(f)$ for some
polynomial-time $f$ and its \emph{PV-provability} for some (possibly different)
polynomial-time $f$. This is a feasible-reflection principle, not a
consequence of any known theorem.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to LSPV-1/357]
LSPV-1/357 shows any \emph{given} correct decider can be modified into an
extensionally equal one whose rejection soundness is unprovable. That is a
per-algorithm firewall. Theorem~1 needs only \emph{some} $f$ with a PV proof,
so 357 does not refute the hypothesis of Theorem~2. Conversely, Theorem~2
does not prove B3: its hypothesis is strictly stronger than SAT$\in$P as far
as is known.
\end{remark}
