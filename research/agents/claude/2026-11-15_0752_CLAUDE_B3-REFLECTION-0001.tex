\section{Target and quantifiers}
Fix the shared directive's objects. $R$ is one preselected sound complete calculus with
unit-weight paid nodes; $W_R(F)=\min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$ grants the
optimal derivation for free; $\overline W_R(N)=\max\{W_R(F): F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}, |\langle F\rangle|\le N\}$.
The primary bridge is
\[
\textbf{B3}(R):\qquad \mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists C,k\ \forall N:\ \overline W_R(N)\le C(N+1)^k .
\]
Let $C$ denote CANON2R. REFEREE365 proves fixed polynomials $p,r$ with
$s_\EF(F)\le p(|F|+W_C(F))$ and $W_C(F)\le r(|F|+s_\EF(F))$ for every UNSAT CNF $F$.
Hence $\overline W_C$ is polynomially bounded iff $\EF$ is polynomially bounded, and
B3$(C)$ is the open implication $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\Rightarrow \EF$ p-bounded.
The question addressed here: \emph{what exactly must a proof of B3$(C)$ establish?}
The corpus (LSPV-1/337 finite reflection, LSPV-1/357 G\"odel firewall, Codex 2026-11-11
Pich--Santhanam narrowing) has \emph{sufficient} reflection conditions. This report proves the
matching \emph{necessary} condition, so the reflection target is exact, not merely sufficient.

\section{Definitions}
For a deterministic decider $A$ with explicit clock $t_A(n)=c_A(n+1)^{k_A}$, let
$Q_A$ be the Cook--Reckhow refutation system: $(F,\pi)$ is accepted iff $\pi=\varepsilon$ and the
clocked run of $A$ on $\langle F\rangle$ halts within $t_A(|\langle F\rangle|)$ steps with output UNSAT.
For every $n$ let $\RS_A^{(n)}$ be the standard propositional translation (Cook 1975) of
\[
\forall F\in\{0,1\}^n\ \forall \alpha:\quad \neg\bigl(Q_A\text{ accepts }(F,\varepsilon)\ \wedge\ \alpha\models F\bigr),
\]
using a fixed tableau circuit for the clocked machine, encoded with extension variables;
$|\RS_A^{(n)}|\le q_A(n)$ for a polynomial $q_A$ depending only on $A$ and its clock.
$\RS_A^{(n)}$ is a tautology iff $A$ never wrongly rejects a satisfiable length-$n$ input within its clock.
For a theory $T$ with polynomial-time proof predicate, $\|\mathrm{Con}_T(n)\|$ is the propositional
translation of ``no $p\in\{0,1\}^n$ is a $T$-proof of $0=1$'', of size $\mathrm{poly}(n)$.

\begin{lemma}[KNOWN THEOREM: reflection gives simulation; Cook 1975, Kraj\'{\i}\v{c}ek--Pudl\'ak 1989]\label{lem:sim}
If $\EF$ has proofs of $\RS_A^{(n)}$ of size $\le s(n)$, then every $F$ accepted by $Q_A$ has an
$\EF$ refutation of size $\mathrm{poly}(s(|F|)+t_A(|F|))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Substitute the bits of $\langle F\rangle$ into $\RS_A^{(|F|)}$; $\EF$ evaluates the constant tableau
(size $O(t_A^2)$) to certify ``$Q_A$ accepts $F$'', derives $\neg(\alpha\models F)$ for the
variables $\alpha$, and the CNF $F$ itself then yields $\bot$ by polynomially many steps.
\end{proof}

\section{Main theorem}
\begin{theorem}[B3 reflection equivalence for CANON2R]\label{thm:main}
The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] B3$(C)$.
\item[(ii)] If $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P$ then for \emph{every} total correct polynomially clocked
decider $A$ there is a polynomial $s_A$ with $\EF$-proofs of $\RS_A^{(n)}$ of size $\le s_A(n)$.
\item[(iii)] If $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P$ then for \emph{some} total correct polynomially clocked $A$
there is such a polynomial $s_A$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii). Assume SAT$\in$P and B3$(C)$; then $\overline W_C(N)\le C(N+1)^k$, so by
REFEREE365 $s_\EF(G)\le p(|G|+C(|G|+1)^k)$ for every UNSAT CNF $G$. Let $A$ be correct and
polynomially clocked. $\RS_A^{(n)}$ is a tautology of size $\le q_A(n)$ (correctness of $A$),
so the Tseitin CNF $G_n$ of its negation is UNSAT with $|G_n|=O(q_A(n)\log q_A(n))$ and has an
$\EF$ refutation of polynomial size; this is an $\EF$ proof of $\RS_A^{(n)}$ up to the standard
polynomial Tseitin overhead.
(ii)$\Rightarrow$(iii). SAT$\in$P supplies at least one correct polynomially clocked $A$
(e.g.\ any polynomial-time decider with its own clock; correctness is a truth, not a provability, condition).
(iii)$\Rightarrow$(i). Assume SAT$\in$P and fix the $A$ of (iii). $A$ is correct, so every UNSAT $F$ is
accepted by $Q_A$; Lemma~\ref{lem:sim} gives $s_\EF(F)\le\mathrm{poly}(s_A(|F|)+t_A(|F|))$, and REFEREE365
gives $W_C(F)\le r(|F|+s_\EF(F))$, polynomial in $|F|$ uniformly. Hence $\overline W_C$ is polynomially bounded.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Necessary finite-consistency consequence]\label{cor:con}
If B3$(C)$ holds and $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P$, then for every consistent theory $T$ with a
polynomial-time proof predicate (e.g.\ PA, ZFC), $\|\mathrm{Con}_T(n)\|$ has $\EF$ proofs of size $\mathrm{poly}_T(n)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Under the hypotheses $\EF$ is p-bounded (proof of (i)$\Rightarrow$(ii)); $\|\mathrm{Con}_T(n)\|$ is a
polynomial-size tautology. Alternatively, apply (ii) to the LSPV-1/357 G\"odel wrapper $A_T$ around any
polynomial-time decider: $A_T$ is extensionally correct because $T$ is consistent, and $\RS_{A_T}^{(n)}$
contains $\|\mathrm{Con}_T(n')\|$ for $n'=\Theta(n)$ as a substitution instance.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[B3 as an optimality statement]\label{cor:opt}
B3$(C)\iff[\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\Rightarrow\EF\text{ is an optimal proof system}]$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$ then $Q_A$ is a p-bounded system for a correct polynomial-time $A$. A system
simulating a p-bounded system is p-bounded, and a p-bounded system simulates every system. So under
$\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, $\EF$ p-bounded $\iff$ $\EF$ optimal; combine with REFEREE365.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What this changes]
\begin{enumerate}
\item The LSPV-1/357 G\"odel firewall showed no single theory $T$ can prove \emph{arithmetic} rejection
soundness of every correct decider. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} shows the relevant object is the
\emph{nonuniform propositional} family $\RS_A^{(n)}$, and that short $\EF$ proofs of it are not just
one route to B3 but \emph{forced} by B3. There is no weaker bridge for this $R$: any proof of B3$(C)$
must, under the hypothesis $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, produce short $\EF$ proofs of finite consistency of ZFC.
This answers LSPV-1/357's NEXT item (``seek a weaker formal reflection condition derivable from
SAT$\in$P for some P-decider''): the ``some'' and ``every'' versions are equivalent, and neither is
weaker than B3.
\item Kraj\'{\i}\v{c}ek--Pudl\'ak (1989) show optimal proof systems exist if $\mathrm{NE}=\mathrm{coNE}$,
which follows from $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$; but the optimal system they construct depends on the
hypothesis and is not the preselected CANON2R. Corollary~\ref{cor:opt} isolates precisely that gap.
\item Pich--Santhanam (ECCC TR23-199) give hypotheses (polynomial-time witnessing of errors of small
circuits failing SAT, or a learning reduction) under which ``$\EF$ not p-bounded $\Rightarrow$
P$\ne$NP''. That is a CONDITIONAL B3$(C)$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, each such hypothesis must imply,
under P$=$NP, short $\EF$ proofs of $\RS_A^{(n)}$; this is a consistency check on their hypotheses, not
a new result about them.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Weak calculi: squeeze, ADOPTED from root main.tex line on Resolution]
For any fixed $R$ with a \emph{proved} superpolynomial $\overline W_R$ (Resolution via Haken; cutting
planes; Res(lin) on suitable families), B3$(R)$ is equivalent to SAT$\notin$P, because its consequent
is false. So the bridge is never easier than the separation for a calculus whose lower bound is already
known. For CANON2R the lower bound is the open $\EF$ p-boundedness problem, and the bridge is the
open optimality statement of Corollary~\ref{cor:opt}. Both halves are open; the program has not reduced
P versus NP to anything known to be easier.
\end{remark}
