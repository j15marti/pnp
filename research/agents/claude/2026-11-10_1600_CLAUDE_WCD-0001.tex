\section{Shared target}
For a fixed sound, complete, polynomial-time-checkable refutation system $R$ with
cost function $\mathrm{cost}_R$,
\[
\overline W_R(N)=\max_{\substack{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}\\ |\langle F\rangle|\le N}}
\ \min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi),
\qquad
\textbf{(B3$_R$)}\quad \mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\ \Longrightarrow\ \overline W_R(N)\le\mathrm{poly}(N).
\]
Write $\mathrm{LB}_R$ for the statement ``$\overline W_R(N)\ge N^{\omega(1)}$''.
Then $\mathrm{LB}_R\wedge\mathrm{B3}_R\Rightarrow\mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm P$.
The free-sequence principle is correct: $\overline W_R$ is a minimum over all
derivations, so no charge for strategy search is needed. Any process that
\emph{executes} an $R$-derivation pays at least $\overline W_R$ on the worst input.
The open question is whether an arbitrary algorithm must execute one. That
question is exactly $\mathrm{B3}_R$.

\section{Results}
\begin{theorem}[Bridge collapse; PROVED/EXACT, elementary]
Let $R$ be any fixed system for which $\mathrm{LB}_R$ is an established theorem.
Then $\mathrm{B3}_R$ is provably equivalent to $\mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm P$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
($\Rightarrow$) $\mathrm{B3}_R$ and the theorem $\mathrm{LB}_R$ give $\mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm P$.
($\Leftarrow$) If $\mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm P$, then $\mathrm{B3}_R$ holds vacuously.
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Consequence (KNOWN THEOREM inputs).} The following are known
theorems for unrestricted DAG proofs:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{LB}_R$ for Resolution: Haken (PHP), Urquhart (Tseitin), and Chv\'atal--Szemer\'edi (random 3-CNF);
\item $\mathrm{LB}_R$ for tree-like Resolution;
\item $\mathrm{LB}_R$ for Cutting Planes (Pudl\'ak, clique--colouring);
\item $\mathrm{LB}_R$ for bounded-depth Frege (Ajtai; Kraj\'\i\v{c}ek--Pudl\'ak--Woods; Pitassi--Beame--Impagliazzo);
\item $\mathrm{LB}_R$ for Polynomial Calculus (Razborov; Ben-Sasson--Impagliazzo).
\end{itemize}
For each of these systems, the ``weakest sufficient bridge'' is not weaker than P $\neq$ NP. It
is the same statement. A route through one of these systems therefore makes no
progress unless the bridge proof also proves P $\neq$ NP outright. The
project's earlier pointwise counterexamples (LSPV-1/392: a robust Tseitin/PHP
recognizer) are the pointwise shadow of this fact.

\begin{theorem}[Anti-monotonicity; PROVED/EXACT, elementary]
Suppose $R'$ polynomially simulates $R$ \emph{in cost}: each $R$-refutation
$\pi$ of $F$ yields an $R'$-refutation of $F$ of cost at most
$p(|\langle F\rangle|+\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi))$. Then
$\mathrm{LB}_{R'}\Rightarrow\mathrm{LB}_R$ and $\mathrm{B3}_R\Rightarrow\mathrm{B3}_{R'}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\overline W_{R'}(N)\le p(N+\overline W_R(N))$. Superpolynomial $\overline W_{R'}$ therefore forces
superpolynomial $\overline W_R$. Polynomial $\overline W_R$ forces polynomial $\overline W_{R'}$.
Apply the latter under the hypothesis $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P$.
\end{proof}
\noindent So strengthening $R$ makes the bridge easier and the lower bound harder.
A closing pair must lie in the region where both $\mathrm{LB}_R$ and $\mathrm{B3}_R$ are
currently OPEN. That region includes Frege, Extended Frege/ER, Res($\oplus$)
(no superpolynomial DAG bound as of ECCC TR26-007, TR26-018, and TR25-188), and the
project's $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$. Under $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P$ and byte cost,
root REFEREE357 already shows that $\mathrm{B3}_R$ is equivalent to p-optimality of $R$.
Combining the two results, the program needs a fixed $R$ satisfying both of the following:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] an unconditional superpolynomial lower bound;
\item[(ii)] P $=$ NP $\Rightarrow$ $R$ is p-optimal.
\end{itemize}
No natural system is known to satisfy (ii). A literature search this run found no
result giving a \emph{fixed} system that is p-optimal under P $=$ NP. Messner's
result concerns other sets, and Kraj\'\i\v{c}ek--Pudl\'ak's concerns E $=$ NE. The
existence of p-optimal systems is OPEN and fails relative to an oracle.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional EF bridge; KNOWN THEOREM, Cook 1975 / Kraj\'\i\v{c}ek]
If a polynomial-time algorithm $A$ satisfies
$\mathrm{PV}\vdash\forall x\,(A(x)=0\rightarrow x\notin\mathrm{SAT})$ and is complete
for UNSAT, then $\overline W_{\mathrm{EF}}(N)\le\mathrm{poly}(N)$. Hence
$\mathrm{LB}_{\mathrm{EF}}$ would imply that no polynomial SAT algorithm is
PV-provably correct. This is a known half-win (cf.\ world-ledger
classification), not P $\neq$ NP.
\end{theorem}
\noindent The gap between this result and $\mathrm{B3}_{\mathrm{EF}}$ is exactly
\emph{provability of correctness}. This matches the corpus finding that adding a
correctness/reflection axiom ``merely hides the missing proof'' (REFEREE357/B3 audit).

\section{Red-team: adaptive strategy switching}
For systems $R_1,R_2$, let $R_1\cup R_2$ accept a proof from either system. Then
$W_{R_1\cup R_2}(F)=\min(W_{R_1}(F),W_{R_2}(F))$, so a hard family must be hard for
every allowed method at once. Known controls show that single families fail this
test:
\begin{itemize}
\item PHP is Resolution-hard but has polynomial CP and ER refutations.
\item Tseitin is Resolution-hard but has native GF(2) proofs (exact $4h-1$ in $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$) and quasipolynomial CP proofs (Dadush--Tiwari).
\end{itemize}
Conjoining hard families on disjoint variables makes the instance \emph{easier}
under switching, because the minimum is taken over components. A disjunctive
combination $F_1\vee F_2$ (CNF-ified with selectors) needs both refuted, but
inherits only the \emph{weaker} of the two lower bounds per system. It is
therefore no harder than $\max$ over systems of $\min$ of components, and gives no
new hardness against a system that refutes both cheaply (for example ER).
\textbf{Status:} the claim that combination by itself defeats switching is FALSIFIED/RETIRED. Any
candidate must be checked against ER/EF directly.
