\paragraph{Status.} P versus NP remains \textbf{OPEN}. Nothing below is superlinear;
the ladder so far records exact small cases and the linear ``bookkeeping'' bounds,
with their limits made explicit. Labels: PROVED/EXACT, KNOWN THEOREM, COMPUTATIONAL,
CONDITIONAL, CANDIDATE, OPEN, FALSIFIED/RETIRED.

\section{Model and parameters}
A circuit $C$ has $n$ inputs, $g$ binary gates (fan-in 2, any of the 16 binary
functions, so negation is free), one output; \emph{wires} $m=2g$. \CSAT\ asks whether
some $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ has $C(x)=1$. Lower bounds are tracked as functions of the
parameters $(n,g,m)$ and, later, structural parameters (treewidth/pathwidth $w$,
depth $d$, fan-out $\Delta$). Two distinct quantities are kept apart:
\begin{itemize}
\item $W(f)=2\cdot$(min gates computing $f$): wires needed to \emph{build} $f$ (circuit complexity);
\item $T(m)$: time to \emph{decide} \CSAT\ on instances of $m$ wires (algorithmic complexity).
\end{itemize}

\section{Ladder, build side (COMPUTATIONAL, exact)}
Script and data: \texttt{mincirc.py}, \texttt{mincirc\_results.json} (exhaustive BFS over
sets of computed truth tables; runs in seconds). Number of $n$-input functions whose
minimum circuit has exactly $k$ gates:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}\toprule
$n$ & $k{=}0$ & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4\\\midrule
1 & 4 & & & & \\ 2 & 6 & 10 & & & \\ 3 & 8 & 30 & 114 & 80 & 24\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
Hence max gates $=0,1,4$ for $n=1,2,3$. Functions with exactly one satisfying point
(the hardest-to-satisfy-yet-satisfiable class) need $0,1,2=n-1$ gates; functions with
exactly two satisfying points need up to $3$ gates at $n=3$.

\begin{lem}[PROVED/EXACT, connectivity] A circuit with $g$ fan-in-2 gates depends on at most $g+1$
inputs. So any $f$ depending on all $n$ inputs has $W(f)\ge 2(n-1)$; this is tight for
$\mathrm{AND}$ of literals (unique-SAT), matching the computation at $n\le3$.
\end{lem}
\emph{Proof.} Induct: the reachable-input set of a circuit grows by at most one per gate
(each gate merges two sub-DAGs; start from $g=0$ with $\le1$ input). \qed

\section{Decide side, linear reading bound (PROVED/EXACT)}
\begin{prop}[Reading bound, parametrized by $m$] In the random-access model with instances
encoded wire-by-wire, every correct deterministic \CSAT\ algorithm makes $\Omega(m)$
probes on some instance with $m$ wires, for all $m\ge 4$.
\end{prop}
\emph{Proof.} Take $n=\lfloor m/4\rfloor$-ish: $C_0=\bigwedge_{i\le n} g_i$, $g_i=x_i\wedge y_i$ with $y_i$
wired to $x_i$ (polarity bit $+$). $C_0$ is satisfiable. $C^{(j)}$ flips the polarity bit of
$y_j$ ($g_j=x_j\wedge\neg x_j\equiv0$), so is unsatisfiable. An algorithm that on $C_0$
never probes the polarity bit of $y_j$ gives the same answer on $C^{(j)}$; so it must probe
all $n=\Theta(m)$ polarity bits. \qed

This is the only decide-side bound proved here; it is the trivial analogue of reading the input.

\section{Parametrized view}
\begin{itemize}
\item KNOWN THEOREM: \CSAT\ with treewidth $w$ is solvable in $2^{O(w)}\,m$.
\item CONDITIONAL (SETH; cited from memory, to be re-verified against primary sources): no $(2-\varepsilon)^{w}\mathrm{poly}(m)$
algorithm for CNF-SAT of primal pathwidth $w$. This would be the matching parametrized
lower bound, but it is conditional and does not touch P vs NP.
\item KNOWN THEOREM (cited from memory): SAT needs time $n^{2\cos(\pi/7)-o(1)}\approx n^{1.80}$ on
machines with $n^{o(1)}$ space (Williams; repo ledger row L13 says alternation-trading
cannot reach exactly this exponent with such proofs). Relevant repository work:
\texttt{research/agents/claude/world-ledger/}, \texttt{tport/} (barrier L34: Williams-style
proofs for the UWI portfolio stall at linear time when the portfolio has $\approx N$ programs).
\item Explicit-circuit side, KNOWN (from memory): best general-basis explicit lower bound is
$\approx 3.1n-o(n)$ gates (Li--Yang 2022). Anything above linear for an NP function is OPEN.
\end{itemize}

\section{Barriers (what the ladder cannot do by itself)}
Counting/connectivity arguments give only $\Theta(n)$ wires. Superlinear bounds for NP
need techniques that evade natural proofs, relativization and algebrization; wire counting
at fixed small $n$ is exact but does not extrapolate: from $n=3$ no growth law is implied.
Classification: small-case tables are COMPUTATIONAL data, not evidence for an asymptotic law.

\section{Next rungs (CANDIDATE plan)}

\item Track $W_{\min}(n,s)$: fewest wires of a circuit with exactly $s$ satisfying points, $s=1,2,\dots,2^n$;

\item Cross-check each rung against repository ARC/FLMV time--space spine for any usable link.
\end{enumerate}
