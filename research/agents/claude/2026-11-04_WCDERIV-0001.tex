\title{Worst-case derivation cost: the bridge $B_R$ is equivalent to its conclusion}

\noindent\textbf{Status: P versus NP remains OPEN.} Nothing below proves a lower bound.

\section{Setting}
For a fixed propositional proof system $R$ with finitely many rules, let
$\overline W_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},|F|\le N}\min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$.
With cost $=$ number of DAG nodes (up to polynomial factors) this is exactly the proof-size function $S_R(N)$.
Granting free knowledge of the best $\pi$ is harmless: $\overline W_R$ is nondeterministic (coNP-type) complexity.
(KNOWN THEOREM, Cook--Reckhow 1979.)

\section{Results}
Let $B_R$ denote the bridge ``$\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm{P}\Rightarrow\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$''.

\begin{prop}[PROVED/EXACT, trivial] If $\overline W_R$ is superpolynomial then $B_R\iff \mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm{P}$.
\end{prop}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} The consequent of $B_R$ is false, so $B_R$ is equivalent to the negation of its antecedent. $\square$

\textbf{Consequence.} A bridge proved \emph{given} the lower bound is the conclusion $\mathrm{P}\ne\mathrm{NP}$. The only non-circular route
is a simulation theorem proved \emph{without} using the lower bound: from a poly-time decider $M$ build short $R$-derivations.
So ``weakest sufficient bridge'' cannot be weaker than a genuine uniform algorithm-to-derivation simulation.

\begin{prop}[KNOWN THEOREM / PROVED] $B_{\mathrm{Res}}\iff \mathrm{P}\ne\mathrm{NP}$ (resolution is unconditionally exponential: Haken 1985; Urquhart; Chv\'atal--Szemer\'edi; Ben-Sasson--Wigderson). Hence resolution (and any system with known unconditional lower bounds) can never carry the bridge, and its lower bounds cannot transfer.
\end{prop}

\begin{prop}[CANDIDATE bridge, Cook/Krajíček-style, formulation not verified here] Let $M$ decide SAT in time $T$. Suppose EF has $\mathrm{poly}(N)$-size proofs of the soundness formulas $\mathrm{Sound}_{M,N}$ (``$M(F)=0\Rightarrow F$ unsatisfiable'', as a propositional formula on the circuit of $M$). Then $\overline W_{EF}(N)\le\mathrm{poly}(N)$. 
\end{prop}
The hypothesis is strictly stronger than $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm{P}$: $M$ may be correct yet unprovably so in EF. This is the genuine gap in $B_{EF}$ and it matches the open status of whether $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$ would be provable in bounded arithmetic. A transfer theorem must therefore either (a) prove that \emph{some} correct $M$ has EF-short soundness proofs, or (b) change $R$ to a system that is closed under adding true $\Pi_1$ reflection (these are not fixed finite-rule systems, so $\overline W_R$ is then no longer a clean combinatorial quantity).

\section{Hard-family audit (from memory, verify before load-bearing use)}
\begin{itemize}
\item Pigeonhole: exponential in Res, polynomial Frege/EF (Buss). Not hard for strong $R$.
\item Tseitin on expanders: exponential in Res, polynomial in Frege/EF. Not hard for strong $R$.
\item Random 3-CNF at constant density: exponential in Res; no superpolynomial Frege/EF bound known. This is the only standard candidate.
\item Superpolynomial EF lower bounds: OPEN; any proof must avoid natural-proof-type barriers (Razborov--Rudich; Pich--Santhanam, ECCC TR23-199, show EF-with-extra-axioms lower bounds imply SAT circuit lower bounds, i.e.\ the hardness is at least as strong as a circuit lower bound for SAT).
\end{itemize}
Classification: ``survives resolution/algebraic/circuit-compression/shared-DAG'' is automatic for shared DAGs (DAG-like proofs are already the size measure); for algebraic systems (PC, IPS) and circuit compression the lower-bound state is: PC/IPS superpolynomial lower bounds known only for restricted variants (not verified here).
