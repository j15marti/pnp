\section{Object}
Fix a Cook--Reckhow system $R$ (polynomial-time verifier, sound and complete for UNSAT 3-CNF).
Let $\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$ be the bit length of $\pi$, or a measure polynomially equivalent to it. Define
\[
 w_R(F)=\min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi),\qquad
 \Wbar_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},\ |\langle F\rangle|\le N} w_R(F).
\]
In the multisign-hypergraph language, the minimal reduced logical state of an UNSAT instance is $\bot$, and
$w_R(F)$ is the minimal work over all valid reduction sequences. The best sequence is granted for free.
Because the search for that sequence is never charged, any lower bound on $w_R$ is a lower bound on any
procedure that \emph{executes an $R$-derivation}. The procedure may discover the derivation however it likes.
That part of the strategy is sound.
The open question is whether a lower bound on $w_R$ also binds machines that never execute an $R$-derivation.
That is exactly the bridge.

\section{Results}
\begin{proposition}[Cook--Reckhow normalisation; EXACT]\label{p1}
$\Wbar_R(N)\le\mathrm{poly}(N)$ iff $R$ is polynomially bounded. Some $R$ has this property iff NP${}={}$coNP.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Known lower bound makes the bridge a reformulation; EXACT]\label{p2}
Suppose $\Wbar_R$ is unconditionally known to be superpolynomial, for example $R=$ Resolution on Tseitin
3-CNFs over expanders (Urquhart 1987) or on random 3-CNFs (Chv\'atal--Szemer\'edi 1988). Then
\[
 B_R:\ \big(\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\Rightarrow \Wbar_R(N)\le\mathrm{poly}(N)\big)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm P .
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$B_R$ has the form $A\Rightarrow Q$ with $\neg Q$ a theorem. Hence $B_R\equiv\neg A$.
\end{proof}
\noindent\emph{Consequence.} The weakest sufficient bridge for Resolution, Polynomial Calculus,
bounded-depth Frege, or any other system with a known lower bound is \emph{itself} P${}\ne{}$NP. In the
world-ledger classification it is a reformulation, not progress. It inherits every barrier that applies
to P vs NP, including relativization (Baker--Gill--Solovay), natural proofs, and algebrization.
So prioritising $B_R$ for such an $R$ does not shrink the problem.

\begin{proposition}[Monotone squeeze; EXACT]\label{p3}
If $R'$ p-simulates $R$, then $\Wbar_{R'}\le\mathrm{poly}(\Wbar_R)$ and $B_R\Rightarrow B_{R'}$.
Conversely, a lower bound for $R'$ implies the same lower bound for $R$, up to a polynomial.
So strengthening $R$ makes the bridge easier and the lower bound harder.
At the weak end (Resolution) the lower bound is known and $B_R\equiv$ P${}\neq{}$NP.
At the strong end, $R_T$ is the system ``$T$-proof that a poly-time algorithm is sound, plus its rejecting
run''. For $R_T$ the bridge holds whenever SAT${}\in{}$P is witnessed by a $T$-provably sound algorithm
(already in the corpus: LSPV-1/337 FINITE-REFLECTION-BRIDGE). The lower bound for $R_T$ says that
$T$ proves no p-bounded system sound (Kraj\'{\i}\v{c}ek--Pudl\'ak 1989). That statement is not known
to imply P${}\neq{}$NP: by LSPV-1/PA-TRAP-GATE, a correct polynomial-time decider can exist whose
correctness $T$ cannot prove. Extended Frege sits in between. By Cook (1975), a PV proof of soundness
translates into polynomial EF refutations, so $B_{EF}$ is CONDITIONAL on PV-provable soundness, and EF lower
bounds are a major open frontier (no general Frege/EF lower bound has been found through 2025).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Pointwise simulation falsified; known-theorem consequence]\label{p4}
Let $A_{\oplus}$ be the total decider that recognises the Tseitin 3-CNF syntax and runs Gaussian elimination
over $\mathbb F_2$ on such inputs, and otherwise runs brute force. $A_\oplus$ is correct and runs in
polynomial time on Tseitin inputs. Resolution needs $2^{\Omega(n)}$ size on Tseitin over bounded-degree
expanders (Urquhart; Ben-Sasson--Wigderson width--size). Hence no instancewise simulation
$w_{\mathrm{Res}}(F)\le\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{time}_A(F))$ holds for all correct $A$. The same holds for
PC over $\mathbb F_p$, $p\neq2$ (Buss--Grigoriev--Impagliazzo--Pitassi) and for bounded-depth Frege
(Ben-Sasson; H\aa stad--Risse 2025).
Random 3-CNF with $m\ge c\,n^{1.4}$ clauses is a second hostile control. It has polynomial-size
TC$^0$-Frege refutations (M\"uller--Tzameret, from the Feige--Kim--Ofek witness), while Resolution needs
exponential size up to $m=n^{1.5-\epsilon}$ (Ben-Sasson--Wigderson). Any bridge must therefore use the
\emph{global} worst case $T_A(N)$, as in the corpus's REFEREE295 scope distinction. A candidate hard
family that must survive strong systems has to stay below density $n^{0.4}$ and must not be linear
(Tseitin/XOR).
\end{proposition}

\section{Role-by-role findings}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{General SAT lower-bound lead.} Propositions~2--3: for every fixed $R$, at least one half
(bridge or lower bound) is at least as hard as a P${}\ne{}$NP-strength statement. The only non-reformulation
regime is an intermediate $R$, such as Frege or EF, where the bridge is conditional on the feasible provability
of soundness and the lower bound is open. Recommended weakest bridge to study:
$B^{\mathrm{PV}}_{EF}$: ``SAT${}\in{}$P $\Rightarrow$ some poly-time SAT algorithm has PV-provable soundness.''
OPEN, and the PA-TRAP-GATE shows it cannot be assumed.
\item \textbf{SAT-circuit reduction builder.} The fixed combined system should be named by its
p-simulation position. A mixed Resolution+GE+circuit calculus is p-simulated by EF, and the squeeze then applies.
Exact shortest \emph{tree-like} Resolution derivations were certified for small Tseitin instances
(Section~5). These are upper bounds on the DAG minimum.
\item \textbf{Hypergraph \& proof certificates.} Tseitin (parity-signed hypergraphs) fails the
``survives alternative reasoning'' test: Gaussian elimination solves it. Random 3-CNF at constant density is
the standard survivor. It has exponential Resolution, PC (Ben-Sasson--Impagliazzo; Alekhnovich--Razborov)
and linear-degree SOS (Grigoriev; Schoenebeck) bounds. Survival in Frege/EF is open.
\item \textbf{Time--space resource auditor.} An $R_T$ proof needs no run transcript. The verifier reruns
$A$ in $|F|^{c}$ steps, so the proof size is $O(|F|)+O_T(1)$ and the verification time is polynomial.
If cost counts lines rather than bits, EF lines must be charged by size.
\item \textbf{Independent red team.} $A_\oplus$ falsifies the pointwise transfer. Proposition~2 shows that
the Resolution bridge is not weaker than the goal. No shorter-derivation counterexample was found in the
small-instance data; the data is consistent with the theory.
\end{enumerate}
