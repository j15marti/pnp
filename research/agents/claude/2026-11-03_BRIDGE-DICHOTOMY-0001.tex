\section*{Setting}
$\overline W_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},|F|\le N}\min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$, strategy discovery uncharged. Let $\mathrm{(B_R)}$ denote the bridge $SAT\in P\Rightarrow\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$ and $\mathrm{(L_R)}$ the lower bound $\overline W_R\not\le\mathrm{poly}$. $(B_R)\wedge(L_R)\Rightarrow SAT\notin P$.

\begin{proposition}[Bridge is vacuous or equivalent to the goal when $L_R$ is already known]
If $(L_R)$ is a theorem (e.g.\ $R$ = Resolution, Haken; Urquhart; Ben-Sasson--Wigderson), then $(B_R)\iff SAT\notin P$. So for such $R$ proving the bridge \emph{is} proving $P\neq NP$; it is not a weaker sufficient step.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Proof.} The conclusion of $(B_R)$ is false, so the implication is equivalent to the negation of its hypothesis. \qed

\begin{proposition}[Free strategy $=$ nondeterminism]
Let $R$ be a Cook--Reckhow system (polytime-checkable proofs, sound, complete) with $\mathrm{cost}=$ size. Then $\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}\Rightarrow NP=coNP$. Conversely if $NP=coNP$ some such $R$ has $\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$. Hence $(L_R)$ for \emph{all} such $R$ is equivalent to $NP\neq coNP$, strictly stronger than $P\neq NP$ unless they coincide.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Proof.} Cook--Reckhow 1979. \qed

\begin{proposition}[Trace systems satisfy the bridge trivially]
If $A$ decides SAT in time $T_A$, the system $R_A$ whose proofs of UNSAT are rejecting runs of $A$ (cost $=$ steps) is Cook--Reckhow and has $\overline W_{R_A}\le T_A$. Dually, if the derivation must be \emph{produced by one fixed uniform procedure}, $\min_\pi\mathrm{cost}$ is exactly deterministic decision cost and $(L_R)$ is $SAT\notin P$ restated.
\end{proposition}

\section*{Consequence: the real content of the weakest bridge}
(KNOWN CONTEXT / CANDIDATE.) For a natural $R$ with \emph{unknown} lower bound (Frege, EF, algebraic, or the repository's $R_{\mathrm{mix}}$), $(B_R)$ is a genuine simulation statement: it requires $R$ to internalize an arbitrary polynomial-time decider \emph{together with a proof of its correctness}. Cook's correspondence gives this only for deciders whose soundness is provable in PV/$S^1_2$ (so EF gets polynomial proofs); it is not known for arbitrary $A$, and an unconditional $(B_{EF})$ would amount to a polynomial-bounded-proof consequence of $SAT\in P$ that is open (EF/Frege are not known to be $p$-optimal; existence of a $p$-optimal system is open, Kraj\'{\i}\v{c}ek--Pudl\'ak). The repository's reflection reports (LSPV-1 336--367) are exactly attempts at this internalization; Report 367 shows the clocked soundness CNF inherits Resolution hardness, i.e.\ reflection pushes the burden onto $(L_R)$, never removing it.

\section*{Program-level rule (adopted, OPEN)}
A candidate pair $(R,\text{lemma})$ is progress only if (i) $(B_R)$ is proved for a $R$ whose lower bound is \emph{not} already known (else Prop.~1), and (ii) $(L_R)$ is a new superpolynomial bound for that same $R$ in a system strictly stronger than Resolution/bounded-depth Frege/Nullstellensatz. Any $R$ failing (i) is a restatement of $P\neq NP$; any $R$ failing (ii) is an unproved bridge to a known-easy-to-lower-bound system, hence vacuous. The sweet spot lies strictly between $\mathrm{AC}^0[p]$-Frege and EF, where $(L_R)$ is open and $(B_R)$ needs a simulation theorem for deterministic algorithms.
