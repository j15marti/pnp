3. Arithmetic error found and confirmed — GROK v2.7 §1's stated Fourier identity for total influence is wrong by a factor of 4
v2.7 §1 states: "total influence Inf(F) = ∑_i Inf_i(F), equivalently the expected sensitivity, equivalently 4 times the sum of |ŝ(S)|² |S| over Fourier coefficients (Kahn–Kalai–Linial; O'Donnell)."
The middle equivalence (total influence = expected sensitivity) is correct — this is the standard identity as(f) = (1/2ⁿ)∑ₓ sens_f(x) = ∑ᵢ Prₓ[f(x)≠f(x⊕eᵢ)] = ∑ᵢ Infᵢ(f). The stated Fourier formula is not correct as written: the exact identity (O'Donnell, Analysis of Boolean Functions, Ch. 2; also derivable directly from Kahn–Kalai–Linial's setup) is
I[f] = ∑_S |S| · f̂(S)²  (no factor of 4).
Checked directly by brute-force truth table + exact Fourier-coefficient computation (±1 convention) against six test functions spanning three families:
| Function | n | Direct Inf(f) (definition) | ∑_S |S|f̂(S)² | 4·∑_S |S|f̂(S)² | |---|---|---|---|---| | XOR | 3 | 3.0000 | 3.0000 | 12.0000 | | XOR | 5 | 5.0000 | 5.0000 | 20.0000 | | Majority | 3 | 1.5000 | 1.5000 | 6.0000 | | Majority | 5 | 1.8750 | 1.8750 | 7.5000 | | Majority | 7 | 2.1875 | 2.1875 | 8.7500 | | AND | 3 | 0.7500 | 0.7500 | 3.0000 |
In every case the direct definition matches ∑_S|S|f̂(S)² exactly (to floating-point precision) and is off by exactly 4× from GROK's stated formula. Status: COUNTEREXAMPLE to the specific stated identity — an arithmetic/transcription error, not a citation-authorship error. This does not touch C74's break conclusion: C74 is falsified by the majority/XOR/padding arguments in §6, none of which depend on the numeric constant in the Fourier identity. Kahn–Kalai–Linial (1988, "The influence of variables on Boolean functions") and O'Donnell's Analysis of Boolean Functions are both real, correctly attributed sources (LITERATURE-VERIFIED via search); the error is in GROK's own restatement of the identity, not in the cited sources.
4. Computational check — algebraic immunity of affine functions (C75)
C75's break rests on: low-AI functions (degree-1 annihilator, i.e. AI=1) are exactly the affine functions, decided by Gaussian elimination without branching. Verified by brute-force minimum-annihilator-degree search (enumerating all ANF coefficient vectors up to each candidate degree, over both the function's 1-set and 0-set) for pure XOR and XOR+1 on n=3 and n=4 variables: AI = 1 in all four cases, matching the claim exactly. A degree-2 control function (ANF = x₁x₂ ⊕ x_last) gave AI = 2 in both n=3 and n=4, confirming the annihilator-degree search correctly distinguishes affine from non-affine residuals rather than trivially returning 1.

6. Computational check — parity's exact prime-implicant count (C77)
Ran an independent Quine–McCluskey-style prime-implicant computation (from scratch, not using a library) on the odd-parity function for n = 3, 4, 5, 6: iteratively combine minterms differing in exactly one coordinate, track un-combined implicants as prime. Result: every minterm is itself a prime implicant, and the count is exactly 2^(n-1) in all four cases (4, 8, 16, 32 for n=3..6), with zero implicants containing a don't-care ("–") symbol at any stage — i.e., parity admits no combination at all, confirming it has no redundancy to exploit. This exactly matches C77's stated fact and supports the break: prime-implicant cardinality is exponential for a function (parity) that is trivially decided in linear time by a non-Shannon method.
7. Computational check — nested-canalizing average sensitivity ≤ 2 (C78)
Implemented the standard recursive NCF construction (a variable order, a canalizing input value and output value per layer, and a final default output) and computed average sensitivity by brute force over all 2ⁿ inputs. Across 100 randomly-parameterized NCFs (20 trials each for n=3,4,5,6,7), the maximum observed average sensitivity was 1.3281 (at n=7), always comfortably under the claimed bound of 2, with the maximum increasing slowly and monotonically with n as expected from the literature's asymptotic (average sensitivity of an NCF approaches but never reaches 2). No counterexample found. AND (a degenerate, maximally "easy" NCF) gave much lower values (0.75, 0.31, 0.11 for n=3,5,7), as expected. This is consistent with, though does not itself constitute a proof of, the Jarrah–Raposa–Laubenbacher / Li et al. bound GROK cites.
