# WCD-0001: worst-case derivation cost W_R(N) and the bridge SAT in P => W_R(N) <= poly(N)

**P versus NP remains OPEN.** Nothing below is a lower bound; it is an audit of what the bridge can and cannot be.

Setup. R is a sound, complete propositional refutation system (a verifier V_R in P). The multisign DAG hypergraph is the derivation DAG of R; cost_R(pi) = size of pi. W_R(N) = max over UNSAT F of size <= N of the shortest R-refutation. Free knowledge of the best sequence is legitimate: the min over pi is the proof-size measure itself, and search cost is never charged.

## Results (status labels per AGENTS.md)

**T1 (PROVED/EXACT, trivial): bridge equivalence.** For fixed R, Bridge(R) := [SAT in P => W_R <= poly] is logically equivalent to [W_R superpoly => SAT not in P] (contrapositive), and to [P=NP => R is p-bounded].
Consequence: LB(R) (W_R superpoly) plus Bridge(R) gives P != NP. Bridge(R) is the whole content of the transfer; nothing is gained by granting the optimal derivation.

**T2 (PROVED/EXACT given KNOWN THEOREM Haken 1985, Chvatal-Szemeredi 1988): Bridge is not weaker than the target for any R with a known lower bound.** For R in {Resolution, Cutting Planes, Polynomial Calculus, bounded-depth Frege}, LB(R) is a KNOWN THEOREM, so Bridge(R) is equivalent to SAT not in P, i.e. to P != NP. Proving the bridge for these R is exactly as hard as the target. So hard-family tests against resolution, algebraic reasoning etc. (agent 3 / agent 5 tasks) cannot by themselves be the bridge.

**T3 (PROVED/EXACT): Cook-Reckhow.** There exists a system R with W_R <= poly iff NP = coNP. So a single R can serve as the bridge only if it is p-bounded under P=NP. Bridge(R) holds for every R iff no R is an obstacle, which is not known: CANDIDATE.

**T4 (CONDITIONAL): sufficient condition for Bridge(R) with R extending Extended Frege.** Suppose SAT is decided by a P-algorithm A. Then F UNSAT has an R-refutation of size poly(|F|) if the propositional translations of Sound_A (A(F)=0 implies F unsat) have poly-size R-proofs, because the trace of A on F is a poly-size EF derivation of A(F)=0 and modus ponens finishes. This is the reflection-principle route (Cook, Krajicek-Pudlak). The hypothesis is not implied by P=NP alone: P=NP does not say the correctness of A is feasibly provable. Whether P=NP => EF p-bounded is, to my knowledge, open (NOT confirmed in literature search; see below).

**T5 (CANDIDATE, new bridge needed): weakest sufficient bridge.** Replace Bridge(R) by Bridge*(R): for every poly-time A with A decides SAT, exists poly-size R-proofs of Sound_A. This is strictly the reflection form; it implies Bridge(R) and is implied by "R is p-optimal among systems with P-time soundness proofs". It is the right target statement, but proving it requires either an optimal proof system (open) or a provability-of-P=NP hypothesis. Hence the program as stated cannot reach P != NP via a bridge that is weaker than the target; every route found so far is either equivalent to the target (T2) or needs an open optimality hypothesis (T5).

## Hard-instance tests (requested item 4)
- Resolution: Tseitin/pigeonhole-type families are exponentially hard (KNOWN). Shared DAGs do not help (DAG-like resolution is the measure). Circuit compression: extension variables take R to Extended Resolution, where no superpoly lower bound is known; every candidate hard family must therefore be rechecked against ER, and the Tseitin families on expanders have poly-size EF proofs (Cook). So Tseitin is not a candidate for EF-hardness.
- Adaptive switching between systems: W of the min over a finite fixed set of systems equals the min of W; it adds no strength beyond the strongest member under p-simulation. A *combined* inference system must be fixed before N, so switching is covered by taking R = union and is p-simulated by EF plus soundness axioms.
- Open: the gap between RESULTS (Resolution-type lower bounds) and EF is the same gap as the bridge.

## Literature check (this run)
- Cook-Reckhow / Krajicek-Pudlak: p-bounded iff NP=coNP; optimal proof systems linked to NE vs coNE (Krajicek-Pudlak 1989). Search did not turn up any result showing P=NP implies EF p-bounded.
- Recent: Bhattacharya-Chattopadhyay (arXiv 2507.23008, Res(xor) lower bound, depth-limited), Alekseev PC with extension rule (Theory of Computing 22, 2026), Khaniki-Pich-Sokolov "Efficient Adversaries" (CCC 2026, constructive lower bounds / canonical pair of EF). None yields a Frege or EF superpolynomial lower bound.
- Hint worth a follow-up: the Khaniki-Pich-Sokolov prover/adversary framing could test whether a *constructive* LB for EF is compatible with Bridge*(EF).
