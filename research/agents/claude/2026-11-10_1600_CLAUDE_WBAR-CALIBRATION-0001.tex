\section*{Shared target (unchanged)}
For a fixed sound, complete, polynomial-time checkable system $R$ with node weights,
\[
\overline W_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},\ |\langle F\rangle|\le N}\ \min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi),
\qquad
\textbf{B3: } \mathrm{SAT}\in P \Longrightarrow \overline W_R(N)\le \mathrm{poly}(N).
\]
The inner minimum gives the best derivation sequence for free. That is a valid way to get a
lower bound on \emph{executing an $R$-derivation}: any procedure that produces or follows an
$R$-derivation pays at least $W_R(F)$. The task asks for this choice, and this report keeps it.

\section*{1. Only the UNSAT side can be hard}
\begin{proposition}[SAT side is polynomially bounded; PROVED/EXACT]
Let $R^{+}$ extend $R$ by one rule: given an assignment $a$, check $F(a)=1$ at cost
$O(|\langle F\rangle|)$ and reach the minimal reduced state ``SAT''. Then
$\max_{F\in\mathrm{SAT},|\langle F\rangle|\le N}\min_\pi\operatorname{cost}(\pi)=O(N)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every satisfiable $F$ has a satisfying assignment with at most $N$ bits. The free optimal
sequence guesses it, and the check costs $O(N)$.
\end{proof}
So over \emph{all} 3-CNFs, the worst-case minimum work to reach the minimal reduced logical
state is superpolynomial exactly when $\overline W_R$ is. The lower-bound half of the
programme is therefore a statement about short proofs (coNP). It is not yet about time.

\section*{2. Calibration of the lower-bound half}
\begin{proposition}[Sandwich; PROVED/EXACT from KNOWN THEOREMS]
(a) If $\mathrm{NP}\neq\mathrm{coNP}$, then $\overline W_R$ is superpolynomial for every
Cook--Reckhow system $R$ (Cook--Reckhow 1979).
(b) If $R$ polynomially simulates Extended Resolution on CNF sources, then a superpolynomial
lower bound on $\overline W_R$ implies that Extended Frege is not polynomially bounded.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) Cook--Reckhow: $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$ if and only if some proof system is
polynomially bounded. (b) ER and EF polynomially simulate each other. If $R$ simulates ER
with polynomial blow-up, then short ER proofs would give short $R$-proofs, so a lower bound
for $R$ is a lower bound for ER, hence for EF.
\end{proof}
For $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$, which has paid two-input DEF and binary RES, part (b) applies
whenever the exact-source ER$\to R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ translation holds. That is CONDITIONAL
on the completion recorded in REFEREE350 and LSPV-1/406. Under that translation, the hardness
target is at least as hard as proving superpolynomial EF lower bounds, a long-standing open
problem.

\paragraph{Literature delta, this run.} Itsykson, Podolskii and Shekhovtsov (ECCC TR26-018;
CCC 2026, LIPIcs vol.~383, paper 13) prove superpolynomial size lower bounds for
$\mathrm{Res}(\oplus)$ refutations of depth $o(n^2/\log^4 n)$, and an unconditional quadratic
size bound. As of January 2026, ECCC TR26-007 still describes unrestricted superpolynomial
$\mathrm{Res}(\oplus)$ size lower bounds as open. The DEF-free part of $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$
(clauses, binary GF(2) row sums, bounded-support conversions) is polynomially simulated by
$\mathrm{Res}(\oplus)$: every one of its lines is a $\mathrm{Res}(\oplus)$ line, and every
step is a polynomial-size $\mathrm{Res}(\oplus)$ derivation. A $\mathrm{Res}(\oplus)$ lower
bound therefore transfers \emph{down} to that fragment. The converse does not hold.
Classification: DIRECTLY TRANSFERABLE THEOREM (fragment only, depth-restricted); it says
nothing about the full system with DEF. Recommended concrete subgoal for the Hypergraph \&
Certificates role: decide whether the DEF-free fragment, which cannot form mixed
clause-or-equation lines, admits a superpolynomial lower bound that is already within reach,
for example by a depth-free argument specific to its separated line types. Status: OPEN,
CANDIDATE.

\section*{3. Calibration of the bridge B3}
\begin{proposition}[B3 equals a known open implication; PROVED/EXACT]
Let $R$ be polynomially equivalent to EF on CNF sources. Then
B3$(R)$ holds if and only if $(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\Rightarrow \text{EF is polynomially bounded})$.
Hence ``superpolynomial $\overline W_R$ $\wedge$ B3$(R)$'' is exactly the Pich--Santhanam
programme: ``EF is not polynomially bounded'' together with ``$\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$
implies EF is polynomially bounded''.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{SAT}\in P$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$. With polynomial equivalence,
``$\overline W_R(N)\le\mathrm{poly}(N)$'' is equivalent to ``EF has polynomial-size
refutations of every UNSAT CNF''. The latter is equivalent to EF being polynomially bounded
on tautologies, via the standard polynomial-size CNF (Tseitin) encoding of DNF tautologies
with extension variables.
\end{proof}
Known sufficient premises, all OPEN: (i) a polynomial-time SAT algorithm whose soundness is
provable in $S^1_2$ or PV gives polynomial-size EF proofs (Cook 1975; corpus LSPV-1/357);
(ii) Pich--Santhanam feasible anticheckers $AC_k$ (corpus LSPV-1/406).
Known barrier: relativizing proofs of B3 are excluded (corpus LSPV-1/360, TQBF oracle).
\emph{No new bridge is claimed here.}

\section*{4. Red-team note on the ``free best sequence'' principle}
Not charging for the search for the optimal sequence is sound for the inequality
$\text{work}(\text{executing }\pi)\ge W_R(F)$. The gap is in a different place: an arbitrary
polynomial-time SAT algorithm $A$ does not have to execute any $R$-derivation. It may
answer UNSAT without producing a sound $R$-certificate. B1, B2 and B3 are exactly the
statements that close that gap, and only B3 is needed. Classification of the user idea
``minimum work to the minimal reduced state'': EXACT OBJECT (it equals $\overline W_R$ by
Proposition~1). By Propositions~2--3, its hardness half is at least as hard as an EF lower
bound, and its transfer half is a known open implication.
