\noindent\textbf{Status.} P versus NP remains OPEN. Nothing below proves a lower bound. This report \emph{audits the proposed bridge} and records one computation.

\section{Setup}
$R$ is a Cook--Reckhow proof system (polynomial-time verifier, sound, complete for UNSAT). $\overline W_R(N)=\max_{|F|\le N,F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}}\min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}|\pi|$; strategy search is free (not charged). Bridge: $B(R)\equiv(\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\Rightarrow\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly})$.

\section{Results}
\begin{theorem}[KNOWN THEOREM, Cook--Reckhow 1979] Free choice of derivation is nondeterminism: $\forall R\;\overline W_R\notin\mathrm{poly}$ $\iff$ $\mathrm{NP}\neq\mathrm{coNP}$. Hence a lower bound for \emph{every} $R$ is strictly stronger than $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$ (it implies it, since $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\Rightarrow\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$).\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[PROVED/EXACT: bridge circularity for weak $R$]
Let $R$ have an unconditional superpolynomial lower bound (Resolution: Haken 1985; bounded-depth Frege: Ajtai, Bellantoni et al.). Then $B(R)\iff \mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm P$ (as a statement about the world: $B(R)$ is false iff $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} If $\overline W_R$ is superpolynomial then the consequent of $B(R)$ is false, so $B(R)$ is equivalent to $\neg(\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P)$.\end{proof}
So proving the bridge for any $R$ with a known lower bound \emph{is} proving $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$; no progress can be extracted from it. In particular the transfer ``poly-time SAT algorithm $\Rightarrow$ short Resolution refutations'' is false-or-vacuous.

\begin{proposition}[PROVED/EXACT: bridge for optimal $R$]
If $R$ is optimal (every proof system $Q$ is $p$-simulated by $R$), then $B(R)$ holds, and in fact $\overline W_R$ poly $\iff$ NP=coNP. So for optimal $R$ the program $\overline W_R\notin$poly is \emph{equivalent} to $\mathrm{NP}\ne\mathrm{coNP}$.\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}$\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\Rightarrow\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}\Rightarrow$ a polynomially bounded $Q$ exists (Cook--Reckhow) $\Rightarrow R$ polynomially bounded. Converse is Theorem 1.\end{proof}
Whether optimal systems exist is open (Krajíček--Pudlák 1989; implied by $\mathrm{NE}=\mathrm{coNE}$). Extended Frege is the natural candidate but is not known optimal.

\begin{proposition}[KNOWN THEOREM, Cook 1975 / Krajíček--Pudlák: constructive bridge needs provable correctness]
If $M$ is a poly-time SAT decider and the soundness of $M$ is provable in $\mathrm{PV}$ (or $\mathrm{S}^1_2$), then Extended Frege has polynomial-size refutations of UNSAT formulas.\end{proposition}
Failure mode (red-team, CANDIDATE barrier): $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$ gives no \emph{provable} correctness for the witnessing $M$; Levin-style universal search is correct only if $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$ is itself provable. It is open whether $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}\Rightarrow\mathrm{EF}$ is polynomially bounded.

\begin{corollary}[Dichotomy for the shared target]
Any non-circular instantiation of the key priority needs $R$ strong enough to simulate arbitrary poly-time computations with their correctness (EF-like or stronger). For such $R$ no superpolynomial lower bound is known, and one would imply $\mathrm{NP}\ne\mathrm{coNP}$-type consequences; for weak $R$ the bridge is circular (Proposition 1). The ``multisign DAG hypergraph'' system must therefore be classified by its simulation position: below Frege (lower bounds possible, bridge circular) or $\ge$ EF (bridge plausible, lower bound is a major open problem).
\end{corollary}

\section{Time--space refinement (Auditor note)}
Cost may be taken as (size, space) pairs. Space lower bounds for Resolution (Atserias--Dalmau 2008; Ben-Sasson--Nordstr\"om 2008--11 size-space tradeoffs) are unconditional but concern restricted systems; by Proposition 1 any bridge-based use of them is circular. Free strategy choice is irrelevant to space bounds, which hold for every refutation.

\section{Computation (COMPUTATIONAL)}
\texttt{min\_res\_dag.py} computes the exact minimum number of derived clauses in a DAG-Resolution refutation by exhaustive search over sets of derived clauses (strategy cost uncharged). Chunked one instance per call, $<30$s. Results (\texttt{min\_res\_dag\_results.json}): all-2-clauses on 2 vars: 3; unit chain $n{=}3$: 3; Tseitin on $C_3$: 5; Tseitin on $C_4$: 7 ($=2n-1$, linear, as expected for cycles); $\mathrm{PHP}^3_2$: search exhausted 25s budget, \textbf{no certified value} (only a lower bound from the explored depth is implied). This is a finite sanity check, not evidence of asymptotics.
