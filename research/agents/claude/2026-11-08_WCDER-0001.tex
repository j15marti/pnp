\noindent\textbf{Status: P versus NP remains OPEN.} No lower bound or transfer theorem is claimed. This report audits the target
$\overline W_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},|F|\le N}\min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$ and the bridge $B_R:\ \mathrm{SAT}\in P\Rightarrow\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$.

\section*{Setup (Cook--Reckhow)}
$R$ is a fixed sound, complete, polynomial-time-checkable refutation system (the multisigned DAG hypergraph calculus is such a system once its rules are fixed and checkable); $\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)\ge|\pi|$ (size; time/space charged on top only increases cost). Free choice of $\pi$ is granted: this makes $\overline W_R$ a \emph{nondeterministic} measure.

\section*{Results}
\begin{prop}[Bridge triviality; PROVED/EXACT] If $\overline W_R$ is provably superpolynomial (Resolution: Haken; Cutting Planes; bounded-depth Frege; Polynomial Calculus over fixed fields on suitable families), then $B_R$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{SAT}\notin P$.\end{prop}
\begin{proof}$B_R$ has the form $A\Rightarrow C$ with $C$ false; this is equivalent to $\neg A$.\end{proof}
Consequence: for every system with a known lower bound the ``weakest sufficient bridge'' is as hard as the goal. Any useful $R$ must have open $\overline W_R$ \emph{and} an independent proof of $B_R$.

\begin{prop}[Free strategy is coNP-strength; KNOWN THEOREM, Cook--Reckhow 1979] ``$\overline W_R$ superpolynomial for every sound complete $R$'' is equivalent to $\mathrm{NP}\ne\mathrm{coNP}$, which implies $P\ne NP$ and is not known equivalent to it.\end{prop}
So the unrestricted version of the programme (free optimal derivation, all systems) targets something at least as strong as $P\neq NP$. A single fixed $R$ avoids this only through $B_R$.

\begin{prop}[What $B_R$ really says; CANDIDATE/standard, not re-verified here]
$B_R\iff(P=NP\Rightarrow R\text{ is $p$-bounded})$. If $P=NP$ via a correct $M$, the trace system $R_M$ is $p$-bounded but only sound because $M$ is correct; $R$ simulates it iff $R$ has short proofs of the propositional translations of ``$M$ answers UNSAT $\Rightarrow$ $F$ unsat'' (cf.\ Krajicek, bounded arithmetic / proof complexity; Pich--Santhanam for the unprovability direction). Hence $B_R$ for $R=$Frege/EF is a \emph{provability-of-correctness} statement, not a combinatorial one.\end{prop}

\begin{prop}[Dichotomy; PROVED/EXACT given the above]
For a candidate $R$ either (i) $\overline W_R$ is already known superpolynomial, and $B_R\iff P\ne NP$ (no progress), or (ii) $\overline W_R$ is open and $B_R$ needs a proof that $P=NP$ yields short $R$-proofs of correctness of some $M$ --- an unconditional statement of strength comparable to proving the target.
\end{prop}

\section*{Consequences for the five roles}
\begin{itemize}
\item Lead: replace $B_R$ by the \emph{conditional} form: ``if $R$ is $p$-bounded then $NP=coNP$'' (always true) and treat a lower bound for the strongest tractable $R$ (EF/ extended hypergraph calculus) as an NP vs coNP programme; do not expect $B_R$ for weak $R$.
\item Builder: any fixed inference system proposed must be tagged by type (i) or (ii); weak systems are type (i).
\item Certificates: hard UNSAT families for resolution/PC/CP are type (i) evidence only; they test no bridge.
\item Auditor: charge time/space on top of size; this cannot weaken a size lower bound.
\item Red team: attack the claim that some explicit $R$ of type (ii) has $B_R$ provable without proving $P=NP$ outright.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Literature check (this run)}
Cook--Reckhow: $p$-bounded system $\iff NP=coNP$. Pich--Santhanam (strong co-nondeterministic lower bounds for NP cannot be proved feasibly): a $P\ne NP$ route exists only under EF-hardness of circuit-lower-bound formulas; later work (arXiv 2312.08163 and follow-ups) weakens their assumptions to iEF. A 2025 result: if a strong theory $S$ is not polynomially bounded then true Resolution lower bounds exist that $S$ cannot derive in polynomial size --- relevant: lower bounds for fixed weak $R$ need not be feasibly provable inside strong systems. Not located: a theorem giving $B_R$ for any explicit $R$. Sources: arxiv.org/abs/2312.08163; arxiv.org/pdf/2506.16956; Pich homepage notes.
