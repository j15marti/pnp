\section{Setup}
Fix a deterministic multitape Turing machine $D$ and a polynomial clock $p$. Take a standard
binary 3-CNF encoding, with $n=|\langle F\rangle|$. In the two-sorted language of $V^0$
(Cook--Nguyen~\cite{CN10}), let $\mathrm{Tab}_{D,p}(F,Q)$ be the $\Sigma^B_0$ formula stating that
the string $Q$ is a valid computation tableau of $D$ on input $F$ that halts within $p(n)$ steps and rejects.
The tableau is a $p(n)\times O(p(n))$ grid, and validity is checked by bounded local window
conditions. Let $\mathrm{Sat}(F,X)$ be the $\Sigma^B_0$ formula saying that assignment $X$ satisfies
every clause of $F$. Define the \emph{clocked UNSAT-soundness} sentence
\[
\Snd_{D,p}:\qquad \forall F\ \forall X\ \forall Q\ \big(\mathrm{Tab}_{D,p}(F,Q)\rightarrow\neg\mathrm{Sat}(F,X)\big),
\]
where all string quantifiers are bounded by polynomial terms. The matrix is $\Sigma^B_0$.
This is the soundness notion behind the $R_T$ construction. Only rejections are
certified, which is exactly what UNSAT refutations need.

\section{Main result}
\begin{theorem}[the $V^0$ rung is empty]\label{thm:v0}
No deterministic machine $D$ with polynomial clock $p$ that correctly rejects every unsatisfiable 3-CNF
within $p(n)$ steps has $V^0\vdash\Snd_{D,p}$. This holds unconditionally, without assuming anything
about $\mathsf P$ versus $\mathsf{NP}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose $V^0\vdash\Snd_{D,p}$.
\emph{Step 1 (translation; KNOWN THEOREM).} By the Paris--Wilkie-type translation for $V^0$
\cite[Thm.~VII.2.3]{CN10}, there are a constant $d$ and a polynomial $q$ such that, for every $n$,
the propositional translation $\|\Snd_{D,p}\|_n$ has a depth-$d$ Frege (bounded-depth PK) proof of size $\le q(n)$.
The atoms of $\|\Snd_{D,p}\|_n$ are the bits of $F$, $X$ and $Q$.
\emph{Step 2 (instantiation).} Fix an unsatisfiable 3-CNF $F_0$ of size $n$. By correctness and the clock,
$D$ rejects $F_0$ within $p(n)$ steps. Let $Q_0$ be its actual tableau, of size $O(p(n)^2)$.
Substitute the constants $F_0$ and $Q_0$ for the $F$- and $Q$-atoms throughout the proof; this yields
a valid proof of the same depth and polynomial size. The closed formula $\|\mathrm{Tab}_{D,p}\|(F_0,Q_0)$
is a true variable-free bounded-depth formula, so it has a depth-$d$ evaluation proof of size polynomial
in its own size. Modus ponens then gives a depth-$O(d)$ proof of $\neg\|\mathrm{Sat}\|(F_0,X)$.
After constant substitution, this formula is equivalent to the negated conjunction of the clauses of $F_0$,
by a polynomial-size constant-depth simplification.
\emph{Step 3 (refutation).} Adding the clauses of $F_0$ as hypotheses gives a depth-$O(d)$ Frege
refutation of $F_0$ of size $\mathrm{poly}(n)$, with the polynomial independent of $F_0$.
\emph{Step 4 (contradiction; KNOWN THEOREM).} Tseitin formulas with odd total charge on bounded-degree
expanders are unsatisfiable 3-CNFs when the degree is 3. Every fixed-depth Frege refutation of them has
size $\exp(n^{\Omega_d(1)})$~\cite{BS02}, and polynomial size forces depth $\Omega(\sqrt{\log n})$~\cite{PRST16}.
Applying Step 3 with $F_0$ ranging over this family gives a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[a quantitative form]
The same argument with a clock $t(n)$ given by a term of a stronger-but-still-$\mathrm{AC}^0$-translatable
theory shows the following. A correct SAT decider whose clocked soundness has proofs that translate
into depth-$d$ Frege proofs of size $\mathrm{poly}(t(n))$ must satisfy $t(n)\ge\exp(n^{\Omega_d(1)})$.
\emph{Provably sound in $\mathrm{AC}^0$ reasoning implies nearly exponential running time.}
This is an unconditional lower bound for a restricted \emph{class of algorithms}: those whose correctness
can be verified with $\mathrm{AC}^0$ reasoning. It is not a lower bound for arbitrary algorithms.
It is \textbf{CANDIDATE} pending an exact choice of theory and an independent check of the parameters.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[KNOWN THEOREM: the $S^1_2$ rung equals the PV rung]\label{lem:s12}
$S^1_2$ is $\forall\Sigma^b_1$-conservative over $\mathrm{PV}_1$ (Buss 1986, via the witnessing theorem;
see~\cite{Bus86,KP89}). After expansion with PV function symbols, $\Snd_{D,p}$ is a universal sentence,
hence $\forall\Sigma^b_1$. So $S^1_2\vdash\Snd_{D,p}$ iff $\mathrm{PV}_1\vdash\Snd_{D,p}$, and
$\mathrm{PROV}_{S^1_2}\equiv\mathrm{PROV}_{\mathrm{PV}}$. Similarly $V^1$ is $\forall\Sigma^B_1$-conservative
over $\mathrm{VPV}$~\cite{CN10}. Choosing $T=S^1_2$ or $V^1$ in place of PV therefore gains nothing.
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[the provable-soundness ladder]\label{cor:ladder}
Let $T\mapsto P_T$ be the standard correspondence between bounded-arithmetic theories and proof systems
(Cook--Nguyen; Krajíček):
$V^0\mapsto\mathrm{AC}^0$-Frege, $V^0(2)\mapsto\mathrm{AC}^0[2]$-Frege, $\mathrm{VTC}^0\mapsto\mathrm{TC}^0$-Frege,
$\mathrm{VNC}^1\mapsto$ Frege, $\mathrm{VPV}\approx\mathrm{PV}\approx S^1_2\mapsto$ EF. For each rung, the argument of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v0} gives:
\[
\exists\ \text{superpolynomial lower bound for $P_T$ on some 3-CNF family}
\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathrm{PROV}_T\ \text{holds only vacuously, i.e. }\mathrm{PROV}_T\equiv\SAT\notin\mathsf P .
\]
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
theory $T$ & system $P_T$ & 3-CNF lower bound for $P_T$ & status of $\mathrm{PROV}_T$\\
\midrule
$V^0$ & $\mathrm{AC}^0$-Frege & KNOWN (Tseitin~\cite{BS02,PRST16}) & equivalent to $\SAT\notin\mathsf P$; route dead\\
$V^0(2)$ & $\mathrm{AC}^0[2]$-Frege & OPEN for 3-CNFs (Tseitin is easy; DNF results~\cite{LST25}) & OPEN\\
$\mathrm{VTC}^0$ & $\mathrm{TC}^0$-Frege & OPEN & OPEN\\
$\mathrm{VNC}^1$ & Frege & OPEN & OPEN\\
$\mathrm{PV}$, $S^1_2$, $V^1$ & EF & OPEN & OPEN (the Cook PV route)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
So a nontrivial instance of ``$\mathrm{PROV}_T$ together with $\mathrm{LB}(R_T)$'' must use a theory at least as strong as
$V^0(2)$. Its lower-bound half then requires a superpolynomial 3-CNF lower bound for
$\mathrm{AC}^0[2]$-Frege or a stronger system, which is a well-known open frontier of proof complexity.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For the $V^0$ row, use Theorem~\ref{thm:v0}. For the other rows, the same four steps go through using the
matching translation theorems in~\cite{CN10}. In each case the proof system can evaluate closed
instances of its own formula class: parity gates for $\mathrm{AC}^0[2]$, threshold gates for $\mathrm{TC}^0$.
The ``equivalent to $\SAT\notin\mathsf P$'' entry follows exactly as in WCDERIV-0001 Theorem 1.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[relation to the user's free-sequence principle]
Theorem~\ref{thm:v0} uses the free-optimal-sequence convention in the intended direction: the minimum over
$\mathrm{AC}^0$-Frege refutations bounds below the particular refutation built from $D$'s tableau. The tableau
is a white-box object, so Theorem~\ref{thm:v0} does not fall under the oracle-collapse barrier of
WR-BRIDGE-0001. The ladder confirms, constructively, that free knowledge of the best derivation is harmless,
while the strength of the reasoning that certifies the solver's correctness is the binding constraint.
\end{remark}

$\mathrm{AC}^0[2]$-Frege, e.g.\ mod-3 Tseitin and random 3-CNFs. Check them against Gaussian elimination
and bounded-treewidth dynamic programming.
\item[Time--space resource auditor.] Charge Step 2's sizes: tableau $O(p(n)^2)$, translation
$q(n)$, and an evaluation proof of size polynomial in $p(n)$. Confirm that the polynomial is independent of $F_0$.
\item[Independent proof red team.] Attack Step 2. Is the closed-evaluation proof really of bounded depth for the
specific tableau encoding? Does the Cook--Nguyen translation apply to a $\Sigma^B_0$ formula with these
polynomial bounds? Is the use of~\cite{BS02} for 3-regular graphs exact?
\end{description}
