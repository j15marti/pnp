\section*{Shared objective in exact form}
Fix a sound, complete, polynomial-time-checkable refutation system $R$ for CNF formulas (Cook--Reckhow \cite{CR}) with a cost function $\operatorname{cost}_R$ that is polynomially related to encoded proof length. Define
\[
\Wb_R(N)=\max_{\substack{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}\\ |\langle F\rangle|\le N}}\ \min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi).
\]
The inner minimum ranges over all valid derivations, so the best reduction sequence of the multisign DAG hypergraph is granted for free. This is exactly the standard proof-size measure; discovering the sequence is never charged, which is legitimate for a lower bound on executing it. The two directive targets are
\[
\mathrm{LB}(R):\ \Wb_R(N)\ge N^{\omega(1)}\ \text{(infinitely often)},\qquad
\mathrm{Bridge}(R):\ \mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\implies \Wb_R(N)\le\operatorname{poly}(N).
\]
$\mathrm{LB}(R)\wedge\mathrm{Bridge}(R)\Rightarrow\mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm P$. Both must hold for the \emph{same} $R$.

\section{Monotonicity under simulation}
\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT]\label{thm:mono}
If $R'$ size-simulates $R$, i.e.\ $\min\operatorname{cost}_{R'}(F)\le q(|F|+\min\operatorname{cost}_R(F))$ for a polynomial $q$ and all UNSAT $F$, then
(a) $\mathrm{Bridge}(R)\Rightarrow\mathrm{Bridge}(R')$ and (b) $\mathrm{LB}(R')\Rightarrow\mathrm{LB}(R)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take maxima over $|\langle F\rangle|\le N$: $\Wb_{R'}(N)\le q(N+\Wb_R(N))$ because $q$ is nondecreasing. A polynomial bound on $\Wb_R$ gives one on $\Wb_{R'}$, which is (a). The contrapositive is (b).
\end{proof}
So the bridge gets \emph{easier} as $R$ gets stronger and the lower bound gets \emph{harder}. Any proof of P$\ne$NP along this route needs one $R$ that is strong enough for the bridge and still weak enough for a lower bound.

\section{Bridge collapse for systems with known lower bounds}
\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT]\label{thm:collapse}
If $\mathrm{LB}(R)$ is a theorem, then $\mathrm{Bridge}(R)\iff\mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm P$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
($\Rightarrow$) $\mathrm{Bridge}(R)$ and $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P$ together give $\Wb_R\le\operatorname{poly}$, contradicting $\mathrm{LB}(R)$. ($\Leftarrow$) If $\mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm P$, the implication holds vacuously.
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Consequence.} $\mathrm{LB}(R)$ is a KNOWN THEOREM for tree-like and general Resolution (Haken: PHP; Urquhart, Ben-Sasson--Wigderson: Tseitin on expanders), Cutting Planes (Pudl\'ak 1997, via clique--colouring interpolation), Polynomial Calculus (Razborov 1998; Alekhnovich--Razborov), and bounded-depth Frege (Ajtai; Beame--Impagliazzo--Pitassi; Kraj\'{\i}\v{c}ek--Pudl\'ak--Woods). For every one of these systems the ``weakest sufficient bridge'' is not weaker than the target: it \emph{is} SAT$\notin$P. World-ledger row L41 records this; the auto-classifier labels it ``Equivalent to P $\ne$ NP''. A hard UNSAT family that survives Resolution, algebraic reasoning or bounded-depth reasoning therefore moves the project forward only if a bridge is proved for a system \emph{at least that strong}, and then Theorem~\ref{thm:mono}(b) requires the hard family to survive the stronger system as well.

The same applies to restricted solver classes. CDCL with restarts p-simulates and is p-simulated by Resolution (Pipatsrisawat--Darwiche 2011; Atserias--Fichte--Thurley 2011), so Haken's bound already gives exponential worst-case CDCL running time. This is a KNOWN THEOREM about one algorithm class, not about all algorithms.

\section{The known fixed-system bridge}
Pich and Santhanam \cite{PS} define, for each fixed $k\ge3$, tautologies $\alpha^{n^k}_n$ built from a fixed $\operatorname{poly}(n^k)$-size circuit $B$. If $\mathrm{SAT}_n\in\mathrm{Circuit}[n^{3k}]$, then $B$ solves search-SAT$_n$; otherwise $B$ is arbitrary. In every case $\alpha^{n^k}_n$ states that either $B$ finds a witness or a free size-$n^k$ circuit $C$ errs on SAT$_n$. The system $\mathrm{EF}+\alpha^{n^k}$ allows substitution instances of these axioms. Its axioms depend on $n$ through $B$, so this is a polynomial-advice (nonuniform) proof system.

\begin{theorem}[KNOWN THEOREM; Pich--Santhanam \cite{PS}, Thm.~2 and the remark after it]\label{thm:ps}
For fixed $k\ge3$: if $\mathrm{SAT}_n\in\mathrm{Circuit}[n^k]$ for all $n$, then every tautology of size $n$ has a $\operatorname{poly}(n)$-size proof in $\mathrm{EF}+\alpha^{n^k}$. Such proofs are even produced by a polynomial-size circuit.
\end{theorem}
\noindent\textbf{Exact transfer into $\Wb_R$.} A deterministic time-$T(n)$ algorithm gives circuits of size $O(T\log T)$ (Pippenger--Fischer). So $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm{DTIME}(n^c)$ gives $\mathrm{SAT}_n\in\mathrm{Circuit}[n^{c+1}]$ for all large $n$, and finitely many small $n$ are absorbed into the constant. CNF refutation is dual to DNF tautology through a linear-size translation. Taking $R_k=\mathrm{EF}+\alpha^{n^k}$ with $k=\max(3,c+1)$ and proof size as cost:
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\implies\exists k:\ \Wb_{R_k}(N)\le\operatorname{poly}(N).}
\]
This is the project's bridge for a family of fixed systems, one for each $k$. It is the strongest bridge in the literature known to this run that needs no correctness or provability assumption about the hypothetical algorithm. Compare LSPV-1/336, whose bridge needs \emph{size-optimality} of $R$; that property is OPEN for every explicit uniform system.

\begin{corollary}[CONDITIONAL; hypothesis OPEN]\label{cor:route}
If for every $k\ge3$ the system $\mathrm{EF}+\alpha^{n^k}$ is not polynomially bounded, then $\mathrm{NP}\not\subseteq\mathrm{P/poly}$, and hence $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
This is the contrapositive of Theorem~\ref{thm:ps}, uniformly in $k$.
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Audit of the hypothesis.} (i) $\mathrm{EF}\le_p R_k$, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:mono}(b) the hypothesis implies superpolynomial EF lower bounds. Those are a major OPEN problem with no known route; recent progress (2025--2026) concerns bounded-depth or algebraic systems only. (ii) The converse of Theorem~\ref{thm:ps} is not known. A circuit lower bound for SAT does not obviously make $R_k$ weak, so Corollary~\ref{cor:route} is a one-way implication; ledger row L43 classifies it as ``Implies P $\ne$ NP (open)''. (iii) The proof concludes a \emph{nonuniform} separation, which is stronger than P$\ne$NP. That is a sign the route is at least as hard as needed and probably harder. (iv) Barriers: Pich--Santhanam's own results show that EF lower bounds reach P$\ne$NP only under additional bounded-arithmetic hypotheses (their conditions I--II and the anticheckers/witnessing assumptions) when one wants a \emph{uniform, standard} EF. The nonuniform axioms $\alpha$ are what remove those hypotheses. A natural-proofs check is needed for any lower-bound method aimed at $R_k$, because $R_k$ talks about circuit correctness directly. That check is not performed here.

\section{What this means for the multisign DAG hypergraph program}
The multisign reduction calculus (historical $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ and its completions) is a fixed polynomial-time-checkable system $R$. Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse} sorts its two possible outcomes. If the calculus is simulated by Resolution, PC, CP or AC$^0$-Frege, any bridge for it implies one for a system with a known lower bound and is therefore exactly P$\ne$NP-hard. If it is strong enough to carry a bridge without circularity, it must be comparable to EF$+\alpha^{n^k}$-strength reasoning, where no lower-bound technique is known. The corpus already shows that the native calculus has linear-size parity proofs ([P2-L5-427]: cubic odd Tseitin has $W=4h-1$). So the Resolution lower-bound families are already defeated inside it, and placing it above Resolution is consistent with the record. The next honest question is whether $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ p-simulates EF. If it does not, a bridge for it would have to come from a new theorem, not from Theorem~\ref{thm:ps}.

\section*{Barrier-transfer preflight}
Obstacles: (1) a lower bound for a system strong enough to carry a bridge; (2) a uniform bridge without correctness reflection.
Proof complexity and bounded arithmetic: Theorem~\ref{thm:ps} is a DIRECTLY TRANSFERABLE THEOREM.
Control theory (barrier certificates): ANALOGY only. A barrier certificate refutes reachability in one fixed system, which matches LB$(R)$, not the bridge.
Electronics and VLSI area--time: FAILED TRANSFER. Those bounds rest on a fixed physical layout that has no counterpart in proof size.
PQ ledger: item~6 (special soundness) is the only one that touches this question. $\alpha^{n^k}$ is ``error of $C$ or witness from $B$'', a construction-specific witness-extraction axiom. This is an ANALOGY/CANDIDATE, and no cryptographic assumption is used.
