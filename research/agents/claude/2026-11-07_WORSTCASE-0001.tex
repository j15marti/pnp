\section{Setting}
Let $R$ be a Cook--Reckhow system: proofs checked in polynomial time, $\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)=|\pi|$ (any cost polynomially related to $|\pi|$ gives the same statements).
Free choice of the best $\pi$ makes $\overline W_R$ a \emph{nondeterministic} quantity.

\begin{proposition}[dichotomy]\label{p:d}
\status{PROVED/EXACT}
(a) \emph{Weak $R$.} If $R$ has an unconditional superpolynomial lower bound (resolution: Haken 1985; also cutting planes, bounded-depth Frege), then
``$\SAT\in P\Rightarrow\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$'' is equivalent to $\SAT\notin P$, i.e.\ to $P\ne NP$. The bridge is circular.
(b) \emph{Optimal $R$.} If $R$ polynomially simulates every Cook--Reckhow system (optimal), then $\overline W_R\le \mathrm{poly}\iff NP=coNP$.
The bridge holds (since $P=NP\Rightarrow NP=coNP$), but the target lower bound is \emph{equivalent to $NP\ne coNP$}, strictly at least as hard as $P\ne NP$ (stronger unless $P\ne NP\Rightarrow NP\neq coNP$).
(c) \emph{Time-charged $R_U$.} If steps are arbitrary Turing computations charged by running time, $\overline W_{R_U}$ is the worst-case time of deciding UNSAT; the bridge is trivial and the lower bound is exactly $P\ne NP$ (a reformulation).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) The consequent is false by the theorem, so the implication is equivalent to the negation of its hypothesis. (b) Cook--Reckhow: a polynomially bounded system exists iff $NP=coNP$; optimality transfers the bound to $R$.
(c) Unfolding the definition. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[what the bridge needs for a fixed $R$]\label{p:n}
\status{PROVED/EXACT}
Suppose $P=NP$ via polynomial-time solver $A$. Define $R_A$: a proof of $F$ is the run of $A$ on $F$ (checkable in polynomial time). Then $R_A$ is polynomially bounded. The bridge for fixed $R$ holds iff $R$ polynomially simulates $R_A$ for every such $A$ (existential in $A$). Hence the bridge is a \emph{uniform reflection} property of $R$: ``$R$ proves correctness of polynomial-time SAT solvers with polynomial overhead''. For Frege/extended Frege whether this holds under $P=NP$ is \status{OPEN} (it is a consequence of, not known equivalent to, optimality; cf.\ Krajicek--Pudlak: $NE=coNE$ gives optimal systems, $E=NE$ gives p-optimal).
\end{proposition}

\section{Consequences for the five agents' targets}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Lower-Bound Lead / Hypergraph agents.} Any superpolynomial bound for a system the multisigned-DAG hypergraph $R$ \emph{p-simulates in a weak fragment} lies under case (a): true, useful as a barrier-check, but cannot support the bridge. Concrete test for every proposed hard family: does it also defeat Extended Frege? If the proof uses only resolution-like width/size arguments, the answer is known to be unrelated.
\item \textbf{Reduction Builder.} Target an $R$ such that $R\ge$ EF, and prove Prop.~\ref{p:n}'s simulation of $R_A$ for all $A$ under $P=NP$ (a reflection theorem). That is the real content of the ``weakest sufficient bridge''.
\item \textbf{Red team.} Natural-proofs / feasible-interpolation barriers: Krajicek's feasible interpolation fails for EF under factoring-type hardness, and known lower bounds for strong systems are conditional. A lower bound against EF would give, via interpolation-style arguments, circuit lower bounds, so relativization/natural-proofs audits are required.
\item \textbf{Auditor.} Peak memory is not part of Cook--Reckhow cost; adding a space charge only strengthens $R$'s restrictions (a weak system, case (a)) unless the space is polynomial.
\end{itemize}

\section{Next target}
T-REFL: for $R=$ EF (or Frege + extension), is ``$P=NP\Rightarrow$ EF $p$-simulates $R_A$'' provable? Either answer is informative: yes gives the bridge for EF, and the whole programme then rests on the single OPEN arrow $\overline W_{EF}\notin\mathrm{poly}$ (equiv.\ to Cook--Reckhow for EF).

\noindent\textbf{Bottom line.} Weak systems: lower bounds exist, bridge false-in-spirit (circular). Strong systems: bridge true, lower bound $\Leftrightarrow NP\ne coNP$. P versus NP remains OPEN.
