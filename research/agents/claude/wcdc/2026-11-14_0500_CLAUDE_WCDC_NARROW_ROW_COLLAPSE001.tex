\section{Results}
\begin{lemma}[subadditivity]\label{lem:mu}
$\mu(C)\le1$ for every source clause. If $E$ is derived in $R^-$ from premises $E_1$ (and $E_2$), then $\mu(E)\le\mu(E_1)+\mu(E_2)$. $\mu(\bot)=s^*(F)$, the minimum size of an unsatisfiable subformula.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Soundness: $S_1\models E_1$, $S_2\models E_2$ give $S_1\cup S_2\models E$. The bound for $\bot$ is the definition.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[boundary support, all object types]\label{lem:boundary}
Let $S$ be inclusion-minimal with $S\models E$, and let $x$ occur in exactly one clause $D\in S$. Then $x\in\mathrm{supp}(E)$.
\emph{Block version:} if $F$ is partitioned into blocks, $S$ is an inclusion-minimal union of blocks implying $E$, $x$ occurs in exactly one block $D$ of $S$, and every assignment falsifying $D$ satisfies $D$ after flipping $x$ (true for a parity constraint and any of its variables), the same conclusion holds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By minimality there is $\alpha\models S\setminus D$ with $\alpha\not\models E$, and then $\alpha\not\models D$. Flip $x$ to get $\alpha'$. It satisfies $D$ (the literal of $x$ in $D$ is false under $\alpha$; in the block version, by repairability). It still satisfies $S\setminus D$ because $x$ does not occur there. If $x\notin\mathrm{supp}(E)$, then $\alpha'\not\models E$ although $\alpha'\models S$, a contradiction. Nothing in the argument uses the syntactic type of $E$, so clauses, factors and GF(2) rows are all covered.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[mixed boundary width]\label{thm:width}
Suppose every $S\subseteq F$ with $|S|\le r$ is satisfiable and has at least $c|S|$ variables that occur exactly once in $S$. Then every $R^-$ refutation of $F$ contains an object $E$ with $|\mathrm{supp}(E)|>cr/2$. The same holds for blocks under the block hypotheses.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\mu(\bot)>r\ge 1\ge\mu(\text{source})$. Walk from $\bot$ toward the sources, always choosing a premise of maximal $\mu$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:mu} the first object reached with $\mu\le r$ has $\mu>r/2$. Take a minimal implying $S$ with $r/2<|S|\le r$ and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[narrow-row collapse]\label{thm:collapse}
If an $R^-$ refutation $\pi$ of $F$ has $W$ nodes and every GF(2) equation occurring in $\pi$ has support $\le k$, then $S_{\mathrm{Res}}(F)\le 2^{O(k)}\,W$.
Consequently, for the Chv\'atal--Szemer\'edi random 3-CNF family $F_n$ (density $\Delta$ above threshold, $S_{\mathrm{Res}}(F_n)\ge2^{\varepsilon n}$ w.h.p.), every $R^-$ refutation of size $W$ contains a GF(2) row of support $k\ge \Omega(\varepsilon n-\log_2 W)$. In particular every $R^-$ refutation of size $2^{o(n)}$ uses parity rows of linear support.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Replace each object by its CNF: an equation of support $k'\le k$ by its $2^{k'-1}$ width-$k'$ clauses, a 3-variable factor by $\le8$ clauses, a constant-support condition by $O(1)$ clauses. RES nodes are unchanged. An AND3 or LOCAL-CONVERT node becomes $O(1)$ Resolution steps (constant support, implicational completeness of Resolution plus weakening elimination, which only shortens a refutation). An XOR node with premises of supports $\le k$ has conclusion support $\le 2k$. Each of its $\le 2^{2k}$ target clauses $T$ is implied by the $\le 2^{k}$ premise clauses on $\le 2k$ variables. Refute (premise clauses) $\wedge\,\neg T$ by a decision tree on those variables, with $\le 2^{2k+1}$ Resolution steps. Each such step derives a subclause of $T$, which is then weakened away in the standard way. Each node therefore costs $2^{O(k)}$ Resolution steps, and the empty clause remains the root. For the corollary, take logarithms in $2^{\varepsilon n}\le 2^{ck}W$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Size--width transfer FALSIFIED for $R^-$.} For Resolution, Ben-Sasson--Wigderson turn width $w$ into size $2^{\Omega((w-w_0)^2/n)}$ via restrictions: a literal set true kills a wide clause. A restricted GF(2) row is never killed, only shortened. Exact counterexample: odd-charge Tseitin on simple connected bridgeless cubic expanders. In the block version of Theorem~\ref{thm:width} (blocks are vertex parity constraints, boundary variables are cut edges), every refutation needs a row of support $\ge\min_{h/3<|U|\le2h/3}|\delta(U)|=\Omega(h)$. Yet $W_{R^-}(F)=W_{R_{\mathrm{mix341}}}(F)=4h-1$ (LSPV-2/341, whose optimum uses no DEF). Hence \emph{wide objects are necessary but cheap} in the mixed system. Any superpolynomial lower bound for $R^-$, let alone $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$, must charge the \emph{linear-algebraic} content of wide rows, not their support. This is the same obstacle that general Res($\oplus$) lower bounds face.

\paragraph{Consequence for the hard-family search.} By Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}, the only way $R^-$ can beat the random-3-CNF Resolution bound is through linear-support parity rows. A random 3-CNF at constant density implies no source-local parity: each triple hosts $\le 3$ source clauses w.h.p., and $\ge 5$ remaining points of $\mathbb F_2^3$ lie in no affine hyperplane. So every row must be manufactured by AND3 from \emph{derived} narrow clauses. The precise open target is then:

\smallskip\noindent\textbf{OPEN target WCDC-T1.} Show that on $F_n$, any $R^-$ derivation of a support-$\Omega(n)$ row whose rows are all needed requires $2^{n^{\Omega(1)}}$ nodes. Or falsify this by an explicit subexponential row-manufacturing scheme. A proof would give $\overline W_{R^-}(N)\ge 2^{N^{\Omega(1)}}$ with $N=\Theta(n\log n)$.

\paragraph{Relation to Res($\oplus$) (CANDIDATE, not proved here).} Each $R^-$ rule is simulated in $O(1)$ lines by Res($\oplus$), whose lines are disjunctions of linear equations. So $R^-\le_p$ Res($\oplus$) is expected, and WCDC-T1 is implied by a general Res($\oplus$) lower bound for random 3-CNF. That is OPEN: current published bounds cover only regular, tree-like and bounded-depth Res($\oplus$). $R^-$ is plausibly strictly weaker, because it cannot keep disjunctions of rows. That makes WCDC-T1 a potentially easier, still unsolved, target.
