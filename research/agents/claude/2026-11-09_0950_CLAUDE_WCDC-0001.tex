\section{Setting}
A \emph{derivation system} $R$ is Cook--Reckhow if derivations $\pi$ are strings, $\pi\vdash_R F$ is decidable in time
$\mathrm{poly}(|\pi|+|F|)$, $R$ is sound, and every $F\in\UNSAT$ has a derivation. (Any inference system with
poly-time checkable rule applications on DAGs, including a multisign DAG-hypergraph system, is of this kind once
$\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$ is polynomially related to $|\pi|$.) Then
$\overline W_R(N)=\max_{|F|\le N}\min_{\pi}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$ is the proof-size function of $R$.
Granting the best derivation for free is exactly the nondeterminism already built into this definition:
the lower bound is a statement about witness size on the coNP side, and charging for search could only
raise the cost, so omitting it is sound for a lower bound. Write $\Bridge(R)$ for
$\SAT\in P\Rightarrow \overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$ and $\mathrm{LB}(R)$ for ``$\overline W_R$ is superpolynomial''.

\section{Results}
\begin{proposition}[Bridge collapse]\status{PROVED/EXACT}
If $\mathrm{LB}(R)$ is already a known theorem (Resolution, Polynomial Calculus, Cutting Planes, bounded-depth
Frege: Haken; Ben-Sasson--Wigderson; Pudl\'ak; Ajtai/Krajicek--Pudlak--Woods), then $\Bridge(R)\iff \SAT\notin P$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}$\Bridge(R)$ is $A\Rightarrow B$ with $A=$``$\SAT\in P$'' and $B$ false, so it is equivalent to $\neg A$. \qed\end{proof}
\noindent\emph{Consequence.} Hard-instance families that defeat Resolution/algebraic reasoning are useful lower-bound
evidence for \emph{those} systems, but a bridge to such a system is not a weaker sufficient step: it is P$\ne$NP itself.
The bridge is non-circular only for systems whose lower bound is open (Frege, Extended Frege, and stronger).

\begin{proposition}[Classification test]\status{PROVED/EXACT}
If a system $S$ with a known lower bound simulates $R$ (polynomial blow-up), then $\mathrm{LB}(R)$ holds and
Proposition~1 applies to $R$. Hence any candidate system (e.g.\ the combined multisign-hypergraph system) must first be
shown \emph{not} to be simulated by Resolution, PC, CP or bounded-depth Frege; otherwise its lower bound is known and its bridge equals P$\ne$NP.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}A poly-size $R$-derivation would convert to a poly-size $S$-derivation. \qed\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Optimal systems satisfy the bridge]\status{PROVED/EXACT}
If $R$ polynomially simulates every Cook--Reckhow system, then $\Bridge(R)$ holds, and $\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}\iff \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}If $\SAT\in P$ with decider $D$, the system $S=\{(F,\varepsilon): D(F)=\text{reject}\}$ is Cook--Reckhow
(sound because $D$ is correct) and has linear-size proofs; $R$ simulates it. Second claim: Cook--Reckhow. \qed\end{proof}
\noindent For optimal $R$, $\mathrm{LB}(R)\iff \mathrm{NP}\ne\mathrm{coNP}$, strictly more than needed. Existence of an optimal system
is itself open (Krajicek--Pudlak: implied by NE$=$coNE). So the route is only ``weakest'' for systems that are
\emph{not} optimal yet satisfy $\Bridge$.

\begin{proposition}[What the sufficient route is]\status{PROVED/EXACT}
$\Bridge(R)\wedge \mathrm{LB}(R)\Rightarrow \SAT\notin P$, and $\mathrm{LB}(R)$ alone does \emph{not} imply NP$\neq$coNP (a non-optimal $R$ may be super\-polynomial while some other system is poly-bounded).
The needed object is therefore a fixed $R$ with (i) a proved $\Bridge(R)$ and (ii) an open lower bound that is plausibly provable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Why proving $\Bridge(R)$ for a fixed $R$ is hard]\status{CANDIDATE obstruction, not a theorem}
Given a correct poly decider $A$, an $R$-derivation of $\neg F$ must certify $A(F)=\text{reject}\Rightarrow F\in\UNSAT$.
$R$ cannot know $A$ is correct; the derivation must contain a size-poly proof of correctness of $A$ on $F$. For EF this is
the statement that $A$'s correctness has poly-size EF proofs, i.e.\ the propositional translation of ``$\SAT\in P$ is provable in PV''.
Pich--Santhanam-type work treats exactly this axis. I record this as the precise gap: \textbf{$\Bridge(\mathrm{EF})$ holds if the correctness of some poly SAT algorithm is PV-provable, and is otherwise open}.
\end{remark}

\item Optimal proof system and Frege/EF lower bounds: no new result found; best known Frege size lower bound is still quadratic (Krajicek, \url{https://msekce.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~krajicek/frege.pdf}). \status{KNOWN, unchanged}.
\item Pich--Santhanam, \emph{Towards P$\ne$NP from Extended Frege lower bounds}, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08163}: abstract states that a concrete lower bound with no poly-size EF proof would give P$\ne$NP. This is the nearest published ``bridge-type'' theorem (EF-unprovability of a lower bound $\Rightarrow$ P$\ne$NP). Relevance to our $\Bridge$: \emph{useful structural analogy requiring a new transfer theorem}. Read from abstract only; full statement not yet audited.
\item Pich--Santhanam, \emph{Consistency of circuit lower bounds with bounded theories}, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12935}: conjecture that PV proves no correctness sentence for a poly SAT algorithm; implied by P$\ne$NP. Directly relevant to the remark above: it is the same unprovability that blocks a naive $\Bridge(\mathrm{EF})$ proof. \status{CONJECTURE in source}.
\item Polynomial Calculus with Extension Rule lower bound (Theory of Computing vol.\ 22, art.\ 4, \url{https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v022a004/}): algebraic-setting partial result; exact system/formula class not audited here, so no transfer is claimed. Lead to audit.
\item Proof Analysis Problem for EF is NP-complete (arXiv 2506.16956v2, \url{https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16956v2}); automatability link only; not a lower bound.
\end{itemize}
