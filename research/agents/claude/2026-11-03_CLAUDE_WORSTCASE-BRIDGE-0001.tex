\title{Worst-case derivation complexity: what the bridge $\mathrm{SAT}\in P\Rightarrow \overline W_R\le \mathrm{poly}$ can and cannot be}
\author{Claude, scheduled run 2026-11-03}\date{}\maketitle
\noindent\textbf{Status: P versus NP remains OPEN.} Nothing below is a lower bound.

\section*{Setup}
$R$ is a Cook--Reckhow system: a polynomial-time verifier $V(F,\pi)$ with $F\in\mathrm{UNSAT}\iff\exists\pi\,V(F,\pi)=1$; $\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$ is polynomially related to $|\pi|$. $\overline W_R(N)=\max_{|F|\le N}\min_\pi \mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$. Free strategy choice is legitimate for lower bounds: it is the min, and any search cost only adds. It makes the bound the strongest (hardest) form.
Let $B_R$ denote $(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP})\Rightarrow(\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly})$.

\begin{prop}[PROVED/EXACT, elementary]
(a) $\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$ iff $R$ is polynomially bounded; then $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$ (Cook--Reckhow).
(b) $B_R\iff(\mathrm P\neq\mathrm{NP})\lor(\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly})$.
(c) If $\overline W_R$ is a theorem to be superpolynomial (Resolution, Cutting Planes, bounded-depth Frege, Polynomial Calculus, \dots), then $B_R\iff \mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$.
\end{prop}
\emph{Proof.} (a) definition plus Cook--Reckhow. (b) $(A\Rightarrow B)\equiv(\lnot A\lor B)$. (c) second disjunct is false. $\square$

\textbf{Consequence.} For every system with a known unconditional lower bound the proposed weakest bridge is \emph{equivalent} to the target, not weaker. A non-circular program needs a bridge justified independently of $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$.

\section*{The only known non-circular bridge: provability}
For a sound theory $T$ (e.g.\ $PV$, $S^1_2$, ZFC) let $R_T$ have derivations $\pi=(p,\tau)$ where $p$ is a $T$-proof of $\mathrm{Corr}(M)$ (``$M$ decides SAT correctly'') and $\tau$ is an accepting trace of $M$ on $F$ answering UNSAT. $V$ is polynomial in $|\pi|$ and $R_T$ is sound.
\begin{prop}[PROVED/EXACT]
(a) If $T\vdash\mathrm{Corr}(M)$ for a polynomial-time $M$, then $\overline W_{R_T}\le |p|+O(T_M(N))=\mathrm{poly}(N)$. (b) $\overline W_{R_T}\le\mathrm{poly}\Rightarrow \mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$. (c) A proved superpolynomial $\overline W_{R_T}$ would show only that no \emph{nonuniformly short} $T$-certified algorithms exist; it would not give $\mathrm P\neq\mathrm{NP}$.
\end{prop}
So the true gap between the displayed bridge and a provable one is exactly \emph{provability of the algorithm's correctness}. For $T=PV$ this is the classical route to polynomial-size Extended Frege proofs (Cook; Krajicek--Pudlak): KNOWN THEOREM in outline, not re-derived here. Converse (b)$\to$uniformity fails: small $\overline W_{R_T}$ may use different $M_N$ for each $N$.

\section*{Literature check (2026-11-03)}
No 2025--26 source gives an Extended Frege lower bound or an optimal proof system (open; follows from NE=coNE by Krajicek--Pudlak). Relevant: Pich--Santhanam arXiv:2312.08163 (EF unprovability of circuit lower bounds implies P$\ne$NP under conditions); Arteche--Atserias--de Rezende--Khaniki arXiv:2506.16956 (EF and automatability); Alekseev, Theory of Computing 22 (2026) a004 (algebraic system simulating EF, lower bound for one principle only). Classification: analogies requiring a new transfer theorem.
