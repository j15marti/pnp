\section{Objective, restated}

The routine's directive: build the multisign DAG hypergraph of a 3-CNF $F$, reduce it with the
rules of a fixed inference system $R$, and charge the minimal work to reach the reduced state.
For $F\in\UNSAT$ the minimal reduced state is $\bot$, a reduction sequence is an $R$-derivation
$\pi:F\vdash_R\bot$, and
\[
  \Wb_R(N)=\max_{F\in\UNSAT,\ |\langle F\rangle|\le N}\ \min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\ \mathrm{cost}_R(\pi).
\]
Choosing $\pi$ freely is an existential quantifier: $\Wb_R$ is the \emph{proof-size} function of
$R$ (a nondeterministic measure). Throughout assume
(A1) $R$ is a Cook--Reckhow system (polynomial-time checkable, sound, complete for UNSAT CNF) and
(A2) $\mathrm{cost}_R$ and bit-size are polynomially related on every derivation.
Then $\Wb_R\le\mathrm{poly}$ iff $R$ is polynomially bounded; write $\Pbd(R)$.
Define $\LB(R):=\neg\Pbd(R)$ and $\Br(R):=(\SAT\in P\Rightarrow\Pbd(R))$.

The user's point that strategy discovery need not be charged is correct and is exactly what makes
$\Wb_R$ a valid lower bound on \emph{executing} any $R$-derivation. It is also why $\Wb_R$ alone
speaks only to algorithms whose runs \emph{are} $R$-derivations; for arbitrary algorithms all
of the work moves into $\Br(R)$. Sections 2--3 make that precise.

\section{Results}

\begin{theorem}[Bridge collapse]\label{thm:collapse}\status{PROVED/EXACT, elementary}
Under (A1)--(A2), for every $R$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\Br(R)\iff\bigl(P\ne NP\ \vee\ \Pbd(R)\bigr)$.
\item If $\LB(R)$ is a theorem, then $\Br(R)\iff P\ne NP$.
\item $\Br(R)$ is true in Heuristica, Pessiland, Minicrypt and Cryptomania (vacuously); its
only content is the Algorithmica statement $P=NP\Rightarrow\Pbd(R)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(1) $\Br(R)$ is the material implication $\neg(P\ne NP)\to\Pbd(R)$, i.e.\
$(P\ne NP)\vee\Pbd(R)$, using $\SAT\in P\iff P=NP$.
(2) With $\neg\Pbd(R)$ a theorem, the disjunction reduces to $P\ne NP$.
(3) Immediate from (1).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No weakening for systems with known lower bounds]\status{PROVED/EXACT}
For $R\in\{$tree-like Resolution, Resolution, Cutting Planes, Polynomial Calculus,
bounded-depth Frege$\}$, where superpolynomial (indeed exponential) $\Wb_R$ is a
\status{KNOWN THEOREM} (Haken 1985; Urquhart 1987; Chv\'atal--Szemer\'edi 1988; Pudl\'ak 1997;
Razborov 1998; Ajtai 1988, Beame--Impagliazzo--Pitassi, Kraj\'i\v{c}ek--Pudl\'ak--Woods 1995),
proving $\Br(R)$ \emph{is} proving $P\ne NP$. Any valid proof of $\Br(R)$ must turn a hypothetical
polynomial-time SAT decider into polynomial-size $R$-refutations, which for these $R$ is a proof
by contradiction that the decider does not exist.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Trade-off along p-simulation]\label{thm:order}\status{PROVED/EXACT}
If $R'$ p-simulates $R$, then $\Pbd(R)\Rightarrow\Pbd(R')$, hence $\Br(R)\Rightarrow\Br(R')$ and
$\LB(R')\Rightarrow\LB(R)$. Consequently the two load-bearing arrows move in opposite directions:
$\LB(R)$ gets easier as $R$ gets weaker, $\Br(R)$ gets easier as $R$ gets stronger, and for every
$R$, $\LB(R)\wedge\Br(R)\Rightarrow P\ne NP$ while $P\ne NP\Rightarrow\Br(R)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A p-simulation maps each $R$-refutation of $F$ to an $R'$-refutation of $F$ in polynomial time, so
polynomial $R$-sizes give polynomial $R'$-sizes. The rest is Theorem~\ref{thm:collapse}(1).
\end{proof}

So the ``weakest sufficient bridge'' is weakest for the strongest $R$. Among standard systems that is
Extended Frege (EF), where the lower bound is wide open. The project's REFEREE365 already showed that
for the CANON2R completion, B3 is exactly $\SAT\in P\Rightarrow\Pbd(\mathrm{EF})$; Theorem~\ref{thm:order}
says that no weaker system can give an easier bridge.

\begin{proposition}[Strongest known form of the EF bridge]\status{KNOWN THEOREM}
(Cook 1975; Kraj\'i\v{c}ek--Pudl\'ak 1990.) EF p-simulates every proof system $Q$ whose reflection
principle is provable in $S^1_2$ (equivalently, for this purpose, in $PV$). In particular, if $A$ is a
polynomial-time algorithm and $PV$ proves ``$A(F)=0\Rightarrow F$ has no satisfying assignment'', then
EF is polynomially bounded.
\end{proposition}

Hence the missing step in $\Br(\mathrm{EF})$ is precisely:
\[
\textbf{FEAS-P (OPEN):}\quad P=NP\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{some polynomial-time SAT decider has }
S^1_2\text{-provable soundness.}
\]
FEAS-P has the same half-win profile as $\Br(\mathrm{EF})$ (vacuous outside Algorithmica). Its
contrapositive use is the known one: an EF lower bound shows that no \emph{$PV$-verified} SAT decider
exists, i.e.\ consistency of $P\ne NP$ with $PV$ (Cook--Kraj\'i\v{c}ek line; see also Pich--Santhanam,
arXiv:2312.08163, for EF-to-circuit-lower-bound transfers under extra axioms). It does not give
$P\ne NP$.

\begin{proposition}[Where the pointwise bridge does hold]\status{KNOWN THEOREM}
If every run of an algorithm $A$ on $F\in\UNSAT$ can be converted in polynomial time into an
$R$-refutation of size $\le\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{time}_A(F))$, then
$\mathrm{time}_A$ on size-$N$ inputs is at least $\Wb_R(N)^{\Omega(1)}$. Instances: DPLL and
tree-like Resolution; CDCL with restarts and Resolution (Pipatsrisawat--Darwiche 2011;
Atserias--Fichte--Thurley 2011). So CDCL-type solvers need $2^{\Omega(N/\log N)}$ time on Tseitin and
random 3-CNF families unconditionally. This is the project's ``secondary pointwise simulation target''
realised for certifying algorithms; it says nothing about non-certifying algorithms.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Charging search (literature check).}
Not charging the search for the optimal derivation is legitimate for lower bounds, and the opposite gap is
real: Resolution (Atserias--M\"uller 2019), Cutting Planes (G\"o\"os--Koroth--Mertz--Pitassi 2020),
Nullstellensatz/PC/Sherali--Adams and Res(2) are not automatable unless $P=NP$. Thus
$\min_\pi\mathrm{cost}(\pi)$ can be exponentially smaller than the cost of \emph{finding} $\pi$, which is
one more reason $\Wb_R$ lower bounds do not by themselves reach general algorithms.

\section{Computation: exact minima at small $N$}

\status{COMPUTATIONAL} \texttt{research/agents/claude/bridge/tree\_res\_exact.py} computes, by memoised search
over all partial assignments, the exact minimum size of a tree-like Resolution refutation (minimum decision
tree for the false-clause search problem; equality with weakening-free tree-like refutation size is a
\status{KNOWN THEOREM}). This is the directive's ``minimum over all reduction sequences, search not charged''
evaluated exactly. Data: \texttt{bridge/2026-11-13\_0348\_tree\_res\_exact\_results.json}.

\end{center}

Controls: every instance is checked UNSAT by brute force before minimisation; the full-clause family matches
the closed form $2^{n+1}-1$. PHP values fit $(4^{m+1}-1)/3$ for $m\le3$, but this pattern \emph{must} fail:
the known tree-like bound for PHP is $2^{\Theta(m\log m)}$, so extrapolating it would be wrong. The random
3-CNF minima at $n\le14$ show no visible exponential trend, although $2^{\Omega(n)}$ is a theorem
(Chv\'atal--Szemer\'edi; Ben-Sasson--Wigderson). Lesson for LSPV-3/LSPV-5: finite exact minima, however
exact, cannot certify or refute superpolynomial growth of $\Wb_R$; they are hostile controls only.
Runtime: one process per instance; the $n=14$ runs took 46--48\,s each, over the 30\,s guideline
(recorded rather than hidden; future runs should cap at $n=12$ or split by first branching variable).
