\noindent\textbf{Status.} PROVED/EXACT (standard, re-derived): Props.~1--3. OPEN: any superpolynomial lower bound for a system $R$ for which the bridge is provable; the bridge itself for such $R$. \textbf{P versus NP remains OPEN.}

\section*{Setup}
$R$ is a fixed finite-rule system whose derivation DAGs are checkable in time polynomial in their size (a Cook--Reckhow system). $W_R(F)=\min_\pi \mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$ with cost = number of paid nodes (size up to poly). $\overline W_R(N)$ as in the directive. Free choice of the optimal $\pi$ is allowed.

\begin{proposition}[Free search is harmless]
$\overline W_R$ is polynomially bounded iff $R$ is a polynomially bounded proof system; then $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$. Charging for finding $\pi$ cannot lower the minimum, so lower bounds on $\overline W_R$ are exactly proof-size lower bounds.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Proof.} Definition of Cook--Reckhow; $\mathrm{UNSAT}\in\mathrm{NP}$ via the verifier. \qed

\begin{proposition}[Bridge from provable correctness]
Let $A$ be a poly-time SAT decider and let $\mathrm{Cor}_{A,n}$ be the propositional translation of ``if $A$'s trace on a formula of length $n$ rejects, the formula is false''. If $R$ is Frege-like (closed under substitution and modus ponens) and $\mathrm{Cor}_{A,n}$ has $R$-proofs of size $\mathrm{poly}(n)$, then $\overline W_R\le \mathrm{poly}$.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Proof.} Given $F$, write the $\mathrm{poly}(|F|)$ trace of $A$ as an extension-variable computation (for $R\supseteq$ EF), substitute into $\mathrm{Cor}_{A,n}$, and detach. \qed\ (Cook; Krajíček--Pudlák.)

\begin{proposition}[Bridge is no weaker than $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$ for weak $R$]
Let $\mathrm{LB}(R)$: $\overline W_R$ is superpolynomial; $\mathrm{Br}(R)$: $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P\Rightarrow \overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$. Then $\mathrm{LB}(R)\wedge\mathrm{Br}(R)\Rightarrow \mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$, and if $\mathrm{LB}(R)$ is a theorem, $\mathrm{Br}(R)\iff \mathrm{SAT}\notin\mathrm P$. In particular for Resolution (Haken, exponential bounds) $\mathrm{Br}$ is equivalent to $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Proof.} Contrapositive of $\mathrm{Br}$ with $\mathrm{LB}$; if $\mathrm{LB}$ holds, $\mathrm{Br}$ has a false conclusion, so it is equivalent to its hypothesis failing. \qed

\section*{Consequence (the real target)}
A bridge can only be useful for $R$ whose lower bound is itself open, so the programme needs simultaneously (i) $\mathrm{Br}(R)$ and (ii) $\mathrm{LB}(R)$ for a system at least as strong as Extended Frege; (ii) implies $\mathrm{NP}\ne\mathrm{coNP}$, strictly stronger than $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$ in logical form. Prop.~2 gives $\mathrm{Br}(\mathrm{EF})$ under the hypothesis that $\mathrm{PV}$ (equivalently EF uniformly) proves correctness of \emph{some} poly-time SAT decider. That hypothesis is not implied by $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm P$ (Levin-type universal search handles search, not refutation certificates) and is an open Krajíček--Pudlák/Cook--Krajíček-type question. Hence the weakest sufficient bridge is still unproved. A hostile test for any new hard family: it must beat EF-style extension, not just resolution, polynomial calculus, or shared DAGs.
