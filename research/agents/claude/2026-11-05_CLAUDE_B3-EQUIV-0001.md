## Claim 1 (B3 is equivalent to P != NP once the lower bound is known)
Fix R and weights. Let LB := "Wbar_R is not polynomially bounded". Let B3 := "SAT in P => Wbar_R is polynomially bounded".
Since SAT is NP-complete, SAT in P <=> P = NP. Hence B3 <=> (P != NP) or (Wbar_R poly), i.e. B3 <=> P != NP or not-LB.
**Consequently LB and B3 together are equivalent to LB and P != NP.** B3 is the weakest sufficient bridge only in the sense that it is also *necessary*: given LB, proving B3 is exactly as hard as proving P != NP.
Proof: propositional logic on the two statements; no complexity input beyond NP-completeness of SAT. QED.

## Corollaries
1. **Systems with known superpolynomial lower bounds are useless for B3.** For Resolution (Haken 1985), LB is a theorem, so "B3 for Resolution" is *equivalent* to P != NP. Any claimed proof of it must be a proof of P != NP in disguise. Same for Cutting Planes, bounded-depth Frege, etc. This sharpens the existing B1-refutation remark (parity recognizer + exhaustive fallback): the B3 failure mode for weak R is not a counterexample but vacuity-by-equivalence.
2. **A proof of B3 must be non-vacuous:** it must work inside the hypothetical world SAT in P, starting from an unknown correct poly-time A, and produce poly-size R-derivations. R must therefore prove (feasibly) the soundness of A's UNSAT answers, with no knowledge of A's code beyond existence. Existence-only is the CONDITIONAL-BRIDGE GAP already flagged in the refocus directive: P = NP gives NP = coNP, hence *some* polynomially bounded system (Cook–Reckhow 1979), not the preselected R.
3. **The only candidates for R are systems whose polynomial-boundedness is not already decided by known lower bounds** (Extended-Frege-like strength or beyond); for those, LB and B3 are both open and, by Claim 1, tied together.
