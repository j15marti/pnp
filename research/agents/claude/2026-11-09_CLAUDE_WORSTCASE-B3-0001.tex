\section{Monotone trade-off}
\begin{proposition}[PROVED/EXACT]\label{p:mono}
If $R'$ p-simulates $R$, then $\W_{R'}\le \mathrm{poly}(\W_R)$; hence $B3(R)\Rightarrow B3(R')$ and $LB(R')\Rightarrow LB(R)$.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Proof.} Simulation maps an $R$-refutation of size $s$ to an $R'$-refutation of size $\mathrm{poly}(s)$; take min and max. $\square$

So bridges get easier upward and lower bounds get easier downward; a useful $R$ must sit at a threshold where both are provable.

\section{No-slack theorem}
\begin{proposition}[PROVED/EXACT]\label{p:noslack}
(a) If $LB(R)$ holds as a theorem (e.g.\ Resolution, Cutting Planes, Polynomial Calculus, bounded-depth Frege/AC$^0$-Frege), then $B3(R)\iff \mathrm{SAT}\notin P$.
(b) If $\W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$ then $B3(R)$ holds and $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$.
(c) $P\neq NP\iff\exists R\,[B3(R)\wedge LB(R)]$.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Proof.} (a) $B3(R)$ is $\neg\mathrm{SAT}\in P\vee\W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$; the second disjunct is false. (b) Cook--Reckhow. (c) ($\Leftarrow$) $B3\wedge LB$ gives $\mathrm{SAT}\notin P$.
($\Rightarrow$) take $R=$ Resolution. $\square$

\begin{corollary}[PROVED/EXACT]
For every system with a known superpolynomial lower bound, \emph{proving $B3$ is exactly as hard as proving $\mathrm{SAT}\notin P$}; the
relaxation ``free optimal derivation'' gives no saving there. A non-circular program must prove $B3$ by a \emph{simulation} argument
(an algorithm yields a derivation) and take $R$ at least as strong as the systems with unconditional bounds, i.e.\ Frege/Extended Frege
or beyond, where $LB(R)$ is itself open. Hence this program has two independent open arrows ($LB(R)$ and a simulation proof of $B3(R)$);
neither is implied by the other.
\end{corollary}
\status{PROVED/EXACT}. This also explains why the Resolution parity/GF(2) bypass refutes only B1: the bypass algorithm is not worst-case correct-and-fast.

\section{The reflection route (KNOWN THEOREM, restated)}
For a polytime decider $A$ with circuits $A_N$ (size $s(N)$), let $\Phi_{A,N}(x,a)$ say: extension variables compute $A_N(x)$ and the evaluation of CNF$(x)$ at $a$,
output $A_N(x)=$``unsat'', and CNF$(x)(a)=1$. $\Phi_{A,N}$ is unsatisfiable iff $A$ never says ``unsat'' on a satisfiable input (soundness).
\begin{proposition}[KNOWN THEOREM, Cook 1975; Krajicek--Pudlak; proof sketch]\label{p:refl}
Let $R\supseteq$ EF-like (closed under restriction, allows definitional extension, unit-propagates circuit evaluation in $\mathrm{poly}(s)$ steps).
If $A$ decides SAT correctly and $\Phi_{A,N}$ has $R$-refutations of size $q(N)$, then $\W_R(N)\le q(N)+\mathrm{poly}(s(N))$.
\end{proposition}
\emph{Sketch.} For UNSAT $F$, $|F|=N$, $A_N(F)=$``unsat'' by completeness. Restrict $x:=F$ in the refutation: definitional clauses unit-propagate to the
true output, leaving $F$'s clauses plus definitions; this is a refutation of $F$. Soundness of $A$ is used \emph{inside} the single proof of $\Phi_{A,N}$;
completeness of $A$ is used only to know $A_N(F)=$``unsat''. $\square$

Consequences (\status{CONDITIONAL}/\status{ANALOGY}): (i) $B3(\mathrm{EF})$ follows if SAT has a polytime decider whose soundness is feasibly
provable in EF (PV-provable, in Cook's translation). (ii) The gap is exactly \emph{feasible provability of the correctness of a correct algorithm}; a trace alone is not a certificate (coordination note, B3 caveat). (iii) The ``fix'' of adding all true soundness statements as axioms is the prohibited hidden validity oracle: the axiom set would not be polytime recognizable without further hypotheses. (iv) Combining with 335 Thm 2: any $R$ with an explicit poly-generated hard UNSAT family cannot satisfy B1; Prop.~\ref{p:refl} shows what B3 would additionally need---a uniform \emph{proof} of soundness, not merely existence of a fast $A$.

\section{Time--space audit and red-team notes}

\item Cost direction: DAG size $S$ gives verification time $\widetilde O(S\cdot w)$ and space $O(\text{max line size})$ on a RAM; this upper-bounds checking, not search. No claim on SAT-decider time/space follows from $\W_R$ alone; transfer to algorithm classes needs R to simulate that class (e.g.\ DPLL traces $\mapsto$ tree-like Resolution).
\item Red-team falsifier for any proposed $R$: (1) is there a known unconditional bound? then Prop.~\ref{p:noslack}(a) says B3 is circular; (2) is there an explicit generator hard family? then B1 fails; (3) does the B3 argument use only a trace and not a proved soundness statement? then reject.
\item Hard-instance sanity: the 3-SAT hard families (Tseitin on expanders, pigeonhole, random 3-CNF) are hard for Resolution but have polynomial EF proofs (Tseitin, PHP) or are open (random $k$-CNF in EF); hence none is a candidate for $LB(\mathrm{EF})$. Random $k$-CNF and Nisan--Wigderson-type formulas remain the standard candidates (cited literature, not re-verified).
\end{itemize}
