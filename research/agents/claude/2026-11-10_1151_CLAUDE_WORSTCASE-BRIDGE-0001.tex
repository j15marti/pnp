\section{Target, as assigned}
\[
\overline W_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},\ |\langle F\rangle|\le N}\ \min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\operatorname{cost}_R(\pi),
\qquad
\mathrm{Bridge}(R):\ \ \mathrm{SAT}\in P\implies \overline W_R(N)\le\mathrm{poly}(N).
\]
The plan is: (i) prove $\overline W_R(N)\ge N^{\omega(1)}$; (ii) prove $\mathrm{Bridge}(R)$; then $\mathrm{SAT}\notin P$. By Cook--Reckhow, $\overline W_R\le\mathrm{poly}$ is exactly the statement that $R$ is polynomially bounded.

\section{Results}
\begin{lemma}[Bridge-collapse dichotomy; PROVED/EXACT, elementary]
Let $R$ be any fixed sound and complete Cook--Reckhow proof system.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If (i) is already a theorem for $R$, then $\mathrm{Bridge}(R)$ is logically equivalent to $P\ne NP$.
This holds for Resolution (Haken 1985), Cutting Planes (Pudlák 1997), Polynomial Calculus, and bounded-depth Frege.
\item If $R$ p-simulates Extended Frege, then step (i) for $R$ is at least as hard as an EF lower bound, which is a decades-old open problem.
The corpus treats $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ (RES plus paid two-input DEF) as such a system; root \texttt{main.tex} records it as PROVED via the EF$\to$ER$\to$RES+DEF simulation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(1) Given (i), $\mathrm{Bridge}(R)$ yields $P\ne NP$ by contraposition. Conversely, $P\ne NP$ makes the antecedent of $\mathrm{Bridge}(R)$ false, so the implication holds vacuously.
(2) A polynomial upper bound for EF would transfer to $R$ through the p-simulation. Hence a superpolynomial $\overline W_R$ gives a superpolynomial EF lower bound.
\end{proof}

\noindent\emph{Consequence.} No choice of $R$ makes the bridge \emph{strictly weaker} than the goal while (i) is already known.
The decomposition helps only for $R\ge$ EF, where (i) carries most of the difficulty.

\begin{corollary}[What step (i) alone buys for $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$; KNOWN THEOREM chain, not original]
If $\overline W_{R_{\mathrm{mix341}}}(N)\ge N^{\omega(1)}$, then EF is not polynomially bounded.
By Cook's translation (PV-provable $\forall$-statements have polynomial EF proofs) and the Cook--Krajíček equivalence (PV$\vdash$P$=$NP iff PV defines a polytime map returning a satisfying assignment or an EF refutation), it follows that \emph{PV does not prove $P=NP$}.
\end{corollary}
\noindent This is a genuine metamathematical milestone, but it is \textbf{not} $P\ne NP$.
The bridge is exactly the gap between ``no PV-verified polytime SAT algorithm'' and ``no polytime SAT algorithm.''
The corpus already records the conditional positive form (root \texttt{main.tex}, LSPV-1/357: a PV/$S^1_2$-provably rejection-sound decider gives polynomial $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ refutations).
The corpus does not appear to state this corollary in its negative form.

\begin{lemma}[Scope of ``free best sequence''; PROVED/EXACT]
For any algorithm $\mathcal A$ whose run on UNSAT $F$ can be converted, in time polynomial in its runtime, into an $R$-refutation, $\mathrm{time}_{\mathcal A}(F)\ge \overline W_R$ up to polynomial factors on the worst $F$.
Charging zero for strategy discovery is sound here.
Instances include DPLL with tree-like Resolution, and CDCL with Resolution (Beame--Kautz--Sabharwal 2004; Pipatsrisawat--Darwiche 2011).
For an arbitrary algorithm, the existence of such a conversion \emph{is} $\mathrm{Bridge}(R)$.
The free-strategy principle therefore adds no leverage on the bridge.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The minimum over all derivations lower-bounds the cost of any particular derivation, including the one extracted from $\mathcal A$.
The hypothesis is exactly what must be supplied for arbitrary $\mathcal A$.
\end{proof}

\section{Computation (COMPUTATIONAL; sanity only)}
\texttt{research/agents/claude/worstcase-bridge/tree\_res\_exact.py} computes the exact minimum tree-like Resolution refutation size.
It does so as the minimum decision-tree size for the falsified-clause search, by dynamic programming over $3^n$ partial assignments.
Each stage runs in under 5\,s, split into two stages: \texttt{php} and \texttt{rand}.
Results: PHP$^2_1$: 5 nodes; PHP$^3_2$: 21; PHP$^4_3$ (12 variables, 22 clauses): 85.
For 40 random unsatisfiable 3-CNFs at clause ratio 6, the maximum is 23, 27 and 31 nodes for $n=6,7,8$, against a trivial bound of $2^{n+1}-1$.
These are tiny-$n$ artifacts with no asymptotic content. They only serve as a checked baseline for later DAG experiments.
Raw data: \texttt{results\_php.json}, \texttt{results\_rand.json}.
