\section*{Setting (unchanged from the 2026-11-02 directive)}
Fix one sound, complete Cook--Reckhow-style system $R$ with a fixed cost convention, polynomially related to encoded proof length. Let
\[
 \Wb_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},\ |\langle F\rangle|\le N}\ \min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi).
\]
The inner minimum gives free, perfect choice of derivation. As the user says, the cost of finding it is
not charged. A lower bound on $\Wb_R$ is therefore exactly a worst-case proof-size lower bound for $R$.
Write
\[
 \PB_R:\ \exists C,k\ \forall N\ \Wb_R(N)\le C(N+1)^k,\qquad
 \LB_R:\ \neg\PB_R,\qquad
 \mathrm{B3}_R:\ \SAT\in\mathrm P\ \Rightarrow\ \PB_R .
\]

\section{Exact logical form}
\begin{theorem}[B3 is a disjunction]\label{t:disj}
For every fixed $R$: $\mathrm{B3}_R \iff (\SAT\notin\mathrm P)\ \lor\ \PB_R$.
Consequently $\mathrm{B3}_R\wedge \LB_R \iff \SAT\notin\mathrm P\wedge\LB_R$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
This is the material-implication identity $(p\Rightarrow q)\equiv(\neg p\vee q)$, with $p=(\SAT\in\mathrm P)$ and
$q=\PB_R$. The second clause follows because $\LB_R=\neg\PB_R$ eliminates the disjunct $\PB_R$.
\end{proof}

So the bridge is not an independent ``transfer'' lemma that sits between proof complexity and machines.
It is the statement ``either SAT is not in P, or $R$ is polynomially bounded''. Anyone proving it must,
in every world where SAT is in P (Algorithmica), produce polynomial $R$-refutations of \emph{all}
UNSAT formulas. That makes the bridge a \emph{reflection} statement. This matches the corpus's
existing reflection routes (LSPV-1/336, /353, /357, /361, /406; REFEREE343). Those routes reached
this form for specific architectures. Theorem~\ref{t:disj} shows that no other form is possible.

\begin{corollary}[Known-lower-bound collapse]\label{c:collapse}
If $\LB_R$ is a theorem (provable in ZFC), then $\mathrm{B3}_R \iff \SAT\notin\mathrm P$ (provably in ZFC).
More generally, if a system $S$ with a proved $\LB_S$ p-simulates $R$, then $\mathrm{B3}_R\iff\SAT\notin\mathrm P$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $S$ p-simulates $R$, then $\PB_R\Rightarrow\PB_S$, and $\PB_S$ is refuted. Apply Theorem~\ref{t:disj}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Resolution control test for any proposed B3 argument]\label{t:control}
Let $\Phi$ be a proof of $\mathrm{B3}_R$ that uses only properties of $R$ that ordinary Resolution
also has (soundness, completeness, polynomial-time checking, locality, DAG sharing, free choice of the
optimal derivation, free source clauses, etc.). Then the same argument yields $\mathrm{B3}_{\mathrm{Res}}$.
By Haken (1985), $\LB_{\mathrm{Res}}$ is a KNOWN THEOREM, so $\Phi$ proves $\SAT\notin\mathrm P$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The argument transfers by hypothesis. Then apply Corollary~\ref{c:collapse} with $S=R=\mathrm{Res}$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Operational rule (red-team checklist item, adopted for this routine).}
Every proposed B3 step must name the property of $R$ that \emph{fails for Resolution} and that the step
uses. For $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$, the only candidate in the corpus is extension (DEF/VARDEF). It lets
$R$ simulate EF proofs of PV/$S^1_2$-provable soundness statements, by Cook's 1975 translation. A step
whose justification never uses such a property is either a proof of P$\neq$NP, which must then be
audited as one, or it is wrong. This test is cheap, and it is stronger than the existing ``four B3
quantifier worlds'' controls: those check quantifier order, while this checks whether the argument has
any content specific to $R$.

\noindent\textbf{Reading the table.} If $R$ is p-simulated by any system in the upper block, the
two-step plan ``prove $\LB_R$, then prove $\mathrm{B3}_R$'' gives no real reduction in difficulty: its
second step is P$\neq$NP itself. The plan gives a nontrivial decomposition only for $R$ at or beyond
the open frontier. $R_{\mathrm{mix341}}$ includes extension and has short BPHP and odd-Tseitin proofs
(LSPV-3/346; REFEREE343). By LSPV-1/592, if its grammar is authenticated, P2-B1 would imply an
EF-size lower bound, which has been open since Cook--Reckhow (1979). Pich--Santhanam (FOCS 2019)
show that EF lower bounds face natural-proofs-type obstacles. So for the project's chosen $R$ both
halves are hard. This is not a defect of the choice: Corollary~\ref{c:collapse} shows that no choice of
$R$ makes both halves easier than P$\neq$NP with currently known lower bounds.

\begin{proposition}[Where the difficulty sits, KNOWN-based]\label{p:pv}
Let $R$ p-simulate ER on CNF refutations. If some polynomial-time SAT decider $A$ has a PV-provable
rejection-soundness statement, then $\PB_R$ holds (Cook 1975; corpus LSPV-1/357). Hence, for such $R$,
\[
 \mathrm{B3}_R \ \Longleftarrow\ \bigl[\SAT\in\mathrm P\Rightarrow \exists A\in\mathrm P\ \text{deciding SAT with }
 \mathrm{PV}\vdash\mathrm{RS}_A\bigr].
\]
The bracket says that P$=$NP, if true, is witnessed by a PV-verifiable algorithm. It is \textbf{OPEN}, and
it is the exact remaining content of B3 along the Cook-translation route. Pich--Santhanam 2023
(Theorem~10, antichecker variant) is a different sufficient condition and is already recorded in LSPV-1/406.
\end{proposition}
