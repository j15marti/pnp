\section{A charged phase-acquisition adapter}
Fix one uniformly enumerated list of $k(N)\ge1$ sound deterministic policies
for an encoded state $x$ of bit length $N$. Policies may adapt parameters.
Accepted outputs are verified answers or complete source-valid phase plans
satisfying a declared interface. Partial plans and timeouts are UNKNOWN.
UNSAT requires a sound decision or a complete negative cover, never sampled
negative branches.

Apply a common hard workspace cap $S(N)$ to every attempted guest prefix,
including temporary normalization, indexes, derived input, replay and output
staging. Abort before an allocation violates the cap. Use a declared charged
simulator: initialization, verification and clearing are included.
Let $\lambda(N,B)$ bound physical overhead per budgeted step through budget
$B$, and let $E(N)$ bound enumeration and dispatch per attempt. These are
costs to be proved for an implementation, not free operations.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional capped restart adapter]
If some policy completes a valid output within $t\ge1$ budgeted steps while
respecting $S(N)$, serial rounds of budgets $1,2,4,\ldots$, restarting every
policy, reach a valid output with fewer than $4k(N)t$ guest steps.
Physical time is
\[
 O\big(k(N)t\lambda(N,2t)+k(N)E(N)(1+\log(t+1))\big).
\]
Peak space is the guest cap plus simulator/dispatch state and
$O(\log k(N)+\log(t+1))$ counter bits, on this same execution.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $B$ be the first power of two at least $t$, so $B<2t$.
The total budgets through that round sum to $k(2B-1)<4kt$.
The successful policy is neither timed out nor space-aborted in that round.
There are at most $k(1+\lceil\log_2t\rceil)$ attempts. Charge each prefix,
initialization, verification and replay to its budget; multiply by the
declared simulator overhead and add dispatch. Only one guest is live.
Reset includes clearing touched state or a separately charged initialization
discipline. Any retained cache or parent frame remains in the space ledger.
\end{proof}

This is standard dovetailing, recovered rather than a new scheduling theorem.
For a fixed compiled finite portfolio, bounded native simulation can have
polylogarithmic counter overhead. For input-dependent program lists, no
constant-overhead universal interpreter is assumed.

For plan-only acquisition, assume uniformly at every reached state that one
candidate completes within $N^{r+o(1)}$ steps, that $k(N)\le N^{q+o(1)}$,
$E(N)\le N^{r+o(1)}$, and $\lambda=N^{o(1)}$.
Assume every accepted plan has maximum child length $N^{h+o(1)}$,
aggregate volume $N^{v+o(1)}$, $h<1$, paid exact access exponent $g$,

\section{Primary literature and exact endpoint}

and expected time relative to an optimal restart schedule. This is a useful
ANALOGY/CANDIDATE, not deterministic worst-case SAT coverage.

FLMV uses random-access Turing machines, indexed read-only input and
one-directional write-only output. Work and index tapes count as space.

\[
 \mathrm{NTIME}[n]\not\subseteq
 \mathrm{DTISP}[n^c,o(n^{d(c)})],\quad
 d(c)=\tfrac12((c+2)/c^2-c).
\]

slightly larger $c$ with $d(c)>1/2$, absorbing polylogarithms in strict slack.
