\section{A genuine new partial member}
Call $x$ a singleton strong Krom backdoor of a proper 3CNF $F$ when deleting
all occurrences of $x$ and $\neg x$ from every clause leaves width at most
two.  Equivalently, both restrictions $F[x=0]$ and $F[x=1]$ are Krom.

\textsc{STRONG-KROM-CUT1} operates as follows.  If $F$ has a width-three
clause, inspect its at most three variables and, by a complete source scan,
retain the first one that occurs in every width-three clause.  If there is
no such variable, return UNKNOWN.  For each $b\in\{0,1\}$, dry-run
$F[x=b]$, enumerate its variable-incidence components using the same
step-capped read-only enumerator proved for \textsc{PIVOT-FACTOR1}, and
return UNKNOWN if a component exceeds
\begin{equation}
 B(N)=N^{1/2}(\log N)^{O(1)}.                            \tag{2}
\end{equation}
Otherwise replay one component at a time and solve it by a conventional
linear-time, linear-space 2-SAT algorithm.  Accept the branch iff all its
components are satisfiable, and accept $F$ iff at least one branch accepts.

\begin{theorem}[charged singleton-Krom cut]\label{thm:krom}
There is one total source-first implementation which, on every input,
either returns UNKNOWN within
\[
 T_{\rm local}(N)\le N^{6/5+o(1)},\qquad
 S_{\rm peak}(N)\le N^{1/2+o(1)},
\]
or correctly decides $\SAT(F)$.  On every DECIDE input it executes zero
ordinary-SAT children.  Discovery, all three failed candidates, both
restrictions, component scans, replay, headers, indexes and solver buffers
are included.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every singleton strong Krom backdoor must meet every width-three clause, so
the candidates occur in any one such clause.  A scan verifies a candidate;
there are at most three.  The two restrictions are Krom, their components
partition their retained clause occurrences, and
\begin{equation}
 \SAT(F)=\bigvee_{b\in\{0,1\}}
            \bigwedge_{C\in\operatorname{Comp}(F[x=b])}\SAT(C).     \tag{3}
\end{equation}
Each source clause produces at most one residual occurrence in each branch,
so the aggregate replay volume, including component headers, is
$N^{1+o(1)}$.  The step-capped implicit component enumerator uses
$N^{6/5+o(1)}$ time and $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ state even on rejection.  One 2-SAT
component of size at most (2) is buffered and solved at a time; hence its
linear state is only $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ relative to the root, and the sum of its
linear running times is $N^{1+o(1)}$.  This proves both correctness and the
joint ledger without silently asserting a sublinear-space 2-SAT algorithm
at the child's own scale.
\end{proof}

This repairs the sign-balanced control from Recovery8.  In
\texttt{balanced\_lifted\_cross} every clause contains $x=1$.  Removing $x$
leaves Krom; after either assignment the residual factors into
constant-variable components.  The positive half is equivalent to $x$ and
the negative half to $\neg x$, so the complete formula is UNSAT and is
decided by Theorem~\ref{thm:krom}.  Thus the portfolio has strictly grown,
but only on a certified partial domain.

The earlier iterative-pure executable stores a sign table for every active

\section{The exact all-input selector and why it does not close (1)}
Fix one deterministic RAM SAT decider $D$ with time $N^{K+o(1)}$, as allowed
by the assumption $\SAT\in\mathrm P$.  On a general residual $F_j$, choose
the least occurring variable $x_j$, execute $D(F_j[x_j=0])$, and retain that
restriction when the answer is YES; otherwise retain $F_j[x_j=1]$.  Stop
when syntax decides the formula or the residual has size at most $N^h$.

\begin{lemma}[selector semantics]
For every CNF, including UNSAT inputs, the retained residual has the same
satisfiability value as its parent.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $D(F[x=0])$ is YES, then both the parent and the retained child are
satisfiable.  If it is NO, $F$ is satisfiable exactly when $F[x=1]$ is.
Induction proves the claim; on UNSAT, both restrictions are UNSAT.
\end{proof}

The lemma is an all-input semantic construction, not a favorable compiler.
There are at most $N$ near-parent calls, so the generic charged upper bound is
\begin{equation}
 T_{\rm generic}(N)\le N^{K+1+o(1)}.                    \tag{4}
\end{equation}
If the calls are exposed as semantic children, their invoked-instance
volume is at most $N^{2+o(1)}$ and their maximum size is $N^{1-o(1)}$.
If they are inlined, those child terms disappear but the $K$-dependent work
in (4) returns.  The construction therefore decides which contraction to
use only by performing endpoint-equivalent near-parent SAT work.

This is the precise location of the old ``divide $2^k$ by $2^x$'' idea.
Branching on $x$ Boolean variables creates $2^x$ restrictions; to turn
$2^k$ leaves into polynomially many leaves one needs $x=k-O(\log N)$.
Without semantic selection, multiplicity is exponential.  With selection,
the parent-scale SAT calls in (4) pay for choosing the successful path.  The
algebra is correct, but it does not provide cheap acquisition.

\section{A hostile trace for aggregate child volume}
For even $n\ge6$, with indices modulo $n$, define
\begin{equation}
 H_n=\bigwedge_i(x_i\vee x_{i+1}\vee x_{i+2})\ \wedge\
               (\neg x_i\vee\neg x_{i+1}\vee\neg x_{i+2}).          \tag{5}
\end{equation}
It is proper 3CNF, connected, sign-balanced, neither Horn nor dual-Horn,
and has no pure literal.  Every pivot leaves exactly $2(n-3)$ original
clauses in one connected component on $n-1$ variables.  Since
$N=\Theta(n\log n)$, the largest residual is $N^{1-o(1)}$.

The alternating assignment satisfies (5).  Flipping any adjacent pair also
satisfies it; the corresponding difference vectors span the even-weight
subspace.  Flipping bits $0,1,2$ also satisfies it and supplies an odd-weight
difference.  Hence the affine hull of the satisfying assignments is all of
$\mathbb F_2^n$: even an ideal affine-hull quotient obtains no nontrivial
equation.

Along the least-variable selector trace, each assigned variable can touch
only six clauses.  During the first $n/6$ assignments at least $n$ clauses
are untouched and survive regardless of the selected truth values.  Thus
the explicitly invoked query encodings have aggregate size
\begin{equation}
 \Omega(nN)=\Omega(N^2/\log N)=N^{2-o(1)}.              \tag{6}
\end{equation}
The family is NAE-structured and easy; (6) refutes this selector/portfolio
implementation, not every possible wrapper.

\section{Parallel wrappers need a coupled checkpoint}
Suppose a deterministic computation is divided into two segments.  Encoding
``there exists a legal first segment ending somewhere'' and independently
``there exists a legal second segment starting somewhere and accepting'' is
unsound: the two existential checkpoints may differ.

\begin{theorem}[checkpoint splice]
Independent existential segment formulas can accept a rejecting
deterministic computation.  Equality of the first endpoint and second start
is necessary and sufficient for the two-segment composition.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For necessity, take the two-state identity transition, start $0$, accept
$1$, and one step in each segment.  The actual checkpoint is $0$ and the
whole computation rejects, but the second segment independently chooses
start $1$ and accepts.  For sufficiency, equality makes the concatenation a
single legal deterministic path.  A strongly connected example is the
two-cycle, start/accept $0$, with segment lengths one and two.
\end{proof}

There are only three repairs: compute the checkpoint by running $D$ (paying
its time and workspace), enumerate possible configurations (configuration
space blowup), or couple shared checkpoint variables in one Cook-style
tableau (no proved strict shrink).  Standard RAM-to-circuit and
circuit/tableau simulations are upper-bound encodings, not a source-first
contraction theorem.

\section{Successor closure remains a separate obligation}
Boundary-only heredity is the correct rule: same-scale virtual aliases may
be flattened inside a finite phase, but every emitted ordinary-SAT child
must carry a fully charged certificate that it re-enters the same covered
domain.  The current portfolio does not satisfy that premise.

For example, let $K=\widehat G(1\vee2\vee3)$ be the exact 720-bit guarded
all-fail core from the red-team audit.  Rename it away from $x=1$ and pad
with clauses containing only positive $x$.  Davis--Putnam elimination of
$x$ emits exactly $K$ and no cross-resolvents.  With $M=|K|$, $M^2$ padding
clauses make the parent $\Theta(M^2\log M)$ bits, so the unique child meets a
square-root/polylog cap while remaining outside the portfolio.  Guarding
the padded parent once more gives an accepted strip-then-pivot parent with
the same uncovered ordinary child.  A type label is not a membership proof.

\section{Literature comparison and exact endpoint discipline}
Nishimura--Ragde--Szeider prove detection of strong Horn backdoors in
$O(2^kN)$ and strong Krom backdoors in $O(3^kN)$; the singleton case
motivates Theorem~\ref{thm:krom}
(\url{https://www.satisfiability.org/SAT04/programme/51.pdf}).
E\'en--Biere bounded variable elimination is exact only when its growth test
passes (\url{https://fmv.jku.at/papers/EenBiere-SAT05.pdf}).  IPZ
sparsification is all-input and source-first, but pays exponentially many
children (\url{https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.2001.1774}).  Maximal-autarky
methods use parent-scale SAT-oracle calls
(\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.02371}).  Compression lower bounds of
Fortnow--Santhanam and Dell--van Melkebeek have hypotheses that collapse in a
$\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$ world, and therefore cannot disprove (1).

The downstream endpoint remains the exact Fortnow--Lipton--van
Melkebeek--Viglas deterministic random-access-machine SAT time--space
theorem (\url{https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dieter/Research/sat-ts.html}).
No unrestricted cosine inequality is substituted, and no endpoint is used
until one fixed implementation supplies a joint time/space point in the
endpoint's model.
