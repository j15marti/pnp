\section{A fixed-radius terminal}

For a bit $\beta\in\{0,1\}$ and a set $S$ of variables, let
$\alpha_{\beta,S}$ be the constant-$\beta$ assignment with precisely the
variables in $S$ flipped.  Fix an integer $r\ge0$.  The terminal
\textsc{EXTREME-$r$} performs the following bounded search for each $\beta$.
At a node $S$, scan the formula.  If $\alpha_{\beta,S}$ satisfies it, return
TRUE.  If the first falsified clause is $C$ and $|S|<r$, recurse on
$S\cup\{x\}$ for each variable $x\in C\setminus S$.  Otherwise decline.
It never returns FALSE.

\begin{theorem}[fixed-radius terminal]
For every fixed $r$, \textsc{EXTREME-$r$} accepts exactly the proper 3CNFs
having a model within Hamming distance at most $r$ of all-zero or all-one.
It executes at most
\[
 2\sum_{i=0}^{r}3^i
\]
complete source scans, runs in $N^{1+o(1)}$ time, uses $O(r\log N)$ writable
bits, and emits no ordinary SAT child.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Acceptance is followed by a complete verification scan, so it is sound.
For completeness, let $T$ be a target flip set with $|T|\le r$.  Inductively
suppose the search is at $S\subseteq T$.  If the current assignment is not a
model, every target model must change some variable of the first falsified
clause.  Since $S\subseteq T$, at least one such variable lies in
$T\setminus S$, and the corresponding branch preserves the invariant.
After at most $|T|$ extensions the target assignment is verified.  Proper
width three gives branching at most three.  A fixed array of at most $r$
read-only variable-identifier substrings, plus pointers and counters, gives
the stated state bound.  For fixed $r$ this is $N^{o(1)}$ space and a
constant number of linear scans.
\end{proof}

Thus every fixed radius supplies a legitimate zero-child VALUE terminal.  It
still does not satisfy PRE-A's essential-child condition and says nothing
about UNSAT inputs outside its yes region.

\section{Portfolio-stable NAE shielding}

On fresh disjoint triples define
\[
 Q_b=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{b}
 (a_j\vee b_j\vee c_j)\wedge
 (\neg a_j\vee\neg b_j\vee\neg c_j).              \tag{2}
\]
Every $Q_b$ model contains at least $b$ true and $b$ false shield variables.
Let the already verified ARC6 map be
\[
 R_b(F)=G\!\left(\widehat G\!\left(
       \mathsf{Iso}(F\wedge Q_b)\right)\right),    \tag{3}
\]
where $\mathsf{Iso}$ is occurrence-private isolation and the two guard layers
are those of ARC6/RedTeam6.  For fixed $b$, this is SAT-preserving, has
quasilinear encoded length, polylogarithmic local bit access, and
$O(\log N)$ construction state.

\begin{theorem}[portfolio-stable shield]
Fix a finite extension of the scoped ARC6 controller containing
\textsc{EXTREME-$s$} terminals with $s\le r$, and standard structural strong
Horn, dual-Horn, or Krom backdoor terminals whose backdoor size is at most
$k$.  Choose fixed
\[
 b\ge r+1,\qquad 2b>k.                              \tag{4}
\]
For every valid nonempty proper 3CNF $F$,
\[
 \SAT(R_b(F))=\SAT(F),\qquad R_b(F)\in U_{r,k},     \tag{5}
\]
where $U_{r,k}$ is the extended controller's UNKNOWN domain.  Moreover
\[
 \A(U_{r,k})=\A(\text{proper-3SAT})=\A(\SAT).       \tag{6}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$Q_b$ is satisfiable on fresh variables, so $F\wedge Q_b$ is satisfiable
exactly when $F$ is.  Isolation and both guard layers preserve the source
values.  Hence every model at each relevant terminal boundary retains at
least $b>r$ true and $b>r$ false coordinates, and every fixed-radius extreme
terminal declines.

Occurrence-private isolation leaves the $2b$ displayed shield-clause
supports pairwise disjoint.  A structural strong-Krom backdoor must hit each
proper width-three clause, so its size is at least $2b$.  A structural
strong-Horn backdoor must select at least two variables from every positive
shield clause; otherwise the assignment making its one selected variable
false leaves a clause with two positive literals.  Thus its size is at least
$2b$; the negative clauses give the dual-Horn statement.  Whole-formula
Horn, dual-Horn, and Krom terminals therefore decline as well.

Finally ARC6's universal old-member failure theorem applies unchanged to the
nonempty proper source $F\wedge Q_b$.  Equation (5) follows.  The old local
access simulation, with only constant fixed shield overhead, transfers any
same-machine algorithm in either direction up to $N^{o(1)}$ factors, proving
(6).
\end{proof}

For the current named portfolio $b=2$ suffices: $r=1$, and the operational
Krom/Horn/dual-Horn candidates have backdoor size at most two.  In this range
no backdoor assignment can fully assign a proper ternary clause, so an
immediate empty-clause branch cannot evade the lower bound.  For operational
backdoors of size at least three, early contradictory branches require a
separate no-small-contradictory-core construction; only the standard
structural theorem is claimed here.  The theorem also does not cover an
arbitrary semantic terminal, an unbounded backdoor, or a future recognizer
tailored to the image of (3).

\section{The fallback is the original problem, not an easier remainder}

Let $\PREA_{r,v,h,p}(L)$ denote the existence of one uniform source-first
phase on every input in domain $L$: local work and live state have exponents
$r,p$; the plan is fixed before a parent-scale semantic SAT decision; it
executes essential source-derived ordinary-SAT children with multiplicity,
aggregate exponent $v$ and maximum exponent $h<1$; and every unresolved
child has charged recursive re-entry.  All costs listed in Section 1 are
included.

\begin{theorem}[shielded-UNKNOWN localization]
For every fixed shield and portfolio covered by the preceding theorem, and
for fixed exponents with $h<1$,
\[
 \PREA_{r,v,h,p}(U_{r,k})
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \PREA_{r,v,h,p}(\text{proper-3SAT}),               \tag{7}
\]
with only common $o(1)$ changes to encoded-size exponents.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The reverse implication is restriction.  For the forward implication,
compose the promised phase with the virtual input $R_b(F)$.  The accessor for
(3) answers each random-access bit request in polylogarithmic time and space,
and $|R_b(F)|=|F|^{1+o(1)}$.  Thus local work, state, aggregate child volume,
and maximum child size retain the same exponents.  The accessor performs no
semantic SAT call, so source-first order and essentiality are preserved.
SAT equivalence gives correctness; the promised closure on $U_{r,k}$ gives
recursive re-entry.  This is one uniform machine with one common modulus.
\end{proof}

Equation (7) is a useful sharpening: terminal additions can enlarge the
favorable promise region, but the shield localizes a residual on which the
missing compiler is exactly as difficult as the original all-input task.
Achievable-region equality alone is not a construction of that compiler.

\section{Exact obstruction to the present fallback compositions}

The recovered wrappers offer three explicit generic fallbacks on the
shielded image: invoke the assumed decider, expose the source core, or pad and
then expose the core.  The following proposition is deliberately scoped to
those compositions, not to all conceivable algorithms.

\begin{proposition}[fallback and scale-laundering trilemma]
On $R_b(F)$, none of the three recovered fallbacks creates a favorable
$K$-independent completed phase.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First, a call to the clocked decider on $R_b(F)$ or on exposed $F$ costs
$N^{K+o(1)}$.  Calling it a terminal does not help: a near-parent terminal
has maximum exponent $g=1$, solver exponent $c=K$, and aggregate generation
exponent $w=1$, so Recovery9 charges
\[
 w+g(c-1)=K.                                         \tag{8}
\]

Second, (3) is quasilinear.  Exposing $F$ as the sole ordinary child gives
$|F|=|R_b(F)|^{1-o(1)}$, hence $h=1$ and $v=1$.  There is no strict recursive
descent.  The aggregate fixed-point expression
$(v-h)/(1-h)$ is not applicable because its denominator is zero.

Third, suppose padding maps size $N$ to $N^a$, $a>1$, before exposing the
core.  In a typed graph the apparent edges have scale exponents
\[
 A_N\longrightarrow B_{N^a}:a,
 \qquad B_M\longrightarrow A_{M^{1/a}}:1/a.
\]
Their cycle product is one.  A complete lap returns to the original encoded
size and is not well founded.  If padding is instead declared an internal
alias, alias flattening exposes the completed $F\to F$ boundary directly,
again with $h=1$.  Changing the reference scale cannot supply contraction.
\end{proof}

Aggregate child volume is valuable once a genuinely smaller residual handler
exists; it cannot repair a same-size recursive child.  Typed cycles may
contain same-scale internal edges, but every reachable completed recursive
cycle still needs strict scale product below one.  The pad--unpad cycle has
equality.

\section{Primary-literature boundary}

SAT's standard disjunctive self-reduction is source-first and essential:
$F$ is satisfiable iff $F[x=0]$ or $F[x=1]$ is satisfiable.  Its encoded
children can nevertheless have length $N-O(1)$, so its maximum-child
exponent is one.  Restricting enough variables to force $N^h$ children
creates exponentially many branches and unfavorable executed volume.

P-selectivity can prune a self-reduction tree.  The exact qualitative result
is that P-selective self-reducible sets lie in P.  Under $\SAT\in P$ a
selector can be obtained by running the assumed decider on candidate
children, but this pays the unknown exponent $K$ before the contraction and
therefore does not satisfy PRE-A.  Sparsification similarly produces up to
$2^{\varepsilon n}$ sparse disjuncts of linear variable scale; it is not a
polynomial aggregate-volume phase.  Williams' self-improvement theorem is a
specialized Large-Circuit-SAT/circuit-analysis result with a time conclusion;
it does not provide jointly bounded smaller ordinary-CNF children from an
arbitrary polynomial SAT decider.

Primary references: Hemaspaandra, \emph{The Power of Self-Reducibility},
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08299}; Hemaspaandra et al.,
\emph{P-Selective Self-Reducible Sets: A New Characterization of P}, JCSS 53
(1996), \url{https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.1996.0062}; Impagliazzo, Paturi and
Zane, \emph{Which Problems Have Strongly Exponential Complexity?}, JCSS 63
(2001), \url{https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.2001.1774}; Williams,
\emph{Self-Improvement for Circuit-Analysis Problems}, STOC 2024,
\url{https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/stoc-self-improvement.pdf}.

The endpoint retained here is FLMV Corollary 5.8, combined with their local
SAT-reduction Theorem 3.1/Corollary 3.2, in the deterministic random-access
machine model:
\[
 \operatorname{NTIME}[n]\not\subseteq
 \operatorname{DTISP}\!\left[n^c,o\!\left(n^{d(c)}\right)\right],
 \qquad d(c)=\frac12\left(\frac{c+2}{c^2}-c\right). \tag{9}
\]
At $c=1.201$, $d(c)\approx0.509108>1/2$.  Fixed exponent slack therefore
rules out the conditional landing $(6/5,1/2)$.  See Fortnow, Lipton, van
Melkebeek and Viglas, \emph{Time-Space Lower Bounds for Satisfiability},
JACM 52(6), 2005, \url{https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/tst.pdf}.
The retired unrestricted $2\cos(\pi/7)$ endpoint is not used.
