\begin{theorem}[aggregate implicit-access closure]
Suppose unresolved child lengths $z_j$ satisfy
\[
 \max_jz_j\le N^{h+o(1)},\qquad \sum_jz_j\le N^{v+o(1)},
 \qquad h<1,
\]
and each access to the exact source-matched implicit representation of a
child costs $N^{g+o(1)}$ parent time, uniformly over children.  Put all
generator preprocessing, replay and nonchild work in $N^{r+o(1)}$.  Then the
conservative no-cache recursive simulation closes at
\[
 \boxed{C=\max\left\{1,r,\frac{g+v-h}{1-h}\right\}}.   \tag{3a}
\]
This is conditional accounting; it supplies neither the generator nor the
children.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If the child algorithm takes $z^{C+o(1)}$ accesses, the simulated child costs
$N^{g+o(1)}z^C$.  Therefore
\[
 \sum_jN^gz_j^C\le
 N^g(\max_jz_j)^{C-1}\sum_jz_j
 \le N^{g+v+h(C-1)+o(1)}.
\]
The induction closes when $C\ge r$ and
$C\ge g+v+h(C-1)$, giving (3a).\qed
\end{proof}

For $g=0$ this is Recovery9.  If a count bound gives
$v\le\eta+h$, it becomes RECURCLOSE1/STREAMCOMP1's
$(\eta+g)/(1-h)$ term.  At the present saturated target
$(C,v,h)=(6/5,11/10,1/2)$, equation (3a) permits only $g=o(1)$ in exponent
notation unless a proved batching or caching implementation replaces the
conservative per-access simulation.  ORBITBATCH1 helps only if its sharing
reduces this actual executed access/consumer charge; address identity alone
does not reduce semantic child volume.

\section{PROVED/EXACT: complete-ledger hard-image simulation}

\[
 |R(F)|=N^{1+o(1)}.                                    \tag{3}
\]

\begin{theorem}[one-initial-image mixed-ledger transfer]
Suppose one fixed exact phase works on every promised $Y\in U$.  Its plan is
fixed before a parent-scale SAT decision.  It has local time/state exponents
$(r,p)$, unresolved ordinary-SAT children with maximum/aggregate exponents
$(h,v)$, and fixed terminal types $\tau$ with maximum/aggregate exponents
$(g_\tau,w_\tau)$ and solvers $(c_\tau,d_\tau)$.  It charges discovery,
failures, generation, replay, output, buffers, indexes and ancestors.  Every
ordinary recursive exit re-enters its declared domain.

Then simulating this phase once on the virtual input $R(F)$ preserves every
displayed exponent up to one common $o(1)$.  Recursive children thereafter
run in exactly the representations declared by the phase.  Consequently the
Recovery9 closure exponents
\[
 C=\max\left\{1,r,
   \max_\tau[w_\tau+g_\tau(c_\tau-1)],
   \frac{v-h}{1-h}\right\},                           \tag{4}
\]
\[
 P=\max\left\{p,h,
   \max_\tau g_\tau\max(1,d_\tau)\right\}             \tag{5}
\]
are unchanged.  Conversely, an all-proper-3SAT phase restricts to $U$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every root-input access made by the promised phase is replaced by one call to
the source-first bit generator.  A polynomial number of accesses therefore
incurs only an $N^{o(1)}$ multiplicative time factor and an $N^{o(1)}$
reusable workspace floor.  Equation (3) converts every child and terminal
length bound from $|R(F)|$ to $N$ without changing its fixed power exponent.
The phase itself already counts output occurrences, replay and retained
state, so no volume or buffer is omitted.  SAT preservation transfers
exactness, and the generator makes no semantic decision, preserving causal
order.  Only the initial image is virtual; recursive children use the
phase's declared representation, so no unproved tower of nested generators
is introduced.  Substitution into Recovery9 gives (4)--(5).  Restriction
proves the converse.\qed
\end{proof}

Construction10's $R_b(F)$ meets these image premises for its fixed scalable

\section{PROVED/EXACT: path coefficient and cycle provenance}

For a realized typed edge $e$, let $a_e\ge0$ contain its multiplicity,
generator and other charged affine intercepts, and let $\theta_e>0$ be its
declared encoded-length scale.  Along a finite path
$P=(e_1,\ldots,e_q)$ define
\[
 \Theta(P)=\prod_{j=1}^q\theta_{e_j},\qquad
 A(P)=a_{e_1}+\theta_{e_1}a_{e_2}+\cdots+
       \left(\prod_{j<q}\theta_{e_j}\right)a_{e_q}.   \tag{6}
\]

\begin{lemma}[path coefficient]
If $P$ ends in the unresolved decider $D$, repeated black-box substitution
charges exponent
\[
 A(P)+\Theta(P)K.                                      \tag{7}
\]
Alias flattening composes exactly the pair $(A(P),\Theta(P))$.
STREAMCOMP changes an intercept by its generator cost but not $\theta$.
ORBITBATCH-style setup/address sharing may change an intercept or delete
proved duplicate evaluations, but it neither changes the logical child scale
nor deletes the final semantic dependency.  Thus these accounting rules do
not erase the $K$ coefficient of an actually executed unresolved path.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Induct on path length in the affine substitution
$x\mapsto a_e+\theta_e x$.  The remaining assertions follow directly from
the declared interfaces of alias flattening, STREAMCOMP1 and ORBITBATCH1.
\qed
\end{proof}
The restriction $\theta_e>0$ is only notational here: a constant-size
$\theta_e=0$ child is already a genuine primitive contraction and is handled
separately by the closure theorems.

\begin{lemma}[closed-walk decomposition]
In a finite directed graph with positive edge gains, every closed walk
decomposes into simple directed cycles and its gain is the product of their
gains.  Therefore, if every simple realized cycle has gain at least one, no
composite or repeated closed walk has gain below one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Repeatedly remove the segment between two consecutive occurrences of a
repeated vertex.  Each removed segment is a simple directed cycle after the
same operation is applied internally.  The remaining closed walk is simple
or empty.  Edge multiplicities are partitioned, so their gain products
multiply to the original gain.\qed
\end{proof}
