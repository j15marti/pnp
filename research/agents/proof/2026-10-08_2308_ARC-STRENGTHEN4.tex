\section{Selected barrier and primary endpoint}

The selected target is the exact encoding/model guard identified by the
latest red-team report.  A completed WRAPPER phase acts on the project's
binary \texttt{CNF1} strings, whereas the primary lower bound states its
transfer for its SAT representation.  A materialized quasi-linear reduction
can consume more than the desired square-root workspace.  The transfer must
therefore be local: a simulated SAT machine asks for a source bit, and that
bit is regenerated without storing the whole source.

Fortnow, Lipton, van Melkebeek and Viglas (FLMV) use deterministic
random-access Turing machines with a read-only indexed input tape.  Work and
index tapes are charged; the input tape and one-way write-only output tape are
excluded from space.  Their Theorem~3.1 states that for a fixed constant $r$,
\[
 \SAT\in\DTISP[t,s]
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{NTIME}[n]\subseteq
 \DTISP\!\left[t(n\log^r n)\log^r n,
                s(n\log^r n)+\log n\right].                 \tag{1}
\]
Its proof constructs a formula of length $d n\log n$ and computes each
requested formula bit in polylogarithmic time and $O(\log n)$ simultaneous
space, rather than materializing the formula.  Their Corollary~5.8 is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{NTIME}[n]\not\subseteq
 \DTISP\!\left[n^c,o\!\left(n^{d(c)}\right)\right],
 \qquad
 d(c)=\frac12\left(\frac{c+2}{c^2}-c\right),
 \quad 1<c.                                                  \tag{2}
\]
At $c=1201/1000$,
\[
 d(c)=\frac{1468676399}{2884802000}
      \approx 0.5091082157>\frac12.                          \tag{3}
\]
Thus an actual all-input SAT machine with joint bounds
$N^{6/5+o(1)}$ time and $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ space would enter this primary
forbidden region after the local transfer.  Equation~(2), not the retired
cosine endpoint, is the only endpoint used here.

Primary source: L.~Fortnow, R.~Lipton, D.~van Melkebeek and A.~Viglas,
\emph{Time-space lower bounds for satisfiability}, JACM 52(6), 835--865
(2005), Sections 2.1, 3.1 and Corollary 5.8,
\url{https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/tst.pdf}.

\section{The exact local adapter}

The project encoding is the following canonical byte stream.  It begins with
the four bytes \texttt{CNF1}; unsigned canonical ULEB128 integers give the
largest variable identifier and the clause count; each clause contains its
ULEB128 width followed by its nonzero signed literals under zig-zag coding.
The complete encoded bit length is the input length $N$.  Malformed bit
strings are rejected by the outer total-language parser.

\begin{theorem}[INDEXED-3CNF-TO-CNF1]\label{thm:adapter}
Fix one input $x$ of length $n$.  Suppose that $Q_x$ is a 3CNF with exactly
three literal occurrences in every clause, $q\ge1$ variables and $C$ clauses,
where
\[
 q,C\le n\log^{O(1)}n.                                      \tag{4}
\]
Suppose one uniform oracle returns the sign and identifier of occurrence
$(j,r)\in[C]\times[3]$ in $\log^{O(1)}n$ time and $O(\log n)$ workspace, and
$q,C$ are computable within the same bounds.  Then one can produce a
satisfiability-equivalent \texttt{CNF1} string $R_x$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $|R_x|=n\log^{O(1)}n$ bits;
\item every requested bit of $R_x$ is computable uniformly in
      $\log^{O(1)}n$ time and $O(\log n)$ workspace;
\item the construction stores neither $Q_x$ nor $R_x$ and uses no oracle for
      satisfiability.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a positive integer $u$, canonical ULEB128 has exactly $b$ bytes precisely
when
\[
 2^{7(b-1)}\le u<2^{7b}.                                    \tag{5}
\]
Choose the least $b\ge1$ with $q\le2^{7b-3}$ and put
$A=2^{7b-3}$.  Rename every variable $v\in[q]$ injectively to $A+v$, keeping
its sign.  Under the convention
\[
 \operatorname{zz}(+u)=2u,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{zz}(-u)=2u-1,                                \tag{6}
\]
every renamed signed literal has zig-zag value $z$ satisfying
\[
 2A+1\le z\le4A=2^{7b-1}<2^{7b},
 \qquad 2A+1>2^{7(b-1)}.                                    \tag{7}
\]
Hence every signed literal occupies exactly $b$ ULEB bytes.  The largest
identifier $A+q\le2A$ also has $b$ bytes.  Because the clause width is always
three, each clause record has the fixed byte length
\[
 L=1+3b.                                                     \tag{8}
\]
The choice of $b$ gives $A<128q$ when $q>1$ (and $A=16$ when $q=1$), so
$b=O(\log(q+1))$ and
\[
 |R_x|=8\bigl(4+b+|\operatorname{ULEB}(C)|+C(1+3b)\bigr)
       =n\log^{O(1)}n.                                      \tag{9}
\]

To answer a requested byte, first compute the $O(\log n)$-bit header.  After
the header, divide the byte offset by $L$ to obtain a clause index and an
in-record offset.  Offset zero is the one-byte width.  Otherwise a division
by $b$ identifies one of the three literal occurrences and one of its $b$
ULEB bytes.  Invoke the supplied occurrence oracle once, add $A$, apply
(6), and emit the selected ULEB byte or selected bit.  Arithmetic on
$O(\log n)$-bit integers takes polylogarithmic time and $O(\log n)$ space on
the declared random-access model.  The variable renaming is a bijection
between assignments on variables appearing in $Q_x$ and assignments on the
renamed set, so satisfiability is preserved.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[virtual-input simulation]
If a deterministic random-access machine decides all \texttt{CNF1} strings
of length $M$ in time $t(M)$ and space $s(M)$, then on the indexed formulas
of Theorem~\ref{thm:adapter} it can be simulated in
\[
 t(n\log^{O(1)}n)\log^{O(1)}n
 \quad\hbox{time and}\quad
 s(n\log^{O(1)}n)+O(\log n)\quad\hbox{space}.                \tag{10}
\]
All simulator state, the simulated input index, division temporaries and the
one occurrence returned by the oracle are included.  The simulated machine's
work and index tapes remain live at the same time, so (10) is a joint
same-machine bound rather than separate witnesses.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Keep the CNF1 machine's charged state and replace each read of its virtual
read-only input by the local bit procedure above.  This multiplies time by a
polylogarithm and adds $O(\log n)$ live workspace.  It never writes the
virtual input to a work tape.
\end{proof}

\section{What this closes and what it does not}

The theorem closes a genuine part of the endpoint guard: variable-length
ULEB literals do not themselves force a prefix scan.  An injective offset
renaming makes the body fixed-layout while increasing length by only a
polylogarithmic factor.  It also shows that annotation marker clauses need
not be stripped before an endpoint use.  They are ordinary tautologies and
can remain in an ordinary CNF1 formula.  A promise-only marked solver,
however, is not an all-input CNF1 solver.

One source-checkable link remains.  FLMV prove local access to a Boolean
formula bit.  The cited statement does not specify a clause-indexed 3CNF
stream or a local map from an arbitrary requested position in their formula
serialization to a clause/literal pair.  A generic conversion from an
arbitrary variable-length formula stream to CNF1 can require prefix
rank/select information; the currently justified fallback scans
$\Theta(N)$ bits.  Therefore this report does \emph{not} claim arbitrary
bidirectional local transcoding.

The precise remaining lemma is:
\[
\boxed{\begin{minipage}{0.86\linewidth}
For the oblivious Cook--Pippenger--Fischer tableau used in the proof of
FLMV Theorem~3.1, construct directly a clause-indexed exactly-3CNF stream of
quasi-linear encoded size whose $(j,r)$ literal is computable in
polylogarithmic time and $O(\log n)$ space, or prove an equivalent local
locator for the serialization actually used there.
\end{minipage}}                                               \tag{11}
\]
This is a narrowly isolated implementation lemma, but it remains a
load-bearing premise and is not promoted to \textsc{Proved} here.

\section{Hostile controls on the latest construction portfolio}

The independent audit supplied two controls that prevent this encoding
advance from being mistaken for an all-input construction.

First, for $m\ge3$, take cyclic triples
$T_i=\{a_i,b_i,a_{i+1}\}$ and encode one complete three-variable parity
constraint on each $T_i$.  The resulting 3CNF has $2m$ variables and $4m$
clauses, is connected and satisfiable, has no common parity hub, is not Horn,
and has no strong Horn backdoor of size two.  Indeed a strong Horn backdoor
must meet every $T_i$ in at least two variables, while each variable occurs
in at most two triples, so its size is at least $m$.  Thus the updated finite
portfolio still returns UNKNOWN on an infinite easy affine family.  The
first member of this family has six variables and twelve clauses; no global
minimality claim is made.

Second, the current parity-star implementation performs at least $16m^2$
clause-pair probes on this UNKNOWN family.  Since its CNF1 length is
$N=\Theta(m\log m)$, this is
\[
 \Omega\!\left(\frac{N^2}{\log^2N}\right),                  \tag{12}
\]
which exceeds the desired $N^{6/5+o(1)}$ local phase budget.  Parallel
execution does not remove deterministic work.  This member must be capped,
replaced, or optimized before inclusion in a favorable all-input phase; a
cap forfeits complete recognition of its asymptotic positive family.

The audit also confirmed the exact quantifier for stagewise ARC transitions:
each selected fixed stage must supply one uniform all-length, all-input SAT
machine jointly attaining its time and space bounds.  Per-length machines,
per-input portfolio members, and promise domains do not establish an ARC
point.  The present flat marked syntax is not literally hereditary because
parent data clauses forbid reserved tags 1 and 2 while child markers use
those tags.  A proposed nested repair is therefore only a candidate until a
new quoted/fresh-tag grammar and its charged renumbering ledger are written.
