\section{The amplified guard theorem}

\begin{lemma}[semantics, encoded size and local access]\label{lem:access}
For every source $F$,
\[
 \SAT(G(F))=\SAT(F),\qquad
 N\le |G(F)|\le N\log^{O(1)}N=N^{1+o(1)}.                \tag{3}
\]
The target length and every requested target bit are computable uniformly
from read-only random access to $F$ in $\log^{O(1)}N$ time and
$O(\log N)$ workspace.  Repeated requests are not cached or hidden.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Restriction gives the forward implication.  Extend a model of $F$ by
$h=r=0$, $w_v=F(v)$ for occurring variables (and an arbitrary value for an
unused identifier), and $a_j=b_j=0$.  All parity and equivalence clauses are
satisfied.

The output contains the source body, two binary records and $q+m$ four-clause
blocks.  Since $q,m=O(N)$, canonical ULEB128 gives (3).  The logarithmic
factor is necessary on the declared occurrence encoding: $m$ duplicate
clauses on three variables have source length $\Theta(m)$, whereas the fresh
per-occurrence identifiers in (1) use $\Theta(m\log m)$ bits.  No linear-size
claim is made.

For local access, compute (2) and its canonical header with logarithmic-bit
counters.  The source-body interval maps directly to the old payload.  In a
guard interval, every identifier is an affine function of its block number.
The prefix length through block $k$ is a sum of ULEB widths; those widths
change only when an affine value crosses a power of $128$.  Split the sum at
those $O(\log N)$ thresholds and binary-search the containing block.  This
computes a requested byte in polylogarithmic time and logarithmic workspace,
without a renaming table or materialized output.  Header, arity, polarity,
attained maximum and exact EOF are thereby source-checkable.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[all-input image in the current fallback]\label{thm:miss}
For every source $F$ above, $G(F)\in U$.  In particular, the result is an
all-input embedding, not merely an infinite missed family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every variable in (1) occurs with both signs, so unit and pure-literal rules
are inert.  The formula is connected through $h$, is not Horn and is not
2-CNF.  The binary equivalence clause triggers the authenticated parity-star
scanner's width check, and the ordinary stream lacks the flat marked
member's magic clause.

For the size-two strong-Horn member, each of the $L=q+m$ parity blocks
contributes a triangle to the positive-pair obstruction graph; the triangles
share only $h$.  Their union has vertex-cover number $L+1>2$.  Thus for every
$B$ of size at most two other than $\{h,r\}$, choose an uncovered positive
pair and an assignment to $B$ that does not satisfy its clause; a non-Horn
residual remains.  For $B=\{h,r\}$, the assignment $h=r=1$ leaves a positive
binary pair in every parity block.  Hence the executable backdoor member,
including its empty-clause shortcut, fails.

It remains to check \textsc{PIVOT-FACTOR1}.  If the pivot $z\ne h$, at most
one parity block contains $z$ apart from the arbitrary source clauses.
At least $L-1$ untouched blocks remain verbatim and share $h$, hence lie in
one primal component.  If $z=h$, resolving the two equivalence clauses with
each $P_0(h,u,v)$ produces all four clauses of $P_0(r,u,v)$; these $L$
translated blocks share $r$.  Additional cross-block resolvents can only add
records.  Duplicate deletion does not remove these distinct blocks.

Here is the complete bit-cap comparison.  Put
\[
 \ell=1+\lfloor\log_{128}(5L+2)\rfloor,qquad
 \mathcal U(L)=8[6+5L+(15L+6)\ell].                       \tag{4}
\]
The parent has at most $\mathcal U(L)$ bits.  Since $L\ge4$ and
$\ell\le\log_2L$,
\[
 |G(F)|\le160L\log_2L,qquad |G(F)|+2\le161L\log_2L.       \tag{5}
\]
The large residual component contains at least $4(L-1)$ proper ternary
clauses and therefore at least $128(L-1)$ bits.  Define
\[
 R(x)=\frac{\sqrt{160x\log_2x}\,
              \log_2(161x\log_2x)}{128(x-1)}.
\]
Direct substitution gives $R(4)<0.963$.  The logarithmic derivative is
negative for $x\ge4$: on $4\le x\le8$ its positive bracket is below
$1.102<8/7$, and for $x\ge8$ it is below $0.925<1$ before subtracting
$1/(x-1)$.  Thus $R$ decreases, and
\[
 128(L-1)>\sqrt{|G(F)|}\log_2(|G(F)|+2)=B(|G(F)|)          \tag{6}
\]
(before the floor).  Every completed pivot therefore fails the maximum-child
test.  If acquisition hits its universal step cap first, it also returns
UNKNOWN with zero semantic calls.  Hence no pivot accepts, and every member
of the fixed policy fails as claimed.
\end{proof}

The scope is important.  Iterated BVE or a dedicated recognizer can strip a
leaf from each parity block and recover $F$.  That exposes a core of size
$|G(F)|^{1-o(1)}$, not a child of size $|G(F)|^{h+o(1)}$ for fixed $h<1$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:miss} is an exact statement about the named current finite
portfolio, not a lower bound on every future compiler.

\section{PROVED/EXACT achievable-region equality}

For a valid-input domain $D$, let $\A(D)$ contain $(t,s)$, $t\ge1,s\ge0$,
iff one fixed uniform deterministic random-access machine, on every input in
$D$ at every length, is correct and simultaneously uses
$n^{t+o(1)}$ time and $n^{s+o(1)}$ workspace.  The $o(1)$ envelopes are
uniform; time and space cannot come from different machines.

\begin{theorem}[fallback-region equivalence]\label{thm:region}
For repaired DENSE-CNF1 and the exact $U$ above,
\[
 \boxed{\A(U)=\A(\mathrm{proper\mbox{-}3SAT})=\A(\SAT).}  \tag{7}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Restriction gives $\A(\SAT)\subseteq\A(U)$.  Conversely, fix an actual
joint witness $M_U$ for $(t,s)\in\A(U)$.  On proper 3CNF $F$, simulate
$M_U$ on the virtual read-only input $G(F)$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:access} answers
every adaptive input request, including repetitions.  Since
$|G(F)|=N^{1+o(1)}$, the simulation takes
\[
 N^{t+o(1)}\cdot N^{o(1)}+N^{1+o(1)}=N^{t+o(1)}
\]
time and
\[
 N^{s+o(1)}+N^{o(1)}=N^{s+o(1)}
\]
peak workspace, on the same machine.  Theorem~\ref{thm:miss} guarantees that
every virtual input lies in the promise.  This proves
$\A(U)\subseteq\A(\mathrm{proper\mbox{-}3SAT})$.

The repaired local clause-splitting/offset-renaming adapter maps valid
DENSE-CNF1 CNF to proper 3CNF with quasilinear length and polylogarithmic
per-bit access; malformed strings are rejected by the total parser.  The same
simulation proves equality with total DENSE-CNF1 SAT.  Initialization,
headers, indices and accessor scratch are included above, including the
$s=0$ case.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[what the prefix can and cannot do]
A source-first partial prefix whose exact failure domain is $U$ supplies no
strict existence-level ARC edge by itself.  Any favorable joint solver for
$U$ already gives a favorable all-input SAT solver with the same exponents.
If the prefix is actually executed before that solver, all failed work is
additionally charged by coordinatewise maxima.
\end{corollary}

This does not say that improvement is impossible.  A genuinely better
$U$-solver would be a genuinely better SAT solver.  It says the finite easy
prefix is not the load-bearing transition.

\section{Stage quantifiers and finite endpoint entry}

At each finite ARC stage the valid implication has the form
\[
 (\exists\hbox{ one uniform all-length joint }(t,s)\hbox{ witness})
 \Longrightarrow
 (\exists\hbox{ one uniform all-length joint }(t',s')\hbox{ witness}).
\]
Different stages may use different witnesses.  Invalid substitutes include
$\forall n\,\exists M_n$, input-dependent branches, or separate time and
space machines.  Coordinatewise infima need not be jointly attainable.  A
limit argument is sound only when actual joint witness pairs enter the
interior of an open forbidden region with fixed slack.

The present numerical target has that slack.  FLMV Corollary~5.8 gives, in
its deterministic random-access model,
\[
 \operatorname{NTIME}[n]\not\subseteq
 \operatorname{DTISP}[n^c,o(n^{d(c)})],\qquad
 d(c)=\frac12\left(\frac{c+2}{c^2}-c\right).              \tag{8}
\]
At $c=1201/1000$,
\[
 c-\frac65=\frac1{1000},qquad
 d(c)-\frac12=\frac{26275399}{2884802000}>0.009108.       \tag{9}
\]
Thus one actual SAT machine at $(6/5,1/2)$, with the declared $o(1)$ and
polylogarithmic transfer overheads, eventually lies strictly inside the
forbidden rectangle.  Recovery's aggregate theorem proves that the ledger
$(r,v,h,p)=(6/5,11/10,1/2,1/2)$ would produce precisely that machine if one
hereditary all-input phase existed.  Theorem~\ref{thm:region} does not supply
that phase.
