\section{Result and relation to the current spine}

The current near-linear WRAPPER contract is sufficient, but it is stronger
than necessary for finite entry into the verified FLMV forbidden region.
One completed phase may execute
\[
  N^{3/5+o(1)}\quad\hbox{children of length at most}\quad N^{1/2+o(1)},
\]
do $N^{6/5+o(1)}$ fully charged local work, and use
$N^{1/2+o(1)}$ peak live local space.  Recursive inlining then gives one
ordinary exact SAT machine with joint bounds
\[
  T(N)=N^{6/5+o(1)},\qquad S(N)=N^{1/2+o(1)}.
\]
The possible executed aggregate child volume is
$N^{11/10+o(1)}$, not near-linear.  At $c=1201/1000$ this joint pair is
strictly inside the primary-source FLMV forbidden region.  Thus the
Construction search may allow this larger volume and local-time budget.
This relaxation does not supply the missing phase.

Earlier project work already derived general typed Bellman recurrences and a
conditional $(6/5,1/2)$ point.  The new contribution here is a one-phase,
actual-machine specialization with the exact FLMV curve, an explicit
$11/10$ aggregate-volume allowance, and hostile controls that make the
transient-space and adaptive-query quantifiers load-bearing.  An earlier
typed report's use of the unrestricted cosine line remains retired.

\section{Exact completed-phase contract}

Fix a canonical SAT encoding and let $N$ always mean its encoded bit length.
Let $W$ be one fixed deterministic uniform SAT-oracle transducer.  There are
fixed constants $N_0,r,\eta,p\ge0$, $0\le h<1$, and one function
$e(N)\to0$ such that, on every input $x$ of length $N>N_0$ and on every
transcript containing the true SAT bit for each previously issued query:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $W$ halts and returns $\SAT(x)$ exactly;
\item it executes at most $N^{\eta+e(N)}$ child calls, counted with
  multiplicity, including repeated identical strings;
\item before a call is answered it has materialized one immutable valid SAT
  encoding $y$ with $|y|<N$ and $|y|\le N^{h+e(N)}$;
\item its total nonchild work is at most $N^{r+e(N)}$; this includes
  classification, discovery, every failed route, certification, every child
  occurrence's generation, acquisition, source rereads, indexing, replay,
  answer combination, cleanup, and output; and
\item apart from the one live child buffer, its peak live workspace is at
  most $N^{p+e(N)}$.  This includes transient workspace, suspended frames,
  indexes, caches, generator/decoder state, stored answers, and a sufficient
  streaming summary of earlier child answers.
\end{enumerate}
Children are evaluated sequentially.  For $N\le N_0$ one fixed exact
procedure is used.  The same $W$, constants, and uniform error envelope apply
at every recursive level.  A lazy child descriptor is not an explicit child:
virtual access requires a separate charged interface theorem.  A parent-size
call to an assumed polynomial SAT decider is likewise not local work.
If $W$ is implemented by a multi-step administrative epoch, every same-scale
internal step must terminate under an explicit well-founded rank and its full
cost must already be included in the local bounds.  Only completed phase-exit
SAT calls enter the shrinking recurrence.

The true-transcript phrasing permits adaptive children while preventing a
favorable-route ledger.  Equivalently, the bounds may be required on all
answer-consistent executions.  Exactness, not a hash collision or common
answer on sampled histories, is required.

\section{Actual-machine composition}

\begin{theorem}[Relaxed completed-phase theorem]
Under the contract above, replacing every oracle query of $W$ by a recursive
call gives one uniform oracle-free exact SAT machine $D_W$ satisfying
\[
 C=\max\left\{r,\frac{\eta}{1-h}\right\},\qquad
 P=\max\{p,h\},
\]
and
\[
 T_{D_W}(N)=N^{C+o(1)},\qquad S_{D_W}(N)=N^{P+o(1)}.
\]
These are simultaneous bounds for the same machine on all inputs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Define $D_W$ by length induction: simulate $W$ and recursively compute the
answer to each issued immutable child.  Every child is strictly shorter, so
the recursion terminates at the fixed base procedure.  Induction on encoded
length proves that every supplied answer is the true SAT bit; the exactness
clause for $W$ then proves $D_W(x)=\SAT(x)$.  Hence this is an actual SAT
machine, not an oracle recurrence.

The monotone worst-case time envelope is
\[
 T(N)\le N^{r+o(1)}+N^{\eta+o(1)}
       T\!\left(N^{h+o(1)}\right).
\]
Fix any $\delta>0$.  Uniform convergence of the error envelope lets us choose
slack small enough that
\[
 r+\epsilon<C+\delta,qquad
 \eta+\epsilon+(h+\epsilon)(C+\delta)<C+\delta.
\]
Strong induction, after increasing one constant for the finite prefix, yields
$T(N)=O(N^{C+\delta})$.  Since this holds for every fixed $\delta>0$, the
time is $N^{C+o(1)}$.  This proof counts adaptive and repeated calls with
multiplicity.

During depth-first evaluation, each level retains its fully charged local
state and the buffer containing the input of the active child.  Thus
\[
 S(N)\le N^{p+o(1)}+N^{h+o(1)}+S(N^{h+o(1)}).
\]
The geometric shrinkage of encoded lengths gives
$S(N)=N^{\max(p,h)+o(1)}$.  This sum explicitly includes every ancestor
frame and child buffer.  Materializing the whole child batch at once is not
permitted by this bound.
\end{proof}

The same time exponent is also visible level by level: level $j$ has local
time exponent
\[
 \eta(1+h+\cdots+h^{j-1})+rh^j,
\]
whose supremum is $\max\{r,\eta/(1-h)\}$.  This calculation is only a check;
the induction above establishes the uniform machine bound.

\section{Finite entry with superlinear aggregate volume}

\begin{corollary}[Concrete relaxed target]
If the contract is realized with
\[
 h=\frac12,\qquad \eta=\frac35,
 \qquad r=\frac65,qquad p\le\frac12,
\]
then $D_W$ has the joint pair $(C,P)=(6/5,1/2)$.  Moreover, the sum of the
encoded lengths of all executed explicit children in one phase is at most
\[
 N^{\eta+h+o(1)}=N^{11/10+o(1)}.
\]
\end{corollary}

This last quantity is an upper allowance, not a requirement and not a live
space allowance.  Children and their answers must be streamed with a summary
that fits the $p$ budget.  In particular, storing one bit for each of
$N^{3/5}$ answers would already violate $p\le1/2$ unless those answers are
combined online.

FLMV use deterministic random-access Turing machines; read-only input and
write-only output are excluded from workspace, but work and index tapes are
charged.  Their Theorem~3.1 locally transfers a SAT algorithm to
nondeterministic-linear-time languages with polylogarithmic time and
logarithmic-space overhead.  Corollary~5.8 proves, for every $c>1$,
\[
 \operatorname{NTIME}[n]\not\subseteq
 \DTISP\bigl[n^c,o(n^{d(c)})\bigr],
 \qquad d(c)=\frac12\left(\frac{c+2}{c^2}-c\right).
\]
At $c=1201/1000$,
\[
 d(c)=\frac{1468676399}{2884802000}
 =\frac12+\frac{26275399}{2884802000}>\frac12,
 \qquad c-\frac65=\frac1{1000}>0.
\]
Therefore the strict margins absorb all $N^{o(1)}$ and polylogarithmic
overheads, and the corollary's same-machine pair contradicts FLMV.  The
primary source is
\url{https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/tst.pdf}, especially its model in
Section~2.1, Theorem~3.1, and Corollary~5.8.  No occurrence-count or
variable-count measure is substituted for encoded bit length.

\section{Falsification controls and precise remaining gap}

The following tempting weakenings are invalid.
\begin{description}
\item[Retained state only.]  A phase may allocate and erase an $N$-bit
scratch region before recursion while retaining $O(1)$ bits.  Its local time
can remain within $N^{6/5}$, but its space is linear.  Thus $p$ must bound all
peak transient and retained local workspace.
\item[Distinct children.]  Repeating one child $N^{3/5}$ times still executes
$N^{3/5}$ calls.  Multiplicity, not the number of distinct encodings, enters
$\eta$.
\item[Implicit children.]  An $O(\log N)$ descriptor does not erase the cost
of regenerating requested bits.  Per-access replay can multiply by the child
solver's accesses and requires its own charged recurrence.
\item[Unchanged fallback.]  A partial portfolio followed by the inherited
same-scale SAT machine does not satisfy strict descent and does not realize
$D_W$.  The robust general-3-SAT fallback families already recovered in the
project remain relevant.
\item[Growing base.]  Stopping after a fixed number of phases and invoking an
$N^K$ solver retains $K$.  The contract descends to one fixed base threshold;
this is the conditional $K$ elimination.
\item[Answer-selected route.]  Bounds only for the ultimately successful
branch omit discovery and failed executions.  Every executed call and all
selection work must be charged on the true adaptive transcript.
\end{description}

The missing implication is now weaker than the near-linear-volume target but
still load-bearing:
\[
 \boxed{\SAT\in P\ \Longrightarrow\
 \text{one uniform exact all-input phase satisfying the concrete contract}.}
\]
No recovered WRAPPER, backdoor, signed-twin, disjunctive, orbit-batch,
streaming, random-bitstream, or typed-cycle construction proves this
implication on unrestricted encoded SAT inputs.  The theorem above proves
what such a phase would do; it does not construct it.  Indeed, FLMV shows
that an unconditional phase with these parameters cannot exist.

\section{Validation, reconciliation, and handoff}

The companion Python audit uses exact rational arithmetic and a 25-second
alarm.  It performs 1,885 checks: the target exponents and exact FLMV margin,
a rational grid comparison of the symbolic fixed point with 512 recurrence
iterations, elimination of five starting exponents up to $K=100$, and
hostile controls for superlinear volume, unit-scale recursion, omitted
transient space, and repeated calls.  It completed in 1.76 seconds.  These
are algebra checks, not empirical evidence for an all-input SAT phase.
