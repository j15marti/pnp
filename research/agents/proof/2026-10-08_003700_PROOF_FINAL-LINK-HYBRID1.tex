\section{Result}

An executable hybrid now combines the strongest recovered source-checkable
WRAPPER mechanisms in one forward constructor: unit propagation, clause
subsumption, component AND, bounded-frontier dynamic programming, Horn and
2-CNF VALUE procedures, fixed-size strong backdoors, backdoor DNFs, disjoint
clause covers, exact residual equality, oriented clause dominance, and exact
source replay.  Every completed plan was independently replayed and every SAT
answer supplied a source-valid lifted witness.

The result is useful but does not close the final link.  The suite completed
376 exact answer checks and 376 full plan-replay checks on 290 formulas, with
346 lifted witness checks and zero errors.  On the 34 heterogeneous cases, 33
plans met the finite shape diagnostic
\[
        q\le \lfloor M^{1/3}\rfloor,
        \qquad
        \max_i |G_i|\le \lfloor M^{2/3}\rfloor.
\]
The remaining case, PHP$(8,7)$, reached the 4,097-node safety cap before the
constructor completed.  More importantly, none of the 34 cases met the
unit-coefficient $M^{2/3}$ explicit-workspace diagnostic, and only 15 met the
unit-coefficient $M^{4/3}$ construction-work diagnostic.  These coefficient-one
checks are finite diagnostics, not asymptotic refutations.  They expose why a
small final leaf set is insufficient: the constructor may already have spent
large work or retained a large plan before producing it.

The scientific status is therefore \textbf{OPEN}.  The run establishes exact
partial rules, witness lifting, and a reproducible hostile control.  It does
not prove that one rule succeeds with favorable simultaneous construction,
fanout, child-size, replay, and workspace bounds on every general SAT input.

\section{Exact target and model}

For an ordinary indexed CNF $F$ of encoded length $M$, the desired forward
procedure must produce an exact Boolean plan whose recursive SAT leaves
$G_1,\ldots,G_q$ satisfy power bounds.  Every successful SAT route must lift
to a witness for $F$.  Construction reads, discarded candidates, dominance
comparisons, base-class recognition, source rereads, output generation, and
live state are charged.  A partial procedure may return \textsc{fail}, but a
fixed portfolio closes the link only if its declared domains cover every
input with the required common resource bounds.

The implementation separates three quantities that earlier experiments could
conflate:
\begin{itemize}
\item[] \textbf{Plan shape:} $q$ and the maximum encoded recursive-child size.
\item[] \textbf{Acquisition:} every literal/clause read, restriction pass,
subsumption or dominance comparison, graph step, frontier transition, and
base-rule check performed before the plan is available.
\item[] \textbf{Replay and live state:} independent syntactic regeneration of
every OR child and component family, plus a conservative explicit-plan
workspace ledger.
\end{itemize}

Leaf SAT calls occur only in the independent validation pass.  They are
counted separately and are excluded from construction work.

\section{Literature bridge}

Backdoor DNFs provide exactly the kind of tautological OR cover used here.
The available detection theorems are fixed-parameter statements for named
base classes; they do not say that every general CNF has a favorable DNF or
that a growing parameter meets this WRAPPER ledger
\cite{ordyniak2021bdnf}.  Backdoor-depth methods likewise provide powerful
restricted-domain algorithms but no bounded-depth theorem for every CNF
\cite{dreier2022}.

The sparsification lemma decomposes fixed-width CNF into an exact disjunction
of sparse formulas, but its $2^{\varepsilon n}$ fanout is not polynomial in
the ordinary input length when $n\gg\log M$ \cite{ipz2001,calabro2010}.
Knowledge compilation explains why decomposability and determinism support
cheap queries once a suitable representation has been compiled; it does not
make unrestricted compilation cheap \cite{darwiche2002}.  These are directly
relevant boundaries, not transfers of a missing general-SAT theorem.

\section{New exact construction}

\subsection{Disjoint clause cover}

\paragraph{CLAUSE-FIRST-COVER1 (\Exact).}
Let $C=(\ell_1\vee\cdots\vee\ell_w)$ be a clause of $F$.  Define
\[
 \tau_i=\{\ell_1=0,\ldots,\ell_{i-1}=0,\ell_i=1\}
 \qquad (1\le i\le w).
\]
Then
\[
 \operatorname{SAT}(F)
   =\bigvee_{i=1}^{w}\operatorname{SAT}(F\upharpoonright\tau_i).
\]
The terms are pairwise disjoint on complete assignments.  Every model of $F$
satisfies $C$ and therefore has a unique first true literal.  Conversely, a
model of a residual combined with $\tau_i$ is a model of $F$.  Thus the rule
is source-checkable, makes no SAT query, and has an explicit witness lift.
For 3-CNF it emits at most three routes rather than the eight assignments to
all variables of a clause.  It does not by itself guarantee power shrinkage.

\subsection{Exact hybrid plan}

At each state the builder first performs unit propagation, tautology deletion,
duplicate deletion, and clause subsumption.  A formula already below the
target is emitted immediately as a recursive child.  Larger formulas may be
settled by Horn, 2-CNF, or fixed-width ordered-frontier algorithms.  A
constant-size strong backdoor or a verified tautological backdoor DNF may
return a zero-child VALUE plan.  Variable-disjoint components are combined by
AND.  Otherwise CLAUSE-FIRST-COVER1 or Shannon branching supplies an OR plan.

Inside an OR frontier, exact equal residuals are identified.  If residuals
$R_i,R_j$ have the same live-variable set and
\[
       \operatorname{Clauses}(R_j)
          \subsetneq \operatorname{Clauses}(R_i),
\]
then $R_i\Rightarrow R_j$ and $R_i$ is deleted.  The retained route still has
its own source assignment, so any retained witness lifts to $F$.  This is the
recovered CLAUSE-DOM-SELECT1 rule in an executable forward plan.

\paragraph{HYBRID-PARTIAL-SOUND1 (\Exact).}
Every completed plan returned by the program has the same SAT value as its
source CNF, and every reported SAT witness lifts to a source witness.
This follows by induction over CONST, VALUE, LEAF, AND, OR, LIFT, equality,
and dominance nodes.  The independent replayer regenerates every OR residual
from its parent and verifies every component split before accepting the plan.
The theorem is a soundness theorem for successful finite plans, not a
favorable general resource theorem.

\section{Why the finite shape success is not the final link}

The main negative observation is visible on PHP.  PHP$(7,6)$ ended with only
one recursive child of 186 bits under a 201-bit child target, but it first
constructed 719 plan nodes, spent 2,314,917 charged operations, retained a
550,551-bit explicit plan at peak, and incurred 74,720 parent-literal rereads
during independent replay.  The apparently excellent $(q,\max|G_i|)$ pair
therefore does not certify cheap acquisition.

On PHP$(8,7)$ the same rule reached the 4,097-node safety cap.  At that point
it had spent 17,117,951 charged operations and retained 3,564,050 logical
bits.  Its six already visible leaves were each below the nominal child
target, but the forward plan was incomplete, so the rule correctly returned
\textsc{fail}.  Declaring success from those leaves would be unsound.

Zero-child plans require the same caution.  They are valuable when a cheap
base recognizer settles the source.  A zero-child answer obtained only after
large backdoor search or recursive construction is a completed SAT algorithm,
not free contraction.  The implementation charges this acquisition before
recording $q=0$.

\section{Corrected Route B exponent ledger}

The recovered SourceLocalization half-scale cell must be evaluated with the
all-block fanout charge.  For child scale $\lambda$, acquisition exponent
$\beta$, parent-machine exponent $k$, and ordinary all-block fanout
$a=1-\lambda$, the total exponent is
\[
       E=a+\beta+\lambda k
        =1+\beta+\lambda \qquad (k=2).
\]
Consequently $E<4/3$ requires
\[
       \boxed{\beta+\lambda<1/3}.
\]
At $\beta=1/6$ one needs $\lambda<1/6$.  As $\beta\to0$, one still needs
$\lambda<1/3$.  The old half-scale point $\lambda=1/2$ gives
$E=3/2+\beta$ and is outside the target.  At
$(\lambda,\beta)=(1/6,1/6)$ the value is exactly $4/3$, with no strict slack.

A genuinely global shared $H$/BatchAcquire theorem could change the ledger by
removing or reducing the repeated all-block acquisition term.  That sharing
must be proved for the same realization and must include source access,
storage, recovery, and every block consumer.  The present component sharing
and source replay measurements do not prove such a theorem.

\section{Duplication and contradiction audit}

\begin{itemize}
\item[] \textbf{Adopted.}  Exact Shannon OR, component AND, base-class
algorithms, bounded-frontier DP, backdoor DNFs, certified partial portfolios,
source replay, and CLAUSE-DOM-SELECT1 remain valid in their stated domains.
\item[] \textbf{New.}  CLAUSE-FIRST-COVER1 is isolated as a simple disjoint
witness-preserving rule and integrated with the recovered mechanisms in one
replay-verified constructor.  A node-cap hostile failure and the all-block
Route B correction are included in the same raw ledger.
\item[] \textbf{Rejected.}  Small $q$, small final children, zero-child VALUE,
or successful finite portfolios do not imply cheap general acquisition.
The explicit-plan workspace is not silently replaced by an unimplemented
source-backed descriptor.
\item[] \textbf{Retained open arrow.}  No reviewed theorem or experiment proves
uniform favorable coverage of unrestricted SAT by this finite partial
portfolio.  One load-bearing arrow remains OPEN, so no P versus NP separation
is claimed.
\end{itemize}
