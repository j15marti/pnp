\begin{abstract}
We recover exact degree-two elimination and test its integration into a
SAT-only recursive witness algorithm on all 44 deletions of the published
33-vertex, girth-eight critical source. Every lifted witness checks, but the
reduced language contains binary relations with 15 or 20 allowed pairs, not
single T5 arcs. A literal same-language recursion is therefore falsified.
Sparse incomparability does give existence of intrinsic critical families of
unbounded girth; it does not supply the required promise-preserving search
reduction. We give a fully charged conditional criticalization procedure and
state the additional hypotheses needed for exponent contraction. P versus NP
and a uniform power-shrinking critical-promise compiler remain OPEN.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Target and quantifiers.}
Let $R(a,b)$ mean $b-a\in\{1,3\}$ in $\Z$.
An input is an explicitly declared simple oriented graph $G$ on $n$ vertices,
with a designated missing oriented edge $e$. Define $\mathcal P$ by requiring
$H=G+e$ to be connected and edge-minimal UNSAT for T5. All vertices are retained
in $G$. Require girth thresholds separately when specified; they are not advice
strings. The output is all $n$ original-label colors satisfying every arc of
$G$. No certificate of the promise is provided free. With binary vertex IDs,
source length $L$ includes declarations, arcs, separators, and $e$; a canonical
explicit encoding has $L=\Theta((n+m+1)\log(n+2))$. The provided ASCII benchmark
is instead measured by its exact 493 source bytes, excluding the designation.
A hypothetical finder means
\[
 \exists A,c,k\ \forall x\in\mathcal P:\quad
 A(x)\text{ emits a correct witness in at most }c(|x|+1)^k\text{ steps}.
\]
The machine and constants are independent of $x$. No behavior off the promise
is assumed. Distinguish this hypothesis from a finder for \emph{every}
satisfiable T5 graph. Use a deterministic multitape bit machine with read-only
explicit input and charged writable storage; both bounds concern one execution.

\paragraph{Mechanism and falsifiers.}
Candidate: suppress low-degree variables, solve a smaller child by the
hypothetical finder, then lift the witness. Pre-test failure conditions are:
(i) any original retained arc fails after lifting; (ii) the projected relation
is not the asserted child relation; (iii) a child falls outside the finder
promise; (iv) full encoded child lengths, calls, or retained state invalidate
the proposed resource recurrence. This run settles (i)--(ii) on an authentic
critical benchmark and falsifies automatic literal language closure in (iii).
It neither proves nor refutes the existence of a more sophisticated compiler.

\section*{Exact relation and witness transport}
\begin{lemma}[Recovered path relation]
For any orientation word $s_1,\ldots,s_\ell\in\{-1,1\}$ on a path,
the endpoint relation has $5\min(5,\ell+1)$ pairs.
For $\ell\ge4$ it is the full binary relation.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write successive differences $s_i(1+2b_i)$, $b_i\in\{0,1\}$.
If $q$ signs are negative, all integer values in $[-q,\ell-q]$ occur
as $\sum_i s_i b_i$. Multiplication by 2 is invertible modulo 5.
For $\ell\le3$ the $\ell+1$ consecutive values give distinct residues;
for $\ell\ge4$ they cover all residues. Each start color is unrestricted.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Literal same-language suppression fails]
The exact suppression of a two-edge or three-edge path cannot, for every
endpoint assignment, be represented by one T5 arc in either direction,
even after arbitrary endpoint color permutations. Deleting the resulting
constraint altogether is also not exact.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The support sizes are 15 and 20; an arc has support 10 and an absent constraint
has support 25. Bijections preserve cardinality. For the smallest forward
two-edge example, $(0,2)$ extends through color 1 but violates a single forward
arc; $(0,3)$ satisfies that arc but has no extension through the path.
\end{proof}
This is a falsification of \emph{literal} suppression followed by an ordinary
arc-instance oracle call, not a lower bound on arbitrary encodings. Reintroducing
the path realizes its existential definition exactly, but restores the removed
variables and does not by itself give power shrinkage. Weighted tables in /081
retain more information than our Boolean tables; Boolean support is sufficient
for witness existence but does not preserve solution counts.

\begin{theorem}[Exact Boolean elimination with lift]
For any finite T5 input and any sequence eliminating variables having at most
two distinct remaining neighbors, retain the conjunction of all incident
factors, existentially quantify the eliminated variable, and store one
realizing color for each allowed boundary tuple. Every witness for the final
factor instance lifts to a witness for the original instance, and conversely
every original witness restricts to a witness of the final instance.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At one step with boundary $B$, replace the incident conjunction $F_v$ by
$Q_B(z)=\bigvee_{c\in\Z}F_v(z,c)$. Store a selected $c$ precisely when
$Q_B(z)=1$. The identity is pointwise for every assignment to all remaining
variables, including unrelated factors and pins. Reverse the elimination
order; all boundary variables of a stored step have already been assigned.
The selected color satisfies its incident factors, so induction reconstructs
the original conjunction. Empty-scope and unary factors are included.
\end{proof}
Pins can invalidate rotation symmetry; our tested instances contain no pins
until after elimination. Simultaneous translation preserves all their factors,
so fixing one remaining vertex to zero is sound. Each nonempty original
solution orbit has exactly five elements; this constant symmetry saving is
not an exponent improvement. A canonical original-label witness uses $3n$
color bits; parsing and the final original-arc scan remain charged.

\section*{Intrinsic critical families: existence versus access}
\begin{lemma}[Structure of a critical source]
A connected edge-minimal UNSAT T5 source has no bridge or articulation vertex,
and no four-edge simple path whose three internal vertices have degree two.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If a bridge exists, each component after its removal is SAT by minimality.
Translate all colors in one component so its bridge endpoint satisfies the
bridge; T5 has a nonempty in- and out-neighborhood. This would color the source.
If an articulation exists, color each proper subgraph consisting of the
articulation and one component after its removal. Translate each coloring to
the same articulation color and glue them, again a contradiction.
For the path assertion, remove its edges and color the resulting proper
subgraph. Its internal vertices have no other incident arcs, so recolor them
to extend the two endpoint colors using the full path relation.
\end{proof}
In particular $H-e$ is connected. The no-long-thread statement is about $H$;
deleting $e$ can create new suppressible paths in $G$. It does not bound
arbitrary algorithms or justify declaring their remaining work hard.

\begin{theorem}[Existence, without a search hardness reduction]
For every integer $g\ge3$ there exists a connected edge-minimal UNSAT simple
oriented T5 source $H_g$ of undirected girth greater than $g$. Every edge
deletion is connected and SAT, and $|V(H_g)|>g$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use Kun's Sparse Incomparability Theorem 1 with template size threshold $t=6$,
girth parameter $g$, and the transitive four-vertex tournament $D$. It is UNSAT
for T5: a tournament homomorphism is injective, but its source has a vertex
dominating three others whereas a T5 vertex has only two out-neighbors.
The constructed $D'$ maps to $D$, has girth greater than $g$, and remains
UNSAT for T5. Mapping to $D$ excludes loops and opposite arcs. Choose an
inclusion-minimal UNSAT edge subset and discard isolated vertices. Its
nontrivial part is connected, since otherwise an UNSAT component would be a
proper UNSAT edge subset. Every forest is T5-colorable by extending leaves,
so this source has a cycle and therefore more than $g$ vertices. Apply the
preceding lemma to its edge deletions.
\end{proof}
This is a pointwise existence theorem $\forall g\,\exists H_g$, not a
polynomial generator in $g$, nor a uniform reduction into these SAT deletion
instances. An input encoding does not include a free construction history.
It also excludes disconnected padding as the explanation of the critical
family: minimality and connectedness are intrinsic.

\paragraph{Verified primary boundary.}
Kun (2013 version, Theorem 1) provides polynomial construction for fixed
relational type and fixed positive $t,k$. Guzm\'an-Pro and Martin (ICALP 2025,
Theorem 7) state the digraph form and define girth via half the incidence-graph
girth; on simple oriented sources it is the ordinary undirected girth.
These statements preserve template satisfiability, not edge minimality or
designated-edge witness promises. No uniform polynomial blowup for a girth
parameter growing with encoded input length is inferred here. No SAT
time-space numerical endpoint is imported into the T5 promise model.

\section*{Conditional criticalization and hereditary resource accounting}
\begin{theorem}[Conditional uniform criticalization]
Suppose one deterministic machine $A$ returns a witness for \emph{every}
satisfiable explicit T5 graph within $p(L)$ steps. Given an UNSAT graph $D$
of length $L$, its connected edge-minimal UNSAT subgraph can be found by at
most $m+1$ bounded calls to $A$ plus parsing, copying, verification and output.
A conservative bound on one sequential bit-machine realization is
\[
 T\le O\bigl((m+1)(p(cL)+L^5)\bigr),\qquad
 S\le O(L^2+\log(p(cL)+2))+\max_{z:\,|z|\le cL} S_{A,p}(z).
\]
Here $S_{A,p}$ is actual writable space in the clocked execution, including
off-promise calls; hence it is always at most a constant times $p(cL)+cL$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On any graph $F$, simulate $A$ for $p(|F|)$ steps and accept exactly when a
complete valid witness was emitted. SAT inputs succeed by hypothesis; UNSAT
inputs can never emit a valid witness. This supplies a decision subroutine
without free SAT/UNSAT advice. First test $D$ and return its witness if SAT;
thus the UNSAT precondition can be established at the charged cost of one call.
Starting with an UNSAT $D$, consider each original edge
once and remove it precisely when its deletion remains UNSAT. A retained
edge stays necessary, since further edge deletions preserve the SAT witness
that caused its retention. The result is edge-minimal; discard isolates and
use connectedness as above. Repeated children, failed witness attempts,
timeouts, counter bits, source copies, source scanning, and final output are
all paid. Execute children sequentially and release their state. The displayed
polynomial overhead is conservative, not optimized.
\end{proof}
If a known polynomial space bound for $A$ holds only on SAT inputs, it cannot
replace $S_{A,p}$ for these UNSAT trials. If $A$ is promised only
$\mathcal P$, the decision subroutine is unjustified: arbitrary SAT deletion
queries need not belong to $\mathcal P$. Choosing a minimal obstruction in an
existence proof therefore does not give free criticalization to a promised
finder. Moreover the procedure has up to $m$ children of length $O(L)$, not
$O(L^\theta)$ for $\theta<1$.

\paragraph{A sufficient recurrence, explicitly conditional.}
Suppose a uniform compiler and decoder, for every promised source, make at
most $O(L^\eta)$ clocked child calls, each of encoded length at most
$cL^\theta$, $0<\theta<1$, and obtain sufficient valid witnesses. All child
calls must be in the finder's promise, or an independently proved bounded
selection scheme must ensure success despite the off-promise ones. Let total
non-child work be $O(L^r)$, and peak retained parent, buffers, indexes, decoded
output, and suspended ancestor storage be $O(L^b)$. These are hypotheses
about the entire execution, including discovery, alternative orders, replay
and failed attempts. If the same child implementation has bounds
$T_A(u)=O(u^k)$ and $S_A(u)=O(u^s)$ on \emph{all invoked executions}, then
\[
 T'(L)=O(L^r+L^{\eta+\theta k}),\qquad
 S'(L)=O(L^b+L^{\theta s}).
\]
The proof is addition of all sequential child times and a maximum of their
spaces plus retained state. With concurrency use total work and aggregate
live space instead. For recursive compilation the bound on $L^b$ must cover
the sum of retained ancestor frames; it cannot mean just the current frame.
All constants may depend on the fixed program, never the individual source.
Thus $k'=\max(r,\eta+\theta k)$ is a conditional upper-bound transform.
Its putative fixed point $\max(r,\eta/(1-\theta))$ is not an impossibility
bound. Repeated application needs a uniform reapplicable compiler and the
same semantic and resource invariants at every level. Eliminating an arbitrary
hypothetical exponent $K$ remains conditional on those unproved premises.
Neither the benchmark's vertex decrease nor general NP-completeness supplies
such an encoded-length recurrence.

\paragraph{Elimination ledger and alternatives.}
Our Boolean step stores at most 25 boundary rows and their selected colors.
There are at most $m+n$ factors and $n$ records. Source scanning, repeated
degree scans, incident-factor acquisition, constant-domain tuple enumeration,
allocation and reverse reconstruction have a conservative simultaneous
$O((L+1)^5)$ time, $O((L+1)^2)$ writable-bit realization using sequential
records. This bounds elimination and lifting, not the residual search.
The Python test retains ancestor domain copies and recomputes supports in
each search call; all child calls and failed branches are counted. Its counters
are logical work, not a physical TM trace or a memory measurement.
The exact residual solver uses exhaustive domain branching with arc
consistency; other variable orders, bucket/treewidth methods, GF(5) cycle
constraints, flows, algebraic compression and compact circuits remain legal.
The cycle equations plus two-valued edge increments explain the path lemma;
discarding the two-valued restrictions to apply Gaussian elimination would
be a relaxation, not an exact general solver. No lower bound on any of these
alternatives is claimed. The /081 weighted root/width method already gives
a competing cheap solution to the finite benchmark.
