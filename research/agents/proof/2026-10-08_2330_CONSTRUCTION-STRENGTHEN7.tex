\section{Selected target and exact encoding}

The preceding Construction handoff asked for certified propagation and
component factoring with acquisition, failed trials and aggregate volume
charged.  The latest Red-Team repair fixes the endpoint language as total
\texttt{DENSE-CNF1}: canonical ULEB128, positive-odd/negative-even zig-zag,
attained maximum identifier, exact EOF, and polynomial density in the
complete encoded bit length $N$.  Malformed strings denote NO.  This report
works on that exact language.

For a variable $x$, write $P_x$ for clauses containing $x$, $M_x$ for
clauses containing $\neg x$, and $K_x$ for the other clauses.  Define
\[
 \operatorname{DP}_x(F)=K_x\cup
 \{(C\setminus\{x\})\cup(D\setminus\{\neg x\}):
 C\in P_x,D\in M_x,\text{ resolvent nontautological}\}.       \tag{1}
\]
The canonical implementation removes duplicate resolvents only after exact
comparison; all comparisons and rejected raw pairs are charged.

\begin{lemma}[projection exactness]\label{lem:dp}
$F$ is satisfiable iff $\operatorname{DP}_x(F)$ is satisfiable.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Any model of $F$ restricted off $x$ satisfies every resolvent.  Conversely,
if an assignment off $x$ cannot be extended with $x=0$, it falsifies the
non-$x$ part of some clause in $P_x$; if it also cannot be extended with
$x=1$, it falsifies the non-$x$ part of some clause in $M_x$.  It then
falsifies their nontautological resolvent, a contradiction.  A tautological
resolvent imposes no condition.
\end{proof}

\section{The source-first member}

Fix
\[
 B(N)=\lfloor N^{1/2}\log_2(N+2)\rfloor,quad
 V(N)=\lfloor N^{11/10}\log_2^2(N+2)\rfloor,quad
 A(N)=\lfloor N^{6/5}\log_2^2(N+2)\rfloor.                 \tag{2}
\]
The deterministic machine \textsc{PIVOT-FACTOR1} tries pivots in increasing
identifier order.  It generates (1) from the immutable source, finds the
variable-disjoint primal components, and dry-runs the exact complete CNF1
encoding of every component.  It accepts the first pivot only if
\[
 \max_i |Q_i|\le B(N),\qquad
 \sum_i|Q_i|\le V(N),qquad |Q_i|<N,                       \tag{3}
\]
every child passes the dense total-language check, and the complete
acquisition plus replay fits $A(N)$.  A charged step counter aborts to
UNKNOWN before the first SAT call on budget exhaustion.  If accepted, the
machine replays the generation, materializes one child at a time, executes
every child occurrence, and ANDs the answers.  Equal encodings are still
executed and charged separately.

\begin{theorem}[PIVOT-FACTOR1]\label{thm:member}
There is a uniform implementation of the preceding member such that on every
input it either returns UNKNOWN with zero child calls, decides a trivial
empty/contradictory case, or returns the correct SAT answer with
\[
 T_{\rm local}(N)\le N^{6/5+o(1)},\quad
 \max_i|Q_i|\le N^{1/2+o(1)},\quad
 \sum_i|Q_i|\le N^{11/10+o(1)},                            \tag{4}
\]
and simultaneous retained space $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ including one child buffer.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Enumerate source clauses and resolution pairs with $O(\log N)$-bit counters.
The implicit primal graph is undirected.  A streaming fast path verifies
monotone disjoint support intervals; otherwise deterministic logspace
connectivity gives a canonical component representative without storing a
union--find table.  Either route is charged and aborts if it does not finish
under the cap.  Exact clause counts, maxima and byte lengths are obtained by
dry passes.  The universal step counter includes every failed pivot, source
scan, raw resolution pair, exact duplicate comparison, connectivity step,
header pass and the complete replay;
on reaching $A(N)$ it erases temporary state and returns UNKNOWN.  Thus
acceptance, not an average-case estimate, proves the time bound.

After all checks, replay emits only the current child, capped by $B(N)$.
Counters, pivot, component representative, connectivity state and answer
accumulator are polylogarithmic.  This proves the same-machine space bound.
Lemma~\ref{lem:dp} and independence of primal components give
\[
 \SAT(F)=\bigwedge_i\SAT(Q_i).
\]
Equations (2)--(3) prove (4), with executions counted with multiplicity.
No parent-scale semantic query occurs in discovery.
\end{proof}

For a descendant solver of time $m^{K+o(1)}$, this accepted phase has
\[
 \sum_i m_i^K\le (\max_i m_i)^{K-1}\sum_i m_i
 \le N^{K/2+3/5+o(1)}.                                   \tag{5}
\]
This is the same useful exponent map as the marked cutset compiler, acquired
here without a supplied marker on a different syntactic region.  The emitted
children are ordinary CNFs and the member may return UNKNOWN on them, so (5)
cannot be iterated to remove $K$.

\paragraph{Nonvacuity.}
Let $F_m$ have a hub $h$ and pairs $(a_i,b_i)$, with clauses
$(h\vee a_i\vee b_i)$, $(\neg a_i\vee b_i)$ and
$(a_i\vee\neg b_i)$ for $1\le i\le m$.  The source primal graph is
connected.  Order the clauses and identifiers by increasing $i$.  Since $h$
occurs only positively, eliminating it produces $m$ independent two-clause
components on monotone disjoint intervals.  The streaming fast path verifies
this in linear passes.  Their aggregate encoding is linear and their maximum
size is logarithmic in the source identifiers.  Therefore all sufficiently
large $F_m$ are accepted by the fixed uniform member.

\section{Why ordinary BVE does not cover all input}

The literature supports the semantics but not a universal resource theorem.
E\'en and Biere generate all nontrivial resolvents and apply elimination only
under a bounded-growth heuristic; failed high-occurrence trials were costly
enough to require cutoffs.  Their bound is on clause count, not complete
encoded bits.  See
\url{https://doi.org/10.1007/11499107_5}.  Biere and Sinz prove the AND rule
for connected components and report nearly linear practical detection, but a
connected input yields one unchanged component and their union--find retains
per-variable state:
\url{https://doi.org/10.3233/SAT190022}.  These are sound mechanisms, not an
all-input contraction theorem.

There are two exact controls.

\paragraph{One-pivot infinite miss.}
For $m\ge2$, let
\[
 X_m=\bigwedge_{i=1}^m(x\vee a_i)\wedge
     \bigwedge_{j=1}^m(\neg x\vee b_j).                   \tag{6}
\]
Eliminating $x$ creates all $m^2$ clauses $(a_i\vee b_j)$ in one connected
component.  Eliminating a leaf drops its one pure clause but leaves a
connected star with $2m-1$ clauses and encoded size $N-O(\log m)$.  Hence for
every pivot the maximum child exceeds $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ for all sufficiently
large $m$, so PIVOT-FACTOR1 returns UNKNOWN.  This family is satisfiable and
easy; it refutes coverage of this member, not the existence of another one.

\paragraph{Non-growing BVE parity miss.}
Take a connected linear 3-uniform, 3-regular hypergraph and encode one XOR
constraint on each hyperedge by its four width-three clauses.  There are no
units or pure literals, no duplicate/subsumed clauses, and the primal graph is
connected.  A variable belongs to three parity blocks, giving six occurrences
of each sign.  Of the $36$ resolution pairs, $12$ within-block resolvents are
tautological while the $24$ cross-block resolvents are distinct width-four
clauses.  Eliminating the variable therefore replaces twelve width-three
clauses by twenty-four width-four clauses.  Standard non-growing BVE accepts
no pivot, and factoring leaves the original $\Theta(N)$ component.  Connected
finite covers of a linear regular base supply unbounded instances.

The smallest checked obstruction in this style uses four parity equations
on six edge variables.  Its 16 clauses occupy 70 canonical bytes; eliminating
any of the six variables yields 16 clauses occupying 78 bytes even after
tautology deletion, deduplication and subsumption.  This distinguishes a
genuine byte ledger from a clause-count claim.  Classical exponential lower
bounds for Davis--Putnam/regular resolution on Tseitin contradictions further
show why allowing unrestricted intermediate growth is not a polynomial
repair; they do not rule out a different semantic compiler.
