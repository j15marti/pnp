\section{Claim card and machine model}

\section{A source-checkable terminal}

\begin{definition}[\textsc{EXTREME-1}]
On a proper $3$CNF $F$, first scan $F$ under the all-zero assignment.  If it
satisfies every clause, return TRUE.  Otherwise retain a pointer to one
falsified clause $C_0$.  For each variable occurring in $C_0$ (at most three),
rescan $F$ under the assignment obtained by flipping only that variable; on a
verified model return TRUE.  Repeat symmetrically from all-one and one
falsified clause $C_1$.  If all tests fail, return UNKNOWN, never FALSE.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[constant-candidate completeness]
\textsc{EXTREME-1} returns TRUE exactly when $F$ has a satisfying assignment
at Hamming distance at most one from all-zero or all-one.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every returned assignment is verified by a complete scan.  Conversely, let
$\alpha$ differ from an extreme assignment $e$ only on variable $x$.  If $e$
is not already a model, every clause falsified by $e$ must contain $x$:
changing any variable absent from such a clause cannot satisfy it.  In
particular $x$ occurs in the first witnessed falsified clause, so the member
tests $\alpha$.  Empty clauses yield no candidate and no Boolean answer.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[joint resource bound]
There is one uniform implementation of \textsc{EXTREME-1} such that, for
every valid proper $3$CNF of length $N$, it returns TRUE or UNKNOWN correctly
within
\[
  T(N)=N^{1+o(1)},\qquad S(N)=N^{o(1)}.
\]
It has zero ordinary SAT children and zero terminal children.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are at most eight full source scans: the two extremes and at most three
one-flip candidates for each.  A literal is evaluated by its sign and by
comparing its read-only ULEB identifier with the retained candidate's source
substring.  Bitwise comparison costs a polylogarithmic factor and uses a
constant number of $O(\log N)$ pointers and counters.  No assignment vector,
clause table, component map, or residual formula is stored.  Thus the charged
cost is $O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)$ time and $O(\log N)$ writable bits.
\end{proof}

\section{Strict promise-domain transition}

For $m\ge2$, let $x$ and the triples $(a_i,b_i,c_i)$ be pairwise distinct and
put
\begin{equation}
\Psi_m=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}
 (a_i\vee b_i\vee c_i)\wedge
 (x\vee\neg a_i\vee b_i)\wedge
 (\neg x\vee\neg b_i\vee c_i)\wedge
 (\neg c_i\vee a_i\vee b_i).                 \tag{1}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[audited strict family]
For all sufficiently large $m$, the exact corrected controller $A_0$ returns
UNKNOWN on $\Psi_m$, whereas \textsc{EXTREME-1} returns TRUE.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All-one satisfies (1), so the new member returns after its first verification
scan.  The formula is connected through $x$, sign-balanced, non-Horn and
non-Krom, and has neither units nor a pure variable.  On support
$\{a_i,b_i,c_i\}$ its sign mask has size two; the other supports have mask
size one.  Hence the parity-star and full-mask rules decline.  It has neither
the guard grammar nor a bounded active-variable set.

Every strong Horn backdoor must hit at least two variables in each disjoint
positive triple, so its size is at least $2m$; in particular the size-two
member declines.  No singleton variable occurs in every width-three clause,
so the singleton strong-Krom member declines.  Eliminating $x$ produces the
non-tautological cross-block resolvents
$(\neg a_i\vee b_i\vee\neg b_j\vee c_j)$ for $i\ne j$, exceeding the charged
PIVOT output/time cap.  Eliminating any local variable leaves the other
$m-1$ blocks connected through $x$, hence a $\Theta(N)$-bit child exceeding
the square-root child cap.  The remaining exact syntactic members therefore
decline.  These checks were independently red-teamed against the named order
of $A_0$.
\end{proof}

Let $E$ be the inputs on which \textsc{EXTREME-1} returns TRUE.  Form $A_+$ by
trying this member and, on UNKNOWN, executing $A_0$.  With the repository's
finite boundary types, define
\[
D_+^{(0)}=D_0^{(0)}\cup E,
\qquad
D_{+,\tau}^{(d+1)}=D_{+,\tau}^{(0)}\cup
\{X:A_{0,\tau}(X)=\operatorname{PLAN}((Q_i,\sigma_i)_i),
      Q_i\in D_{+,\sigma_i}^{(d)}\ \forall i\},
\]
and $D_+^\star=\bigcup_dD_+^{(d)}$.  Repeated children remain repeated
executions.

\begin{theorem}[actual strict promise transition]
For one fixed uniform depth-first machine and one common $\rho(N)\to0$,
\[
D_0^\star\subsetneq D_+^\star,qquad
T(N)\le N^{6/5+\rho(N)},\qquad
S(N)\le N^{1/2+\rho(N)}
\quad\text{on }\SAT\!\upharpoonright D_+^\star.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The old controller is preserved after a zero-child $N^{1+o(1)}$ prepass.
Recovery9's terminal-absorption theorem charges terminal local work but
requires heredity only for unresolved ordinary-SAT exits.  Therefore the old
joint envelope remains $(6/5,1/2)$, including discovery, failed members,
replay, buffers, indexes and ancestor frames.  The preceding lemma places
$\Psi_m$ in $D_+^{(0)}$ but outside $D_0^\star$ for all sufficiently large
$m$, proving strictness.
\end{proof}

This is a genuine language transition, but only for a larger promise.  It
adds SAT yes-instances and gives no UNSAT answer.  It does not imply that
every SAT input or every successor belongs to $D_+^\star$.

\section{Rejected promotion: singleton Horn/dual-Horn components}

The candidate scanner itself is exact.  From the first non-Horn clause, a
singleton strong-Horn variable must be one of at most two positive literals;
a clause with three positives rules it out.  A full scan verifies each
candidate.  Negating literals gives the dual rule.  Empty residual clauses
must immediately make a branch false, the empty residual true, and duplicate
residual clauses must remain a raw multiset unless deduplication is charged.

The proposed terminal then branches on the singleton, partitions each Horn
or dual-Horn residual into primal components, buffers a component of at most
$N^{1/2+o(1)}$ bits, solves it and discards it.  Semantically this is sound,
and a hard operation cap makes every Boolean answer safe.  It is not yet a
proved favorable member: the experiment's \texttt{components()} stores
\texttt{remaining}, component indices and support for the whole residual,
using $\Theta(N)$ derived state.  A generic canonical-minimum connectivity
enumerator can be step-capped, but no $N^{6/5+o(1)}$ completion proof on the
strict family follows.  Buffer plus Horn work arrays must also share, not
each consume, the global half-space allowance.  Finally PIVOT may create
width-four children, so root width-three totality alone is not successor
totality.  These are proof obligations, not implementation details.

The minimal empty-residual control is
$(x)\wedge(\neg x)\wedge(x\vee y)$: both restrictions contain an empty
clause.  The duplicate control
$(x\vee a)\wedge(a)$ at $x=0$ contains two copies of $(a)$.  Both are now
recorded in the runner.

\section{Validation and literature boundary}

The bounded runner finished in 3.57 seconds under a 25-second alarm.  It
checked 41,448 combinations of one through four exact-width-three clauses on
four variables and found zero singleton Horn/dual structural or SAT
mismatches.  A second suite exhausted all 17,902 formulas with zero through
four distinct proper clauses of widths one through three on three variables:
zero scan mismatches and zero \textsc{EXTREME-1} semantic mismatches.  The
strict family was sampled through $m=64$.  The Python witness dictionary and
component sets deliberately do not certify machine space; the theorem uses
the source-pointer implementation above.

Nishimura--Ragde--Szeider give fixed-parameter detection for small strong
Horn and binary backdoors, not an all-input bound on backdoor size.  Recursive
backdoors validate component-sensitive accounting when bounded depth is
supplied, but their existence/detection results remain parameterized.
Bounded backdoor-depth algorithms likewise do not force every formula into a
fixed depth.  Thus none supplies cheap all-input acquisition.  The exact
endpoint retained by this project is FLMV Corollary~5.8 on deterministic
random-access machines,
\[
\operatorname{NTIME}[n]\not\subseteq
\operatorname{DTISP}[n^c,o(n^{d(c)})],\qquad
d(c)=\tfrac12\bigl((c+2)/c^2-c\bigr).
\]
At $c=1201/1000$, $d(c)>1/2$.  No theorem found turns an arbitrary
$N^K$ SAT algorithm supplied by $P=NP$ into the needed all-input favorable
phase with joint space.

\section{Proof-spine consequence and next target}

The ARC spine now has a source-level strict promise step whose resource proof
does not depend on hidden component state.  Adding it invalidates any
unqualified reuse of ARC6's old claim that the enlarged UNKNOWN domain has
the same achievable region as general SAT: the prior hard embedding may be
caught whenever its output has a model within one bit of an extreme.  No such
fallback equality is asserted in this ARC report.  The subsequent independent
REDTEAM-STRENGTHEN7 report supplies an exact repair: conjoin two disjoint NAE
triples before the already universal ARC6 isolation/guard map.  This restores
the scoped fallback equality but still gives no PRE-A edge.

The main barrier remains unchanged: prove a finite source-first controller
whose favorable domain contains every valid SAT input and every ordinary-SAT
successor, or derive such a controller from $\SAT\in P$ without paying the
unknown parent-scale exponent $K$.  The most local construction target is an
exact read-only component enumerator with total charged
$N^{6/5+o(1)}$ work on a nontrivial strong-Horn/dual-Horn family and
$N^{1/2+o(1)}$ peak live state.  Until that or another all-input acquisition
theorem is supplied, P versus NP remains OPEN.
