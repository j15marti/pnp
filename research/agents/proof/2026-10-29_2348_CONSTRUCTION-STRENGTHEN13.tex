\section{Exact adaptive procedure}
Initialize one module for each clause. At each round enumerate all unordered
module pairs. Scan their scopes and reject pairs which are disjoint or whose
union has more than $w$ variables. For each remaining pair form the merged
clause partition and recompute the entire exact boundary compilation.
This deliberately simple implementation avoids unproved incremental reuse.

The strict policy accepts only candidates with $d'<d$.
The repaired policy accepts $d'\le d$.
Among admissible candidates choose lexicographically smallest
$(d',\text{canonical clause-ID partition})$.
Retain only the current partition, current candidate and best candidate,
discarding all other candidate data. If none is admissible, stop acquisition
and run the signed-affine terminal on the final compilation.
The prototype's scope cap is five for the connected tests.
No search restarts or hidden successful decompositions are used.

\begin{theorem}[Charged termination and correctness]
Let $m$ be the original number of clauses. Each policy performs at most
$m-1$ successful merges, at most $m$ complete rounds and at most
$m\binom m2$ pair scans. Let $C(N,w)$ bound one full source-authenticated
compilation, including exact support enumeration and affine recognition.
Then acquisition time is at most
\[
 O(m^3(C(N,w)+\operatorname{poly}(N))).
\]
A conservative compiler bound is
$C(N,w)=\operatorname{poly}(N)\,m2^{2w}$.
For $w\le c\log_2(N+2)$ with fixed $c$, acquisition time and peak workspace
are polynomial on one deterministic bit machine. This does not imply
that the final terminal runs in polynomial time.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every accepted merge reduces the module count by one; every rejected
candidate is included in the complete round cost. Pair enumeration is
finite and its scope scan has polynomial cost. Each supported module
has at most $2^w$ assignments. Enumerating its exact projected support and
testing constant linear forms by brute force is covered by $2^{2w}$
times polynomial source overhead, as in CONSTRUCTION12.
At any point only a constant number of candidate compilations and
partitions coexist. Each has polynomial encoded size when $w=O(\log N)$,
so candidate storage is polynomial, including source buffers.
Each chosen partition is authenticated and covers every original clause
exactly once. CONSTRUCTION12's projection/gluing argument therefore
preserves SAT and UNSAT at each round and at the final terminal.
Its final $2^d\operatorname{poly}(N')$ replay cost, or coalescing cost
$O((N')^4V)$ with its actual storage, must be added.
\end{proof}
A polynomial number of polynomial-cost discovery steps cannot eliminate
an exponential final solve by itself. If invoked inside WRAPPER, retained
ancestor workspace must also be added. No sharp favorable $(t,s)$ is claimed.

\section{First-move obstruction on the exact target class}
Here strict22 means exactly three distinct variables per clause and exactly
two positive and two negative occurrences per variable, counted with clause-ID
multiplicity. Distinct clauses additionally exclude repeated signed clauses.

\begin{lemma}[No private variables in a module of at most three clauses]
In a proper strict22 formula, every variable appearing in a module of at most
three original clauses also appears outside that module.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
There are four source occurrences of the variable, in four clause IDs because
a proper clause contains it at most once. Three clauses cannot contain all four.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Strict pairwise defect descent cannot start]
For every proper distinct-clause strict22 formula, the strict pair-merge
policy initialized with singleton clauses accepts no merge, irrespective
of its width cap.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Initially every singleton's complete three-variable scope is boundary and its
support has seven assignments, hence is nonaffine and contributes one defect.
Consider two distinct clauses with union scope size $b\in\{3,4,5,6\}$.
No variable becomes private on merging, by the lemma. Thus all other module
boundary relations are unchanged. Each clause excludes $2^{b-3}$ assignments.
Their two excluded cubes intersect in either one assignment, when the
shared literal falsity pins agree, or none. For $b=3$, distinct clauses
give two different excluded assignments. The exact defect of the merged
nonaffine support is therefore as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
b&\text{excluded assignments}&\text{allowed assignments}\\\hline
3&2&6\\
4&3\text{ or }4&13\text{ or }12\\
5&7\text{ or }8&25\text{ or }24\\
6&15&49
\end{array}
\]
None of the allowed cardinalities is a power of two, so none is a nonempty
affine relation. The merged contribution is at least two, which was the
sum of its two singleton contributions. Scope-cap rejection cannot create
a better candidate. Hence total $d$ cannot strictly decrease in the first round.
\end{proof}
This holds even if the algorithm tests disjoint pairs; our implementation
omits those pairs. Duplicate clauses are an explicit exception: merging them
can replace two copies of one forbidden tuple by one.

\begin{lemma}[Small-module affine barrier]
A module of one, two or three proper clauses in a strict22 formula has
no private variables. Its support is nonempty, nonaffine and nonuniversal.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first assertion was proved above. In a scope of $b$ variables each
clause excludes precisely $2^{b-3}$ assignments. At most three clauses
exclude at most $3\cdot2^{b-3}$, leaving at least $5\cdot2^{b-3}>2^{b-1}$.
At least one assignment is excluded. Every proper nonempty affine subset
of $\F_2^b$ has at most $2^{b-1}$ points. This proves the claim.
\end{proof}
Thus at least four original clauses must cooperate before this compiler can
possibly expose a nontrivial affine module or hide an original variable.
This is a source-representation fact, not a minimum-step lower bound for SAT.

\section{Neutral repair and bounded tests}
For the four standard clauses encoding one three-variable XOR, every proper
subset of one, two or three clauses has its original variables on the boundary.
Such a module has exactly its number of clauses as missing assignments.
All partitions using only proper submodules therefore retain $d=4$.
The strict policy stops immediately. Neutral merges form the complete block;
its unshared variables become private and its boundary support is TRUE,
so the repaired policy reaches $d=0$. This example is an isolated strict22
component, not a larger connected-instance guarantee.
Opposite XOR blocks give a separate ordinary-CNF UNSAT control.

\paragraph{Hostile controls and barrier audit.}
The four-clause plateau is exhaustive over all 14 nonempty proper subsets.
The complete mixed XOR control is rejected exactly. Every merge retains
all original source clause IDs and recomputes exact boundary supports;
a source solution cannot be invented by forgetting a shared variable.
CONSTRUCTION12's Horn and dual-Horn exponential coalescence controls remain
applicable, so this policy does not promote $d$ or state count to inherent
hardness. A semantic oracle could bypass the entire representation;
no nonrelativizing, nonalgebrizing, Natural-Proofs, or cryptographic bridge
is claimed. Arbitrary regrouping, variable elimination and symbolic algebra
remain possible alternatives.
