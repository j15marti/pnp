\section{A strictly more permissive numerical contract}
Replace the separate fanout bound of FORMAL-SPINE1 by an aggregate bound.
At a state of encoded size $N$, let $m_1,\ldots,m_q\ge1$ be the full
encoded lengths of the actual child evaluations, counted with multiplicity.
Zero-child direct decisions have $q=0$ and $M(N)=0$.
Require a common $e(N)\to0$ with
\[
 V(N):=\sum_{i=1}^q m_i\le N^{1+e(N)},\qquad
 M(N):=\max_i m_i\le N^{h+e(N)},\qquad 0<h<1.
\]
All local work, including failed routes, source reads, emission and
combination, is $O(N^{1+e(N)})$. Parent suspension and other live local
state use $O(N^{p+e(N)})$ bits for fixed $0\le p<1$, besides one explicit child
buffer of $O(N^{h+e(N)})$ bits. Children run sequentially. Every buffer,
ancestor state and retained answer is charged; live simultaneous use must
satisfy this bound. The constructor has a fixed base case, strict size
decrease above it, and identical uniform contracts on descendants.
Call this $\mathcal V(h,p)$.

Deduplication is legitimate only if exact equality or a sound equivalence
test actually avoids an evaluation, with lookup, proof, storage and replay
charged. Summing only distinct hypothetical nodes while reevaluating them
does not satisfy this definition. Constant-size children may be handled
locally instead, but their total processing still belongs to local work.

\begin{theorem}[Aggregate-volume composition]
If $\mathcal V(h,p)$ holds, one uniform exact recursive decider has
\[
 T(N)\le N^{1+o(1)},\qquad
 S(N)\le N^{\max(p,h)+o(1)}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Correctness and termination follow by induction on size. Fix $\delta>0$,
and choose $\epsilon>0$ with $h+\epsilon<1$, $\epsilon<\delta$ and
$\epsilon(1+\delta)<\delta(1-h)$. Above a fixed threshold $e(N)\le\epsilon$.
For smaller children assume $T(m)\le C m^{1+\delta}$. Then
\[
 \sum_iT(m_i)\le C\sum_i m_i^{1+\delta}
 \le C M(N)^\delta V(N)
 \le C N^{1+h\delta+\epsilon(1+\delta)}.
\]
The exponent is strictly less than $1+\delta$; the local time exponent
$1+\epsilon$ is also smaller. Their sum fits $C N^{1+\delta}$ above a
sufficiently large threshold. Increasing $C$ handles the finitely many
smaller inputs. The algorithm is independent of $\delta$, so this proves
the claimed near-linear bound for one machine.

Put $s=\max(p,h)$. At each level, suspension, current explicit child
buffer and scratch together use $O(N^{s+\epsilon}+\log N)$ bits. Along
the live depth-first path sizes contract by the fixed power $h+\epsilon$,
giving $O(\log\log N)$ levels above a fixed threshold. Charging all live
ancestors gives $O(N^{s+\epsilon}\log\log N)$ space. The bounded-size
bottom contributes only a constant. Arbitrarily small $\epsilon$ gives
the asserted exponent. Child count creates no extra retained array because
the sequential resumability requirement expressly forbids such uncharged
storage.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Relation to the earlier contract]
The scalar fanout/maximum-size bounds in FORMAL-SPINE1 imply the aggregate
volume bound, after replacing the uniform error by twice that error.
The reverse numerical implication does not hold.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The forward implication is $V\le qM\le N^{1+2e(N)}$.
For the reverse, in normalized size units take $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$
children of size one and one child of size $\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$.
For $N\ge4$, volume is at most $N$ and maximum size at most $N^{1/2}$.
Yet $q=\Theta(N)$ violates $q\le N^{1-h+o(1)}$ for every fixed $h>0$.
If valid encodings have minimum size $b>1$, use
$\lfloor N/(2b)\rfloor$ children of size $b$ instead. This is a numerical
contract witness, not an all-input SAT construction.
\end{proof}

Thus heterogeneous children need not be charged as though every child
had the maximum size. This can make an actual assembly easier to certify.
It does not remove the local-time, maximum-size, space or closure gates.
The existing scalar theorem and its elimination of $K$ remain valid.

\section{Hostile controls and limits}
\begin{description}
\item[] \emph{Volume without shrinkage.} One child of size $N-1$ has
volume below $N$. With local work $N$, the recurrence
$T(N)=N+T(N-1)$ is quadratic. A volume ledger alone cannot establish the
conclusion. Its explicit child buffer can also be linear.
\item[] \emph{Volume without streaming.} Storing all children at once
can require $\Theta(N)$ bits even when each has sublinear size. The space
hypothesis is independent and essential.
\item[] \emph{Repeated histories.} A small set of distinct child encodings
does not bound actual invocation volume. Exact retained reuse or charged
recomputation must be recorded.
\item[] \emph{Pure restriction tree.} A correct OR over all assignments to
$r$ variables can have $2^r$ residuals of almost parent size. Correctness
does not establish either the maximum-size or volume condition.
\item[] \emph{Easy families.} Horn, 2-SAT and affine direct solvers may be
zero-child cases when their actual time and workspace meet the declared
contract. Their existence implies no general coverage. Independent
products and renamings do not change this quantifier requirement.
\end{description}

\section{Testing concrete leverage from SAT in P}
Let $D$ be one hypothetical SAT decider with time at most $N^K$.
There is an exact constant-output compressor: run $D(F)$ and return a
fixed satisfiable or unsatisfiable formula according to its answer.
This proves cheap \emph{output length}, not cheap acquisition. The
available construction bound is $O(N^K)$, so it does not meet the
required near-linear local-time hypothesis for arbitrary fixed $K$.
An upper bound $N^K$ is not a runtime lower bound. One may deliberately
delay a supplied decider to make this specific execution expensive, but
that does not defeat an existence-level compiler allowed to choose a
different realization.

SAT self-reducibility similarly gives polynomial witness search from
polynomial decision: fix one variable, ask whether a completion exists,
and continue along a viable branch. Its initial queries may have
parent-scale encoded length. Substituting $D$ establishes polynomial
cost, not the near-linear/sublinear joint bounds here. Cook's notes
provide the standard search-to-decision theorem; no improved quantitative
compiler is imported from it \cite{cooksearch}.

A generic computation-tableau encoding of a $T$-step solver is also not
an automatic shrinker: the standard bound is $O(T\log T)$ output bits,
so a polynomial-time assumption alone supplies no sublinear output bound.
The small-workspace output generator in the Cook reduction does not make
the emitted output or later evaluator free \cite{fortnow}.

\section{Endpoint, open arrow and reconciliation}
Fortnow's matched deterministic consequence excludes simultaneous
$N^{1+o(1)}$ time and $N^{1-\eta}$ space for fixed $\eta>0$
\cite{fortnow}. Therefore the aggregate contract, just like the scalar
contract, is unconditionally impossible on every SAT input on that model.
The possible separation route is the still-OPEN implication
\[
 \mathrm{SAT}\in P\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists h,p\;\mathcal V(h,p).
\]
No constructor satisfying this implication has been established here.
The implication is classically equivalent to $P\ne NP$ once the endpoint
obstruction is granted. Its logical difficulty has not been reduced by
renaming the contract.
