\section{PROVED/EXACT: aggregate terminal absorption}

\begin{theorem}[Terminal absorption with range-restricted heredity]
\label{thm:absorb}
Suppose one uniform exact source-first phase, on an encoded CNF of length
$N$, has charged local time $N^{r+o(1)}$ and retained local state
$N^{p+o(1)}$.  It produces a streaming exact plan with two kinds of leaves.

The unresolved ordinary-SAT leaves have lengths $z_1,\ldots,z_q$ satisfying
\[
  \max_j z_j\le N^{h+o(1)},\qquad
  \sum_j z_j\le N^{v+o(1)},\qquad 0\le h<1.              \tag{1}
\]
Every executed occurrence is counted.  The same phase is defined on every
such unresolved leaf (or charged membership places the leaf in a smaller
promise domain with the same property).

For each member $\tau$ of a fixed finite set of terminal types, the terminal
leaves have lengths $m_{\tau,1},\ldots,m_{\tau,k_\tau}$ satisfying
\[
 \max_i m_{\tau,i}\le N^{g_\tau+o(1)},\qquad
 \sum_i m_{\tau,i}\le N^{w_\tau+o(1)}.                 \tag{2}
\]
The type is source-recognized and has one exact solver taking
$m^{c_\tau+o(1)}$ time and $m^{d_\tau+o(1)}$ work space, where
$c_\tau\ge1$.  Terminal instances are generated, solved, and discarded
sequentially; all replay and ancestor state is included in the local ledger.

Then recursive substitution only at the unresolved ordinary-SAT leaves gives
one exact same-machine realization with
\[
 \boxed{C=\max\!\left\{1,r,
      \max_\tau\bigl[w_\tau+g_\tau(c_\tau-1)\bigr],
      \frac{v-h}{1-h}\right\}},                         \tag{3}
\]
\[
 \boxed{P=\max\!\left\{p,h,
      \max_\tau g_\tau\max(1,d_\tau)\right\}}.        \tag{4}
\]
No recursive-domain or successor-closure premise is required for a terminal
leaf, because its answer is returned locally rather than by wrapper re-entry.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a terminal type $\tau$, monotonicity and $c_\tau\ge1$ give
\[
 \sum_i m_{\tau,i}^{c_\tau}
 \le \left(\max_i m_{\tau,i}\right)^{c_\tau-1}
       \sum_i m_{\tau,i}
 \le N^{w_\tau+g_\tau(c_\tau-1)+o(1)}.                 \tag{5}
\]
Sequential generation and disposal retains one terminal input and one
solver workspace, contributing exponent
$g_\tau\max(1,d_\tau)$ rather than $w_\tau$.  This is the load-bearing
streaming premise: retaining the complete terminal batch would instead
contribute $w_\tau$ space.

For an induction hypothesis $T(z)=z^{C+o(1)}$ on the unresolved calls,
\[
 \sum_j T(z_j)
 \le (\max_j z_j)^{C-1}\sum_j z_j
 \le N^{h(C-1)+v+o(1)}.                                 \tag{6}
\]
The inequality closes precisely when $C\ge v+h(C-1)$, equivalently
$C\ge(v-h)/(1-h)$, together with the local and terminal floors in (3).
Strict encoded-length descent proves termination and strong induction proves
exactness.  Depth-first execution retains one local frame and one explicit
unresolved child per level.  Their encoded lengths power-shrink, so all
ancestor frames and child buffers form geometric exponent sequences and give
(4).  Standard positive-slack and finite-prefix absorption makes the uniform
$o(1)$ statement rigorous.  Time and space belong to the same machine.
\end{proof}

If a terminal solver has sublinear time exponent, a separate bound on the
number of terminal calls is needed; (3) deliberately assumes
$c_\tau\ge1$.  This prevents a hidden child-count term.

\begin{corollary}[Uniform totality already supplies re-entry]
If the fixed phase is proved total with the same bounds on every canonical
CNF, then every emitted ordinary-CNF child is automatically in its recursive
domain.  A separate hereditary certificate is necessary only for a partial
or promise-domain phase.  In that case each unresolved child must carry
charged membership in the next promise domain.  Terminal leaves need neither
form of re-entry.
\end{corollary}

This repairs a repeated overstatement that ``every emitted child needs a
certificate.''  The correct disjunction is: all-CNF totality, or a charged
promise-membership certificate.  All-input coverage and successor heredity
are therefore not two independent final gaps once genuine all-CNF totality
has been established.

\section{Primary-literature check and endpoint}

Nishimura, Ragde, and Szeider prove fixed-parameter detection for strong
Horn and binary-clause backdoors; at fixed backdoor size this supports cheap
syntactic detection, but not an all-input sublinear-space component cap:
\url{https://www.satisfiability.org/SAT04/programme/51.pdf}.
M\"ahlmann, Siebertz, and Vigny introduce recursive backdoors and obtain
tractability from bounded recursive depth; the supplied structure gives
multiplicity-aware aggregate leaf bounds, but its discovery remains a
parameterized premise:
\url{https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2021.73}.
Williams' self-improvement theorem concerns circuit-analysis algorithms and
explicitly discusses the difficulty of further improvement; it is not a
general-CNF compiler from $P=NP$:
\url{https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/stoc-self-improvement.pdf}.

The endpoint remains Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas,
Corollary 5.8, in their deterministic random-access Turing-machine model with
read-only indexed input and charged work/index tapes:
\url{https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/tst.pdf}.  The applicable boundary
is
\[
 \operatorname{NTIME}[n]\not\subseteq
 \operatorname{DTISP}\!\left[n^c,o\!\left(n^{d(c)}\right)\right],
 \qquad
 d(c)=\frac12\left(\frac{c+2}{c^2}-c\right).             \tag{7}
\]
At $c=1.201$, $d(c)\approx0.509108>1/2$.  Therefore a genuine all-input
same-machine SAT algorithm with time $N^{6/5+o(1)}$ and space
$N^{1/2+o(1)}$, after the proved local encoding transfer, would enter the
forbidden region with slack.  The current portfolio does not supply that
algorithm.
