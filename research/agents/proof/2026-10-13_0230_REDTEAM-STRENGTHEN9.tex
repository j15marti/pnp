\section{Target, literature preflight, and model}
The two obstacles are the precise language decided on and off a compiler
image, and closure when a decoder emits a smaller source. Let $\mathcal S$
consist of nonempty ordered proper 3-CNFs with distinct clauses and dense
active identifiers. Clause order and literal order are part of the input;
``canonical'' here means unique bytes for an ordered list, not canonical
under renaming or clause permutation. $U$ is the deterministic transparent
seven-clause balancing plus clone-cycle compiler in
\path{experiments/review/universal22.py}.

The test instantiates $\enc$ as compact ASCII JSON arrays of signed integers,
without spaces or trailing bytes; $N=8|\enc(G)|$ is encoded input bit length.
The proof also works with a polynomial-time parsable order-preserving binary
encoding. It does not apply without a new argument if a serializer first
sorts all clauses or quotients variable names. No succinct formulas, advice,
semantic oracle, or unit-cost large integers are supplied.

D\"ocker proves NP-completeness for unique sign-monochromatic proper
3-clauses with exactly two occurrences of each sign \cite{docker}. This is
a subset of the signed target domain and of $\mathcal S$, after dense
renaming. The promise-problem framework distinguishes correctness on a
domain from rejection outside it \cite{esy}. The exact endpoint is FLMV's
random-access Turing-machine result, not a bound on an unrestricted sum of
time and space exponents \cite{flmv}. These primary sources were searched
and checked before formalizing the candidate.

\section{Independent review of IMAGE22COMP1}
Write $m=|F|_{\rm clauses}$, $g=g(F)\le9m$, and $b=m+7g$.
The source compiler gives
\[
 |U(F)|_{\rm clauses}=12b,\quad v(U(F))=9b,\quad
 \SAT(U(F))=\SAT(F).
\]
Let $I=\{\enc(U(F)):F\in\mathcal S\}$ and
\[
 K_U=\{\enc(U(F)):F\in\mathcal S,\ F\in\SAT\}.
\]
These are different languages. A compiled UNSAT formula is in $I$ and is
not in $K_U$; a satisfiable off-image formula is not in either.

\begin{proposition}[Reviewed completeness and totalization]
Under the compiler's verified semantic hypotheses, $K_U$ is NP-complete.
No polynomial-time image recognizer is needed for this conclusion.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Given target bytes $y$, an NP witness supplies $F$ and an assignment. Check
the proper dense source syntax, regenerate $U(F)$, compare every output
byte to $y$, and check the assignment on $F$. For any successful preimage,
$m\le |U(F)|_{\rm clauses}/12$ and $v(F)\le3m$, whence
\[
 |\enc(F)|=O(m\log(m+2))=O(N\log(N+2)).
\]
Thus witnesses and verification are polynomial in target bits. Different
preimages of the same output cannot disagree in satisfiability, because
both have the output's SAT value. For hardness use D\"ocker's language,
densely renumber a syntactically valid input and apply $U$. To make the
reduction total on arbitrary strings, send a malformed or out-of-domain
source to $U(C_\bot)$, where $C_\bot$ is the eight proper clauses on three
variables, one falsified by each assignment. This is a fixed NO image.
All steps are polynomial.\qedhere
\end{proof}

The reviewed composition law is exactly
\[
 T_{A\circ U}(s)\le O(s^a)+c_A O(s^{qd_A})
       =s^{O(\max\{a,qd_A\})}.
\]
Every fixed $d_A$ remains polynomial. It is not eliminated. This verifies
the two peer claims as \textbf{PROVED/EXACT} within the stated compiler and
encoding hypotheses. It is ordinary Karp transfer, not a separation.

\section{CANDIDATE: exact ordered-image inversion}
For the exact output order of \texttt{compile22}, the first $b$ clauses are
the cloned balanced source, the next $R=3b/2$ clauses are padded directed
cycle edges, and the final $19R/3$ clauses are $Q$ copies. Clones are numbered
consecutively in increasing balanced-variable order. Every cycle has length
at least two. The $j$th edge, indexed from zero, is
\[
 (-(j+1),v_j,R+15\lfloor j/3\rfloor+p_{j\bmod3}),
 \qquad (p_0,p_1,p_2)=(1,7,13).
\]
Within a cycle $v_j=j+2$; on its last edge it is the first clone of the cycle.

\begin{theorem}[ORDERED-IMAGE22-INVERSE1]
There is one uniform deterministic polynomial-time algorithm $D$ on all
byte strings such that $D(y)=\bot$ exactly when $y\notin I$; otherwise it
returns some $F\in\mathcal S$ with $\enc(U(F))=y$. No SAT query is made.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Reject invalid noncanonical syntax, noninteger literals, nonternary clauses,
and identifiers exceeding three times the target clause count. A genuine
image never violates these conditions. From target clause count $M$ require
$12\mid M$, put $b=M/12$, require $b$ even, and set $R=3b/2$.

Read the declared edge block. Require each first literal and padding port
to equal the displayed values. Track the start of the current consecutive
cycle; each second literal must either advance to the next clone or close
the current cycle of length at least two. Reject unterminated cycles. This
recovers the clone-to-balanced-variable map in its original order. Decode
the first $b$ clauses through this map, rejecting out-of-range clone IDs.
The result is a candidate balanced source $B$.

For every $1\le t\le b$, take the first $t$ clauses of $B$. If they form an
eligible source $F_t$, regenerate $U(F_t)$ and compare its complete canonical
bytes with $y$. Return the first exact match; otherwise return $\bot$.
Every accepted string is an image by the final equality, even if earlier
syntactic checks could admit a forged gadget. Conversely, for $y=\enc(U(F))$
the genuine cycle partition is recovered, $B=B(F)$, and its prefix with
$t=m$ is exactly $F$. That attempt succeeds. No uniqueness assumption is
needed.

There are at most $b\le N$ attempts. Each decoded candidate has at most $b$
clauses and identifiers at most $R$. The compiler adds at most $9b$ gadgets
and produces $O(b)$ clauses with $O(\log(N+2))$-bit identifiers. Validating,
regenerating and fully comparing each candidate has polynomial bit cost;
the loop therefore has polynomial total cost. All attempts terminate.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Charged implementation.}
The input parse, cycle map, decoded prefix, candidate compiler structures,
regenerated output and equality scan are charged. Sequential attempts allow
discarding each failed output; they do not make it free. A direct deterministic
array/sorting implementation uses $N^{O(1)}$ time and
$O(N\log(N+2))$ writable bits. The Python prototype likewise materializes
these objects and does not claim sublinear space. In particular the counter
of comparison bytes is a work proxy, not a complete Python bit-operation
measurement. There is no recursive child execution in the recognizer.

\paragraph{Useful consequence and explicit scope.}
For this ordered encoding, $I\in P$ and
\[
 K_U=I\cap\SAT.
\]
A total general-SAT decider can decide $K_U$ after the charged image test;
using a SAT decider alone on off-image strings can give a wrong answer for
$K_U$. For example the one-clause formula $(x_1\lor x_2\lor x_3)$ is SAT
but is not in $I$, because its clause count is not a multiple of 12.
On the promised image, $D$ recovers a source with the same answer.
This is a useful syntactic reduction. It is not a polynomial SAT solver.

\section{Smallest source counterexample to recursive closure}
Take $F=(x_1\lor x_2\lor x_3)$. Each of its three variables requires three
balancing gadgets, so $g=9$, $b=64$, and $U(F)$ has 576 variables and 768
clauses. Decoding recovers $F$. But $F\notin I$, as just shown. A second
decoder invocation rejects. This is the smallest nonempty proper source in
clause count; no claim is made about smallest target byte count.

Consequently
\[
 y\in I\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D(y)\in\mathcal S
 \qquad\not\Longrightarrow\qquad D(y)\in I.
\]
The candidate ``inverse decoding automatically supplies recursive PRE-A
coverage'' is \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}. Wrapping the decoded child back
through $U$ returns the exact parent bytes and gives a nonshrinking cycle.
Calling an assumed $s^K$ solver on the recovered source retains its exponent.
The source is linearly related to the target clause count within constants;
this does not establish a maximum-child bound $N^{1/2+o(1)}$.
Constant-factor descent remains legitimate under a suitable full recurrence,
but this decoder alone supplies neither recursive closure nor the joint
resource bound. Construction10, Recovery10 and conditional $K$ elimination
are preserved.

\section{Endpoint, model and barrier audit}
FLMV Section 2.1 charges index and work tapes on one random-access Turing
machine; only original input and write-only output are exempt. Corollary 5.8
states, for constants $c>1$,
\[
 \operatorname{NTIME}[n]\not\subseteq
 \operatorname{DTISP}[n^c,o(n^{d(c)})],\qquad
 d(c)=\tfrac12\bigl((c+2)/c^2-c\bigr).
\]
At $c=6/5$, $d(c)=23/45$ and $d(c)-1/2=1/90$. To absorb $o(1)$ errors and
polylogarithmic transfer overhead, choose a slightly larger fixed $c'>6/5$
with $d(c')>1/2$. Theorem 3.1 supplies a locally computable quasilinear
reduction to its SAT encoding. It does not say every polynomial Karp
reduction preserves a joint near-linear-time, half-space bound.
The present materialized image decoder does not meet that endpoint contract.

The main theorem transfers only deterministic parsing, regeneration and
byte equality. Compiler inversion is a useful transfer from program
verification: recovered source and recompiled output have an exact round-trip
specification. No circuit, VLSI, communication or proof-system lower bound
is transferred. The image recognizer is expressly an algorithmic exploitation
of algebraic/combinatorial structure, not evidence of hardness. PC/PCR and
Resolution size retain their own declared representation models.

The algorithms and the closure counterexample relativize; no oracle answer
is needed. Algebraic-oracle access supplies no separation hinge. No large
constructive circuit property is defined, so no Natural-Proofs evasion is
claimed. COPY, DEAD, DELAY and RECOMP may increase costs but cannot force a
solver to use the decoder. Exact comparison replaces hash equality. Hash or
random-oracle binding is a \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} to unconditional hardness.
Schur squares, quadratic ranks and syzygies do not address this interface and
are \textbf{FAILED TRANSFERS} here. LWE-style black-box extraction remains
\textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}; no cryptographic hypothesis is used. Collision
entropy is an \textbf{EXACT OBJECT/THEOREM TRANSFER} only for a specified map
and supplies no runtime bound for this decoder.
