## Proof of exact indistinguishability

For every proper subset J of the k switches, parity(S) is fair and independent of S_J. Pair each fixed assignment to S_J with completions differing at one missing switch; the two parity values occur equally often. Consequently the joint law of (S_J,Y) equals that of (S_J,Ytilde), including every bit of their 3N-bit output strings.

Consider the ancestor cone of the single output of a circuit with g noninput gates, each of indegree at most two. If it has t distinct primary input nodes, its underlying connected graph has at least g+t−1 edges, and at most 2g edges entering gates. Hence t≤g+1, after discarding unused gates; a direct input output is the g=0 case. NOT gates, constants and shared subgraphs cannot increase this bound. Parallel uses of the same input do not create a new independent input node.

If the cone contains no supplied-count bits, both experiments have identical X marginal, so advantage is zero. Otherwise, for g<k it has at most k primary nodes, at least one of which is an output-string bit. It consequently refers to at most k−1 encoded input positions, which involve at most k−1 switches. Its entire view has the identical joint law just proved. It follows that for every such deterministic circuit D,
|Pr[D(X,Y)=1]−Pr[D(X,Ytilde)=1]|=0.
Independent randomization or a mixture over such circuits also preserves zero advantage. This proof assumes no restriction on depth or sharing, and does not infer hardness from the number of distinct inputs.

## Matching distinguishing circuit

D(X,z)=parity(X[a_1],...,X[a_k], z[ℓ_k]) XOR parity(v_1,...,v_k).
This outputs 1 on every true pair and on exactly half the fake pairs. Its advantage is 1/2, equal to the total variation distance between the full joint distributions. Each x has true probability concentrated on one of the two counts, while the fake splits it evenly, which proves that no test can exceed this advantage.

Thus k gates are sufficient in the class of arbitrary two-input Boolean functions, matching the strict lower threshold.
