## 1. Exact clique saturation theorem

Use the existing exact phase-conflict compiler: satisfiable 2-CNF B, ternary owner groups, at-most-one conditions in B, SCC-equivalent feasible phase literals merged. Graph G has vertex weights w_v equal to number of owners containing its SCC representative, each owner O_i is a clique of size at most 3 (if empty, reject); h owners. An independent set of weight h is equivalent to selecting exactly one from each O_i, and pairwise compatibility extends to B by existential 2-CNF projection. Retain representatives and group witness maps. This theorem uses the pre-twin-contraction owner graph; arbitrary weighted module replacement need not preserve explicit owner rows.

Let C be an explicitly enumerated catalogue of cliques of fixed maximum size k >=3. Let lambda_C >=0, sum_C lambda_C=h, and sum_{C contains v}lambda_C >= w_v for every v. Such a cover exists using one copy of each owner. For every independent I of weight h,
h = sum_{v in I}w_v <= sum_C lambda_C |I intersect C| <= sum_C lambda_C=h.
Therefore every C with lambda_C>0 is an exact-one constraint on I, and a strictly overcovered vertex cannot belong to I. This is an exact equality-face certificate, not an approximation or assumed integer LP optimum.

Acquire a maximal support certificate by maximizing each coordinate lambda_C over the rational feasible cover polytope, retaining every positive result (or averaging the rational witnesses). With k fixed, O(n^k) columns and polynomial rational encoding, exact LP solves and bit complexity are polynomial. The support can be larger than the original owner family. Equivalent and potentially stronger direct LP: x>=0, owner sums Ax=1, enumerated clique sums Dx<=1. Minimize c_C^T x. If the min is positive, any integer feasible set must hit C (its clique sum is 0 or 1); no need for min=1. An exact dual certificate y free, z<=0, A^T y+D^T z<=c_C, 1^T y+1^T z>0 suffices. Similarly min x_v>0 forces x_v=1; max x_v<1 forces x_v=0. Certify infeasibility via rational Farkas, not floating status. The local Python audit used the conservative min>=1 criterion only; the strict-positive/rounding strengthening is proved, not included in those counts.

## 2. Finite phase grouping by saturated triangles

Build adjacency on certified mandatory triangles when they share two variables. A connected component of this triangle graph has at most three Boolean exact-one words: pick one of the three root vertices, propagate through a spanning tree, reject inconsistencies and check every mandatory triangle and internal conflict edge. A component with no word proves UNSAT. If a single component covers a connected conflict atom, test all three words against all original owners and graph edges, lift accepted words through B and original region witnesses. This is an exact two-label terminal, not merely a SAT-only 3-color certificate. When several regions remain, represent each by its <=3 words, keep equalities for shared vertex selections and all external binary conflicts; keep uncovered phase vertices Boolean and unsatisfied owner constraints explicit. Compile these tables back into 2-CNF plus ternary groups, retaining source witness maps. This transfer is exact even if region size is unbounded, but no universal decrease in group count follows. One pass is polynomial; repeated passes require a strict charged progress measure or a polynomial cap returning UNKNOWN rather than false UNSAT. One may also enumerate r residual region phases at 3^r cost if the remaining constraints become 2-CNF.

This differs from earlier geometric face grouping: the extra triangles can be non-facial, not members of the source formula, and discovered using global owner conservation even when no variable is individually forced. It is complementary to clique-separator, modular and footprint decompositions: solve a whole prime atom at its target weight, not all induced MWIS subproblems. Under pins recompute the guarded LP/owner rows or preserve the exact original guard; never reuse an unguarded cover for a changed target without proof.

## 3. Infinite authenticated source family: square medial construction

Let J be a simple 3-connected cubic bipartite plane graph. Its dual is an Eulerian triangulation. For the explicit family, take T_L as the triangular-grid subdivision of each face of the octahedron, side length L (integer barycentric lattice on |x|+|y|+|z|=L); J=T_L^*. Then |V(J)|=8L^2, |E(J)|=12L^2. The primal vertex color (|x|+2|y|) mod 3 is proper; color each edge of J by the missing color of its two corresponding primal endpoints. At every J vertex the three edge colors are distinct. For e=uv in J let s_u(e),s_v(e) be clockwise successors at its two endpoints. The successor edges lie on opposite sides of e, and their two missing colors differ; hence e,s_u(e),s_v(e) form a rainbow triple. Same property for predecessors.

Define directed medial arcs e->s_u(e), e->s_v(e). For every arc i=(e,f) introduce variable X_i and clause C_i = {X_i} union {X_j: j leaves f}. Each X_i occurs in C_i and the two clauses indexed by arcs entering e. Partition C_i by color(e); predecessor rainbow proves exactly one occurrence per partition. Distinct variables have distinct signatures: same-tail variables differ in own clause; different-tail variables have two occurrences in distinct square groups. Number of variables/clauses = 2|E(J)| =24L^2. Planarity: L(J) is embedded as a medial graph; at each vertex its two incoming and two outgoing ports alternate. Replace each by a square with two outgoing-variable vertices and two incoming-clause vertices, alternating, and connect own-clause incidence edges along original medial arcs. This realizes the formula.

3-connectivity: L(J) is 4-edge-connected. Any edge cut across a partition of E(J) has two crossing adjacencies for each mixed J vertex; at least two vertices are mixed, since otherwise deleting the single mixed vertex leaves J connected, contradicting a nonempty other edge class. Delete <=2 vertices of the square-expanded graph in different squares: remaining squares are connected, at most two external medial edges deleted, so the whole graph is connected. If both deleted in one square, all other squares connect via L(J)-e (J-e connected), and surviving vertices of the damaged square attach by intact external edges. Thus source incidence 3-connected. Each square's two incoming clauses share the two outgoing variables, forcing its two incoming arc variables equal. Write their common value z_e. The entire formula is bijective to
z_e + z_{s_u(e)} + z_{s_v(e)} = 1, for all e in E(J).
Lift X_(e,f)=z_f. The natural residual conflict graph G_J has vertex E(J); each displayed triple is an owner. It contains L(J), since every pair of incident edges appears together in a successor triple; extra adjacencies join successors of the same edge. Each edge appears in three owner rows, so w=3 and h=|E(J)|. This is the square equality quotient; no need to assume a hidden planted solution.

At each J vertex its three incident edges form a clique of G_J. These endpoint triangles give a second tight clique cover: each weight 3 vertex belongs to two endpoint triangles, each assigned lambda=3/2; total 3|V(J)|/2=|E(J)|=h. Thus every endpoint triangle is mandatory. Equivalently sum of owner equations gives sum z=|E(J)|/3=|V(J)|/2; line-graph independence makes a matching of this size necessarily perfect. This is a conservation proof of saturation, independently of LP computation.

Every mixed owner triangle shares an edge with the endpoint triangles at both ends of e. The mandatory triangle adjacency is therefore connected across J (deduplicate coincident rows if necessary). The phase propagation theorem gives <=3 words. The three missing-color classes each satisfy every rainbow owner, so there are EXACTLY THREE z words and, by square lift, exactly three original satisfying assignments. This is a polynomial source terminal for the entire family, even without supplying T_L or its coloring: generic polynomial clique LP + propagation recognizes it. Direct geometric construction is a faster specialized route; no arbitrary source classification asserted.

## 4. Why this is a genuine prime-atom obstruction to earlier group bounds

All 3-cycles of T_L are facial: neighboring vertices lie in a common signed octant, so three pairwise adjacent vertices lie in one octant (consistent nonzero coordinate signs). In the triangular grid each 3-cycle is elementary. Thus J has no nontrivial 3-edge bond: a bond in the plane dual corresponds to a cycle of T_L; length 3 bounds one face, isolating a single J vertex. J is 3-connected by polyhedral duality. G_J is 3-colorable, clique size <=3. Deleting any <=3 edge vertices leaves at most one edge-containing component of J; otherwise a nontrivial <=3 edge cut with both sides containing edges would contradict the preceding bond property. Thus L(J)-S, hence G_J-S, is connected for every clique S. G_J is a clique-separator atom, not a collection of bounded-size atoms.

Every vertex of G_J has its four line-graph neighbors and at least one disjoint mixed-triangle neighbor: choose its predecessor p at an endpoint and the successor at the opposite endpoint of p; bipartiteness excludes a triangle connecting it back to the original edge. There are at most two extra neighbors, so 5<=degree<=6. In the proper 3-coloring, at least three neighbors have one other color, forming an induced claw. This claw persists under any sound pairwise-conflict learning or removal of impossible vertices because its leaves occur jointly in a satisfying word and its center occurs in another. In bounded degree, greedily packing anticomplete claws eliminates at most 4(Delta+1)^2 potential original centers per chosen claw. Hence their packing size grows linearly, excluding any universal fixed number of anticomplete claws or logarithmic claw-deletion bound for this representation.

For L>=2, G_J is also modularly prime. In a connected graph of maximum degree 6, a proper module M has size <=6: an outside neighbor sees every member. Each optimum color class has |E(J)|/3>=16 vertices. If M meets two color classes, each nonempty intersection must be a maximum independent set of G_J[M], else replacing it would improve the optimum; both have equal size, and outside selections in the two words see none of M. Swapping these intersections yields a fourth optimum containing two colors, contrary to the exact three-word theorem. Thus a proper nonsingleton module must be independent and monochromatic, so contains false twins. Suppose distinct disjoint edges e,f were false twins. Of four line-graph neighbors of e, at least two must meet f (f has at most two extra neighbors). Bipartiteness forces these to be exactly the two cross connectors of a 4-cycle with e,f opposite. Every 4-cycle in cubic 3-edge-connected bipartite plane J is facial: if both sides had vertices, at least three edges would attach on each side, but there are only four free ports and no chord. Write e=uv, f=st, u,s on one bipartition side. The connectors are ut,sv. The two external edges a=uw,b=vz of e must be extra neighbors of f. For a the only possible intermediary edge is ut, because sw would yield a K2,3 with a nontrivial 3-edge cut, forbidden by cyclic 4-edge connectivity; likewise sv is the sole intermediary for b. Thus f must be the clockwise successor of both connectors at its endpoints, impossible for two neighboring boundary edges on the same facial 4-cycle (one precedes and the other succeeds f in consistent rotations). No false twins. L=1 prime checked computationally. Therefore neither clique nor modular decomposition separates these exact phase-conflict atoms, even though the saturated grouping terminal solves them.

Unbounded quantitative obstruction: a triangular octahedron patch contains q by q rhombi (q=floor(L/2)); contract each pair of triangles in J to obtain a q by q grid minor. So tw(J)>=q up to harmless additive constants. A decomposition of L(J) yields one of J by replacing every edge vertex by both endpoints; incident edges are a clique, and their bag subtrees intersect. Thus tw(J)<=2 tw(L(J))+1; G_J contains L(J), giving tw(G_J)=Omega(L). Every bag in a degree-six graph has an independent subset of at least bag size/7, so tree-independence number is Omega(L). In a subcubic branch decomposition with max crossing-edge count b, a tree decomposition with bags consisting of all endpoints of crossing edges of the three incident cuts has width <=6b+O(1). Therefore every branch decomposition has a cut with Omega(L) crossing edges. Greedily choose a matching induced in the FULL graph across that cut: each edge selected eliminates at most 2 Delta(Delta+1) crossing edges incident to its endpoints' closed neighborhoods. Both sides' chosen endpoints are independent, giving Omega(L) independent neighborhood generators and at least 2^{Omega(L)} distinct independent-set footprints in BOTH directions. This defeats raw fixed-K or polynomial footprint decomposition, including variants measuring induced matching on one side, but does not limit other representations or unrestricted algorithms.
