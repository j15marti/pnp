## Lemma and constructive proof
Let B={0,5,6,9,10,15} in four-bit notation, bit 0 least significant, x0+x1=x2+x3. For every nonempty explicit T subset B, a graph with four marked ports realizes T by perfect matchability after deleting ports set to 1 iff T satisfies even symmetric exchange: for each a,b in T and differing bit i there exists a DIFFERENT differing bit j with a xor 2^i xor 2^j in T. Necessity follows from alternating paths pairing differing ports in the symmetric difference of matchings for a,b. Flip the path incident to i.

For sufficiency twist by t in T, U=T xor t containing 0. The signs of the balance equation change at the bits of t, leaving two positive and two negative coefficients since t is balanced. Denote their two-element sides A,D. U contains only 0, cross pairs, optionally 15.
* If 15 is present, exchange between 0 and 15 shows every port belongs to an allowed cross pair. A bipartite graph on 2+2 vertices with no isolated vertex has a perfect matching. On four port vertices put an edge for the complement of each allowed pair. It has the desired signature: removal of a pair succeeds iff allowed, removal of none succeeds by complementary pairs, removal of all succeeds trivially.
* If 15 absent but pairs present, allowed cross pairs form a complete bipartite graph on nonisolated vertices. If two disjoint pairs are allowed, exchange at a bit of the second pair not in the first forces the unique cross swap (adding both gives forbidden 15, same-side pairs forbidden); both cross swaps are forced. Any missing cross edge between nonisolated vertices would have disjoint witness edges, contradiction. Add two internal vertices u,v with no edge between them. Connect u to the other port on side A for every eligible A-index and v to the other port on side D for every eligible D-index. Add one edge joining any allowed pair. After removal of two ports the internal vertices must match remaining ports, giving exactly the cross product. Empty removal succeeds by matching the chosen port edge and the remaining ports to u,v. All-four removal fails.
* If U={0}, attach one private leaf to each port; only zero removal succeeds.
Undo twist at each bit of t by adding a fresh leaf to old port and designating leaf as new port. Its presence forces the old port to be matched to it; deleting it releases the old port. Thus signature is exactly T. At most 12 vertices; constant recognition/construction. 36 of the 64 submasks are nonempty admitted, 27 nonempty rejected, one empty. Full MATCHING4 admitted. {0,15} rejected. This specializes known delta-matroid theory, not a new general dichotomy.
