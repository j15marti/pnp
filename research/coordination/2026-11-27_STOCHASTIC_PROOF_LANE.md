## 3. Probabilistic theorem target and proof standard
Let Phi(S) be a nonnegative integer credit, bounded initially by P(N)=poly(N), on the entire live state and all required branches; require every admissible trial to leave Phi nonincreasing and every certified productive trial to decrease Phi by at least one or give a checked terminal. Count terminal as success. Establish or falsify the conditional progress hypothesis: on EVERY reachable unresolved NO state, conditional on the entire adaptive history, the trial succeeds with probability at least p(N)>=1/poly(N), and costs at most B(N)=poly(N) bit operations including all failed work and verification. No independence assumption. If established, waiting time per progress step has tail <=(1-p)^t and expected value <=1/p. Thus at most P(N)/p(N) expected trials (or use max(1,P(N)) as bound including terminal), and with P'=max(1,P(N)) and 0<delta<1, R=ceil(ln(P'/delta)/p) trials per progress stage and at most P' stages suffice with failure probability <=delta by union bound, PROVIDED no unresolved zero-credit state exists and all reached states satisfy the hypothesis. Use a computable rational lower bound on p and a charged integer upper bound on R. These are conditional randomized guarantees, not a universal theorem already proved. In a research run a timeout is UNKNOWN; if a uniform all-NO guarantee were proved, accepting YES on timeout would give one-sided error for SAT (coRP), with deterministic verification of every rejection. It would not by itself give P=NP. Expected-time sound rejection on NO alone does not imply ZPP or a complete Las Vegas decision algorithm. Finite success rates and favorable seeds do not prove a uniform probability floor.

If candidate pool is polynomial and a productive candidate exists, deterministic full enumeration already supplies a polynomial acquisition bound; random sampling may improve constants or ordering but not resolve the existence gap. For exponentially large pools prove a uniform inverse-polynomial productive mass, an efficiently samplable distribution, and paid verification; nonzero probability or existence alone is insufficient. Seek derandomization by a uniformly constructible polynomial-size hitting family, computable pessimistic estimator or conditional expectations with explicit hypotheses. Record if no deterministic bridge is available. For P!=NP, distributional/restricted lower bounds do not establish unrestricted separation.
