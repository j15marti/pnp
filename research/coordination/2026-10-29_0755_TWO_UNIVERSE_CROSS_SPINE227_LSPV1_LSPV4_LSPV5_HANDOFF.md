## Priority result

The all-input closed polynomial-time single-child physical SAT contraction is equivalent to P=NP (prior ARC207), whereas one-shot compression on an NP-complete padded image is compatible with both conditional universes (prior ONE-SHOT-PARITY-PADDING226). Neither observation resolves P versus NP.

Prior RACE77 contains a sharp but conditional theorem: for any one fixed correct total deterministic SAT decider A, **either** a uniform polynomial upper bound on its SAT-side runtime or a uniform polynomial upper bound on its UNSAT-side runtime would imply P=NP by the opposite-label timeout default. Under P!=NP, both T_+ and T_- have infinitely often superpolynomial worst cases for every A, but no particular exponent or simultaneous time-space tradeoff follows. Do not attribute the cutoff theorem to the present run.

Under P!=NP, uniform all-input exact table discovery for b=0 is impossible because it is SAT.
- A decreasing rank of polynomial *bit length* has no polynomial iteration bound. The integer rank value (not its encoding length) must be polynomial if one counts one unit decrease per stage.

## Separation assumption switches

- Native random-access SAT with simultaneous N^{o(1)} work space and time exponent below 2 cos(pi/7) = 1.8019377358 is excluded unconditionally (Williams), regardless of the P=NP universe.
- If conversion costs N^c, virtual child size N^alpha, child solver M^d, and per-step virtual-bit accessor N^u, conservative time exponent is max(c,alpha*d+u). FLMV/Williams applies only if *the same correct native solver* also satisfies its full space bound.
- Materialized child workspace costs at least its physical writable size; virtual output is not free and must pay for all indices, parsing, source reads and caches. One-tape crossing limits are not automatically indexed-RAM bounds.
- P=NP allows an arbitrary unknown polynomial K; neither PH collapse nor P-immunity of superpolynomial-time-bounded random strings forces K into a forbidden small-space region. A fixed polynomial Kolmogorov time bound t=n^e yields a coNP bounded-randomness predicate; under P=NP a polynomial search exponent K(e) may exceed e. Hirahara's unconditional P-immunity theorem instead concerns superpolynomial time bounds.
