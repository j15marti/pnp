## 1. Previous Lipschitz work retained at its actual scope

The October Lipschitz audit established the following guards.

1. **Raw semantic mass fails.** One compact XOR gate can change half of a truth table, so exponentially many changed semantic coordinates do not imply many computational steps.
2. **Log mass collapses.** Taking logarithms repairs the local scale but reduces total mass to only linear size.
3. **Source entropy is capped by source length.** A deterministic object derived from an `L`-bit input contains at most `L` bits of Shannon information from that input.
4. **Description length is not computation cost.** Exponentially long semantic tables can have short descriptions.
5. **A large prescribed DAG is not an arbitrary-function lower bound.** A long identity DAG can compute the identity function.
6. **Minimum remaining complexity is Lipschitz.** Adding one primitive operation can reduce minimum remaining cost by at most the cost of that operation, but a large initial lower bound is then the real theorem.
7. **Sharing requires a fusion-safe measure.** Node occupancy, leaf count, and tree unfolding are invalid when a DAG can share and reconverge.
8. **Separate per-scale lower bounds cannot simply be multiplied.** A valid multiscale argument needs one global charge that controls joint discharge across all scales.

## 3. Exact mathematical target

Let a complete concrete configuration be

$$
C=(F,A,G,P,\Gamma,\mathcal D,\mathcal I,\mathcal O),
$$

where, as applicable:

- `F` is the exact authenticated input;
- `A` is the fixed machine/program or explicit calculus authority;
- `G` is the complete execution or shared proof DAG;
- `P` is the globally paid-identity/history ledger;
- `\Gamma` contains active and discharged assumptions;
- `\mathcal D` contains definitions, affine facts, and derived objects;
- `\mathcal I` contains private/exported interfaces and source provenance;
- `\mathcal O` contains unresolved AND obligations, branches, joins, or terminal conditions.

Every actual resource must be represented or conservatively overapproximated. Unknown operations are MAY behavior, not silently illegal.

### 3.1 Zero-cost closure

Let

$$
\widehat C=\operatorname{cl}_0(C)
$$

be saturation under every authentic zero-cost transition. Collapse zero-cost strongly connected components before assigning a positive lower-bound potential. A proposed potential is immediately falsified if a legal zero-cost path decreases it.

### 3.2 Reduced-product obstruction vector

Construct a vector

$$
\mathbf p(\widehat C)=
\bigl(
 p_{\mathrm{CNF}},
 p_{\oplus},
 p_{\mathrm{sep}},
 p_{\mathrm{fus}},
 p_{\Gamma},
 p_{\mathrm{int}},
 p_{\mathrm{machine}}
\bigr).
$$

Candidate components include:

- residual CNF or near-q-Horn/backdoor obstruction;
- affine obstruction after full Gaussian-elimination closure;
- inequivalent residual relations across separators;
- fusion/intersection obstruction that survives shared DAG reuse;
- unresolved assumption/discharge obligations;
- private/exported interface incompatibility;
- actual machine access, retention, retrieval, recomputation, or transition obligation.

A component is admissible only after quotienting the known cheap mechanisms that defeat it. Mandatory controls include affine/Tseitin, Horn, dual-Horn, Krom/2-SAT, bounded-treewidth dynamic programming, autarkies, equality clouds, compact circuits, extension variables, learning, preprocessing, sharing, renaming, padding, auxiliary variables, input rereading, caching, and recomputation.

### 3.3 Rule tariff matrix

For every authentic legal transition `r:C -> C'` with nonnegative cost `w(r)`, define

$$
\Delta_r(C)=\mathbf p(\widehat C)-\mathbf p(\widehat {C'}).
$$

Seek nonnegative dual weights `lambda` such that

$$
\boxed{\lambda^\top\Delta_r(C)\le w(r)}
$$

for **every** legal transition and every applicable state.

Then

$$
\Phi(C)=\lambda^\top\mathbf p(\widehat C)
$$

satisfies

$$
\Phi(C)\le w(r)+\Phi(C').
$$

Every completed accepting or refuting goal must satisfy `Phi(goal) <= 0`. All-step cost-underestimating simulation and telescoping then give

$$
\Phi(C_0)\le W(C_0),
$$

where `W` is actual machine work or minimum whole-DAG paid work in the exact qualified system. Shared paid identities are counted once. AND branches and discharge obligations are all required. Free optimal proof or execution-path selection remains allowed.

The tariff matrix must be published in a machine-readable format. Once finite candidate discharge bounds are available, solve the dual-weight feasibility problem with an LP or exact rational solver. A feasible solution is a candidate potential certificate. Infeasibility or a violated row must produce a concrete legal shortcut or a precise missing component, not be hidden.

### 3.4 Multiscale initial gap

For overlapping scales `j`, define obstruction families `\mathcal F_j` and weights `a_j`:

$$
M(C)=\sum_j a_j\lvert\mathcal F_j(C)\rvert.
$$

For a legal operation `r`, let `d_j(r)` be the independently justified scale-`j` discharge. Prove one global inequality

$$
\sum_j a_j d_j(r)\le w(r)
$$

or its vector-tariff equivalent. Do not multiply separately obtained stage bounds.

The lower-bound target is one explicit family `F_n` of encoded lengths `N_n -> infinity` for which

$$
\forall k\ \forall n_0\ \exists n\ge n_0:
\quad
\Phi(C_{0,n})>N_n^k.
$$

Strict quasipolynomial growth `N^{(\log N)^c}`, `c>0`, is superpolynomial and would suffice for this lower-bound premise. An upper bound of that form does not settle P versus NP.
