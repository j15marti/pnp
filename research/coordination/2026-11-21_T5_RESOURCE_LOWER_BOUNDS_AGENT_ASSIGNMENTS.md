## Common mathematical requirements

Use a fixed correct solver and a fixed proposed cap T(N) <= K N^a, with K and a independent of the input. Compare all demands using the same encoded length N, machine model, realization, and resource convention.

The handoff's cap ledger, under its declared deterministic model and input-charge conventions, is:

| Quantity | Ceiling implied by the fixed polynomial cap |
|---|---|
| Peak physical storage S | O(N^a) |
| Physical cumulative storage CM | O(N^(2a)) |
| Minimum-description cumulative information CM_min | O(N^(a+1)) |
| Encoded-input count-map circuit size C_N | O(N^a log N) |

Maintain physical storage and descriptive information as distinct notions. State where T >= N or an input-reading convention is needed. A lower bound must exceed the corresponding ceiling on the same input regime. Padding changes N. Excluding one cap exponent is not a proof against every fixed polynomial exponent.

The matching-family transport and cumulative bounds in the handoff are model-specific baselines. Parameterized branching, DNNF, DLDD, monotone arithmetic, specified DAG and other restricted lower bounds retain their restrictions. No transfer to arbitrary scalar computation is licensed without a separate theorem.

For entropy, name the random variables, source distribution, side information, actual decoder, capacity and reuse bound. Do not treat a virtual residual query as a mandatory machine event. For circuits, target the map from encoded instances to their exact count or decision output; distinguish it from a fixed-instance witness predicate or hardwired scalar answer. Any indistinguishability hypothesis must be independently proved or explicitly conditional.

1. Exact target lemma and quantifiers.
2. Model, encoding, fixed cap, and resource definitions.
3. New result or legal counterexample.
4. Status and independent-review status.
5. Consequence for a named dependency in the overall program.
6. One next concrete task and intended receiving role.
