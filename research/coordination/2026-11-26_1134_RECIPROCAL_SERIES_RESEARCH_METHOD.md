## Research protocol for each substantive quantitative theorem
1. Identify a positive sequence f(n) that actually expresses the theorem's quantitative content: worst-case work in original encoded bit length, required/attainable interface states, residual core size, rank, paid progress, defect, number of guarded calls, or a scoped lower bound. State the exact source family, hypotheses, quantifiers, units, parameter n, and whether f is exact, an upper bound, a lower bound, or an observed statistic. A logical theorem does not literally have a reciprocal; if no natural scalar exists, say not applicable. Separate zero/infinite values rather than divide by zero.
2. Analyze sum_(n>=1) 1/f(n), and its partial sum/tail, including whether it converges or diverges and why. Where useful analyze the weighted reciprocal family S_p=sum_(n>=1) n^p/f(n) for nonnegative integer p; use log factors, ratio/root/comparison/integral/condensation tests under their hypotheses. For monotone positive summands a_n, Cauchy condensation compares sum a_n with sum 2^j a_(2^j); preserve multiplicity when sampling exponential scales. A convergent unweighted reciprocal does not imply a superpolynomial bound, and a divergent reciprocal does not imply a uniform polynomial algorithm.
3. On recursive/cooperative schemes also analyze the sum of ACTUAL paid work (normalized by a fixed polynomial potential), not only its reciprocal. Retain every branch multiplicity, failed route, acquisition, verification, cache and witness cost; separate time, peak space and cumulative memory. Establish exact guard and witness compatibility. A formal convergent series must have a uniform finite-input truncation and a sound exact decision/lift. Distinguish progress of effective residual from raw padded size.
4. Try a constructive and a contradiction interpretation of the result on the SAME authenticated residual/guard, then check SAT and UNSAT neighbors, affine, matching and padding controls. Report whether the series yields a rigorous bound, a restricted obstruction, a diagnostic only, or no useful implication. Do not turn finite partial sums into an asymptotic proof. Preserve original source IDs, hypotheses and quantifiers. Use existing issue #327 and owned handoffs to communicate concrete derivations and counterexamples.

## Two exact mathematical anchors
A. Let f(n)>0 finite for each n. All the series S_p converge for every nonnegative integer p if and only if f(n)/n^p tends to infinity for every fixed nonnegative integer p (eventual superpolynomial growth).
Proof: convergence of S_p forces its nonnegative terms n^p/f(n) to tend to zero. Conversely eventual superpolynomial growth implies, for each fixed p, eventually f(n)>=n^(p+2), so comparison with sum 1/n^2 proves convergence. No monotonicity needed. The quantifier over ALL p is essential. For f(n)=Theta(n^a), S_p converges iff p<a-1 (with the equality boundary harmonic); for f(n)=2^n, all S_p converge. The unweighted series converges for BOTH n^2 and 2^n, while f(n)=n gives harmonic divergence even though its cost is polynomial. A failure of eventual superpolynomial growth permits infinitely many polynomially bounded sizes but not necessarily one polynomial upper bound valid at all sizes.
For a specified uniform correct decider D, define f_D(n)=max(1,max running time over encoded inputs of length at most n). If D has a fixed polynomial bound f_D(n)<=C n^a eventually, S_p diverges for every integer p>=a. To obtain P!=NP from the all-p convergence criterion would require establishing it for EVERY uniform correct decider of one authenticated NP-complete decision language. That is a sufficient and stronger-than-necessary target (P!=NP rules out a global polynomial cap, not necessarily eventual superpolynomial growth). An exponentially large representation, an upper bound for an exponential solver, a counting lower bound, or a single decider's deliberately wasted time does not imply that quantifier. Lower/upper bound polarity matters: f>=L yields 1/f<=1/L; f<=U yields 1/f>=1/U.

B. For a finite exact paid computation tree with acquired integer measure m>0 and fixed c>0, let Phi(v)=m(v)^c. If for every internal v, sum_(new paid children u) Phi(u)<=q Phi(v) for a common fixed 0<=q<1, and each vertex's complete charged work <=P(N) Phi(v) with fixed polynomial P in ORIGINAL input length, then depth-j total potential <=q^j Phi(root). Thus total work <=P(N) Phi(root)/(1-q), by geometric summation. For a DAG charge each new evaluation once and pay all requests, checks, sharing and reconstruction in the local budget; otherwise use the expanded tree. This proves a scoped full-run budget, NOT existence or coverage of accepted contractions. q=1 may still be polynomial if depth is polynomial, and q>1 may still be polynomial if depth is O(log N) with fixed q. Divergence of an infinite majorant does not by itself prove superpolynomial finite-input work. For a single successor with m'<=rho*m+C*m^alpha, fixed rho<1 and alpha<1, above fixed threshold m'<=((1+rho)/2)m; the finite exact kernel plus geometric budget is valid under paid uniform availability. See C02-05 report: https://github.com/j15marti/pnp/blob/7830943fdd4dc117ace7c75bbf32154b7350ca3e/research/proof-spine-2026-11-26/closure-cooperative-contraction/2026-11-26_1124_C02_05/report.md . Its exact native padding result also shows why shrinking an arbitrary core to M^epsilon alone does not establish tractability.
