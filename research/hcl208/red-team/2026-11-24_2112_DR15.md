## Exact reductions and scope
DA15 excludes rank-mass progress by every raw one-bit affine branch under its specified closure. A tempting repair is to canonically identify all semantically equivalent residuals, or identify complete projected relations by compact support summaries. DR15 rules out treating these tasks as already proved polynomial subroutines.

Take arbitrary nonempty-clause 3-CNF F(x); write B=not(s and t), G=B or F(x). Explicit G packets are source packets with two fresh independent equations s=t=1. Both charts have a known zero witness, full affine hull, and every packet rank >=2 (at most five). Singleton closure starts and stagnates without any deduction. G and B agree on every assignment iff F UNSAT. Any source model distinguishes them at selector 11. This proves coNP-complete explicit semantic equality under those restrictions (membership: differing assignment). A polynomial-time complete semantic key would decide SAT. The same argument with H=not t or F(x), rank <=4, shows a specified coordinate being forced is coNP-complete even with known SAT witness and stagnant local closure. Complete affine closure therefore cannot be assumed free (BD14's paid support queries essential).

For an arbitrary quantified 3-CNF forall u exists v F(u,v), make G(u,v,s,t) as above and project out v. The projected relation is B or exists v F(u,v). It is the full exterior cube iff the quantified input is true. This proves Pi2^P-complete projected fullness, and hence equality of two implicit projected relations (the other may be the full cube), under the same restrictions. Membership for equality: two containments forall u,v exists w [not R1(u,v) or R2(u,w)] with private variables renamed apart, combined in one universal-existential block. Both full hulls do not imply equal support. A universal polynomial-size, polynomial-verifiable certificate system for true projected equivalence would imply Pi2^P subset NP and PH collapse; this is not an impossibility proof. None of these claims requires that the general cooperative SAT solver actually ask those questions. G and H are trivially SAT, so these are deliberately NOT simultaneous hard decision inputs. The reductions are unconditional complexity classifications; neither P=NP nor P!=NP follows.
