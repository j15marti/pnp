## 2. Theorem A (PROVED): polynomial recognition of a common basis for binary planes
Let U_1,...,U_m be explicit two-dimensional subspaces of a finite GF(2) vector space. After identifying duplicate planes (keep all source predicate IDs and RHSs), each plane has exactly three nonzero vectors. Let D_i contain the nonzero vectors of U_i appearing in any OTHER distinct plane.

A common ambient basis simultaneously adapted to all planes exists iff:
(1) |D_i| <= 2 for every i;
(2) choose all D_i in each plane and arbitrarily complete to two distinct nonzero vectors; the union B of these choices is independent.
The arbitrary choices in (2) do not affect acceptance.

Proof: if the planes are coordinate subspaces of a basis, two distinct planes meet only in zero or a line spanned by a common basis vector. Over GF(2) that line has one nonzero vector, which MUST occur in the basis. Thus every shared vector is mandatory. Three shared vectors in one plane are dependent. When all |D_i|<=2, complete each to two vectors. All optional selected vectors occur in just one distinct plane. Hence the total number of distinct selected vectors is |union D_i|+sum_i(2-|D_i|), independent of choices. Their span is the sum of the planes, also independent of choices. An independent union exists iff this count equals the rank of that sum. If so every completion works, and Gaussian elimination extends B to an ambient basis. Conversely any adapted basis restricts to such choices, so the criterion is necessary. Empty family accepted; duplicate constraints must not be deleted merely because their observation planes agree.

Recognition uses sorting/hashing with exact comparison of 3m vectors, then Gaussian elimination; deterministic polynomial bit time even without hashing. After consistent affine guard Lx=b, use canonical quotient forms modulo rowspan L; evaluating any form differs from its canonical representative by a known RHS constant. For each surviving rank-two forbidden affine word, change basis within its plane (invertible 2x2 map), carry constants, and forbid one assignment of the two chosen independent observations: an ordinary 2-clause. Solve by implication SCC and lift by Gaussian elimination of the guard plus selected coordinate values. Independent quotient forms ensure every assigned coordinate word lifts. Check the original source constraints and reconstruct private rims by BW03's path DP when source provenance exists. Thus the BW03 sufficient terminal is now constructively recognizable for explicit rank-two guards, not merely existential. Not a criterion for arbitrary existential reductions to 2-SAT or mixed higher-rank guard families.

## 3. Theorem B (PROVED): exact unguarded boundary
For simple graph G=(V,E), c_e=e_u+e_v and U_e=c_e tensor GF(2)^2. Distinct edge planes intersect trivially. Their combined span has rank 2(|V|-components). A common basis must choose two disjoint vectors per plane, so 2m must equal that rank. This happens iff G is a forest. The converse follows from direct independence of forest edge columns. In particular triangles and arbitrarily long cycles fail recognition despite easy coloring. Signs of forbidden words do not change the criterion.

## 4. Theorem C (PROVED): feedback vertex set certificate from a successful guard
Let K be any linear guard space of rank r (possibly adaptively selected; consistent RHS fixed). Run DA03's exact local affine closure: when a plane intersects accumulated equation span L, its allowed fiber in that affine domain is empty or affine; reject empty fiber, otherwise add its equations, delete the represented edge and repeat immediately. Apply root closure before branching; retain full guard/RHS/source context. Let D be removed edges, t=rank{c_e:e in D}, E'=E-D. In nonrejected closure L is consistent and all E' projected planes have rank two.

Independent audit of DA03: W=span of removed columns, L subset K+(W tensor GF(2)^2). A new independent removed column consumes at least one dimension of the image of K modulo W tensor GF(2)^2: its hit vector belongs to K+W tensor GF(2)^2 but not to W tensor GF(2)^2. Enlarging W by that column reduces remaining image rank by at least one. Initially it is r. Thus t<=r and dim L<=dim(K+W tensor GF(2)^2)<=r+t<=2r.

Assume all E' projected planes are adapted to one basis B of the quotient. Take any spanning forest T of G'=(V,E'). The original T planes form a direct sum of rank 2|T|. Each projected T plane uses exactly two B coordinates. Let s=2|T|-|union of these coordinates|. This is exactly the rank loss caused by quotienting their span by L, hence 0<=s<=dim L.

Mark a T edge bad if either of its two coordinates is used by another T edge. If a coordinate has multiplicity k>=2, its k incidences satisfy k<=2(k-1). Summing over repeated coordinates shows the number of bad edges <=2s. Every good T edge has two coordinates unique among T planes.

For any nonforest edge e=uv in E', its original incidence column is the sum along the unique T path from u to v. The projected plane of e is the image of the two-bit vector h mapped to the sum of the projected vectors for that same h on each path edge. For each GOOD path edge, projection onto its two unique coordinates is an invertible two-dimensional map (the edge plane has rank two). No other path edge contributes there. An aligned plane uses precisely two basis coordinates, so the path can contain at most ONE good T edge. Contract the bad forest edges. Every original edge of G' is now a loop or joins adjacent vertices of the contracted forest. Pick one original vertex per contracted component; deleting all others removes exactly the number of bad forest edges. The induced graph on the representatives is acyclic because it injects into a forest and the source graph is simple.

For D, choose all but one vertex in each connected component of the removed-edge graph. This is a vertex cover of D of size t. Add it to the preceding deletion set. What remains is an induced forest in ORIGINAL G. Therefore, writing tau_fvs for minimum feedback vertex set size,
  tau_fvs(G) <= t+2s <= t+2 dim L <= 2r+3t <= 5r.
In particular every nonrejected guard that reaches BW03 common-basis 2-SAT via this closure must have r >= tau_fvs(G)/5. The proof is constructive: the accepted basis gives a polynomially verifiable feedback vertex set witness of that size. It makes no claim of optimality of constant 5. Other affine equations established by nonlocal certified methods may be included in initial K, but their rank AND acquisition costs must be paid; nonlinear changes of factor geometry are outside the theorem. Early closure rejections require no alignment and are NOT subject to this bound.

For connected d-regular graphs d>=3, removing k vertices deletes at most dk edges, while a nonempty forest on n-k vertices has at most n-k-1 edges. Thus tau_fvs >= (n(d/2-1)+1)/(d-1), giving linear guard rank for successful raw alignment (and if all vertices deleted the bound is trivial). This is a structural parameter lower bound, not a lower bound on SAT computation.

## 5. Simultaneous restricted neighboring families (PROVED symbolically)
For any fixed g choose DO03's fixed simple 5-critical graph H of girth >=g; H is >=4-edge-connected and H-e is >=3-edge-connected and 4-colorable. Form a chain of k copies of H-e glued at single vertices (use distinct left/right glue vertices in internal blocks). Replace one block by H to create UNSAT G_k; deleting the designated edge restores SAT G'_k. A proper coloring of each SAT block can be relabeled to agree on each shared vertex in sequence. The graphs are simple, connected, bounded degree <=2 Delta(H), girth >=g and edge connectivity >=3 (any nontrivial cut splits a connected block). Alternate blocks are vertex-disjoint, each has a cycle (minimum degree >=3), so tau_fvs >= floor(k/2) on BOTH labels. Size is linear in k for fixed H. Thus every nonrejected successful alignment requires linear rank on these raw sources, simultaneously with DO03's pair-fiber/period and small-projection stagnation. The SAT neighbor has a surviving branch, so a method that insists on rank O(log N) local-closure-plus-alignment on every successful branch cannot cover it.

This is deliberately an EASY FAMILY control: articulation decomposition reduces each block to a fixed-size problem, with polynomial discovery and reconstruction, and fixed high-girth gadgets may be hardcoded. It demonstrates the failure of universal parameter smallness, NOT simultaneous hardness against all terminals. No uniform construction or size bound for growing g is assumed. No claim of edge-criticality of the entire chain. DO03's Fourier projection threshold remains dependent on g, not n. The high-girth chain was not executed in this run.

## 6. Aggregate branching and diagonal audit
A direct fixed-rank guard enumeration visits 2^r assignments if it actually enumerates all of them; it is not a lower bound on adaptive branching or shared coverage. For an individual graph, choose an initial guard pinning the first color bit of any vertex v, whose form is outside the span of edge difference observations (all have even coordinate parity). Both children have identical raw rank-two observation geometry, same remaining factors, same feedback vertex set number; no edge is hit by this guard. Their feasibility labels agree under global first-bit translation. A potential based solely on remaining edges or feedback vertex number cannot satisfy sum of both positive child credits <= parent credit-1 on this legal split for any fixed exponent c. Even if free source dimension is included additively, for large n the ratio approaches two. Exact source-aware gauge sharing can avoid duplicate decision work: retain lift translation and guard IDs, charge attempted edges and canonicalization. This refutes a universal ALL-branches contraction rule, not the existence of a better chosen rule.

A fixed exact portfolio A that lacks any uniform polynomial bound has arbitrarily long inputs exceeding N^j for every fixed j (otherwise finite exceptions give a polynomial bound). Select increasing lengths F_j with T_A(F_j)>|F_j|^j. Only fixed registered channels with explicit fair/capped simulation and polynomial discovery/verification overhead are excluded eventually on this sequence for each fixed time exponent. Per-input existence of a different hardcoded fast decider, unknown representation, or uninvoked branch is not evidence of a polynomial channel or its failure. Even if P=NP, A might be inefficient. The new obstruction removes one proposed coverage lemma from a fixed-portfolio proof; it does not establish the antecedent of no polynomial bound for A, still less for every SAT algorithm.
