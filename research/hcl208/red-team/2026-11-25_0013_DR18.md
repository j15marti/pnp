\section{Unified mixed-radix credit and guarded row-probe audit (DR18)}\label{sec:dr18}
The explicit-table output barriers of DO18/BS17 and the incompatible row/term counts of GD24/DA18 require an aggregate invariant, not an arbitrary switch of progress measures. We give a common reserve for the certified S23 and GD24 dispatches, without assuming that a dispatch is always available.

\begin{lemma}[Mixed-radix lifting of aggregate credit]\label{lem:dr18-credit}
Fix the original input length $N$. Suppose the base integer credit $q(s)\ge1$ is polynomially bounded in $N$ and a secondary integer measure satisfies $0\le h(s)\le B(N)$ for a single fixed polynomial integer cap $B$. Accept exact moves only of the following kinds: (I) a list of child occurrences with $\sum_i q(s_i)\le q(s)-1$; (II) one child with $q(s_1)=q(s)$ and $h(s_1)<h(s)$. All moves must meet the same uniform size, discovery, verification and lift requirements as Theorem~\ref{thm:aggregate}. Then a single polynomial integer credit for both kinds, with exponent one, is
\[
W(s)=(B+1)(q(s)-1)+h(s)+1.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $L=B+1$. For any state, $L(q-1)+1\le W\le Lq$. Hence for type I, $\sum_i W(s_i)\le L\sum_i q(s_i)\le L(q(s)-1)\le W(s)-1$, regardless of the number of children or their secondary measures. For type II, the base term cancels and the integer secondary measure drops by at least one. This counts duplicate occurrences, not only distinct DAG keys. All terminal and failed-attempt costs are still charged by the aggregate theorem. The argument is valid only for actual preflight-bounded charts and exact typed moves; it does not supply an eligible move or prove a small representation exists.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Instantiation and representation discipline.}
Take $q=(1+r)^4$, where $r$ is the dimension of the declared observation span in actual free coordinates after guard absorption. Keep declared observation scopes for unchanged owners (do not alternately expand a representation and count the smaller semantic rank for free). Take $h$ to be the number of owners plus the total number of registered accepted rows (or one registered forbidden word for a packet). On S23's allowed rank-credit dispatch each recursive child and the continuation have the proved strict aggregate $q$ contraction; on a GD24 coordinate-table semijoin or allowed pair merge, declared span cannot increase and either $q$ decreases or $h$ strictly decreases. DA18's local point-ideal compiler and Gaussian cancellations are scratch computations: commit only certified row deletions or guard quotients that satisfy the same credit test, not a growing equivalent polynomial registry as an uncharged new active state. For exact coordinate or affine quotient moves, preserve the actual guard and coordinate maps; no new free variable or scope-increasing encoding is implicitly permitted. Other terminals need their own adapter and cost proof. A cap can be fixed independently of success for this restricted mixed representation: initialize each 3-CNF owner by at most seven accepted rows, or one forbidden word. Owner count never increases along a path in the specified S23/GD24 operations; each S23 continuation adds at most $(N+2)^4$ interface words (direct small-span enumeration may use a larger fixed power). Guard restriction does not increase row count. At most $N$ strict rank drops occur on a path; same-rank GD24 moves do not increase rows. For example, with direct enumeration cap $(N+2)^C$, $B=10(N+2)^{\max(4,C)+2}$ bounds these owner-plus-row counts. Arbitrary representation conversion, table materialization, or an added terminal not meeting these assumptions requires a separately proved cap or must decline. Uniform polynomial bit work and storage for the admitted operations, including all failed scans, tables, guard/source maps and lift consumers, remain necessary. The theorem combines two previously separate credits but is conditional on coverage; U-PROGRESS is not resolved.

\begin{lemma}[Paid conditional row rejection]\label{lem:dr18-probe}
For an explicit coordinate-table chart of polynomial encoded size, fix one registered row of one owner as a temporary guard and compute certified pair-semijoin and local-affine-hull closure. If closure proves contradiction, that row can be deleted unconditionally relative to the original chart guard. Repeat closure after deletion. A full round with no successful row rejection certifies only stagnation of this particular rule, not global feasibility. A fixed explicit chart admits polynomial total work for repeated full row-probe scans (or a smaller fixed cap).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every global solution using the selected row survives the temporary guard and every sound closure operation. Empty support or inconsistent necessary affine equations thus exclude that row. A nonempty guarded fixed point supplies no converse. Discard other conditional deletions unless independently justified. Each successful probe permanently deletes at least one of the initially registered rows and reduces $h$ (or the base span after certified quotient). Between successful probes there are at most the initial number of rows to test, so at most quadratic many probes in that count. Each semijoin/affine round is polynomial in the bounded explicit chart, and any strict round deletes a row. Guarded hull equations are computed from actual surviving rows and projected by Gaussian elimination onto each owner scope; all query work is scratch overhead, not free recursive decisions. Reclose the original chart after accepted deletion and before fallback. This is relational singleton consistency strengthened by affine-hull propagation, not complete semantic closure.
\end{proof}
