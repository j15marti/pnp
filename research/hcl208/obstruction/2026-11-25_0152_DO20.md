\section{DO20: Bounded-pin forest saturation and a consistent local adversary}\label{sec:do20}
This addresses Obligation~\ref{obl:progress}, BH19's forest gates (Lemma~\ref{lem:bh19-gate}) and RT25's bounded-pin obstruction. In the raw signed NAE-3 language on actual variables, even the union of all acyclic regional pin consumers can be redundant. The scope is not arbitrary transformed charts or affine guards.

\begin{lemma}[Small seed closure is a forest]\label{lem:do20-closure}
Let the 3-uniform owner hypergraph have Berge girth $g>k$. For a seed set $S$ of at most $k$ actual variables, start with isolated seeds and repeatedly add any not-yet-added owner meeting the current variable set in at least two vertices, including all its vertices. Denote the resulting variable and owner sets by $V_S,E_S$. They are independent of the order, form an incidence forest, and for nonempty $S$ satisfy $|E_S|\le |S|-1$, $|V_S|\le2|S|-1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The monotone finite closure is the least fixed point. Before an addition, each existing component is a tree. If two touched vertices are in the same component, the new owner closes a Berge cycle of length at most the number of previously added owners plus one. Otherwise the addition merges at least two components and creates at most one new variable. Inductively, before any addition there are at most $|S|-1$ previously added owners, so the cycle case contradicts $g>k$. Each addition decreases the number of components at least once, hence the bounds. Empty seeds give empty closure. Track owners even when an addition introduces no new variable.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Exact saturation of arbitrary forest pin consumers]\label{lem:do20-forest}
For any actual pin word on $S$, $|S|\le k<g$, the following are equivalent: (i) the word extends to a solution on $E_S$; (ii) exhaustive certified coordinate forced-bit closure on the original raw registry does not reject; (iii) every acyclic subregion of the original registry admits an extension of the word. Subregions may have unbounded size; no enumeration of them is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every owner outside $E_S$ touches at most one variable in $V_S$. Given a satisfying assignment on $V_S$, fix it, discard already satisfied owners of any forest subregion, and remove fixed vertices from the remaining incidence graph. Every remaining owner has at least two free variables, and the graph is still a forest. Root each component at a free variable, choose its bit, then descend: each owner has a free child, which can be chosen to prevent all three signed bits from agreeing. This extends the word throughout the subregion. Conversely the closure itself is an acyclic subregion. For (ii), a forced-bit step requires two equal signed fixed bits in an owner, and every such step stays inside $V_S$ by its definition. A contradictory all-fixed owner is also inside $E_S$. On the closure forest, at a nonrejecting fixed point all remaining unsatisfied owners have at least two free variables, so the same rooted construction yields a solution. Thus coordinate closure decides the closure query exactly. This is not a claim of global extendability. An actual pin word may be supplied by flags, but all pins and owners must be authenticated raw coordinates, not an unproved adapter.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Simultaneously stable bounded-view family]\label{lem:do20-views}
Let $P_S$ be the projection onto $S$ of the solutions on $E_S$. For $S\subseteq T$, $|T|\le k$, one has $\pi_S P_T=P_S$. Every $P_S$ is nonempty. If $x\notin V_S$ and $|S|<k$, both values of $x$ extend each word in $P_S$ to $P_{S\cup\{x\}}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Closure is monotone, so restriction is sound. Choose a witness on $V_S$ for any word in $P_S$. The larger closure is a forest, and every new owner touches at most one old variable. The preceding rooted construction extends the entire old witness; when a specific new free variable is requested, root its free component there and choose either value. Starting with empty seeds proves nonemptiness. This family satisfies every raw owner whose three variables lie in the view, since such an owner belongs to the closure. Every forest projection of a view contains its $P_S$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:do20-forest}.
\end{proof}
Consequently, arbitrary repetition of bounded-view relational semijoins, restrictions and projection consistency on at most $k$ actual variables, even seeded by all forest-gate projections on those views, cannot reject these raw registries. The $P_S$ give a common nonempty invariant under all such operations. For owner-row scopes of at most $r$ owners use $k\ge3r$ and transport by the actual variable-to-row map; do not infer that every compatible tuple survives. Nor do these sets supply consistent fractional, integer or PSD higher moments: that requires another proof.

\begin{lemma}[Restricted direct-search cap obstruction]\label{lem:do20-cap}
On an UNSAT raw registry of girth $g>k$, a finite binary actual-bit decision tree whose rejecting leaves are certified only by acyclic subregion infeasibility under the branch pins must contain at least $2^k$ distinct nodes. This remains true with forced-bit propagation used as a rejection test.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Maintain an adversary witness on the closure of a seed set. At a query inside that closure follow the value of its witness; no new seed is needed. At a query outside, retain both branches and extend the witness separately by Lemma~\ref{lem:do20-views}, adding that query to the seeds. Until $k$ external choices have been made, every queried bit agrees with the witness and no forest consumer can reject: the extension argument applies to the whole witnessed closure, hence also to any subset of its pins. A finite sound UNSAT tree must therefore reach $k$ external choices on each retained path. There are $2^k$ distinct retained nodes at the $k$th external choice. This is a tree bound; sharing, general learning, arbitrary affine queries and nonlocal terminals are outside it.
\end{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:rt25-neighbors}, for every fixed $k$ there are minimally UNSAT positive registries of girth greater than $k$ and single-sign SAT neighbors on the same triples. All survive the bounded-view invariant, including every local owner row for $k\ge3$. For a fixed direct cap $B$, choosing $k$ with $2^k>B$ excludes the specified tree method. This does not prove that the actual complete portfolio fails or even that BH19's transformed chart search fails. It is not an explicit efficient generator or an unrestricted time lower bound. In particular $k=O(\log n)$ yields only a polynomial tree bound.

\paragraph{Charged constructive consequence.}
On the eligible raw domain, use a single coordinate-closure query instead of enumerating, acquiring and evaluating forest consumers for bounded actual-pin tests. This preserves their exact collective rejection power; it does not remove a forest gate's global symbolic role under other guards or many pins. Source parsing, girth eligibility (BFS in the incidence graph with cycle length measured in owners), closure membership and forced-bit scans, conditional pin identity, witness reconstruction for a requested forest and every consumer access are polynomial. For fixed $k$, explicit bounded-view tables cost at most $O(n^k2^k)$ entries and their acquisition and all tuple accesses are paid. The forest closure uses at most $2k-1$ variables; enumeration is unnecessary for a single pin decision. For growing $k$, table storage is not uniformly polynomial. No reset of original input credit, no universal aggregate contraction. Serial fixed-scope implementation has polynomial $T,S$ and $\mathrm{CM}\le(T+1)S$ on the same execution.
