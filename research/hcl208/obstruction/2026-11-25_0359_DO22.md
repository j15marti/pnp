## Exact theorem (PROVED, scoped)
Let E(s)=C+sum_{i<j} J_ij s_i s_j, J_ij in {-1,+1}, n spins; no fields, no guard. In every nongauge affine direction, the exact minimized energy, expressed as a Walsh polynomial on the direction quotient (optionally also quotienting global complement), has at least 2^(c sqrt(n)) - 1 - binom(n,2) nonzero coefficients for an absolute c>0 and all sufficiently large n. Uniform over all signs and all directions. In particular it defeats every fixed original-input polynomial explicit-sparse materialization cap on signed projective Steiner NAE charts, including the SAME adjacent S27 pair; not a statement about arbitrary post-cut charts or implicit circuits.

Proof. A direction splits the actual coordinates into S,T, 1<=p=|S|<=q=|T|. The odd part B is the signed crossing bilinear sum. Fix spins in S and write B=sum_{j in T} w_j s_j, w_j=sum_{i in S} J_ij s_i. Under independent uniform S spins, each w_j is a sum of p independent Rademachers, E[w_j^2]=p, E[w_j^4]<=3p^2. By Cauchy-Schwarz on {w_j^2>=p/2}, P(w_j^2>=p/2)>=1/12. Markov gives P(w_j^2>24p)<=1/24. Thus expected number with sqrt(p/2)<=|w_j|<=sqrt(24p) is >=q/24. Hoeffding (or product cosh moment bound) and union bound show that max |w_j|>L=sqrt(2p ln(96q)) with probability <=1/48. Subtracting at most q/48 from the expected count proves the existence of one S word with max <=L and at least q/48 middle weights. Pigeonhole their positive integer magnitudes: there is a group of t>=q/[48(floor(sqrt(24p))+1)] equal absolute weights w>=sqrt(p/2). Thus t=Omega(sqrt(n)) uniformly.

Gauge-flip signs within T to make group weights positive. Fix the other T spins greedily opposing the accumulated sum; residual c0 satisfies |c0|<=L. Pick f>=max(1,ceil(L/w)) with f congruent to t-1 mod 2 and minimal with these properties. Then f<=2 sqrt(ln(96q))+2. For sufficiently large n, l=t-f is odd and Omega(sqrt(n)); fix f group spins to make residual c1 satisfy |c1|<=w (the attainable shifts range from -fw to +fw by 2w). One T coordinate is fixed, as is all of S, so the remaining l-dimensional slice embeds in the joint direction/global-complement quotient. On this slice |B|=w|sum_{i=1}^l z_i+c1/w|. Its even symmetrization under z->-z is w|sum z_i| since the sum is odd and |c1/w|<=1. Restriction and symmetrization cannot increase Walsh support.

For odd l>=3 every even coefficient of |sum z_i| is nonzero. Constant is positive. For a nonempty even set of size 2j, differentiate in two of its variables in the Walsh sense; the derivative is (1/2) 1_{|sum remaining|=1}. For l-2=2h+1, on the h-negative layer the sum of all characters of size 2j-2 equals coefficient [u^(2j-2)](1-u)^h(1+u)^(h+1)=(-1)^(j-1) binom(h,j-1), nonzero. The complementary layer contributes the same, and symmetry makes every coefficient of that degree equal. Thus at least 2^(l-1) coefficients of |B| survive before restriction. A=(E(s)+E(s xor direction))/2 is quadratic before affine quotient and has <=1+binom(n,2) characters after quotient. E_min=A-|B| cancels at most that many.
