## 1. Model and exact local criterion

Let G=(V,E) be a finite loopless signed multigraph, with independent full-list colors x_v in F2^2. Each edge e=uv excludes x_u+x_v=s_e, where s_e is a fixed two-bit translation. Write d_e=e_u+e_v in F2^V, and U_e=span{(d_e,0),(0,d_e)} in the two-channel dual chart. Arbitrary consistent affine guard Hx=h has rowspace H (notation overloaded). Its observed edge rank is dim U_e - dim(U_e intersect H). A rank-two residual edge rejects one of four possible words and accepts the other three, hence its accepting set is nonaffine. Rank <=1 gives an affine accepting set or contradiction. Therefore H linearizes every original local edge iff H intersects every U_e nontrivially. This remains true for any consistent right-hand side, even if a factor is deleted because its forbidden word is impossible. It is NOT necessary for the global conjunction to be affine, nor for an alternate existential reduction or a terminal to decide the instance. If preexisting guards, affine signs, or shared external identifications change U_e, the independent-vertex theorem must be reverified in that chart.

After fixing a basis of such H, every surviving edge contributes at most one necessary affine equality, or is tautological or contradictory. Gaussian closure and a representative lift give exact decision and exact count within the original chart. Both SAT directions hold since no original guard or edge is dropped without equivalence.

## 2. Minimum rank and cycle rigidity (DA12-A)

Choose a nonzero row r_e=d_e tensor l_e in H intersect U_e, l_e in {01,10,11}. For any edge subset X write r(X)=|V|-c(V,X) (ordinary incidence rank, including isolated vertices) and R(X)=rank{r_e:e in X}. Forest rows are independent by leaf elimination: a leaf coordinate has a single nonzero l_e. Hence R(X)>=r(X), regardless of chosen directions. The all-hit minimum is precisely r(E): take a uniform character on every edge and use a spanning forest. This proves lower bound for arbitrary dense H, not just coordinate pins.

Let e(X)=R(X)-r(X). It is nonnegative and monotone under edge insertion: if incidence rank increases, the new edge joins distinct old connected components and its row cannot belong to their span (sum color-vector coordinates within one old component is zero for all old rows and nonzero for the new row). Both ranks increase by one. If incidence rank stays fixed, R cannot decrease. Thus e(X)<=e(E).

For a simple cycle, including a two-edge parallel cycle, any proper subset is a forest. Its only possible row dependency uses every edge once, and the XOR at each vertex is zero iff its two adjacent characters agree. A mixed-character cycle has excess 1. Consequently e(E)=0 implies every cycle is monochromatic. Conversely if every cycle is monochromatic, each nonforest edge row equals the XOR of the rows on its fundamental forest path, so e(E)=0. Equivalently the character is constant on each cycle-matroid component, and bridges may choose independently. For any minimum-dimensional all-hit H the selected rows span H; their labels necessarily obey this rigidity. Each U_e intersects H in exactly a line: choose a spanning forest containing e, whose selected rows form a basis of H; a combination supported only on endpoints of e must use only that forest edge by leaf elimination. No second character is possible. Verification and a minimum uniform construction are polynomial (forest plus Gaussian rank).

Falsifiers considered: mixed triangle with rank2 (impossible); nonuniform bridge path (allowed); dense guard below forest rank (impossible); arbitrary prior chart with identified colors (outside hypotheses).

## 3. Exact excess formula and cut tradeoff (DA12-B)

For each direction j in {1,2,3}, let A_j be the ordinary incidence rowspace of edges assigned j. The selected rowspace is (A_1,0)+(0,A_2)+{(a,a):a in A_3}. Its rank is
R(E)=dim A_1+dim A_2+dim A_3-dim(A_1 intersect A_2 intersect A_3).
Proof: first two summands are direct, and intersection with the diagonal third summand is exactly the common triple space. Same formula for every edge subset.

For any edge partition X,Y, define lambda(X)=r(X)+r(Y)-r(E), lambda_l(X)=R(X)+R(Y)-R(E). Direct substitution gives
lambda_l(X)=lambda(X)+e(X)+e(Y)-e(E)>=lambda(X)-e(E).
For any all-hit H of dimension h, e(E)<=h-r(E), therefore
**h+lambda_l(X) >= r(E)+lambda(X)**.
For any full binary edge decomposition the maximum cut obeys the same bound, so minimizing over all decomposition trees preserves the inequality with optimal graphic width. At minimum h=r(E), all subset excesses are zero and lambda_l=lambda on every cut. The original two-channel observation cut is 2 lambda. This is an exact rank tradeoff on the chosen guard observation registry, NOT a lower bound on residual width after conditioning, not a bound on a compressed or nonlinear representation, and not an identification with the matching-specific unstable rank without a verified adapter. Mixed character choices can reduce guard interface width only by paying extra dimension in this representation. It is possible to avoid the whole guard registry by DA11/BW11 projection or alternative terminals.

## 4. Partial guard selection firewall (DA12-C)

For a rank budget h, maximize the number of original simple graph edges whose U_e intersects H. For any candidate H, hit-edge subgraph S has incidence rank <=h by the forest argument. Conversely taking uniform character on a spanning forest of S gives a space of rank r(S) hitting every edge internal to its connected components. Thus optimum equals the maximum number of edges internal to a vertex partition with sum_j(|C_j|-1)<=h. This is a purely graphic optimization, no dense guard improves it.

In unrestricted SIMPLE graphs, for 1<=h<n, hitting at least binom(h+1,2) distinct edges with rank <=h iff G has a clique on h+1 vertices. Indeed a component with k_i=|C_i|-1 has at most k_i(k_i+1)/2 internal edges; the sum is <=h(h+1)/2, with equality only when all positive budget lies in one component, whose graph must be complete. Conversely choose uniform character on the clique. Thus partial maximum-coverage guard acquisition is NP-complete (verify H by rank tests) even though minimum all-hit guard is easy. This reduction does not apply to maximum degree four with growing h; no hardness transfer to strict physical E4/B4. Symbolic only, not tested in the checker.

## 5. Monotone/signed NAE analogue on the common benchmark (DA12-D)

For a triple owner abc with one rejected relative word (monotone NAE rejects 00), its U=span{d_ab,d_ac}; the three nonzero rows are the three pair differences. A rank-one all-hit guard is exactly a span hitting these planes. Choose one pair per triple; the rank of these selected rows is n-c(selected-pair graph). Conversely a uniform span of selected rows is a valid guard. Exact optimum therefore equals the minimum graphic rank of a pair choice per owner, or n minus the maximum number of partition classes subject to every triple having a same-class pair. This is not a polynomial discovery claim. For maximum incidence degree D and m owners, let W be the vertices incident to chosen pairs. Since 2m<=D|W| and |W|<=2 rank(selected rows), every all-hit guard has rank >=m/D. This holds for signed relative-word rejection too, independent of RHS, on the independent source chart; deletion or nonlocal projection changes the registry. On bounded-degree linear-density DO01 cores, this is linear, hence no universally logarithmic all-hit guard on that registry. It says nothing about a cheaper terminal or the count of actually visited fibers.

Fano example: seven triples cover all 21 unordered pairs once. Rank <=3 can cover at most six distinct pairs by the component-capacity bound, so cannot hit seven owners. A partition of a four-point complement of a Fano line, two points of the line, and its remaining singleton has rank4 and hits every triple (other lines meet the original line once). Exact min rank4.
