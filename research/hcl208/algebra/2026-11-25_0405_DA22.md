## Degree-four moment obstruction (proved conditional, not executed)
Suppose an unguarded UNSAT signed NAE chart has a degree-four real pseudoexpectation E on Boolean spins, normalized, respecting s_i^2=1, positive on squares of degree <=2, and E[Q_e p]=0 for every owner violation quadratic Q_e and polynomial p of degree <=2. Assume |E[s_i s_j]|<1 for every distinct pair. For either proposed pair relation s_i s_j=epsilon, let h=(1+epsilon s_i s_j)/2; E[h]>0. Define X_ab=E[h s_a s_b]/E[h]. X has unit diagonal and is PSD because h=((s_i+epsilon s_j)/2)^2 modulo Boolean relations and E[h l^2]>=0 for every linear l. X realizes the probe parity with certainty and has zero expected owner energy. Under a fixed pair relation, equality in the PSD correlation Cauchy inequality identifies the two Gram vectors up to sign. Local NAE closure preserves feasibility: if two signed literals coincide, zero expected owner energy forces the third to be their negative; if they are opposite the owner imposes nothing, and if all three are in one component the forbidden case would give positive expected energy. Thus all local joins can be imposed by the Gram vectors and quotient retains PSD zero-energy support. Any BS21 row-bound or PD certificate, or positive slack-minimum certificate, would contradict this feasible X (linear trace identities and PSD trace product). Hence neither probe side can be certified UNSAT and there is no first feedback cut; a true UNSAT instance has no verified SAT witness either. This is a precise obstruction criterion, not an existence theorem for such strict degree-four pseudoexpectations on hard NAE charts. Do not import pairwise-independent predicate results: ternary NAE itself admits no uniform pairwise-independent satisfying law (expected pair-sum must be -1). Random regular basic SDP failure is not a degree-four existence proof.
