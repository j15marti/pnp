\begin{lemma}[Factored resultant and resolution simulation]\label{lem:da20-sim}
For an oriented bucket $\operatorname{NAE}(v,L_i)$ let $A_i=\prod_{\ell\in L_i}(1+\ell)$, $B_i=\prod_{\ell\in L_i}\ell$ in the Boolean ring, and $q_i=(1+v)A_i+vB_i$. The violation polynomial of the BS19 pair resolvent is
\[
r_{ij}=A_iB_j+B_iA_j=q_i(A_j+B_j)+q_j(A_i+B_i).
\]
Its factored identity can be recorded and syntactically verified with polynomial work in the two explicit parent scopes. On the paired-CNF encoding $E(\operatorname{NAE}(L))=(\bigvee L)\land(\bigvee\bar L)$, every pair resolvent has a derivation of two ordinary resolution lines, one per complementary clause. Deduplication, tautology deletion and signed subsumption require no new proof lines; exact binary parity substitution costs at most one resolution per affected clause, using the corresponding implication from the binary owner. Singleton/empty owner rejection yields the empty clause. Thus any UNSAT execution of BS19 with these rules yields a resolution DAG of size polynomial in the paid number and bit length of explicit operations. The same applies to ordinary coordinate unit propagation and resolution-derived learned clauses when all discharged proofs are paid. Arbitrary affine guards, extension axioms, semantic keys, nonresolution terminals and implicit factored coverage are not included.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand the two products in the Boolean ring and cancel repeated terms. The two CNF resolvents follow by resolving $v\vee\bigvee L_i$ against $\neg v\vee\bigvee\bar L_j$ and the complementary pair. Both are tautological or neither is. A binary owner $E(\operatorname{NAE}(x,\ell))$ gives $x\vee\ell$ and $\neg x\vee\neg\ell$, so substitution of $x$ by $\neg\ell$ follows by resolving with the appropriate clause. Maintain immutable clause IDs and parent IDs despite physical deletions, charging each consumer and all proof lines, not the unfolded tree. The resultant does not promise that repeated circuit elimination or its coverage test is cheap.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Expanding critical ternary NAE family]\label{lem:da20-exp}
There is an absolute $\delta>0$ and arbitrarily large raw positive NAE-3 UNSAT registries with $n$ available variables, at most $4n$ owners, and every subregistry of $t\le \lfloor\delta n\rfloor$ owners having more than $7t/4$ incident variables. Their inclusion-minimal UNSAT subregistries retain this property and have single-sign SAT neighbors on identical triples. Every ordinary resolution refutation of their paired 3-CNF encodings has size $2^{\Omega(n)}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $4n$ independent uniformly random distinct-vertex triples (sampling triples with replacement). For any Boolean word, the fraction of monochromatic triples is at least $1/4-O(1/n)$, by minimizing $\binom a3+\binom{n-a}3$. Hence the expected number of satisfying words is at most $2^n(3/4+O(1/n))^{4n}=o(1)$. For $t$ chosen owners and $u=\lfloor7t/4\rfloor$ vertices, the chance that all their triples lie there is at most $(u/n)^{3t}$. A union bound is
\[
\binom{4n}{t}\binom{n}{u}(u/n)^{3t}
\le [C(t/n)^{1/4}]^t
\]
for an absolute $C$, using the standard binomial bound (the case $u<3$ has probability zero). Sum over $1\le t\le\delta n$, choosing $C\delta^{1/4}<1/2$. Split at $\sqrt n$: the first sum tends to zero geometrically with ratio $Cn^{-1/8}$, the second is bounded by a geometric tail with ratio $1/2$. Thus both properties coexist. For any such small subregistry the number of variables occurring exactly once is at least twice the union size minus $3t$, hence greater than $t/2$. Every such subregistry is satisfiable: recursively remove an owner having a unique variable and extend by choosing that variable to satisfy NAE. Minimal UNSAT extraction preserves expansion, and flipping one literal of a deleted owner makes its deletion witness satisfy the original modified registry. This is existential extraction, not a polynomial acquisition algorithm.

Let $\nu(C)$ be the minimum number of original owners jointly implying a clause $C$ (including both CNF clauses of an owner). Axioms have measure at most one, tautologies zero, and resolution is subadditive. The empty clause has measure greater than $s=\lfloor\delta n\rfloor$. In any topologically ordered refutation there is a first line with measure greater than $s/2$, whose measure is at most $s$ by subadditivity. For a minimum supporting owner set of that size $t$, every unique variable must occur in $C$: otherwise removing its owner gives a word falsifying $C$ and satisfying the other owners, and changing that unique variable repairs the removed owner without changing $C$, a contradiction. Therefore the line has width greater than $t/2>s/4$. Initial width is three. Ben-Sasson--Wigderson, \emph{Short proofs are narrow}, Theorem 3.5 / Corollary 3.6 (author PDF \url{https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/MYPAPERS/B-SW99/final.pdf}, pp. 8--9), converts this linear minimum width to size $2^{\Omega(n)}$, for general DAG resolution, not merely tree resolution. Renumbering occurring variables cannot increase the variable count; input bit length is $O(n\log n)$. No assertion of bounded occurrence, four-partition, logarithmic girth or explicit efficient generator for this family.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[No universal polynomial BS19 cap]\label{lem:da20-cap}
For every fixed polynomial cap $D(N)$, there are arbitrarily large raw ternary inputs on which the explicit BS19 projection-only procedure cannot finish with a correct decision under that cap, irrespective of pivot order. This remains true with paid resolution-compatible binary substitution, subsumption and polynomially many resolution derivations. In particular no aggregate polynomial potential can certify universal completion of this restricted dispatch.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
BS19 has at most $n$ elimination steps, at most $O(nD(N)^2)$ generated pair candidates, and polynomial work for its prescribed closure and representation. If it decided an UNSAT instance of Lemma~\ref{lem:da20-exp}, Lemma~\ref{lem:da20-sim} would give polynomial-size general resolution, contradicting the exponential size lower bound. It must decline or violate the charged cap. Neither a stronger proof system nor a compact gate whose evaluator is not resolution-simulable is excluded. In particular this is not an unrestricted runtime lower bound or simultaneous obstruction against the full cooperative portfolio.
\end{proof}
