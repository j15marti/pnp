## Lemma DA19-A (exact neighborhood quotient and affine residual)

For each pair of owners, partition each side into classes of identical live neighborhoods in the other side. Allowed pairs are constant on the product of two classes: equality of first-side neighborhoods makes membership independent of first endpoint, and equality of second-side neighborhoods makes membership independent of second. Allocate ceil(log2 c) bits per c classes, deterministically labeling classes. If the reduced class graph is a matching covering both sides (equivalently the live relation is a disjoint union of complete bipartite components), share the same class-label bits across the pair using the matching bijection; if the relation is complete, allocate zero bits. Otherwise use separate bit labels and retain the explicit allowed class-pair table. For each owner acquire the distinct concatenated class-label words of its live rows, storing one actual representative row per word. The conjunction of owner and nonrectangular pair factors is exactly decision-equivalent. Every original transversal maps to a word. Conversely each owner factor selects a real row, and every class-pair factor or shared label certifies the original edge for ANY representatives of those classes. Thus one may reconstruct an actual transversal, then apply earlier source lifts. This preserves the full projection to edge-class words, not necessarily all internal row choices or new guards on representatives. Use DP19's firewall; no new source facts follow merely from a representative restriction.

For each factor T on bit scope I, find its affine hull by Gaussian elimination of differences from an anchor. T equals its hull iff |T|=2^rank (T is a deduplicated subset). This is complete; all words with the same projection agree on all remaining factor tests. ## Lemma DA19-B (label-independent obstruction)
Every affine binary relation is rectangular: from (a,b),(a,b'),(a',b) closure under ternary XOR implies (a',b'). Thus nonrectangular pair factors cannot be made affine by any separate injective binary encodings. For any raw unpinned signed linear 3-uniform NAE registry in which every variable occurs at least twice, each nontrivial pair relation is rectangular (equality on the sole shared bit). Each incident edge-class label is a copy or complement of that actual bit. Each owner sees all three of its bits in its signature, so it has six distinct signature words, not a power of two, and cannot be an affine factor under any injective encoding. With minimal one-bit labels the local hull imposes exactly agreement among duplicate labels of each of the three actual bits, because NAE has full 3-bit affine hull. Global hull therefore has one free bit per actual variable, all appearing in nonlinear factors, so r=n. This applies to opposite-label Fano controls and arbitrary growing such registries. It defeats a universal small-rank theorem for this adapter even with every pair rectangular, not arbitrary nonlinear algorithms or P vs NP. Nonrectangular pair growth under learned edge deletion may even raise code dimension: this occurred on DR17. Counting code rank as a monotone universal potential is invalid.
