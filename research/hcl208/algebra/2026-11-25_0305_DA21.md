\section{DA21: Direct-sum indicator spans and alternating affine closure}\label{sec:da21}
\paragraph{Status.} PROVED scoped, independent review pending. This attacks BS20 joint high-degree cancellation and Obligation~\ref{obl:progress}. GD28 pays joint boundary width for Boolean composition; the following linear consumer instead pays the sum of individual packet widths, not the product. It is not a complete SAT decision procedure.
\begin{lemma}[Exact simultaneous indicator evaluation span]\label{lem:da21-span}
Let $P_j=\{x:A_jx=b_j\}$ be explicitly specified Boolean affine packets, $G$ an actual affine guard and $f_\ell$ supplied XOR sums of packet indicators (with the constant allowed). Fix a common order of the $n$ source bits. Intersect each packet with $G$, include $G$ and $G\cap\{x_i=1\}$, and discard inconsistent systems. For each resulting system $A_jx=b_j$, let $U_{jt},V_{jt}$ be the column spans before and after cut $t$; define $W=\max_t\sum_j2^{\dim(U_{jt}\cap V_{jt})}$. There is a deterministic algorithm with bit work and space polynomial in the explicit encoded length and $W$ that computes the exact linear span
\[
E=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_2}\{(f_1(x),\ldots,f_m(x),1,x):x\in G\}.
\]
It stores actual representative words for a spanning set. For any fixed polynomial cap in the original input length on the individual-state sum, it discovers the required span or declines with polynomial charged work. It does not enumerate the Cartesian product of the packet states.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
After reading prefix $x_{\le t}$, each packet retains its residual syndrome $s=b_j+A_{j,\le t}x_{\le t}$ if $s\in V_{jt}$, otherwise enters a permanent rejecting sink represented by the zero vector. The live syndromes lie in $(b_j+U_{jt})\cap V_{jt}$, empty or a coset of $U_{jt}\cap V_{jt}$. Use a one-hot vector for a live syndrome. Each next bit acts by a linear map on these vectors: send the basis vector for $s$ to that for $s+a_{j,t+1}x_{t+1}$ if live, else to zero. Take the direct sum across packets. At each layer apply both maps only to an independent basis of reachable prefix vectors, then retain an independent subset of the results by Gaussian elimination. Inductively their span is the span of all actual prefix vectors, since the maps are linear. The basis can consist of actual prefix representatives by retaining the original candidate upon a successful pivot; keep its actual prefix. At the final layer the only live syndrome is zero, so the individual states are indicators. Apply the supplied linear map for the XOR sums, the guard indicator and coordinate indicators; outside the guard every output is zero. This proves the exact span identity, including a zero guard. Suffix-span membership, row normalization and all state labels use polynomial bit length in the input. An implementation generates at most twice the previous basis size in candidate vectors per layer; a live state coordinate not reached by those candidates could not occur in their span. Thus dynamic state discovery with a fixed cap, including failed probes, is sound. Gaussian elimination, bit vectors, prefix retention and state dictionaries cost at most a fixed polynomial in input size and cap, with no semantic equality oracle. Witnesses for a nonzero linear functional are obtained from actual representatives. This is elementary white-box linear-span computation related to read-once oblivious algebraic branching programs; no black-box PIT theorem is required.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Affine relaxation, feedback and paid reserve]\label{lem:da21-close}
For the equations $f_\ell(x)=0$ on $G$, compute $H=\{x:(0,\ldots,0,1,x)\in E\}$. If empty, UNSAT is certified. Otherwise $H$ is an affine space containing every feasible word. Its annihilator equations are unconditional necessary information within $G$. If an actual representative satisfies every equation, it is a checked witness. One may include the consequences $x_i f_\ell=0$ via intersecting each indicator packet in the expression with $x_i=1$, or any explicitly charged fixed list of such multipliers. For a fixed root registry and a fixed polynomial cap on every direct-sum phase, alternating this procedure and Gaussian rank-one forbidden-packet closure, adding only independent necessary affine equations, is polynomial in the original input length, or declines. The proof does not require a bounded polynomial degree of individual indicator expressions.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every feasible word contributes its evaluation vector, hence belongs to $H$. Row-reduce the evaluation span with the constraint coordinates first and constant next; this decides whether a row with zero constraints and constant one exists and supplies one affine base point and the remaining direction rows. Annihilator equations follow by ordinary elimination. Intersect new equations with the current guard, retaining original provenance. For a registry of individually forbidden packets, an empty packet after quotient is removed, a packet equal to the guard rejects, and a rank-one forbidden packet forces the opposite equation. Do not apply those singleton rules to individual summands of a general XOR equation. Reclose after each strict deduction, and at stagnation. Guard rank increases at most $n$ times; keep original equations plus at most $n$ independent new equations, rather than retaining all generated consequences across rounds. The one-coordinate multiplier list has at most $(n+1)(m+n)$ entries and polynomial explicit input length; intersections are charged. Thus at most $n+1$ polynomial capped rounds (and polynomially many local closure scans) give same-execution polynomial $T,S$, and $\mathrm{CM}\le(T+1)S$. In a recursive portfolio all invocations, including declines, still require the original aggregate reserve. The affine space $H$ need not equal actual support or its affine hull. In particular the Fano UNSAT control below has full $H$ even after the chosen multiplier pass, so universal progress does not follow.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Unbounded-degree BS20 chain extension]\label{lem:da21-chain}
For integers $k,d\ge2$, use $k$ disjoint exterior blocks of $d$ bits and transitions $z_i+z_{i-1}=\prod_{j=1}^d x_{ij}$. This has an explicit $O(kd)$-packet private two-bit representation: forbid both inconsistent private parity words when all exterior bits are one; for each exterior bit equal to zero forbid the two inconsistent private words for parity zero. Endpoints $z_0=0,z_k=b$ are pinned. Thus its projected equation is $q(x)=b$, $q=\sum_i\prod_j x_{ij}$, proved by summing authenticated transition equations and reconstructing all private states by prefix parity. Two copies with opposite endpoints are UNSAT; same endpoints zero have the zero witness. The projected equations are XORs of coordinate-cube indicators, each individual live width one in every coordinate order, and their joint contradiction is certified by Lemma~\ref{lem:da21-span} with polynomial work independent of $d$. Separate affine covers certified by one component still require at least $2^k+1$ pieces: restrict the last $d-2$ bits of every block to one, obtaining BS20's quadratic levels and its bound. This is an escape from separate coverage, not a universal algorithm for arbitrary parametric implication components.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The transition packets described in the statement enforce exactly the indicated parity: if every exterior bit is one only the all-one activation applies, otherwise at least one zero activation applies and all applicable zero activations agree. The sum telescopes to $z_k+z_0=q$. Prefix parity is a polynomial witness lift. Opposite equations sum to $1=0$; the span algorithm discovers this identity from the supplied indicator representations. Restriction of any affine flat is empty or affine, so the quadratic separate-cover lower bound transfers. No claim is made that arbitrary implication components admit a polynomial-size XOR-of-packets projection, or that a jointly feasible syndrome is determined by its affine span.
\end{proof}
