## Lemma 1: transparent partial-partition completion
Let n be a multiple of 3 and let a positive linear ternary NAE registry be supplied as four partial matchings of its variables. For each color p, group the missing variables into triples (possible since the count is n-3m_p). For each such triple allocate two fresh copies of a 21-vertex SAT template T. Vertices A_k,B_k,C_k, k in Z_7; color q has triples (A_k,B_{k+q},C_{k+2q}), q=0,1,2,3. These are perfect partitions and linear: differences q and 2q are distinct modulo 7; distinct groups are used in every triple. Assign A=0,B=1,C=0 in copy 0 and complement in copy 1. Delete the k=0 clause of color p in each copy and for each of its three corresponding vertex pairs add a color-p clause with one missing original variable. The two filler vertices have opposite fixed values, regardless of the original value. Pair across the private copies has not appeared before; original/filler pairs are new and all original missing variables occur once in color p. Every resulting partition is perfect, every pair appears at most once. Every original assignment extends with exactly the same fixed filler word, and restricting a completed model preserves all retained clauses. Missing triples <=4n/3, so completed variable count <=57n. Filling, checking and reconstructing are polynomial bit operations.

## Lemma 2: slack per connected component without changing decision

If k of nine bits equal 1, the monochromatic pair count is binom(k,2)+binom(9-k,2)>=16; each legal triple contributes one, each violated triple three. Thus every assignment violates >=(16-12)/2=2 clauses.
Remove color-0 lines (0,3,6) and (1,4,7) to obtain the fixed 10-owner gadget G. Every model must violate both omitted lines, so terminals 1,4,7 are equal. ## Theorem: single-mismatch search equivalence on genuine S21 geometry
Assume one fixed deterministic polynomially capped search routine which, given genuine positive linear four-perfect-partition input and two explicitly feasible paired circulations with exactly one differing actual variable arc, returns a verified common word/flows whenever one exists. On negative inputs it can decline or time out; invalid results never accepted. Then P=NP.

Proof: apply Lemma 2, root a spanning tree of each clause-intersection component at a clause containing a vertex with a permanent spare color. Order clauses reverse BFS. For every activation e of color p, some v in e still misses a color q!=p after activation: a shared vertex with a later parent, or the root's permanent spare. Start arbitrary word on the empty registry; process prefixes monotonically. If current word satisfies the added clause, keep it. Otherwise exactly e is violated on the new prefix. Complete this prefix by Lemma 1 with the fixed filler colors, obtaining y violating only e. The color-q clause at v is a filler with opposite private bits. Flip only v to form x. All color p and q constraints are satisfied by x, and the other two colors by y. Pair colors (p,q) and the remaining pair (permute labels if fixed pairing required); the S21 decoder gives explicit integral flow witnesses. The two projections differ in exactly one coordinate. Every possible joint model is a monotone repair endpoint (S21 lemma). Invoke the hypothesized routine once and verify both side flows, variable identities, exact agreement and all clauses, then restrict to partial-source variables. If routine declines/invalid/timeouts, new prefix is UNSAT by the guarantee, so entire source is UNSAT by monotone constraint addition and exact gadget projection. If all stages succeed, verify and restrict to original source. All oracle inputs are in genuine geometry, not partial or synthetic signed networks. At most m' polynomially sized calls. Converse: P=NP yields standard witness search for the common word and exact flow lift. No claim of many-one hardness of one-mismatch decision, nor of hardness for fixed graph, fixed seed, or guarded subclasses. The query can change partition pairing and source by a paid exact adapter; this is not a same-network constant-radius procedure.
