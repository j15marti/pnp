\begin{lemma}[Exact pair registry and locally recognizable no-fill elimination]\label{lem:ds18-tree}
On an explicitly acquired coordinate-table chart with coordinate guards absorbed, let selector $i$ have domain $D_i$ of its actual accepted rows. Let $C_{ij}$ be the complete binary registry (initially equality on the full overlap). Arc consistency deletes unsupported rows; path consistency deletes $(a,b)$ in $C_{ij}$ if no current row of some third selector $k$ supports both. These are exact deletions relative to the actual guard. At their common fixed point, selector $k$ may be eliminated without adding any constraint if the family
\[
{\cal S}_k=\{\{c\in D_k:(a,c)\in C_{ik}\}:i\ne k,\ a\in D_i\}
\]
consists of connected subsets of one tree on $D_k$. The condition is deterministically recognizable in polynomial time, and any solution of the remaining registry lifts. No complete semantic closure is assumed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A selection of one row per owner satisfying all initial pair relations glues to a unique word on the union of scopes, and conversely; unused coordinates may be chosen subject to the actual coordinate pins. Every complete solution survives each consistency deletion. The registry retains every previous necessary pair restriction. At a fixed point, every pair of supports chosen by a feasible assignment of the remaining selectors intersects, by path consistency, and each individual support is nonempty by arc consistency. Connected subsets of a tree have the Helly property: root the tree, choose the deepest closest-to-root vertex among the selected subtrees, and use pairwise intersection to see it belongs to every selected subtree. Thus choose a common row for $k$. For zero remaining selectors choose any row; for one remaining selector use arc consistency. Keep the actual support and domain maps at elimination; reverse order reconstructs a word satisfying all eliminated edges. Later restrictions on survivors can only strengthen the premises. This is a decision-equivalent projection, not an assertion that eliminated row values are forced.

For recognition, discard duplicate supports and full-domain supports if desired. On the complete graph on $D_k$, weight $\{u,v\}$ by the number of supports containing both. For every spanning tree $T$, its weight is
\[
 w(T)=\sum_{S\in{\cal S}_k}|E(T[S])|
 \le \sum_{S\in{\cal S}_k}(|S|-1).
\]
Each induced graph is a forest. Equality holds precisely when every nonempty support induces a connected graph. A maximum spanning tree therefore decides the condition, including a numerical deficit when it fails. Empty domains reject; singleton domains are trivial. This is an explicit use of the established tree-convex consistency principle (Zhang, \emph{Consistency and Set Intersection}, IJCAI 2003, \href{https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2003/39}{paper}); compare Cooper--Jeavons--Salamon, \emph{Generalizing constraint satisfaction on trees}, AI 2010, \href{https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/publications/publication4995-abstract.html}{paper}. We do not claim novelty for tree convexity or path consistency.

Let $R$ be the original total number of acquired rows. There are at most $R^2$ live pair entries, and at most $R$ live rows and $R$ nonempty selectors. Each strict deletion or elimination decreases this count. Repeated full AC/PC scans, failed tree tests, support weight construction, maximum spanning trees, bit-mask operations, all guard and row identity checks, and retained reconstruction maps are polynomial in explicit input length. A conservative naive bit-operation bound for the frozen chart is $O((L+2)^8)$ when $L$ includes its explicit input rows, scopes and pins; this includes repeat scans and all lifts, but not unbounded recursive fallback. Polynomial peak space and cumulative memory follow on the same serial realization. Stop with UNKNOWN if no further permitted move or paid decision applies. The certificate does not cover every residual.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Complete no-fill safety is coNP-complete even after path closure]\label{lem:ds18-nofill}
For general explicit binary CSPs, deciding whether \emph{every} feasible assignment of the remaining selectors extends to a specified selector $k$ is coNP-complete, even on an arc- and path-consistent star with Boolean leaves and unrestricted leaf-leaf relations.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A countercertificate is one feasible remaining assignment whose support intersection is empty; it is polynomially checkable. Reduce from UNSAT of non-tautological CNF $F$, adding three unit clauses on fresh distinct variables. This preserves satisfiability. The center has one value per resulting clause, and leaf $i$ takes a Boolean value $b$. Its support is the list of clauses not yet satisfied by the literal of variable $i$ set to $b$ (including clauses not containing that variable). A complete leaf word extends to the center precisely when at least one clause is unsatisfied. Hence every leaf word extends iff the padded formula is UNSAT. Arc consistency holds: each center clause can be falsified on any one leaf, and each leaf choice leaves at least one of the three new units untouched. Every pair of leaf choices leaves at least one new unit untouched, so extends to the center. Every allowed center-leaf pair extends to every other leaf independently, since each clause is nontautological. All other triples are unrestricted; this is path consistency. Construction and checking are polynomial. The star itself is always satisfiable (pick any center clause and falsify it), so this is an obstruction to assuming complete projection-safety recognition is cheap, NOT a decision lower bound, and not a hardness claim for the genuine native tetravalent subclass. The reduction neither asserts that arbitrary binary compatibility tables already arise as raw equality-on-overlap charts nor rules out sound incomplete recognition.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Universal pair-tree progress fails on genuine linear NAE charts]\label{lem:ds18-stall}
For a signed linear 3-uniform NAE registry in which every vertex occurs in at least two owners, the unpinned raw six-row selector encoding is already arc- and path-consistent, and no selector has the tree-convex support certificate of Lemma~\ref{lem:ds18-tree}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Any pair of owners overlaps in at most one coordinate. To extend an allowed pair to a third owner, at most two distinct coordinates of that owner are fixed consistently; every such word extends to signed NAE. A single row also extends to every other owner. Hence AC/PC makes no deletions. For each of the three coordinates of a pivot owner, each value occurs in exactly three of its six rows; since the coordinate occurs elsewhere and overlaps are linear, both corresponding three-element supports occur. The six supports are distinct. A tree simultaneously connecting them needs at least $6(3-1)=12$ total induced edges counted with multiplicity. Distinct rows agree on at most two coordinates, so each tree edge belongs to at most two of the six supports; a six-vertex tree has only five edges, giving at most ten. Contradiction. This excludes the tree certificate on both the original Fano UNSAT registry and its single-sign SAT neighbors, not nonlocal rewrites, actual symmetry quotient, other certificates, or unrestricted decision.
\end{proof}
