## C. Strict gain and barrier (PROVED)
Let guard bits a,b be protected and private bit x. Reject exactly x=a*b. Its rejection indicator is [x=0] - [x=0,a=b=1] + [x=1,a=b=1]. Both accepting and rejecting supports affinely span the entire cube, so ordinary local affine closure is stagnant and U(0)=0. Yet for P=(a,b), U(P) contains x and the safe value is x=1 XOR (a*b), a nonlinear RHS. With k private x_i and two protected bits per owner (shared or distinct), all x_i directions are independent modulo P. The entire k-owner system peels; for distinct a_i,b_i there are 4^k guard assignments, but acquisition and a requested lift are polynomial. For shared a,b the same theorem holds. Two opposing bad graphs x=a*b and x!=a*b retain two owners, and are UNSAT; independent row selection cannot satisfy both. The theorem is not a general coverage shortcut.

Further boundary: for a compact circuit giving B(x,t)=(NOT t) OR F(x), with P=x, t belongs to U(P) iff 3CNF F is UNSAT. For SAT, a model x gives a same-P bad pair at both t values; for UNSAT all bad words have t=0. Nonmembership has a pair witness, so this direction test for arbitrary circuit inputs is coNP-complete. This does not contradict the explicit signed-list theorem; converting a circuit to a suitable packet list is not free. It is a conditional barrier, not a P!=NP result. BW11's arbitrary 3SAT joint positive-escape compatibility, DO11's local Hall conservation and DR11's explicit branching obstruction remain valid.
