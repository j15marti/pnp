Proof: form a graph on owners; two-owner directions are edges, one-owner directions are dangling edges. Gaussian eliminate the independent private columns at owner i, giving its forbidden event as E_i y=h_i and x_e=f_ie(y) for each incident edge, with affine f_ie and rank(E_i)=r_i-d_i. If E_i y != h_i, i is inactive and automatically satisfied. A connected component containing a cycle (parallel edges allowed) or dangling edge has an injection of owners into their incident edges: orient a cycle cyclically and root remaining trees at it, or root at the dangling edge. Choose the designated edge opposite f_ie(y) for every owner; assignments are consistent since edges are distinct. Every exterior word extends. In a tree without dangling edges, if an inactive root exists, root there and let every other vertex choose its parent edge opposite its own forbidden bit. If an edge has equal endpoint forbidden bits, set it opposite both and root the two resulting trees at its endpoints. If all owners are active and every edge's two forbidden bits are opposite, any edge satisfies at most one endpoint; the tree has one fewer edge than owners, so no full extension. The exact forbidden exterior packet is the intersection of E_i y=h_i and f_ie(y)+f_je(y)=1 over tree edges. It can be empty (delete), or the whole space (UNSAT). Its rank is at most sum_i(r_i-d_i)+|E|=sum_i r_i-|E|. Cyclic/dangling components contribute zero, with sum_i r_i >= sum_i d_i >= |E|. Summing proves the rank claim. This is an exact decision/projection theorem, not a model-count identity.
