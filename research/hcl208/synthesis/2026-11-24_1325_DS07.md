## DS07-A: exact sparse binary-tree signed joins
Normalize affine guard G by Gaussian recharting to a d-dimensional cube (M=2^d). Each explicit bad event E_i is an affine conjunction; drop inconsistent events, reject universal event, complement a rank-one bad event into a necessary guard, drop contained bad events, repeat to fixed point. Each deduction checked relative to the retained guard. These are paid restricted closure rules, not complete semantic closure.

Fix any authenticated full binary tree with one event per leaf. For region J let U_J be the span of coefficient rows of its events, and I_J=U_J intersect U_out where outside includes every other event. For subset S of J let K_S=intersection of its events, A_S its consistent equation space, and H_S its affine consequences on I_J. Maintain table
T_J[H] = sum_{S subset J, K_S consistent, H_S=H} (-1)^|S| |K_S|.
Both coefficient rows and RHS retained, canonical RREF, zero mass removed. Empty intersection has mass M. Leaf has skip +M and include -M/2^rank(E), projected onto I.

For children L,R, let H_L,H_R be retained keys. Their underlying equation spaces A_L,A_R can overlap only on U_L intersect U_R, contained in both interfaces. Thus any inconsistency between the original intersections is exactly inconsistency of H_L+H_R. For compatible pair define o=rank H_L+rank H_R-rank(H_L+H_R), where the last rank is the coefficient rank of the joined system. This equals dim(A_L intersect A_R) on coefficient spaces. Counting affine fibers gives
|K_L intersect K_R| = |K_L| |K_R| 2^o / M.
Consequently multiply signed masses using the same factor, sum identical unprojected keys H=H_L+H_R into Q_J, then project H to I_J for T_J. Pairwise divisions are exact integers, since the expression is the sum of signed integer cardinalities of original intersections. Cancellation is valid by bilinearity. For any row p in I_J in A_L+A_R write p=l+r. Since p is observable outside J, l is in U_L intersect (U_R+U_out)=I_L and similarly r in I_R. Thus projection of H gives every parent consequence, including RHS, and no spurious ones. Root I=0, so total mass is the exact legal selector count. No need to materialize all interface assignments.

## DS07-D: all-tree exponential obstruction, even on easy 3-CNF
For complete graph on n vertices introduce private bit y_e for each of m=n(n-1)/2 edges. Bad event E_uv=[x_u=0,x_v=0,y_uv=0]. Every ordinary clause is x_u OR x_v OR y_uv. Root SAT by y=1, no rank-one or containment deductions, complete affine hull (all-ones word and all its single-bit flips legal). Also count is easy:
Z=sum_{k=0}^n binom(n,k) 2^(m-binom(k,2)).
This is polynomial bit arithmetic (condition on number of zero vertex bits).

Consider any binary event tree. There exists a node cut J with between m/3 and 2m/3 leaves: descend to a child with >2m/3 until none, then select a child of size >=m/3. Its boundary B consists of vertices incident to edges on both sides. Each side has >=m/3 edges hence support size >=sqrt(n(n-1)/3). Therefore |B| >= 2 sqrt(n(n-1)/3)-n = Omega(n). The interface is precisely the span of boundary coordinate rows (private bits never shared). For subset S of J the projected key pins the union of its boundary endpoints to zero. Every attainable boundary union K has nonzero weight: the maximal subset S* containing all edges of J whose boundary endpoints lie in K is unique and realizes K. Each summand is (-1)^|S| 2^(n+m-|S|-|V(S)|); strict subset has strictly smaller |S|+|V(S)|, hence larger power of two. The unique lowest 2-adic valuation cannot cancel. To obtain exponentially many unions, greedily choose maximal disjoint nonempty edge boundary supports (size at most two). If there are >=|B|/4 such supports, their subsets give 2^(|B|/4) unions. Otherwise >|B|/2 vertices are uncovered. Each uncovered vertex belongs to some support that meets the covered set and has no other uncovered vertex, by maximality; choose one per uncovered vertex. Subsets are distinguished by their uncovered vertices. Thus every binary event tree has a table with >=2^(|B|/4)=2^Omega(n) actual nonzero signed keys. The encoded input is polynomial in n. No ordering, balanced tree, or zero cancellation rescues this fixed explicit signed projection representation. This is NOT hardness against arbitrary algebraic circuits, nonlinear preprocessing, or the portfolio. The private y variables are an immediate SAT terminal, so this family is deliberately excluded from the computational diagonal sequence. It also disproves universal small signed-tree capacity on syntactically closed inputs, not universal paid terminal coverage.
