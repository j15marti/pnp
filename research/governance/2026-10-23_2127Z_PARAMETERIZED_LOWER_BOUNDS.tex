\section*{1. Parameterized lower bounds are a primary candidate}
Do not require a candidate lower bound to have one universal scalar form depending only on source-variable count. Investigate bounds of the form
\[
T_A(F)\ge L\bigl(N,\boldsymbol\theta_A(F),S_A(F)\bigr),
\qquad N=|F|,
\]
and rigorously charged trajectory-dependent alternatives. The algorithm is a uniform computable policy over states, not a fixed branching rule or an assumed fixed-parameter-tractable procedure. State includes the source and current representation, assignments, learned constraints, cache, decomposition, remaining budget, and implementation control. Permit propagation, SCC, Horn and affine methods, branching, learning, backtracking, restarts, decomposition, caching, recomputation, rereading, and direct decision in arbitrary state-dependent combinations.

\section*{2. Define parameters independently}
Each coordinate of $\boldsymbol\theta$ must have an explicit mathematical definition independent of observed runtime, solver failure, and the desired conclusion. Candidate coordinates may concern compatibility interfaces, non-affine residual constraints, representation-relative rank, backdoors, or source information, but none implies hardness without a separate proof. Distinguish the input parameters from their changing values along an execution and from quantities available only to the analyst. A parameter need not be supplied to the solver. If the solver uses it, charge discovering or estimating it and verifying the claimed structure. A finite menu of methods remains an experimental restriction, not coverage of all algorithms.

Different SAT-side and UNSAT-side parameters and success conditions are allowed. The solver must not receive the true label in advance. Choose or interleave procedures using acquired evidence and charge all attempts. SAT decision does not require counting models, enumerating backward paths, reconstructing histories, or materializing every context.

\section*{3. Exact quantified claim and growth obligation}
Fix one NP-complete language, explicit encoding, machine model, source-access convention, and word width before stating a lower bound. State whether the bound applies to every input, a promise subclass, or suitable algorithm-dependent worst-case families. Distinguish unconditional bounds, restricted-model bounds, and assumptions such as ETH/SETH.

For example, a sufficient candidate route is: for every total uniform deterministic exact decider $A$, there are fixed constants $a_A>0,b_A\ge0$ and a sequence $F_{A,j}$ with $N_j=|F_{A,j}|\to\infty$ such that
\[
T_A(F_{A,j})\ge\frac{2^{a_A\rho_A(F_{A,j})}}{N_j^{b_A}},
\qquad
\limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{\rho_A(F_{A,j})}{\log_2N_j}=\infty.
\]
Constants may depend on the fixed algorithm only when explicitly stated; they must not depend on the input. A common $a,b$ would be stronger and is not necessary. On a suitable subsequence the logarithm of this candidate bound divided by $\log_2N_j$ exceeds any fixed polynomial exponent. Thus this combination would imply that $A$ has no polynomial worst-case time bound. The work inequality, parameter-growth condition, and coverage of every adaptive policy are three separate obligations. None is assumed proved.

A parameterized exponential bound can coexist with polynomial-time behavior when the relevant parameter stays $O(\log N)$. Conversely, a large parameter alone does not prove the work inequality. The familiar $2^r\operatorname{poly}(N)$ enumeration algorithm is an upper bound, not a lower bound. The required hard inputs may differ between deciders.

\section*{4. Switching-stable accounting}
Test every candidate bound against changes of representation, variable order, and solver rule. A large table in one forward or reverse DP is not mandatory for a policy that switches to SCC, Gaussian elimination, a compact relation, or direct decision. Prove why the full adaptive trajectory must incur the claimed cost, or label the result restricted. Charge parameter discovery, policy evaluation, rule selection and switching, unsuccessful attempts, preprocessing, compatibility, reconstruction, source reads and replay, shared work, output, and peak live space. Do not add phase lower bounds without a valid amortization or non-double-counting proof.
