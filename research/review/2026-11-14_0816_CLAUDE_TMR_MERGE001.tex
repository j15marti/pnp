\section{New exact polarity geometry}
For \(n\ge1\), let \(a,b\in\{0,1\}^n\), and \(d\) be their Hamming distance. For fresh auxiliaries, define
\[
U(\ell;u,v)=
(\ell\lor u\lor v)\land(\ell\lor u\lor\neg v)
\land(\ell\lor\neg u\lor v)\land(\ell\lor\neg u\lor\neg v)
\]
and
\[
E_n(a,b)=
\bigwedge_{i=1}^n U(\ell_i(a);p_i,q_i)
\land
\bigwedge_{i=1}^n U(\ell_i(b);r_i,s_i),
\]
where \(\ell_i(a)=x_i\) if \(a_i=1\), and \(\neg x_i\) otherwise.
There are \(5n\) variables and \(8n\) clauses, each with three distinct variables.
The TMR encoding has length
\[
L(n)=13n+2+24n\bigl(1+\max(1,\lceil\log_2(5n)\rceil)\bigr)
=\Theta(n\log(n+1)).
\]

Edits change only the \(24n\) literal-polarity coordinates. Headers, variable identities, incidence and clause order remain fixed, so every edit is a valid same-length proper-3-CNF source. Individual sensitivity counts single flips changing the SAT/UNSAT label. Block sensitivity counts the largest collection of pairwise disjoint nonempty coordinate sets, each of whose flips changes the label. Opposite-label distance and nearest neighbors refer to this polarity Hamming cube.

\begin{theorem}[Exact family geometry]\label{thm:geometry}
For every \(n\ge1\) and every \(a,b\in\{0,1\}^n\):
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c|c}
d&\text{label}&\sens(E)&\bs(E)&\text{opposite distance}&\text{nearest neighbors}\\\hline
0&\mathrm{SAT}&0&2n&4&2n\\
1&\mathrm{UNSAT}&24&24&1&24\\
d\ge2&\mathrm{UNSAT}&0&24&d&24^d
\end{array}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The gadget \(U\) forces \(\ell\): if false, its binary clauses exclude all four auxiliary assignments; if true, every clause holds. Thus \(E_n(a,b)\) is SAT exactly when \(a=b\). Different \(i\) have disjoint variable sets.

For a diagonal \(a=b\), fix \(x_i=a_i\). A clause remains true unless its central \(x_i\)-literal sign was flipped. Each clause with flipped central sign excludes exactly one of four auxiliary assignments, regardless of simultaneous auxiliary-sign changes. At most three such clauses cannot exclude all four assignments. Fresh auxiliaries allow the local choices to combine.

Every UNSAT-producing block therefore contains all four central signs of at least one of the \(2n\) gadgets. Flipping precisely this quadruple reverses its forced value and contradicts its untouched partner. These \(2n\) sensitive quadruples are disjoint. Any disjoint collection of sensitive blocks must use distinct quadruples. Hence \(\bs=2n\), distance \(4\), exactly \(2n\) nearest UNSAT neighbors, and individual sensitivity zero.

In a conflicting component \(a_i\ne b_i\), any single one of its \(24\) signs repairs it. Set \(x_i\) to satisfy the untouched gadget. In the edited gadget the central literal was originally false. A central-sign flip removes one active binary exclusion; an auxiliary-sign flip duplicates another exclusion and leaves one assignment unexcluded. Either permits an auxiliary assignment.

If \(d=1\), exactly these \(24\) signs change the formula's label. Other components cannot repair its conflict. If \(d\ge2\), one edit leaves another conflicting component, giving individual sensitivity zero.

Any SAT-producing block must touch each of the \(d\) conflicting components. One arbitrary sign flip per conflict suffices. Thus the minimum distance is \(d\), and its nearest shell has exactly \(24^d\) strings.

Every sensitive block intersects the \(24\) signs of any fixed conflicting component, so block sensitivity is at most \(24\). Enumerate each conflict's \(24\) positions consistently. For each position number, flip that position in every conflict. The resulting \(24\) blocks are disjoint and repair all conflicts, attaining the bound.
\end{proof}

TMR-2/001 already supplied the \(2n\) disjoint quadruples, a block-sensitivity lower bound. TMR-2/021 supplied general polarity criteria. The new synthesis adds matching upper bounds and the complete distance/count table for this family. TMR-5/019's repeated three-variable cube is a different construction. No literature-priority claim is made.

The subsequent publication check found TMR-2/022 at commit
\texttt{be1b20fc5526c07d82e55ef8492845a662875625}; its full report was then read. It independently gives the general identity
\[
d_{\mathrm{sign}}(F,\mathrm{SAT})
=\min_a \#\{\text{clauses of }F\text{ falsified by }a\}
=m-\operatorname{MAXSAT}(F).
\]
For a fixed assignment, each false clause needs one changed polarity, and one such change per false clause suffices; minimizing proves the identity. On an off-diagonal \(E_n(a,b)\), every conflicting component contributes at least one violation and an assignment with exactly one is available, so this identity also yields the distance \(d\) in our table. The diagonal's distance to UNSAT, block-sensitivity upper bounds and nearest-neighbor counts require their separate arguments above.

TMR-2/022 further recognizes the maximally robust, zero-Walsh-moment sublanguage in deterministic polynomial time, including overlapping variable triples \cite{maxsat}. This strengthens the easy-family control beyond fixed-three-variable duplication. This report reviews those symbolic statements; it does not claim to have rerun that report's V8 experiment.

\begin{corollary}
For every fixed correct machine in TMR-3/002's model, for all sufficiently large \(n\), some diagonal simultaneously has
\[
\sens(E)=0,\quad \bs(E)=2n,\quad
T_A(E)(S_A(E)+1)\ge c_A L(n)n.
\]
\end{corollary}
The geometry holds on every diagonal, including the one selected by the crossing theorem; different hard inputs are not combined. Single-bit sensitivity misses this cut-based distinction requirement. Conversely, growing block sensitivity, robust opposite-label distance, and exponentially many nearest neighbors coexist with the cached linear-time equality algorithm. They are controls, not semantic-hardness certificates.

\section{When an abstraction can carry the crossing proof}
TMR-4/020--022 and TMR-5/018 supply authenticated tape-factor relations, charged head motion, and finite caches. A proof relation may describe remote information, but an executable transition needs an authentic cached value or paid physical access.

A safe composition requires:
\begin{itemize}
\item[] One fixed uniform quotient with one sequential source head, correct both-label behavior and source-independent transitions.
\item[] Every concrete execution represented at no greater abstract time, with initial, terminal and source binding preserved.
\item[] Complete crossing interfaces containing retained tape contents, relevant head positions, control and every authenticated cache entry.
\item[] A valid cross-source splice for equal accepting transcripts. Same-source replay alone does not establish this for a symbolic abstraction.
\item[] Crossings charged to physical movements, with no invisible opposite-side source read during a purported local segment.
\end{itemize}
For an ordinary quotient in the theorem's model, the existing crossing theorem applies directly. If \(T_{\widehat A}\le T_A\), on one selected input
\[
T_A(E)(S_{\widehat A}(E)+1)\ge c_{\widehat A}L(n)n.
\]
Current exact quotients have \(S_{\widehat A}=S_A\), reproducing the existing bound. Counting only two new cache registers while omitting retained \(C\)-tape information is invalid. Two independent source heads also break the cut decomposition: one can read the opposite side during a supposedly local segment. Cost preservation alone supplies neither cut locality nor a stronger exponent.

\section{A quantitative limit on workspace savings}
The factor abstractions retain a physical dummy \(B\) tape and satisfy
\[
T_{\widehat A}(x)=T_A(x),\qquad S_{\widehat A}(x)=S_A(x).
\]
They preserve simultaneous resource bounds. Erasing redundant descriptions is not a physical workspace reduction.

\begin{proposition}[Deleting a bounded-separation dummy tape]
Suppose throughout a run
\[
h_C(t)-h_B(t)-d_0=\delta(t)\in D,
\]
where \(d_0\) is fixed, \(D\) is fixed and finite, and
\(\Delta=\max D-\min D\). For visited-cell counts \(S_B,S_C\),
\[
|S_B-S_C|\le\Delta.
\]
If a new implementation removes \(B\), preserves the full \(C\)-head trace and other tape traces, and charges new bookkeeping, then
\[
S_{\mathrm{old}}\le2S_{\mathrm{new}}+\Delta.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each locally moving head visits an interval. Maxima and minima of the pointwise relation bound either interval length by the other plus \(\Delta\). If \(R\) counts other work cells,
\(S_{\mathrm{old}}=S_B+S_C+R\le2S_C+R+\Delta\le2S_{\mathrm{new}}+\Delta\).
\end{proof}
This deletion cannot turn a growing \(L^s\) footprint, for fixed \(s>0\), into \(L^{o(1)}\). It concerns the retained trace, not every algorithmic reorganization. TMR-4/022's erased tape visits only two cells. A length-growing cache cannot be hidden in one fixed machine's finite control.

\section{Repairs and qualifications}
\subsection{Parked guess-head coordinates}
Claude's sequential portfolio extension counts crossing states by \(2^{O(S+\log g)}\), where \(g\) is guess length. This straightforward count requires confinement to the bounded guess tape. An unbounded head coordinate instead requires \(O(\log(g+T+1))\) bits.

The asymptotic result is repairable. If \(T\ge L^2/\log L\), the product bound is already true for \(S\ge1\). Otherwise \(T\) is polynomial in \(L\). With \(g\le L\) and \(S\ge\log L\), the coordinate cost is \(O(S)\), retaining \(2^{O(S)}\) in the nontrivial regime. This applies TMR's newer head-accounting discipline.

\subsection{Simulation implication and encoding}
Quadratic multitape-to-single-tape simulation supports a one-way implication: an appropriate superquadratic single-physical-tape SAT time exclusion gives a superlinear multitape exclusion, with exponents halved. Claude's later word \emph{equivalent} is stronger than this proves. The converse cannot follow by inverting a simulation upper bound. Time-class exclusion also does not automatically give an eventual lower bound at every length.

Claude's finite encoder supplies identifier width externally and omits a self-describing header. A common header repairs it without changing the asymptotic construction. TMR's \(E_n\) grammar supplies an explicit alternative.

\subsection{Portfolio verification and uniformity}
DIRECTPRODUCT's witness-producing portfolio requires charged witness access and verification. TPORT-0001 corrects it to sound decision portfolios; search transfers only if verification fits the same joint budget. One fixed uniform short-choice predicate is not an arbitrary nonuniform program collection chosen separately at each length.

\section{Missing implications}
\subsection{Time-space contraction}
TPORT concerns a specified alternation-trading rule system and random-access, subpolynomial-workspace model. Its base-rule barrier is \(c\ge1\), \(c+d\ge2\). TMR's exact quotients leave time and workspace unchanged and do not erase portfolio choice costs. They cannot automatically place an arbitrary polynomial-time SAT decider inside that small-space region.

The displayed exponent algebra and restricted-rule potential passed independent review. The limited-nondeterminism hierarchy lemma is only sketched: its scheduling and uniform subpolynomial-space bound require expansion before fresh certification of the stronger SAT-level extension. This is an unresolved proof-detail audit, not a counterexample. The long rational certificate was not rerun here. Published Buss--Williams limitations concern their specified methodology, not arbitrary proofs or algorithms \cite{bw}.

\subsection{Direct products and complexity worlds}
A direct-product theorem amplifies an existing hardness premise. The sensitivity and transport results here supply neither average-case hardness against all relevant algorithms nor a deterministic hitting-set generator. Claude already retired the proposed unconditional direct-product derivation and retained conditional statements. The literature also separates base hardness from amplification \cite{dp}.

The world ledger remains useful for classification. These restricted results are compatible with the unresolved P versus NP question. An equivalent reformulation of separation is an open target, not an independently established intermediate lemma.

\subsection{Where processing occurs}
A computational charge must cover the whole execution. TMR-5/002 shows that a correct machine can postpone its last physical source observation until almost all computation is finished, by solving possible completions first. Expensive work strictly after the final newly observed bit is therefore not universally necessary. This does not make total computation cheap.

\section{Barrier audit and reconciliation}
The equality family is a disjoint collection of constant-size components and an easy algorithmic control. Renaming and variable complementation preserve semantic statements by coordinate bijections; these are symbolic observations here, not additional claimed executions. Clause order affects physical geometry. Reencoding and additional heads require charged model translations.

COPY and caching are admitted by the product bound. DELAY can enlarge one run without making work necessary; RECOMP trades storage for access. An oracle or nonlocal algebraic service can violate cut locality. No nonrelativization, nonalgebrization, or Natural Proofs barrier bypass is claimed.

\textbf{Adopt:} scoped crossing constructions; the TMR-3/002 same-execution theorem and TMR-5 review; TMR-2 polarity criteria; authenticated cache accounting.

\textbf{Add as reviewed candidates:} exact geometry, its same-input crossing corollary, bounded-separation footprint bound, and parked-head repair.

\textbf{Reject as inferences:} information erasure implies exponent contraction; zero sensitivity removes transport requirements; high sensitivity or robustness proves semantic hardness; same-source replay licenses cross-source splicing automatically.

\textbf{Still OPEN:} all-machine coverage, actual joint contraction into a matched forbidden region, and total computational lower bounds excluding every polynomial exponent. No general polynomial SAT algorithm is supplied.

The next integration step is to use this equality control in the charged machine-composition audit specified by the concurrent review \cite{composition}. Any proposed summary or abstraction must retain both the product lower bound and the cached linear-time upper bound in their respective resource regimes. TMR-1/TMR-2 can review the geometry and TMR-4/TMR-5 the cut-interface conditions as supporting checks; this report does not reassign the scheduled team or make further sensitivity variants the main target. The deliverable remains one actual, fully charged computation service or a precise counterexample to its proposed implementation.
