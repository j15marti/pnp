\section{Parameters and charging convention}
Let $N$ be the complete encoded bit length of an ordinary CNF, rather than its
variable count. Let $A$ be one fixed exact SAT decider with time $N^{K+o(1)}$
and writable space $N^{\sigma+o(1)}$. Its finite code may be hardwired;
length-dependent advice may not. For each actually executed phase, charge
\[
 W=D+V+U+R+E+X+C,
\]
where $D$ is successful and failed discovery, $V$ verification, $U$ update,
$R$ replay, $E$ emission, $X$ physical source/child access, and $C$ combination.
Terms describe disjoint charged operations, to avoid double counting.
Peak space includes all simultaneously live states, buffers, indexes,
certificates, and stack frames. A reused result is charged once only if its
reuse is implemented; recomputation is charged each time.

\section{Aggregate closure with physical access}
The following restates the audited Recovery7/Recovery10 accounting with the
interface hypotheses made explicit.
\begin{theorem}[Conditional aggregate closure]
Suppose a uniform exact phase covers every input and every recursively reached
ordinary CNF. At input length $N$, local work is $N^{r+o(1)}$, total executed
child length is at most $N^{v+o(1)}$, and every child has length at most
$N^{h+o(1)}$ for a fixed $0<h<1$. All bounds use one uniform asymptotic
envelope over the same reachable inputs and phases. Fix $N_0$ such that
each child is strictly shorter than its parent for $N>N_0$, and use one
exact terminating base solver on all lengths at most $N_0$.
Repeated child calls count with multiplicity.
Suppose a proved physical simulation incurs at most $N^{g+o(1)}$ cost per
child-machine step, with no additional uncharged semantic call. Then recursive
execution has time $N^{C+o(1)}$, where
\[
 C=\max\left\{1,r,\frac{g+v-h}{1-h}\right\}.
\]
For explicitly materialized children, $g=0$; if local retained state is
$N^{p+o(1)}$ and depth-first composition meets the stated memory model,
peak space is $N^{\max\{p,h\}+o(1)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $c\ge1$ and child lengths $m_i$,
\[
 \sum_i m_i^c\le(\max_i m_i)^{c-1}\sum_i m_i
 \le N^{v+h(c-1)+o(1)}.
\]
Physical simulation contributes exponent $g+v+h(c-1)$. Choose any fixed
$c>C$. Then $c>r$ and $c>g+v+h(c-1)$, so standard induction absorbs the
uniform $o(1)$ terms for sufficiently large inputs. Since this holds for
every $c>C$, the exponent bound follows. Along a contracting depth-first
path, retained states and explicit child buffers sum to the stated maximum
exponent. This argument presupposes the actual composable access and memory
implementation; an abstract generator does not establish it.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Saturation.} At $(r,v,h,p)=(6/5,11/10,1/2,1/2)$, the conditional
pair is $(C,P)=(6/5,1/2)$ when $g=0$. Any conservative per-step access exponent
$g>0$ instead gives $C\ge6/5+2g$. Batch or cache improvements may beat this
simulation bound but need their own proved operation ledger. This is not an
unconditional lower bound on every possible implementation.

\paragraph{Open link CC-A.} Construct the required all-input, successor-closed,
source-visible phase, or the corrected main9 strict successor with
$(r,h,p)=(1,2/3,2/3)$. All acquisition, failed candidates, semantic guards,
encoding, and physical access must fit one simultaneous ledger. Conditional
closure cannot create a transition at a portfolio's UNKNOWN state.

\section{Finite exponent elimination and its limited conclusion}
\begin{lemma}[Fixed-depth elimination]
Suppose one uniform exact SAT-preserving shrinker emits an ordinary CNF of
length $N^{h+o(1)}$, uses time $N^{r+o(1)}$ and space $N^{p+o(1)}$, and has
$0<h<1$. For fixed $d$, apply it $d$ times and then run $A$. Including explicit
successor storage, the resulting bounds are
\[
 T(N)\le N^{\max\{r,h,h^dK\}+o(1)},\qquad
 S(N)\le N^{\max\{p,h,h^d\sigma\}+o(1)}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The $j$th successor has length $N^{h^j+o(1)}$. A fixed number of phases has
total cost dominated by the first phase and the final decider call. Retaining
the first explicit child and smaller descendants gives the space maximum.
\end{proof}
For $(r,h,p)=(1,2/3,2/3)$ and finite $K,\sigma$, some fixed $d$ reaches
$(1,2/3)$. Fortnow's near-linear-time/sublinear-space theorem excludes that
pair under the matched random-access model \cite{fortnow2000}. Therefore
the implication from $P=NP$ to existence of the shrinker is itself the
load-bearing unproved statement. The universal product bound
$2\cos(\pi/7)$ is not used.

\section{A separate certificate spine}
Fix one efficiently universal plain-description machine $U$, with polynomial
simulation overhead for each fixed machine. Fix a time bound $t(n)=n^{\omega(1)}$
and a polynomial witness bound $L(n)$. Define a polynomial-time relation
$V(1^n,p,x,\pi)$ and
\[
 I_{n,p}=\{x\in\{0,1\}^n:\exists\pi,\ |\pi|\le L(n),\ V(1^n,p,x,\pi)=1\}.
\]
The same bound $L$ applies throughout. Require, for all $|p|\le n-2$:
\begin{description}
\item[] Completeness: if $U(p)$ halts with the unique final output
$x\in\{0,1\}^n$ within $t(n)$,
then $x\in I_{n,p}$.
\item[] Global ambiguity: $|I_{n,p}|\le2$, after union over all accepted proofs.
\end{description}
Spurious outputs are permitted within the two-element limit. This does not
require exact pointwise endpoint soundness or polynomial-time proof discovery.

\begin{theorem}[Recovered conditional two-list implication]
Such a verifier implies $P\ne NP$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $n\ge2$, let $E_n=\bigcup_{|p|\le n-2}I_{n,p}$. Define
$E=\{x:|x|<2\}\cup\bigcup_{n\ge2}E_n$. The uniform polynomial bound $L$
gives $E\in NP$. There are $2^{n-1}-1$ eligible programs, hence
$|E_n|\le2^n-2$. Completeness implies
\[
 \{0,1\}^*\setminus E\subseteq
 R_{K_U^t}:=\{x:K_U^t(x)\ge |x|-1\}.
\]
Under $P=NP$, the complement of $E$ is an infinite P subset of this set,
contradicting Hirahara's immunity theorem with the same $U,t$ conventions
\cite[Theorem 3.9]{hirahara2020}.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{Open link CC-B.} Existence of $V$ for a suitable fixed $t$ remains
unproved. It is not supplied by hashes, short source code, implicit tableaux,
or tractable-class invariants. The measure is plain time-bounded $K^t$, not
Levin $Kt$. Proof discovery is unnecessary for membership in NP, but must be
charged if a constructive compiler later needs it.

\section{A certificate-length diagonalization boundary}
\begin{theorem}[Exhaustive certificate search obstruction]
Assume a fixed verifier $V$ with polynomially computable witness bound $L(n)$
and verification-time bound $q(n)$ satisfies global ambiguity. There is a
fixed program family $p_n$ of length $O(\log n)$ that outputs an $n$-bit
string outside $E_n$. On a fixed direct machine, its running time is bounded
by
\[
 B(n)=2^{2n+L(n)+O(1)}\operatorname{poly}(n+L(n))\max\{1,q(n)\}.
\]
Let $B_U(n)$ include the efficient universal simulation of this procedure.
For all sufficiently large $n$ with $t(n)\ge B_U(n)$, completeness fails.
Consequently, no polynomial-witness two-list verifier exists for
$t(n)=2^{2^n}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Enumerate $n$-bit strings $x$ lexicographically. For each $x$, enumerate all
$p$ of length at most $n-2$ and all $\pi$ of length at most $L(n)$, checking
$V$. Output the first $x$ with no accepted pair. Global ambiguity guarantees
at least two such strings. At most $2^n(2^{n-1}-1)(2^{L(n)+1}-1)$ verifier
calls suffice. Enumeration overhead yields $B(n)$.
The machine code is fixed and only $n$ needs to be self-delimitingly encoded,
so the resulting $U$ program has length $O(\log n)\le n-2$ for sufficiently
large $n$. If it terminates by $t(n)$, completeness puts its output inside
$E_n$, contradicting its construction. Efficient universal simulation gives
$B_U(n)=2^{\operatorname{poly}(n)}$ for polynomial $L,q$; this is eventually
less than $2^{2^n}$.
\end{proof}
\paragraph{Explicit exponent form.} If $L(n)\le n^a$, $q(n)\le n^b$, and
the fixed universal simulation is bounded by a constant times the $d$th
power of the direct running time, then
\[
 B_U(n)\le 2^{d n^a+2dn+O(\log n)}.
\]
Thus a proposed completeness bound $t(n)=2^{n^c}$ is impossible whenever
$c>\max\{a,1\}$. This is a necessary certificate-degree constraint in that
regime, not a converse existence theorem at equality or below.
\paragraph{Scope.} This refutes an overbroad ``all superpolynomial time
bounds'' certificate claim. It does not refute existence for a slower fixed
bound, such as $n^{\log n}$. A proposed verifier must declare the witness
degree and the decompression time together. The procedure is an elementary
diagonalization, not a SAT lower bound.

\section{Finite hash labels and provenance}
\begin{proposition}[Hash-label capacity]
An exact history identifier over an alphabet of size 62 with $d$ characters
can distinguish at most $62^d$ equivalence classes. Distinguishing $2^k$
classes requires $d\log_2 62\ge k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} Apply the pigeonhole principle to any injective class identifier.
\end{proof}
The attached 6- and 8-character FNV-1a tools have capacities at most
$35.7252$ and $47.6336$ bits. Extra histories or transcripts may enlarge the
state, but their representation and recovery costs must then be counted.
Collision resistance alone would not imply that a SAT decider must invert a
hash or retain every history.

\paragraph{Exact generator defect.} In the attached randomized generator,
let $s>0$ be cleaned seed length and let $r_0,\ldots,r_{49}$ be the random
pool. At iteration $i=0,\ldots,6$, the index $s+7i$ does not wrap. The $i$
previous deletions shift it to original position $s+8i$, so the output uses
$r_0,r_8,\ldots,r_{48}$ independently of seed contents and positive seed
length. The formatting adds an independent space placement. Thus the seed
does not encode source provenance in this implementation.

\paragraph{Finite experiment.} The companion executable evaluates the actual
HTML scripts. It reproduces identical outputs for distinct nonempty seeds
under identical draws, and an explicit 6-character collision. These finite
observations support the implementation audit, not asymptotic hardness.


\section{Witness acquisition versus existential certification}
The bounded-list PFCERT implication above uses existential witnesses only.
An executable wrapper that consumes a witness has a distinct acquisition
obligation.

\begin{theorem}[Exhaustive acquisition window]
Let \(V(1^n,p,x,\pi)\) have witness bound \(w(n)\), verification time
\(q(n)\), and verification space \(s(n)\). A lexicographic uniform acquirer
finds the first accepted witness, or correctly reports that none exists, in
\[
 O\!\left((2^{w(n)+1}-1)
 (q(n)+n+|p|+w(n)+1)\right)
\]
time and \(O(s(n)+n+|p|+w(n))\) writable bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are exactly \(2^{w+1}-1\) binary strings of length at most \(w\).
Enumerate them with a reusable counter and run \(V\). Only the counter,
current witness, verifier state and fixed inputs need be live together.
\end{proof}
This is an implementation upper bound, not a lower bound on every acquirer.
If \(q(N)=N^{v+o(1)}\) and
\(w(N)=\kappa\log_2N+o(\log N)\), this method has time exponent
\(\kappa+\max\{v,1\}\) under the displayed per-candidate copying charge.
The earlier shorthand \(v+\kappa\) is valid from that display only when
\(v\ge1\).  The following separate implementation recovers the sharper
bound in the declared shared-input random-access model.

\begin{theorem}[Reset-aware shared-input exhaustive acquirer]
Let the fixed input tuple \(y=(1^n,p,x)\) reside on read-only random-access
input tapes and let \(M=|y|\).  Suppose that, for a read-only candidate
witness \(\pi\) of length at most \(w(M)\), one invocation of \(V(y,\pi)\)
starts from a blank writable configuration, uses at most \(q(M)\) time and
\(s(M)\) writable bits, and visits at most \(b(M)\) writable cells, including
its input probes and address work.  Let \(r(M)\) bound restoration of that
workspace and finite control.  Then one deterministic lexicographic acquirer
finds the first accepted witness, or correctly reports that none exists, in
\[
 O\!\left((2^{w(M)+1}-1)
       (q(M)+r(M)+w(M)+\log(M+2))+w(M)\right)
\]
time and
\[
 O\bigl(s(M)+w(M)+\log(M+2)\bigr)
\]
writable bits.  The input tuple is not recopied between invocations.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Keep one \(w+1\)-bit counter/witness buffer and reuse one verifier workspace.
Before each counter value, restore every visited writable cell, the verifier's
finite control, and its \(O(\log M)\)-bit input addresses.  Every input symbol is read directly from
the shared read-only tapes, and its cost is already one of the \(q(M)\)
steps.  Incrementing and exposing the candidate costs \(O(w+\log M)\);
on success, copying the final witness costs \(O(w)\) once.  The buffers are
sequentially reused, so their sizes add rather than multiply by the number
of candidates.
\end{proof}
An explicit visited-cell list gives the conservative implementation
\[
 r(M)=O\!\left(b(M)\log(b(M)+2)\right).
\]
If \(b(M)\le q(M)\), \(q(M)=M^{v+o(1)}\), \(v\ge0\), and
\(w(M)=\kappa\log_2M+o(\log M)\), this implementation has time exponent
\(\kappa+v\) and writable-space exponent
\(\max\{\operatorname{sexp}(s),0\}\).  This proves the formerly open
shared-input interface C2a for an already explicit direct input.  The earlier
proof that reset only finite control and addresses is
\textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED as a blank-start proof}: a one-cell verifier can
make later invocations depend on dirty state.  It does not
materialize an implicit child, recover a source from a digest, or pay for a
nonconstant-cost bit accessor.  If one input bit costs \(M^{g+o(1)}\) to
regenerate, the verifier bound must include that access, giving the generic
tax \(v\mapsto v+g\).  If acquisition or verification invokes a current-scale
SAT solver of exponent \(K\), the resulting exponent is at least \(K\), so it
cannot eliminate \(K\).

Thus a near-linear executable constructor based on exhaustive acquisition
needs \(\kappa+v\le1\) in the shared-input model, together with compatible
space, output, replay and ancestor bounds.  Polynomial witness length is
harmless for the existential NP envelope but makes this generic deterministic
acquisition superpolynomial.  The random-access convention is model-matched
to the SAT time-space literature, where read-only input is excluded from
writable space while input-head addresses and reduction-bit computation are
charged \cite{flmv2005}.  It is an implementation theorem, not a universal
lower bound on all acquirers.

\section{The quasipolynomial window and pointwise binding}
Set \(t(n)=n^{\log_2 n}=2^{(\log_2 n)^2}\).  The two-list condition above is
extensional and global: it permits one spurious endpoint.  Replacing it by
pointwise endpoint soundness is not a harmless implementation repair.

\begin{theorem}[Pointwise quasipolynomial-window gate]
Let \(U\) be an efficiently universal deterministic machine with polynomial
simulation overhead.  Suppose one fixed deterministic polynomial-time
relation \(V\) and one polynomial \(L\) have the following property for every
\(n\), every program \(p\) of length at most \(n-2\), and every
\(x\in\{0,1\}^n\):
\[
 U(p)\text{ has final output }x\text{ within }t(n)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists\pi,\ |\pi|\le L(n),\ V(1^n,p,x,\pi)=1.
\]
Then, for every fixed \(c>0\),
\[
 \operatorname{DTIME}\!\left(2^{c(\log m)^2}\right)
 \subseteq NP\cap coNP.
\]
Consequently this premise implies \(P\ne NP\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a language \(A\) decided in time \(2^{c(\log m)^2}\), and let \(z\) have
length \(m\).  Choose a fixed integer \(a\) large enough to absorb the fixed
universal simulation degree, and put \(n=m^a\).  A program \(p_z\) of length
\(O(m+\log n)\le n-2\) simulates the decider for \(A(z)\) and writes
\(1^n\) on acceptance and \(0^n\) otherwise.  The choice of \(a\) makes this
computation finish within
\[
 t(n)=2^{a^2(\log m)^2}.
\]
The displayed equivalence gives an NP witness for \(z\in A\) by taking
\(x=1^n\), and an NP witness for \(z\notin A\) by taking \(x=0^n\).
The verifier inputs have polynomial length in \(m\), so \(A\in NP\cap coNP\).

If \(P=NP\), the inclusion would put every such quasipolynomial-time language
in \(P\).  Deterministic time hierarchy supplies a language in
\(\operatorname{DTIME}(n^{\log n})\setminus P\), a contradiction
\cite{hartmanisstearns1965}.
\end{proof}
This proof relativizes with a matched oracle machine and therefore does not
provide the missing nonrelativizing hinge.  It instead shows that pointwise
binding is already separation-strength.  The original two-list condition
escapes this argument precisely because a verifier may accept both
\(0^n\) and \(1^n\) for the same program.  A generic selector that chooses
one candidate is wrong for one possible true endpoint.

\paragraph{Checkpoint conservation.}
An explicitly expanded binary midpoint proof of a deterministic \(T\)-step
run, recursed down to one-step transitions, has exactly \(2T-1\) nodes when
\(T\) is a power of two.  Hence for \(T=t(n)\) its explicit witness is
superpolynomial.  Succinct tableaux, interactive proofs, algebraic
fingerprints, and probabilistic checking can avoid literal expansion only by
changing the verification model or adding a new exact soundness theorem.
Known doubly efficient interactive-proof results do not directly instantiate
this deterministic noninteractive relation \cite{cookrothblum2023,chenetal2026}.

\paragraph{PFCERT trilemma.}
At this window the alternatives are: (i) a globally two-valued extensional
cover, whose construction is \textbf{OPEN} and whose existence already
implies the desired separation through Hirahara's theorem; (ii) pointwise
endpoint binding, which the theorem above shows is itself
separation-strength; or (iii) randomness, interaction, or oracles, which
changes the target model and is a failed direct transfer.  Compact transcript
descriptions do not establish cheap acquisition, exact semantics, or an
original-SAT-scale executable wrapper.

The initial two-list diagonal extends to a polynomial list bound \(B(n)\).
Set
\[
 k(n)=n-\lceil\log_2B(n)\rceil-1.
\]
If \(V\) accepts at most \(B(n)\) distinct \(n\)-bit outputs for every
program of length at most \(k(n)\), globally over all witnesses, then a fixed
direct routine finds the first outside output within
\[
 O\!\left(2^n(2^{k(n)+1}-1)(2^{w(n)+1}-1)
 (q(n)+\operatorname{poly}(n+w(n)))\right).
\]
Its self-delimiting program has \(O(\log n)\) bits. Therefore completeness
is impossible whenever the declared decompression time eventually includes
the same universal machine's simulation of this routine. This does not rule
out a slower window such as \(n^{\log n}\).

\section{Exact semantic quotients and collision entropy}
\begin{theorem}[Finite-hash quotient criterion]
For finite \(D,Z,Y\), a semantic map \(f:D\to Y\) factors as \(f=g\circ h\)
through \(h:D\to Z\) if and only if every fiber of \(h\) is
\(f\)-monochromatic:
\[
 h(u)=h(v)\Longrightarrow f(u)=f(v).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Factorization implies the condition. Conversely, define \(g(z)\) to be the
common semantic value on every nonempty fiber and define it arbitrarily on
empty fibers.
\end{proof}
Thus a fixed \(b\)-bit hash on \(q>b\) input bits has a collision and fails
for some Boolean semantics that separates a colliding pair. A short hash can
still be exact for a particular problem, but proving fiber monochromaticity
is precisely the missing quotient theorem. For fiber sizes \(a_z\),
\[
 \operatorname{Coll}(h)=\sum_z a_z^2,\qquad
 |\operatorname{im}h|\ge |D|^2/\operatorname{Coll}(h).
\]
This collision-entropy identity is an exact object transfer. It supplies
neither semantic fiber purity nor cheap collision discovery. Cryptographic
collision resistance and random-oracle assumptions are not admissible
substitutes for exact semantics.

\section{Full compatibility rank is not an extraction cost}
CC-0005 adds a mandatory hostile control for EXTRACTOR/G2.  Four explicit
families---2-SAT equality, Horn implication, diagonal affine parity, and a
treewidth-one independent product---have $2^n\times2^n$ compatibility
matrices of rank $2^n$ over $\mathbb F_2$.  Equality and affine give identity
or permutation matrices; the Horn subset matrix is triangular with diagonal
one under an inclusion order; the independent product is $I_2^{\otimes n}$.

Despite full rank, their canonical source problems are decided with at most
$2n$ bit probes or $n$ diagonal checks.  Under indexed CNF encoding this is
$O(M)$ time and $O(\log M)$ writable space.  Explicitly constructing the
compatibility table takes $4^n$ predicate evaluations in the tested
implementation, but this is optional extraction work and is not charged to
the direct decider.  Duplicate padding, DEAD clauses, proper-three-CNF versus
2-CNF encoding, variable transformations, and independent products preserve
the semantic conclusion while changing source length or representation.

Thus compatibility rank, semantic type count, explicit table-acquisition
cost, and partition communication are \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED} as
stand-alone arbitrary-SAT time, space, or state-exposure resources.  A
membership-only oracle extractor also has identical transcripts for every
exact implementation, by induction on adaptive queries.  The surviving
\textbf{OPEN} target is a wrapper-covariant white-box relation
$\mathsf{WExt}(A,F,Q,\tau)$ binding $Q$ to the actual execution without
replaying all of $\tau$, together with a same-realization extractor-versus-
resource theorem.  P versus NP remains OPEN.

The bounded suite checked 349,524 source pairs through $n=9$, exact ranks
through 512, four direct algorithms, alternate encodings, padding, redundant
clauses, and DEAD/COPY/DELAY/INTERP/RECOMP ledgers in 0.54 seconds.  This is
\textbf{COMPUTATIONAL} validation only; the rank and upper-bound statements
have separate symbolic proofs in CC-0005.
