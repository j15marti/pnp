\paragraph{Status.}
This document formalizes elementary transfer lemmas, exact external
search-to-decision, and the project's existing conditional proof spine.
It does not prove $P\ne NP$.
The missing all-input construction is stated as an open hypothesis.
These lemmas are recovered or direct consequences of standard simulation,
not claimed new complexity results.

\section{Fixed model and semantics}

Fix a uniform deterministic random-access Turing machine model with
read-only original input and charged work tapes, including index tapes.
Every derived child buffer, cached formula, suspended frame and auxiliary
index counts as workspace. Let $N$ denote original encoded bit length.
All logarithmic addressing costs are absorbed only into explicitly allowed
$N^{o(1)}$ factors. Standard random-access conventions are as in
\cite{flmv}. Any different encoding must have a proved local simulation.

An ordinary language has a fixed membership predicate on finite strings.
A specification requiring an online interaction with an external random
stream, or a hash of one designated solver's execution, must first specify
its inputs, required outputs and computational resources. It is not
automatically the ordinary $\SAT$ language.

\section{Polynomial wrappers and their surviving exponent}

\begin{lemma}[MB1: direct-call wrapper closure]
Let $A$ be a fixed exact SAT decider with
$T_A(m)\le m^{K+o(1)}$ for a fixed $K\ge1$.
Let $W$ be a deterministic direct-call wrapper whose local work is at most
$N^{a+o(1)}$, and which makes at most $N^{b+o(1)}$ adaptive calls to $A$,
each on a formula of encoded length at most $N^{c+o(1)}$.
Assume query construction, output, scheduling and simulation overhead are
included in the local bound, apart from subpolynomial overhead per simulated
step. Then
\[
T_{W[A]}(N)\le
N^{\max\{a,b+cK\}+o(1)}.
\]
In particular, polynomial-time SAT implies polynomial-time execution of
this fixed polynomial wrapper.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
All oracle answers are computed sequentially by the fixed program $A$.
The sum of subroutine costs is bounded by the number of calls times the
worst permitted call cost:
\[
N^{b+o(1)}
 \bigl(N^{c+o(1)}\bigr)^{K+o(1)}
 =N^{b+cK+o(1)}.
\]
Add the local work. Adaptivity changes which queries occur, not their
assumed worst-case bounds.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope.}
This is an upper bound. It gives no lower bound for a wrapper's semantic
task and no automatic elimination of $K$. A current-scale call has $c=1$.
Requiring a specified implementation to print a long trace may make that
implementation expensive without making its SAT decision task expensive.

\section{Partial portfolios and exact fallback}

\begin{definition}
A sound partial SAT decider $B$ halts on every formula with output
$\mathsf{SAT}$, $\mathsf{UNSAT}$, or $\mathsf{UNKNOWN}$.
Every non-UNKNOWN output is correct.
Its unresolved domain is
\[
U_B=\{F:B(F)=\mathsf{UNKNOWN}\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[MB2: fallback composition]
Suppose $B$ has time $N^{r+o(1)}$ and space $N^{p+o(1)}$.
Suppose an exact solver $A$ for $U_B$ has time $N^{t+o(1)}$ and
space $N^{s+o(1)}$ on every input in that domain, using the same input
encoding and model. Run $B$ first; on UNKNOWN dispose of its work state
and run $A$ on the unchanged original input.
The result is an exact total SAT decider with
\[
T(N)\le N^{\max\{r,t\}+o(1)},\qquad
S(N)\le N^{\max\{p,s\}+o(1)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A non-UNKNOWN answer is correct by soundness. Otherwise the input is
in $U_B$, where $A$ is exact. Both computations terminate. Sequential
execution adds time; disposing of preliminary state before invoking $A$
makes peak workspace the maximum, up to addressing and control overhead.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Limit of the lemma.}
The displayed envelope by itself supplies no strict improvement in $t$.
Such improvement requires additional facts about the residual domain or a
different exact algorithm for it. Finite coverage growth is not an
asymptotic theorem about that domain.

\section{A precise local reduction obligation}

\begin{lemma}[MB3: virtual-input resource transfer]
Let $R$ map every source instance $x$ of size $N$ into a promise domain $U$
and preserve its Boolean answer. Suppose $|R(x)|\le N^{\lambda+o(1)}$,
where $\lambda>0$. Assume one uniform procedure obtains the output length
and setup data in time $N^{r+o(1)}$ using space $N^{\beta+o(1)}$.
Assume every requested output bit is computable from the original $x$
and charged setup data in time $N^{\alpha+o(1)}$ and additional
space $N^{\beta+o(1)}$, uniformly for all valid bit addresses.
All setup data retained during simulation are included in this space bound.

If a uniform exact solver for $U$ uses time $m^{t+o(1)}$ and
space $m^{s+o(1)}$, then its simulation decides the source language in
\[
T(N)\le
N^{\max\{r,\lambda t+\alpha\}+o(1)},\qquad
S(N)\le
N^{\max\{\beta,\lambda s\}+o(1)}.
\]
Here $\alpha,\beta,r,s\ge0$ and $t\ge1$; logarithmic costs are included.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Simulate the solver without materializing $R(x)$. Its ordinary internal
steps are simulated directly. Whenever it reads its virtual input,
call the accessor for that bit. There are at most
$N^{\lambda t+o(1)}$ simulated steps and hence at most that many accesses.
Multiplying by the per-access bound and adding setup proves the time
claim. The solver work tapes and the accessor/setup workspace coexist;
their sum has the stated maximum exponent. The accessor is deterministic
and returns the correct bit, so induction on simulated steps proves
correctness. The image lies in the solver's promise domain.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Consequence for the current shield.}
If a reduction into an UNKNOWN domain has $\lambda=1$, $\alpha=0$,
$r\le1$, and $\beta=0$, it preserves all jointly achievable exponents
with $t\ge1,s\ge0$, up to the allowed subpolynomial factors.
Restriction gives the reverse direction when $U$ is a subdomain of SAT.
This is the precise transfer underlying the project's proposed equality
of achievable regions. It remains necessary to verify the concrete
shield, its bit accessor, and its image-membership theorem.
A sequential emitter alone does not satisfy the accessor premise.

\section{Universal external extraction: exact positive boundary}

Let $F$ be a canonical exact 3-CNF on $n$ variables and $m$ clauses;
repeated literals are permitted.  Split the variables into $X,Y$ and define
the semantic compatibility matrix
\[
 H(F)_{u,v}=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F(u,v)=1.
\]
Use the encoding $\gamma(n)\gamma(m+1)$ followed by three signed,
fixed-width literals per clause.  With
$w=\max\{1,\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil\}$ and
$\ell_\gamma(q)=2\lfloor\log_2q\rfloor+1$, its length is
\[
 N=\ell_\gamma(n)+\ell_\gamma(m+1)+3m(1+w).
\]

\begin{theorem}[MB4 / EDGE-EXTRACT1]
For every total uniform deterministic exact 3-SAT decider $A$, one uniform
external extractor $E_A$ finds one satisfying assignment or returns UNSAT.
It makes at most $n+1$ calls to $A$, and every call has length at most
\[
 L=N+\ell_\gamma(m+n+1)-\ell_\gamma(m+1)+3n(1+w).
\]
If $A$ uses time $T(q)$ and space $S(q)$, materialized queries give
\[
 T_{E_A}=O(n(T(L)+L)+N),\qquad S_{E_A}=O(S(L)+L+n).
\]
On the declared read-only random-access model, a local virtual-input
simulation gives
\[
 T_{E_A}=O(nT(L)\log L+nL),\qquad
 S_{E_A}=O(S(L)+n+\log L).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First call $A(F)$.  If it answers UNSAT, stop.  Otherwise process the
variables in order.  At stage $i$, append repeated-literal 3-clauses pinning
the previously chosen prefix and one candidate clause pinning $z_i=0$.
Keep zero exactly when this query is satisfiable; otherwise keep one.
Induction shows that the chosen prefix always extends to a satisfying
assignment.  The length formula counts the at most $n$ appended clauses.
Materialization proves the first ledger.  For the second, simulate each call
while computing queried header, original-clause, or pin bits from the
read-only source and the retained prefix.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact limitation.}
MB4 extracts one nonzero entry of $H(F)$ by invoking $A$ on new inputs.  It
does not show that the original execution $A(F)$ materializes $H(F)$, its
rows, rank, residual DAG, OBDD order, d-DNNF, or provenance.  Charging the
extractor's calls to that one execution changes the measured realization.
Equality, Horn, affine and treewidth-one controls, plus DEAD/COPY/DELAY/
RECOMP wrappers, falsify free promotion from external witness recovery to a
preferred internal representation.

\begin{hypothesis}[CORE-ACQUIRE1, open]
For one explicit infinite clean-core family, give a solver-independent
same-execution transformation that maps every uniform deterministic 3-SAT
decider to one fixed lower-bound interface, charging compilation, access,
replay, output, retained and transient space, and preserving the lower-bound
direction strongly enough to bound the original decider.
\end{hypothesis}

Under $P=NP$, MB4 yields polynomial-time witness search.  It gives no
contradiction.  It relativizes and supplies no nonalgebrizing hinge.
P versus NP remains OPEN.

\section{The existing conditional proof spine}

\begin{definition}[Shrinking contract $\mathcal W(h,p)$]
For fixed $0<h<1$ and $0\le p<1$, one uniform exact resumable wrapper works
on every input and every recursive descendant. At size $N$, it performs
$N^{1+o(1)}$ local work, makes at most $N^{1-h+o(1)}$ recursive calls
of size at most $N^{h+o(1)}$, and suspends using $N^{p+o(1)}$ workspace
besides the current child buffer. Children are sequential.
Discovery, failed attempts, access, replay, exact certification, ancestor
state and all derived buffers are charged. Sizes strictly decrease above
a fixed finite base threshold. One common error envelope tends to zero
uniformly on the domain.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Recovered composition]
The contract yields one uniform exact SAT decider with
\[
T(N)\le N^{1+o(1)},\qquad
S(N)\le N^{\max\{h,p\}+o(1)}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any fixed $\delta>0$, choose $\epsilon>0$ with
$h+\epsilon<1$ and
$\epsilon(2+\delta)<\delta(1-h)$.
Above a fixed threshold, all error exponents are at most $\epsilon$.
Inductively bounding smaller calls by $Cm^{1+\delta}$ gives total child
time at most
\[
C N^{1-h+\epsilon+(h+\epsilon)(1+\delta)}
=C N^{1+h\delta+\epsilon(2+\delta)},
\]
strictly below exponent $1+\delta$. Local work has exponent
$1+\epsilon<1+\delta$ if necessary after reducing $\epsilon$.
Increasing $C$ covers finite exceptions and establishes the induction.
The same algorithm works for every $\delta$.

A depth-first path has $O(\log\log N)$ large levels. At each level the
charged suspension and child-buffer space is at most
$N^{\max(h,p)+\epsilon}$ measured in root scale.
Their sum, including ancestor-held buffers, has exponent
$\max(h,p)+o(1)$. Correctness and termination follow by induction on
strictly decreasing size.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Published endpoint.}
By FLMV's local SAT reduction and time-space lower bounds,
near-linear SAT time together with a fixed sublinear workspace power
is excluded on the matched model \cite{flmv}.
For the finite pair used in current main4/main5, let
\[
c=\frac{1201}{1000},\qquad
d(c)=\frac12\left(\frac{c+2}{c^2}-c\right)
=\frac12+\frac{26275399}{2884802000}.
\]
The strict inequalities $6/5<c$ and $1/2<d(c)$ absorb the local reduction's
polylogarithmic overhead. This excludes the joint pair $(6/5,1/2)$.
It does not exclude unrestricted polynomial-time SAT.

\begin{hypothesis}[Open bridge $\mathsf B$]
If SAT has a uniform polynomial-time decider, then there exist fixed
$h,p<1$ and one all-input wrapper satisfying $\mathcal W(h,p)$.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional separation]
If $\mathsf B$ is proved on the matched model and encoding, then $P\ne NP$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume $P=NP$. Then SAT is in P. By $\mathsf B$, the shrinking contract
exists. Composition gives a near-linear-time, sublinear-power-space SAT
decider, contradicting the published endpoint.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Logical status.}
Since the all-input contract is already excluded by the endpoint,
$\mathsf B$ is itself equivalent in classical logic to $P\ne NP$.
The reverse implication is vacuous when SAT is not in P.
A substantive source-level construction establishing $\mathsf B$ would
therefore carry the separation, not finish a routine bookkeeping step.

\section{Quantifier and output guards}

\begin{description}
\item[] An existential witness may vary with the implementation:
$\forall A\,\exists I\,R(A,I)$ does not require one identical $I$ across
wrappers. A counterexample must exhibit an admissible implementation with
no qualifying witness at all.
\item[] A membership-preserving map from SAT to a wrapper language is the
wrong direction for transferring a lower bound of that wrapper back to
SAT. The needed simulation must solve the wrapper using SAT with controlled
overhead, or supply another explicitly proved bridge.
\item[] If the sole required answer is $\SAT(F)$, provenance history can
be discarded unless the proof establishes an independently necessary
future service. Requiring a larger output changes the problem.
\item[] A finite hash is not injective on an unbounded history domain.
Exact collision resolution belongs in the merge ledger. Hash length is
not input length or a lower-bound exponent.
\item[] Closure and accounting facts alone do not identify the missing
ordinary-machine premise that fails under relativization. The
Baker--Gill--Solovay theorem requires this barrier to be addressed by a
complete separation argument \cite{bgs}.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Next proof obligation.}
Give a source-checkable all-input rule, or an all-input ARC transition,
whose uncovered fallback also contracts with verified physical access and
joint workspace bounds. Until then the construction remains OPEN.
