\begin{abstract}
The missing clean-core transfer is narrowed by one exact positive leg and one
exact negative interface theorem.  After correcting an occurrence-label typo
in Darmann--D\"ocker's Theorem~5 construction, their gadget gives a
linear-size, bit-local reduction from \emph{incidence-tagged} Monotone
3-Sat-$(3,3)$ to Monotone 3-Sat*-${}(2,2)$: $n$ source variables and $2n$
source clauses become $15n$ variables and $20n$ clauses, and every target bit
is computable with $O(1)$ source-field reads, polylogarithmic time and
$O(\log N)$ workspace.  The printed mapping assigns positive occurrences to
$x_{i,1},x_{i,2},x_{i,3}$ and negative occurrences to
$\bar x_{i,2},\bar x_{i,4},\bar x_{i,6}$; taken literally it gives
$x_{i,2}$ occurrence profile $(3,2)$ and $x_{i,5}$ profile $(1,2)$, not
$(2,2)$.  The unique parity-respecting repair uses odd clones for positive
occurrences and even clones for negative occurrences.

The tags are load-bearing.  On an unindexed clause stream, even deciding the
occurrence rank of a literal at a fixed middle position has deterministic
query complexity $\Omega(L)$: two streams can agree on every probed field but
place the remaining matching occurrence on opposite sides of the position.
Thus the naive claim that occurrence splitting/cyclic equality is already a
polylogarithmic-bit-access reduction is false.  This does not rule out another
reduction or structured FLMV output.  The remaining target is now the exact
OPEN arrow from the FLMV source to tagged Monotone 3-Sat-$(3,3)$, or a direct
construction that locally supplies equivalent incidence information.  P
versus NP remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Primary-literature and barrier preflight}

The two obstacles are (i) preserving exact occurrence balance while keeping
the reduction locally addressable, and (ii) obtaining occurrence rank or
successor information without a global scan or an uncharged index.

FLMV explain the relevant simultaneous time--space issue exactly: a reduced
SAT instance cannot be stored in the allowed workspace, so every requested
output bit must be recomputed on demand in polylogarithmic time and
logarithmic space; their Theorem~3.1 supplies the quantitative transfer only
after that interface exists \cite{flmv}.  Darmann--D\"ocker Theorem~5 gives
the fixed equality construction from Monotone 3-Sat-$(3,3)$ to the star
$(2,2)$ variant, but its prose assigns appearances by their first, second,
and third positions \cite{dd}.  D\"ocker's later paper uses the star problem
as the source for the 99-variable/133-clause enforcer leg already localized
by MODELBRIDGE-0012 \cite{doecker}.  Tovey and
Berman--Karpinski--Scott establish bounded-occurrence hardness, but their
qualitative statements alone do not provide FLMV target-bit access
\cite{tovey,bks}.

The closest exact cross-domain mechanism is a rank/select index: succinct
indexable dictionaries support rank and select rapidly because an auxiliary
representation is stored \cite{rrr}.  This is a \textbf{direct theorem
transfer} only for the data-structure interface.  It is an
\textbf{analogy/candidate} for the reduction because the index's construction,
storage and local availability must be charged.  Treating it as free advice
is a \textbf{failed transfer}.  VLSI routing, distributed cuts, pebbling,
observability and proof-space bounds remain restricted-model analogies and
do not force arbitrary SAT execution state.

\section{Corrected tagged fixed-block theorem}

\begin{definition}[Tagged source]
$\Mthree$ contains monotone proper 3-CNFs in which every variable has exactly
three positive and three negative occurrences.  Each literal record carries
a tag in $\{1,2,3\}$, and for each variable/sign the three tags occur exactly
once.  The header gives $(n,m)$; records use fixed-width identifiers and tags.
Because $6n=3m$, every valid source has $m=2n$.
\end{definition}

Let $x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,6}$ and $y_{i,1},\ldots,y_{i,9}$ be fresh target
variables for source variable $x_i$.  Replace positive tag $t$ by
$x_{i,2t-1}$ and negative tag $t$ by $\bar x_{i,2t}$.  Add the following
18 monotone clauses for each $i$:
\begin{align*}
&(x_{i,1}\vee y_{i,9}\vee y_{i,9}),
 (\bar x_{i,1}\vee\bar y_{i,1}\vee\bar y_{i,1}),
 (\bar x_{i,1}\vee\bar y_{i,2}\vee\bar y_{i,2}),\\
&(x_{i,2}\vee y_{i,1}\vee y_{i,1}),
 (x_{i,2}\vee y_{i,2}\vee y_{i,2}),
 (\bar x_{i,2}\vee\bar y_{i,3}\vee\bar y_{i,3}),\\
&(x_{i,3}\vee y_{i,3}\vee y_{i,3}),
 (\bar x_{i,3}\vee\bar y_{i,4}\vee\bar y_{i,4}),
 (\bar x_{i,3}\vee\bar y_{i,5}\vee\bar y_{i,5}),\\
&(x_{i,4}\vee y_{i,4}\vee y_{i,4}),
 (x_{i,4}\vee y_{i,5}\vee y_{i,5}),
 (\bar x_{i,4}\vee\bar y_{i,6}\vee\bar y_{i,6}),\\
&(x_{i,5}\vee y_{i,6}\vee y_{i,6}),
 (\bar x_{i,5}\vee\bar y_{i,7}\vee\bar y_{i,7}),
 (\bar x_{i,5}\vee\bar y_{i,8}\vee\bar y_{i,8}),\\
&(x_{i,6}\vee y_{i,7}\vee y_{i,7}),
 (x_{i,6}\vee y_{i,8}\vee y_{i,8}),
 (\bar x_{i,6}\vee\bar y_{i,9}\vee\bar y_{i,9}).
\end{align*}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{DD-THEOREM5-ODD-EVEN-REPAIR1}]
The preceding 18-clause block extends an assignment to
$x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,6}$ if and only if all six $x$-values are equal.  With
the odd-positive/even-negative occurrence map, every $x_{i,s}$ and every
$y_{i,t}$ occurs exactly twice positively and twice negatively.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The implication cycle exhibited by Darmann--D\"ocker forces all six $x$
variables equal.  If they are all true, setting all nine $y$ variables false
satisfies the block; complementing this assignment handles the all-false
case.  Direct occurrence counting in the displayed clauses gives internal
profiles $(1,2)$ for odd $x$-indices and $(2,1)$ for even $x$-indices.
Their one source occurrences add respectively one positive and one negative
appearance, yielding $(2,2)$.  Every duplicated $y$ literal occurs in one
positive and one negative clause, twice in each, also yielding $(2,2)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{TAGGED-DD22STAR-LOCALIZER1}]
There is one uniform deterministic map
\[
 R:\Mthree\longrightarrow\Mstar
\]
such that for every valid source encoding of length $N$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item $F$ is satisfiable if and only if $R(F)$ is satisfiable;
\item if $F$ has $n$ variables and $m=2n$ clauses, then $R(F)$ has
$n'=15n$ variables and $m'=m+18n=20n$ clauses, hence encoded length
$\Theta(N)$;
\item any valid target bit is returned in $\log^{O(1)}N$ time and
$O(\log N)$ workspace using $O(1)$ source-field reads.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Replace each source literal by the tagged clone above and append one fixed
18-clause block per source variable.  The preceding theorem makes all clones
of one source variable equal, so satisfying assignments restrict and extend
exactly.  Its occurrence ledger proves membership in $\Mstar$.

For local access, place the $m$ transformed source clauses first and the
$18n$ gadget clauses second.  A source-clause address reads its three source
identifier/sign/tag fields and computes $2t-1$ or $2t$ by constant arithmetic.
A gadget address uses $\operatorname{divmod}(j-m,18)$ to recover the source
variable and template row, then emits fixed signs and identifiers.  Header,
address and identifier arithmetic use $O(\log N)$ bits.  No prefix sums,
sorting, full target, or incidence table is materialized.
\end{proof}

\section{Raw occurrence-index lower bound}

\begin{definition}[Raw occurrence-rank problem]
An input is a random-access array $A[0],\ldots,A[L-1]$ of literal identifiers
and a position $p$.  The required answer is
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_A(p)=
 1+|\{q<p:A[q]=A[p]\}|.
\]
Only field probes reveal identifiers; no precomputed index is supplied.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{RAW-OCCURRENCE-INDEX-BARRIER1}]
For infinitely many $L$, every deterministic exact algorithm for raw
occurrence rank makes $\Omega(L)$ field probes on some input even when the
distinguished identifier occurs exactly three times.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $p=\lfloor L/2\rfloor$, fix $A[p]=a$, and fix another $a$ at $L-1$.
Answer ``not $a$'' to every other probe.  If fewer than
\[
 \min\{p,L-p-2\}=\Omega(L)
\]
fields are probed, an unprobed position remains on both sides of $p$.
Complete one consistent input by placing the third $a$ at the unprobed left
position, and another by placing it at the unprobed right position; fill the
remaining fields with identifiers unequal to $a$.  The transcripts are
identical, but the rank at $p$ is respectively two and one.  The algorithm
errs on one completion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Naive untagged localizer fails]
Any target accessor that labels an occurrence by scanning the raw clause
stream for its ordinal, or that reconstructs a cyclic equality edge by raw
successor search, has worst-case $\Omega(L)$ access cost.  It therefore does
not establish the polylogarithmic-access part of
\texttt{LOCAL-COOK-MONOTONE22STAR1}.
\end{corollary}

This is a lower bound on the declared locator subroutine, not on all
reductions and not on SAT.  An upstream generator may emit valid tags from
its coordinates, or an auxiliary incidence index may be constructed and
charged.  Those possibilities are precisely the remaining OPEN interface.

\section{Hostile controls and arbitrary-algorithm audit}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Clause permutation and padding can move a matching occurrence
arbitrarily far, realizing the raw locator adversary.  Variable renaming does
not help.  Explicit tags neutralize these controls only because the tag is a
charged input field.
\item Horn, dual-Horn, 2SAT, affine/Tseitin Gaussian elimination,
bounded-treewidth formulas and independent products remain polynomial
semantic shortcuts.  The theorem is an external syntactic reduction and
makes no claim that any decider exposes its gadgets.
\item DIRECT, COPY, DELAY, INTERP, RECOMP and DEAD-code wrappers do not alter
the source-defined target bits.  They continue to refute internal-state
necessity claims.
\item The output is one decision bit.  No witness, compatibility matrix,
trace, proof or occurrence index is required from the target decider.
\item Assuming $P=NP$ supplies a polynomial fallback and search-to-decision;
it does not make the tags free and does not force an arbitrary solver to
materialize $H(F)$ or any chosen state.
\end{itemize}

The external reduction relativizes and algebrizes when its source and target
machines receive the same oracle.  The raw-query adversary also survives
oracle forwarding unless the oracle is explicitly given the occurrence
index, in which case the failed proof line is exactly the no-index premise.
Neither result crosses Baker--Gill--Solovay or Aaronson--Wigderson, and no
Natural-Proofs circuit property is constructed.

\paragraph{Post-quantum technique ledger.}
Schur squares, quadratic relation spaces, Hilbert functions, syzygies,
collision entropy and special-soundness extraction are \textbf{FAILED
TRANSFER} for this locator: there is no code or collision map in the exact
object.  The LWE lesson of building an external reduction rather than forcing
internal materialization is an \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}; it motivated the
tagged interface but supplies no NP worst-case/average-case theorem.  Hashes
and random oracles cannot replace exact occurrence tags.
