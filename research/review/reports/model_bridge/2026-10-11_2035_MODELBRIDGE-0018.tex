\section{Claim card and exact model}

\paragraph{Status.}
\texttt{CONDITIONAL-WITNESS-COMPLEXITY-EQUIV1}: \EXACT.
\texttt{PLAIN-WITNESS-INCOMPRESSIBILITY1}: \RETIRED.
\texttt{POLYTIME-WITNESS-INCOMPRESSIBILITY-AS-BRIDGE1}: \RETIRED\ as an intermediate route.
\texttt{WORSTCASE-IMPLICIT-PREDECISION-ACCESS1}: \OPEN.

Fix an efficient universal deterministic machine $U$.  Let $C$ be a polynomial-time recognizable language of encoded $3$-CNF instances such that the satisfiability problem restricted to $C$ is NP-hard under uniform polynomial-time many-one reductions.  A witness is an ordinary assignment to the variables of the encoded formula.  If $N=|\langle F\rangle|$, define
\[
K_U^t(w\mid F)=\min\{|p|: U(p,F)\text{ outputs }w\text{ within }t(N)\text{ steps}\}.
\]
The exponent in $t$ includes the fixed universal-simulation overhead.  Programs, the machine $U$, and the bound below are uniform and chosen before $F$; the formula itself is free conditional input.

\begin{theorem}[source-relative easy-witness equivalence]
The following statements are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\SAT\in\mathsf P$.
\item There exist a polynomial $t$ and a constant $c$ such that every satisfiable $F\in C$ has a satisfying assignment $w$ with $K_U^t(w\mid F)\le c$.
\item There exist a polynomial $t$ and a constant $a$ such that every satisfiable $F\in C$ has a satisfying assignment $w$ with $K_U^t(w\mid F)\le a\log N+a$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume (i), and fix a polynomial-time SAT decider $D$.  One fixed program, with $F$ as conditional input, performs standard SAT self-reduction: process variables in order, query $D$ on the restriction setting the next variable to zero, retain that branch when satisfiable, and otherwise retain the one-branch.  It makes at most $n+1$ calls, outputs the lexicographically first satisfying assignment, and runs in $N^{O(1)}$ time.  Its description length is a constant independent of $F$.  Thus (i) implies (ii), and (ii) implies (iii).

Assume (iii).  On input $F\in C$, enumerate all programs of length at most $a\log N+a$, simulate each for $t(N)$ steps, and accept if any output verifies as a satisfying assignment.  There are at most $2^{a+1}N^a$ programs; simulation and verification are polynomial.  Unsatisfiable instances admit no accepted output.  Hence restricted satisfiability on $C$ is in $\mathsf P$, and the assumed uniform reduction from SAT to $C$ gives (i).
\end{proof}

The theorem is invariant under any polynomially related reasonable encoding: $O(\log N)$ changes by at most a constant factor.  It also matches the one-bit output problem; witnesses are generated only inside the proof of an equivalent decider and are not asserted to occur in every implementation.

\section{Why the proposed bridge collapses}

There are two distinct collapse mechanisms.

\paragraph{Unbounded descriptions.}
Plain conditional Kolmogorov complexity is already constant on every satisfiable formula.  A fixed program can enumerate assignments lexicographically until it finds one.  The time may be exponential, but the program length is $O(1)$.  Thus a claim that clean-core witnesses have growing $K(w\mid F)$ is false independently of P versus NP.

\paragraph{Polynomial-time descriptions.}
A claim excluding every $O(\log N)$ polynomial-time conditional description on some satisfiable core instance is, by the theorem, precisely the negation of an equivalent formulation of $\SAT\in\mathsf P$.  Writing the exponent explicitly, a separation-strength form is
\[
\forall d\,\forall a\,\exists F\in C_{\mathrm{sat}}\,\forall w\models F:
K_U^{N^d}(w\mid F)>a\log N+a,
\]
with routine slack for universal-machine overhead.  This is a valid target, but it is not a derived compatibility or extraction lemma.  Proving it unconditionally proves $\mathsf P\ne\mathsf{NP}$.

Absolute complexity $K^t(w)$ does not repair the route: the computation receives $F$, so charging a description that is forbidden to use the actual input is representation-dependent and model-mismatched.  Likewise, choosing one exponent $d$ before an arbitrary polynomial SAT algorithm silently excludes algorithms with larger exponents.  Under the counterfactual $\SAT\in\mathsf P$, the self-reduction inherits that algorithm's exponent plus polynomial overhead.

\section{Bounded computational audit}

The reproducible audit is \texttt{experiments/review/witness\_incompressibility\_quantifier\_audit.py}; its verified output is \texttt{research/review/results/model\_bridge/MODELBRIDGE-0018-witness-audit.json}.  It completed in 0.429 seconds with 25,875 checks and no failures.  It exhaustively enumerated the 256 subformulas of the eight proper clauses on three variables, identified 255 satisfiable and one unsatisfiable formula, ran self-reduction with 1,021 total decision-oracle calls, and checked all 48 signed variable isomorphisms per formula.

Hostile controls included duplicate-clause padding, all six wrapper implementations \texttt{DIRECT/COPY/DEAD/DELAY/INTERP/RECOMP}, and paired $K_{3,3}$ products through 32 blocks (288 variables).  The audit also records polynomial semantic shortcuts for 2SAT (SCC), Horn SAT (forward chaining), affine/Tseitin systems (Gaussian elimination), width-one formulas (dynamic programming), and independent products (componentwise solution).  Candidate assignment counts reached $2^{48}$ for the product control and $2^{32}$ for the bounded-treewidth control while the designated outputs remained succinctly generated.  These are finite falsification controls only; they do not establish an asymptotic lower bound.

\section{SAME\_REALIZATION and arbitrary-algorithm audit}

The forward implication constructs one witness-finding implementation from a hypothetical decider.  It does \emph{not} infer that the original one-bit decider exposes a witness, a compatibility graph, a restriction, a transcript, or any named semantic state.  Thus SAME\_REALIZATION is respected: the result concerns existence of a uniform program relative to the input, not an internal state of every equivalent program.  COPY, delay, dead-work, recomputation, and interpreter wrappers preserve the accepted language while altering internal traces, so trace-based necessities remain unproved.

The exact remaining bridge would have to show that black-box access to every polynomial SAT decider yields, in the same exponent-uniform model and with all oracle calls charged, a forbidden object covered by an unconditional lower bound.  No such transfer theorem was found.

The theorem relativizes when the same oracle is supplied to the SAT decider and to the conditional program, so it offers no route around the Baker--Gill--Solovay barrier.  It also supplies no nonalgebrizing hinge.  Razborov--Rudich natural-proofs conditions are not directly triggered because no large constructive circuit property is proved; any promotion to circuit lower bounds would require a fresh audit.  Nonuniform advice is excluded: allowing a different program per input destroys the uniform quantifiers, while polynomial advice per length does not yield the uniform conclusion.

\section{Post-quantum technique ledger}

Schur squares, quadratic-relation rank spectra, and Hilbert/syzygy invariants have no exact object here because no code representation is forced; transfer is \textbf{FAILED}.  Collision entropy of the map from short programs to verified witnesses is an \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}: enumeration proves the easy direction, but a collision bound does not force computation by an arbitrary decider.  LWE-style worst-case/average-case extraction is an \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}; it lacks a black-box reduction from every SAT decider to a charged clean-core resource and faces known worst-case/average-case barriers for NP.  Special-soundness/collision extraction is also an \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}: no construction-specific theorem turns two openings into an unconditional forbidden witness.  Random-oracle or cryptographic binding assumptions are inadmissible here.

\section{DUPLICATION / CONTRADICTION AUDIT}

The corpus already recorded the weaker fact that $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$ puts NP witness search in polynomial time.  The new contribution is the exact two-way characterization by $O(\log N)$ source-relative time-bounded descriptions and the resulting retirement of witness incompressibility as an intermediate bridge. \section{STALE OR OVERSTATED CLAIMS FOUND}

Any text suggesting that large witness volume or lack of a canonical witness forces a polynomial SAT decider to expose, store, or reconstruct a witness is overstated without a transfer theorem.  Any fixed-exponent lower-bound target not quantified against the solver's arbitrary polynomial exponent is also too weak or circular.  These statements should be read as candidates only.

\section{Highest-value handoff}

Do not seek hardness from conditional witness description length alone.  The next admissible target is a genuinely external, black-box extraction theorem:
\[
\forall D\,[D\text{ is a uniform polynomial SAT decider}]
\Longrightarrow
\mathcal E^D(F)\text{ produces a model-matched object }R(F)
\]
with total time, query count, representation size, and the exponent of $D$ charged, followed by an unconditional lower bound for the \emph{same} object and model.  Whether any such $R$ exists remains \OPEN.
