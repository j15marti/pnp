\begin{abstract}
This run tested a precise candidate bridge: every polynomial-time SAT decider
must expose, before its final decision, one object from a fixed portfolio of
tractable recognizers, decompositions, small autarkies, small backdoors, or
symmetry rules.  The claim fails at the required quantifier.  A partial exact
controller $P$ can return a correct bit or $\bot$; for any total SAT decider
$B$, the wrapper ``use $P$, otherwise use $B$'' is total, exact, uniform, and
polynomial whenever $B$ is.  On an input where $P$ returns $\bot$, correctness
of the wrapper supplies no successful portfolio transcript.  A bounded audit
of an explicit eight-rule portfolio found 26 noncovered instances among 36
seeded Monotone $3$-SAT-$(2,2)$ clean cores, while easy Horn, 2-CNF,
decomposable, and affine controls behaved as expected.  Noncoverage is not
hardness, and the experiment is not an asymptotic theorem.  The result blocks
only a source-local fixed-portfolio transfer; it does not prove that an
arbitrary SAT computation avoids every possible semantic object.  The missing
arrow remains an all-input, representation-invariant extraction theorem with
fully charged acquisition cost.  P versus NP remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Encoding, clean core, and semantic compatibility}

The input is a canonical indexed 3-CNF.  Its bit length $N$ charges clause
delimiters, literal signs, and $\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$-bit variable indices.
Let $\Htwo$ be the duplicate-free family in which every clause contains three
distinct literals of one sign and every variable occurs exactly twice
positively and twice negatively.  Thus $m=4n/3$ and
$N=\Theta(n\log n)$.

For a declared split $V(F)=L\mathbin{\dot\cup}R$, define the complete semantic
compatibility relation
\[
 H_{F,L}(a,b)=1 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (a,b)\models F,
 \qquad a\in\{0,1\}^{L},\ b\in\{0,1\}^{R}.                 \tag{1}
\]
A decision algorithm need output only
$\bigvee_{a,b}H_{F,L}(a,b)$, one bit.  It is not required by the problem
specification to materialize (1), a satisfying assignment, a cut table, an
autarky, a backdoor, or any intermediate certificate.  A transfer theorem
must therefore derive its requested object from the charged execution of
every decider; semantic state count alone is not runtime.

The desired lower-bound target is still
\[
 \forall A\ \forall d\ \forall N_0\ \exists F\in\Htwo:
 |\operatorname{enc}(F)|\ge N_0\ \land\
 T_A(F)>|\operatorname{enc}(F)|^d,                          \tag{2}
\]
where $A$ ranges over total correct uniform deterministic deciders.  Statement
(2) is \OPEN and is not used as a premise.

\section{Candidate and exact wrapper theorem}

\begin{definition}[Polynomial partial controller]
A deterministic uniform procedure $P$ maps an encoded formula to
$\{0,1,\bot\}$ in time $N^{d_P+o(1)}$.  It is sound if every Boolean answer
equals $\SAT(F)$.  Its success domain is
$D_P=\{F:P(F)\ne\bot\}$.  A successful transcript contains the declared
portfolio rule, all acquired data, and the returned bit; a $\bot$ trace is not
a successful extraction.
\end{definition}

The explicit portfolio audited below lets $P$ succeed when it detects at
least one of: unit/pure simplification, disconnected incidence components,
Horn, dual-Horn, 2-CNF, an autarky supported on at most two variables, a
strong backdoor of size at most two to the union of Horn/dual-Horn/2-CNF, or
identical signed incidence signatures.  Each declared test is polynomial;
the constant support bound is part of the rule.  Affine XOR systems are kept
as a hostile control because Gaussian elimination is a different
representation and must not be smuggled into an ordinary-CNF recognizer.

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{PARTIAL-CONTROLLER-WRAPPER1}]
Let $P$ be any sound polynomial partial controller, and let $B$ be any total
correct uniform deterministic SAT decider.  Define
\[
 A_{P,B}(F)=
 \begin{cases}
 P(F),&P(F)\in\{0,1\},\\
 B(F),&P(F)=\bot.
 \end{cases}                                                \tag{3}
\]
Then $A_{P,B}$ is total, correct, and uniform.  If
$T_B(N)=N^{c_B+o(1)}$, then
\[
 T_{A_{P,B}}(N)=N^{\max\{d_P,c_B\}+o(1)}.                  \tag{4}
\]
For every $F\notin D_P$, the source-level $P$ phase of this realization has no
successful portfolio transcript.  Hence $\SAT\in\mathsf P$ does not by itself
imply that $P$ covers all inputs or succeeds on every member of $\Htwo$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Run $P$ and inspect its three-valued output.  Soundness proves correctness on
$D_P$, and correctness of $B$ proves it on the complement.  Uniformity is
closed under this fixed composition.  The costs add, yielding (4).  When
$P(F)=\bot$, the declared success predicate is false by definition; the mere
existence or correctness of $B$ cannot change that trace.  No premise about
$\mathsf P\ne\mathsf{NP}$ is used.
\end{proof}

The theorem does \emph{not} show that $B$ internally avoids all analogous
information.  A stronger, representation-invariant extractor might inspect
$B$'s execution and derive another object.  That is precisely the missing
universal implication; it cannot be replaced by coverage of a chosen front
end.  The wrapper observation is consistent with and specializes the
source-code/wrapper warning in MODELBRIDGE-0008.

\section{Bounded reproducible falsification test}

The script
\path{experiments/review/predecision_portfolio_escape_audit.py} used seed
202610111100.  For each $n\in\{6,9,12\}$ it generated 12 exact members of
$\Htwo$, exhaustively evaluated all $2^n$ assignments, ran the eight-rule
portfolio, and compared a wrapper's answer to direct semantics.  All 36
wrapper comparisons passed in 0.311 seconds.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrrrr}
\toprule
$n$ & samples & portfolio escapes & satisfiable & solution-count range\\
\midrule
6  & 12 & 2  & 12 & 18--26\\
9  & 12 & 12 & 12 & 74--90\\
12 & 12 & 12 & 12 & 232--366\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Across all sizes, the strong-backdoor rule hit 10 instances, the small
autarky rule hit one, and signed twins hit one (hits can overlap); 26 formulas
escaped every declared rule.  The raw JSON records every formula, hit list,
truth value, and exact solution count.  Horn, 2-CNF, disconnected-product,
unit, and small-autarky controls triggered expected portfolio branches.  An
affine XOR control records Gaussian elimination as an external semantic
shortcut.

These are deliberately hostile controls against overclaiming.  Every sampled
clean core happened to be satisfiable, and the experiment says nothing about
the asymptotic density of escapes.  An escape is not hard: it merely refutes
coverage by this fixed portfolio.  Conversely, a portfolio hit does not
measure the runtime of an arbitrary decider.  The exact result is the wrapper
theorem, not an extrapolation from 36 instances.

\section{Transfer and barrier audit}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.4cm,style=nextline]
\item[THEOREM TRANSFER.]
Schaefer's dichotomy legitimizes Horn, dual-Horn, bijunctive, and affine
tractable controls for fixed Boolean constraint languages.  It does not say
that every 3-CNF execution must expose which tractable relation it used.
The wrapper theorem transfers exactly to any sound polynomial partial
controller.

\item[FAILED TRANSFER.]
Noncoverage by a preprocessing portfolio, large $H_{F,L}$, large treewidth,
or a difficult decomposition is not a lower bound for arbitrary computation.
The one-bit decision interface permits semantic shortcuts, alternate
encodings, and algorithms whose states do not name the proposed object.

\item[RELATIVIZATION.]
Equation (3) relativizes: for every oracle $O$, a sound $P^O$ composes with a
total $B^O$.  Thus this lemma cannot overcome the Baker--Gill--Solovay barrier
and supplies no oracle-independent separation.

\item[ALGEBRIZATION.]
The proof is only closure under program composition and makes no arithmetized
lower-bound claim.  It does not evade the Aaronson--Wigderson barrier.

\item[NATURAL PROOFS.]
No large constructive property of Boolean functions and no general-circuit
lower bound is claimed, so the Razborov--Rudich framework is not directly
invoked.  Any later transfer to unrestricted circuit lower bounds must be
audited separately.

\item[NONUNIFORMITY AND EXPONENTS.]
Both $P$ and $B$ are uniform; no advice is introduced.  The exponent $c_B$ is
algorithm-dependent and is never bounded by a project-wide constant.  All
portfolio recognition, parsing, and fallback work is charged in (4).
\end{description}

Padding and equivalent wrappers sharpen the warning.  They can alter traces,
timing, and syntactic exposure without changing the decided language.
Therefore a universal result must be invariant under such transformations or
fix one SAME\_REALIZATION and prove its simulation cost.  Circuit, branching-
program, communication, pebbling, and time--space lower bounds remain
model-specific until that compiler is supplied.

\section{Precise surviving arrow}

The next admissible bridge must prove, not assume, an implication of the form
\begin{align}
 &\forall B\,[B\text{ is a total correct uniform polytime SAT decider}]
 \notag\\[-2pt]
 &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \forall F\in\Htwo\ \exists W:\quad
 \begin{cases}
 W=E(B,F),\\
 \operatorname{cost}(E)+\operatorname{cost}(W)\le N^{O(1)},\\
 W\text{ satisfies a model-matched obstruction}.
 \end{cases}                                                \tag{5}
\end{align}
where $E$ is representation-invariant or tied to a fully specified charged
simulation, includes acquisition and reconstruction, tolerates the unknown
exponent of $B$, and yields an unconditional contradiction in the same model.
No such $E$ or obstruction is known here.  Requiring a fixed portfolio rule
to succeed is not (5).  This is the exact missing universal implication.

\section{Primary literature and proof status}

Schaefer supplies the relevant tractable Boolean-relation classification;
D"ocker supplies NP-completeness of the balanced monotone clean core.  FLMV
gives a genuine SAT time--space lower bound only for its specified machine
model and does not automatically constrain arbitrary polynomial deciders.
Baker--Gill--Solovay, Razborov--Rudich, and Aaronson--Wigderson remain the
appropriate barrier checks.  None of these sources supplies (5).
