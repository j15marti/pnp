\section{Primary-literature and barrier preflight}

The two obstacles are (i) an exact bit-local reduction into the proposed
H22 clean core, and (ii) a model-matched path from the FLMV source language to
that reduction's domain.  Primary sources were checked first.

Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas explicitly identify why ordinary
NP-completeness is insufficient for simultaneous time--space transfer: the
reduced formula cannot be materialized, so each requested bit must instead be
computed on demand in polylogarithmic time and logarithmic workspace
\cite{FLMV05}.  Their Theorem 3.1 gives the resulting transfer from SAT to
$\mathsf{NTIME}[n]$.

D\"ocker defines Monotone 3-Sat-(2,2) with three distinct variables per
clause, unique clauses, and exactly two positive and two negative occurrences
per variable.  His Section 4 reduces from the star variant, where variables
may repeat inside a clause, by a monotone enforcer with 99 fresh variables and
133 clauses \cite{Doecker19}.  The paper proves the two semantic directions:
all three false ports are impossible, while any assignment with at least one
true port extends to the internal variables.  Berman--Karpinski--Scott and
Tovey provide important bounded-occurrence hardness constructions
\cite{BKS03,Tovey84}, but qualitative polynomial reductions do not alone meet
FLMV's local-access ledger.

\paragraph{Barrier classification.}
The fixed-gadget composition below is a \textbf{directly transferable
theorem}: source clauses correspond to constant templates and target indices
are decoded arithmetically.  VLSI routing, pebbling, distributed cuts,
observability, proof-space, circuit and branching-program lower bounds remain
\textbf{useful structural analogies requiring a new transfer theorem}; none
forces an arbitrary SAT decider to expose the corresponding state.  Treating
NP-completeness alone as a time--space transfer is a \textbf{misleading analogy
/ failed transfer}.

\section{Exact source, target, and encoding}

Let $\mathcal M^{*}_{2,2}$ be the set of indexed monotone 3-CNFs in which
each variable occurs exactly twice positively and exactly twice negatively;
the three positions of a clause may repeat a variable.  Thus an instance with
$n$ variables has $m=4n/3$ clauses.  Let $\mathcal H_{2,2}$ be D\"ocker's
proper unique target: every clause has three distinct variables, clauses are
unique, and every variable has exactly two occurrences of each sign.

Use a self-delimiting header for $(n,m)$ followed by fixed-width clause
records, each containing one sign bit and three variable identifiers of width
$w=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$.  Any permutation or variable renaming is part of
the encoded output and must be locally recoverable; the theorem below uses the
canonical source order and consecutive block-local namespaces.

\section{Fixed-block localizer theorem}

\begin{theorem}[FIXED-BLOCK-H22-LOCALIZER1]
There is one uniform deterministic map
\[
 R:\mathcal M^{*}_{2,2}\longrightarrow\mathcal H_{2,2}
\]
such that for every encoded source $F$ of length $N$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $R(F)$ is satisfiable if and only if $F$ is satisfiable;
\item if $F$ has $n$ variables and $m$ clauses, then $R(F)$ has
\[
 n'=n+99m,\qquad m'=133m;
\]
in particular $|R(F)|=\Theta(N)$ under the declared encodings;
\item given $F$ and a valid target-bit address, one uniform accessor returns
that bit in $\log^{O(1)}N$ time and $O(\log N)$ workspace, without
materializing $R(F)$.
\end{enumerate}
All setup, address arithmetic and fresh-variable naming are included.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each positive source clause $(v_1\vee v_2\vee v_3)$ insert a fresh copy
of D\"ocker's monotone enforcer $\mathcal S(v_1,v_2,v_3)$.  For a negative
source clause insert its literalwise complement.  Use disjoint internal
namespaces for different source clauses.

The enforcer semantics from \cite{Doecker19} say exactly that its block is
satisfiable over the internal variables iff at least one port is true.
Internal namespaces are disjoint, so extensions can be chosen independently.
This proves equisatisfiability clause by clause.

Each source occurrence becomes one port occurrence, so each original variable
retains its two positive and two negative occurrences.  The published block
has 99 internal variables, each with two occurrences of each sign, and 133
monotone clauses.  Its ports occur in separate clauses; its internal clauses
are proper and unique.  Fresh namespaces prevent clauses from different
blocks from coinciding.  Hence the image lies in $\mathcal H_{2,2}$.
The incidence identity
\[
 3\cdot133-3=396=4\cdot99
\]
checks all internal occurrence slots, and summing the fixed block counts gives
the displayed $(n',m')$.

For local access, decode the fixed-width output header or clause record.  If
$j$ is the zero-based target-clause index, compute
\[
 (i,t)=\operatorname{divmod}(j,133).
\]
Here $i$ identifies the source clause and $t$ a fixed template clause.  Read
only the needed source sign/identifier fields, and translate each template
symbol either to the appropriate source port or to
$n+99i+\delta$, where $\delta$ is a constant template offset.  Arithmetic on
$O(\log N)$-bit integers and a constant number of random-access input reads
give the asserted bounds.  No prefix sums or target buffer are used.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{SAME\_REALIZATION.}
The simulated H22 decider, the target-bit accessor, its address tapes, the
source input, and retained setup all belong to one execution.  The target
formula is virtual, not free.  Every read made by the target decider invokes
the accessor and is charged.  This is exactly the virtual-input simulation
interface already formalized in the model-bridge spine.

\begin{corollary}[Second-leg resource preservation]
If $\mathcal H_{2,2}$ has a uniform deterministic decider using time
$M^{c+o(1)}$ and space $M^{p+o(1)}$, then $\mathcal M^{*}_{2,2}$ has one using
time $N^{c+o(1)}$ and space $N^{p+o(1)}$.  In the synthesis notation the
reduction has $q=1$ and only logarithmic setup/access/workspace exponents.
\end{corollary}

The constant 133 can dominate finite encodings but does not change $q$.
Conversely, padding to $M=N^{1.004}$ already exceeds the current ideal
synthesis boundary $q_*=1.0038366955\ldots$; exponent accounting cannot be
replaced by a finite ratio.

\section{What the theorem does not prove}

FLMV's Cook construction outputs a quasi-linear SAT formula whose individual
bits are locally computable.  The theorem above begins instead with an
instance already in $\mathcal M^{*}_{2,2}$.  The missing implication is
therefore not ``H22 is NP-complete'' but the following exact arrow:
\[
 \mathsf{NTIME}[N]
 \xrightarrow[O(\log N)\ \mathrm{space}]
 {\mathrm{bit\ local},\,N\log^{O(1)}N}
 \mathcal M^{*}_{2,2}.
\]
Neither D\"ocker's qualitative NP-completeness proof nor the Berman--Karpinski--
Scott/Tovey reductions were shown in this run to supply that arrow.  Without
it, composing a hypothetical H22 algorithm with the fixed-block localizer
does not yet reach the FLMV contradiction.

Assuming $P=NP$ changes none of this bookkeeping.  It supplies polynomial
decision algorithms and standard search-to-decision, but it does not force an
arbitrary algorithm to materialize the compatibility relation
\[
 H(F)_{u,v}=1\Longleftrightarrow F(u,v)=1
\]
or to expose any chosen rank, circuit, branching program, proof, trace, or
memory state.  The present reduction is external and source-defined, so it
avoids that false necessity claim; it also stops one full local-reduction leg
short of an unconditional contradiction.

\section{Hostile controls and barrier audit}

\begin{itemize}
\item Horn, dual-Horn, 2SAT, affine/Tseitin systems with Gaussian elimination,
bounded-treewidth formulas, independent products and parity families remain
controls against universal state-exposure or extraction lower bounds.  They
do not refute this syntactic, source-defined localizer.
\item DIRECT, COPY, DELAY, INTERP, RECOMP and DEAD-code wrappers do not affect
the target bits: the reduction never inspects the decision implementation.
This is a strength of the transfer theorem, not a lower bound on the wrapped
algorithm.
\item One-bit decision output is respected.  No witness, proof, compatibility
matrix, trace or reconstruction object is required.
\item Alternate encodings require a local translation theorem.  Arbitrary
padding or redundant clauses are not harmless when they change the output
exponent or target promise.
\item The construction relativizes and algebrizes.  It is a transport lemma,
not the nonrelativizing hinge needed for a P-versus-NP separation.  It asserts
no Natural-Proofs property and no circuit lower bound.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Post-quantum technique ledger.}
Schur squares, quadratic-relation ranks, Hilbert functions/syzygies,
collision entropy, and special-soundness-style extraction are
\textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} for the fixed-block localization claim: no code,
proof-to-node collision map, or binding assumption appears.  The LWE lesson
of extracting a useful object externally rather than requiring internal
materialization is an \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}; here it motivated the
source-defined reduction, but supplies no worst-case/average-case theorem.
Hash/random-oracle methods remain inadmissible for an unconditional proof.
