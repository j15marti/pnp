\section*{Exact algebraic threshold}
For $0\le x\le n$ and an explicitly enumerated residual assignment domain of size $2^{n-x}$, polynomial residual size is equivalent to $n-x=O(\log n)$. This is PROVED/EXACT accounting, not a SAT machine lower bound.
\section*{Oracle-shadow theorem (scoped, PROVED/EXACT)}
Let $A$ be any exact Boolean SAT decision oracle, and $F$ an $n$-variable CNF. The standard answer-preserving restriction wrapper first queries $A(F)$ and then queries one zero-restriction at each variable. It produces a full assignment prefix preserving the original SAT bit in exactly $n+1$ calls. The construction is extensional: replacing $A$ by a SAT oracle reproduces the entire transcript. Thus an unconditional lower bound of $n^{\omega(1)}$ for this wrapper with unit-cost SAT queries is false. With a physical decider of time $T_A(N)$, the wrapper cost is at most $O(n\,T_A(\mathrm{poly}(N))+\mathrm{poly}(N))$ with encoding/access charges. Under $P=NP$, this is polynomial but the unknown exponent is not erased.
\section*{New precise no-go guard}
Suppose a claimed universal acquisition lower bound asserts $A^*(F,n-O(\log n))=N^{\omega(1)}$ for every correct polynomial-time implementation under the hypothesis $P=NP$, while the admissible acquisition interface includes the above wrapper and its calls. The claim contradicts the conditional wrapper upper bound. Therefore any proposed acquisition lower bound must (i) restrict the interface in a source-checkable way, (ii) supply an independent all-algorithm lower bound, or (iii) charge current-scale calls and avoid claiming exponent erasure. Merely changing notation to Boolean-operation cost does not close this gap.
\section*{Bounded test}
The test exhaustively enumerated assignments for four CNF/Boolean families, $n=2,\ldots,9$ (32 cases), and verified each prefix maintains the original decision bit and uses exactly $n+1$ calls. This is COMPUTATIONAL confirmation only; the theorem is the elementary induction. No general gate-minimality claim is made.
