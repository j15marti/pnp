\paragraph{Status.} P versus NP remains OPEN. The branching certificate is PROVED/EXACT for the declared recursion. The 192 small-formula tests are COMPUTATIONAL. No all-input favorable SAT successor or lower bound is established.
\section*{Model}
Let $\mu(F)$ be the number of live variables after unit propagation. A Shannon branch chooses one live variable and makes at most two children with $\mu(F_i)\le\mu(F)-1$. For $\rho\ge2$,
\[
\sum_i \rho^{\mu(F_i)-\mu(F)}\le 2/\rho\le1.
\]
Thus a standard Measure-and-Conquer induction yields an $O^*(2^{\mu(F)})$ bound for the declared branching algorithm with polynomially bounded local work. This is a worst-case upper bound, not a compression lower bound. A sharper base requires provably larger decreases or other exact structure on every relevant branch.
\section*{Fully charged wrapper}
For a generic certified reduction with local work $a(F)$ and children $F_i$, the time recurrence is $T(F)\le a(F)+\sum_iT(F_i)$. Sequential peak space must include retained parent state, current child encoding, recursion frames, and memo tables. Memoization may save repeated evaluations but has nonzero key construction, lookup, and retained-state costs.
\section*{Finite test}
Seed 20261010, 192 random 3-CNFs on 3 through 10 variables, 24 per size, each with $3n$ clauses. Unit-propagating DPLL, with and without canonical memoization, matched brute-force SAT on every case. Maximum recursive calls was 12 for either implementation. No case exhibited fewer calls with memoization. Runtime 0.0517 seconds. This negative control shows that adding a cache need not improve a particular solver/workload. The prototype counts calls and cache entries, not RAM bit operations or full encoded peak space.
