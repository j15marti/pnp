\paragraph{Status.} P versus NP remains OPEN. The finite optimum is PROVED/EXACT as an exhaustive characterization within the fixed reduced ordered binary decision diagram (OBDD) representation model; the reported numerical instances are COMPUTATIONAL. No unrestricted SAT lower bound follows.
\section*{Model and theorem}
For an $n$-variable Boolean function $f$, a variable permutation $\pi$ determines a unique reduced OBDD up to graph isomorphism. Let $s_\pi(f)$ count nonterminal nodes and $s^*(f)=\min_{\pi\in S_n}s_\pi(f)$. Exhaustively enumerating all $n!$ permutations and reducing the Shannon expansion computes $s^*(f)$ exactly. The minimum is within the OBDD model only. The full construction cost, including $2^n$ truth-table acquisition, $n!$ order trials, node generation, hashing, and memory, is not $s^*(f)$. A constant SAT decision bit cannot substitute for $f$ when the consumer requires all assignment queries.

\section*{Falsification and next arrow}
Small OBDDs can be expensive to discover by exhaustive order search. Large OBDDs do not imply large unrestricted circuits or SAT runtime. A favorable all-input source-first wrapper needs one uniform charged algorithm, strict ordinary encoded-child shrinkage, and no current-scale SAT calls; the OBDD optimum alone provides none. The next experiment should compare certified transition shortest paths with exact OBDD order optima, and test whether an efficiently checkable dual potential survives source rereads, independent products, parity and connected UNSAT instances.
