\begin{abstract}
The newest four specialist reports leave no intermediate query-scale resource
between a fixed implementation and its extensional equivalence class.  This
report proves the exact synthesis.  For one fixed polynomial-query SAT
transducer, vanishing reached-query scale becomes an ordinary source
algorithm under $P=NP$, while positive scale prevents any exponent uniform
over all polynomial SAT implementations because of delay wrappers.  If one
instead minimizes over all extensionally equivalent programs, then under
$P=NP$ the infimum is zero because an oracle-free representative exists.  A
total computable complete canonicalizer for clocked polynomial-time Boolean
programs is impossible by a direct halting reduction.

Thus actual query scale is charged but wrapper-sensitive; extensional scale
is wrapper-invariant but collapses in the counterfactual; and complete
effective normalization is unavailable.  This retires query-scale
normalization as the missing P-versus-NP bridge.  It does not refute a fixed,
source-specific compiler proved directly to land in a model-matched
time--space forbidden region.  That compiler, the arbitrary-decider transfer,
the nonrelativizing/nonalgebrizing hinge, and P versus NP remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exact normalization theorem}

Let $E^{\SAT}$ be one uniform deterministic polynomial-time oracle
transducer computing a Boolean function $g$.  On length-$N$ inputs let
\[
 q_E(N)=\max\bigl(2,\max\{|Q|:Q\text{ is a SAT query reached on some }x,
 |x|=N\}\bigr)
\]
and
\[
 \sigma(E)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log q_E(N)}{\log N}.
\]
Suppose all nonoracle work, including reading, query construction, retained
state, replay and output, is $N^{r+o(1)}$ time and $N^{p+o(1)}$ writable
space, and at most $N^{\eta+o(1)}$ queries are reached.  Let
$\mathsf{PolySAT}$ be all total uniform deterministic polynomial-time
machines deciding the same canonical SAT language; no common exponent is
assumed.

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{QUERY-SCALE-NORMALIZATION-TRILEMMA1}]
Assume $P=NP$.  Then the following statements hold.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item For a fixed $E$, if $\sigma(E)=0$, replacing its oracle calls by any
fixed polynomial SAT decider gives one ordinary uniform algorithm for $g$ in
time $N^{\max\{r,\eta\}+o(1)}$ and writable space
$N^{\max\{p,0\}+o(1)}$.
\item For a fixed $E$, if $\sigma(E)>0$, then for every constant $c$ there is
an extensionally identical $A\in\mathsf{PolySAT}$ for which the fully charged
composite $E[A]$ exceeds $N^c$ on infinitely many inputs.
\item For
\[
 \underline\sigma(g)=\inf\{\sigma(D):D^{\SAT}=g,
 D\text{ is a uniform polynomial transducer}\},
\]
one has $\underline\sigma(g)=0$ for every $g\in P^{\SAT}$.
\item There is no total computable map on clocked polynomial-time Boolean
programs whose outputs are equal exactly when the programs compute the same
total Boolean function.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently reached-query scale cannot simultaneously be actual and fully
charged, invariant under extensionally equal polynomial implementations,
positive under $P=NP$, and completely effectively normalized.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For item 1, fix a SAT decider running in time $M^K$ and polynomial space.
Every reached query has length $N^{o(1)}$, so its time and space are still
$N^{o(1)}$ for this fixed $K$.  Sequentially evaluating at most
$N^{\eta+o(1)}$ queries gives the stated time; peak query storage and solver
space are subpolynomial and the transducer's declared local space remains
charged.

For item 2, positive limsup gives an $\varepsilon>0$ and infinitely many
lengths with a reached query of length at least $N^\varepsilon$.  Starting
from any polynomial SAT decider, prepend $M^d$ dummy transitions, with
$d>c/\varepsilon$.  Exact answer bits and therefore the adaptive query
transcript do not change, but the charged call costs at least
$N^{\varepsilon d}>N^c$.

For item 3, under $P=NP$ replace every SAT call of any polynomial transducer
for $g$ by one fixed polynomial SAT decider.  The resulting ordinary
polynomial program asks no oracle queries, has scale zero, and is an allowed
representative.  Nonnegativity gives equality.

For item 4, given a machine $M$ and word $w$, construct a clocked polynomial
program $P_{M,w}$ which on inputs of length $n$ simulates $M(w)$ for $n$
steps and returns one iff it halts in that horizon.  It is extensionally
equal to the all-zero program exactly when $M(w)$ never halts.  A complete
computable canonicalizer would decide the halting problem.
\end{proof}

Items 1 and 2 reconcile LB-0015 with MODELBRIDGE-0015; item 3 accepts
RT-0015's exact counterfactual collapse; item 4 independently verifies its
canonicalizer no-go.  TESTS-0010 explains why actual positive scale is not a
semantic necessity: DEAD/CANCEL wrappers can insert or remove it.  There is
no contradiction among the four results because their quantifiers differ.

\section{What the theorem does and does not retire}

The theorem retires \texttt{QUERY-SCALE-NORMALIZATION-BRIDGE1}: choose actual
scale, semantic infimum, or a complete computable representative and treat
the choice as a positive all-algorithm obstruction under $P=NP$.  Actual
scale is implementation-sensitive; the semantic infimum is zero; and the
complete effective quotient is uncomputable.

It does not retire a fixed external transformation.  A compiler
$\mathcal T$ may inspect the source and, where explicitly allowed, a supplied
program, provided its construction and covariance are proved rather than
obtained by minimization.  To be useful it must establish, under $P=NP$, one
ordinary SAME\_REALIZATION machine in a known forbidden class, for example
an FLMV-compatible simultaneous random-access time--space box.  The compiler
must charge source reads, program inspection, failed routes, normalization,
query/child creation, transport, replay, reconstruction, output, suspended
ancestors and transient workspace.  Its bounds must be independent of the
unknown fixed SAT exponent $K$.  That statement is separation-strength and
remains \OPEN.

\section{Primary literature and barrier audit}

Baker--Gill--Solovay construct relativized settings with opposite answers to
the P-versus-NP question, so a purely black-box relativizing normalization
cannot be the final separation hinge \cite{bgs}.  Aaronson--Wigderson show
that nearly all central open problems, including P versus NP, also require
techniques beyond algebrization \cite{aw}.  The present theorem relativizes
in its query-substitution and delay-wrapper parts and supplies no such hinge.

Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas prove a simultaneous time--space lower
bound for SAT on deterministic random-access Turing machines and an on-demand
local reduction preserving the relevant resource accounting
\cite[Theorem~3.1]{flmv}.  Their endpoint transfers only after one actual
ordinary realization meets both bounds; zero semantic query scale or a
unit-cost oracle statement does not meet those hypotheses.  Jahanjou--Miles--
Viola prove a quasilinear 3SAT reduction whose indexed output bits have strong
locality \cite{jmv}; this supports the separate clean-core reduction leg but
does not normalize every SAT implementation.  Rice and Blum supply the
classical computability/complexity context for semantic program properties
and machine-independent measures \cite{rice,blum}; the canonicalizer theorem
here uses its own direct reduction and no speedup premise.

Natural Proofs is not triggered by this theorem because no constructive
large property against general circuits is proposed.  Schur squares,
quadratic-relation spaces, Hilbert functions, syzygies, collision entropy,
special soundness, VLSI routing, distributed cuts and proof-system lower
bounds are \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} to arbitrary one-bit SAT computation
without an independently proved SAME\_REALIZATION compiler.  Certified
compilers, bisimulation quotients and LWE-style external extraction remain
\textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}; their missing bridge is exact coverage and
resource preservation for every relevant uniform SAT implementation.
