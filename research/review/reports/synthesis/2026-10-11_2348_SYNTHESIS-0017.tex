\begin{abstract}
The current specialist cycle sharpens, but does not close, the universal
clean-core spine.  LB-0020 proves the exact recursive compiler algebra once a
favorable hereditary pass exists.  TESTS-0016 proves an $m-1$ locator depth
for a one-survivor family in a declared no-preprocessing physical-record
interface.  ORDERLOC1 then gives the identical clause multiset an equivalent
sign-grouped presentation with zero locator depth, one-record answer access,
and a linear-time logarithmic-space normalizer.  TAGGLUE1 independently shows
that an answer tag is not a source-matched reconstruction binding.  This run
packages those results as an exact representation-robustness gate: a physical
locator floor is either presentation-specific, charged through normalization
or indexing, or quotiented away; no case yields an arbitrary-SAT-decider
obligation without a new algorithm-to-interface and BIND theorem.  The
worst-case fixed-layout floor remains valid, but is only
$\Theta(N/\log N)$ in encoded length and has a linear scan upper bound.
P versus NP remains \OPEN.
\end{abstract}

\section{Representation-robustness gate}

For a CNF $F$, let $\mathcal O(F)$ be its orbit under clause permutations
that preserve the formula and encoded length.  Let $d(e)$ denote locator depth
in a specified physical-record interface for a presentation $e\in\mathcal
O(F)$.

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{REPRESENTATION-ROBUSTNESS-GATE1} --- \EXACT]
Any use of a physical locator floor in a universal clean-core argument must
pass all of the following gates.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.7em]
\item If the claim is pointwise invariant over equivalent presentations, its
locator floor is at most $\min_{e\in\mathcal O(F)}d(e)$.
\item If the claim fixes one presentation and one locator interface, the
floor is restricted to that interface until an algorithm-to-interface
transfer theorem covers arbitrary uniform SAT deciders.
\item If a normalizer or index is used to select a presentation, its
construction, storage, access, and replay costs are part of the same
realization.
\item A semantic decision procedure may quotient source locations entirely;
preventing this requires an exact source-matched BIND/reconstruction theorem,
not the one-bit SAT specification.
\end{enumerate}
For the ORDERLOC1 family, the orbit minimum is zero, the raw block-order
maximum is $m-1=\Theta(N/\log N)$, and a one-pass normalizer costs $O(N)$
time and $O(\log N)$ workspace.  Hence the locator supplies neither a
representation-invariant positive floor nor a superpolynomial obstruction.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first item is the quantifier inequality
\[
 \bigl(\forall e\in\mathcal O(F)\;d(e)\ge L(F)\bigr)
 \Longrightarrow L(F)\le\min_{e\in\mathcal O(F)}d(e).
\]
ORDERLOC1 constructs the same mixed-answer proper-$3$CNF clause multiset in
block order and sign-grouped order.  The former has exact worst-case locator
depth $m-1$ in the declared no-preprocessing block-probe model; the latter
places the live block at a known suffix, so $d=0$ and one clause record
distinguishes YES from NO.  Thus the orbit minimum is zero.

Restricting the quantifier to block order restores the TESTS-0016 adversary,
but changes the theorem's model and does not show that an arbitrary SAT
machine exposes the locator object.  Alternatively, a scan emits every
inactive block, retains the four-clause active block, and emits it last.  This
is $O(N)$ time and $O(\log N)$ workspace; those costs cannot be omitted.
Finally, the source map $L(t,\beta)=\beta$ has fibers of size $m$,
\[
 |\operatorname{dom}L|=2m,
 \qquad \operatorname{Coll}(L)=2m^2,
 \qquad |\operatorname{im}L|=2,
\]
so the source position is not required by decision semantics.  A BIND theorem
would have to add and justify that obligation. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope guard.}
The theorem does not invalidate the worst-case fixed-layout statement
$\forall D\ \exists t$ in the locator model.  It invalidates its silent
promotion to a pointwise semantic, representation-independent, or
arbitrary-algorithm obligation.  Even the surviving raw floor is
$\Theta(N/\log N)$ and the scan upper bound is $O(N)$, so the family is an
input-access test rather than a P-versus-NP lower bound.

\section{Hostile controls, post-quantum ledger, and barriers}

The one-bit quotient, DIRECT/COPY/DEAD/DELAY/INTERP/RECOMP-equivalent answers,
padding, redundant inactive blocks, alternate clause orders, compact circuits,
and independent products all preserve the failure of a source-location
necessity claim.  2SAT, Horn, affine/Tseitin Gaussian elimination, and
bounded-treewidth dynamic programming remain easy semantic shortcuts.  A
fixed source order may still give a valid input-probe lower bound, but not a
general machine lower bound.

The collision calculation above is an \textbf{EXACT OBJECT/THEOREM TRANSFER}
from the lossiness ledger.  Sparse-bit-vector rank/select is a
\textbf{USEFUL STRUCTURAL ANALOGY REQUIRING A NEW TRANSFER THEOREM}: its
preprocessed read-only model exactly illustrates why interface and index
costs matter, but is not arbitrary SAT.  Schur squares, quadratic relation
ranks, Hilbert functions, syzygies, special soundness, VLSI congestion, and
communication cuts are \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} for this locator claim.
Worst-case/average-case black-box extraction remains an
\textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE needing a new bridge}.  Hashes and random oracles
are inadmissible as unconditional binding.

The gate relativizes and algebrizes; it supplies no barrier escape.  It uses
no large constructive circuit property, so Natural Proofs is not invoked or
evaded.  A final proof still needs a load-bearing nonrelativizing and
nonalgebrizing implication in the matched model.
