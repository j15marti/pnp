\begin{abstract}
This run converts an imprecise clean-core handoff into an exact resource
threshold.  Let a reduction map a source SAT encoding of length $N$ to the
duplicate-free Monotone $3$SAT-$(2,2)$ core with length
$M=N^{q+o(1)}$.  If a core decider uses time $M^{6/5+o(1)}$ and space
$M^{1/2+o(1)}$, and reduction setup and random access add no larger
exponents, then the induced source exponents are $C=6q/5$ and $P=q/2$.
The FLMV frontier used by MODELBRIDGE-0011 and RT-0011 excludes this pair
exactly when
\[
 q<q_* = 1.003836695566892\ldots,
\]
where $q_*=5c_*/6$ and $c_*$ is the positive root of
$11c^3-6c-12=0$.  Thus $N^{1+o(1)}$ encoded length is sufficient in the
ideal local-access ledger, but an arbitrary polynomial reduction is not;
even a fixed $N^{1.004}$ blow-up misses this particular endpoint.  D\"ocker's
paper supplies NP-completeness and constant-size gadget replacements from
already bounded-occurrence sources, but it does not by itself state the
full FLMV-model, general-source, local-bit, simultaneous-space transfer.
That transfer remains the smallest clean-core theorem needed.  A
10,027-check exact-rational audit passed.  P versus NP remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Claim card and clean core}
The clean core $\Htwo$ consists of canonically indexed, duplicate-free
monotone proper 3-CNFs in which every variable occurs exactly twice
positively and twice negatively.  Hence $m=4n/3$ and its canonical encoded
length is $\Theta(n\log n)$.  D\"ocker proves this family NP-complete and
gives direct gadget reductions in Section~4 \cite{doecker}.  This is a
language classification, not a quantitative transfer theorem.

The tested claim has prefix
\[
 \forall R\ \forall B\ \forall N\gg1:
 \quad \SAT(x)=\Htwo(R(x))
 \Longrightarrow \text{the ledger below},                         \tag{1}
\]
where $R$ is a fixed uniform reduction in the same random-access machine
model and $B$ is a total uniform $\Htwo$ decider.  Input length means encoded
bits, not variables or clauses.  The SAME\_REALIZATION includes reduction
setup, canonicalization, every target-bit access, $B$'s execution, final
output, transient storage, and peak workspace.

For source length $N$, suppose
\begin{align*}
 M&=|R(x)|\le N^{q+o(1)},\\
 T_R(N)&\le N^{a+o(1)},\qquad S_R(N)\le N^{b+o(1)},\\
 \text{one virtual target-bit access}&\le N^{\alpha+o(1)}\text{ time},\\
 T_B(M)&\le M^{c+o(1)},\qquad S_B(M)\le M^{p+o(1)}.
\end{align*}
The access bound includes address translation and source rereads.  If the
target is explicitly materialized, its construction, storage medium, and
conversion into the declared read-only input interface must be charged in
$a,b$ rather than silently treated as free.

\section{Exact transfer budget}
\begin{theorem}[\texttt{CLEANCORE-FLMV-TRANSFER-BUDGET1}]
Under the preceding uniform SAME\_REALIZATION, $R$ followed by $B$ decides
the source language in
\[
 T(N)\le N^{C+o(1)},\quad C=\max\{1,a,qc+\alpha\},
 \qquad
 S(N)\le N^{P+o(1)},\quad P=\max\{b,qp\}.              \tag{2}
\]
Consequently, the FLMV frontier quoted in RT-0011 gives a contradiction only
if
\[
 C<\varphi,qquad P<d(C),qquad
 d(C)=\frac12\left(\frac{C+2}{C^2}-C\right).           \tag{3}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Setup costs $N^{a+o(1)}$.  The target execution has at most
$M^{c+o(1)}=N^{qc+o(1)}$ steps; replacing each input access by the declared
accessor multiplies time by at most $N^{\alpha+o(1)}$.  The source scan gives
the harmless linear floor.  Peak workspace is the maximum of the reduction
workspace and $M^{p+o(1)}=N^{qp+o(1)}$ target workspace, with logarithmic
addresses absorbed in $o(1)$.  This proves (2).  Equation (3) is then the
same-model composition accepted by RT-0011, with strict inequalities.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharp ideal threshold at the current rational box]
Set $c=6/5$, $p=1/2$, $a\le6q/5$, $b\le q/2$, and $\alpha=0$.  Then (3)
holds exactly for $1\le q<q_*$, where
\[
 q_*=\frac56c_*,\qquad 11c_*^3-6c_*-12=0,
 \qquad q_*=1.003836695566892\ldots .                  \tag{4}
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Here $C=6q/5$ and $P=q/2=5C/12$.  Equality $P=d(C)$ is equivalent, after
multiplying by $12C^2$, to $11C^3-6C-12=0$.  On the relevant interval the
left side is strictly increasing.  At $q=1$,
\[
 d(6/5)-1/2=23/45-1/2=1/90>0,
\]
while at $q=1.01$ the margin is negative.  The unique crossing is (4), and
$C<\varphi$ is weaker at that crossing.
\end{proof}

The corollary makes ``near linear'' precise.  Constant-factor and
polylogarithmic encoding overheads have exponent $q=1+o(1)$ and fit with
strict slack.  A generic polynomial reduction gives only an unspecified
fixed $q$ and cannot be composed with this endpoint.  Padding the same
language to $N^2$ gives $C=12/5$ and $P=1$, demonstrating why
NP-completeness alone is the wrong invariant.

\section{Primary-source audit of the proposed core}
D\"ocker's definition exactly matches $\Htwo$: proper unique monotone
3-clauses and two positive plus two negative occurrences per variable
\cite{doecker}.  Section~4 replaces duplicate or mixed bounded-occurrence
clauses by fixed enforcers, including a 99-variable, 133-clause monotone
enforcer, and uses three disjoint copies to group mixed clauses.  This
supports a linear clause/variable-count observation for the already
bounded-occurrence source.  It does not, as stated, close all of the following
resource obligations:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item reduce the exact general SAT encoding used by FLMV to the required
bounded-occurrence source with $N^{1+o(1)}$ encoded output;
\item generate any requested canonical target bit in $N^{o(1)}$ overhead, or
charge full materialization and canonical sorting;
\item preserve FLMV's random-access machine conventions and simultaneous
workspace measure; and
\item prove the composed correctness and resource bounds with the same
uniform $o(1)$ envelope.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the paper is a \textbf{DIRECT THEOREM TRANSFER} for NP-completeness and
fixed-gadget semantic replacement, an \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE} for the
missing local reduction, and a \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} if cited alone for
the FLMV exponent pair.  FLMV itself is a direct theorem only after these
model and encoding obligations are discharged \cite{flmv}.

\section{Falsification and hostile controls}
The attempted claim ``any polynomial Karp reduction to an NP-complete clean
core transfers the endpoint'' is false as a quantitative proof rule.  Take
an otherwise valid reduction and append a polynomially padded, semantically
ignored encoding field.  The language and NP-completeness are unchanged,
but $q$ can be made $2$ (or any larger fixed value), moving (2) outside (3).
This does not prove that the core is easy; it proves that qualitative
completeness does not carry a fixed exponent frontier.

Horn, dual-Horn, 2SAT, affine/Tseitin Gaussian elimination, bounded
treewidth, and independent products remain hostile semantic controls: their
large compatibility spaces or gadget descriptions do not force the stated
runtime.  DEAD/COPY/DELAY/INTERP/RECOMP wrappers and one-bit output preserve
the language while changing traces and exposure.  Alternative encodings and
padding change $q,a,b,\alpha$ and must be re-entered into (2).  No semantic
state count is used as a time lower bound.

\section{Barrier audit and relation to RT-0011}
RT-0011 proves that an extensional all-polynomial-solver compiler into the
empty FLMV-forbidden class is logically equivalent to $\SAT\notin P$.  The
present transfer budget does not repair that quantifier: it only says what a
source-to-core reduction must preserve before a core endpoint can be used.
A proposed $A$-relative compiler must still start from a nonvacuous premise
known for every polynomial solver and cannot assume a favorable core
algorithm.

The transfer lemma relativizes whenever $R$, its accessor, and $B$ use the
same oracle; therefore it does not cross Baker--Gill--Solovay.  Its algebraic
version also forwards low-degree oracle access and does not cross
algebrization.  No large constructive circuit property is used, so Natural
Proofs is not the immediate hinge.  Hardness magnification is a useful
architectural analogy---a weak-looking lower bound plus a careful reduction
can magnify to a major separation---but existing locality magnification
theorems do not supply this arbitrary-RAM SAT reduction and are
\textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}, not a transfer \cite{locality}.

Schur squares, quadratic relation ranks, Hilbert functions, syzygies,
collision entropy, and special-soundness ideas do not enter (2).  Without a
same-realization acquisition theorem they are \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} for
an arbitrary one-bit decider.  LWE-style black-box reduction architecture
remains an analogy; cryptographic assumptions and random oracles are not
admissible in an unconditional proof.
