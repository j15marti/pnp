\paragraph{Result and strict status.}
This run tests the proposed treewidth interpretation of the recurring
$2^k/2^x=2^{k-x}$ quantity.  For a supplied primal tree decomposition of
width $w$, putting $x=k-(w+1)$ makes $2^{k-x}=2^{w+1}$ exactly the number of
Boolean assignments to one maximum bag.  That identity supports a standard
dynamic-programming \emph{upper bound}; it does not force every SAT decider
to materialize, acquire, visit, or communicate all bag assignments.

\textsf{TW-DP-UPPER-NOT-LOWER1} below is \textbf{PROVED/EXACT}.
\textsf{TREEWIDTH-2K2X-LOWER1}, the claim that every deterministic SAT
decider must perform $\Omega(2^{k-x})$ work merely because the primal
treewidth is $w=k-x-1$, is \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}.  Dense Horn formulas
and a tautological-clause wrapper give infinite exact counterfamilies with
$w=k-1$ and nominal scale $2^k$ but linear-time or trivial semantics.  This
does not prove a polynomial algorithm for the NP-complete clean core and does
not resolve P versus NP.  P versus NP remains \textbf{OPEN}.

\section{Clean core, encoding, and exact target}
\begin{definition}[Canonical clean core]
For this review, $\mathcal H_{2,2}$ is the set of canonically encoded CNF
formulas such that every clause contains exactly three distinct variables,
every clause is monotone (all positive or all negative), no clause is
duplicated, and every variable occurs exactly twice positively and exactly
twice negatively.  Variables are renamed to $1,\ldots,n$ by first canonical
occurrence; literals and clauses are lexicographically sorted.  Let
$N(F)=|\enc(F)|$ be the complete bit length, including the self-delimiting
counts, signs, and binary variable identifiers.  This is the project's
Monotone 3-SAT-(2,2) core; its NP-completeness is known in the primary
literature.
\end{definition}

The desired P-versus-NP-strength endpoint must have the following quantifiers,
or imply them by a proved same-model transfer:
\[
 \tag{UCORE-TIME1}
 \forall A\ \Bigl[A\text{ is a uniform deterministic total decider for }
 \SAT\Bigr]
 \ \forall d\in\mathbb N\ \exists F\in\mathcal H_{2,2}:\quad
 T_A(\enc(F))>N(F)^d.
\]
An infinitude requirement can be added by padding only after proving that the
padding preserves the clean core and the lower-bound model.  Since
$\mathcal H_{2,2}$ is NP-complete under polynomial reductions,
UCORE-TIME1 would imply $\mathsf P\ne\mathsf{NP}$.  Consequently it cannot be
smuggled in as a premise.  Under the hypothesis $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$,
there is instead some uniform deterministic $N^K$ decider for the entire
language and for this restriction; the exponent $K$ is arbitrary and must be
eliminated by a $K$-independent, model-matched contradiction.

For any claimed internal object $J$, SAME\_REALIZATION means that the source
bits, preprocessing, decomposition or service construction, writable reset,
random-access mechanism, every query and replay, child encoding,
reconstruction, one-bit output, time, and peak space are all charged to the
same execution $\tau_A(\enc(F))$.  External self-reduction or a different
algorithm proves extractability, not occurrence in $\tau_A$.

\section{Candidate and exact falsification}
Let $G_F$ be the conventional primal graph: variables are vertices and two
variables are adjacent when they co-occur in a clause.  If a decomposition of
$G_F$ of width $w$ is supplied, the usual bag-assignment interface has at most
$2^{w+1}$ Boolean keys per bag.  With $k=|\operatorname{var}(F)|$ and
$x=k-(w+1)$,
\[
             2^{k-x}=2^{w+1}.                                           \tag{1}
\]
Equation (1) is a normalization of one realization, not a runtime lower bound.

\begin{theorem}[TW-DP-UPPER-NOT-LOWER1]
For every $n\ge 3$, let
\[
 H_n=\bigwedge_{1\le i<j<\ell\le n}
       (\neg x_i\vee\neg x_j\vee x_\ell).
\]
Then $H_n$ is Horn, its primal graph is $K_n$, and hence
$\tw(G_{H_n})=n-1$.  Therefore (1) assigns it the nominal table scale $2^n$.
Nevertheless $H_n$ is satisfiable by the all-zero assignment and Horn
satisfiability decides it in time linear in its explicit encoding length.

Moreover
\[
 U_n=H_n\wedge(x_1)\wedge(x_2)\wedge(\neg x_3)
\]
is unsatisfiable: the clause
$(\neg x_1\vee\neg x_2\vee x_3)$ and the first two units derive $x_3$,
contradicting the last unit.  It has the same complete primal graph and is
decided by the same linear Horn procedure.  Finally, adding the tautologies
$(x_i\vee\neg x_i\vee x_j)$ for every $i<j$ to the unit formula $(x_1)$
creates a conventional primal $K_n$ without changing the represented Boolean
function at all.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every three-element subset occurs as the variable set of a clause of $H_n$;
therefore every pair is an edge and the primal graph is $K_n$, whose treewidth
is $n-1$.  Each clause of $H_n$ has at most one positive literal, so it is
Horn.  The stated assignments and unit-propagation contradiction prove the
two semantic claims.  Dowling--Gallier forward chaining runs in linear time
on Horn input.  Each wrapper clause contains complementary literals and is
therefore a tautology, while its two distinct variables add the desired
primal edge.  Thus syntactic primal width alone cannot force (1) as work for
an arbitrary decider.
\end{proof}

The explicit lengths satisfy $N(H_n)=\Theta(n^3\log n)$ under the declared
binary-ID encoding, whereas the nominal table scale is $2^n$.  This gap is
not itself a lower or upper bound in $N$; it only makes the category error
visible.  The counterfamilies are hostile controls, not members of
$\mathcal H_{2,2}$, so they retire the \emph{universal treewidth inference}
but not UCORE-TIME1 on the NP-complete core.

\section{Primary-literature and barrier audit}
Dowling and Gallier prove linear-time Horn satisfiability, which supplies the
model-matched semantic bypass used above.  Samer and Szeider's
incidence-treewidth algorithm for \#SAT gives an explicit decomposition-DP
upper bound $O(4^w w\ell N)$ in its own incidence-width parameters; it does
not claim that every decider pays the table size.  Lokshtanov, Marx, and
Saurabh prove many tight treewidth dependences only under SETH and for named
graph problems.  Such conditional endpoints cannot close this project: if
$\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$ then SETH is false.

Relevant primary sources, checked before forming the claim, are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item W.~F. Dowling and J.~H. Gallier, ``Linear-time algorithms for testing
the satisfiability of propositional Horn formulae,'' 1984,
\url{https://doi.org/10.1016/0743-1066(84)90014-1}.
\item M.~Samer and S.~Szeider, ``A Fixed-Parameter Algorithm for \#SAT with
Parameter Incidence Treewidth,'' 2006,
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0610174}.
\item D.~Lokshtanov, D.~Marx, and S.~Saurabh, ``Known Algorithms on Graphs
of Bounded Treewidth are Probably Optimal,'' 2010/2011,
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.5450}.
\item J.~Döcker, ``Monotone 3-Sat-(2,2) is NP-complete,'' 2019/2021,
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08032}.
\item T.~Baker, J.~Gill, and R.~Solovay, ``Relativizations of the
$P=?NP$ question,'' 1975, \url{https://doi.org/10.1137/0204037};

No relativizing diagonalization, nonuniform circuit lower bound, Natural
Proofs argument, or algebrizing identity is used in the exact counterexample.
Conversely, none of those barriers supplies the missing positive transfer.
A lower bound for OBDDs, branching programs, DNNF, proof systems, or a chosen
tree-decomposition DP remains a lower bound for that representation until a
uniform compiler forces every decider's same execution into it with
preserved costs.  Nonuniform per-input choice of a decomposition or circuit
cannot be silently substituted for a uniform SAT machine.

\section{Surviving arrow and P=NP audit}
The most economical surviving statement is not ``large treewidth implies
large work.''  It is the explicitly quantified transfer below.
\[
\tag{TW-MESSAGE-FORCING1}
\begin{split}
&\forall A\in\mathcal A_{\SAT}^{\rm det,unif}\ \exists C_A\ \forall
F\in\mathcal H_{2,2}:\\
&\quad C_A\text{ runs before the SAT bit is known, in }N^{c}\text{ time
with fixed }c\text{ independent of }A,\\
&\quad C_A\text{ maps the same execution to a specified separator-message
protocol }J(F),\\
&\quad \operatorname{cost}(J(F))\le O(\operatorname{cost}(\tau_A(F)))
\text{ including source access, reset, replay, and space},\\
&\quad \text{and an unconditional same-model theorem gives }
\operatorname{cost}(J(F))>N^d\text{ for every fixed }d
\text{ on some }F.
\end{split}
\]
RT-0006 supplies the hostile source representation $Q=\enc(F)$: a decider
may reread the source and recompute residual answers.  Therefore a
description-size or acquisition-only lower bound for $J$ is insufficient.
The missing arrow is \emph{joint forced-interface evaluation} in the same
realization, plus a nonrelativizing/nonalgebrizing unconditional endpoint.

Assume $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$ and let $A$ be an $N^K$ SAT decider.  A
decomposition-DP implementation gives one optional route; $A$ may ignore it.
A constructor that first calls $A$ has local exponent at least $K$, exactly
the SYNTHESIS-0002 failure.  A source-program service evaluates a residual of
length $N^a$ in exponent $aK$ plus construction, exactly the RT-0006 ledger.
No contradiction follows unless TW-MESSAGE-FORCING1 eliminates $K$ before
current-scale decision and lands in an unconditional same-model forbidden
region.  This run finds no such theorem.
