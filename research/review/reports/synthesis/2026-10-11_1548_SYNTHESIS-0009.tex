\section{Clean core and exact target}

The clean core remains duplicate-free proper Monotone
$3$-SAT$^*$-$(2,2)$: every clause has three distinct variables and one sign,
and every variable occurs twice with each sign.  For the canonical indexed
encoding, $N_F=\Theta(n\log n)$.  The universal lower-bound target is still
\[
\forall A\in\mathrm{Dec}_{\rm unif}(3\mathrm{SAT})\ \forall d\ \forall N_0\
\exists F\in\mathcal H_{2,2}:
N_F\ge N_0\ \wedge\ T_A(F)>N_F^d.
\]
It is \status{Open}.  A structural index theorem does not imply this
all-algorithm statement.

The model-bridge alternative remains: from one assumed polynomial SAT
decider derive one single random-access realization in the FLMV model with a
forbidden simultaneous time--space pair.  At the current target,
\[
T(N)=N^{6/5+o(1)},\qquad S(N)=N^{1/2+o(1)},
\]
and FLMV's displayed frontier at $c=6/5$ is $g(c)=23/45>1/2$.

\section{OFFLINE-INDEX-SPACE-GATE1}

Let a reduction produce an explicit formula of $M=N^{q+o(1)}$ encoded bits.
Suppose its realization materializes an auxiliary forward/inverse table with
$\Theta(M)$ bits, as in the verified TESTS-0008 compiler.  If the child
algorithm uses $M^{p+o(1)}$ workspace, the composed writable-space exponent
is at least
\[
P_{\rm mat}=\max\{q,qp\}=q\qquad(q\ge1, 0\le p\le1).
\]
At $p=1/2$, even a quasilinear $q=1+o(1)$ reduction has essentially linear,
not square-root, workspace.  Calling the completed table ``read-only input''
after constructing it does not repair the same execution: its production and
storage must still be charged.  Donating it as nonuniform advice changes the
model and uniform quantifier.

By contrast, a genuine on-demand generator with logarithmic local workspace
would contribute the child term $N^{qp+o(1)}$ plus its own explicitly charged
workspace.  This explains precisely why the remaining target is forward-port
locality, not an offline inverse table.

This theorem concerns the actual materialized realization.  It is not an
information-theoretic lower bound against every succinct data structure.
Succinct rank/select indexes show why that stronger claim would require a
separate theorem.  Nor does the calculation lower-bound arbitrary SAT
computation.

\section{Primary-literature preflight}

The two obstacles were (i) clause-output locality versus port-label locality,
and (ii) offline materialization versus simultaneous time--space locality.
The primary sources checked were Jahanjou--Miles--Viola,
\emph{Local reductions}, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.3171}, and
Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas, \emph{Time-space lower bounds for
satisfiability}, \url{https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/tst.pdf}.
The former directly transfers clause-bit locality but does not state the
needed inverse/port interface; the latter directly supplies the endpoint only
after one model-matched machine has both bounds.

Raman--Raman--Rao indexable dictionaries and P\u{a}tra\c{s}cu's succinct
rank/select work are \status{Useful Structural Analogy Requiring A New
Transfer Theorem}: they justify compact explicit indexes, not free on-demand
ports for a succinct Cook generator.  Hellman-style inversion preprocessing
is also analogy only.  Importing cryptographic hardness or random-oracle
assumptions would be a \status{Failed Transfer}.

\section{Barrier and SAME\_REALIZATION audit}

The new gate relativizes and is merely an accounting identity; it supplies no
nonrelativizing hinge.  It does not evade algebrization and constructs no
Natural-Proofs property.  Nonuniform advice deletes the online cost but also
deletes the required uniform model.  Arbitrary polynomial exponents are not
normalized.  A one-bit decider need not reveal ports or indices.  Most
importantly, the time of an on-demand accessor cannot be combined with the
space of a different materialized-index implementation.

The PQ ledger yields no exact transfer here.  LWE-style external extraction,
collision entropy, Schur squares, quadratic relations, Hilbert functions and
special soundness are \status{Analogy/Candidate} at best; none supplies the
missing forward-port theorem, and exploitable algebraic structure may make
the instances easier.
