\begin{abstract}
SYNTHESIS-0008 proved that a uniformly recognizable constant-answer island
defeats any pointwise all-decider lower bound.  A tempting repair is to demand
that every infinite clean-core subfamily contain both SAT and UNSAT instances.
That repair fails.  If a uniformly recognizable subfamily has any uniform
polynomial-time classifier for the language restricted to it, a recognize-first
wrapper decides every member at recognizer-plus-classifier cost and never calls
the arbitrary fallback solver.  This report proves that guard exactly and gives
a mixed-answer family in ordinary Monotone 3-SAT-$(2,2)$: canonical paired
$K_{3,3}$ blocks are satisfiable, while adjoining D\"ocker's fixed 198-variable,
264-clause unsatisfiable component makes the disjoint union unsatisfiable.  Both
answers occur infinitely often and encoded length is $\Theta(k\log k)$.
Consequently, ``not monochromatic'' does not repair pointwise compatibility
extraction, state-exposure, memory, communication, or superpolynomial-time
necessity.  The worst-case source-pass target in SYNTHESIS-0012 is untouched.
A bounded audit passed 4,009,386 checks.  P versus NP remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Barrier-transfer literature preflight}

The two obstacles were: (i) distinguishing a large semantic family from an
unavoidable execution resource, and (ii) preserving both answers inside the
exact clean core.  Schaefer's dichotomy supplies primary evidence that many
nontrivial Boolean constraint languages with both answers are polynomial-time
decidable; answer variation is not hardness \cite{schaefer}.  D\"ocker proves
both an unsatisfiable ordinary Monotone 3-SAT-$(2,2)$ instance with 198
variables and 264 clauses and that the complementary-clause special case is
always satisfiable \cite{docker}.  These are directly transferable at the
formula level.  Hopcroft--Karp is relevant only as an algorithmic analogy for
general bipartite matching \cite{hk}; the explicit $K_{3,3}$ blocks below use
the diagonal matching and need no matching lower bound.

The electronics/VLSI slogan ``every signal must be routed'' is a misleading
transfer here: a circuit lower bound needs fixed terminals, fan-out, locality
and layout, none of which is forced on an arbitrary Turing-machine SAT decider.
Control-theoretic observability is likewise only an analogy: the one-bit SAT
output does not make a chosen compatibility object an observable state.
Communication-cut and proof-system lower bounds remain model-specific until a
SAME\_REALIZATION compiler is proved.  Coding-theoretic Schur squares and
quadratic-relation spaces attach to a chosen code representation; the wrapper
below never constructs that code.  Thus the only exact transfer used in this
run is the finite formula construction and the source-code guard theorem.

\section{Exact target and quantifiers}

Let $L\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$ be a decidable language and let $C$ be a declared
clean-core set.  A typical pointwise target has the form
\begin{equation}
 \exists b\ \forall A\ \exists N_0\ \forall x\in C:
 |x|\ge N_0\Longrightarrow
 \operatorname{cost}_{A}(x)\ge b(|x|),
 \tag{PW}
\end{equation}
where $A$ ranges over all uniform deterministic total exact deciders for $L$.
Here cost may be time, peak workspace, communication, or an asserted mandatory
materialization event.  For a superpolynomial lower bound, $b$ eventually
dominates every polynomial.  The extraction version replaces the last
inequality by ``the actual execution of $A(x)$ materializes the prescribed
compatibility object.''

The project needs the different worst-case prefix
\begin{equation}
 \forall A\ \forall d\ \forall N_0\ \exists x\in C:
 |x|\ge N_0\ \land\ \operatorname{time}_{A}(x)>|x|^d.
 \tag{WC}
\end{equation}
The theorem below falsifies (PW) whenever $C$ contains a suitable easy island;
it says nothing against (WC), since the wrapper can fall back elsewhere.

\begin{definition}[Recognizable easy island]
A set $S\subseteq C$ is a uniformly recognizable easy island for $L$ if one
uniform deterministic machine $R$ recognizes $S$ and one uniform deterministic
machine $B$ satisfies $B(x)=1$ iff $x\in L$ for every $x\in S$.  No correctness
is required of $B$ off $S$.  Let their fully charged time/space bounds be
$(r,s)$ and $(q,t)$, including parsing and the one-bit output.
\end{definition}

\section{The guard theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{RECOGNIZABLE-EASY-ISLAND-BARRIER1}]
For every uniform deterministic total exact decider $A$ for $L$, the machine
\[
 A_{R,B}(x)=
 \begin{cases}
 B(x),&R(x)=1,\\
 A(x),&R(x)=0
 \end{cases}
 \tag{1}
\]
is a uniform deterministic total exact decider for $L$.  On every $x\in S$ it
runs in $O(r(|x|)+q(|x|))$ time and
$O(\max\{s(|x|),t(|x|)\})$ reusable workspace (or the conservative sum if
memory is not reused).  The fallback $A$ is not invoked on those inputs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $x\in S$, recognition accepts and the definition of $B$ gives the exact
language bit.  If $x\notin S$, recognition rejects and total exactness of $A$
gives the bit.  Sequential composition gives the displayed charges.  On
$x\in S$, control returns after $B$, so no instruction, state, query, message,
or representation used only by $A$ is reached.  This is one concrete execution,
not an extensional comparison between different runs.
\end{proof}

The theorem is independent of whether $L$ is in P.  Under the hypothetical
$P=NP$, one may choose a polynomial $A$ and obtains only another polynomial
machine; no unconditional contradiction follows.  Conversely, demanding that
$S$ contain both language answers changes none of the proof.

\section{A mixed-answer exact-$(2,2)$ island}

Use a canonical binary DIMACS-like encoding in which variable indices occupy
$\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$ bits and signed clauses and component boundaries have
self-delimiting separators.  Let $P_k$ be the disjoint union of $k$ canonically
numbered $K_{3,3}$ edge blocks.  For every vertex include its three incident
edge variables as a positive clause and also the complementary negative
clause.  Every edge variable lies in two vertex neighborhoods, hence occurs
exactly twice positively and twice negatively.  All clauses contain three
distinct variables and all signed clauses are distinct.  The diagonal perfect
matching selects exactly one variable in each vertex neighborhood; therefore
every positive clause has one true literal and every negative clause has two
true literals.  Thus $P_k$ is satisfiable.

Let $U$ be D\"ocker's fixed ordinary Monotone 3-SAT-$(2,2)$ unsatisfiable
instance with $n_U=198$ and $m_U=264$ \cite[Proposition~1]{docker}.  This
component is \KNOWN{}; the experiment did not reconstruct or independently
SAT-check its 264 clauses.  With fresh canonical variable names define
\[
 S^+=\{\operatorname{enc}(P_k):k\ge1\},\qquad
 S^-=\{\operatorname{enc}(P_k\sqcup U):k\ge1\},\qquad S=S^+\cup S^-.
 \tag{2}
\]
Disjoint union preserves exact occurrence counts.  It preserves SAT for
$P_k$ and is UNSAT when $U$ is a component.  Each of $S^+,S^-$ is infinite.
For $P_k$, $n=9k$ and $m=12k$; adjoining $U$ adds constants.  Hence the
encoded length is
\[
 N_k=\Theta(m\log n)=\Theta(k\log k).
 \tag{3}
\]

A uniform recognizer parses the canonical syntax, checks the block pattern and
optionally the fixed suffix $U$; a restricted classifier returns 1 without the
suffix and 0 with it.  Even a simple comparison/generation implementation is
polynomial in $N_k$.  Therefore (2) is a mixed-answer recognizable easy island.
The guard theorem supplies a total exact SAT decider whose executions on all
members of (2) do not enumerate assignments, expose a compatibility matrix,
construct a residual DAG, communicate all branches, or call the fallback.

\begin{corollary}[\texttt{NONMONOCHROMATIC-REPAIR1}]
The proposed repair ``exclude only constant-answer islands'' is
\RETIRED{}.  In particular no superpolynomial pointwise lower bound, nor a
pointwise all-decider materialization necessity, follows from the facts that
the family is infinite, exact-$(2,2)$, and contains infinitely many YES and NO
instances.
\end{corollary}

This does not make all of $\Htwo$ easy.  D\"ocker's NP-completeness result is a
\KNOWN{} worst-case theorem, and (WC) remains exactly the missing implication.
Nor does the construction refute an input-reading lower bound: the recognizer
must inspect enough source bits to certify canonical membership.

\paragraph{Affine, Horn, 2SAT and bounded treewidth.}
Each admits mixed YES/NO recognizable subfamilies and a semantic shortcut.
Gaussian elimination, implication closure/SCCs, and bounded-width dynamic
programming reinforce the theorem: visible combinatorial state count need not
be the actual state exposed by the decider.

\paragraph{Products, padding and redundant clauses.}
For disjoint products the answer is the conjunction of component answers, so a
single recognized fixed UNSAT component decides the product.  A declared
padding grammar may be recognized and stripped at charged polynomial cost.
Arbitrary padding, renaming, alternate serialization, or redundant clauses can
make the canonical recognizer reject, in which case the wrapper safely calls
$A$.  Thus the counterexample is representation-sensitive but still defeats
the universal pointwise quantifier on its included canonical encodings; it does
not establish a representation-invariant easy core.

\paragraph{Compact representations and one-bit output.}
A small circuit or branching program for membership in (2) is an algorithmic
shortcut, not evidence for arbitrary 3SAT.  The interface returns only one bit;
no reconstruction object is demanded.  Any extraction theorem must therefore
be external and source-specific or must prove why that single bit forces the
chosen internal object in the same run.

\section{Barriers, nonuniformity and post-quantum ledger}

The guard construction relativizes: $R$ and $B$ may ignore an oracle and the
fallback may use it only off $S$.  It also survives algebraic-oracle access.
Accordingly it cannot supply the nonrelativizing/nonalgebrizing final hinge
required by Baker--Gill--Solovay and Aaronson--Wigderson
\cite{bgs,aw}.  The argument is not a large constructive circuit property, so
the Razborov--Rudich Natural Proofs framework is not the operative obstruction
\cite{rr}; any future circuit lower bound must nevertheless audit it.  Uniform
$R,B$ already suffice, so allowing nonuniform advice only strengthens the
counterexample.  Polynomial exponents remain arbitrary.

For the post-quantum ledger: Schur squares, shortening/puncturing, low-rank
quadratic relations, Hilbert functions and syzygies are \textbf{FAILED
TRANSFER} because the wrapper constructs no code.  Lossiness is an
\textbf{EXACT OBJECT/THEOREM TRANSFER} only for the finite map actually chosen:
the classifier collapses all of $S^+$ to 1 and all of $S^-$ to 0, demonstrating
high collision rather than time hardness.  The LWE worst-case/average-case
architecture and black-box solver extraction are
\textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE needing a new bridge}; no unconditional NP
worst-case/average-case theorem is supplied.  Special soundness,
collapse-binding and hash/random-oracle methods are \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER}
without an explicit unconditional witness-extraction theorem.  Here hidden
structure is exploitable by the classifier and is therefore a weakness, not a
hardness source.

\section{DUPLICATION / CONTRADICTION AUDIT}

The source-code wrapper is a strict generalization of SYNTHESIS-0008's
constant-answer theorem, not a new method.  The new content is the removal of
the monochromatic premise and the explicit mixed-answer exact-$(2,2)$ family.
TESTS-0012's one-branch matching shortcut supplies the paired YES side but did
not add a recognizable UNSAT side.  RT-0012 already showed a structurally rich
connected formula with a matching shortcut; this report does not relabel that
finite witness as an asymptotic theorem.  LB-0016's exponential output bound

not implement that interface.

\section{Surviving implication}

The smallest viable repair is not another intrinsic richness condition.  It is
the worst-case quantifier (WC), instantiated by one externally fixed source
transformation whose actual execution forces a charged pass or lands in a
model-matched unconditional lower-bound region.  Recognition, parsing,
compiler work, child access, replay, reconstruction, peak space and the
one-bit endpoint must be charged in the SAME\_REALIZATION.  That solver-
independent implication, arbitrary-exponent elimination, and a
nonrelativizing/nonalgebrizing contradiction remain \OPEN{}.  P versus NP
remains open.
