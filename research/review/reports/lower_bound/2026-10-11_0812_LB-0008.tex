\section{The paired cubic-bipartite family}
\begin{definition}[Paired incidence formula]
Let $G=(U\mathbin{\dot\cup}W,E)$ be a finite simple $3$-regular bipartite
graph.  Introduce one Boolean variable $x_e$ for every edge $e\in E$.  For
each vertex $v\in U\cup W$, put
\[
 C_v^+=\bigvee_{e\ni v}x_e,
 \qquad
 C_v^-=\bigvee_{e\ni v}\neg x_e,
 \qquad
 F_G=\bigwedge_{v\in U\cup W}(C_v^+\wedge C_v^-).
\]
\end{definition}

If $|U|=|W|=r$, then $G$ has $2r$ vertices and $3r$ edges; $F_G$ has
$3r$ variables and $4r$ clauses.  Every clause contains three distinct
variables and is all-positive or all-negative.  Each edge variable occurs
at its two endpoints, exactly twice positively and twice negatively.
Simplicity prevents duplicate endpoint triples.  Thus
\(F_G\in\mathcal H_{2,2}\).  Under the project's indexed self-delimiting
encoding,
\[
 N_G=|\operatorname{enc}(F_G)|=\Theta(r\log r).
\]

\begin{theorem}[CLEANCORE-EXPANDER-GUARD1]
There are a constant $\varepsilon>0$ and an infinite family of simple
$3$-regular bipartite graphs $G_r$ with algebraic connectivity
$\lambda_2(G_r)\ge\varepsilon$ such that the associated formulas satisfy
all of the following:
\[
 F_{G_r}\in\mathcal H_{2,2},\qquad
 \tw(P(F_{G_r}))\ge
 \frac{3r\varepsilon}{4+\varepsilon}-1
 =\Omega(N_G/\log N_G),
\]
yet every $F_{G_r}$ is satisfiable and a satisfying assignment is computable
uniformly in polynomial time.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Infinite families of $3$-regular bipartite spectral expanders are known; in
particular, Chiu gives explicit cubic Ramanujan graphs, and the modern
expander literature records the bipartite cubic family and a uniform
positive algebraic-connectivity bound.  Choose such a family.

The primal graph of $F_G$ has one vertex for each edge of $G$, with two
variables adjacent exactly when their edges share an endpoint.  Therefore
\[
 P(F_G)=L(G),
\]
the line graph of $G$.  Let $B$ be the unsigned vertex--edge incidence
matrix.  Since $G$ is cubic,
\[
 BB^{\mathsf T}=3I+A(G),\qquad
 B^{\mathsf T}B=2I+A(L(G)).
\]
The nonzero eigenvalues of the two Gram matrices agree.  Consequently the
nonzero Laplacian spectrum of $G$ appears in the Laplacian spectrum of
$L(G)$, with the remaining line-graph eigenvalues equal to $6$; in
particular
\[
 \lambda_2(L(G))=\lambda_2(G).
\]
The line graph has $3r$ vertices and maximum degree $4$.  Applying Gima,
Hanaka, Noro, Ono, and Otachi's spectral treewidth theorem
\[
 \tw(H)\ge \frac{|V(H)|\lambda_2(H)}{\Delta(H)+\lambda_2(H)}-1
\]
gives the displayed lower bound.

Finally, every regular bipartite graph has a perfect matching $M$.  Set
$x_e=1$ for $e\in M$ and $x_e=0$ otherwise.  Each cubic vertex has exactly
one incident matching edge and two nonmatching edges, so both $C_v^+$ and
$C_v^-$ are true.  Standard bipartite matching constructs $M$ in polynomial
time.  Thus the same source family that has linear-in-$r$ primal treewidth
has an efficiently exploitable global certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Inside-core separator tables are optional]
The quantity $2^{\tw(P(F))+1}$ cannot, by itself, lower-bound time, space,
communication, state exposure, compatibility extraction, or predecision
service cost for arbitrary deterministic uniform exact SAT deciders, even
when $F\in\mathcal H_{2,2}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On the theorem family the quantity is
$2^{\Omega(N/\log N)}$, whereas a uniform partial procedure verifies the
paired-clause incidence form, checks that the reconstructed cubic graph is
bipartite, finds a perfect matching, and returns SAT in polynomial time.  A
one-bit version may return SAT after recognition without materializing any
separator table.  An arbitrary total SAT decider may run this procedure on
the recognized subclass and use any exact fallback elsewhere.
\end{proof}

The recognition claim is source-checkable.  Pair every positive clause with
the negative clause on the same variable triple; make each pair a graph
vertex; and make each Boolean variable an edge joining the two clause pairs
in which it occurs.  The declared $(2,2)$ incidence makes the reconstructed
graph cubic.  Bipartiteness and matching are then ordinary graph operations.
This is a partial solver, not an algorithm for arbitrary
\(\mathcal H_{2,2}\).

\section{Hostile controls and arbitrary-algorithm audit}
\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.0cm,style=nextline]
\item[Affine elimination.] The line-graph spectrum is an exact algebraic
invariant, but the matching assignment exploits rather than suffers from the
hidden structure.  As with Tseitin systems and Gaussian elimination, visible
algebra can make a high-width source easier.
\item[Alternative encodings.] Variable renaming, clause ordering, and global
complementation preserve the family after the corresponding decoder.
Indexed length remains $\Theta(r\log r)$.  A compact graph-plus-rule encoding
would make the representation smaller and therefore cannot establish an
ordinary-CNF lower bound without a charged decoder.
\item[One-bit output.] The SAT answer is always one on this subclass.  A
decider need not emit the matching, bag states, or residual service.
\item[Wrappers.] COPY and DEAD padding preserve semantics but can leave the
clean core.  INTERP composes the recognizer with a renaming.  RECOMP reruns
matching instead of storing a witness.  Independent products remain easy and
their treewidth is the component maximum.
\item[Communication.] A separator-table protocol is one optional algorithm.
The matching route does not furnish the same messages, so no lower bound on
that protocol transfers to the actual one-bit execution.
\end{description}

\section{PREDECISION-SERVICE1 and the $P=NP$ audit}
RT-0006 proves that retaining the source plus a fixed program gives an
$N+O_A(1)$-bit representation of any uniformly compiled residual service,
with query time retaining the supplied solver exponent.  The present theorem
adds a different obstruction: even if a separator service has
$2^{\Omega(N/\log N)}$ potential bag states, a source recognizer can answer
the endpoint through matching before acquiring that service.

Assume $\Pclass=\NPclass$ and let $A$ be an $N^{K+o(1)}$ exact SAT decider.
On the paired family, the matching branch is polynomial with a fixed degree;
off the family, $A$ remains a legal fallback.  A constructor that instead
runs $A$ on current-scale residuals retains $K$, while a theorem asserting
that every solver must supply the separator service is contradicted by the
matching branch.  No unconditional contradiction follows.  The surviving
target must be a joint forced-interface theorem whose premise is not merely
treewidth, bag-table cardinality, or source description size.

\section{Barrier-transfer and post-quantum ledger}
The graph transfers are exact only at the structural level.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.1cm,style=nextline]
\item[THEOREM TRANSFER.] Cubic bipartite expansion transfers through the
incidence identities to constant algebraic connectivity of the primal line
graph; the spectral treewidth theorem then gives the displayed structural
lower bound.  Object correspondence and numerical parameters match exactly.
\item[FAILED TRANSFER.] Treewidth, VLSI routing, separator communication, or
dynamic-programming table size does not lower-bound an unrestricted random-
access SAT execution.  The matching solver is an explicit counterroute.
\item[ANALOGY / CANDIDATE.] A future proof could seek a representation-
covariant service with an independent joint evaluation lower bound, but it
must prove an arbitrary-decider compiler and defeat source replay, matching,
Gaussian elimination, DIRECT, and RECOMP.
\end{description}

The construction and its counterroute relativize: an oracle-enhanced machine
may still recognize the family or use matching.  Algebraic-extension access
only strengthens the structural shortcut.  No Natural-Proofs property is
used.  Hence Baker--Gill--Solovay, algebrization, and Natural Proofs remain
barrier audits, not promotion theorems.

For the post-quantum ledger, the line-graph Gram identities are an
\status{EXACT OBJECT/THEOREM TRANSFER}.  Schur-square dimensions,
quadratic-relation ranks, Hilbert functions, syzygies, and collision entropy
are \status{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE} only; none binds them to an arbitrary SAT
execution.  Hidden algebraic/combinatorial structure is exploitable here,
exactly as the ledger requires agents to test.  Hash or random-oracle binding
is a \status{FAILED TRANSFER} for an unconditional separation.

indexed service on a non-product, non-paired clean-core family and prove or

representation class containing source plus program.  Both arrows are
\status{OPEN}; either one may already be separation-strength.
