\begin{abstract}
The proposed repair to EXTRACTOR/G2 asks an arbitrary SAT decider to support
a large indexed residual or pinning service before returning its one-bit
answer.  This report isolates four different contracts and proves an exact
simulation theorem.  A single coordinate is one ordinary residual SAT call;
a polynomial batch is a polynomial number of such calls and retains the
unknown SAT exponent; the source plus the fixed decider is already a compact
evaluator for all coordinates; and an explicit exponential table has an
output-size lower bound only because it changes the task.  A genuine
preprocessing/query data-structure lower bound is model-specific and does not
bind the original one-bit SAT execution.  A bounded audit passed 50,178
semantic, batch, wrapper, product, easy-family, recomputation, and accounting
checks.  P versus NP remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exact service-contract theorem}

Let $L\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$, let $R(x,q)$ be a total uniform compiler, and
define the indexed service
\[
S_x(q)=\mathbf 1[R(x,q)\in L].                            \tag{3}
\]
On total input length $n=|x|+|q|$, suppose $R$ runs in time $C_R(n)$,
uses writable space $W_R(n)$, and writes at most $p(n)$ bits.  Let $A$
decide $L$ in time $T_A(m)$ and writable space $S_A(m)$.

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{RESTRICTION-SERVICE-SIMULATION1}]
The following statements hold uniformly.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.2cm,style=nextline]
\item[Coordinate.] There is an evaluator $E_{A}$ for (3) with
\[
T_E(n)\le C_R(n)+T_A(p(n))+O(1),\qquad
S_E(n)\le W_R(n)+S_A(p(n))+p(n)+O(1),                  \tag{4}
\]
where the $p(n)$ term may be replaced by the charged buffer/access cost of
a valid streaming implementation.
\item[Batch.] A list of $B$ queries is answered sequentially within
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{B}\bigl(C_R(n_i)+T_A(p(n_i))+O(1)\bigr),    \tag{5}
\]
plus the required $B$-bit output.  In exponent notation, if
$B=N^{\alpha+o(1)}$, every compiled residual has length at most
$N^{\beta+o(1)}$, and $T_A(m)=m^{K+o(1)}$, this route contributes
\[
N^{\alpha+\beta K+o(1)}.                               \tag{6}
\]
\item[Compact evaluator.] A self-contained description consisting of $x$,
the fixed code of $A$ and $R$, and delimiters has length
\[
|x|+|A|+|R|+O(\log |x|),                               \tag{7}
\]
and evaluates every coordinate using (4).  If $x$ remains on a declared
read-only source, a source-bound evaluator can retain a pointer/accessor
instead; if source-free self-containment is required, copying $x$ is a
linear charged operation.
\item[Explicit table.] If the required output is the vector
$(S_x(q))_{q\in Q_x}$, any ordinary explicit-output machine writes

$|Q_x|$ bits and therefore takes $\Omega(|Q_x|)$ output steps.  This is
an output lower bound for the table-generation problem, not for deciding
one coordinate or deciding $x\in L$.
\end{description}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For one coordinate, compute $R(x,q)$, run $A$ on the compiled instance,
and return its bit.  This proves (4), with the displayed conservative explicit
buffer; a source-certified streaming composition may replace that term but
cannot omit its access cost.  Sequential repetition gives (5), and substituting
the exponent assumptions gives (6).  A universal interpreter supplied with
$x,A,R$ implements the same evaluator and has description length (7).
Finally, an explicit vector has one output position per query and must be
written.  No step asserts that the original decision execution used this
evaluator or emitted the vector.
\end{proof}

For SAT, $R(F,\rho)=F\!\upharpoonright_\rho$, with ordinary syntactic
simplification.  The residual is no larger than the explicit source up to the
declared encoding overhead.  Thus the theorem applies to arbitrary CNF, not
only the paired flow family of RT-0007.

\begin{corollary}[Contract separation]
An exponential number of possible restrictions cannot by itself imply
superpolynomial time, space, communication, or state exposure for a requested
coordinate or for the original one-bit SAT decision.  A lower bound can arise
only after specifying a stronger output or a restricted preprocessing/query
model and proving its hypotheses for the same execution.
\end{corollary}

\section{Effect on PREDECISION-SERVICE1}

The theorem does not prove that every imaginable service route fails.  It
closes the generic cardinality argument and exposes the remaining burden.
There are four distinct contracts.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.3cm,style=nextline]
\item[One requested coordinate.] Equation (4) is merely SAT on a residual.
Any lower bound strong enough to exclude every polynomial evaluator is
already separation-strength.
\item[Polynomial batch.] Equation (6) retains $K$.  It supplies no
$K$-independent contradiction and does not show that the original execution
paid the batch cost.
\item[Full explicit table.] The output-size bound is exact but applies to a
different function problem with exponentially many required output bits.
\item[Preprocessing/query structure.] A legitimate lower bound must fix
preprocessing time, retained space, word size, access model, query time,
randomization/error, and whether the source remains accessible.  It then
lower-bounds that data-structure model, not unrestricted one-shot SAT, unless
a separate compiler forces the model on every decider.
\end{description}

Therefore \texttt{FORCED-NONOPTIONAL-SERVICE1} remains \OPEN, but it can no
longer use service cardinality, source description length, or ordinary
coordinate evaluation as its lower-bound resource.  It needs both an
independent service lower bound and a representation-covariant compiler that
acts before current-scale SAT work with fully charged overhead.

\section{The $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$ and exponent audits}

Assume $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$ and fix a uniform SAT decider with running
time $M^{K+o(1)}$.  Equation (4) places every polynomially compiled
restriction coordinate in P, exactly as the assumption predicts.  Equation
(6) places every polynomially encoded batch in P but retains
$\alpha+\beta K$.  An exponential explicit table requires exponential
output length but SAT never asks for that table.  No contradiction follows.

Dell and van Melkebeek prove strong sparsification and oracle-communication
lower bounds under the hypothesis $\mathsf{coNP}\not\subseteq\mathsf{NP/poly}$.
That hypothesis cannot be inserted into a proof by contradiction from
$\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$, because the latter gives
$\mathsf{coNP}=\mathsf P\subseteq\mathsf{P/poly}\subseteq\mathsf{NP/poly}$.
Their theorem is an exact conditional compression boundary, not the missing
unconditional contradiction.

\section{Hostile controls and barriers}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.1cm,style=nextline]
\item[Easy families.] Horn, 2-SAT, affine, unit, bounded-component, matching,
and flow families all satisfy the semantic service identity, while their
coordinates may have specialized polynomial evaluators.  A service definition
does not erase exploitable structure.
\item[Alternative encodings.] Renaming, complementation, clause order, COPY,
DEAD, and interpreter wrappers preserve the answer after their charged
decoders.  RECOMP answers a coordinate by rerunning the evaluator and stores
no table.
\item[One-bit output.] The original SAT contract is one bit.  A proof cannot
charge unrequested coordinates to that execution.
\item[Relativization.] The simulation relativizes by replacing $A$ with
$A^O$.  A black-box or oracle-only forced-service argument therefore does not
cross the Baker--Gill--Solovay barrier.
\item[Algebrization.] Low-degree or algebraic-oracle access can only strengthen
the evaluator.  No line of the classification supplies a nonalgebrizing hinge.
\item[Natural Proofs.] No large constructive circuit property is used.  If a
future service lower bound is converted to general circuit lower bounds, its
largeness, constructivity, usefulness, and cryptographic hypotheses must be
audited separately.
\end{description}

\section{Literature and cross-domain transfer audit}

Fortnow, Lipton, van Melkebeek, and Viglas give genuine SAT time--space lower
bounds on specified random-access models.  Their model and exponent region do
not quantify over all polynomial exponents and do not force a residual service.
Dell--van Melkebeek's communication theorem is a genuine conditional
compression theorem, but its noncollapse hypothesis is unavailable under the
counterfactual $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$.  Aaronson--Wigderson and
Baker--Gill--Solovay remain mandatory barrier tests.

Static and dynamic cell-probe lower bounds are a useful cross-domain model.
For example, Chakraborty, Kamma, and Larsen prove a storage--query tradeoff for
succinct Boolean matrix--vector multiplication when the retained side
information and cell probes are explicitly bounded.  The object
correspondence is exact only after a SAT service is compiled into that fixed
data-structure model.  No current theorem forces a one-shot random-access SAT
decider to retain that representation.  Classification:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.4cm,style=nextline]
\item[THEOREM TRANSFER.] Output length lower-bounds time when the explicit
service vector itself is the required output.
\item[ANALOGY / CANDIDATE.] Cell-probe storage/query tradeoffs suggest what a
proper service theorem must declare, but need a same-execution SAT compiler.
\item[FAILED TRANSFER.] Communication, cell-probe, VLSI routing, separator,
or proof-system lower bounds do not automatically lower-bound unrestricted
SAT decision time.
\end{description}

\section{Post-quantum technique ledger}

The source-plus-program evaluator is an algorithmic exploitation of compact
structure.  Lossiness and collision entropy of a proof-to-node map are
\textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} for this barrier because the evaluator need not use
such a map.  Schur squares, quadratic-relation ranks, Hilbert functions,
syzygies, and graded Betti numbers are also \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} absent a
forced code/algebra object in the same execution.  Worst-case/average-case
extraction and collision-extraction or special-soundness are
\textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}; they require a new unconditional bridge and cannot
import cryptographic hardness.  Hash and random-oracle binding is inadmissible
for an unconditional separation.
