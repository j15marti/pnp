\begin{abstract}
This run formalizes the obstruction left by the white-box EXTRACTOR audits.
If an extractor applied to every exact SAT implementation produces
representatives whose quotient class is independent of the implementation,
then that quotient class is a function of the source formula.  If the
extractor and a canonicalizer are uniformly efficient, one may fix any one
exact SAT decider and compute the canonical class externally.  More
quantitatively, if extraction performs $N^{c+o(1)}$ local work and at most
$N^{b+o(1)}$ calls to a fixed $M^{K+o(1)}$ SAT decider on inputs of length at
most $N^{a+o(1)}$, canonicalization costs $N^{d+o(1)}$, and all access is
charged, then the source functional is computable in
\[
 N^{\max\{d,c,b+aK\}+o(1)}.
\]
Thus current-scale invariant extraction retains the unknown exponent $K$;
source-only extraction does not establish same-execution cost; and an
inefficient canonicalizer merely moves the open acquisition theorem.  This
does not rule out a genuinely covariant, representation-sensitive extractor
with strictly fractional hereditary channels.  A bounded audit passed 80,806
checks.  P versus NP remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Claim card and clean core}

The source is a canonical indexed proper 3-CNF of complete encoded bit length
$N$.  The clean core $\Htwo$ consists of duplicate-free monotone clauses with
three distinct variables per clause and exactly two positive and two negative
occurrences of each variable.  Hence $m=4n/3$ and $N=\Theta(n\log n)$.
D\"ocker proves this restriction NP-complete, but NP-completeness supplies no
runtime lower bound.

The desired target remains
\[
 \forall A\ \forall d\ \forall N_0\ \exists F\in\Htwo:\quad
 |\operatorname{enc}(F)|\ge N_0
 \quad\land\quad
 T_A(F)>|\operatorname{enc}(F)|^d,                         \tag{1}
\]
where $A$ ranges over total correct uniform deterministic random-access
deciders and every source read, work bit, and output operation is charged.
Statement (1) is \OPEN and is not assumed below.

The attacked candidate has the exact prefix
\[
 \forall A,B\in\mathcal A_{\SAT}\ \forall F\in\Htwo:\quad
 \pi(E(A,F))=\pi(E(B,F)),                                  \tag{2}
\]
where $E$ is a uniform charged extractor, $E(A,F)$ is its representative,
and $\pi$ is a declared quotient map.  The candidate inference was that (2)
itself makes the quotient a cost paid by each execution of $A(F)$.  That
inference is false.

\section{Exact factorization and cost theorem}

\begin{definition}[Implementation quotient]
Let $\mathcal A_{\SAT}$ be a nonempty class of codes for total correct exact
SAT deciders.  Let $E(A,F)\in\mathcal Q$ be a total extractor and let
$\pi:\mathcal Q\to\mathcal Z$.  The quotient is implementation-invariant if
(2) holds.  A canonicalizer is a total map
$C:\pi(E(\mathcal A_{\SAT},\Htwo))\to\{0,1\}^*$ that gives one encoding for
each quotient value.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{IMPLEMENTATION-QUOTIENT-FACTOR1}]
If the quotient is implementation-invariant, there is a unique source
functional $W:\Htwo\to\mathcal Z$ satisfying
\[
 W(F)=\pi(E(A,F))\qquad
 \text{for every }A\in\mathcal A_{\SAT}.                  \tag{3}
\]
For any fixed $A_0\in\mathcal A_{\SAT}$,
\[
 W(F)=\pi(E(A_0,F)).                                       \tag{4}
\]
Consequently quotient invariance alone cannot establish that a different
implementation $A$ materialized, retained, or causally used a representative.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose any $A_0$ and define $W(F)=\pi(E(A_0,F))$.  Equation (2) makes this
independent of the choice.  It also yields (3) and uniqueness pointwise.
Equation (4) is the definition.  The final statement follows because (4) is
an external source computation using $A_0$; it contains no assertion about
the states of another implementation $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{CANONICAL-EXTRACTION-COST1}]
Fix $A_0$ with $T_{A_0}(M)\le M^{K+o(1)}$.  Suppose $E(A_0,F)$ performs at
most $N^{c+o(1)}$ charged local work and invokes $A_0$ at most
$N^{b+o(1)}$ times on explicitly constructed inputs of length at most
$N^{a+o(1)}$.  Suppose $\pi$ and $C$ together cost at most $N^{d+o(1)}$,
including representation access, decoding, output writing, and workspace.
Then $C(W(F))$ is uniformly computable from $F$ in
\[
 \boxed{N^{\max\{d,c,b+aK\}+o(1)}}.                       \tag{5}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Run the fixed program $E(A_0,F)$, substituting $A_0$ for each declared call,
then apply $\pi$ and $C$.  The aggregate call cost is
$N^{b+o(1)}(N^{a+o(1)})^{K+o(1)}=N^{b+aK+o(1)}$.
Taking the maximum with the charged local and canonicalization costs proves
(5).  This is a direct application of the verified polynomial-query
composition ledger, now attached to the implementation quotient.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Three-way guard]
An implementation-invariant extraction proposal satisfies at least one of the
following.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=0cm,labelwidth=0cm]
\item[] \textbf{Source route.}  It uses no solver-dependent computation; then
the object is externally source-computable and supplies no same-execution
cost theorem.
\item[] \textbf{Current-scale route.}  It invokes or replays a current-scale
SAT computation, so (5) retains the unknown exponent $K$.
\item[] \textbf{Unacquired quotient.}  Canonicalization, transport, or
representative construction lacks the required bound, so the acquisition
arrow remains open.
\end{description}
This trichotomy does not exclude strictly fractional recursive channels or a
separately proved nonrelativizing model-matched obstruction.
\end{corollary}

\section{What survives: covariance rather than invariance}

The theorem does not retire EXTRACTOR/G2.  It narrows the admissible object.
A useful successor must be implementation-sensitive but wrapper-covariant:
\[
 E(w(A),F)=S_w(E(A,F)),                                    \tag{6}
\]
where every transport $S_w$ is explicit and charged.  It must also survive
DIRECT and RECOMP, avoid a current-scale invocation before contraction, and
produce either an ordinary-CNF successor with fixed scale exponent $a<1$ or
another resource having an independently proved lower bound on the same
realization.  If one canonicalizes (6) back to a fully invariant value, the
factorization theorem applies to that canonical quotient.

The next theorem therefore cannot merely say that all solvers have the same
semantic object.  It must explain why acquiring an appropriate
implementation-specific representative is unavoidable and why efficient
transport does not collapse the claim to external source computation.

\section{Literature and cross-domain transfer audit}

The direct complexity literature reinforces the model boundary.  FLMV gives
simultaneous SAT time--space lower bounds only in its declared random-access
model; it does not force an arbitrary decider to expose this quotient.
Huang--Li--Zhong characterize several $\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}}$ range-
avoidance algorithms by circuit lower bounds for specified circuit classes;
this is an exact transfer with a fixed search object and oracle model, not a
generic extractor from every SAT execution.  Li--Tell's 7 October 2026
plasticity results derive low-space and alternating simulations from hardness
of a low-space range-avoidance problem under explicit hardness and
derandomization assumptions; those assumptions cannot enter an unconditional
$P$ versus $NP$ proof.  Chen--Hu--Ren's 2025 results show that several circuit
lower-bound approaches that already overcome relativization still algebrize.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0cm,labelwidth=0cm]
\item[] \textbf{THEOREM TRANSFER.}
Polynomial-query substitution and quotient factorization transfer exactly to
the declared extractor model.  Range-avoidance equivalences transfer only to
their stated circuit classes, output problem, and $\mathsf{FP}^{\mathsf{NP}}$
model.
\item[] \textbf{ANALOGY / CANDIDATE.}
LWE-style external extraction, special-soundness collision extraction, and
range avoidance suggest looking for a fixed search relation whose witness
forces a lower-bound consequence.  The missing bridge is an unconditional,
source-matched compiler from every SAT decider with all costs preserved.
\item[] \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER.}
Representation-independent data-structure, communication, VLSI, pebbling,
and fault-tolerance bounds constrain declared interfaces.  They do not show
that a one-bit unrestricted SAT computation instantiated that interface.
\end{description}

For the post-quantum ledger, quotient collision entropy is an exact object but
cannot distinguish implementations once the quotient is source-only.
Schur-square, quadratic-relation, Hilbert-function, and syzygy invariants may
still classify source structure; without a charged covariant compiler they
are \textbf{FAILED TRANSFER} for this target.  Cryptographic binding and
random-oracle assumptions are inadmissible.

\section{Barrier and hostile-control audit}

DIRECT and RECOMP need not materialize the representatives exposed by
MATERIALIZE or INTERP.  DEAD, COPY, and DELAY change raw traces while the
quotient stays fixed.  Renaming, complementation, clause duplication, and
tautological padding preserve the decision.  Horn, 2-SAT, affine/Tseitin,
bounded-treewidth, and independent-product families show that compact
source-derived quotients may be easy.  The one-bit SAT interface requires no
explicit representative.

The proofs relativize: fix $A_0^O$ and repeat (3)--(5) relative to the same
oracle $O$.  Algebraic extension access can only strengthen the external
computation.  Thus the result identifies rather than crosses the
Baker--Gill--Solovay and Aaronson--Wigderson barriers.  No large constructive
property of general circuits is proved, so Natural Proofs is not the present
hinge.  Nonuniform advice would only add another representation escape.
