\begin{abstract}
MODELBRIDGE-0013 isolated an $\Omega(L)$ raw occurrence-location cost in the
last transfer into proper unique Monotone 3-Sat-$(2,2)$.  This report gives
the exact interface theorem.  In an unindexed length-$L$ occurrence stream
where a designated key occurs exactly three times and one occurrence is at
position $p$, the deterministic decision-tree complexity of its rank is
$\min\{p,L-p-1\}$, apart from the constant-output boundary cases.  Thus a
clause-local representation need not be incidence-local.  Conversely, on a
Monotone 3-Sat-$(3,3)$ source, attaching a two-bit sign-specific occurrence
tag to each literal increases standard indexed encoding length only by a
constant factor and makes the rank available with no scan.  Therefore the
raw bound is an exact black-box-composition warning, not a semantic
obstruction, not an arbitrary-decider resource, and not a P-versus-NP lower
bound.  The local-reduction target must explicitly demand \emph{bi-local}
clause and incidence access.  That target remains OPEN, as do UCORE-TIME1
and P versus NP.
\end{abstract}

\section{Primary-literature and barrier preflight}

The first obstacle is interface composition.  FLMV's Theorem 3.1 needs each
bit of its SAT reduction on demand in polylogarithmic time and logarithmic
workspace, because the reduced formula cannot be stored or regenerated in
full \cite{flmv}.  Jahanjou--Miles--Viola give an even more local theorem:
the indexed output clause of a quasilinear-size 3SAT instance is produced by
an explicit circuit and each output bit depends on $O(1)$ input bits
\cite{jmv}.  Neither theorem, as stated, supplies sign-specific occurrence
rank or successor access for an arbitrary postprocessing gadget.

The second obstacle is model transfer.  Raman--Raman--Rao show that rank and
select can be supported in constant RAM time by a succinct indexable
dictionary after an auxiliary representation is supplied \cite{rrr}.
P\u{a}tra\c{s}cu proves a redundancy/query tradeoff for succinct rank in the
cell-probe model \cite{patrascu}.  These are exact theorems for preprocessed
data structures; they do not say that a SAT decider builds such a structure,
and they refute any suggestion that raw rescanning is the only realization.

Darmann--D\"ocker's fixed equality gadget, with the corrected odd/even clone
assignment independently rechecked here, supplies the second tagged leg
\cite{dd}.  The remaining issue is thus not the gadget semantics but the
input/output access interface.

\paragraph{Transfer classification.}
The FLMV and Jahanjou--Miles--Viola clause-output guarantees are
\textbf{directly transferable theorems} only at their declared clause-bit
interfaces.  Succinct rank/select is a \textbf{direct theorem transfer} to
a charged preprocessed occurrence dictionary.  Promoting either a cell-probe
bound or raw-array adversary to unrestricted SAT time is a
\textbf{misleading analogy / failed transfer}.  A native structured reduction
whose variable identifiers expose their incident slots is a
\textbf{useful structural analogy requiring a new transfer theorem}.

\section{Exact raw decision-tree theorem}

\begin{definition}[Three-occurrence rank problem]
Fix $L\ge3$ and a pivot $p\in\{0,\ldots,L-1\}$.  The input is a bit vector
$x\in\{0,1\}^L$ promised to have Hamming weight three and $x_p=1$.  A query
reveals one $x_i$ for $i\ne p$.  The required output is
\[
 R_{L,p}(x)=\sum_{i<p}x_i.
\]
This is exactly the sign-specific occurrence rank of the pivot after
collapsing ``same variable and sign'' to one and every other literal to zero.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{CLAUSE-LOCAL-NOT-INCIDENCE-LOCAL1}]
Let $a=p$ and $b=L-p-1$.  The exact deterministic decision-tree complexity is
\[
 D(R_{L,p})=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&a=0\text{ or }b=0\text{ or }(a,b)=(1,1),\\
 \min\{a,b\},&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
In particular, for a central pivot it is $\Theta(L)$, although reading any
specified clause/literal field is one raw probe.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The pivot accounts for one of the three ones, so exactly two other positions
are one.  Querying every position on the smaller side reveals its count; if
the right side is scanned, subtract its count from two.  This gives the upper
bound.  The three exceptional cases have a forced answer.

For the lower bound assume neither side is empty and not both have size one.
Follow the all-zero answer path of any decision tree of depth
$q<\min\{a,b\}$.  At least one unqueried position remains on each side.
Moreover, the two unqueried sides cannot both have size one: that would give
$q=a+b-2<\min\{a,b\}$, possible only for $a=b=1$.  Hence one side has at
least two unqueried positions.  Put both remaining ones on that side, or put
one there and one on the other side.  Both promised inputs agree with every
query answer but have different ranks.  Thus the tree cannot halt before
$\min\{a,b\}$ queries.
\end{proof}

This sharpens MODELBRIDGE-0013's sufficient central-pivot lower bound.  It
does not prove a lower bound for a preprocessed dictionary, a native tagged
encoding, a structured source reduction, or SAT decision.

\section{Constant-overhead tag lift}

\begin{definition}[Tagged $(3,3)$ encoding]
For every literal occurrence in a Monotone 3-Sat-$(3,3)$ instance, append a
tag $r\in\{1,2,3\}$ such that for each pair (variable, sign) its three
occurrences receive distinct tags.  The tags are metadata; deleting them
recovers the original formula and does not change satisfiability.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{TAG-LIFT-NEUTRALIZES-RAW-BARRIER1}]
Let the source have $n\ge2$ variables and therefore $6n$ literal
occurrences.  Under a fixed-width indexed encoding, adding the tags costs
exactly $12n$ bits.  If the untagged literal fields use at least
$6n(\lceil\log_2 n\rceil+1)$ bits, then
\[
 N_{\rm tag}=N_{\rm raw}+12n=\Theta(N_{\rm raw}).
\]
The tag of a specified occurrence is directly readable, so the corrected
Darmann--D\"ocker clone choice is computable by constant local arithmetic.
Variable renaming, clause permutation and carrying the tags with their
literal records preserve validity and size.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
There are three occurrences of each sign per variable, so two bits encode
the tag and exactly $6n$ tags are stored.  The size equation follows.  The
clone index is $2r-1$ for a positive occurrence and $2r$ for a negative
occurrence.  This uses only the sign and tag of the selected record.  The
tag multiset $\{1,2,3\}$ for every (variable, sign) is invariant under the
listed representation changes.
\end{proof}

The theorem is intentionally not a free raw-to-tagged compiler.  If the
source is supplied only as an arbitrary clause stream, constructing or
validating all tags may require a scan, sort, dictionary or stored index.
But if the earlier reduction emits tags natively from its structured
coordinates, no generic occurrence search occurs.  The OPEN target must
therefore concern that exact source construction, not a universal raw-array
necessity.

\begin{corollary}[No arbitrary-decider transfer]
Assume for contradiction that SAT is in P, witnessed by a total uniform
decider $A$.  The program that runs $A$ directly on the declared canonical
formula encoding is still polynomial and need not compute any occurrence
rank or tag.  Hence neither theorem forces a contradiction, and
\texttt{RAW-INDEX-TO-UCORE1} is \RETIRED.
\end{corollary}

The same point holds unconditionally for easy controls: Horn propagation,
2SAT strongly connected components, affine/Tseitin Gaussian elimination,
and bounded-treewidth dynamic programming can decide their inputs without
materializing this tag service.  A one-bit output requests no incidence
dictionary.

\section{Hostile controls and barrier audit}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=3.0cm,style=nextline]
\item[Alternative encodings.] The two-bit tag lift is the direct hostile
control.  It preserves semantic content and asymptotic encoded length while
destroying the raw scan cost.
\item[Padding and products.] Constant tag padding changes neither exponent
nor satisfiability.  Independent products do not make tag lookup harder;
each occurrence retains its local label.
\item[Wrappers.] DIRECT and RECOMP may ignore or regenerate metadata;
INTERP may decode it; COPY, DEAD and DELAY alter exposure without changing
the answer.  No raw field is universally causally necessary.
\item[Easy semantics.] Horn, dual-Horn, 2SAT, affine/Tseitin, and bounded
treewidth retain their standard polynomial shortcuts.  Tags add no clauses
and no semantic obstruction.
\item[Communication/memory.] The raw decision tree is one stateless query
model.  It supplies neither a communication cut nor a retained-space bound
for the SAT execution.  Preprocessed rank/select must be charged under its
own space/query contract.
\end{description}

Both exact theorems relativize: an unrelated oracle changes neither the
raw adversary nor direct tag access.  They also survive algebraic-oracle
extension for the same reason and therefore provide no nonrelativizing or
nonalgebrizing hinge.  Baker--Gill--Solovay and Aaronson--Wigderson remain
barriers \cite{bgs,aw}.  No large constructive circuit property is used, so
Natural Proofs is not the active obstruction.

\paragraph{Post-quantum ledger.}
No Schur square, quadratic-relation space, Hilbert function, syzygy or
collision map is part of the occurrence query; importing one is a
\textbf{FAILED TRANSFER}.  The LWE lesson of designing a native externally
queryable reduction is an \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}: it motivates bi-local
output access but supplies no worst-case/average-case theorem for SAT.
Succinct rank/select is the relevant exact data-structure transfer and is
algorithmically exploitable, reinforcing that hidden indexing structure may
be a shortcut rather than a hardness source.
