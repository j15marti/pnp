## 2. Correction: SW01 section 5 balanced circuit sufficiency is false

Take the MFC43 K5 instance, all edge equations x_u+x_v=1, with groups:
1: 01,23; 2: 02,14; 3: 03,24; 4: 04,13; 5: 12,34.
It is strict deficiency one: total rank 4, five groups. For proper s groups the 2s distinct simple edges have graphic rank >=s (for s=4, rank<=3 allows at most 6 edges; for s<=3 use the same simple-graph bound). All five groups are disjoint edge pairs within each group, simple connected four-regular graph. UNSAT: a 0/5 or 1/4 binary partition cuts fewer than five edges; in a 2/3 partition, the internal edge on the smaller side has a disjoint partner internal to the larger side, so that owner fails.

Nevertheless the four original edges 23,14,24,13 from distinct groups 1,2,3,4 form the balanced rainbow cycle 2-3-1-4-2 (four inequality signs sum zero). Its row span W has rank 3. The remaining owner 5 has BOTH directions 12 and 34 in W. Alternation on the cycle forces x_1=x_2 and x_3=x_4; both its escape equations fail. The quotient rank of that owner modulo W is 0, not 1. Therefore the proposed inference from rank-Hall of COMPLETE owner spaces to rank-Hall modulo ONLY the chosen circuit rows is invalid. The same counterexample applies even when a third XOR alternative is allowed, since the two original rows are included. This retracts only SW01's circuit sufficiency, not its exact star/tree projection, exposure theorem, max-deficiency core or mixed-arity two-type theorem. A circuit with additional extendability certificate is sufficient, as below.

## 3. New theorem: consistency-preserving rank augmentation using original alternatives

Input: explicit lists G_i of tagged affine equations a*x=b over GF(2), each group a disjunction, all groups required. Allow duplicate rows, arbitrary signs, arbitrary positive list size (zero coefficient allowed after normalization). Let m be number of groups, and R be the maximum rank of a full UNSIGNED representative list selecting exactly one row per group. Equivalently R is the maximum size of a partial independent transversal, because a partial independent transversal can be completed arbitrarily to a full list and a basis of a full list is partial independent. R=m-Delta for Delta=max_S(|S|-rank union G_i for i in S), by Rado's rank theorem and ordinary partition/linear matroid intersection.

**Theorem.** Given ANY consistent full representative list F (e.g. from any actual model), deterministically obtain a consistent full list of rank R in polynomial bit time, using only the original alternatives and at most m rank-increasing rounds of at most m signed row replacements each. At termination also obtain a rank-deficiency upper-bound certificate on R. The assignment may change. It is not a free model generator and does not claim that every original model belongs to such a rank-maximal original-row chart.

Proof. Write f_i for the coefficient row selected by group i and W=span(f_i), r=rank W. If all f_i independent, done. Let C be indices of noncoloops of this labeled list: deleting f_i leaves rank r. Let J be remaining coloop indices. Every linear dependence is supported in C; W is direct sum of W_C=span(f_i:i in C) and independent one-dimensional coloop spaces. The coordinates of any a in W on the coloop rows are unique, computable by Gaussian elimination or testing membership after deletion.

Create an owner graph, with a terminal symbol *. For every alternative a in group i:
- If a is outside W, put arc i -> * with a and its actual RHS as label.
- If a is in W, for each coloop j whose coefficient in a is 1 put arc i -> j with a and RHS.
Search from all owners in C. If no terminal is reachable, let S be the reachable owners (including C). Every alternative of S lies in W and has zero coefficient on all coloops outside S, so rank union G_S = rank span(f_i:i in S)=|S|-(m-r). Any full unsigned list has rank <= rank union G_S +m-|S|=r. Hence r=R, with S a checkable certificate. This includes the all-independent case trivially.

Otherwise choose a shortest path i_0,...,i_k,* starting in C. All i_1,...,i_k are distinct coloops, because arcs land only in coloops and the path is shortest. Let a_j be the labeled alternative at i_j for the next arc, and a_k the outside-W alternative. For j<k, a_j is in W, has coefficient 1 on f_(i_(j+1)), and zero coefficients on f_(i_l) for l>j+1; any such later coefficient would provide a shortcut to *. Other coloop coordinates are unrestricted.

Replace f_(i_k) by a_k, then f_(i_(k-1)) by a_(k-1), continuing in reverse order. Before replacement at i_j, j<k, the current span intersects W in the span of the retained original rows plus a_(j+1),...,a_(k-1); the outside vector a_k cannot cancel in this intersection. Project W onto the coordinates of the removed old coloop rows i_(j+1),...,i_k. The retained original rows vanish on these coordinates. Each added a_l has last nonzero coordinate i_(l+1) with coefficient 1. These vectors have distinct last coordinates >=j+2. No combination of them equals the projection of a_j, which is the unit vector at i_(j+1). Thus a_j is outside the current span. Moreover if j>=1 the old f_(i_j) is still a coloop: a linear expression of it using the other rows would have zero projection on removed coordinates, forcing all added a_l coefficients zero by the same triangular argument and the outside coefficient zero, contradicting its original coloop property. For j=0, the original dependence in C containing f_(i_0) still exists, since no C row was previously replaced; it is still a noncoloop. The initial replacement at i_k is outside W and removes a coloop, except for the direct k=0 case where it removes a noncoloop.

Every replacement therefore adds an equation independent of the retained coefficient rows. Those retained affine equations were consistent, and an independent equation with either RHS always extends them by Gaussian elimination. Thus consistency is preserved at every step, ranks are unchanged until the last step which removes a noncoloop and increases rank exactly one. Recompute W,C,J and repeat. Termination after <=m increases, with either all independent or an explicit S proving global maximum rank. QED.

This is an original-alternative, actual-model-preserving *existential* upgrade, not preservation of a particular model or guard if the guard is not included. For a protected affine guard, normalize all offered rows modulo its consistent affine equation space, apply the theorem in that quotient, and lift only to models satisfying that guard. Do not transfer a guarded result unconditionally. It can change all unprotected coordinates.

Complexity: <=m rounds, each using polynomially many explicit Gaussian rank operations for coloop and alternative membership, a graph with <=m+1 vertices and <=m times the total number of offered alternatives labeled arcs, BFS, <=m replacements and optional Gaussian witness after each. Naive dense polynomial bit cost, e.g. O(N^7) conservative array-bit work for explicit input length N, polynomial workspace, deterministic TM polynomial simulation overhead. No subset enumeration in production; the final S is independently verified by row ranks. Charge all rank checks, arc scans, unsuccessful reachability, witnesses and guard substitutions. Same-execution CM <=(T+1)S. Starting with an actual model is an explicit premise, not counted as acquired for free.

## 4. Exact certificate for any deficiency; strict d1 graph terminal

Let Delta=m-R. A full consistent transversal exists iff there is a partial selection C of distinct owners, one actual equation per selected owner, with rank(C)=|C|-Delta, consistent affine equations, and all remaining groups admit independent representatives modulo span(C).

Sufficiency: choose those quotient representatives by ordinary matroid intersection and solve all equations. Necessity: take any model, select one satisfied original equation per owner, then apply section 3 to rank R. Take C to be the noncoloops of the resulting full list; all dependence is supported there, and the remaining coloop rows are independent modulo span(C). Delta=0 is Rado absorption; for Delta=1 take the support of the unique nonzero dependency, i.e. a circuit. In characteristic two its balanced sign condition is XOR of RHS bits zero. The condition on remaining groups is essential. No synthetic derived direction is needed for the existential criterion.

For a connected signed graph on n hubs with n groups of actual edges and full strict d1 rank-Hall, a satisfying assignment exists iff there is a rainbow SPANNING UNICYCLIC selection (one edge per group) whose unique cycle is balanced. Equivalently there is a balanced rainbow simple circuit that extends to such a selection. In the graphic setting the quotient test simply contracts the cycle and finds a rainbow spanning forest using the remaining groups (with loops rejected). Both directions have actual Gaussian/graph parity lift. This exact original-edge result strengthens the corrected SW01 statement, and separates an ordinary rainbow cycle from a paid SAT certificate.

Conditional new terminal: enumerate all simple cycles up to length L in a bounded-degree Delta_G graph (including length-two parallel cycles). There are <=n Delta_G^L rooted walks up to a polynomial factor for fixed Delta_G>=2. For each rainbow balanced cycle, test quotient rank-Hall by MI and return actual witness on success. For L<=c log N and fixed c,Delta_G, this is polynomial. Failure is UNKNOWN unless a proved promise ensures every SAT member has such an extendable cycle. More generally for fixed deficiency Delta, enumerate Delta actual short cycles, take their union with distinct owner IDs, require nullity Delta and exact sign consistency, and test remaining independent quotient reps. Any rank-maximal consistent graphic selection has cycle-space dimension Delta; if it has a cycle basis of length <=c log N, its union is the noncoloop core and the enumeration covers it. The work is polynomial for fixed Delta,Delta_G,c. General affine noncoloop core of constant size likewise admits polynomial enumeration. No universal short-core bound, and no cycle-enumerating/MI backend executed in this audit. This is complementary to CC01's small codimension chart existence, not a complete discovery oracle for the chart cover.

## 5. Infinite both-label obstruction to universal short original-edge certificates

For even n>=8 coprime to 3, take the doubled n-cycle, with edge form a_i=x_i+x_(i+1) modulo n. Group i offers equation a_i=c_i OR a_(i+3)=1+c_(i+3). Each underlying edge has two copies with opposite RHS signs, each group consists of two disjoint edges, and graph is connected four-regular multigraph. Any proper nonempty group set S touches edge types S union (S+3), at least |S|+1 because shift by 3 is one cycle. Any proper subset of distinct n-cycle edge forms independent, full rank n-1; hence strict d1.

Set z_i=a_i+c_i. Each group requires z_i=0 OR z_(i+3)=1, i.e. implication z_i -> z_(i+3). All z_i must be equal. Since n is even and sum a_i=0, a model exists iff sum c_i=0. In the SAT case exactly two a-words, four hub assignments (two global translations per a-word). For z=0 only the first equation of every group holds, and for z=1 only the second holds. EVERY actual full consistent original-edge transversal is a spanning n-cycle. Its unique circuit has length n. Parallel 2-cycles are unbalanced; every longer simple circuit uses all n edge types, and the rainbow condition forces one of the two full selections because shift by 3 is a single cycle. With sum c=1 both full cycles unbalanced, no balanced rainbow circuit. Flip c_0 to get an explicit UNSAT neighbor (two occurrence signs change). Thus shortest balanced extendable original-edge cycle can have linear length in strict disjoint-pair multigraphs. This is an easy parity/implication family, not a lower bound for general SAT or for all other charts. The simple-graph-specific logarithmic ordinary rainbow theorem is not contradicted; parallel edges violate its premise, and it does not ensure balance or extendability anyway.
