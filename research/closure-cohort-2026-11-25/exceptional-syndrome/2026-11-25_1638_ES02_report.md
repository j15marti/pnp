## 2. Theorem A: exact all-one tournament repair span
Let M be n by n over F2, M_ii=1, M_ij+M_ji=1 for i!=j. Write i->j when M_ij=1 off diagonal. Let F={p: p_i(Mp)_i=0 for all i}. A support S is feasible exactly when each vertex of the induced tournament T[S] has odd outdegree. Let G consist of indicator vectors of directed triangles and of quadruples consisting of a directed triangle and one vertex dominating all its three vertices (the W4 tournament). Every member of G is an actual repair.

**Theorem:** span F = span G. Consequently for every b, existence of an actual repair with b^T p=1 is decided by scanning G; a YES returns an actual repair of support 3 or 4. This is a single-linear-demand theorem, not equality of actual repair support with the span.

Proof. Fix b annihilating G and any feasible S. Color the selected vertices B={v in S:b_v=1} and W=S minus B. If B empty, done. B is transitive since any cyclic triangle within B would be odd. For x in B, write I={y in W:y->x} and O={y in W:x->y}. Every I->O: a contrary edge would make a cyclic triangle with exactly one B. Therefore no SCC of T[W] straddles I and O; I is a prefix of the totally ordered SCCs. Also x cannot dominate a cyclic triangle in W, because its W4 would have odd b. Thus every nontrivial W SCC belongs to I for every x. If W has a nontrivial SCC, delete the W prefix through its last nontrivial SCC. All deleted vertices dominate every B and the remaining W suffix. Every retained vertex has the same outdegree as before deletion, so the retained induced tournament is feasible and the B count is unchanged. The remaining W is transitive.

Order B as x_1->...->x_k and remaining W as y_1->...->y_l. For each i let c_i in [0,l] satisfy y_j->x_i iff j<=c_i. Such cutoff exists by I->O. There cannot be a strict peak c_h<c_i and c_j<c_i for h<i<j: y_{c_i} with x_i,x_j forms a cyclic triangle dominated by x_h, a W4 of odd b. Thus the cutoff sequence is weakly decreasing then weakly increasing (a weak valley). Feasibility at x_i requires k-i+l-c_i odd, hence adjacent cutoffs have opposite parity and cannot equal. The valley is therefore strict on its two limbs, every positive value occurs at most twice, and zero at most once. At y_j, outdegree is l-j + #{i:c_i>=j}, odd. Taking adjacent differences gives odd multiplicity for each value j=1,...,l (the top value is odd by the last row). Each occurs exactly once. Hence k=l or l+1, including the empty-W case. A tournament of q=k+l vertices all having odd outdegree must satisfy q(q-1)/2=q mod 2, hence q is 0 or 3 mod 4. If k is odd, q=2k is 2 mod 4 or q=2k-1 is 1 mod 4, contradiction. Therefore b^T 1_S=0 for every actual feasible S. The two spaces have the same annihilator, proving equality. QED.

Algorithm: verify input tournament and diagonal, repeatedly force zero on any active row having its diagonal one and no active off-diagonal outgoing entries (sound since all removed coordinates are zero); enumerate directed triangles in remaining vertices, return a triangle with odd demand, or a common predecessor in demand support added to an even-demand triangle. Otherwise NO by theorem A. Full repair-span basis, if requested, is obtained by ordinary elimination retaining actual independent generators. No arbitrary XOR of witnesses is returned as a repair.

## 3. Theorem B: exact normal form and acquired rank-two feedback for strong tournaments
Suppose off-diagonal tournament is strongly connected and b!=0, with all-one diagonal. Then b annihilates the repair span iff the following three conditions hold:
1. Both B=supp b and W=V minus B induce transitive tournaments.
2. In their transitive orders every B vertex has a threshold c_i, so y_j->x_i iff j<=c_i.
3. The cutoff sequence c_1,...,c_k is a weak valley (weakly decreasing then weakly increasing).

Necessity follows from the preceding local triangle/W4 exclusions applied to the full tournament. If W has any nontrivial SCC, its prefix dominates all B and remaining W, contradicting strong connectivity. The strict-peak argument requires no feasible whole support. Sufficiency: cyclic triangles with 0 or 3 B are impossible by transitivity, with 1 B by the threshold condition. A cyclic triangle thus has 2 B, and a B vertex dominating it would constitute a strict peak. So all triangles and W4 have even b, and theorem A applies. Strong connectivity further forces minimum cutoff zero (otherwise the initial W prefix dominates its exterior); hence B directly out-dominates W. Empty W and singleton cases are interpreted literally.

Every such UNSAT tournament chart has an explicitly discoverable ordering in which its minimum feedback-completion rank is at most two. Split the B sequence at a valley minimum into a weak descending prefix D and weak ascending suffix U, and order all vertices by decreasing keys c_i+1/2 for B and j for y_j in W. For ties within D preserve original B order; within U reverse it; place D before U at ties. W order is reversed. For lower-triangular positions (later row, earlier column), cross B/W entries equal one, W/W entries equal one, D/D entries zero, U/U entries one, and later D or U relative to an earlier B of the other limb has the entry determined by the earlier limb (D gives zero, U gives one). Hence a completion of the lower triangular entries is the type-constant matrix with rows and columns D,U,W:
    [0 1 1]
    [0 1 1]
    [1 1 1]
which has rank at most 2. Verification of the prescribed entries is direct: descending keys mean a later B has smaller or equal cutoff than an earlier B; B vertices in D precede all U in the original transitive order, and equal-key ties follow the stated rule. For cross B/W, if later B has smaller key than earlier W then B->W; if later W has smaller key then W->B. Thus M=T+C where T is upper triangular and rank C<=2. Acquired normal form is a finite-group characterization of the NO instances in this chart class, not of all strict deficiency-one geometries.

## 4. Exact target transport, all signs under dominating demand, and scope
Given explicit strict punctured planes U_i in ambient F2^(n+1), rank(sum_{i in J} U_i)>=|J|+1 for nonempty J. Rado's theorem / ordinary linear-partition matroid intersection obtains independent e_i in U_i. Pick f_i in U_i minus {0,e_i}, and w outside span E of e_i. Express f_i=sum_j A_ji e_j+b_i w and M=A+I. Targets in sum f_i+E are directly attainable by choosing f_i or f_i+e_i. Other targets have the form t=w+sum f'_i with f'_i=f_i+x_i e_i; this changes only the diagonal of M, not b or off-diagonal entries. The exact repair correspondence for the changed M is q=Mp, y_i=f'_i+p_i(f'_i+e_i)+q_i e_i, with p_i q_i=0 and b^T p=1. Conversely these equations exactly enforce each nonzero choice and target sum. Verify each actual source lift.

If the acquired off-diagonal chart is a tournament and B=supp b directly out-dominates W, all diagonal signings are decided as follows. A zero diagonal at B gives a singleton witness. A zero diagonal at v in W has an incoming edge from j in B; if j has diagonal one, {j,v} is an actual odd-demand repair, else the singleton j works. Thus every non-all-one diagonal is SAT, and theorem A decides the sole candidate. Strong all-one tournament with arbitrary b is also covered for that diagonal. In general strong tournaments with non-dominating B, the shortest-path candidate is d_v=1 for v in B and d_v=dist(B,v) mod 2 elsewhere. To see necessity, a shortest path from B has no forward shortcuts, no interior B, and in a tournament every nonconsecutive earlier vertex beats no later one. A prefix of length k+1 is feasible iff all earlier path diagonal values are 1 at the first position and j mod 2 at each interior index j>=1, and its last diagonal is (k-1) mod 2 (k>=1). Failure of the candidate equality thus produces an actual odd-demand prefix. This is candidate acquisition only, not proof that a mixed candidate is unattainable.

The five-plane atom is a genuine chart obstruction to universal tournament coverage: exact support misses only 29, and all 5568 independent-e and allowed-f charts with w=t+sum f_i were examined, none a tournament. This is a bounded exhaustive computation, not an all-geometries theorem. Six-vertex mixed-candidate counterexample, row masks [52,3,22,15,26,62], demand mask 20: strong tournament with BFS layers {2,4},{1,3},{0},{5}; its shortest-path candidate diagonal is all ones except vertex 0. The sole odd-demand repair is mask 47, support size five. Therefore do not extend the 3/4 support theorem to arbitrary candidate diagonals. All these are exact explicit counterexamples; audit checks source strict ranks and lifts. Likewise arbitrary joint guards are not solved by the span: the 4-vertex near-transitive tournament, edges i->j for i<j except 3->0, has actual nonempty repairs {0,1,3},{0,2,3}; their XOR {1,2} does not satisfy the actual repair equations. Joint pins p_1=p_2=1 are infeasible even though their linear projections occur in the span.

## 5. Infinite family, acquisition barrier and relation to ES01
For l>=2, take B vertices x_0,...,x_l, W vertices y_1,...,y_l, both forward transitive, with c_i=l-i. The tournament is strong (x_l->y_1->y_l->x_0->x_l, and all intermediate vertices access these), and B dominates W. All-one diagonal with b=1_B is NO by theorem B. With b'=b+e_{y_1}, a cyclic triangle x_0,x_l,y_1 is odd, hence YES; this changes geometry, not just target. On the original geometry the neighboring syndrome t+w is directly attainable. Strict plane ranks follow from the construction e_i coordinate vectors, f_i=(M+I) column i plus b_i w: any proper nonempty J receives an edge from outside to inside, adding a coordinate beyond span e_J; full set spans w since b!=0. For the order B then W the prescribed lower W-by-B submatrix has l independent nested prefix rows; minimum fixed-order feedback completion rank is at least l. The alternating block M+M^T has rank 2l (all off-diagonal ones), so any diagonal-plus-rank-r representation has r>=l. Underlying undirected graph is complete, treewidth n-1. Theorem B acquires a different ordering with feedback rank <=2 for the NO case, without an order-search oracle. Tests through n=257.

There also exist strong tournaments requiring feedback rank >floor(n/8) in EVERY order of their displayed chart: the number of strong labeled tournaments is (1-o(1))2^{n(n-1)/2}, by union bound over directed cuts without a reverse arc. For fixed order and rank k completion, encode its U,R using <=2^{2nk} choices; the lower half determines the entire tournament. Multiplying by n! orders gives exponent <=n^2/4+O(n log n) at k=floor(n/8), strictly below the strong tournament exponent. Choose b=all ones and all-one diagonal; any strong tournament with n>1 has a directed triangle, hence an actual YES at its candidate. Thus the new terminal covers some high-feedback charts even after order optimization. This does not exclude alternate e/f chart changes, and it is not a time lower bound. Theorem B says why its UNSAT subfamily behaves differently.
