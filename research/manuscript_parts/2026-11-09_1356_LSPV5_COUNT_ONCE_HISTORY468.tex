\paragraph{REFEREE468 (LSPV-5, 2026-11-09 13:56 PST; proved/restricted plus computational).}
Fix $n$ immutable positive-weight paid origins.  A concrete count-once history
is a subset $S$, and using origin $i$ costs zero when $i\in S$ and $w_i>0$
otherwise.  Any deterministic Markov abstraction preserving every such next
charge exactly must distinguish all $2^n$ subsets: two distinct histories
differ on some $i$, and their next charges are zero and $w_i$.  For a coarse
abstract block $B$, the greatest uniform lower charge safe for every
concretization is
\[
 \widehat\Delta_i(B)=\min_{S\in B}\Delta_i(S),
\]
which is zero whenever $B$ mixes paid and unpaid status for $i$.  Shared-DAG
reconvergence uses set union, so the result covers count-once reuse rather than
a tree unfolding.  Python and independent JavaScript checked 698027 history
pairs, 669924 join/action triples, all 4157 quotient partitions through three
origins and 99484 MAY inequalities.  This is an abstract-state precision
theorem, not an exponential evidence, time, space or proof-minimum lower bound:
the history has an $n$-bit representation and the control optimum is linear.
Historical all-rule coverage, a superpolynomial mixed minimum, noncircular B3
and P versus NP remain open.
