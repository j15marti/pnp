\section{Recovered finite-entry endpoint with strict margins}
The recovered ARC-STRENGTHEN1 report has been independently checked
against Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas,
\emph{Time-Space Lower Bounds for Satisfiability}, author manuscript,
7 September 2004, Section 2.1, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 5.8
(\url{https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/tst.pdf}).
For uniform deterministic random-access SAT machines, let
$\mathcal A$ denote simultaneous achievable exponents measured in
original encoded bit length. With
\[
 d(c)=\frac12\left(\frac{c+2}{c^2}-c\right),
\]
strict inequalities $t<c$ and $s<d(c)$ exclude $(t,s)$ from
$\mathcal A$. Strict margins absorb the local SAT reduction's
polylogarithmic time and logarithmic workspace overhead.
The particular pair $(6/5,1/2)$ is excluded using
$c=1201/1000$ and
$d(c)=1/2+26275399/2884802000$.

Since $d(c)\to1$ as $c\downarrow1$, it suffices to derive, under
SAT in P, a sequence of actual achievable pairs with $t_j\to1$
and eventually $s_j\le\sigma<1$. One fixed finite stage then
contradicts the endpoint. Stages may use different uniform all-length
machines; neither a limiting machine nor a computable stage-to-program
map is needed. Separate machines for different input lengths, or
separate time and space witnesses at one stage, remain inadmissible.
The conditional drift $t_{j+1}\le1+\lambda(t_j-1)+b$ suffices
if $0\le\lambda<1$, $1+b/(1-\lambda)<c$, and $s_j<d(c)$
with a fixed margin. This is a recovered conditional conclusion,
not a new SAT transition. The parity-star extension above supplies
no such contraction; P versus NP remains OPEN.
