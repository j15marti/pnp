\section{Red-team WRAPPER audit: terminal-safe and shielded fallback repair}

The constant-candidate \textsc{EXTREME-1} wrapper is a valid zero-child
terminal and can be absorbed into the existing aggregate ledger.  It adds a
strict favorable promise region but cannot meet the essential-smaller-child
PRE-A guard and does not cover all inputs.

The singleton Horn/dual-Horn component wrapper remains CANDIDATE because the
tested implementation stores a linear component map.  A hard time/space cap
keeps returned answers sound by falling back to UNKNOWN; it does not establish
completion or half-space resource use on the proposed family.

There is also an exact composition regression.  The old nested fallback
embedding of $(\neg x\vee\neg y\vee\neg z)$ is all-false satisfiable and is
therefore accepted by \textsc{EXTREME-1}.  The old SAT-hard UNKNOWN-domain
claim cannot be reused unchanged after this terminal is added.  It is repaired
by inserting two disjoint proper NAE triples before isolation.  Every model
then retains at least two true and two false source values, so the extreme
terminal declines.  Because ARC6's old guard theorem was universal over
nonempty proper sources, applying it to $F\wedge Q$ reuses every old member
and cap proof unchanged and restores the scoped fallback equality exactly.

Consequently the conditional $K$-elimination theorem survives, while its
all-input acquisition premise, general-SAT ARC entry, and P versus NP remain
OPEN.
