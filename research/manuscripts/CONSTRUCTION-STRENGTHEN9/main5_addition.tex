\section{Construction9: strong-Krom progress and the general selector barrier}
\label{sec:construction9-main5}

A new source-first controller member, \textsc{STRONG-KROM-CUT1}, finds a
singleton strong Krom backdoor among at most three candidates, dry-runs both
restrictions, and accepts only when every resulting Krom component is at
most $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ bits.  It then solves one component at a time.  Reusing
the verified step-capped component enumerator yields simultaneous
$N^{6/5+o(1)}$ local time and $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ state, with zero recursive SAT
calls.  It exactly decides the earlier balanced lifted-cross control.

The all-input gap is unchanged.  A decider-guided Shannon selector is
semantically exact, but it discovers the favorable branch with near-parent
SAT calls.  Inlining them retains the assumed exponent $K$; exposing them
violates shrink and aggregate-volume bounds.  The explicit connected
sign-balanced family
\[
 H_n=\bigwedge_i(x_i\vee x_{i+1}\vee x_{i+2})\wedge
 (\neg x_i\vee\neg x_{i+1}\vee\neg x_{i+2})
\]
has full affine hull, near-parent pivot residuals, and
$N^{2-o(1)}$ invoked volume along the least-variable selector prefix.
Parallel wrappers do not remove this cost: independently existentially
quantified segments can splice incompatible checkpoints, whereas coupling
the checkpoint restores the missing state/tableau.  Moreover every emitted
ordinary child still needs a charged portfolio-membership certificate.
The iterative-pure member's previous joint-space claim is withdrawn pending
a step-capped read-only implementation.  P versus NP remains open.
