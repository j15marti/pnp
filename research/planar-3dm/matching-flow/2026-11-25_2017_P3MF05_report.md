## 2. Exact signed submodular admission
For fixed positive w and the natural gauge, E is submodular on the closed-set lattice iff every remaining q_ij<=0. Sufficiency by termwise submodularity on Boolean cube after folding. Necessity: for any incomparable i,j, let S be union of strict successor sets; neither i nor j in S; S, S+i, S+j, S+i+j all closed. Mixed second difference is exactly q_ij. Positive q violates submodularity. This is an exact characterization of this objective on this lattice, NOT of all possible algorithms or objectives.

Allow x_i=g_i+(1-2g_i)y_i with g_i in {0,1}. Require g_i=g_j for every implication arc, so hard arcs remain implications (reverse arc when both flipped). Then the feasible y sets are again a closed-set lattice. A comparable opposite-gauge pair would destroy this lattice property: original feasible 00 and 11, after complementing one, give two feasible incomparable points whose meet or join violates the hard implication. For each incomparable q_ij!=0 require g_i XOR g_j = 1 if q_ij>0, else 0, so transformed coefficient nonpositive. Thus fixed-w gauge discovery is a signed parity-constraint graph with equality on hard arcs. BFS or union-find with parity solves it in polynomial time. Failure certifies an inconsistent signed cycle in this representation only.

For fixed gauge, choose positive weights w_z>=1 by rational LP: for every incomparable pair require (1-2g_i)(1-2g_j) sum_z w_z d_iz d_jz <=0. Feasibility gives exact signed-submodular weighted objective. Scaling removes rational denominators with polynomial bit size by standard rational LP solution bounds.
