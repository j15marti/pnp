## 3. Theorem A: polynomial acquisition of matching-support periods by a forbidden-edge certificate

Let G be an indexed bipartite graph with at least one perfect matching (planarity is not needed for this theorem), labels l_e in F2^d, S the set of XOR signatures of all perfect matchings. Suppose H is a subspace of Per(S) = {h:S+h=S}; initially H=0. Let r_H be a fixed linear quotient reduction. For a nonzero quotient direction p, call e switchable if a parallel edge e' with the same two endpoints satisfies r_H(l_e+l_e')=p. Construct B_p with only unswitchable indexed edges (collapse parallel edges for ordinary existence testing if desired). Then:

**If B_p has no perfect matching, p is a period of r_H(S).** Proof: every actual matching of G must contain some switchable indexed edge. Replace just that edge by its parallel alternative. The result remains an actual perfect matching and changes quotient signature by p. This proves inclusion r_H(S)+p subset r_H(S), hence equality, since translation is involutive. This does not assume all labels in a pair bundle are switchable. A pair belongs to the bad graph if at least one of its labels is unswitchable, so any bad-graph matching indeed chooses a bad indexed edge at each pair. A bad-graph matching falsifies the certificate, not necessarily the existence of the semantic period.

Since any certified nonzero p must be a difference of two actual parallel-edge quotient labels, enumerate at most m^2 candidate directions, test each using ordinary bipartite matching. A successful test raises dim H by one; lift its direction to the chosen quotient representative and retain previous H, p, and matching-certificate provenance. At most d updates, with a full final unsuccessful scan. Every update preserves S as the preimage of the new quotient support, by the period invariant and the identity S = r_H^{-1}(r_H(S)).

**Canonical fixed point within a lane.** If H is enlarged, any certified direction maps to either zero or another certified direction: all original switchable edges remain switchable for its image. Thus the extensive update map adding all currently certifiable directions is monotone; arbitrary one-direction updates starting at zero remain within the least fixed point, and any exhaustive terminal scan has reached that least fixed point. This is order independence for this certificate family, not all semantic periods or different pair projections.

**Exact reverse lift.** Given an actual quotient-target matching, undo period updates in reverse. For a step H -> H+<p>, the residual difference between target and current actual signature reduces modulo old H to 0 or p. If p, the certificate guarantees at least one switchable edge in the current actual matching. Replace it by a suitable parallel alternative. This reduces the residual to H while preserving actual matching and earlier-later compatibility in the reverse induction. At H=0 the exact target is attained. No arbitrary XOR of matching words is used. In appended guard space the exact original guard RHS is restored at the end; intermediate matching words need not satisfy the full guard. Do not export those words as original source witnesses until restoration.

This is a paid, target-free special case of FG03's semantic period method: no exact full support tables, guessed matching, SAT oracle, or exponential signature acquisition is needed to certify and remove each period.

## 4. Theorem B: deterministic sparse support acquisition independent of affine rank

Let G now be planar bipartite, with arbitrary binary edge-label vectors l_e in dimension d, and nonnegative integer signature counts c_s. There is a deterministic algorithm which, given integer cap K>=1, either returns the exact list {(s,c_s):c_s>0} if its size is <=K, or certifies that the number of distinct signatures exceeds K. It uses <=1+dK signed weighted matching determinant evaluations, polynomial bit work in the explicitly encoded graph, d and K, and no dependence exponential in signature affine rank. A target witness can subsequently be recovered with <=mK more determinant probes. This holds for quotient labels and appended affine guards as well. Counts after quotient are aggregate counts only, not original exact-cover counts.

### 4.1 Paid character query

For a in F2^d, define F(a)=sum_s c_s (-1)^(a dot s). Use a Kasteleyn signing of the planar multigraph and weight its indexed edges by (-1)^(a dot l_e). All reference determinant monomials have the same sign; multiplying by that fixed reference sign yields F(a) exactly, even when it is negative or zero. Obtain a planar rotation from the subdivided edge graph, read its original-edge face walks and solve binary face-sign equations: for each bounded face of original length 2k, product of signs equals (-1)^(k+1). Omit an exterior face per connected component. A graph with no matching gives zero unweighted determinant; otherwise its sign calibrates every probe. This is the planar bipartite permanent-determinant theorem, not a general exact matching oracle. Parallel edges are summed with their own signs and labels. Zeroing indexed edges for later deletion queries retains the original coherent signing and calibration.

### 4.2 Prefix acquisition with a small character basis

After j coordinates, maintain actual prefix set P_j with positive counts w_u = sum_{s extending u} c_s, plus a set B_j of |P_j| characters supported on the first j coordinates, whose evaluation matrix E_j=( (-1)^(a dot u) )_(a in B_j,u in P_j) is invertible over Q. Choose it also invertible over F3. Start from P_0={empty}, count F(0), B_0={0}; F(0)=0 returns empty support.

For next bit j+1 and each a in B_j, query F(a+e_{j+1}). Let h_u be the difference between the counts of the two children u0,u1. Then the query vector is exactly E_j h. Solve this linear system over the rationals; the two true counts are (w_u+h_u)/2 and (w_u-h_u)/2. They are nonnegative integers. Retain only positive children. Since each prefix extends to a full word and distinct prefixes cannot merge later, if their number exceeds K, report certified cap overflow. Otherwise all prefixes have been exactly acquired, and each such stage queried at most K characters.

Acquire the new basis without searching all characters. Restrict the old basis and its products by the new coordinate character to the new positive prefixes. They span all functions there over F3: any function on the new prefixes extends arbitrarily to missing children, and decomposes on each old prefix into average plus new-character times half-difference (2 invertible in F3). Each old-prefix function is in the old basis span. Therefore greedy elimination of these <=2K rows over F3 obtains exactly |P_{j+1}| independent rows, which also have nonzero integer determinant over Q. Reuse this basis for the next stage. At j=d the true support and counts, not just an affine hull or low-degree moment approximation, have been recovered.

This is a self-contained deterministic sparse Walsh acquisition special to exact nonnegative support; related general deterministic sparse Fourier algorithms are already known. It does not establish small support for arbitrary input. A cap overflow is UNKNOWN for P3DM decision, never an UNSAT verdict.

### 4.3 Witness restoration without reacquiring the full table

At the end retain square E and its inverse. For any edge-deletion subgraph of the original graph, all its signatures belong to the previously acquired support S. Thus the same basis B gives a query vector F_D and the same inverse E^{-1} yields its exact subgraph counts (including newly zero coordinates). For any desired positive signature, greedily delete one indexed edge whenever its desired count remains positive. At most m edge attempts, each <=K determinant probes. The final graph has one inclusion-minimal matching realizing the signature: if it had any edge outside one such matching, deletion of that edge would have remained feasible, contradicting its earlier failed deletion (feasibility monotone under further deletions). Read its actual indexed edges. Restore Theorem A's periods if used, then restore source singleton choices and verify every original exact-one count. Can also return every support witness at cost <=m K^2 probes, or keep compact inverse+deletion provenance for on-demand local lift.

## 5. Cooperative algorithm and exact limits

For each of the three pair projections separately, compute the switch-period closure H before trying cap K on the quotient signature support. If one lane returns a complete list, test its target exactly; on YES lift and verify, on NO the source is UNSAT. If all capped lanes return overflow, use other certified terminals, not false rejection. This is a deterministic polynomial-time partial portfolio with exact decision on every admitted input. Unlike a 2^r rank Fourier table, support cardinality controls acquisition. Unlike FG03's full-table semantic period closure, Theorem A may contract dense support before acquiring it.

Can apply this to matching components independently, then transfer their actual acquired local tables and reversible periods into FG03's exact semantic quotient/seam joins or AB08's paid boundary joins when the requisite interface bounds hold. Global join cost is still separate; polynomially many small tables do not automatically imply polynomial target compatibility. An exact global lane can consume an acquired component period because a local period stabilizes the global sum. Do not mix signature families from different pair projections as if they were one matching.

Resource ledger: with m indexed edges, d-bit labels, cap K, input length N including labels and all guards, candidate discovery plus matching certificates uses at most (d+1)m^2 ordinary matching tests, each with polynomial label comparisons/quotient reduction and graph work. Store <=d independent quotient updates; reverse lift scans actual pair alternatives in polynomial time. Planar embedding, signs and matrix construction polynomial. A signed determinant is of order <=n, entries absolute <=m and intermediate exact integer minors have polynomial bit length (Hadamard); counts bounded by 2^m, signed queries same absolute bound. Linear systems of order <=K with ±1 matrix entries have denominator/numerator bit lengths O(K log K + m + log K), by determinant/minor bounds; fraction-free or polynomial-time rational elimination yields polynomial bit work. Basis selection uses field F3 and O(K^3) operations per stage. Query bounds as above, with polynomial-time encoding of query masks; caching optional and bounded by query count. For fixed K=N^c, all unsuccessful capped work is polynomial too. Three lanes add at most a constant factor; no simultaneous parallel space assumption. Store O(poly(N,K)) bits including matrices, masks, cache, provenance, inputs and output; cumulative memory bounded by same-realization T*S. Explicit component factors charge their actual join separately. Verification of the returned original triple set is linear in explicit incidences plus indexing. No claimed polynomial cap for all P3DM instances.

## 6. Genuine planar SAT/UNSAT neighbors with linearly growing rank but sparse support

For n>=2, k=n-1, define the YES source by triples
(0,0,0), (0,0,k),
(i,i,i), (0,i,i-1), (i,0,i-1) for i=1,...,k.
The NO neighbor removes just (0,0,0). Both are connected and incidence planar: draw the XY graph as length-three internally disjoint paths x0-y_i-x_i-y0 plus the two central parallel edges. Arrange paths in increasing i between the central edges. Place z_i in the adjacent face between paths i and i+1 (z0 next to first central edge; zk next to second). Its incident triple midpoints are on that face and can be joined as a star. Deletion preserves planarity. No element has degree 0 or 1. XY supports every edge (all diagonals or swap one index with 0, either central choice); XZ and YZ have identity edges, row 0 can use every label, row i can use i or i-1. Every such edge occurs in a matching via a cyclic shift from 0 through the downward chain. Thus the specified singleton/pair-support closure does not delete any edge. Stronger affine checks may solve these simple examples.

Every XY matching either chooses all diagonals with one of the two central labels, or swaps exactly one i with 0. Both cross edges then have label i-1 and cancel in parity. Write t=1^n. YES support is exactly {t} union {t+e0+ei: 1<=i<=k}, of cardinality n and affine rank n-1. NO support is exactly the latter set, of cardinality n-1 and affine rank n-2. Thus a rank-based full Fourier table is exponential even on this elementary family, but Theorem B uses O(n^2) character queries. Counts need not be all one: the central k choice and swap k give the same signature. Source YES has the diagonal matching; NO has no target signature. This is a rigorous infinite family, not an asymptotic decision lower bound.

## 7. Dense-support family bypassed by period acquisition

Adapt P3AGE03 octahedral face gluing. Start all 8 triples on two indices per color, choose unused face f=(0,1,0). For k=2,...,n-1 remove f and insert the seven other faces of the octahedron on each color's old coordinate f_i and new index k; choose next f=(k,1,0). Embedding is the connected sum of colored octahedral triangulations, with triple vertices in faces. This never removes x=y triples, so (i,i,i) and (i,i,0) exist for every i. Independently choose either at each diagonal pair: the n-1 nonzero shifts e0+ei are independent. Since every n-edge matching has the same total parity, actual support has exactly 2^(n-1) words. All three pair-support tests retain all triples inductively: old projected edges remain; new diagonal extends any old matching; two cross edges replace a matching edge at the old seam. Connectedness preserved.

Theorem A certifies every e0+ei without support acquisition. For i>=2, y_i is adjacent to exactly x_i and x_(i-1), both pairs carrying labels 0,i; every matching covers y_i with a switchable edge. For i=1, y0 has exactly the two original pairs each carrying labels 0,1. These certificate directions span the even-parity space; monotonicity ensures any exhaustive scan reaches it. Quotient support is a singleton. This is a genuine complementary family: sparse acquisition alone would overflow at every fixed polynomial cap eventually, period contraction reduces it before any such table. Does not imply every dense support is a period coset.

## 8. Explicit simultaneous obstruction to this two-terminal portfolio (not to unrestricted P3DM algorithms)

Take the four even-parity triples on {0,1}^3: 000,011,101,110. For n>=3 add 220,212,022; then for k=3,...,n-1 add (k,k,k-1), (k,k-1,k), (k-1,k,k). This is the black-face subset of a planar properly 3-colored octahedral connected sum. Start checkerboard coloring of octahedron faces, glue along unselected face 010, then along successive unselected faces (k,k,k). Selected faces never share a triangulation edge, so all three projections have no parallel edges. In each projection one appends a square along an existing supported edge; all old projected edges remain and the same extend/swap proof gives all new edges supported. All elements have degree>=2 and each pair projection is connected. Thus the explicit singleton/pair-support closure stagnates. No single-switch certificate is possible in ANY lane, since there are no parallel pairs.

For any lane choose all diagonal coordinate pairs as reference. On disjoint tail pairs (i,i+1), i=3,5,... <=n-2, swap the matching edges. Each such swap replaces two distinct labels i-1,i by two copies of label i+1, changing signature by e_(i-1)+e_i. The shifts are independent (disjoint label pairs), and swaps are simultaneously realizable since coordinate pairs are disjoint. Hence each lane has at least 2^floor((n-3)/2) distinct signatures in ONE connected matching component, and all three exceed any fixed polynomial cap eventually. This falsifies the conjecture that single parallel-switch periods plus sparse support universally cover a lane after the specified closure; it also defeats independent connected-component factoring of that exact lane before other transformations.

The family is easy UNSAT: each of the last index's three element vertices occurs in exactly two of the same three triples, each of which covers exactly two of those elements. Their equations sum to 2(a+b+c)=3, impossible even modulo 2. Degree-two/affine closure or a small separator detects it. Therefore this is NOT an obstruction after arbitrary stronger forced-information closure, not a counterexample to the full multi-lane portfolio, and not a runtime lower bound. It pinpoints the need for semantic contraction involving nonparallel alternating paths/cycles, rather than refining the sparse Fourier query mechanism.
