## 2. Exact signed planar ground-state evaluator
For an arbitrary connected plane zero-field graph with rational binary-encoded couplings J, prefer edge product p_e=-sign(J_e) (choose + for zero). An edge is frustrated iff actual product differs. Its extra cost over -|J_e| is 2|J_e|. Let T consist of faces with product of preferred signs around the boundary walk equal -1. Frustration edges must form a T-join in the geometric dual: their degree parity is prescribed at each face. Conversely every dual T-join produces actual edge products with product +1 around every primal face. Facial boundaries span binary cycle space, so assign a root spin and traverse a spanning tree; every edge equation then holds. Bridges are dual loops and may be left unfrustrated. Among parallel dual edges keep the cheapest representative, storing original edge ID. Minimum T-join is found by nonnegative shortest paths between T vertices and minimum-weight perfect matching of their metric closure: any T-join decomposes into paths pairing odd vertices plus cycles; discard cycles, and shortest paths bound from below. Conversely XOR the matched shortest paths, cancel duplicated edges, and obtain a T-join no more expensive. These inequalities prove exactness. Use exact integer-scaled weights, including zero edges. Reconstruct actual spins. Disconnected components solved independently. Polynomial bit time and space; no exponentiation in magnitude of weights.
For at most three boundary spins B forming a clique of real or zero-weight virtual edges, condition on a relative word with first spin +1. Add coupling -L b_i to edge (B0,Bi), L>sum |J|. A violation costs 2L, greater than the entire unpinned objective range; hence minimizer satisfies all relative pins. Global flip fixes B0. Pins use existing clique edges so preserve planar topology. Evaluate unpinned energy on returned spin word. Empty B has one mode; size 1 also one, size 2 two, size 3 four. This differs from adding arbitrary hard fields at interior vertices.

## 4. Four-port group cancellation (new algebraic extension)
Theorem B. Let any number of regions have disjoint interiors and all meet a common four-spin separator S, with no other private cross-interactions. Suppose each exact conditional energy table is acquired with actual witnesses for its eight relative modes. Each even table has unique expansion
 f_i(y)=c_i+sum_{u<v} J^i_uv y_u y_v+q_i y_1 y_2 y_3 y_4.
The quartic coefficient is the average of f_i times y_1 y_2 y_3 y_4 over the eight modes. The regions can be jointly replaced by one constant and six pair interactions on S iff sum_i q_i=0 (as an exact energy identity on all boundary words). Necessity by Walsh orthogonality; sufficiency by additivity of conditional minima because interiors are disjoint, then retrieve all actual local witnesses for common boundary. A clique fill on S can be nonplanar in larger graph; verify admission of the resulting torso, do not infer planar gluing from algebraic cancellation. If sum q_i !=0, reject this contraction, not source. If used on a supplied decomposition, charge every region once, at most 8 states per group; no universal selection or polynomial acquisition of arbitrary large four-port tables claimed. Polynomial for polynomially many admitted tables plus resulting admitted backbone; repeated cancellation must retain exact ownership and strictly shrink private interiors.
Explicit obstruction to universal pairwise four-port elimination: eliminate a central spin with four +1 couplings; f(y)=-|y1+y2+y3+y4|, quartic coefficient +1/2. No exact pairwise energy on these same four ports. A second star with first coupling -1 has g=-|-y1+y2+y3+y4| and quartic -1/2. Their aggregate is exactly quadratic: both nonquadratic groups cancel after combining. This is the precise finite-type multisign collaboration analogue: 8 boundary values per group, only one signed obstruction scalar for four-port quadratic absorption. The example is an abstract zero-field network, not a claimed authentic P3DM region or source-wide coverage theorem. More than four boundary spins have further even Walsh terms; no finite universal type assertion.
