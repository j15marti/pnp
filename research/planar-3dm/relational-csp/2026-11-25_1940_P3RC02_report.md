## 1. Color-paired conservation, any element degree
Represent each incidence edge by a bit. A region R is a vertex subset in the incidence graph; on cut edges the endpoint inside has either a triple (out) or element (in). Let K be selected internal triples, A_c the number of internal elements of color c, P_c,Q_c the sum of active outgoing/incoming bits of color c. Counting internal selected incidences yields K-A_c=P_c-Q_c over integers. This uses one incidence of each color per selected triple and exact-one at each element, not cubicity.
If one color c0 has no boundary incidences, K=A_c0. Suppose each of the other two colors has at most one outgoing and at most one incoming port. Then P_c-Q_c=A_c0-A_c. For a pair of ports the only possibilities are equality (difference 0), a uniquely pinned pair (difference +/-1), or no assignment (absolute difference >1). For one or zero ports, the equation likewise pins or rejects. Thus the entire boundary has at most two free Boolean coordinates, each copied to its paired ports. Every exact regional mask, including arbitrary unary internal guards, is a subset of the cube on those two coordinates. All such subsets have a 2-CNF representation: forbid every absent word by a clause of length at most 2. This is exact, not an affine hull. No cyclic-order assumption or new geometric edges; port identifications use original indexed incidences.
Corollary for cubic 4-cuts: balanced colors with one out/in of each of two distinct colors give equal internal counts by T_R-3A_c=p_c-q_c; both pairs equal. For the same-color 2-out/2-in case only sum equality is guaranteed. The other possible color pattern, up to reversing in/out and permuting colors, has p-q=(1,1,-2). The two outgoing bits agree, and their common bit plus the two incoming bits sums to 1. Proof: cubicity makes p-q congruent across colors mod3; total out=in=2. Differences are either all zero or permutations of (1,1,-2) and its negative. In the skew case A=(a,a,a+1), K=a+out_bit, so incoming sum=1-out_bit. Exact relation can be arbitrary subset of these necessary states. Do not insert full three-way exact-one into a 2-SAT terminal.

## 3. Genuine connected scalable SAT/UNSAT neighbors
Q=001,010,011,100,101,110, three complementary-pair covers. In copy i choose distinct left and right seam colors; permute Q coordinates so left is original X, right Y. Cut left incidence of tuple 001 and right incidence of tuple 100. In consecutive blocks swap the two endpoints at the shared seam color (right of i, left of i+1). Each block has two paired colors, no third boundary, equal internal counts; the seam bits must agree on the two swapped wires. Closed Q restored from any consistent local seam word has one of three covers: selected port pairs 10,01,00. Delete original local tuple 010 (third pair's member) to realize exact XOR mask {01,10}. Retain it in block 0 to realize NAND {00,01,10}. Thus a k-block all-XOR ring is SAT iff k even; restoring one tuple makes all odd rings SAT (all other XORs force equal incident bits at relaxed block, choose 00). Global witness: choose local cover for each accepted mask and take union of actual swapped triple IDs. All k>=3 choose seam colors alternating 0,1, replacing last by 2 for odd k. Planarity: base two cut edges lie on common face t0,Y0,t3,Z0,t1,X0, both traversed element->tuple in same facial orientation. Draw each block in a disk, expose port pairs in two distinct boundary intervals, orient consistently around a ring; opposite-facing intervals have reversed linear order so cross-join tuple to element with two nonintersecting arcs. Deleting 010 preserves embedding. Planarity checked independently computationally at all reported sizes; no claim of hardness of this class.
All-XOR odd ring supplies a genuine obstruction to independent local support, even after initial source closure. It is NOT a barrier to GF2 closure, which can detect this parity example; no superiority to entire portfolio claimed.
## 4. Counterexample: not every actual four-port mask is bijunctive
