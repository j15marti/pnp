## 2. Exterior-connected groups and cactus quotient (PROVED)
Apply exact-one singleton/zero closure and indexed allowed-edge support in all three bipartite projections until stable. Any rejection is sound; positive marginal support is not a source solution. Consider one connected component C of the XY multigraph with every indexed edge supported in some XY perfect matching. Each indexed edge corresponds to one original triple vertex with third label z. Let a=|X(C)|=|Y(C)|. Delete the X/Y vertices and all triple vertices of C from the actual residual incidence graph. For each exterior connected component B touching C via its Z vertices, let S_B be the set of indexed C edges with z in B. These partition E(C). No embedding or facial identification necessary.

Any global source solution uses exactly q_B=|Z(B)|-|X(B)| of S_B, because all other XY components inside B use exactly their X cardinalities of triples and each covers one Z in B. Similarly |X(B)|=|Y(B)| on a feasible projection. Reject impossible quotas; q=0 deletes S_B, q=|S_B| forces all S_B, with original closure repeated. This refines the local-face viewpoint of CPS04 without claiming all exterior components coincide with faces.

For any subset S of a single S_B, contract connected components of C-S in the XY multigraph, retaining each S edge as an indexed edge (loops possible), to get Q. Q with loops removed is a cactus multigraph: its blocks are bridges or cycles, including parallel 2-cycles. Proof: in the actual planar incidence graph, contract each subdivided component of C-S and the disjoint exterior B to an apex b; each S triple vertex remains a subdivision vertex adjacent to b. If Q contains a theta (three internally vertex-disjoint paths between the same two distinct vertices, including parallel edges), choose one subdivided edge on each path, contract the two sides of each path toward its endpoints, and delete other edges. The two endpoints and apex on one side, three retained triple subdivision vertices on the other give a K3,3 minor. Impossible. A loopless multigraph with no theta has only bridge or cycle blocks: any non-cycle 2-connected block has a cycle with a chord or an additional ear, yielding a theta. Thus the claim.

Fix any indexed perfect matching M. Orient nonloop S edges from X component to Y component when not selected in M and oppositely when selected. For any other perfect matching M', the symmetric difference restricted to S, with this orientation, is a nonnegative integral circulation on Q, since each contracted component has fixed X-minus-Y occupancy. Hence its nonloop edges form a union of complete directed cycle blocks. Each loop can be treated separately. The independent cycle/loop switch envelope is a necessary relaxation, not an exact claim of joint local lift. Any complete S mask can be tested and lifted by forcing its chosen indexed edges, deleting its other S edges, and solving ordinary remaining XY matching. A polynomial count DP on the envelope is possible: each cycle contributes one of its two selected-edge counts; loop contributes 0/1; other edges fixed. Counts <=m. If target q_B unreachable, reject, or pin a switch and reject its corresponding guarded option. Exact lift may still fail. This DP filter is theorem-level, not implemented in this run.

## 3. Constant-count bound and classification (PROVED)
Suppose EVERY XY perfect matching of C takes exactly r edges in S, with S in one exterior connected B as above. Then |S|<=2r. Let A be unsigned vertex-edge incidence matrix of C and w=1_S. The nonnegative polytope Ax=1 equals convex hull of indexed perfect matchings: Hall applied to positive support gives a perfect matching, subtract its minimum positive entry and renormalize, iterating, or use bipartite integrality. Because every edge is supported, average one witness per edge to obtain a strictly positive feasible point. For any v in ker A, sufficiently small positive and negative perturbations remain feasible. Constancy of w therefore implies w is in row space of A. Change Y signs: there exist potentials p with p(x)-p(y)=w_xy. Potentials constant on C-S components; no S edge is a loop. Integer potentials can be acquired by a single BFS of the indexed equations with one potential normalized to zero. Conversely potentials imply constant count by summing over matching edges. Thus certificate acquisition polynomial, no matching-support enumeration needed.

On each directed residual Q cycle, an unused S edge decreases potential by 1 and a selected edge increases by 1. Hence equal numbers of selected and unselected S edges per directed cycle. Each unselected S edge appears in some matching and therefore in a directed cycle. The cycles are edge-disjoint by cactus structure. Number unselected <=number selected=r, proving bound. In particular if r=1, at most two actual triples, with a single fixed choice or complementary Boolean selections. Exterior connectivity is essential: full planar six- or eight-triple 2x2x2 base has a three- or four-edge X star with occupancy 1 but its Z labels are disconnected outside C.

Corollary: If every exterior group of C is constant-count, m_C=sum |S_B|<=2 sum r_B=2a. A nontrivial connected matching-covered multigraph has minimum degree >=2 (degree-one edge forced, all other edges at its neighbor unsupported). Since |V|=2a, m_C>=2a; equality forces one even cycle, allowing parallel 2-cycle. Single-edge case separate. Hence every non-cycle supported projection component has at least TWO nonconstant exterior groups, because the counts sum a. In the quotient C–Z incidence graph (one node per XY component, one per Z, edge iff some triple connects them), every non-articulation component node in its connected component is necessarily a single edge or cycle: outside connected implies one group S=all edges, automatically constant. Complexity of noncycle components is localized at component articulations. This is not a bound on the number of articulations or on their joint local mask complexity. With all C single-edge/cycle, acquire <=2 actual indexed matchings each and discard duplicate-Z choices. Pairwise mask collisions between binary options give exact 2SAT; total n chosen ensures coverage, and stored original IDs lift. This integrates R03 cycle terminal, not a new claim of novelty for that terminal.

## 5. Exact automatic 2-cut factoring bypass (PROVED, executed)
The splice graph is a tree of cores with two cross incidence edges per tree adjacency. Any cut splitting vertices of a base unit crosses >=3 original base edges. For each swapped edge of that unit there is an external replacement path through its own disjoint branch (connected cores); paths for different swapped edges use disjoint branches. Thus the global cut must have >=3 distinct crossing edges. So no 1- or 2-edge cut splits a unit. The 2-edge cuts are exactly the pairs of splice edges: a cut not splitting units corresponds to a cut of the unit tree, with two actual edges per tree edge. This gives an automatically discoverable decomposition, not a supplied oracle.

General admitted algorithm: in a bridgeless incidence graph find all edges participating in any 2-cut, by deleting each edge e and finding bridges in G-e. With standard DFS O(E(V+E)) index work; remove union of these edges, compute components, verify quotient is a forest with exactly two actual cross edges per adjacency, no retained cross loop. If not, UNKNOWN. For each atom enumerate its internal triple selection bits and all incident boundary-edge bits; enforce triple-node equality on all its incident bits, element-node exact-one occupancy on internal/boundary bits. Keep exact local witness per feasible row. Each atom exports at most four possible two-bit messages to parent; bottom-up retain rows extendible in every child, with actual witnesses. Forest roots compatible iff exact source YES. Every joined row shares the same two original cross incidence bits, so all original equations hold; final verify original triples. If a=max atom (#internal triple vertices + #incident boundary edges), total time <=poly(N)+2^a poly(N), not product over atoms. Polynomial for a=O(log N). In constructed family a<=12 (six triples, at most six cross edges), <=5 feasible local rows observed. No need to enumerate the 2^k central masks.

One-triple genuine SAT/NO neighbors: delete 001 and 010 of first central unit, forcing A there. Across a Y splice the two selected cross bits must agree by color conservation on the cut (equal original X/Y counts and one Y incidence exchanged each direction). Likewise Z swaps have equal bits. Undoing all swaps recovers base-unit covers. Hence A forced through central chain; removing the 111 triple at the final central unit makes NO, adding it back yields YES.
