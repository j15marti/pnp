## Exact local theorem
On a region U of incidence vertices, retain physical boundary incidence bits x. Let K be count of selected internal triple vertices, a_c number internal elements of color c. Exact equations imply K=a_c+sum outgoing selected bits -sum incoming selected bits. Complement outgoing triple-side bits to obtain b; d_c=a_c+#outgoing ports of color c; K=d_c-sum_{color c} b_e. R (exact feasible boundary table, including any local unary guards) is a subset of this integer conservation set E. If a color is absent, K fixed. Thus R has constant normalized Hamming weight, and fixed physical parity. Otherwise all 3 colors occur; for <=4 ports the multiplicities are (1,1,1) or (2,1,1). K has at most two consecutive possible values by the singleton equations. Each parity layer fixes K because total normalized weight=sum d_c-3K. Each such layer has <=2 rows differing at most two bits, since only one color can have two ports.

Any nonempty fixed-parity relation on <=3 free bits is an even delta-matroid: for any distinct rows their symmetric difference is two bits, both flipped to reach the other row. Hence for constant-parity arity-four R, if not already even delta, pin any one wire (each nonempty fiber then has <=3 free bits). For mixed-parity R use guard XOR of all its wires; each nonempty fiber is even delta by preceding argument. Affine coordinate translation preserves exchange and parity differences. Direct even delta or repaired fibers, normalized by the above complement, all have constant Hamming weight: if all colors occur, parity fixes K. Such fixed-weight even delta relations are matroid bases, since the symmetric exchange axiom necessarily swaps one chosen and one unchosen element. For rank <=1 or corank <=1 use zero/one matching slot and complementary bits where necessary. For rank 2 on <=4 ports, allowed pairs form the complete multipartite graph on nonloops (parallel classes): basis exchange implies nonadjacent nonloops are equivalent by the standard parallel-element relation, or direct exchange check. With two parallel classes assign one exclusively to each slot. With >=3 classes there is at most one class of size >=2; assign that class to slot1 only, all singleton classes to both slots. This realizes exactly the bases; loops get no slot edges. For four singleton classes both slots accept all. For all cases <=2 slots after global local complementation. This is an ordinary general graph matching gadget, NOT necessarily planar/cofacial. It need not preserve counts or source syntax; store local source witnesses. Empty fibers reject.

Bonus exact representation: any conservation E of arity <=4 and subset R can be written E intersect B, with B a 2-CNF (empty R handled separately). When a color absent, each color block has fixed weight; a row can be uniquely identified within E by specifying its minority bits per group (<=2 total across <=4 coordinates). If all colors occur, E is singleton/two rows or at most three rows: under flips/permutation the maximal four-port relation is {1000,0100,0011}; each row uniquely identifiable by one positive coordinate. Each omitted row is eliminated by negating its identifying conjunction. Hence a constant-size binary supplement suffices; this does NOT make E itself 2-CNF.

## Sharp obstruction: NP-hardness of a bounded group language with paid acquisition
Take Dyer-Frieze degree-2/3 source. Hall: for any triple subset S, 3|S| incidences go to neighbors of degree <=3, so |N(S)|>=|S|. Compute a bipartite matching saturating all triple vertices. Contract each matched triple-element edge into a pair block, leaving unmatched element vertices as singleton blocks. Source planarity preserved by edge contraction (rotation inherited); quotient multiedges retain original IDs. A paired element of degree d has d-1 incoming bits a_j and two outgoing bits c,d' of other colors. Its exact mask obeys sum a_j+c=1 and c=d'. Thus arity 3 or4 and <=3 rows, acquired by constant evaluation. Unmatched elements are exact-one arity <=3. All actual original witnesses correspond exactly to mask-network solutions. This proves NP-hardness of this precise source-derived planar bounded-type language: a polynomial solver for all such networks would imply P=NP. Finite masks, source-derived grouping, bounded arity, and one local guard do not imply global tractability. No unconditional separation claim.

In this paired chart, each paired block's guard parity is 1 XOR its selected original triple bit, because sum incoming=1-s and outgoing bits both s. If all triples globally supported both ways, there is one mixed parity guard per triple. Thus guard vector identifies the entire actual source selection. A family with 2^k distinct original solutions requires affine guard image rank >=k. This statement only concerns the specified paired chart and exact guard enumerator; other partitions/lanes may compress.
