\begin{abstract}
The exact obstruction in this run is whether adding three or more disjoint copies of the critical-clause enforcer can repair the two failed minimum target closures from REF256.  It cannot, in the same restricted slot-faithful architecture.  If $k\geq3$ copies are used, target occurrence balance forces the minimum completion to have one new balanced variable $z$ and $k+2$ new ternary clauses.  After two binary clauses are padded, $k$ negative closure clauses remain, but at most two of them can contain a negative occurrence of $z$.  Thus at least $k-2\geq1$ closure clause consists entirely of ports forced false by the deleted-clause certificate, and the completion is unsatisfiable.  Combined with REF256's exhaustive $k=1,2$ boundary-relation enumeration, no minimum closure in this architecture works for any $k\geq1$.  This is a restricted construction theorem.  Nonminimum $q\geq4$ closures, other gadgets, source-aware correlations, global re-encoding, arbitrary-$A\mapsto\Phi(A)$, and the FLMV numerical contraction remain \status{OPEN}.  P versus NP remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exact obstruction and falsifiers}

The obstruction is \emph{minimum target-quota re-entry after a critical-clause deletion}.  In U198, delete the positive clause
\[
 C=(x_{194}\vee x_{196}\vee x_{197})
\]
to form $D=U198\setminus\{C\}$.  The checked formula $D$ is satisfiable while $D\wedge C=U198$ is unsatisfiable.  Consequently every model of $D$ sets all three literals of $C$ false.  Each polarity-oriented copy of $D$ therefore exports three same-polarity ports that are simultaneously forced false.

Four concrete falsifiers were tested.
\begin{enumerate}[label=F\arabic*.]
\item Some $k\geq3$ minimum closure avoids every all-forced-false negative closure clause.
\item The minimum integer quota solution has $q\neq1$ or $c\neq k+2$.
\item The independently archived $k=1$ or $k=2$ enumeration contains an exact raw boundary relation.
\item Offline logical XOR delta alone yields an arbitrary-machine lower bound.
\end{enumerate}
F1--F3 are false in the stated architecture.  F4 is rejected as a model error: reads, moves, delays, source rereads, unchanged writes, lazy flags, and changed-then-restored cells need not appear in endpoint logical XOR.

\section{All-$k$ minimum-closure theorem}

\subsection{Architecture}
The architecture is exactly the REF256 one-pivot cofactor interface:
\begin{itemize}
\item four missing selected-polarity occurrences from the old cofactor;
\item two opposite-polarity binary clauses that each require one padding literal;
\item $k$ disjoint polarity-oriented copies of $D$, exporting $3k$ forced-false opposite-polarity ports;
\item $q$ new balanced variables and $c$ new monotone ternary clauses;
\item no identification of distinct ports, no duplicate variable inside a clause, and no source-aware global rewiring.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[All-$k$ minimum critical-port firewall]
For every integer $k\geq3$, every minimum slot-faithful proper Monotone 3-SAT-$(2,2)$ closure in the architecture above is unsatisfiable.  Together with REF256, no minimum closure in the architecture realizes the raw two-binary boundary relation for any $k\geq1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Occurrence balance gives the exact integer equation
\[
        3c-4q=2+3k.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Modulo three, $q\equiv1\pmod3$.  Since $q\geq0$, the minimum is $q=1$, whence $c=k+2$.

Write the unique new balanced variable as $z$.  On the selected polarity there are four old deficit slots and the two selected-polarity occurrences of $z$, exactly six slots, hence two ternary clauses.  On the opposite polarity there are $3k$ forced-false port occurrences and two occurrences of $\neg z$.  Two of these $3k+2$ slots pad the two binary clauses; exactly $3k$ slots remain and form $k$ negative closure clauses.

Let $a\in\{0,1,2\}$ be the number of the two $\neg z$ occurrences used as binary pads.  Only $2-a$ occurrences of $\neg z$ remain for the $k$ closure clauses.  Properness permits at most one occurrence of $z$ in a clause.  Therefore at least
\[
       k-(2-a)\ \geq\ k-2\ \geq\ 1                    \tag{2}
\]
of those clauses contain only forced-false critical ports.  Such a clause is false in every extension of the $k$ copies of $D$, so the whole closure is unsatisfiable.

For $k=1,2$, (2) alone is not uniform.  REF256 exhaustively enumerated all proper labelled minimum layouts and projected the complete free boundary relation.  For $k=1$, all 78 layouts differ from the raw relation; for $k=2$, all 2,580 layouts differ.  Hence no $k\geq1$ minimum closure in this architecture realizes the raw relation.
\end{proof}

The independent program checked (1)--(2) for all $a\in\{0,1,2\}$ and $1\leq k\leq256$, while the proof is symbolic for all $k$.  The verifier made 3,963 assertions without importing producer code.

\subsection{Why this is not a universal closure theorem}
The theorem does not cover the next congruence class $q=4$ or any larger nonminimum closure.  Additional balanced variables can spread negative $z$-type occurrences over more clauses and defeat the simple pigeonhole.  Nor does the theorem cover a different enforcer, sharing variables between copies, a source-aware compiler, or global re-encoding.  It therefore closes one natural escalation of REF256, not the general port-realization problem.

\section{Affine, controller, and $\Phi(A)$ audit}

The affine small-bias descent theorem remains only a restricted semantic/MCSP no-go.  Its YES/NO quantifiers, set-size counting, and nonlinear controls cannot be promoted to an arbitrary SAT runtime lower bound. This run proves only that one minimum local closure architecture fails.  It neither certifies nor falsifies all possible $\Phi(A)$ constructions.
