\begin{abstract}
This run audits whether a one-child source-access lower bound may be multiplied
over the children of a persistent adaptive ARC controller.  It may not: repeated
children can reuse cached source answers.  For a fixed public batch schedule
whose union contains $t$ distinct inverse-permutation demands, the exact
restricted transcript argument is
\[
  2^s m^Q\ge (m)_t,
  \qquad s+Q\log_2m\ge\log_2 (m)_t,
\]
where $s$ is retained source-dependent state and $Q$ is the total number of
source-word probes across the whole batch.  The number of child rounds is absent.
A sixteen-round countercontrol with two repeated demands falsifies naive
round-by-round tensorization.  This is a metadata theorem, not SAT hardness and
not an ARC contraction.  The arbitrary-source power-short child, hereditary
same-model correctness, and entry into the actual FLMV forbidden box remain
\OPEN.  Consequently P versus NP remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Claim card and falsifiers}
The missing lemma tested was: ``does a valid single-child source-probe bound
direct-sum over all children of one stateful adaptive controller?''  Four
falsifiable mechanisms were separated.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Round multiplication:} multiply one inverse-demand information
bound by the number of children.  This is \RET\ without a fresh-demand theorem.
\item \emph{Union accounting:} count the distinct demands across the entire
persistent execution.  This yields the restricted theorem below.
\item \emph{Metadata promotion:} infer arbitrary SAT hardness from a hard
permutation interface.  This is rejected: the interface is a chosen restricted
encoding, and source retention or representation change is legal.
\item \emph{Message promotion:} identify an exact frontier table or extracted
affine theory with a power-short ordinary-SAT child.  This remains false as an
identification and \OPEN as a construction problem.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Restricted batch-union theorem}
\paragraph{Model.}
Let the source be a permutation $\pi\in S_m$, stored as $m$ source words.  A
deterministic preprocessor retains at most $s$ source-dependent bits.  It then
answers $q$ online child rounds.  The public query schedule is fixed independently
of $\pi$; the union of all rounds requests inverse images of $t$ distinct values.
Across the whole persistent run the controller makes at most $Q$ adaptive source
word probes.  It may cache, recompute, and retain state between rounds.

\paragraph{Theorem (\EXACT, restricted fixed-schedule transcript bound).}
Every correct controller in this model satisfies
\[
  2^s m^Q\ge (m)_t=m(m-1)\cdots(m-t+1).
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
For the fixed schedule, the required output is the ordered $t$-tuple of inverse
images.  As $\pi$ ranges over $S_m$, exactly $(m)_t$ such tuples occur.  The
initial retained state has at most $2^s$ possibilities.  A length-$Q$ transcript
of probed source words has at most $m^Q$ possibilities (padding shorter runs to
length $Q$ only enlarges this set).  Determinism makes equal retained states and
equal transcripts produce equal outputs.  Correctness therefore requires at
least $(m)_t$ distinguishable state--transcript pairs.  Taking logarithms gives
the stated inequality.  \hfill$\square$

The proof counts the whole persistent transcript once.  It neither assumes nor
produces a factor $q$.  If later child schedules adapt to source-dependent prior
answers, one must condition on the schedule transcript or prove a stronger
communication/direct-sum theorem; the fixed-schedule statement is not silently
extended to that setting.

\section{Minimal countercontrol to per-child tensorization}
Take $m=8$.  Sixteen child rounds all ask for $\pi^{-1}(0)$ and
$\pi^{-1}(1)$.  A controller scans until both positions are found, stores the
two three-bit answers, and uses no further source probes.  Over all $8!$ sources,
its total probe count is between $2$ and $8$ (average $6$), while its answer
cache is six bits.  The true union has $t=2$ and only
\[
  \log_2(8\cdot7)=5.807\ldots
\]
bits of output information.  The invalid round-multiplied right-hand side is
$16\log_2(56)=92.918\ldots$ bits.  Thus a claimed per-child direct sum is
contradicted by an explicit legal persistent execution.  The correction is not
``free time'': the first scan and the six retained cache bits are charged.

\section{FLMV and ARC accounting consequence}
The actual ARC ledger must charge, on the same realization, discovery, controller
selection and switching, failed children, source access, child construction,
output transport, retained cache, reconstruction, and peak space.  The theorem
allows one of two honest descriptions:
\begin{itemize}
\item retain inverse/source answers and charge their persistent space; or
\item discard them and charge later re-probes or rescans.
\end{itemize}
It does not allow multiplying a one-child lower bound by the child count while
also permitting persistent caches.  A genuine $q$-factor needs a separately
proved fresh-independent-demand, state-erasure, or model-matched direct-sum
hypothesis.  Even such a theorem would constrain source transport only; it would
not construct the required complete ordinary-SAT child $\Phi(A)$ with a strict
power reduction, nor establish hereditary same-machine FLMV entry.

\section{Same-source SAT and UNSAT hostile controls}
The reproducer also keeps the older original-clause extreme diagnostics as
adversarial controls, not as a decision oracle.  The SAT source consists of seven
of the eight sign patterns over $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ plus
$(x_4\vee x_5\vee x_6)$; exact enumeration finds seven models.  The UNSAT source
contains all eight sign patterns and has no model.  For every original signed
occurrence the raw output records the local all-literals-true target $S_j$, the
all-literals-false target $U_j$, both inversions, and every clause-pair
$SS,SU,US,UU$ compatibility value.  It also records $0^n$, $1^n$, flip trails,
complement histograms, and a common complete DPLL backend.

The SAT control required 9 DPLL nodes, 5 branches, 84 clause visits and 168
literal tests; the UNSAT control required 7 nodes, 3 branches, 60 visits and 124
literal tests.  These finite numbers verify same-source YES/NO handling; they are
not asymptotic speedups or lower bounds.

permutation constructions of Munro--Raman--Raman--Rao explicitly trade
