# META-LIFT2: Polylog-Space Succinct Lifting and the Self-Succinctness Barrier

**Status:** Research direction with two exact conditional lifting lemmas and several no-go results. No separation is claimed.

**Date:** 2026-10-16

## 1. Executive update

The earlier META-LIFT1 proposal had the right high-level idea but overemphasized bit-local or one-probe reductions. The literature and a direct generalization of the Allender-Holden-Kabanets argument point to a cleaner target.

For a contradiction from \(P=PSPACE\), the relevant reduction resource is **workspace**, not local query complexity or running time.

The new target is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{P-hardness under deterministic polynomial-output }
\log^{O(1)} n\text{-space reductions}
}
\]

into a language whose succinct version stays in \(PSPACE\), preferably \(PH\).

This strictly broadens the logspace theorem used by Allender, Holden, and Kabanets and avoids a known locality barrier for MCSP.

The main candidate examined here is MOCSP, the Minimum Oracle Circuit Size Problem. Its canonical succinct version has a simple \(\Sigma_2^P\) upper bound. Thus a deterministic polylog-space P-hardness reduction to MOCSP would already imply \(P\neq PSPACE\).

The obstacle is equally sharp: the known MOCSP hardness proof is randomized and deliberately inserts a large random truth table. Making that truth table coherently deterministic and polylog-space generable would amount to supplying the kind of explicit unrestricted circuit lower bound that the meta-complexity program is trying to obtain. Short hashes do not solve this.

## 2. The published starting point

Allender, Holden, and Kabanets prove:

> If MCSP or MKTP is hard for \(P\) under logspace many-one reductions, then \(P\neq PSPACE\).

Their proof assumes \(P=PSPACE\), takes a succinct SAT instance \(D\), and considers a logspace reduction \(f\) from SAT to MCSP. The bit language

\[
B_f=\{(D,i): f(\operatorname{tt}(D))_i=1\}
\]

is in \(PSPACE\): the exponentially long string \(\operatorname{tt}(D)\) never has to be materialized, because each requested source bit can be recomputed from \(D\).

Under \(P=PSPACE\), \(B_f\in P\), so for each fixed \(D\) the output-bit function has a polynomial-size circuit. That circuit is a succinct description of the MCSP instance \(f(\operatorname{tt}(D))\).

Succinct MCSP is in \(\Sigma_2^P\): if the size threshold is larger than the size of the circuit already describing the truth table, the instance is trivially YES; otherwise guess a small circuit and universally verify equality on all inputs.

Thus, under \(P=PSPACE\),

\[
\operatorname{succ\text{-}SAT}\in P.
\]

Since succinct SAT is NEXP-complete,

\[
NEXP=P,
\]

contradicting the deterministic time hierarchy through

\[
P\subsetneq EXP\subseteq NEXP.
\]

The introductory sketch in the same paper also presents the analogous succinct-QBF formulation, yielding an \(EXPSPACE=P\) contradiction. Either hierarchy contradiction is sufficient.

## 3. New lemma: polylog-space lifting

The logspace hypothesis can be relaxed.

### Lemma 3.1: Polylog-space Succinct Lifting

Let \(M\) be a language satisfying:

1. \(\operatorname{succ}(M)\in PSPACE\);
2. \(M\) is P-hard under deterministic many-one reductions \(R\) with polynomial output length and
   \[
   S_R(N)\le(\log N)^k
   \]
   for some fixed \(k\).

Then

\[
\boxed{P\neq PSPACE.}
\]

### Proof

Assume for contradiction that \(P=PSPACE\).

Then \(SAT\in P\). By P-hardness of \(M\), fix a deterministic polylog-space many-one reduction

\[
R:SAT\to M.
\]

Let \(D\) be a circuit describing an exponentially long SAT instance

\[
x=\operatorname{tt}(D),
\qquad
|x|=N=2^{r},
\]

where \(r=\operatorname{poly}(|D|)\).

Consider

\[
B_R=\{(D,i): R(\operatorname{tt}(D))_i=1\}.
\]

To decide \(B_R\), simulate \(R\) on the virtual input \(x\).

Whenever \(R\) asks for source bit \(x_j\), evaluate \(D(j)\). No materialization of \(x\) is needed.

The reduction workspace is

\[
S_R(N)
\le
(\log N)^k
=
r^k
=
\operatorname{poly}(|D|).
\]

The virtual input-head address, output-position counter, and evaluation workspace for \(D\) are also polynomial in \(|D|\). Hence

\[
B_R\in PSPACE.
\]

Under \(P=PSPACE\),

\[
B_R\in P.
\]

Therefore the output-bit function \(i\mapsto R(\operatorname{tt}(D))_i\), with \(D\) hardwired, has a polynomial-size uniformly constructible circuit \(E_D\).

The map

\[
D\mapsto E_D
\]

is therefore a polynomial-time reduction from \(\operatorname{succ\text{-}SAT}\) to \(\operatorname{succ}(M)\).

By assumption,

\[
\operatorname{succ}(M)\in PSPACE=P.
\]

Thus

\[
\operatorname{succ\text{-}SAT}\in P,
\]

so \(NEXP=P\), contradicting the time hierarchy theorem. \(\square\)

### Why this matters

The required reduction does **not** need to be one-probe, NC\(^0\), or even fast.

Its running time may be enormous. For the lifting step, what matters is that a requested output bit can be generated by a simulation whose live workspace is polynomial in the succinct descriptor size.

For an exponentially long virtual source,

\[
N=2^{\operatorname{poly}(m)},
\]

the maximal natural workspace scale that remains polynomial in \(m\) is

\[
\boxed{(\log N)^{O(1)}}.
\]

This makes polylogarithmic workspace the correct LOCAL-META target.

## 4. Correction: extreme locality is the wrong target for MCSP

Murray and Williams prove that MCSP cannot be hard even for PARITY under very local reductions. In particular, for every \(\delta<1/2\), there is no \(O(n^\delta)\)-time projection reduction from PARITY to MCSP.

They also show that NP-hardness under logtime-uniform \(AC^0\) reductions would itself imply major circuit lower bounds.

Therefore the project's earlier goal

\[
T_{\rm bit}=\operatorname{polylog}(n),
\qquad
\text{one or constantly many source probes}
\]

is too strong as a final ordinary-MCSP hardness interface.

The corrected target is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{global computation is allowed, provided writable workspace is }
\log^{O(1)}n.
}
\]

LOCALSHIELD1 remains useful because it developed exact virtual-input accounting, but the final reduction need not preserve its strongest one-probe locality property.

## 5. Candidate target: MOCSP

Rahul Ilango defines MOCSP as follows.

An instance consists of:

- a truth table \(T\) for a Boolean function,
- a size threshold \(s\),
- a truth table \(O\) for an oracle Boolean function.

The question is whether \(T\) can be computed by an \(O\)-oracle Boolean circuit with at most \(s\) wires.

Ilango proves MOCSP NP-hard under randomized polynomial-time reductions.

A stronger 2023 result of Huang, Ilango, and Ren, published in SICOMP in 2025, proves unconditional near-optimal NP-hardness of **GapMOCSP**: Yes instances have very small oracle-circuit complexity while No instances are nearly as hard as random truth tables. This is highly relevant, but their stated MOCSP hardness theorem is still under a **polynomial-time randomized mapping reduction**. The stronger approximation gap therefore does not by itself remove the coherent-randomness problem discussed below.

This target is attractive because its succinct version has the same self-succinct property as MCSP.

---

## 6. New lemma: canonical succinct MOCSP is in \(\Sigma_2^P\)

Use the canonical succinct encoding

\[
\langle D_T,D_O,s\rangle,
\]

where \(D_T\) and \(D_O\) are ordinary Boolean circuits computing the target function and oracle function respectively.

### Lemma 6.1

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{csucc\text{-}MOCSP}\in\Sigma_2^P.}
\]

### Proof

Let \(m\) be the descriptor length.

The circuit \(D_T\) itself is an ordinary circuit for \(T\) and therefore is also an \(O\)-oracle circuit that simply ignores its oracle gates. After a fixed basis normalization, it gives a witness of size

\[
q(m)=\operatorname{poly}(m).
\]

Hence if

\[
s\ge q(m),
\]

the answer is automatically YES.

It remains to consider

\[
s<q(m).
\]

Now \(s=\operatorname{poly}(m)\). Existentially guess an \(O\)-oracle circuit \(C\) of at most \(s\) wires.

Universally quantify over an input \(x\) to \(T\). Evaluate \(C^O(x)\). Every oracle gate query can be answered by evaluating \(D_O\) on the query string. Compare the result with \(D_T(x)\).

The verification predicate runs in polynomial time in the succinct descriptor and guessed circuit sizes. Therefore

\[
\exists C\ \forall x:
\quad C^{D_O}(x)=D_T(x)
\]

is a \(\Sigma_2^P\) predicate. \(\square\)

The lemma uses a canonical descriptor tuple to avoid irrelevant encoding ambiguities about how two exponentially long truth tables and the threshold are concatenated.

---

## 7. Immediate conditional theorem for MOCSP

Combining Lemmas 3.1 and 6.1:

### Theorem 7.1: MOCSP-POLYLOGSPACE

If MOCSP is P-hard under deterministic polynomial-output

\[
\log^{O(1)}n
\]

space many-one reductions, then

\[
\boxed{P\neq PSPACE.}
\]

This is now the cleanest concrete target in the project.

It is weaker than asking for a very local reduction and stronger than the currently known randomized hardness theorem.

## 8. Why Ilango's randomized reduction does not immediately lift

The known MOCSP hardness constructions remain randomized. Ilango's original construction reduces Set Cover to MOCSP using coherent random objects; Huang-Ilango-Ren obtain a much stronger gap by cryptographic/witness-encryption ideas, but their GapMOCSP theorem is likewise a randomized mapping reduction.

In the original Ilango reduction the random objects include:

1. a uniformly random truth table \(T\) of polynomial length in the ordinary source size;
2. a uniformly random partition used to form masked oracle tables.

The same random \(T\) is reused throughout the target instance.

This creates a **coherent-seed barrier**.

A succinct lifting argument needs one polynomial-size circuit that consistently answers every output-bit query. We cannot derandomize each output bit independently: taking the majority value of each bit may produce a target string corresponding to no single random tape.

Under a succinct source of descriptor length \(m\), the ordinary source length can be

\[
N=2^{\operatorname{poly}(m)}.
\]

A random tape of length \(\operatorname{poly}(N)\) is then exponentially long in \(m\). It cannot simply be included in a polynomial-size succinct descriptor.

The statement \(P=PSPACE\) implies decision derandomizations such as \(BPP=P\), but that does not by itself provide a **polylog-space coherent seed selector** for the many-one reduction.

Any seed-selection algorithm that uses polynomial space in the ordinary source length consumes exponential space in the succinct descriptor length and therefore cannot be used in Lemma 3.1.

## 9. Stronger obstruction: the hard-table collapse barrier

Ilango's reduction works because a random truth table is hard for the relevant small oracle circuits.

A tempting idea is to replace the random truth table with a deterministic explicit hard table \(T_r\).

For the succinct-lifting program this is self-defeating if the bit predicate of \(T_r\) is computable in PSPACE.

Suppose:

\[
(i,r)\mapsto T_r[i]
\]

is in PSPACE, and the correctness proof requires

\[
CC(T_r)>r^c
\]

for every fixed \(c\), or stronger \(2^{\Omega(r)}\) complexity.

Under the contradiction hypothesis

\[
P=PSPACE,
\]

the bit predicate of \(T_r\) lies in P. Therefore the family \(\{T_r\}\) has polynomial-size circuits.

That destroys the required unrestricted circuit lower bound.

Hence:

\[
\boxed{
\text{a PSPACE-uniform explicit hard truth table cannot be the object
that closes the }P=PSPACE\text{ contradiction.}
}
\]

If we could unconditionally construct such a PSPACE-uniform family with superpolynomial unrestricted circuit complexity, we would already have proved \(P\neq PSPACE\).

This explains precisely why replacing Ilango's random table with SHA-256, FNV, a pseudorandom-looking wrapper history, or any other efficiently generated deterministic table does not establish the reduction.

## 10. A general abstraction: self-succinct minimization

The useful property of MCSP, MKTP, and MOCSP can be isolated.

### Definition 10.1

Call a minimization problem **self-succinct** if a polynomial-size circuit describing an exponentially long explicit instance automatically supplies a polynomial-size feasible representation, so that:

1. thresholds above \(\operatorname{poly}(m)\) are trivial, where \(m\) is the succinct descriptor size;
2. for smaller thresholds, a candidate representation can be guessed with \(\operatorname{poly}(m)\) bits;
3. correctness of that representation can be universally checked in PSPACE, preferably in \(PH\).

For such problems,

\[
\operatorname{succ}(M)\in PSPACE.
\]

### Theorem 10.2: Self-Succinct Lifting

If a self-succinct problem \(M\) is P-hard under deterministic polynomial-output polylog-space reductions, then

\[
P\neq PSPACE.
\]

This is just Lemma 3.1 packaged as a design criterion.

## 11. The self-succinctness versus explicit-hardness tension

There is a structural difficulty.

Suppose a representation class \(\mathcal R\) is universal enough that every ordinary circuit of size \(m\) can be converted into an \(\mathcal R\)-representation of size \(\operatorname{poly}(m)\).

Then Min-\(\mathcal R\) is naturally self-succinct.

But a superpolynomial explicit lower bound against \(\mathcal R\) would then imply a superpolynomial lower bound against ordinary circuits, up to the polynomial simulation.

Conversely, if \(\mathcal R\) is restricted enough that strong explicit lower bounds are known, such as shallow circuits, formulas, or restricted proof representations, then an arbitrary succinct descriptor circuit need not have a polynomial-size \(\mathcal R\) representation. The self-succinct upper bound can disappear.

This explains a recurring pattern:

\[
\boxed{
\text{easy succinct target}
\quad\leftrightarrow\quad
\text{hard explicit lower-bound problem}.
}
\]

The proof-system portfolio does not automatically evade this tension. Resolution, CP, PC/PCR, and SoS lower bounds concern restricted proof representations, while the succinct descriptor is an unrestricted circuit.

## 12. Partial MCSP was investigated and does not immediately solve the problem

Sparse Partial MCSP looked promising because an early version of work by Allender, Cheraghchi, Myrisiotis, Tirumala, and Volkovich stated deterministic NP-completeness for a sparse partial-function variant.

However:

1. that work was subsequently revised after errors were reported in parts of the original paper;
2. later Partial-MCSP hardness results, including Hirahara's FOCS 2022 result, use randomized reductions;
3. more importantly for this project, a sparse-list representation is not automatically self-succinct.

If an explicit sparse partial function has dimension \(n\) and only polynomially many specified samples, a succinct descriptor can implicitly raise \(n\) to exponential size. Hardwiring all specified samples can then require exponential size in the descriptor length.

Thus the MCSP trick

\[
\text{descriptor circuit itself gives a small candidate}
\]

does not automatically apply to sparse-list Partial MCSP.

Dense Partial MCSP would recover self-succinctness, because the descriptor circuit can directly provide a completion, but deterministic low-space hardness for the dense unrestricted-circuit version is not known.

## 13. Relation to UWI

The project's UWI quantity

\[
H(F)
=
\min_{|p|\le b(|F|)}
\bigl(t_p(F)+q(|F|)+1\bigr)
\]

has the same broad meta-complexity flavor as KT/MKTP.

The project has established the exact equivalence

\[
P=NP
\iff
H(F)\text{ is polynomially bounded on all satisfiable }F.
\]

That equivalence is valuable conceptually but does not itself provide the weak hardness reduction needed for META-LIFT.

The useful question is now narrower:

> Can the witness-search information encoded by UWI or the wrapper be converted into a self-succinct minimization instance by a deterministic polylog-space reduction?

If yes, Lemma 3.1 supplies the hierarchy contradiction. If no, the failure should be characterized as either a non-self-succinct target or a hidden unrestricted circuit lower-bound requirement.

## 14. Role of the wrapper and hashes

The wrapper can still contribute to **source encoding** and **provenance**, but its hashes cannot be used as semantic hardness certificates.

The valid role is:

\[
\text{source computation}
\to
\text{locally / low-space generated target bits}.
\]

The invalid step remains:

\[
\text{short deterministic hash looks random}
\Rightarrow
\text{large unrestricted circuit or KT complexity}.
\]

Any proposed wrapper-to-meta-complexity reduction must therefore prove its minimum-complexity gap without cryptographic assumptions.

## 15. Revised research program

### SPACE-LIFT1

Prove and audit polylog-space output generation rather than one-probe locality.

For a proposed reduction \(R\), establish:

\[
S_R(n)=\log^{O(1)}n,
\qquad
|R(x)|\le |x|^{O(1)}.
\]

Charge:

- every source reread,
- input and output addresses,
- counters,
- normalization,
- malformed-input handling,
- retained preprocessing state.

Running time is secondary as long as the machine halts and remains within the stated workspace.

### MOCSP-DET1

Attack the gap between Ilango's randomized reduction and the deterministic polylog-space reduction required by Theorem 7.1.

Every proposal must answer:

1. What replaces the random truth table?
2. Why is the replacement hard for unrestricted oracle circuits?
3. Is its bit predicate polylog-space computable?
4. If yes, why does the proof not collapse under \(P=PSPACE\)?
5. How is one coherent choice shared across all target bits?

The hard-table collapse barrier should be applied immediately to reject invalid constructions.

### SELF-SUCCINCT1

Search for minimization targets \(M\) other than MCSP/MKTP/MOCSP satisfying:

\[
\operatorname{succ}(M)\in PSPACE
\]

with a known or plausibly provable deterministic polylog-space P-hardness reduction.

A target should be rejected early if its succinct version requires an exponentially large witness.

### WRAPPER-META1

Try to transform the continuation/witness objects produced by WRAPPER-SHIELD into a self-succinct target without claiming that the SAT decider itself computes those objects.

The wrapper's job would be reduction construction, not mandatory solver behavior.

## 16. Current strongest target

The most concrete load-bearing theorem is now:

\[
\boxed{
\textbf{Find a deterministic polynomial-output }
\log^{O(1)}n\textbf{-space reduction from a P-hard language to MOCSP.}
}
\]

If such a reduction exists, then the combination of:

1. Polylog-space Succinct Lifting,
2. \(\operatorname{csucc\text{-}MOCSP}\in\Sigma_2^P\),
3. \(P=PSPACE\Rightarrow PSPACE=P\),

forces a hierarchy contradiction.

This does not solve \(P\) versus \(PSPACE\), because obtaining that reduction is itself a major unresolved hardness problem. But it replaces the previous arbitrary-SAT-decider exhaustion problem with a much more sharply specified reduction problem and identifies exactly which resource matters.
