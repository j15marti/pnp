\section{Independent proof of the cut criterion}
For a free occurrence $e=(v,C)$ put $x_e=1$ when its completed literal is true under $a$.  Let $p_v$ and $f_v$ be the pinned-true and free counts at variable $v$, and let $t_C,f_C$ be the analogous clause counts.  Define
\[
R_v=2-p_v,\qquad L_C=\max(0,1-t_C),\qquad U_C=\min(f_C,2-t_C).
\]
Local feasibility requires $0\le R_v\le f_v$ and $L_C\le U_C$.  Globally one needs
\[
\sum_{e\ni v}x_e=R_v,qquad L_C\le\sum_{e\in C}x_e\le U_C. \tag{1}
\]
For $X\subseteq V,Y\subseteq\mathcal C$, write $R(X),L(Y),U(Y)$ for subset sums and $e_f(X,Y)$ for the number of free sockets between them.

\begin{theorem}[Antipodal cut and certificate ceiling, PROVED/EXACT]
After the local tests, (1) is integrally feasible if and only if, for every $X,Y$,
\begin{align}
R(X)&\le e_f(X,\mathcal C\setminus Y)+U(Y), \tag{2}\\
L(Y)&\le R(V\setminus X)+e_f(X,Y). \tag{3}
\end{align}
Moreover every YES instance has a certificate of at most $3m$ bits, and every NO instance has either one failed local test or a pair of characteristic vectors $(X,Y)$ of $n+m$ bits.  Both certificate types are verified in $O(n+m)$ word operations.  A certificate is found in polynomial time without enumerating all $2^{n+m}$ pairs.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use arcs $s\to v$ with exact flow $R_v$, one capacity-one arc $v\to C$ for every free socket, arcs $C\to t$ with bounds $[L_C,U_C]$, and an exact return arc $t\to s$ of value $R(V)$.  Conservation is exactly (1); integer bounds and max-flow integrality give an integral completion whenever the circulation is feasible.

Hoffman's circulation theorem says feasibility is equivalent to lower incoming capacity not exceeding upper outgoing capacity for every vertex set.  For a set containing $s$ but not $t$, with variable part $X$ and clause part $Y$, that inequality is
\[
R(V)\le R(V\setminus X)+e_f(X,\mathcal C\setminus Y)+U(Y),
\]
which is (2).  A set containing $t$ but not $s$ gives (3) after complementing its clause part.  The cases containing both or neither terminal repeat the same two inequalities.  Thus (2),(3) are complete.

A YES certificate is the free-socket vector $x$, checked against all variable and clause sums.  A failed local interval identifies one variable or clause.  Otherwise Hoffman's alternative supplies a violated cut, encoded by the two bit vectors $X,Y$ and checked by one scan of vertices, clauses, and free sockets.  The standard lower-bound-circulation reduction and max-flow/min-cut algorithm returns either a circulation or such a cut in polynomial time.
\end{proof}
This is an exact theorem transfer from feasible-circulation duality.  It does not transfer a network cut into an unrestricted Turing/RAM lower bound.
