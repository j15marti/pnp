\section{PROPOSE: exact missing lemma and falsifiers}
The preceding LSPV-1 report proved the classical conditional inclusion--exclusion identity for original clause-falsification cubes and exhibited a 542-compatible-subset certificate that SWAP6 entails $x_2$. The missing lemma for this referee run was falsifiable: can this forced literal be replaced by a short source-authenticated resolution/RUP certificate that survives root UP and the residual binary-only 2SAT check, and does discovering that certificate reduce total charged work?

Four mechanisms were tested: bounded-width source resolution under $\neg x_2$; independent RUP replay; an information audit of the complete pair-extreme matrix versus its aggregate histogram; and a uniform width-three prepass plus the same UP--DPLL backend on matched SAT, UNSAT, Horn, padded-2SAT, affine-XOR, duplicate-support, dense planted, exact22 and polarity-renamed controls. The proof certificate succeeds. The fully charged speedup fails.

\section{Original source extremes first}
For every original clause $C_j$ and signed occurrence $x_{i,j}$ the raw audit records the local all-literals-TRUE target $S_j$, its inversion $U_j$, the local all-literals-FALSE target $U_j$, and its inversion $S_j$. Occurrences are never treated as independent Boolean variables. Ordinary clause satisfaction means at least one true literal; $S_j$ means all three.

For every indexed pair $(j,k)$, the audit independently enumerates $SS,SU,US,UU$. As required, $SS,UU$ are feasible exactly when no shared variable has opposite signs, while $SU,US$ are feasible exactly when no shared variable has equal signs. Triple target combinations are checked separately. Globally coherent $0^n,1^n$, complements and one-through-six-variable flip trails retain each original clause's true-literal count and $(N_0,N_1,N_2,N_3)$. Every stored non-tautological source resolvent has its falsifying partial cube and an exhaustive parent-pair exclusion check. These diagnostics do not supply a SAT or UNSAT answer bit.

\section{What the pair-extreme matrix contains}
\begin{theorem}[Labelled occurrence-relation reconstruction]\label{thm:code}
Fix the unsigned original occurrence structure. If the audit retains which pairs of a variable's occurrences have equal versus opposite polarity, then the signed formula is determined up to independent complementation of each source variable. A star of $d_v-1$ relation bits per variable is sufficient and necessary for arbitrary $d_v$-occurrence signings modulo complementation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose one occurrence of each variable as positive reference. Its equal/opposite bit sets every other occurrence. Any signing with the same relations differs only in the reference signs, hence by substitutions $x_v\leftrightarrow\neg x_v$. This bijects assignments and preserves SAT and model count. With the reference fixed there are $2^{d_v-1}$ relative vectors, proving necessity.
\end{proof}

For strict exact22, the six choices of two positive positions among four collapse under complementation into exactly three classes. One trit per variable is necessary and sufficient: fixed binary coding needs at least $\lceil n\log_2 3\rceil$ bits and two bits per variable suffice. At $n=15$ the bounds are 24 and 30 bits. Exhaustive controls obtained $3^n$ classes for $n=1,\ldots,5$. This compresses polarity relations, not unsigned scopes, proof search or SAT semantics.

\begin{theorem}[Aggregate extreme data do not decide SAT]\label{thm:collision}
W1 and SWAP6 have identical endpoint histograms and identical aggregate counts of pair-feasibility types, but W1 is UNSAT and SWAP6 has 54 models.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both have $N(0^{15})=(1,10,7,2)$ and $N(1^{15})=(2,7,10,1)$. Their aggregate pair classes are 117 disjoint pairs with all modes, 13 same-only pairs with $SS,UU$, 43 opposite-only pairs with $SU,US$, and 17 mixed pairs with no extreme mode. Complete enumeration gives 0 and 54 models.
\end{proof}
The full variable-labelled relations can re-encode source signs by Theorem~\ref{thm:code}; the aggregate loses decisive placement. Neither is a free semantic shortcut.
