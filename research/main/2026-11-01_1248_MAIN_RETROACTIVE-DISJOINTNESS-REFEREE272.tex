\section{Exact joint decision-query theorem}
The promised compact pin encoding exposes one discriminating physical slot per
odd profile (for example the $x_4$ pin-presence slot) and one per even profile
(the $x_1$ pin-presence slot).  All remaining promised profile bits are fixed
functions of these indicators.  Querying a redundant correlated slot can reveal at
most the same one indicator and cannot improve the bound.

\begin{theorem}[Exact retroactive pin-query work]
For every $t\ge1$, deterministic exact decision-tree complexity of the promised
same-original residual label is
\[
D(\mathrm{DISJ}_t)=2t.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The upper bound reads $\beta_i,\sigma_i$ for each $i$ and rejects upon seeing
$11$; this uses at most $2t$ queries.

For the lower bound, answer the first queried bit of every still-unseen pair with
$1$ and its mate with $0$.  Until both bits of every pair have been queried, choose
an incomplete pair.  The transcript has a disjoint completion by setting all
unread bits to $0$, and it has an intersecting completion by setting both bits of
that incomplete pair to $1$; the latter respects the first-bit answer when one bit
was already queried.  Thus the Boolean label remains undetermined.  The adversary
forces both bits in all $t$ disjoint pairs, hence $2t$ queries.  The completed
adversarial input has one $1$ and one $0$ per pair and is SAT.  No recovery of the
profile word after output is assumed.
\end{proof}

The three exact regimes are therefore: current $\beta$ before contexts, zero
profile queries after promise authentication; fixed $\sigma=1^t$, exact $t$ on the
all-$A$ accepting input; jointly unknown $(\beta,\sigma)$, exact $2t$ in the worst
case.  The last two are linear, not exponential.
