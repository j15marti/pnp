\section{Construction and theorem}
Let $m=2^k$ source clauses be pairwise variable-disjoint and label them by the
vertices of $\{0,1\}^k$.  The local-gauge NAE frontend gives clause $v$ a gauge
copy $g_v$.  Let $H$ be any connected graph enforcing equality of all $g_v$.
Replace every edge $uv\in E(H)$ by a fresh copy of the seven-clause Fano
equality gadget from FANO-LINEAR-PORT27.  Its unsigned clause supports are
\[
abu_1,\ au_2u_3,\ au_4u_5,\ bu_2u_4,\ bu_3u_5,
\ u_1u_2u_5,\ u_1u_3u_4.
\]
For coordinate $j$, place each source clause and all its private source
variables on the side determined by label bit $j$.  Every such source cut has
boundary zero.  Let $q_j$ count edges of $H$ whose endpoints lie on opposite
sides.

\begin{theorem}[Hypercube multi-cut obstruction]
For every connected $H$,
\[
\max_{1\le j\le k}q_j\ge
\left\lceil\frac{m-1}{k}\right\rceil.
\]
Moreover, after arbitrary placement of every fresh Fano auxiliary on either
side, the corresponding target cut has at least $3q_j$ crossing signed-NAE
clause supports.  Hence at least one of the $k$ aligned target cuts has load
\[
3\left\lceil\frac{m-1}{k}\right\rceil
=\Omega(m/\log m).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For an edge $uv$, the number of coordinate cuts it crosses is the Hamming
distance $d_H(u,v)$, which is at least one.  Therefore
\[
\sum_{j=1}^k q_j=\sum_{uv\in E(H)}d_H(u,v)
\ge |E(H)|\ge m-1.
\]
The first claim is pigeonhole.  For a separated Fano macro fix $a$ on the
left and $b$ on the right.  Exhausting the $2^5$ auxiliary placements gives
crossing-support counts $3$ twice, $5$ ten times, and $6$ twenty times.  Thus
the minimum is three.  Different equality edges have fresh clause supports,
so the loads add.
\end{proof}

The argument applies to paths, trees, or denser connected topologies.  It is
about lifting a specified family of source cuts; it does not say that every
target separator is large.

\section{Experiment and hostile challenge}
The standard-library checker
\texttt{experiments/review/fano\_hypercut\_tree\_audit.py} enumerated all
$1$, $16$, and $262{,}144$ labelled trees for $k=1,2,3$.  The minimum possible
maximum coordinate loads were respectively $1,2,3$, exactly matching
$\lceil(m-1)/k\rceil$, with no violations.  It also verified that the Fano
support hypergraph is linear and that a separated macro has minimum crossing
load three.

The strongest challenge is an explicit direct-solving escape.  Inside one
Fano gadget every pair of its seven variables occurs together in one clause,
so its primal graph is $K_7$.  If $H$ is a path, consecutive Fano $K_7$ blocks
intersect only in a single gauge-copy articulation vertex.  The equality
layer therefore has treewidth exactly six.  Attaching pairwise-disjoint
constant-size clause frontends keeps treewidth bounded by a constant, and
standard dynamic programming solves the constructed family in linear time.
Thus the theorem falsifies simultaneous preservation of the displayed
source cuts, but it supplies no proof-complexity or machine lower bound.
