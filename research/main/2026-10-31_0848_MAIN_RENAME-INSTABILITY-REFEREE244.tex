\section{Exact same-source SAT and UNSAT counterfamily}
Let $s\ge4$, $n=s^2-1$, and let the recognizer use candidate boundaries
$b\le B(N)=O(\log N)$ and block width
$r=\lceil\sqrt{n-b}\rceil$.  Define
\[
 F_n^+=(x_1\vee x_2)\wedge\bigwedge_{i=3}^{n}(x_i),
\]
and
\[
 F_n^-=(x_1)\wedge(\neg x_1)\wedge(x_1\vee x_2)
 \wedge\bigwedge_{i=3}^{n}(x_i)\wedge(x_n)\wedge(x_n).
\]
The first source is SAT with exactly three models.  The second is UNSAT by the
opposite unit pair on $x_1$.  At $b=1$, $r=s$.  In $F_n^+$ the first private
bin contains the coupling clause and $r-1$ units.  In $F_n^-$ the last private
bin contains $r-2$ ordinary units and the two balancing copies of $(x_n)$.
Thus both canonical encodings satisfy the ordered promise.

Let $q=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ and let $\pi$ swap $1\leftrightarrow n$ and
$2\leftrightarrow q$.  The renamed coupling is $(x_n\vee x_q)$.  For every
$b\le B(N)$ and all sufficiently large $n$, its two variables lie in distinct
private bins, so the recognizer rejects $\pi(F_n^+)$ and $\pi(F_n^-)$.  The
renaming preserves truth, model count, and clause structure.

\begin{theorem}[Representation instability]
There are infinitely many same-length SAT pairs and same-length UNSAT pairs
$(F,\pi(F))$ such that the GATE277 ordered recognizer accepts $F$ and rejects
$\pi(F)$ for every candidate boundary it tests.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The recognition claims follow from the preceding bin calculation.  It remains
to check physical source length.  In $F_n^+$ variables $x_1,x_n$ each occur
once and $x_2,x_q$ each occur once.  In $F_n^-$ variables $x_1,x_n$ each
occur three times and $x_2,x_q$ each occur once.  Swapping their textual names
therefore preserves even the native DIMACS character count, not merely the
number of variables and clauses.  SAT status and model count are invariant
under a variable bijection.  Since $B(N)=O(\log N)$ while $q,n-q=\Theta(n)$,
the renamed coupling crosses private bins for every tested $b$ once $n$ is
large enough.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
Ordered recognition success cannot by itself count as all-input ARC coverage.
An all-input compiler must either fall back exactly or provide and fully charge
a permutation-invariant decomposition/normalization finder.  The theorem is
not a lower bound on such a finder.
\end{corollary}
