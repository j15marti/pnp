\section{Target and exact quantifiers}
The concrete missing lemma was the sharp minimum original-clause count for an
UNSAT formula in the strict class
\[
 \mathcal S_{22}=\{F: \text{$F$ is a set of proper width-three clauses and
 every variable occurs twice in each polarity}\}.
\]
The claim proved here is
\[
 \min\{|F|:F\in\mathcal S_{22},\ F\text{ UNSAT}\}=20. \tag{1}
\]
This is a structural extremal theorem.  It is not a running-time lower bound,
an arbitrary-input SAT algorithm, an ARC/FLMV bridge, or evidence that a
$20$-clause instance is difficult for a particular solver.

The attempted falsifiers were: (i) a $12$-clause strict source (the first
arithmetic value after the earlier eight-clause obstruction); (ii) a
$16$-clause source; (iii) a hidden non-strict use of the selector construction;
and (iv) a branch order that makes the claimed selector speedup disappear.
Mechanisms (i)--(iii) are excluded by a directly applicable published theorem;
(iv) occurs in the finite source audit.

\section{Primary theorem and exact corollary}
For a proper 3CNF, Zhang--Peitl--Szeider call it a $(3,p,q)$ formula when every
variable occurs positively at most $p$ times and negatively at most $q$ times.
Their Table~1 states $\mu(3,2,2)=20$.  In the proof, Lemma~13 shows that a
smallest $(3,2,2)$ formula has even size, Theorem~15 proves
$\mu(3,2,2)>18$, and the appendix supplies the known $20$-clause witness
$M_{3,2,2}$ \cite{ZPS}.  Their lower bound uses QBF encodings and
isomorph-free SAT Modulo Symmetries; it is a peer-reviewed computational
classification theorem, not something reproduced by the present small
Python audit.

\begin{theorem}[Exact strict22 minimum]
Equation~\textup{(1)} holds.  The conclusion remains true if connectedness is
added to the definition of $\mathcal S_{22}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every member of $\mathcal S_{22}$ is a $(3,2,2)$ formula, since exact two-per-
polarity occurrence is stronger than the published at-most-two hypothesis.
Thus the known theorem gives $|F|\ge20$ for every UNSAT $F\in\mathcal S_{22}$.

For the reverse inequality, take the project's original indexed source $W_1$:
\[
\begin{array}{llll}
(-1,3,-14)&(2,-8,9)&(10,-13,14)&(3,7,-13)\\
(-4,-9,-11)&(5,8,-14)&(4,-8,-11)&(1,7,13)\\
(1,-7,10)&(-5,-6,-15)&(-2,-6,12)&(-4,9,-10)\\
(-2,5,-12)&(-3,6,-15)&(6,-7,11)&(2,-9,11)\\
(4,8,-10)&(-3,12,15)&(-5,-12,15)&(-1,13,14).
\end{array}
\]
It has $15$ variables, $20$ distinct proper clauses, exactly two occurrences
of each polarity per variable, and a connected primal graph.  Independent
complete enumeration checks all $2^{15}=32768$ coherent assignments and finds
zero models.  A separate fixed-order source-UP--DPLL replay has $76$ conflict
leaves, $75$ branch events and $227$ unit propagations.  Hence a connected
member of size $20$ exists, proving equality.
\end{proof}

The elementary incidence identity $3m=4n$ only forces $m\equiv0\pmod4$.
Together with the earlier eight-clause argument it improves ``nine'' to
``twelve'', and PAIRKERNEL73 already proved all strict sources with defect at
most three SAT.  Neither reaches the sharp value $20$.  The 2024 theorem is
therefore the load-bearing lower bound; the present contribution is its exact
hypothesis match, independent witness replay, and reconciliation into the
active clause-extreme project state.

\section{Mandatory original-clause extreme experiment}
For every ORIGINAL occurrence $x_{i,j}$ with sign bit $\nu_{ij}$, the raw trace
records
\[
 S_j:x_i=1\oplus\nu_{ij},\qquad U_j:x_i=\nu_{ij},
\]
and both inversions.  These are local targets on shared source variables, not
new Boolean variables.  For example,
$C_7=(x_1\vee x_7\vee x_{13})$ has three $S$ targets equal to $1$ and three
$U$ targets equal to $0$.  The indexed pair $C_7,C_{19}$, where
$C_{19}=(\neg x_1\vee x_{13}\vee x_{14})$, shares $x_1$ with opposite sign
and $x_{13}$ with equal sign.  Consequently none of $SS,SU,US,UU$ is feasible.
Both disjunctions can nevertheless be satisfied in middle states.

Source resolution on $x_1$ produces
\[
 (x_7\vee x_{13}\vee x_{14}), \tag{2}
\]
whose falsifying cube $x_7=x_{13}=x_{14}=0$ is source-authenticated: the two
parents reduce to opposed unit clauses on $x_1$.  The independent checker
replays every emitted non-tautological resolvent cube in this manner.  This is
genuine pruning, but is standard resolution and is already visible to source
UP after the cube is pinned.

The globally coherent endpoint histograms for both $W_1$ and its SAT sign twin
are
\[
 (N_0,N_1,N_2,N_3)(0^{15})=(1,10,7,2),\qquad
 (N_0,N_1,N_2,N_3)(1^{15})=(2,7,10,1).
\]
Thus both endpoints fail on both formulas.  The twin has $54$ exact models,
first mask $19186$, while $W_1$ has none.  Endpoint failure, opposing local
demands, and equal endpoint histograms are therefore non-certificates of
UNSAT.  The full indexed pair matrices do differ, as already reported by
LSPV-4; no full-information indistinguishability is claimed.

The raw JSON includes, for every control, every original source clause and
occurrence target, the complete indexed four-way pair matrix, bounded

single/multiple flip trails with exact update checks, all non-tautological

the selected-event solver ledger.  Ordinary SAT always means at least one true
literal, not local $S=111$.

\section{Selector theorem replay and branch-order firewall}
The new LSPV-1 module has, for each $b\in\{0,1\}^3$,
\[
 A_b=(s\vee\ell_1(b)\vee z_b),\qquad
 B_b=(\neg z_b\vee\ell_2(b)\vee\ell_3(b)),
\]
where $\ell_i(b)$ is false exactly at $x_i=b_i$.  The independent replay
confirms the exact claim: eight resolutions on $z_b$, four on $x_3$, two on
$x_2$, and one on $x_1$ derive $(s)$.  Deleting any pattern admits an explicit
$s=0$ model.  This is a valid source-authenticated multi-parent consequence
surviving root UP, but it is not strict22: $s$ occurs eight times in one
polarity, and each $x_i$ occurs eight times in each polarity in the double
module.

used $4103$ nodes on the opposing double module.  In the independently declared
order with selector $s$ first, saturated source-UP--DPLL uses only $15$ nodes,
$8$ conflict leaves, and $24$ unit propagations.  Acquiring the full mandatory
extreme diagnostics costs $3680$ selected logical events before the DPLL
ledger.  This does not invalidate the selector recognizer or its $15$-step
resolution proof; it falsifies only a representation-independent or
branch-order-independent speedup interpretation.
