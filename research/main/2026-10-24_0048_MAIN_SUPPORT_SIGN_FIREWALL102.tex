\begin{abstract}
We prove that every simple 4-regular 3-uniform clause support has an exact signed-(2,2)
signing satisfied by the all-true assignment.  Applying this construction to the explicit
198-variable, 264-clause exact-(2,2) unsatisfiable formula checked in
DOECKER-CORE-CERT61 produces a satisfiable formula with exactly the same ordered unsigned
supports, variable and clause identifiers, record count, and encoded length.  A direct-sum
block family then gives an exact $p$-probe lower bound in a fixed sign-field encoding, where
$p=\Theta(N/\log N)$, but a template recognizer decides that promise family in linear input
time.  Moreover, a paid reduction from the $p$-field implication-chain baseline writes more
than $p$ target words, leaving no positive transport remainder.  Thus unsigned incidence
invariants---including Hall deficiency, width, expansion, and separator data---cannot alone
be label obstructions at this NP-complete endpoint.  This is a representation firewall and
a restricted query lower bound, not an unrestricted machine-time theorem.  P versus NP
remains \textbf{OPEN}.
\end{abstract}

The target language is signed proper exact-(2,2) 3-CNF SAT in a fixed-width record

\section{Three falsifiable mechanisms}

\paragraph{M1: capacity-two clause coverage.}
Can one choose at most two occurrences of each variable while choosing one occurrence in
every clause?  \textbf{PROVED/EXACT} by Hall's theorem; it yields a satisfying balanced
signing.

\paragraph{M2: unsigned-support obstruction.}
Could degree, matching deficiency, width, expansion, separator profile, or clause-dual
geometry distinguish the two labels?  \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED as a stand-alone
mechanism}: the constructed formulas have identical ordered unsigned supports and opposite
labels.

\paragraph{M3: direct-sum amplification.}
Could disjoint blocks turn one sensitive signing into a machine-time lower bound?
\textbf{PROVED} for $p$ fixed-encoding queries, but \textbf{FALSIFIED} as an unrestricted
amplification: a source-aware template scan is linear, and a paid reduction has nonpositive
transport remainder.

\section{The support-sign theorem}

\paragraph{Theorem (balanced satisfiable signing).}
Let $H=(V,E)$ be a simple 3-uniform hypergraph in which every vertex has degree four.
There is a signing of its incidences such that each variable has exactly two positive and
two negative occurrences and every clause has a positive literal.  Consequently the
resulting proper exact-(2,2) 3-CNF is satisfied by the all-true assignment.  The signing is
constructible in polynomial time.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Make a bipartite graph whose left vertices are the clauses and whose right vertices are two
clones $(v,0),(v,1)$ of every variable.  Join a clause to both clones of each of its three
variables.  For a set $Q$ of clauses, its $3|Q|$ incidences enter $N_H(Q)$.  Since every
variable has total degree four,
\[
  3|Q|\leq 4|N_H(Q)|.
\]
The clone neighbourhood therefore has size
$2|N_H(Q)|\geq 3|Q|/2\geq |Q|$.  Hall's condition holds, so there is a matching saturating
all clauses.  Mark the matched occurrence in every clause positive.  No variable is used
more than twice.  For a variable used fewer than twice, mark arbitrary further occurrences
positive until it has exactly two; all remaining occurrences are negative.  Every clause
retains its matched positive literal.  Simplicity of $H$ ensures that distinct supports do
not become duplicate signed clauses.  Hopcroft--Karp uses a graph with $m$ left vertices,
$2n$ right vertices and $6m$ edges, giving time $O(m\sqrt{m+n})$ plus linear validation and
completion. \hfill$\Box$

The degree-four threshold matters: the proof is a capacity statement, not a SAT lower
bound.  It applies to the unsigned support before any runtime-dependent residual is defined.

\section{Concrete opposite-label pair}

DOECKER-CORE-CERT61 reconstructed an explicit support-simple formula $U$ with
$n=198$, $m=264$, four occurrences per variable, and an independently checked \unsat{}
certificate.  Running the theorem's deterministic matching construction on that support
gives $S$ with the following properties:
\begin{itemize}
\item $U$ and $S$ have identical ordered triples of unsigned variable identifiers, equal
metadata, 264 clause records, and equal fixed-width encoding length;
\item $S$ is exact-(2,2), proper, and support-simple, and all TRUE satisfies it;
\item 398 of the 792 literal-sign fields differ;
\item the matching size is 264.
\end{itemize}
The constructed compact formula has SHA-256
\path{67e4955833ab2704f55ab4e1b13077e7be0bace85641afbacb9f59488111e088};
the compact unsigned-support hash is
\path{b74c7384063fc4c7aa2fe5c882599925a2d0eee8787e5f3f56d612b2d2b23fab}.
This pair is stronger than merely sharing degree counts or deficiency: every invariant of
the complete unsigned incidence representation is identical.

\section{Exact block-query lower bound}

For $p\geq1$, make $p$ disjoint renamed copies, choosing independently either $S$ or $U$
in each block.  Call the resulting formula $F_x$ for $x\in\{0,1\}^p$, with $S$ for bit one.
Then
\[
 F_x\in\st \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x=1^p.
\]

\paragraph{Proposition.}
On $F_{1^p}$, every deterministic exact decision tree over sign fields makes at least $p$
queries.  The same bound holds when a query returns one signed-literal word or one complete
clause record in the fixed block-major encoding.

\paragraph{Proof.}
If no queried sign field belongs to the 398-field difference set of block $i$, the transcript
is identical after replacing that block by $U$, which flips the label.  Exactness therefore
requires a query in each of the $p$ pairwise disjoint difference sets.  A literal word or
clause record belongs to only one block, so the same adversary gives $p$ queries. \hfill$\Box$

With four words per clause plus two header words,
\[
 n_p=198p,\qquad m_p=264p,\qquad W_p=1056p+2.
\]
For word width $w_p=\lceil\log_2(64(462p+1))\rceil$ and bit length
$N_p=W_pw_p$, the lower bound is $p=\Theta(N_p/\log N_p)$.  This conversion is
encoding-specific.  A packing that places signs from many blocks in one word changes the
word-probe statement; no model-free bit-to-word conversion is claimed.

\section{Why transport and amplification stop}

Let the source be the $p$-field implication-chain problem.  Its all-one input requires $p$
exact orientation-field queries, and flipping any unread field changes the label.  A direct
reduction maps source bit one to $S$, source bit zero to $U$, and complements the target
answer.  But materializing the target writes at least $W_p=1056p+2$ words.  In the required
transport ledger,
\[
 L_{\rm old}(p)\leq R(p)+H(p)+Q(p)\tau(p)T_{\rm new}^{\leq}(g(p)),
\]
we have $L_{\rm old}(p)=p$, $R(p)\geq1056p+2$, $Q=\tau=1$, and $H\geq0$.
Thus $L_{\rm old}-R-H<0$ and no positive target lower bound follows.  Direct inclusion of
the block family avoids construction but only preserves the $p$-query bound; it does not
increase its order.

Nor is the promise family intrinsically difficult.  A recognizer scans each block and tests
whether its signs equal the fixed $S$ or $U$ template, deciding the family in
$O(p\cdot264)=O(N_p/w_p)$ word operations.  An arbitrary SAT decider can exploit other
representations or algorithms.  Hence this family is a hostile control for claims that a
large residual, many sensitive fields, or additive direct products force exponential
continuation.

\section{Independent audit of REGULAR-DEFICIENCY63}

For a proper exact signed-(2,2) source, $n=3t$ and $m=4t$.  The maximum Hall deficiency of
its clause--variable incidence graph is $t$; the full clause set is the unique maximizer.
Indeed $|Q|-|V(Q)|\leq |Q|-3|Q|/4=|Q|/4\leq t$, and equality forces
$|Q|=m$.  Deleting one clause lowers the maximum to exactly $t-1$: the surviving full set
attains it, while any proper subset already had deficiency at most $t-1$.

The Horn-selector lift was also rechecked.  In one selector group, let $a\in\{0,1\}$ say
whether the positive ternary clause is selected and let $b$ be the number of selected binary
implications.  The selected-clause-minus-selected-selector contribution is zero if $a=0$
and $b-2$ if $a=1$.  It is positive only for the complete group $a=1,b=3$, where it equals
one.  Therefore an arbitrary target subset is bounded by the source deficiency of its
complete groups.  Equality at $t$ forces all source groups complete; incomplete groups
cannot create a hidden equality case.  This validates the claimed deficiency preservation,
uniqueness, and deletion decrement.  It remains a structural theorem, not a runtime lower
bound.

builds 1,584 matching edges, validates 198 positive/negative occurrence pairs, checks

\path{1b7627073c1330b62b5b0f08734edb2b3fbb478735cab62a09db5bdd0978fbc5}.

Charged resources include source reads, parsing, matching acquisition, sign completion,

circuit count is presented as a SAT-solving lower bound.

\paragraph{Highest-value next lemma (OPEN).}
Define a signed-semantic obstruction on one fixed explicit NP-complete encoding and prove,
for every total uniform deterministic exact decider, an acquisition/compression/continuation
dichotomy whose charged lower bound (i) survives same-support opposite-label pairs,
template recognition, multi-pass branching programs, caching, and alternate representations,
and (ii) leaves a positive remainder after parsing and construction costs.  Without that
all-decider lemma, the exact chain stops at
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{signed obstruction}&\not\Longrightarrow\text{charged dichotomy},\\
\text{charged dichotomy}&\not\Longrightarrow\text{all algorithms},\\
\text{all algorithms}&\not\Longrightarrow\text{superpolynomial contradiction}.
\end{aligned}
\]
P versus NP remains \textbf{OPEN}.
