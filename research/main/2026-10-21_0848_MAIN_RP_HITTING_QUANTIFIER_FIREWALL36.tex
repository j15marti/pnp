\section*{Executive status}
\textbf{ESTABLISHED:} a fixed RP machine has length-wise polynomial advice
consisting of at most $n+1$ random tapes; logarithmic randomness is exactly
enumerable in polynomial time.  \textbf{FALSIFIED:} existence of such advice,
or failure of a universal public seed list, does not decide P versus RP.
\textbf{OPEN:} a uniform deterministic simulation of every RP machine, and an
explicit RP language outside P.  Both P versus RP and P versus NP remain open.

\section*{Mechanisms fixed before primary-source search}
Four falsifiable mechanisms were fixed before literature checking: (i) use a
machine-indexed length-wise hitting set rather than one public list; (ii) audit
the quantifier swap between $\forall x\exists r$ and
$\exists H\forall x$; (iii) exactly enumerate verifiers with $O(\log n)$
coins; (iv) under the conditional assumption P=NP, construct the nonuniformly
known hitting set by search, while refusing to promote that conditional sanity
check to an unconditional derandomization.

\section*{Exact theorem: machine-specific hitting advice}
Let $M$ be a fixed RP machine using $r(n)$ random bits and polynomial time.
For $x\in\{0,1\}^n$, write
\[
 A_x=\{\rho\in\{0,1\}^{r(n)}:M(x;\rho)=1\}.
\]
For NO inputs $A_x=\varnothing$; for YES inputs
$|A_x|\ge 2^{r(n)-1}$.

\textbf{Theorem.} For every $M,n$ there is a list $H_{M,n}$ of at most $n+1$
random tapes that intersects every nonempty $A_x$.  Consequently
$RP\subseteq P/poly$.  If $r(n)=O(\log n)$, exhaustive enumeration instead
gives a uniform deterministic polynomial-time decider.

\textbf{Proof.}  Among all random tapes, the average number of currently
unhit YES rows accepted by a tape is at least half the number of remaining
rows.  Choose a tape attaining the average and delete the rows it hits.
After $n$ choices at most one of the at most $2^n$ rows remains; one additional
tape hits it.  Hardwire the resulting $(n+1)r(n)$ bits and OR the simulations.
Soundness is exact because every NO row is empty.  If $r(n)=O(\log n)$,
enumerate all $2^{r(n)}=n^{O(1)}$ tapes uniformly and OR. \hfill$\Box$

The theorem has quantifier pattern
\[
 \forall M\;\forall n\;\exists H_{M,n}\;\forall x,
\]
not a polynomial-time constructor for $H_{M,n}$.  Hence it gives advice, not
P=RP.

\section*{Public-list diagonalization and its exact limit}
Let a uniform procedure output a polynomial-size public list $H_n\subseteq
\{0,1\}^n$ with $|H_n|\le 2^{n-1}$.  A verifier for the easy language
$\Sigma^*$ can reject precisely the tapes in $H_n$ and accept every other
tape.  Membership in the public polynomial list is polynomial-time, acceptance
probability is at least one half, and every listed tape fails.  Thus no one
machine-independent public list hits all RP verifiers.

This is a simulator diagonalization only.  The language is already in P via
the algorithm that always accepts.  It therefore cannot witness P$\ne$RP and
does not refute machine-indexed $H_{M,n}$.  This is the strongest current
falsifier of a proposed RP conclusion: every separation argument must defeat
all deterministic polynomial-time algorithms, including bypass algorithms,
not just seed replay for one verifier.

\section*{Conditional construction under P=NP}
For fixed $M,n,H$, invalidity has the NP predicate
\[
 \exists x,\rho\quad M(x;\rho)=1\ \wedge\
          \bigwedge_{h\in H}M(x;h)=0.
\]
Thus validity is in coNP.  Under P=NP, coNP=P and polynomial-hierarchy search
can construct a valid polynomial-length $H_{M,n}$ bit by bit.  This is a useful
quantifier check, but it only rederives the trivial sandwich
$P\subseteq RP\subseteq NP=P$; it is not evidence for an unconditional
derandomization.  Likewise SAT$\in RP$ gives NP=RP under deterministic
reductions, not P=RP.

\section*{Finite adversarial audit}
The standard-library checker exhausts every nonempty family of half-dense
acceptance rows over seed universes of sizes two and four, computes exact
minimum transversals, and verifies greedy halving.  It also checks 32,768
deterministic hostile families over eight seeds, public-list complements up to
sixteen seeds, and exhaustive branch-OR tables through four random bits.
These are implementation controls only; the symbolic averaging proof carries
the asymptotic theorem.
