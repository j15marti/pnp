\section{Uniform growing-depth theorem}
At a node of current ordinary virtual length $n$, suppose $\mathcal R$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item emits and processes sequentially at most
$n^{a}(\log(n+2))^C$ typed children;
\item gives each child length at most
$n^\beta(\log(n+2))^C$;
\item spends at most $n^r(\log(n+2))^C$ total local charged work and retains
at most $n^\delta(\log(n+2))^C$ writable/index bits, including discovery,
all source/work reads, all writes including unchanged writes, failed tests,
headers, descriptors, joins, buffers, counters and the active ancestor frame;
\item exposes each requested child bit by one fixed evaluator which performs
at most $(\log(n+2))^C$ local operations and parent-bit requests, with the
same fixed $C$ at every scale;
\item is exact on every source in $\mathcal H$, and emits only children in
$\mathcal H$.
\end{enumerate}
Siblings are not retained simultaneously.  An ancestor frame is retained
until its current descendant returns.  All virtual reads recurse to the
original read-only source and all such work is charged.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform deepening]\label{thm:deep}
Under the preceding hypotheses, one fixed uniform depth-first program can
iterate $\mathcal R$ until current length is at most
$L(N)=\lceil\log_2\log_2(N+4)\rceil$, decide every leaf by exhaustive search,
and combine the exact joins.  Its simultaneous bounds are
\[
 T(N)\le N^{\max\{r,a/(1-\beta)\}+o(1)},\qquad
 S(N)\le N^{\delta+o(1)}.
\]
The stopping depth is $D(N)=\Theta(\log\log N)$, computed by the program;
it is not advice.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Ignoring fixed polylogarithmic factors momentarily, level $i$ contains at
most
\[
 N^{A_i+o(1)},\qquad
 A_i=a\sum_{j<i}\beta^j
 =\frac{a(1-\beta^i)}{1-\beta}
\]
nodes, each of length $N^{\beta^i+o(1)}$.  Its total local work exponent is
\[
 A_i+r\beta^i
 =\frac{a}{1-\beta}
 +\left(r-\frac{a}{1-\beta}\right)\beta^i,
\]
so every level is bounded by
$\max\{r,a/(1-\beta)\}+o(1)$.  There are only
$O(\log\log N)=N^{o(1)}$ levels.  At the last level the number of leaves has
exponent $A_{D(N)}=a/(1-\beta)+o(1)$.  An ordinary encoding of length
$O(\log\log N)$ contains at most that many occurring variable identifiers,
so exhaustive leaf decision takes $2^{O(\log\log N)}=N^{o(1)}$ work per leaf.

For the fixed evaluator, a recursive bit request multiplies at most one
fixed-degree polylogarithmic factor per active scale.  Across
$D(N)=O(\log\log N)$ scales the logarithm of this multiplier is
$O((\log\log N)^2)=o(\log N)$; hence it is $N^{o(1)}$.  The same estimate
absorbs fixed-degree headers, counters, arithmetic and node-count factors.

Depth-first execution retains one frame per active scale.  Their principal
sizes are
\[
 N^{\delta+o(1)},\quad
 N^{\delta\beta+o(1)},\quad
 N^{\delta\beta^2+o(1)},\ldots .
\]
Their sum is $N^{\delta+o(1)}$ (and is $N^{o(1)}$ when $\delta=0$).
Only one descendant workspace and one recursive access stack are live.
Exactness follows by induction from the leaf decisions and the exact typed
joins.  The controller computes $L(N)$, repeats the same fixed code, and
halts because the child-length recurrence is power shrinking.  Thus the
machine is uniform over all input lengths.
\end{proof}

\section{Wrong-quantifier counterexample}
The fixed-degree clause is essential.  Let
$N_D=2^{2^D}$ and let the ideal length at level $i$ be
$n_i=N_D^{2^{-i}}$.  Suppose that, for each fixed level $i$, someone supplies
a separate accessor whose overhead is called ``polylogarithmic'' but whose
degree is $C_i=2^i$.  Every individual $C_i$ is a constant when $i$ is fixed.
Nevertheless the product through depth $D$ has $N_D$-exponent
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^{D-1}
 \frac{2^i\log_2\log_2 n_i}{\log_2N_D}
 =\sum_{j=1}^{D}\frac{j}{2^j}
 =2-\frac{D+2}{2^D}\longrightarrow2.
\]
Thus the composed overhead approaches $N^2$, not $N^{o(1)}$.

By contrast, one common fixed degree $C$ contributes
\[
 \frac{C}{2^D}\sum_{i=0}^{D-1}(D-i)
 =\frac{CD(D+1)}{2^{D+1}}\longrightarrow0.
\]
Therefore ``for every fixed depth there exists a polylog accessor'' does not
imply one uniform growing-depth accessor.  This is an exact quantifier
counterexample, not a SAT lower bound.  Separate finite rules remain valid
for the cofinal finite-route theorem because its depth is fixed after choosing
the starting program; they cannot be reused in Theorem~\ref{thm:deep} without
a uniform construction.
