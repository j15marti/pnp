\section*{Target}
Determine exactly how much charged local time and live workspace the proposed
ARC recurrence can tolerate before it loses the strict FLMV margin.  This
turns the previous finite-entry theorem into a rejection test for actual
stage implementations.  It does not construct the stage.

\section*{Exact quantifiers}
The conditional target has the form
\[
\forall A\,[A\text{ is a total correct uniform deterministic polynomial-time
SAT decider}]
\ \exists \mathcal C_A
\ \forall F
\ \forall j< J:
\]
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item $\mathcal C_A$ is correct on every ordinary encoded SAT input $F$,
  including inputs outside any generated image;
  \item its output decider is recursively eligible for the next stage;
  \item one deterministic random-access realization jointly charges source
  reads, descriptor discovery, selection, construction, query addressing,
  solver calls, cache, replay, combination, output, and all simultaneously
  live state; and
  \item its resource exponents obey the envelope below with fixed charged
  overhead exponents $q,r$.
\end{enumerate}
No such $\mathcal C_A$ is supplied here.  The theorem is an implication from
these quantified hypotheses.

\section*{Model and SAME\_REALIZATION}
Input length is the ordinary SAT encoding length $N$.  Time and writable space
belong to the same deterministic random-access Turing-machine execution.
Read-only input and write-only output are treated only as allowed by FLMV;
output serialization time remains charged.  Let $q$ be the largest unavoidable
local-time exponent incorporated into the stage envelope and $r$ the largest
unavoidable live-workspace exponent.  The conditional recurrence is
\begin{equation}
 t_{j+1}\le
 \max\left\{\frac{11}{10},q,\frac35+\frac{t_j}{2}\right\},
 \qquad
 s_{j+1}\le
 \max\left\{\frac12,r,\frac{s_j}{2}\right\}.
 \tag{1}
\end{equation}
Static gates, transistor counts, proof degree, rank, fold multiplicity, and
source-only splitters are not SAME\_REALIZATION machine resources.

\section*{Primary literature}
CHMY use truth-table length $N=2^n$.  Their abstract states an unconditional
$N^{1.99}$ lower bound for randomized two-sided-error one-tape machines with
an additional one-way read-only input at a threshold $2^{\mu_2 n}$, while the
magnifying $N^{1.01}$ hypothesis is at a smaller $\mu_1<\mu_2$.  No threshold
transport follows automatically \cite{CHMY}.

MMW define search-$\MCSP^A[s]$ to output a size-$s$ AND/OR/NOT/$A$-oracle
circuit or report none.  Their stated magnification premise is the absence of
a one-pass deterministic $\operatorname{poly}(s)$-space streaming algorithm
with $\operatorname{poly}(s)$ update time, for reasonable $s\ge n$ and
$A\in PH$ \cite{MMW}.  The $\Omega(s\log s)$ circuit description length is not
such a workspace lower bound.

OPS require, for some $\varepsilon>0$ and \emph{every sufficiently small}
$\beta>0$, a circuit lower bound for
$\mathsf{GapMCSP}[2^{\beta n}/(cn),2^{\beta n}]$ to conclude
$NP\nsubseteq P/\mathrm{poly}$ \cite{OPS}.  One fixed $\beta$ is insufficient.

FLMV work with deterministic random-access Turing machines and prove a
simultaneous SAT time--space exclusion.  The project uses their Corollary~5.8
sufficient curve
\begin{equation}
 b(c)=\frac{(c+2)/c^2-c}{2},
 \tag{2}
\end{equation}
with strict margins and the local SAT reduction charged \cite{FLMV}.

\section*{Claim / construction}
\begin{theorem}[ARC overhead--space phase frontier]
Define the equality envelope associated with (1) and any finite initial pair
$t_0,s_0\ge0$.  It converges to
\[
 C=\max\left\{\frac65,q\right\},
 \qquad
 R=\max\left\{\frac12,r\right\}.
\]
If
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{R<b(C),}
 \tag{3}
\end{equation}
then a finite number of valid hereditary stages places the same-model SAT
decider strictly inside the FLMV sufficient forbidden region.  Conversely, if
$R\ge b(C)$, this envelope's limiting pair has no strict margin, so endpoint
arithmetic based only on (1)--(2) cannot certify the FLMV contradiction.
The converse is a failure of this certificate, not evidence for a SAT
algorithm or for $P=NP$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The scalar time map
$f(t)=\max\{11/10,q,3/5+t/2\}$ is monotone and $1/2$-Lipschitz away from its
constant branches.  Its unique attracting fixed point is
$C=\max\{6/5,q\}$: the affine branch has fixed point $6/5$, and for
$q\ge6/5$, $3/5+q/2\le q$.  Similarly,
$g(s)=\max\{1/2,r,s/2\}$ converges to $R=\max\{1/2,r\}$.
The FLMV curve is continuous and strictly decreasing for $c>0$, since
\[
 b'(c)=\frac{-1-c^{-2}-4c^{-3}}{2}<0.
\]
When (3) is strict, continuity supplies a positive neighborhood of $(C,R)$
inside the sufficient region, and convergence enters it after finitely many
stages.  If $R\ge b(C)$, the limiting envelope lacks a strict margin; (1)--(2)
alone therefore supply no finite-entry certificate.
\end{proof}

Two exact slices are operationally useful.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item If $r\le1/2$, the time overhead must satisfy
  \[
  q<c_*=1.2055694304005903117\ldots,
  \]
  where $c_*$ is the positive root of
  $c^3+c^2-c-2=0$.  Thus $q=2411/2000=1.2055$ still has positive margin,
  whereas $q=12057/10000=1.2057$ does not.
  \item If $q\le6/5$, the live-space overhead must satisfy
  \[
  r< b(6/5)=\frac{23}{45}.
  \]
  At $r=1/2$ the exact margin is $1/90$; at $r=23/45$ the strict certificate
  disappears.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Falsification first}
The strongest implementation falsifier is now quantitative: exhibit one
unavoidable all-input branch whose fully charged local time has exponent
$q\ge c_*$ while live space is at least $N^{1/2-o(1)}$, or whose live-space
floor is $r\ge b(\max\{6/5,q\})$.  That candidate stage cannot reach FLMV by
the proposed recurrence.  Examples to measure are full-source rescans per
child, parent-scale output, retained child arrays, ancestor stacks, and replay
logs.  A current-scale padded-decider call remains an earlier, stronger
black-box failure mode.

Easy fixed circuits, including the new $4$-variable/$8$-clause cube-matching
inventory, do not falsify the arithmetic; they falsify any attempt to infer an
asymptotic lower bound from finite gate counts.

\section*{Experiments}
\texttt{experiments/review/2026-10-19\_arc\_overhead\_phase\_audit.py}
uses exact \texttt{Fraction} arithmetic.  It checked 2,091 pairs
$q=k/1000$ for $1050\le k\le1300$ in increments of five and
$r=\ell/1000$ for $450\le\ell\le650$ in increments of five.  For every pair,
the sign of the exact endpoint margin agreed with finite entry from $(17,14)$
within 256 stages.  Representative exact controls include both sides of the
time cutoff, the space equality boundary, and the baseline stage-12 entry.
There is no randomness.  Raw results are at
\texttt{results/raw/synthesis/2026-10-19\_1748\_ARC\_OVERHEAD\_SPACE\_PHASE1.json}.
The experiment checks arithmetic, not existence of a SAT contraction.

\section*{Hostile controls}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{DELAY/COPY/RECOMP:} an arbitrary decider may be padded; every
  compulsory call and replay must satisfy the charged envelope.
  \item \textbf{Horn, dual-Horn, 2-SAT, affine/Tseitin, bounded treewidth:}
  direct algorithms may bypass the proposed structure.  The stage must remain
  correct without claiming its ledger is compulsory for these families.
  \item \textbf{Compact representation:} virtual child access counts its
  accessor time and workspace; physical materialization counts output volume.
  \item \textbf{Algebraic/maximal oracle:} no oracle answer may erase source
  acquisition, off-image recognition, or same-machine costs.
\end{itemize}
