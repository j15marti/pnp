\begin{abstract}
The exact obstruction in this run is the use of a complete-state XOR trace as a free internal oracle.  Two elementary but fully quantified theorems close that bookkeeping loophole in declared models.  For two arbitrary aligned $L$-bit snapshots, deterministic exact Hamming distance has worst-case bit-probe complexity exactly $2L$; if the previous snapshot is already retained, the current snapshot still costs exactly $L$ probes.  For a one-pass stream of toggle indices with no prefix reread, an exact tracer needs at least $L-1$ persistent bits at an $L$-event cut.  These are tracing-cost theorems only.  A SAT decider may omit the tracer, reread the source at charged cost, or use a lazy representation, so neither theorem is an arbitrary-machine SAT lower bound.  Same-language controls independently re-certified SAT Y6/V198 and UNSAT U198, rejected five malformed strings, and retained the original signed S/U extreme audit.  The arbitrary-$A\mapsto\Phi(A)$ ARC contraction, tight FLMV transport, and P versus NP all remain \status{OPEN}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exact lemmas}
\subsection{Post-hoc snapshot acquisition}
\paragraph{Lemma 1 (\status{PROVED/EXACT}).}
Let $X,Y\in\{0,1\}^L$ be independent read-only arrays.  Every deterministic adaptive bit-probe algorithm that outputs $d_H(X,Y)$ on every pair has worst-case query complexity exactly $2L$.  If $X$ is already fully known or retained, the exact complexity of reading an arbitrary $Y$ is $L$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Reading all relevant bits is an upper bound.  For the lower bound, answer $0$ to every query.  If any of the $2L$ cells remains unread, flip only that cell.  The complete query transcript is unchanged, but the correct answer changes from $0$ to $1$.  Hence every cell must be queried.  The same adversary on the $L$ cells of $Y$ proves the retained-$X$ statement. \qed

This theorem is sharper than the communication-complexity analogy because it is proved directly in the declared bit-probe model.  It charges only the trace computation, not the underlying SAT computation.

\subsection{One-pass toggle retention}
\paragraph{Lemma 2 (\status{PROVED/EXACT}).}
Consider a deterministic one-pass tracer initially holding the all-zero parity mask for $L$ fixed cells.  Each stream event names one cell and toggles it.  The tracer cannot reread the prefix and must remain exact after every common future suffix.  At a cut after exactly $L$ events, it needs at least $L-1$ persistent bits.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Exactly the $2^{L-1}$ parity masks whose Hamming-weight parity equals $L\bmod 2$ are reachable after $L$ events: list every $1$ coordinate and pad with repeated pairs of coordinate $0$.  Suppose two different masks $p,q$ share a tracer state.  Append the same suffix that toggles every coordinate in $p$.  The continuation from $p$ has final changed-cell count $0$, while that from $q$ has count $|p\oplus q|>0$.  Determinism then contradicts exactness.  Thus the cut has at least $2^{L-1}$ distinguishable states and requires $L-1$ bits. \qed

Rereading the prefix, recomputing parity, or retaining an event log evades the state lower bound, but its source/query/address/time/log space is charged.  This is the same model boundary already exposed by the HEC/RCCN exploration: it is not an unrestricted random-access lower bound.
