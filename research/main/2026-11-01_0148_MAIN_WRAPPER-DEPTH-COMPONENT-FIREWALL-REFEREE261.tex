\section{Claim card, exact obstruction, and falsifiers}
\textbf{Exact obstruction.} Can a shallow source-identity-preserving wrapper turn every connected nonterminal target residual into small independent components without already using a semantic quotient or solving the branch?

\textbf{Quantifiers.} Every well-formed connected target formula, every coherent source-variable partial assignment, and both SAT and UNSAT source labels.  Malformed strings are rejected.  The broader proper strict-2P2N grammar is only a comparative superset.

\textbf{SAME\_REALIZATION and model.} The residual is the ordinary raw cofactor of the same source: delete satisfied clauses and falsified assigned occurrences, retain source variable/clause identities, then form its incidence components.  No guest answer, hidden best-policy oracle, semantic target merger, or parent-scale assignment repair is supplied for free.

The predeclared falsifiers were:
\begin{enumerate}[label=F\arabic*.]
\item some coherent partial assignment erases more than $2\min(k_0,k_1)$ additional free identities;
\item some nonterminal raw residual has more than $1+5b$ incidence components;
\item some residual has all components of size at most $M$ below the inverse pin threshold;
\item the restricted bound is promoted to semantic re-encoders, pruned proof branches, DELAY-wrapped guests, or arbitrary SAT machines.
\end{enumerate}
F1--F3 did not occur and are excluded by the proof.  F4 is a model violation: the theorem expressly leaves these routes open.

\section{Exact theorem and proof}
\begin{theorem}[All-$b$ raw-cofactor boundary]
Let $F$ be a connected valid canonical source on $n$ variables.  Let $p$ coherently pin $k_0$ variables to zero and $k_1$ variables to one, with $b=k_0+k_1$.  Form the raw cofactor $F\restriction p$.  If it is neither an empty SAT terminal nor an empty-clause UNSAT terminal, let $e$ be the number of additional unassigned source variables with no surviving occurrence, $r$ the number of retained unassigned identities, $c$ the number of nonempty residual incidence components, and $L$ their largest variable count.  Then
\[
e\le2\min(k_0,k_1),\quad r=n-b-e\ge n-2b,
\quad c\le1+5b,
\quad L\ge\left\lceil\frac{n-2b}{1+5b}\right\rceil
\]
whenever $n>2b$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An erased free variable loses both positive and both negative occurrences.  A deleted positive clause must contain a pinned one; at most two positive clauses are satisfied per pinned-one occurrence-pair, and after accounting for its pinned literal each such clause contains at most two free-variable occurrences.  Hence deleted positive clauses supply at most $4k_1$ erased-variable incidences.  Since every erased variable needs two such incidences, $2e\le4k_1$, so $e\le2k_1$.  The negative side gives $e\le2k_0$.  Therefore $e\le2\min(k_0,k_1)$ and
\[
r=n-b-e\ge n-b-2\min(k_0,k_1)\ge n-2b.
\]

Start with the connected bipartite incidence graph.  Removing one pinned variable of degree four increases the number of components by at most three, so all $b$ remove at most $3b$.  Every subsequently deleted satisfied clause contains a pinned literal and therefore has residual degree at most two; deleting it increases component count by at most one.  The $b$ pinned variables satisfy at most $2b$ distinct clauses.  Thus $c\le1+3b+2b=1+5b$.  Removing any isolated vanished free variable cannot increase this count.  Pigeonhole over the $r$ retained identities proves the last inequality.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact inverse depth firewall]
If every component of a nonterminal raw cofactor has at most $M$ original identities, then
\[
 b\ge \left\lceil\frac{n-M}{2+5M}\right\rceil
\]
for $n>M$.  In particular, $M=\lfloor n^\lambda\rfloor$ with fixed $0<\lambda<1$ forces $b=\Omega(n^{1-\lambda})$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The premise and theorem give $n-2b\le M(1+5b)$.  Rearrangement and integrality give the displayed threshold.  For fixed $\lambda$, the right-hand side is asymptotic to $n^{1-\lambda}/5$.
\end{proof}

For U198/V198 the exact minimum depths certified only by this inequality are $29,17,9,5,3,2,1,1$ for $M=1,2,4,8,14,24,48,96$.  These are necessary, not sufficient.  They do not count branch leaves: propagation, early certificates, component choice, memoization, and semantic sharing can alter tree size.  Component-backdoor literature explicitly distinguishes depth from backdoor size and charges the decision tree rather than identifying a component bound with an arbitrary-machine lower bound \cite{depth,recursive,treewidth}.

\section{Controller synthesis and the surviving semantic escape}
The verified implications are a four-way firewall for a connected source-faithful controller:
\begin{enumerate}
\item produce a checked SAT witness or sound UNSAT certificate and terminate;
\item pay at least the inverse pin depth before every surviving component is size $\le M$;
\item leave a component larger than $M$;
\item invoke a genuinely semantic/noninjective re-encoder, whose all-input both-label correctness, output access, and resources need a separate proof.
\end{enumerate}

The fourth case is the project bottleneck, not a contradiction.  LSPV-3 GATE294's exact boundary-signature cache is a sound projection/memoization lemma, but U198/V198 have no paired supports and $r=264$, so its $3^r$ continuation is not an all-input power-short compiler.  LSPV-2 PIVOT-RECTANGLE-ACCESS293 gives exact Davis--Putnam projection and a child-bit accessor, but the declared child is noncanonical and longer than the compact canonical source for $n\ge21$.  CONSTRUCTION-STRENGTHEN17 remains a materialized width-$\le4$ sorting-trace candidate without the final target-valid logarithmic-space arbitrary-bit emitter.  None closes case 4.

The affine small-bias theorem is unaffected.  Its corrected target-set count and pointwise source YES/NO quantifiers prove only a source-independent affine MCSP semantic no-go.  Nonlinear/source-dependent preprocessing and arbitrary SAT runtime are outside its hypotheses.  The present theorem is instead a raw source-identity combinatorial obstruction.  Neither can be promoted to universal SAT hardness.
