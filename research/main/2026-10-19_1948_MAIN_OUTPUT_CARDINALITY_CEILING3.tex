\section*{Primary-source check}
McKay--Murray--Williams define search-$\mathrm{MCSP}^A[s(n)]$ on every
$N=2^n$-bit truth table: output an at-most-$s(n)$ AND/OR/NOT/$A$-oracle
circuit, or correctly report that none exists.  Their Theorem 1.3 requires
nonexistence of any one-pass $\operatorname{poly}(s)$-space,
$\operatorname{poly}(s)$-update algorithm.  Cheraghchi--Hirahara--Myrisiotis--
Yoshida instead prove randomized two-sided-error one-tape hardness at a larger
constant MCSP threshold, with one-way read-only input.  Oliveira--Pich--
Santhanam require an $N^{1+\varepsilon}$ circuit lower bound for
$\mathrm{GapMCSP}[2^{\beta n}/(cn),2^{\beta n}]$ for every sufficiently small
$\beta$.  Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas concern deterministic
random-access SAT time and space.  None of these statements turns code
distance, polynomial fibers, or code-family recognition into a full-problem
lower bound.

\section*{The output-cardinality ceiling}
\paragraph{Theorem 1 (\textbf{PROVED/EXACT}).}
Fix $s\ge n$.  In the fan-in-two AND/OR/NOT basis, all circuits on $n$ inputs
with at most $s$ gates have descriptions of length at most
\[
 L(n,s)=s\left(2+2\left\lceil\log_2(n+s)\right\rceil\right)
       +\left\lceil\log_2(n+s)\right\rceil.
\]
Consequently there are at most $2^{L(n,s)}$ distinct size-at-most-$s$
functions.  In an end-output one-pass model with no post-stream source access,
any terminal-state injection argument based solely on correct output identity
(one valid circuit for each YES function and one rejection symbol) can certify
at most
\[
 \log_2(2^{L(n,s)}+1)=O(s\log s)
\]
bits of workspace.  Such an argument cannot establish an
$s^{\omega(1)}$ space lower bound.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Pad a circuit to $s$ gates without changing its designated output.  For each
gate, two bits encode its type and two pointers of
$\lceil\log_2(n+s)\rceil$ bits upper-bound its incoming wires; unary NOT may
ignore one pointer.  One final pointer selects the output.  This proves the
description bound.  After the last stream symbol, equal deterministic states
have identical future output.  Two distinct functions cannot use one correct
output circuit, while every NO input may share the rejection output.  Hence
the output-identity partition has at most $2^L+1$ classes.  Since $n\le s$,
$L=O(s\log s)$.  \hfill$\square$

This theorem limits the \emph{counting proof method}; it does not give an
upper bound on the computational workspace needed for full search-MCSP.
An MMW-grade lower bound must exploit online computation, prefix/suffix
communication, update time, or another property beyond final-output count.

\section*{Mutation closure}
\paragraph{Theorem 2 (\textbf{PROVED/EXACT}).}
If $f$ has a size-$s$ circuit and $g$ is obtained by flipping $h\ge1$ entries
of its truth table, then
\[
 \mathrm{CC}(g)\le s+hn+n+3.
\]
In particular, every one-bit mutant has complexity at most $s+2n+3$.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Share at most $n$ input negations.  The $h$ exceptional assignments have
point minterms requiring $h(n-1)$ AND gates and their disjunction needs
$h-1$ OR gates, for $hn+n-1$ gates total.  XOR this indicator with the output
of the original circuit using four AND/OR/NOT gates.  \hfill$\square$

Thus rejection of one-bit mutants by an RS-code recognizer is not an MCSP-NO
test at threshold $s+2n+3$; it checks code membership.  At the original
threshold $s$, mutant circuit lower bounds would still require proof.

\section*{Dense-distance hostile control}
For $q=2^r$, $1\le k\le q/2$, and $N=2q^2$, the source-backed
RS--Hadamard construction has $q^k=2^{kr}$ words.  Every nonzero word has
density in $[1/8,1/4]$, and distinct words have distance
\[
 \frac{(q-k+1)q}{2}\ge \frac N8.
\]
Yet the reported compiler emits an at-most-$s_0=20kr^2$ gate circuit using
$O(kr+r^2)$ state and polynomial update work.  Since $n=\log_2N=2r+1$ and
$s_0\ge n$, this is polynomial in $s_0$.  Hence exponential family size,
constant density, constant relative distance, and a common zero prefix do not
imply the MMW streaming lower bound.

The independent audit constructed GF($2^r$) codewords for $r=2,3,4$ using
three explicit irreducible polynomials.  It checked 876 codewords, 165746
pair distances and 214656 truth-table evaluations.  A symbolic sweep checked
1048574 pairs $(r,k)$ for $2\le r\le20$.  All distance, density, circuit-size
and output-description inequalities passed with zero failures.  This verifies
the code algebra; the specialist recognizer and $20kr^2$ netlists remain
source-backed because their full code was not published.
