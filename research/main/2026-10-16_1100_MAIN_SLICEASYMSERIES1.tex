\section{LITERATURE CHECK AND REVISIONS}
Jukna's corrected finite-limit criterion says that a small monotone circuit forces either a small family of long sets covering every selected positive input outside a small exception, or a small family of long sets such that every selected negative input avoids one set. The cardinality specialization is model-matched but fails here by the disjoint-block theorem below. Jukna's nontrivial length measures remain a candidate.

Hajiaghayi--Sorkin prove random 3-SAT satisfiable with high probability below density 3.52. At density three, conditioning on distinct clauses changes probability by only $O(1/q)$ because $t=O(q)$ and the clause universe has size $\Theta(q^3)$. This supports the CNF counting bound. Wegener and Hiltgen--Paterson remain hostile controls: strong depth-two bounds need not survive arbitrary set-circuit mass production.

\section{CONSTRUCTION OR PROOF ATTEMPT}
Let
\[
N=8\binom q3,\qquad t=3q,
\]
and let $U_{q,t}$ be zero below weight $t$, UNSAT on weight $t$, and one above weight $t$.

\begin{theorem}[DNF lower bound]
Every monotone DNF computing $U_{q,t}$ has at least
\[
\binom{N-8}{t-8}
\]
terms. Hence
\[
\log L_{\rm DNF}\ge6q\log q+O(q)=\Theta(N^{1/3}\log N).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
No term of size below $t$ is valid because it accepts its own below-layer vector. A term accepting a positive exact-$t$ input is contained in that input, so its size is exactly $t$ and it equals that input. Fix all eight signed clauses on one variable triple; this is UNSAT, and every exact-$t$ extension remains UNSAT.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Valid CNF clauses are large]
Every monotone CNF clause valid on all positive exact-$t$ inputs has at least $N/8$ variables.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If its complement has more than $7N/8$ coordinates, some variable triple contributes all eight signed clauses to the complement. Extending that UNSAT 8-clause bundle to an exact-$t$ input yields a positive input missing the clause.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[CNF lower bound]
\[
L_{\rm CNF}(q)\ge(1-o(1))\frac{\binom Nt}{\binom{7N/8}{t}}.
\]
Consequently
\[
\log L_{\rm CNF}\ge3q\log(8/7)+o(q)
\]
and
\[
L_{\rm CNF}\ge\exp((0.363963965+o(1))N^{1/3}).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
One valid clause rejects at most $\binom{7N/8}{t}$ exact-layer inputs. The density-three exact layer is SAT with probability $1-o(1)$, so a CNF must reject $(1-o(1))\binom Nt$ negative inputs.
\end{proof}

The ratio has the exact convergent series
\[
\log\frac{\binom Nt}{\binom{aN}{t}}
=t\log(1/a)+\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{a^{-m}-1}{mN^m}\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}i^m,
\qquad a=7/8.
\]
Every correction is positive and the infinite tail is $O(t^2/N)=O(1/q)$. This gives a rigorous asymptotic lower bound from an infinite power series, but only for depth-two CNF.

\begin{theorem}[Cardinality finite-limit obstruction]
If $(t+1)r_0\le N$, there exist $t+1$ pairwise disjoint $r_0$-sets such that every exact-$t$ input avoids one of them.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An exact-$t$ input cannot intersect all $t+1$ disjoint blocks.
\end{proof}
Thus Jukna's negative alternative is universally witnessed by $O(q)$ sets whenever $r_0\le N/(t+1)$. To disable it requires $r_0=\Omega(N/t)=\Omega(q^2)$; with cardinality defect one, the criterion's correction factor is then on the same $p^{-r_1}$ scale as the generic local-spread cover bound, where $p=t/N=\Theta(q^{-2})$. The cardinality finite-limit plus local-spread template is therefore \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}; non-cardinality measures remain open.

Let $Z(F)$ be the number of satisfying assignments of a uniform exact-$t$ formula. Inclusion--exclusion gives the exact finite series
\[
\Pr[Z=0]=\sum_{r=0}^{2^q}\frac{(-1)^r}{r!}\mathbb E[(Z)_r].
\]
For ordered distinct assignments $\mathbf a=(a^1,\ldots,a^r)$,
\[
\mathbb E[(Z)_r]=\sum_{\mathbf a\,\mathrm{distinct}}\frac{\binom{S(\mathbf a)}t}{\binom Nt},
\]
where $S(\mathbf a)$ is the number of signed clauses satisfied by all assignments. If the column type of variable $j$ is $\sigma_j\in\{0,1\}^r$, then
\[
S(\mathbf a)=\sum_{\{u,v,w\}}(8-d(\sigma_u,\sigma_v,\sigma_w)),
\]
with $d$ the number of distinct 3-bit rows.

For a distribution $x$ on $\{0,1\}^r$, define
\[
s_r(x)=\frac18\mathbb E[8-d(\sigma,\tau,\upsilon)].
\]
For fixed $r$, the method of types yields
\[
\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac1q\log\mathbb E[(Z)_r]
=\Lambda_r:=\sup_x\{H(x)+3\log s_r(x)\}.
\]
The uniform type gives
\[
\mathbb E[(Z)_r]\ge(686/512)^{rq-o(q)}.
\]
Hence every fixed-order factorial-moment term is exponentially large. UNSAT is encoded in cancellations extending to order growing with $q$, not in a bounded number of local moments.

For $r=1$, $\Lambda_1=\log(686/512)\approx0.292553003$. For $r=2$,
\[
\Lambda_2=\max_{0\le\delta\le1}\left[\log2+h(\delta)+3\log\frac{6+(1-\delta)^3}{8}\right],
\]
with
\[
\delta_*\approx0.354927487,\qquad \Lambda_2\approx0.611842418,
\]
so $\mathbb E[(Z)_2]\approx(1.843825368)^q$ on the exponential scale.
