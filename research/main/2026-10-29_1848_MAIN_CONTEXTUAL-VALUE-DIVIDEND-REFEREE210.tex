\begin{abstract}
We formalize the requested notation $x\mid_v$ without treating value as intrinsic.
For a fixed state, target, cost model, known certified facts and independently
defined potential, the exact gains of a finite menu of facts form a set
function.  M\"obius inversion gives a unique interaction-dividend ledger:
positive higher-order terms expose complementarity, negative terms expose
redundancy, and actual contextual marginals telescope along every certified
execution.  This is a useful exact model but not free compression: an arbitrary
normalized Boolean gain table on $m$ facts has $2^m-1$ independent bits.
We separately prove the exact quantifier condition behind reusable-summary
counting.  A detached summary supporting all later contexts must distinguish
answer vectors, but retained source access or a joint solver seeing both sides
invalidates that injection.  Hence neither value scores nor boundary-function
counting yields a general SAT lower bound without an explicit access contract.
P versus NP remains \textbf{OPEN}.
\end{abstract}

\section{PROPOSE: falsifiable missing lemma and mechanisms}
The missing lemma was whether a datum-level contextual value could drive an
amortized construction--storage--continuation proof without hiding the optimal
SAT computation inside the score.  Four falsifiers were fixed before search:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item duplicate certified facts, which must not be counted twice;
  \item complementary XOR shares, whose singleton gains vanish although their
  joint gain is positive;
  \item a high-value direct answer or witness whose acquisition can dominate
  its verification and downstream usefulness;
  \item the legal controls that retain the original source $A$, or let one
  solver see both $A$ and $B$, rather than requiring a detached reusable summary.
\end{enumerate}
The candidate repair was a unique interaction ledger plus a quantifier-explicit
summary theorem.  Both were falsifiable by finite enumeration and explicit
source/context controls.

\section{BUILD I: contextual value as an exact interaction ledger}
Fix a declared computational model ${\cal C}$, complete current state $s$,
already certified knowledge $K$, target task $T$, score scale, and an
independently defined potential $\Phi$.  For a certified information object
$I$, write
\[
 I\mid_{v_\Phi(I\mid s,K,T,{\cal C})},\qquad
 v_\Phi(I\mid s,K)=\Phi(s,K)-\Phi(s,K\cup\{I\}).
\]
For a factual bit use $(x=1)\mid_v$.  The vertical bar is neither probability
conditioning nor divisibility.  Acquisition, source authentication,
verification, application and score computation are a separate cost
\[
 C_s(I)=C_{\rm acquire}+C_{\rm authenticate}+C_{\rm verify}
       +C_{\rm apply}+C_{\rm score}.
\]
Thus $v/\max(1,C_s(I))$ is only a heuristic until a policy theorem is proved.

Let $E=\{I_1,\dots,I_m\}$ be a finite menu and define the normalized gain
set function
\[
 g(S)=\Phi(s,K)-\Phi(s,K\cup S),\qquad g(\varnothing)=0.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Unique interaction-dividend decomposition]\label{thm:mobius}
There is a unique function $d:2^E\to\mathbb R$ such that
\[
 g(S)=\sum_{U\subseteq S}d(U).
\]
It is given by
\[
 d(U)=\sum_{V\subseteq U}(-1)^{|U|-|V|}g(V).
\]
Moreover, for $I_i\notin S$ the actual contextual marginal is
\[
 v_\Phi(I_i\mid s,K\cup S)
 =g(S\cup\{I_i\})-g(S)
 =\sum_{\substack{U\subseteq S\cup\{I_i\}\\I_i\in U}}d(U).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first two displays are M\"obius inversion on the Boolean-subset lattice.
Subtract the expansions of $g(S\cup\{I_i\})$ and $g(S)$; precisely the terms
containing $I_i$ remain.  Uniqueness follows from the triangular system ordered
by inclusion.
\end{proof}

The dividends provide a precise vocabulary.  A positive $d(\{I,J\})$ is
pair synergy; a negative one is pair redundancy.  Higher-order terms record
effects invisible to every smaller subset.  Singleton labels alone are
additive exactly when all dividends of order at least two vanish.

\begin{corollary}[Path telescoping, not static additivity]
For a certified sequence $I_{\pi(1)},\dots,I_{\pi(r)}$ and
$K_j=K\cup\{I_{\pi(1)},\dots,I_{\pi(j)}\}$,
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^r v_\Phi(I_{\pi(j)}\mid s,K_{j-1})
 =\Phi(s,K)-\Phi(s,K_r).
\]
The terms can vary with the order even though their sum has fixed endpoints.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}The sum telescopes.\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact hostile controls.}
For target $T=a\oplus b$ under independent uniform shares, the gain table in
the order $\varnothing,a,b,ab$ is $(0,0,0,1)$ and the dividends are
$(0,0,0,1)$.  Each singleton has value zero at the empty context and value one
after the other share.  For two duplicate facts revealing the same bit, the
gain table is $(0,1,1,1)$ and dividends are $(0,1,1,-1)$: the negative pair
term removes double counting.  Independent additive facts have
$(0,1,1,2)$ and $(0,1,1,0)$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact arbitrary-ledger size and a useful restriction]
There are $2^{2^m-1}$ normalized Boolean gain functions on $m$ named facts.
Consequently any fixed exact encoding of every such function needs at least
$2^m-1$ bits in the worst case.  The M\"obius table has exactly that many
nonempty positions.  If, however, the potential is certified to be
$k$-additive, meaning $d(U)=0$ for $|U|>k$, then only
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{k}\binom mj=O(m^k)
\]
coefficients remain for fixed $k$; their bit lengths, discovery and
verification must still be charged.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After fixing $g(\varnothing)=0$, each of the remaining $2^m-1$ table entries is
an independent Boolean choice.  Counting gives the lower bound.  The second
claim counts the subsets of size at most $k$.
\end{proof}

This theorem is about arbitrary value ledgers, not SAT instances.  Compactly
described SAT families may induce structured $g$; conversely, calculating the
coefficients may be harder than using the facts directly.  ``Low certified
interaction order'' is therefore a legitimate new restricted parameter, not
an assumed universal property.

\section{BUILD II: construction--storage--continuation quantifiers}
For $F=A(X,Y)\wedge B(X,Z)$ define
$f_A(x)=\exists y\,A(x,y)$.  A same-realization resource record is
\[
 \mathfrak R=(P,L,Q,W,R,H,D;\mathsf{access}),
\]
where $P$ is offline construction work before $B$, $L$ retained summary bits,
$Q$ online continuation work including source probes, $W$ peak workspace,
$R$ communication rounds, $H$ physical head travel/crossings, $D$ cumulative
memory-symbol changes, and \textsf{access} says whether $A$ is detached,
retained read-only, writable, sequential or indexed.  The source footprint and
output/certificate length are recorded separately.  These coordinates form a
Pareto ledger; they are not silently collapsed to one score.

\begin{theorem}[Answer-vector injection for detached reusable summaries]
Let ${\cal A}$ be a source family, ${\cal B}$ a future-context family, and
$D(A,B)\in\{0,1\}$ the required decision.  Suppose deterministic preprocessing
maps $A$ to a detached fixed-length $L$-bit summary $\sigma(A)$ before $B$ is
known, and a decoder seeing only $(\sigma(A),B)$ is exact for every pair.
Let
\[
 r_A=(D(A,B))_{B\in{\cal B}},\qquad
 R=|\{r_A:A\in{\cal A}\}|.
\]
Then $R\le2^L$, hence $L\ge\lceil\log_2R\rceil$.  If summaries have variable
length at most $L$, then $R\le2^{L+1}-1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Two sources with distinct answer vectors differ on some future context.  If
their summaries collided, the deterministic decoder would give the same answer
on that context, contradicting exactness.  Count fixed or bounded-length binary
strings.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Boundary-function specialization and counting ceiling]
If ${\cal B}$ contains every pin $B_x$ and
$D(A,B_x)=f_A(x)$, then $R$ is the number $M$ of distinct boundary functions,
so $L\ge\log_2M$.  But sources with encodings of length at most $N$ have at
most $2^{N+1}-1$ descriptions, and sending/storing the original source is a
legal $O(N)$-bit control.  Therefore this counting route alone cannot force an
exponential detached summary length relative to source length $N$.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Quantifier firewall.}
The theorem is false if the decoder retains query access to $A$.  Let
$A_a$ be a source bit vector $a\in\{0,1\}^m$ and context $B_i$ ask for $a_i$.
With indexed read-only access, the auxiliary summary is empty ($L=0$) and the
continuation uses one source probe.  A detached source instead requires $m$
bits because all $2^m$ answer vectors occur.  A joint solver that receives both
$A$ and $B$ is again the indexed control, not an offline-summary scheme.
On a sequential head the same control pays physical travel, exactly the kind
of model-specific coordinate isolated by LSPV-4; it is not a RAM lower bound.

For the affine easy control
$A_{\alpha,\beta}(X):\langle\alpha,X\rangle=\beta$ and all pins, there are
$2^{b+1}$ distinct answer vectors.  Detached summaries need $b+1$ bits, while
retaining the affine row attains the bound and Gaussian/Tseitin processing
remains polynomial.  This preserves rather than excludes the affine shortcut.

\begin{theorem}[Reusable amortized ledger]
For $r$ future contexts executed from one authenticated prepared state,
\[
 T_{\rm total}=P+\sum_{j=1}^r Q_j+C_{\rm reset/replay},\qquad
 W_{\rm peak}=\max\{W_P,L+\max_j W_{Q_j},W_{\rm replay}\},
\]
with communication, crossings and memory changes added along the same physical
execution.  Failed strategies and score discovery are included in the relevant
$P$ or $Q_j$.  Independent minima of these coordinates need not be jointly
attainable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}Partition the actual execution into preparation, query and replay/reset intervals and count the declared resources.\end{proof}

\section{Red-team consequences for proposed value policies}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \FALSIFIED: intrinsic or context-free additive datum values.  Duplicate
and XOR controls force respectively negative and positive interaction terms.
\item \FALSIFIED: a policy that selects only actions having strictly positive
current singleton $v/C$ has any uniform approximation guarantee.  Give it two
unit-cost XOR shares and a direct answer of cost $K\ge3$.  The shares each have
initial value zero; the only positive action costs $K$, while the two-share
policy costs two.  The ratio $K/2$ is unbounded in the action-menu parameter.
This is a policy falsifier, not a SAT hardness theorem.
\item \PROVED\ (restricted): if $\Phi$ is monotone under certified information
and the algorithm may ignore information, raw $v_\Phi\ge0$.  Negative raw
values diagnose a nonmonotone potential, a changing target/state, or costs
folded into the score.  Net value may of course be negative after charged cost.
\item \FALSIFIED: treating a witness's verification cost as its acquisition
cost.  A supplied complete assignment may be checked cheaply, but finding it
is a separate charged search.  No unknown witness or answer bit is granted.
\item \OPEN: proving a low interaction order, a polynomial score-discovery
algorithm and bounded upward/nonprogress drift for arbitrary connected
strict22 residuals.  Defining $\Phi$ as optimal remaining runtime merely moves
the unknown algorithm into score evaluation unless an independent evaluator is
proved.
\end{enumerate}
