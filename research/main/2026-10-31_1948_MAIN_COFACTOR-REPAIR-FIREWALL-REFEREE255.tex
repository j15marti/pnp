\section{Exact minimum-repair theorem}
Let $F$ have $n$ variables and $m=4n/3$ clauses.  Fix $x=b$.  Exactly the two
clauses in which the pivot literal is true disappear and the two clauses in
which it is false become binary.  The residual therefore has $n-1$ active
variables, $m-2$ clauses and $4n-8$ literal records.

\begin{theorem}[Minimum occurrence-preserving canonical repair]
Suppose a completion of $R(F,x=b)$ retains every surviving clause and every
surviving old literal occurrence, pads the two binary clauses rather than
deleting them, and obtains a valid duplicate-free proper monotone
$3$-SAT-$(2,2)$ formula by adding $z\ge0$ active variables and $c\ge0$
clauses.  Then
\[
3c-4z=2,
\qquad
(z,c)=(1+3q,2+4q)\quad(q\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}).
\]
In particular the minimum completion has $z=1,c=2$, hence exactly $n$
variables and $m$ clauses: it is not a power-short child in any ordinary
fixed-record encoding.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
After completion there are $n-1+z$ variables and $m-2+c$ ternary clauses.
Exact two-positive/two-negative balance counts four literal occurrences per
variable, while ternarity counts three per clause.  Thus
\[
3(m-2+c)=4(n-1+z).
\]
Using $3m=4n$ gives $3c-4z=2$.  Reduction modulo $3$ gives $z\equiv1
\pmod3$, so $z=1+3q$; substitution gives $c=2+4q$.  The least nonnegative
solution is $(1,2)$, which restores $(n,m)$.  Equal record counts imply
parent-scale length; exact byte equality depends only on the declared
fixed-record naming convention and is not needed for the no-power-short
conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Form and semantic fork of every minimum repair]
In a minimum completion, let $y$ denote the reintroduced variable.  The two
binary clauses must be padded by the two occurrences of the sign of $y$
that keeps each clause monotone.  The two added clauses have the opposite
sign of $y$ and consume exactly the four missing old co-occurrences from the
two deleted clauses.  Consequently minimum completions are precisely the
three pairings of those four occurrences into two pairs, subject to
properness and uniqueness.  The original pairing reconstructs $F$ with
$x$ renamed to $y$; the other pairings are signed degree-preserving
two-switches.

For every such completion $G$,
\[
G[y=b]=R(F,x=b).
\]
But $G[y=1-b]$ is the complementary branch of the switched parent.  Thus
$G$ is equisatisfiable with the selected residual only if that additional
branch is proved UNSAT or $y=b$ is otherwise soundly forced.  Grammar repair
alone supplies neither condition.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The raw child is short by eight literal occurrences relative to a balanced
$n$-variable target: four pivot occurrences and the four co-occurrences in
the two deleted clauses.  Minimum repair adds two padding occurrences and
six occurrences in two new clauses.  Every old active variable must regain
exactly its missing signed multiplicity, so the four missing co-occurrences
must occupy four of those eight slots.  The new variable must occupy the
remaining four slots, twice in each polarity.  Monotonicity forces its sign
in each padded and added clause.  Only a pairing of the four co-occurrences
remains free.  On $y=b$, the added clauses containing the true $y$ literal
disappear and the padded false-$y$ clauses reduce to the two raw binaries,
giving the selected residual.  On $y=1-b$ the roles reverse, exposing the
other pairing-dependent branch.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Branch-binding tariff]
A controller using a minimum repaired target instance as a single recursive
query must additionally (i) force $y=b$ inside the target using a sound
charged gadget, (ii) certify the alternative branch UNSAT, or (iii) query
and combine both branches.  Treating target grammar as branch binding is
unsound.  The first option adds records, the second can contain the original
decision difficulty, and the third restores ordinary Shannon branching.
\end{corollary}

\section{Certified same-residual opposite-label controls}
The producer and a verifier importing none of the producer independently
replayed D\"ocker's six-module boundary certificate for $U_{198}$ and found
the joint boundary row set empty.  Therefore every cofactor of $U_{198}$ is
UNSAT.  For $x=2,b=1$ and $x=197,b=1$, both alternative pairings produce
valid 198-variable, 264-clause target formulas.  All four are satisfied by
the same checked 198-bit witness.  Their true-literal histograms are
\[
(N_0,N_1,N_2,N_3)=(0,159,78,27).
\]
Each repaired formula and $U_{198}$ are two different minimum completions of
the same raw UNSAT residual: the original pairing is UNSAT, while the
alternative is SAT.  This establishes semantic failure without timeout,
endpoint inference, or occurrence-local pseudoassignments.

The exhaustive local audit covered all three pairings for both polarities of
all variables in Y6, $U_{198}$ and $V_{198}$: 2,412 completion rows and
21,708 producer assertion units.  The independent verifier performed 71,934

cap returned \texttt{UNKNOWN} at 301 nodes on $U_{198}$, $V_{198}$, and one
SAT repair.  Those timeouts supplied no truth label.

The raw artifact contains every signed occurrence's local $S/U$ targets and
