\section{Question, missing lemma, and falsifiers}
The integrated project needs an ordinary-SAT controller whose \emph{realized}
executions, not its counterfactual policy menu, satisfy a same-machine ARC
recurrence.  Firewalls 227--229 showed respectively that repeated child rounds
do not multiply a persistent source-query union, a detached all-continuation
summary can be nearly linear, and retained-source point access can escape that
summary floor.  The missing lemma is therefore:

\begin{quote}
When does a small realized union of original-source addresses actually imply
the local time and peak-space terms needed by the ARC recurrence?
\end{quote}

The falsifiable proposals were: (F1) distinct source addresses alone upper-bound
local time; (F2) the same union upper-bounds repeated reads; (F3) it upper-bounds
physical child output and retained ancestors; (F4) zero compiler-layer source
probes excludes a parent-scale fallback.  All four are false.  The repair is a
trace-exact sum/max ledger, not another source-query lower bound.

\section{Explicit machine and realized quantities}
Use the deterministic indexed read-only-input random-access multitape Turing
machine of Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas (FLMV).  Every input-index,
work-tape, output, policy, cache and guest-simulation transition is charged.
Fix one deterministic total exact ordinary-SAT decider $A$.  A fixed compiler
$C^A$ on a source $F$ of bit length $N$ executes $q(F)$ serial ordinary-SAT
guest calls on children $G_i(F)$ of exact physical length $m_i(F)$.  Define:
\begin{itemize}
\item $D(F)$: every realized compiler transition outside simulated guest
steps, including discovery, failed policies, address production, every source
read including repeats, child construction or accessor evaluation, output,
verification, joins, replay and reconstruction;
\item $\rho_i(F)$: a proved upper bound on native simulation transitions per
guest transition for child $i$ (equal to a constant for a directly presented
physical child; an accessor tariff for virtual input);
\item $H(F)$: peak counted non-guest live bits, including caches, exact head
shadows, retained ancestors and any writable child buffer;
\item $V_i(F)$: peak counted simulator/accessor scratch concurrently live with
guest $i$.
\end{itemize}
The original input is read-only and not itself counted as work space.  A newly
materialized child is not a second free read-only input; its buffer is included
in $H$ unless a native total accessor proves another implementation.

\section{Realized-trace composition theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Trace-exact sufficient ARC ledger]\label{thm:ledger}
Suppose that on every $N$-bit source, both SAT and UNSAT, one fixed uniform
controller has serial trace bounds
\[
 D(F)\le N^{\alpha+o(1)},\quad q(F)\le N^{\gamma+o(1)},\quad
 m_i(F)\le N^{\lambda+o(1)},\quad \rho_i(F)\le N^{\nu+o(1)},
\]
and
\[
 H(F)+\max_i V_i(F)\le N^{\sigma+o(1)},\qquad 0<\lambda<1.
\]
If $A$ runs simultaneously in time $m^{K+o(1)}$ and counted space
$m^{L+o(1)}$ on that same model, then the declared controller runs in
\[
 K'\le\max\{\alpha,\gamma+\nu+\lambda K\},\qquad
 L'\le\max\{\sigma,\lambda L\}.
\]
The statement is an upper-bound composition lemma for a supplied controller;
it neither constructs the controller nor discovers $K,L$ from arbitrary code.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Partition the actual native trace into compiler transitions and the disjoint
serial simulations.  The former contribute $D(F)$.  Call $i$ contributes at
most
$N^{\nu+o(1)}(N^{\lambda+o(1)})^{K+o(1)}$ transitions.  Summing at most
$N^{\gamma+o(1)}$ calls and adding $D$ gives the time exponent.  At one time
only one guest is live.  Its work uses at most
$(N^{\lambda+o(1)})^{L+o(1)}$ bits, concurrently with the already charged
$H+V_i$; addition changes only subexponential factors, giving the maximum
space exponent.  Concurrent guests would require their simultaneous sum and
are not covered.  Every failed strategy and every emitted/accessed bit belongs
to $D$ or $H$, so no successful-path-only term is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Output and source tariffs are obligations, not consequences]
Let $U(F)$ be the number of distinct original-source addresses read and $R(F)$
the total number of source-read transitions.  Then $U(F)\le R(F)\le D(F)$.
For a bit-output machine, physically writing children costs at least
$W(F)=\sum_i m_i(F)$ transitions, hence $W(F)\le D(F)$.  Neither
$U(F)\le N^{u+o(1)}$ nor the separate caps on $q$ and $m_i$ prove the required
upper bound on $D$.  In particular, an invocation of Theorem~\ref{thm:ledger}
must prove the actual total $D$, not substitute a count of distinct addresses.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every first visit is a read, every read is a native transition, and every
physical output bit requires a write transition.  These are inclusions into the
defined trace partition.  They are lower bounds on $D$, not upper bounds.
\end{proof}

\section{Counterexample to ``small union implies contraction''}
\begin{theorem}[One-address non-implication]\label{thm:counter}
For every deterministic exact controller $P$ that answers a paired SAT/UNSAT
source family after one source read, and every fixed integer $d>0$, there is a
deterministic exact controller $P_d$ with the same answer and the same
distinct-address union $U=1$ whose running time is larger by $N^d$ transitions.
There are likewise exact implementations with $U=1$ but arbitrarily many
repeated reads, arbitrarily large physical output, or arbitrarily large retained
live state.  A controller-layer union $U=0$ is compatible with passing the
parent-scale input to $A$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$P_d$ performs $N^d$ answer-independent transitions and then runs $P$.  It is
correct on the same YES and NO sources and reads the same address.  Repeating
that one read, writing a prescribed dummy/child record before returning the
same answer, or retaining a prescribed record gives the other executions.
Finally a wrapper may make no compiler-layer source read and invoke $A$ on the
unchanged parent.  These constructions establish only non-implication: another
algorithm can omit every artificial cost.
\end{proof}

Thus the inference
\[
U(F)\le N^u\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
D(F),H(F)\le N^{O(u)}
\]
is \RET.  A source-probe lower bound can still refute a proposed low-access
implementation.  A small source-probe count is not a time upper bound.

\section{Primary literature and transfer audit}
\begin{description}
\item[Directly transferable.] FLMV fixes the indexed read-only-input random-
access Turing-machine model and supplies the actual SAT forbidden region.  Its
machine accounting, not a word-RAM or Python counter, controls the final ARC
claim.
\item[Useful structural analogy.] Hong--Kung's red--blue pebble game separates
I/O traffic from available fast memory.  It motivates charging total movement
and simultaneous live state, but its DAG/I/O lower bounds do not transfer to
arbitrary SAT algorithms without a reduction.
\item[Failed transfer.] P\u{a}tra\c{s}cu--Demaine cell-probe bounds deliberately
count memory probes while allowing free computation between probes.  Therefore
they can provide strong \emph{lower} bounds on data access, but a small probe
count cannot be imported as an upper bound on native FLMV time.  This model
difference is exactly the present firewall.
\end{description}

certify the ARC time/space recurrence.  Its exact scope is a non-implication
for arbitrary controller implementations, not an unavoidable SAT lower bound.
