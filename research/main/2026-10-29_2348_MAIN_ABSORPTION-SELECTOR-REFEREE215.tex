\begin{abstract}
We execute the issue-3 clause-indexed experiment on eleven SAT and UNSAT controls.  For every original clause and signed occurrence the raw audit constructs the local all-literals-true target $S_j$, its inversion $U_j$, the local all-literals-false target $U_j$, and its inversion $S_j$; it separately evaluates coherent global assignments $0^n,1^n$, complements, and flip trails.  It exhaustively verifies the four-way $SS,SU,US,UU$ pair matrix, bounded multi-clause modes, complement identities, and every prohibited resolvent cube.  The new exact result is a local absorption theorem: for two proper 3-clause parents having exactly one opposite-polarity shared pivot and $s$ same-polarity shared tail variables, their direct resolvent is absorbed by the two-parent formula iff $s=0$.  Thus $s\geq1$ is an exact parent-level selector, but hostile affine controls falsify its use as a full-formula selector.  Charged fixed-order UP--DPLL comparisons show benefit on two UNSAT instances and overhead on most SAT/easy controls.  This is a restricted logical and computational result, not a general SAT algorithm.  P versus NP remains \OPEN.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, mechanisms, and machine ledger}
The missing lemma was whether clause-extreme overlap gives a sound, cheap selector for first-generation source resolvents rather than merely another compatibility diagnostic.  Four falsifiable mechanisms were tested:
\begin{enumerate}
\item retain every non-tautological direct source resolvent;
\item retain exactly candidates with a same-polarity shared tail variable;
\item retain exactly candidates not absorbed by the full source formula under unit propagation;
\item reject all candidates and run the same fixed-order UP--DPLL baseline.
\end{enumerate}
The experiment uses a deterministic word-RAM-style Python accounting model.  ``Work'' is the declared sum of pair/literal tests, candidate construction reads, UP clause scans, UP literal reads, branch nodes, conflicts, and propagation events.  Derived-clause literal storage, peak assigned cells, and measured Python peak bytes are separately recorded.  The full-formula selector performs and charges one UP probe for every target literal of every candidate.  The comparison is simultaneous per execution; it does not combine an independently smallest time with an independently smallest space.  It is not a Turing-machine lower bound and not industrial CDCL.

\section{Clause-indexed extremes and exact compatibility}
Let a proper source clause be $C_j=(\ell_{j1}\vee\ell_{j2}\vee\ell_{j3})$.  A signed occurrence $x_{i,j}$ records the source variable and its sign; it is not an independent Boolean variable.  The local target $S_j$ assigns every occurrence so all three literals are true, while $U_j$ assigns every occurrence so all three are false.  Bitwise inversion swaps these targets.  The raw record lists, for every occurrence, its sign, preferred variable value in $S_j$, preferred value in $U_j$, and both inversions.

For two distinct clauses define $s$ as the number of shared variables with equal polarity and $o$ as the number with opposite polarity.  Direct enumeration and the elementary variable-consistency argument agree:
\[
 SS,UU\text{ are feasible}\iff o=0,
 \qquad
 SU,US\text{ are feasible}\iff s=0.
\]
If $s,o>0$, none of the four joint extremes is feasible; hence every coherent full assignment places at least one parent in a middle state with one or two true literals.  This is a local invariant only.  It neither falsifies either disjunction nor certifies SAT or UNSAT.

For every coherent full assignment $a$, the audit also checks
\[
 t_j(\bar a)=3-t_j(a),\qquad N_r(\bar a)=N_{3-r}(a),
\]
and the exact flip update
\[
 t_j(a\oplus H)=t_j(a)+|H\cap F_j(a)|-|H\cap T_j(a)|.
\]
The endpoints $0^n$ and $1^n$, complement pairs, and intermediate flips remain coherent source-variable assignments.  Local $S/U$ targets are never merged across incompatible occurrences.

\section{The parent-pair absorption theorem}
For a CNF $G$, a clause $R$ is \emph{absorbed} when, for every literal $\ell\in R$, unit propagation on $G$ after falsifying $R\setminus\{\ell\}$ either derives $\ell$ or derives a conflict.  This is the standard CDCL absorption notion used by Beame, Kautz, and Sabharwal in their analysis of non-restarting SAT solvers.

\begin{theorem}[exact parent absorption]
Let $C=(p\vee A)$ and $D=(\neg p\vee B)$ be proper 3-clauses.  Assume that $p$ is their unique opposite-polarity shared variable, so the direct resolvent $R=A\vee B$ is non-tautological.  Let $s$ be the number of variables occurring with the same polarity in both tails $A,B$.  Then $R$ is absorbed by $C\wedge D$ if and only if $s=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose first that $s=0$.  Fix a target literal $\ell\in R$ and falsify every other literal of $R$.  If $\ell\in A$, then all literals of $B$ are false, so $D$ becomes the unit $\neg p$.  Setting $p=0$ makes $C$ unit on $\ell$.  The case $\ell\in B$ is symmetric.  Thus every target literal is forced, and $R$ is absorbed.

Conversely suppose $s\geq1$ and choose a literal $\ell$ appearing with the same sign in both tails.  Falsify every literal of $R\setminus\{\ell\}$.  The two parents reduce to
\[
(p\vee\ell)\wedge(\neg p\vee\ell).
\]
Neither clause is unit and there is no conflict.  Unit propagation therefore does not derive $\ell$, so $R$ is not absorbed by the parent pair.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[three distinct selectors]
Among first-generation direct source resolvents, $o=1$ is exactly the non-tautological-direct-resolvent condition; $o=1,s\geq1$ exactly selects non-absorption by the two parents; and non-absorption by the entire formula requires testing or another independently proved sufficient condition.  Extra clauses can absorb a parent-nonabsorbed resolvent.
\end{corollary}

The theorem is independently recreated here rather than inferred from finite tests.  Exhaustive enumeration nevertheless checked all 1,776 candidates generated by the controls and found no counterexample.

\section{Prohibited cubes and proof provenance}
For opposite occurrences $(p\vee A)$ and $(\neg p\vee B)$ with a non-tautological resolvent $R=A\vee B$, falsifying every literal of $R$ yields both units $p$ and $\neg p$.  Therefore that partial-assignment cube is impossible.  The experiment stores both source clause indices, pivot variable, parent signs, resolvent literals, and the two-step UP/RUP certificate.  Exact enumeration verifies that no model lies in any recorded cube.  Tautological resolvents are rejected.  Duplicate cubes are not counted as disjoint coverage, and no union-of-cubes size is used as a progress measure.

This rule is ordinary resolution, not a novelty claim.  Kullmann's conflict-graph and hitting-clause-set work supplies the relevant structural context; the present result only refines which direct parent resolvents are locally propagation-novel.  Affine/XOR instances are retained as adverse controls because Gaussian elimination can bypass large CNF resolution sets.

\section{Status ledger and falsifications}
\begin{description}
\item[\PROVED.] The four-way pair compatibility characterization; the middle-state invariant; complement and flip identities; sound non-tautological resolvent cubes; and the parent-pair absorption theorem.
\item[\COMP.] The finite exact audit and charged fixed-order UP--DPLL comparison on eleven controls.  These observations do not establish asymptotic superiority.
\item[\FALSE.] ``Same-polarity overlap exactly predicts full-formula non-absorption.''  Dense XOR $K_6$ has 60 locally selected candidates and zero full-formula nonabsorbed candidates.
\item[\FALSE.] ``Adding every sound direct resolvent cannot increase total work.''  W1 nodes fall but charged work rises from $15371$ to $18539$; on S15 both nodes are 9 while work rises from $729$ to $4274$.
\item[\FALSE.] ``Exact full-formula absorption selection is free.''  Its candidate-by-candidate UP probes dominate several controls.
\item[\OPEN.] A cheap sufficient full-formula non-absorption criterion, or an amortized uniform controller that improves charged total work on matched infinite SAT and UNSAT families.  P versus NP remains open.
\end{description}
