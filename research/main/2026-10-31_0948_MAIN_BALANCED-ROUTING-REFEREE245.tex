\section{Claim card and four falsifiers}
The target is the newest GATE278--279 articulation policy.  For every fixed
uniform total correct ordinary-SAT decider $A$ with unknown exponents $(K,L)$,
the desired same-machine compiler would satisfy
\[
 K'\le\max\{11/10,3/5+K/2\},\qquad
 L'\le\max\{1/2,L/2\}.                              \tag{1}
\]
SAME\_REALIZATION includes the read-only source and input-index head,
separator discovery, rejected candidates, component routing, physical or
virtual children, every guest and guest-index tape, join certificates, output,
retained ancestors and peak simultaneous workspace.

Four falsifiers were tested.  F1: $N^{1/2}$ children occurring
$N^{1/2}$ times necessarily violate the guest term in (1)---
\textbf{FALSIFIED}; their total volume yields exponent $1/2+K/2$.
F2: the resulting guest bound completes ARC---\textbf{FALSIFIED}; discovery
and routing are separate.  F3: Reingold's logspace theorem automatically
supplies the $N^{11/10}$ local time bound---\textbf{FALSIFIED as an
inference}; it gives polynomial time, not this exponent.  F4: the three
explicit routing failures below prove a universal router lower bound---
\textbf{FALSIFIED}; they delimit implementations, not every algorithm.

\section{Exact child-volume aggregation theorem}
Let an exact both-label controller make sequential calls on ordinary-SAT
children of positive physical lengths $M_1,\ldots,M_q$.  Put
$V=\sum_iM_i$ and $M=\max_iM_i$.

\begin{theorem}[Volume--maximum guest aggregation]
For every real $K\ge1$,
\[
 sum_{i=1}^q M_i^K\le V M^{K-1}.                  \tag{2}
\]
Consequently, if $V=N^{v+o(1)}$ and $M=N^{\lambda+o(1)}$, sequential calls to
a monotone guest with time $T_A(m)=m^{K+o(1)}$ use at most
\[
 N^{v-\lambda+\lambda K+o(1)}                     \tag{3}
\]
guest time.  If the guest workspace is $m^{L+o(1)}$ and calls are serial with
discarded state, guest peak space is $N^{\lambda L+o(1)}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each $M_i^K=M_iM_i^{K-1}\le M_iM^{K-1}$.  Summing proves (2), and substitution
proves (3).  Serial reuse gives the maximum, rather than the sum, of guest
workspaces.  This accounts only for the guest portion of the realization.
\end{proof}

At $(v,\lambda)=(1,1/2)$, (3) is
$N^{1/2+K/2+o(1)}$, strictly below the proposed
$N^{3/5+K/2+o(1)}$ guest allowance.  Thus the balanced articulation family
does not fail because it has many children.  The inequality is also tight up
to subpolynomial factors when the children have comparable maximum size.

\section{One-pivot physical volume and both-label semantics}
For a verified pivot $x$, let $V_1,\ldots,V_q$ be the components of the primal
graph after deleting $x$.  For $b\in\{0,1\}$, restrict $x=b$ and assign every
surviving original clause to its unique private component.

\begin{theorem}[Restricted articulation volume]
On the fixed-width indexed clause-list encoding used by GATE279, the total
physical length of all nonempty dense-renumbered children over both values of
$b$ is $O(N)$, including headers.  Moreover
\[
 \operatorname{SAT}(F)\iff
 \bigvee_{b\in\{0,1\}}\left[B_x(b)\land
       \bigwedge_i\operatorname{SAT}(F_{i,b})\right].               \tag{4}
\]
Thus if every child has length $N^{1/2+o(1)}$, the guest time and serial guest
space meet the corresponding terms of (1).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Distinct non-$x$ variables in one clause are adjacent, hence lie in one
component.  For each $b$, every source clause contributes to at most one
child; restriction only removes literals or clauses.  Two values of $b$ give
constant duplication.  There are at most $3m$ nonempty components, and the
fixed indexed source length is $\Omega(m\log(n+2))$, so dense child headers
also total $O(N)$.  This proves the volume statement.  A parent model
restricts to a common $b$, boundary model and component models.  Conversely,
the private variable sets are disjoint, so compatible child witnesses combine
with $x=b$ and satisfy every original indexed clause.  Negating the same
identity gives exhaustive UNSAT correctness.
\end{proof}

This independently certifies the logical core of GATE278--279 and sharpens its
guest ledger.  It does not pay for finding the pivot or delivering children.

\section{The remaining routing transaction}
On the matched STAR family, $q=N^{1/2+o(1)}$,
$M=N^{1/2+o(1)}$, and $V=N^{1+o(1)}$.  Three direct implementations have exact
but unfavorable ledgers:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Implementation & Charged resource & STAR exponent\\\midrule
one full parent scan per child & $\Theta(qN)$ time & $N^{3/2+o(1)}$\\
retain every physical child & $\Theta(V)$ space & $N^{1+o(1)}$\\
retain linear state for every child/guest & $\Theta(V)$ space & $N^{1+o(1)}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The first exceeds setup exponent $11/10$; the latter two exceed workspace
exponent $1/2$.  These are \emph{implementation theorems}, not a trilemma for
all machines.  Batching, recomputation, a source index, or a virtual accessor
may interpolate.  GATE279 proves that a source-implicit articulation selector
and indexed child-bit transducer exist in $O(\log N)$ writable space by
logspace composition with Reingold USTCON.  Its proved time is only
$N^{O(1)}$; no bound of $N^{11/10+o(1)}$ or a sufficiently cheap per-guest-
probe tariff is supplied.  This routing/accessor theorem is now the precise
missing arrow.

Borodin--Cook's sorting tradeoff is a useful structural analogy for read-only
input routing, but its sorting function and branching-program proof do not
transfer to this child-partition task.  Kosowski's randomized USTCON
time--space tradeoff likewise concerns graph connectivity, not exact SAT child
emission on the FLMV machine.  These are \textbf{ANALOGY/CANDIDATE}, not ARC
lower bounds.  Allender et al.'s width-parameterized SAT algorithms show how
careful balanced splitting can realize time--space tradeoffs when a valid
decomposition is supplied; they do not prove all-input balanced articulation.

\section{Same-source SAT/UNSAT proof and finite controls}

proper clauses in block $i$ resolve in three original-source steps to a unit
$\ell_i\in\{x,\neg x\}$.  All $\ell_i=\neg x$ gives a SAT source; changing the
last to $x$ gives an UNSAT source, and the two derived opposite units resolve
