\begin{abstract}
This run independently reconstructs LSPV-4's raw-cofactor characterization and adds an exact one-pivot fragmentation bound.  For any valid D\"ocker duplicate-free proper Monotone 3-SAT-$(2,2)$ source, a nonempty raw cofactor is target-valid up to relabeling iff it is an unchanged union of untouched source incidence components and every outside clause is satisfied.  Hence a connected source has no nonempty target-valid raw cofactor after a nonempty assignment.  Nevertheless the ordinary one-pivot residual retains all $n-1$ nonpivot identities and has at most eight incidence components; its largest component has at least $\lceil(n-1)/8\rceil$ variables.  Therefore the route ``raw restriction, component split, hereditary target recursion'' fails before power shrink: connected nonterminal branches require semantic/noninjective re-encoding or an explicit repair.  REF258 repairs them but grows to $n+201$ variables.  Independent replay passed 110,602 checks on connected U198 UNSAT, V198 SAT, and Y6.  Arbitrary-$A\mapsto\Phi(A)$, numerical FLMV transport, and P versus NP remain \status{OPEN}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Exact raw-cofactor characterization}
Let $B(F)$ be the bipartite variable--clause incidence graph.  A source component is a connected component of $B(F)$.  For a coherent partial assignment $p$, write $F|p$ for the raw syntactic cofactor.

\begin{theorem}[Untouched-component characterization]
Let $F$ be any nonempty valid canonical target and let $p$ be coherent.  A nonempty $F|p$ is itself target-valid up to bijective variable relabeling iff there is a nonempty collection of source components such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item every selected component is wholly unassigned and every one of its clauses remains unchanged;
\item every source clause outside the selected union is satisfied by $p$ and hence deleted.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, if $F$ is connected and $p$ is nonempty, then $F|p$ is either an empty SAT terminal, a contradiction, or a noncanonical ordinary residual.  It is never a nonempty canonical child.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose a source variable $y$ occurs in a target-valid residual.  The source gives exactly two occurrences of each polarity, while target validity requires the same counts.  Thus all four source occurrences of $y$ survive.  Every incident source clause therefore survives as a full unchanged triple.  Its other two variables then occur in the residual and the same argument propagates across the entire source incidence component.  No clause of that component is shortened or deleted.  Conversely, a union of untouched source components retains proper monotone triples, uniqueness, and exact two-per-polarity counts.  Deleting all outside clauses requires each to be satisfied.  If $F$ is connected, any nonempty surviving union is the whole source; nonempty $p$ would then assign a variable in that component, contradicting untouchedness.
\end{proof}

This independently certifies ARC-CONNECTED-COFACTOR293.  It is a syntax/identity theorem, not an arbitrary-algorithm lower bound.

\section{New one-pivot fragmentation bound}
The raw residual may be useful as an ordinary noncanonical CNF even though it is not a hereditary target child.  Its component structure is nevertheless tightly bounded.

\begin{lemma}[Identity survival]
After assigning one pivot $x$, every other source variable occurs in the raw cofactor.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Exactly two pivot clauses are satisfied and deleted.  A nonpivot variable can lose at most its occurrences in those two clauses.  It had four source occurrences, so at least two remain.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Eight-component bound]
Let $F$ be connected and valid with $n$ variables.  For any pivot $x$ and branch value, every noncontradictory raw cofactor has exactly $n-1$ variable identities, at most eight incidence components, and a largest component of size at least
\[
 \left\lceil\frac{n-1}{8}\right\rceil.                  \tag{1}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The lemma gives the vertex count.  The pivot has four incident clauses, each with two co-variables, so it has at most eight distinct source neighbors.  Let $C$ be a residual component and take an original primal-graph path from a vertex of $C$ to $x$.  The first path edge that leaves $C$ cannot come from an unaffected clause, since that edge would remain and join the two residual vertices.  It is therefore either incident to $x$, or is the co-variable edge of a deleted satisfied pivot clause.  In either case the endpoint still in $C$ is a source neighbor of $x$.  Distinct residual components contain distinct pivot neighbors, so there are at most eight.  Pigeonhole gives (1).
\end{proof}

For every fixed $\lambda<1$, $\lceil(n-1)/8\rceil>n^\lambda$ once $n$ is sufficiently large.  A solver that recursively decides every component on a SAT branch therefore still has a linear-size component.  An UNSAT branch may stop at one refuted component, but finding it is not a free best-policy oracle.  The theorem does not rule out semantic compression, noninjective maps, learned clauses, global elimination, or other algorithms.
