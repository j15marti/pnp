For FLMV let $f_0=1$, $f_k=cf_{k-1}/(1+f_{k-1})$ and
\[
 d_*(c)=\max_{k\geq1}\min\left\{
       \frac{1/f_k-c}{k},c^{-k}\right\}.

The conditional recurrence lands in the forbidden region at time coordinate

\begin{theorem}[Baseline ARC--FLMV Pareto supremum]
Let $c_\star$ be the unique root in $(1,\varphi)$ of
\[
 5c^3+10c^2-11c-10=0,
\]
and put $\lambda_\star=1-3/(5c_\star)$.  For the baseline conditional
recurrence and $\lambda\geq 1/2$, there exists an FLMV time coordinate $c$
with both strict inequalities if and only if
\[
 \lambda<\lambda_\star.
\]
Numerically,
\[
 c_\star=1.204607086823560\ldots,
 \qquad \lambda_\star=0.501912277818201\ldots .
\]
The endpoint is not feasible.  In particular $5019/10000$ passes (for example
at $c=6023/5000$), whereas $251/500$ fails for every $c$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $h(c)=1-3/(5c)$.  The time condition is equivalent to
$\lambda<h(c)$, and $h$ is strictly increasing.  For every $k$, $f_k(c)$ is
increasing on $c>1$ by induction through
$cf/(1+f)$; hence both terms defining $d_k(c)$ are decreasing, so
$d_*(c)$ is nonincreasing.

At the claimed crossing, the $k=2$ term wins and
\[
 d_2(c)=\frac{c+2-c^3}{2c^2}.
\]
Indeed $d_2-d_1$ has positive numerator $(c-1)^2(c+2)$; moreover
\[
 6c^3(d_2-d_3)=-c^4+c^2+4c-4>0,
\]
and
\[
 2c^4(d_2-c^{-4})=c^3+2c^2-c^5-2>0
\]
on the rational isolating interval
$1204607/10^6<c_\star<1204608/10^6$.  Since $d_k\leq c^{-k}\leq c^{-4}$
for $k\geq4$, this proves $d_*(c_\star)=d_2(c_\star)$.  The executable
certificate checks the polynomial signs and the monotonicity derivatives at
both rational endpoints using exact fractions.

Solving $h(c)=d_2(c)$ gives exactly the displayed cubic.  Its derivative is
positive for $c\geq1$, and the isolating interval has opposite polynomial
signs, so the root is unique.  For $c<c_\star$, increasing $h$ gives
$h(c)<\lambda_\star$; for $c>c_\star$, nonincreasing $d_*$ gives
$d_*(c)\leq\lambda_\star$.  Conversely, every
$\lambda<\lambda_\star$ satisfies both strict inequalities at
$c=c_\star$.  Equality fails both strict tests.  This proves the global
statement, including the entire infinite FLMV envelope.
\end{proof}
