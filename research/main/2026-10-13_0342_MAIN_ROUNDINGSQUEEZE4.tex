This report proposes a repair of the precise parameter obstruction in
ROUNDINGSQUEEZE3 and current LIFTDIM-FIREWALL1. It does not refute that
obstruction within its old sufficient-bound package. It replaces the
probabilistic full-image estimate and rebuilds the subsequent cover/error
argument. The result concerns proof size and depth in named models, not
arbitrary SAT-machine runtime. The source family and general lifting

\section{Statement and conventions}
Let $F$ be an unsatisfiable CNF on its actual $\nu$ variables, let $b\ge3$
be an integer, $1\le w\le\nu$, and $0<\delta<1-1/\log_2b$. Rows are
$x\in[b]^\nu$, columns are $y\in\{0,1\}^{b\nu}$, and
$\IND(x,y)_i=y_{i,x_i}$. A triangle has row neighborhoods linearly ordered
by inclusion. A binary triangle DAG has a full-domain root, internal nodes
contained in the union of their two children, and each leaf contained in the
violation set of one fixed clause after applying $\IND$. Its size $s$ counts
nodes; depth $d$ counts edges on a longest dependency path.

\begin{theorem}[Proposed spread-budget lifting]
There is a universal constant $A$ such that if
\[
 b^\delta\ge A(w+1)\log_2(2\nu)\log_2(2b\nu),\qquad
 s\le\tfrac12 b^{(1-\delta)w},                              \tag{1}
\]
then a triangle DAG of size $s$ and depth $d$ solving
$\operatorname{Search}(F)\circ\IND_b^\nu$ yields a Resolution refutation
of $F$ of width at most $w$ and depth at most $(w+1)d+1$.
No upper bound on the extracted Resolution proof size is asserted.
\end{theorem}
The construction below temporarily allows weakening. Replacing each derived
line by an available subclause removes weakening without increasing width
or depth. All logarithms below are base two.

\section{Normalized spread and robust full image}
Theorem 1.2 of Mossel--Niles-Weed--Sun--Zadik supplies a universal constant
$C\ge1$ with the following consequence. If a probability measure on sets of
size at most $\nu$ is $R$-spread, meaning
$\Pr[S\subseteq A]\le R^{-|S|}$ for every fixed $S$, then an independent
Bernoulli-$p$ set contains a supported set with probability at least $0.9$
when $p\ge C\log(2\nu)/R$. We absorb the logarithm convention in $C$;
for $\nu=1$ use their stated size cap $k=2$. This is the normalized
probability convention, not an unproved conversion from an absolute
family-cardinality spread convention.

\begin{lemma}[Amplified full image]
Suppose uniform $x\in X\subseteq[b]^\nu$ satisfies
\[
 \Pr[x_I=\alpha]\le2b^{-\delta|I|}\quad(\varnothing\ne I\subseteq[\nu]).
\]
Put $\ell=\log(2\nu)$ and
$T=\lfloor b^\delta/(4C\ell)\rfloor\ge1$. If
$|Y|\ge2^{b\nu-h}$ with $h<T$, then some single row $x\in X$ satisfies
$\IND(\{x\}\times Y)=\{0,1\}^\nu$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The sets $A_x=\{(i,x_i):i\in[\nu]\}$ carry an $R$-spread measure for
$R=b^\delta/2$, since $2b^{-\delta r}\le R^{-r}$ for $r\ge1$.
Take $p_0=C\ell/R$. Each Bernoulli-$p_0$ sample misses all $A_x$ with
probability at most $1/10$. The union of $T$ independent samples has
Bernoulli parameter $q=1-(1-p_0)^T\le Tp_0\le1/2$. Missing all $A_x$ in
that union implies missing them in every sample, so its probability is at
most $10^{-T}$. A fair Bernoulli set has no larger miss probability.

If every row misses some output $z_x$, then every column of $Y$ satisfies
the CNF forbidding $z_x$ at positions $A_x$. Making every literal in this
CNF negative cannot decrease its number of models: with all coordinates
but one fixed, the number of allowed bit values changes from
$\mathbf1[P]+\mathbf1[N]$ to $1+\mathbf1[P\land N]$, where $P,N$ are the
positive and negative residual requirements. Iterate this operation.
The all-negative CNF holds precisely when the set of one-bits contains no
$A_x$. Its density is at most $10^{-T}<2^{-T}$, contradicting the assumed
density of $Y$. This uses the same monotonization principle as Fact B.3
of the source paper, with a different probabilistic tail bound.
\end{proof}
A subset of at least half a dense row set loses at most one bit in the
marginal condition. Half of a column set of deficiency $\Delta$ has
deficiency at most $\Delta+1$. Fixing a pointer and projecting to the free
blocks does not increase column deficiency. Thus the lemma remains usable
after these two losses when $T>\Delta+1$. With no free block, the output
cube is a singleton and no spread lemma is needed.

\section{A parameterized cover with actual neighborhoods}
Call a row set $\delta$-dense if
$\Pr[x_I=\alpha]\le b^{-\delta|I|}$ for every nonempty $I$.
A pointer fixes some coordinates; predensity means containing a nonempty
subset dense on the free coordinates.

Order the rows of a triangle $U$ by decreasing neighborhood. For each
pointer $\alpha$ fixing $I$ with $|I|\le w$ whose matching rows $S_\alpha$
are predense, choose the earliest prefix containing a dense witness
$D_\alpha$, and let $x_\alpha$ be its last row. Secure every matching row
at or below $x_\alpha$. For every output pattern $\beta\in\{0,1\}^I$ set
\[
 Y_{\alpha,\beta}=U[x_\alpha]\cap
       \{y:\IND(\alpha_I,y_I)=\beta\}.                       \tag{2}
\]
Accept the cell $S_\alpha\times Y_{\alpha,\beta}$ when
$|Y_{\alpha,\beta}|\ge2^{b\nu-\Delta}$; otherwise mark its columns as
errors. An accepted cell has inner rectangle
$D_\alpha\times Y_{\alpha,\beta}\subseteq U$.

The intersection with the actual neighborhood in (2) is essential.
A global output-pattern fiber does not generally give an inner rectangle.
We do not invoke the source Triangle Lemma's stronger maximality property;
we handle the root directly.

The error densities obey
\[
 |X_{\rm err}|<b^{\nu-(1-\delta)w},\qquad
 |Y_{\rm err}|/2^{b\nu}\le M2^{-\Delta},\quad
 M=\sum_{j=0}^w\binom\nu j(2b)^j\le(2b\nu)^{w+1}.             \tag{3}
\]
Security holds for every strip securing a row: its accepted cells and column
errors cover that row's entire neighborhood. Indeed the neighborhood is
contained in $U[x_\alpha]$ and the cells partition the latter.

Here is the row bound in detail. If the unsecured set $E$ had cardinality at
least $b^{\nu-(1-\delta)w}$, either $E$ is dense, or choose an
inclusion-maximal coordinate set $I$ with some fiber of probability greater
than $b^{-\delta|I|}$. The elementary bound on a fiber's size gives
$|I|<w$. Conditional on this fiber the remaining coordinates are dense;
otherwise a violating conditional fiber would enlarge $I$. In the dense
case use the empty pointer. In either case a dense fiber in $E$ has a last
row at or below the associated strip threshold, so that row is secured, a
contradiction. The column bound simply counts all pointer/output pairs and
sums their rejected cell densities.

\section{Proof of the lifting statement}
Set $A=64C$, $h_0=\log(2b\nu)$ and $\Delta=2(w+1)h_0+2$.
Condition (1) ensures
$T=\lfloor b^\delta/(4C\log(2\nu))\rfloor\ge\Delta+2$.
Process the DAG from leaves to root. Before making strips at a node, remove
all whole rows and columns placed in earlier error sets. After making the
strips, add that node's errors to the global sets. Triangles remain
triangles. There are at most $s$ error contributions. Row-error density is
less than $1/2$, and column-error density is bounded by
\[
 sM2^{-\Delta}
 \le(2b\nu)^w(2b\nu)^{w+1}2^{-\Delta}<1/2.                  \tag{4}
\]
A parent avoids every child error, including errors present when the child's
strips were formed. Its inner rectangles are therefore still covered by the
corresponding cleaned child triangles.

To each accepted cell associate the clause $C_\beta$ falsified precisely by
the cube $z_I=\beta$. The full-image lemma implies that at a leaf this cube
falsifies the leaf's fixed source clause, so $C_\beta$ is a weakening of it.
At the root, the remaining row and column densities are at least one half.
The rows are dense since
$2b^{-|I|}\le b^{-\delta|I|}$ under $\delta<1-1/\log b$. All root
neighborhoods equal the same remaining column set, of deficiency at most
one. Hence the empty pointer gives an accepted cell and the empty clause.
No maximality property is used.

For an internal node, take an accepted inner rectangle $D\times Y_0$.
A rectangle covered by two triangles has a subrectangle in one child with
at least half of each marginal. For a proof, order rows by the first child's
neighborhoods and take a median row. If that row sees at least half the
columns, the upper half of rows works in that child. Otherwise the lower
half of rows and complementary columns are outside the first child and
hence inside the second. Including the median handles odd sizes.

The robust full-image lemma supplies one row $x$ in this subrectangle that
realizes the entire parent output cube using its columns. Since $x$ is not
a child-error row, some child strip secures it, with pointer support $J$,
$|J|\le w$. Security and absence of child column errors imply that the
retained clauses of this single strip, all supported on the same $J$,
block every assignment in the parent cube. In particular each assignment on
$J$ compatible with $z_I=\beta$ has its corresponding blocking clause.
Resolve these clauses in a complete binary tree on $J\setminus I$.
This gives the restriction of $C_\beta$ to $J$ at depth at most
$|J\setminus I|$ and width at most $w$; one weakening gives $C_\beta$.
Thus each DAG edge adds at most $w+1$ depth, with one possible leaf
weakening, proving $(w+1)d+1$.

The extraction may enumerate enormous row and cell families. It is a
proof-existence simulation with controlled width and depth, not an efficient
SAT procedure. Error union bounds count original DAG nodes, not unfolded
path multiplicities.

\section{Exact-(2,2) transfer and fixed-parameter repair}
Let $d_F(w)$ be minimum source Resolution depth at width at most $w$.
For $H=\mathcal T(P(\widetilde{\IND}_b(F)))$, the supplied
ROUNDINGSQUEEZE2 semantic projection uses at most $s+1$ lines and depth
$D+1$. The separated index extension induces the corresponding triangle
DAG, as in the source's Section 2.6. Consequently, under (1),
\[
 s+1\le\tfrac12b^{(1-\delta)w}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 D\ge\frac{d_F(w)-1}{w+1}-1.                                \tag{5}
\]
This retains the helper-line and depth losses; it is conditional on the
stated preceding compiler interfaces as well as the candidate proof above.

The source width-depth theorem provides bases with
$2k^2t^{c+1}\le\nu\le40k^2(2t)^{c+1}$, a Resolution refutation of width
$k+3$ and size $O(k^2(4t)^k)$, and depth $\Omega(t^k)$ for every refutation
of width at most $k+c$. Fix
\[
 k=400000,\ c=164000,\ w=564000,\ \delta=1/200,\ b=t^{500}.
\]
For large $t$ all source hypotheses hold. The left side of (1) is $t^{5/2}$;
its right side is $O_{k,c}((\log t)^2)$ at the actual dimension. Thus the
new sufficient bound holds, unlike the old one. The constant $C$ is
symbolic, so no finite threshold $t$ is certified.

The final target has encoded length $L_t=O_{k,c}(t^q\log t)$, known
Resolution proof size $O_{k,c}(t^u)$, and lifting cap $\tfrac12t^z$, with
\[
 q=166001,\quad u=200402000,\quad z=280590000.
\]
The last compiler preserves the known Resolution line bound linearly:
recover properized clauses, derive Q ports, telescope clone implication
cycles in both directions, recover selector-guarded source clauses, and
simulate the supplied Resolution proof twice with opposite guards. This
uses $O(m+S)$ lines, not an unknown polynomial power. The compiler map is
supplied or computed from the source, not recovered anonymously for free.

Take the named proof budget $r_t=t^{u+1}$. The exact margins are
\[
 k-\tfrac{12}{5}q=\tfrac{7988}{5}>0,\qquad
 z-\tfrac75(u+1)=\tfrac{135993}{5}>0.
\]
For large $t$ the target has a Resolution proof of at most $r_t$ lines,
while every semantic Cutting Planes proof of size at most $r_t^{7/5}$ has
depth $\Omega(t^k)$ and hence $\Omega(L_t^{12/5})$. The logarithm in the
encoding is absorbed by the strict margin. The size cap is essential and
nonvacuous; $r_t$ is polynomially bounded in $L_t$. This is a candidate
repair of the fixed-parameter application, not independent verification of
all regimes of the original paper.
