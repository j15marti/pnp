\begin{abstract}
We independently reproduce the clause-indexed all-literals-true/all-literals-false
experiment required by coordination issue~\#3.  Each original signed occurrence is
retained, local targets are inverted, all four pair modes are checked, and bounded
multi-clause modes are compared with exact enumeration.  The main red-team theorem
is a zero-pruning result: an $SS/SU/US/UU$ compatibility test cannot reject any
globally coherent source-variable assignment, because every realized extreme mode
already inherits one common assignment.  It can prune only a representation that
independently guesses occurrence-level clause targets.  By contrast, a
source-authenticated non-tautological resolvent certifies a genuine impossible
partial-assignment cube.  Twelve SAT/UNSAT controls and 967,957 exact checks support
the implementation claims.  This is neither a general SAT algorithm nor a lower
bound.  P versus NP remains \OPEN.
\end{abstract}

\section{Status and precise target}

\paragraph{Single target.}
For every proper signed 3-CNF $F$ and every node of a DPLL search that assigns the
\emph{original shared variables}, determine whether adding the local-extreme
compatibility matrix can eliminate a node that the ordinary coherent search would
otherwise visit.

\paragraph{Quantifiers and model.}
The symbolic theorem quantifies over every proper signed 3-CNF and every coherent
partial source-variable assignment.  The computational comparison uses a declared
fixed-order DPLL with saturated unit propagation, source scanning, and optional
bounded resolution preprocessing.  It is a conventional baseline, not industrial
CDCL and not a tape-machine measurement.

\paragraph{Mechanisms proposed before testing.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Add the four-way extreme compatibility matrix as a guard to source-variable DPLL.
\item Search clause-extreme labels and reject inconsistent $S/U$ combinations before
      reconstructing a source assignment.
\item Derive source-parent, non-tautological bounded resolvents and prune the cube that
      falsifies each resolvent.
\item Switch to Gaussian elimination when the source is recognized as affine/XOR.
\end{enumerate}
Mechanism 1 is falsified as a pruning mechanism; mechanism 2 is representation-specific;
mechanisms 3 and 4 remain sound with their acquisition and recognition costs charged.

\section{Clause-indexed occurrence experiment}

Let original clause $C_j=(\ell_{1j}\vee\ell_{2j}\vee\ell_{3j})$ and let
$x_{i,j}$ denote the occurrence of source variable $x_i$ in $C_j$, not a fresh
Boolean variable.  Write the sign bit $\nu_{ij}=0$ for $x_i$ and $1$ for
$\neg x_i$.  The local targets are
\[
 S_j:\quad a_i=1\oplus\nu_{ij},
 \qquad
 U_j:\quad a_i=\nu_{ij}.
\]
Thus inversion swaps $S_j$ and $U_j$ occurrence by occurrence.  Ordinary clause
satisfaction is only $t_j(a)\ge1$; $S_j$ is the stronger $t_j(a)=3$, while $U_j$
is $t_j(a)=0$.

For two distinct clauses $C_j,C_k$, the exact pair matrix is
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccl}
\toprule
Mode & Feasibility & Obstruction\\
\midrule
$SS$ & no opposite-polarity shared variable & opposite signs demand opposite values\\
$UU$ & no opposite-polarity shared variable & opposite signs demand opposite values\\
$SU$ & no same-polarity shared variable & same signs demand opposite values\\
$US$ & no same-polarity shared variable & same signs demand opposite values\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The reproducer emits, for every source clause and occurrence, its explicit sign,
$S$ target, inverted $S\to U$ target, $U$ target, and inverted $U\to S$ target.
It emits every pair matrix with source IDs and the precise conflicting variables.
For a bounded subset of at most eight clauses it enumerates all $2^h$ proposed
$S/U$ mode vectors and compares demand consistency with exact source assignments.

\paragraph{Global endpoint and inversion controls.}
For each complete assignment $a$, the program records each three-bit literal truth
vector, $t_j(a)$, and $(N_0,N_1,N_2,N_3)$.  It checks
\[
 t_j(\bar a)=3-t_j(a),\qquad N_r(\bar a)=N_{3-r}(a),
\]
and emits deterministic $0^n\to1^n$ and $1^n\to0^n$ single-flip trails.  The
three-clause control
\[
 (x_1\vee x_2\vee x_3)\wedge
 (x_1\vee x_2\vee\neg x_3)\wedge
 (\neg x_1\vee\neg x_2\vee\neg x_3)
\]
falsifies both endpoints but has five satisfying assignments.  Endpoint failure is
therefore not an UNSAT certificate.

\section{The representation-firewall theorem}

\begin{theorem}[coherent extreme compatibility firewall]\label{thm:firewall}
Let $F$ be any CNF over shared source variables and let $\alpha$ be any coherent
partial assignment.  Let $E_\alpha$ contain $(j,S)$ when every variable of $C_j$
is assigned and all its literals are true, and $(j,U)$ when every variable is
assigned and all its literals are false.  Then every pair in $E_\alpha$ passes the
four-way compatibility matrix, and all of $E_\alpha$ is jointly feasible.

Consequently, adding only this compatibility check to a DPLL search whose nodes are
coherent source-variable assignments rejects no node and cannot reduce its search
tree.  It can detect errors or prune only a different representation that guesses
local occurrence targets independently.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every $(j,m)\in E_\alpha$, the local target demand on each variable occurring
in $C_j$ is exactly its value under the one assignment $\alpha$.  Hence two realized
targets cannot demand different values from any shared variable.  The restriction
of $\alpha$ to all variables occurring in $E_\alpha$ is a joint witness.  Therefore
the guard never rejects a coherent search node.  Since the guarded and unguarded
source-variable DPLL perform the same branching and propagation, their recursion
nodes are identical; the guard can only add checking work.  \qedhere
\end{proof}

This is not the signed-balance theorem rediscovered: the exact multi-clause
$S/U$ feasibility characterization was already published by LSPV-1 as
EXTREME-SIGNED-CYCLE240.  The present theorem identifies the algorithmic consequence
for the \emph{source-variable} search space.

\begin{corollary}[dense incompatibility need not mean difficulty]
For every $r\ge1$, take $r$ copies each of
\[
 A=(x\vee y\vee z),\qquad B=(x\vee y\vee\neg z).
\]
All $r^2$ cross-copy pairs have no feasible $SS,SU,US,$ or $UU$ mode because the
clauses share $x,y$ with the same sign and $z$ with opposite sign.  Nevertheless the
formula is equivalent to $A\wedge B$ and has six of eight assignments as models.
Thus arbitrarily many incompatibility edges can arise from duplication without
increasing semantic or decision difficulty.
\end{corollary}

\section{What genuinely forbids a source-assignment cube}

From source clauses $(x\vee A)$ and $(\neg x\vee B)$, resolution gives
$R=A\vee B$.  When $R$ is non-tautological, the partial cube falsifying every literal
of $R$ has no extension satisfying both parents: either value of $x$ falsifies one
parent.  The reproducer retains both parent IDs, pivot, resolvent, and falsifying cube,
then independently checks every extension against the source formula.

For the duplicate-support control, resolving $A$ and $B$ on $z$ yields
$(x\vee y)$ and certifies the entire cube $x=y=0$ impossible.  Unlike the matrix
firewall, this is a real logical consequence over source variables.  Tautological
resolvents are recorded but never treated as conflicts.  The solver comparison keeps
only non-tautological resolvents of width at most three and charges all literal-pair
tests and output construction.

The guard performed 192 realized-pair checks across the reported solver runs and
rejected zero nodes.  In the dense XOR $K_6$ control, baseline instrumented work was

oracle the program checked 777,337 pair-mode cases, 89,344 multi-clause mode cases,
15,520 realized extreme pairs, 10,768 complements, 67,888 single-flip clause updates,
and 7,100 extensions of prohibited cubes: 967,957 checks total.

Bounded resolution sometimes helped node count but not uniformly total work.  On the
contradictory padded-unit core, nodes fell $19\to3$ and charged work $589\to573$.
On dense XOR $K_6$, nodes stayed $3\to3$ while charged work rose $911\to39{,}531$.

This is the required distinction between successful-path pruning and discovery cost.

\paragraph{Affine control.}
The dense XOR source is easy after recognizing its linear system: Gaussian elimination
operates on the compact affine representation even though the CNF primal graph is
$K_6$ and the extreme matrix has 840 all-four-incompatible pairs.  Existing SAT/XOR
work explicitly integrates Gauss--Jordan reasoning because ordinary clausal reasoning
can be a poor representation for parity.  No structural score may suppress that switch.

\section{Literature and duplication audit}

Kullmann's conflict matrix counts clashes between clauses and the hitting-clause-set
literature studies clause-sets where clauses conflict pairwise.  Our four-mode matrix
is related but not identical: it distinguishes equal versus opposite local extreme
modes and retains parallel shared-variable demands.  We make no novelty claim for
clause conflict graphs, hitting clause-sets, resolution, unit propagation, clause
learning, or XOR reasoning.

The primary sources checked were Kullmann's \emph{The Combinatorics of Conflicts
between Clauses} (SAT 2003, DOI \url{https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-24605-3_32})
and \emph{Constraint Satisfaction Problems in Clausal Form II} (2011, DOI
\url{https://doi.org/10.3233/FI-2011-429}).  For learned-resolution context we checked
Beame, Kautz, and Sabharwal, \emph{Understanding the Power of Clause Learning}
(IJCAI 2003) and the later resolution-simulation formulation at
\url{https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~beame/papers/cdclres.pdf}.  For the parity
representation control we checked Han and Jiang, \emph{When Boolean Satisfiability
Meets Gaussian Elimination in a Simplex Way} (CAV 2012),
\url{https://alcom.ee.ntu.edu.tw/assets/publications/cav12.pdf}.

\paragraph{Duplication / contradiction audit.}
The pair matrix and global signed-balance characterization are adopted as prior work,
not relabeled.  LSPV-2 already showed that an extreme-count rule adds no pruning after
UP in its balanced exact22 regime.  The new result concerns a different leak: applying
the raw compatibility predicate to coherent source-variable DPLL cannot prune in
\emph{any} CNF.  The previous forbidden-cube result remains valid, but exact overlap
counting remains optional and potentially hard.

\paragraph{What changed relative to root main.}
The synthesis should now explicitly separate three objects: (i) occurrence-target
compatibility, a representation firewall; (ii) source-variable consequences such as
resolvents, which can prune; and (iii) alternate compact representations such as
affine row spaces.  No P-versus-NP proof arrow is strengthened.

\section{Statuses, limitations, and next lemma}

\begin{itemize}
\item \PROVED: Theorem~\ref{thm:firewall} and the duplicate-support corollary.
\item \COMP: the twelve finite controls, solver counters, and exact enumeration.
\item \FALSE: ``The $SS/SU/US/UU$ matrix by itself prunes a coherent
      source-variable DPLL tree.''
\item \OPEN: whether a polynomial-time, proof-carrying closure combining UP,
      bounded resolution, Horn/2-SAT recognition, and affine switching gives a net
      worst-case reduction on a substantially broader class.
\end{itemize}

The work units are logical instrumentation, not physical head crossings or native
RAM bytes.  Exact enumeration is an external small-instance oracle.  No finite
benchmark, clause-conflict density, or restricted proof system gives an
arbitrary-algorithm SAT lower bound.

\paragraph{Next specific lemma.}
Define a polynomially recognizable class of partial source assignments for which
the signed-extreme graph plus a certified bound on middle-state clauses derives a
new non-tautological source clause not already absorbed by UP; prove the derivation,
charge recognition and proof checking, and compare its net DPLL/CDCL work on SAT and
UNSAT controls.  Without such a source-level consequence, extreme compatibility is
only a representation consistency check.
