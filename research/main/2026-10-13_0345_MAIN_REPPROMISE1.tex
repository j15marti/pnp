\section*{Exact model, encoding, and target quantifiers}
Let $L=\SAT_{3\mathrm{CNF}}\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$ under the ordinary
self-delimiting explicit encoding.  Put $N=|x|$ for an explicit input.  Let
$R:\{0,1\}^*\to\{0,1\}^*$ be a total uniform polynomial-time reduction with
\[
 x\in L\Longleftrightarrow R(x)\in L,
 \qquad |R(x)|\le |x|^{\lambda+o(1)},
\]
and let $I=\operatorname{im}R$.  A promise decider for $(L\cap I,I\setminus
L)$ is a total deterministic program $B$ required to be correct only on $I$;
its off-promise answers are unrestricted.

For the representation branch, a descriptor $D$ of length $s$ denotes an
explicit formula $y_D$ of length $M=M(D)$.  A lazy evaluator may answer a bit
query to $y_D$ in cost $a(s)$ and a purported solver may make $q(D)$ such
queries.  Preprocessing, descriptor evaluation, materialization, solver time,
extraction, and reconstruction are all charged.  The target theorem would have
to quantify over every fixed uniform deterministic explicit-3SAT decider
$A$, each with its own polynomial exponent $K_A$, and bind any charged
semantic object to the \same{} execution.

\section*{Theorem: REPRESENTATION-PROMISE-ENDPOINT-TRILEMMA1}
For every reduction $R$ and image $I$ satisfying the preceding hypotheses:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Image-promise equivalence.}
\[
 L\in\mathsf P
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists B\in\mathsf P\;\forall z\in I\,[B(z)={\bf1}[z\in L]].
\]
No polynomial-time recognizer for $I$ is needed for the forward composition
$x\mapsto B(R(x))$.  Conversely, a polynomial decider for $L$ restricts to a
valid promise decider on $I$.

\item \textbf{Sparse recognizable images are already separation-bearing.}
If $I\in\mathsf P$ and $S=I\cap L$ is sparse, then $S\in\mathsf{NP}$ and
$R$ many-one reduces $L$ to $S$.  Hence $S$ is sparse and NP-hard, so
Mahaney's theorem yields $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$.

\item \textbf{Descriptor endpoint trilemma.}  An eager route that writes the
ordinary explicit formula pays $\Omega(M)$ bit writes (or
$\Omega(M/\log M)$ word writes) before running the explicit solver.  A total
all-descriptor lazy procedure with $q(D)a(s)=s^{O(1)}$ and polynomial
remaining work would decide standard Succinct-3SAT in polynomial time.  By
NEXP-completeness of Succinct-3SAT and the deterministic time hierarchy
($\mathsf P\ne\mathsf{NEXP}$), that all-descriptor endpoint is impossible.
Restricting the lazy procedure to descriptors in the special image of $R$
returns to item 1: it is equivalent to a polynomial SAT decider, not an
unconditional contradiction weaker than $\mathsf P\ne\mathsf{NP}$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Proof.}
For item 1, if $B$ is correct on $I$, then $x\mapsto B(R(x))$ is a uniform
polynomial-time decider for $L$ because $R(x)\in I$ and membership is
preserved.  The converse is restriction.  For item 2, $I\in\mathsf P$ and
$L\in\mathsf{NP}$ imply $S\in\mathsf{NP}$; moreover
$x\in L\Longleftrightarrow R(x)\in S$, so Mahaney applies.  For item 3,
materializing $M$ output bits has the stated output-size cost.  If the lazy
algorithm were total and polynomial on every succinct descriptor, composing
its accessor and evaluator would be a polynomial-time Succinct-3SAT decider,
contradicting the known NEXP-completeness and time hierarchy.  The promise
restriction is item 1. \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Exact consequence.}
This theorem is a type and endpoint classification.  It does not prove that
an explicit-input SAT decider reads all $N$ bits, exposes a witness, constructs
$H(F,\alpha)$, or has superpolynomial cost.  An image-promise algorithm may be
arbitrary off $I$, yet $B\circ R$ still decides the source language.  Thus
off-promise freedom is irrelevant to the composition and cannot be charged as
extra work by the original decider.

\section*{Scale and exponent ledger}
Suppose a source computation of scale $T$ produces an explicit instance of
length $M=T^{\lambda+o(1)}$, construction costs
$T^{\rho+o(1)}$, and a hypothetical explicit solver has time $M^{K_A}$.  The
honest materialized composition costs
\[
 T^{\max\{\rho,\lambda K_A\}+o(1)}.
\]
A hierarchy speedup requires both $\rho<1$ and $\lambda K_A<1$.  Standard
tableau reductions have $\lambda=1$ and $K_A$ is an arbitrary machine-specific
constant; no fixed hierarchy landing follows.  Replacing $M$ by descriptor
length $s=O(\log M)$ changes the language to Succinct-3SAT unless a special
image promise is imposed, in which case the exact promise equivalence above
applies.  No asymptotic runtime is inferred from the bounded experiment.

\section*{Barrier and model audit}
\paragraph{Relativization and algebrization.}
Reduction-image composition and the eager/lazy type split relativize; an
oracle can be used by both source and promise deciders.  They provide no
nonrelativizing or nonalgebrizing final arrow.  No arithmetization is used.

\paragraph{Natural proofs and model transfer.}
No large constructive circuit property is obtained.  Circuit,
branching-program, proof-system, VLSI, or time--space lower bounds apply only
to their stated representations and resource models.  No solver-to-model
transfer for every uniform deterministic SAT decider is proved.

\paragraph{Uniformity and arbitrary exponents.}
The exact equivalence is uniform.  Replacing the promise algorithm by a
polynomial-size circuit family would yield at most a nonuniform conclusion
such as $\mathsf{NP}\subseteq\mathsf{P}/\mathsf{poly}$, not a uniform SAT
algorithm.  Each uniform machine retains its own exponent $K_A$.

\paragraph{One-bit output and reversible/read controls.}
SAT requires one output bit.  READCEIL1 prevents a superpolynomial
read-certificate argument, while REVUNCOMP1 prevents a universal logical
merger/erasure charge.  Neither fact forces witness, compatibility, transcript,
or reconstruction materialization.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Worst-case/average-case architecture: ANALOGY/CANDIDATE.}
External black-box extraction is the right shape, but no distributional

\item \textbf{Lossiness/collision entropy: ANALOGY/CANDIDATE.}
A many-to-one $R$ can have large fibers, but collision statistics of $R$ do
not charge an explicit SAT decider.  The exact bound
$|\operatorname{im}R|\ge |A|^2/\operatorname{Coll}(R|_A)$ concerns image
size, not arbitrary computational work.
\item \textbf{Schur squares, quadratic relations, syzygies, and binding:
FAILED TRANSFER for this barrier.}  They can expose algebraic structure in a
chosen code or proof-to-node map; no corresponding object is forced in an
arbitrary one-bit SAT execution.  Hidden structure could instead make the
image easier.
\item \textbf{Circuit design and communication analogies: FAILED TRANSFER.}
Output-size, fan-out, locality, routing, and cut lower bounds are useful only
after a matching realization theorem.  Compact descriptors deliberately evade
explicit routing by changing the input model.
\end{itemize}

\section*{P=NP counterfactual and missing universal implication}
Assume $L\in\mathsf P$.  Then every polynomial reduction image $I$ has a
polynomial promise decider by restriction, and self-reduction can reconstruct a
witness in polynomial time.  This is consistent with the assumption.  There is
no contradiction unless one additionally proves, in the same general uniform
machine model, either:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an unconditional lower bound for deciding the explicit clean core, or
\item a solver-independent extractor that turns every such SAT decider into a
restricted realization already known to violate an unconditional bound.
\end{enumerate}
Declaring the reduction image hard, forbidding polynomial promise decision on
it, or demanding $\lambda K_A<1$ for every hypothetical solver would smuggle
the desired separation into a premise.
