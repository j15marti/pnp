\section{Qualitative target and one attempted contradiction}
Fix the total two-tree compatibility language $L_{\rm TT}$ of TWOTREEBRIDGE1.
An input has the eight-bit header \texttt{01010100}, unary universe size
$1^h0$ with $h\ge1$, and for each tree an $h$-bit membership vector and
$\binom h2$ upper-triangular adjacency bits. Each declared graph must be a
nonempty tree, edges must stay within its vertex set, and every universe
label must be used. All other strings reject. Its length is
\[
                  N=9+h^2+2h.
\]
A YES witness is a proper coloring of the union using labels $\Col$. These
labels refer to the three nonzero field colors in the earlier $\mathbb F_4$
construction. They do not introduce an extra allowed field value.

To prove $P\ne NP$ it suffices to show $L_{\rm TT}\notin P$, since its
NP-completeness and polynomial witness maps are supplied by the preceding
construction. The negated hypothesis is precisely
\[
 \neg\exists D,k,C\;\forall I:\quad
 D(I)=\chi_{L_{\rm TT}}(I)\ \land\
 T_D(I)\le C(|I|+2)^k.
\]
The machine $D$, exponent $k$ and constant $C$ are fixed but arbitrary.
An argument that contradicts their existence does not need an explicit
function such as $2^{\Omega(n)}$. Hard inputs may depend on $D$.

The candidate attacked here is that progressively fixing variables in two
interlocking tree summaries inevitably creates unmanageable auxiliary
correlations, even for a hypothetical polynomial decider. The new source
normalization disproves that inference on the actual canonical constructions.
It neither proves nor assumes that every polynomial decider exists.

same realization. Description length is not workspace. Parsing, recovery,
recompilation checks, query writing, reconstruction and output are charged.

\section{Exact source recovery without tags}
Recall the canonical reduction $R$ on a labelled source graph
$\Gamma=([n],E)$ with its edges in lexicographic order. For each source vertex
create $d'_v=\max(1,\deg v)$ occurrence copies, all numbered before any helper.
Consecutive occurrences $x,y$ have fresh helpers $a,b$ and diamond edges
\[
 xa,xb,ab,ay,by.
\]
The first forest contains the path $x-a-b-y$; the second contains $xb,ay$
and each original edge between its reserved occurrences. The first forest
has one path component per source vertex. The second becomes a matching
when its helper leaves are removed. Each forest is connected by a private
hub and private length-two paths from the minimum-labelled representative
of each component. Every such representative is an occurrence vertex.

\begin{theorem}[Exact canonical-image recognition]\label{thm:decoder}
One polynomial-time algorithm, on the unannotated bits of a valid two-tree
input, either recovers its unique source under this canonical construction
and verifies every target bit by recompilation, or reports that the input is
not a canonical image. It uses no source graph, witness, decomposition tags,
or graph-isomorphism oracle as advice.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Intersect the two vertex sets to obtain the shared set $S$. In each tree the
private hub is the unique private vertex having no shared neighbor; every
other private vertex is a connector with exactly two neighbors, the hub and
one shared vertex. Verify this pattern and strip the private vertices.

The first remaining forest must consist of paths. Orient each path from its
smaller-labelled endpoint. Its length must be a multiple of three, and the
vertices at positions $0,3,6,\ldots$ are its source occurrences. The two
intervening vertices reconstruct each diamond's helpers. Order the paths by
their first occurrence label to obtain the source labels. Verify the two
required second-forest helper edges per diamond. All remaining second-forest
edges must join occurrences of distinct source vertices, use each occurrence
at most once, and project to distinct source edges. Reconstruct those edges
in lexicographic order.

Run the known canonical forward construction on this recovered graph and
require exact equality with the entire original bit string. This last check
rejects spurious layouts or noncanonical numbering. For every genuine
$R(\Gamma)$, the structural steps recover exactly its paths, occurrences,
helpers and original edges, so the check succeeds. The path count and
occurrence matching fix the recovered labelled graph uniquely. Every scan,
component computation, sort and re-encoding is polynomial in $N$.
\end{proof}

The decoder's negative result is \emph{not} a NO answer for the full language.
An input outside the canonical image can be either satisfiable or
unsatisfiable. A full decider must use another correct method there. Malformed
bit strings are separately rejected by the language's parser. The result does
not recognize all isomorphic copies of canonical images; no such requirement
is hidden in the claim.

\section{All partial target assignments compress to source lists}
A partial coloring $\mu$ assigns any subset of the $h$ target vertices one
of three colors. Pins may be on occurrences, diamond helpers, private hubs,
or private connectors. They need not arise from a source coloring or from
a particular elimination order.

For each source vertex $v$, define a mask $A_v(\mu)\subseteq\Col$ by
intersecting the following local requirements. An occurrence pinned to $c$
requires its source color to equal $c$. In a diamond $x,y,a,b$ belonging to
$v$, retain a source color $c$ exactly when its two helpers can receive the
two other, distinct colors consistently with any pins on $a,b$. A connector
pinned to $d$ excludes $d$ from the color of its occurrence representative.
For each private hub define $B_i(\mu)\subseteq\Col$ using its own possible
pin and excluding the color of every pinned adjacent connector.

All these sets are obtained by constant-size local tests after decoding.
For example, if exactly one helper is pinned to $d$, its source list loses
$d$. If both helpers are pinned equally, the diamond is impossible. If both
are pinned differently, the source must take the remaining color.

\begin{theorem}[Pin-core equivalence]\label{thm:pin}
For every source graph $\Gamma$, every canonical target $R(\Gamma)$ and
every partial target coloring $\mu$,
\[
 \begin{split}
 &\exists w\supseteq\mu\ [w\text{ colors }R(\Gamma)]\\
 &\quad\iff B_1(\mu)\ne\varnothing\ \land\ B_2(\mu)\ne\varnothing\ \land\
 \exists c\ [c\text{ colors }\Gamma\text{ and }c_v\in A_v(\mu)\ \forall v].
 \end{split}
\]
Every compatible source coloring on the right has a polynomial-time lift
satisfying \emph{all} original target edges and pins. Every target witness
on the left projects to one of those source colorings. Beyond the recovered
fixed source graph, the decision core contains only $3n+6$ mask bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Diamond constraints force every occurrence of a source vertex to agree.
The original occurrence edges then force a proper source coloring. Each
pinned occurrence or helper imposes exactly the corresponding local mask
condition. A pinned connector must differ from both its representative and
its hub, which gives precisely the two indicated exclusions. Thus every
complete target witness yields all conditions on the right.

Conversely choose a compatible source coloring and a color in each nonempty
hub mask. Give every occurrence its source color. Independently choose each
diamond's two other colors consistent with its helper pins; the definition
of $A_v$ guarantees that choice. For a pinned connector use its pin, which
differs from both neighbors by the mask conditions. For an unpinned connector
choose any color absent from its two neighbors; three colors suffice. These
choices satisfy the whole target and $\mu$. Helper pairs are private to their
diamond, hubs/connectors are private to their respective tree, and the only
remaining sharing is through already fixed occurrence colors. Hence there
is no unaccounted compatibility condition. Verify the original edges and pins.
\end{proof}

Original pins must remain accessible during full witness reconstruction.
The $3n+6$ bits are a decision core, not a claim that the original pin record
or output can be erased. Retaining the pins uses $O(h)$ bits, with ordinary
indexing overhead for the decoded roles. The chosen source coloring may
differ from a previous witness; correctness, not replay of a preferred
history, is the target condition.

\begin{corollary}[No auxiliary-table growth under accumulated pins]
For a nested sequence of partial assignments extending one another, every
$A_v$ and $B_i$ can only lose colors. The total number of color-membership
bits ever removed is at most $3n+6$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Adding a pin cannot add a feasible local extension. Thus the masks are
componentwise decreasing subsets of their initial three-element sets.
\end{proof}

This does not solve the source list-coloring question. Even when every mask
is initially full, the source graph may be non-three-colorable. No count of
remaining masks or feasible local options is being promoted to a runtime
lower bound or a complete decision rule. This theorem normalizes every pin
query; it is not a small quantifier-free circuit for all extension answers.

\section{An exact same-language reduction for every input}
The previous theorem applies to canonical images. The following closure
applies to \emph{every} valid two-tree input, so a hypothetical decider for
one fixed language is enough; no stronger assumption about all of NP is
needed for the search construction.

\begin{theorem}[Constant-size-factor pin query]\label{thm:closure}
There is a polynomial-time map $(I,\mu)\mapsto J(I,\mu)$, with
$J(I,\mu)$ again a valid input of $L_{\rm TT}$, such that
\[
 J(I,\mu)\in L_{\rm TT}
 \iff \mu\text{ extends to a coloring of }I,
 \qquad |J(I,\mu)|\le1369|I|.
\]
The numerical constant is an encoding bound, not a claimed runtime lower bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let the input universe have $h$ vertices. Its two trees' union has at most
$2h-2$ edges. Add a three-vertex palette triangle $p_0,p_1,p_2$. For a pin
$\mu(v)=c$, join $v$ to both palette vertices other than $p_c$. The new
source graph $\Gamma_\mu$ has $n'=h+3$ vertices and
$m'\le(2h-2)+3+2h=4h+1$ edges. It is colorable exactly when the original
pins extend: any palette coloring is a permutation of the three colors,
and its inverse relabels the target witness so that every pin has its
specified label. The converse assigns the three named palette colors.

Apply the canonical two-tree construction to $\Gamma_\mu$. If $n_0$ denotes
its number of isolated vertices, then $n_0\le h$, since the palette is a
triangle. With $s=2m'+n_0$ occurrence vertices, the construction's union size is
\[
 H=4s-n'-m'+2=7m'+4n_0-n'+2\le31h+6\le37h.
\]
Consequently its full binary encoding satisfies
\[
 |J|=9+H^2+2H\le1369(9+h^2+2h)=1369|I|.
\]
Both witness directions and every encoding step are polynomial. Invalid
inputs or pin records can be mapped to a fixed rejecting string separately.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A polynomial decision budget would support witness search]
Let $D$ be any total decider for the fixed language, and let
$T_D^{\le}(M)$ denote its worst runtime over strings of length at most $M$.
A uniform program using $D$ returns a valid witness on every YES instance,
and reports NO on every NO instance, within
\[
        (3h+1)T_D^{\le}(1369N)+P(N)
\]
native subroutine work, up to a fixed multiplicative constant depending on $D$,
where query construction, input access and verification are covered by a
fixed polynomial overhead $P$. A generic universal interpreter may add
polynomial simulation overhead; it leaves the qualitative conclusion unchanged.
If $D$ is polynomial, this procedure
is polynomial. The unknown exponent is not contracted or contradicted.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Ask $D$ whether $I$ is satisfiable. On a YES instance, fix one vertex at a
time, trying its three colors through Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}. At least one
choice preserves extendability. After at most $3h$ such queries every vertex
is fixed, and the resulting coloring is checked on the original input.
All queries are reconstructed from the same $I$, not from successively
expanded encodings of previous queries. Include the original call and charge
the polynomially many construction and output steps.
\end{proof}

For a native implementation with $T_D^{\le}(N)\le C(N+2)^k$, the displayed
budget is polynomial, for example $O((N+2)^{k+1/2})+P(N)$ because
$h\le\sqrt N$. This is an algorithmic implication, not a proposed numerical
bound for the desired impossibility theorem. Under $P=NP$ it is exactly the
kind of alternative a valid contradiction must exclude, rather than
assuming every solver follows recursive cofactor expansion.

\section{One current proof chain and one next assignment}
The current chain is
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 L_{\rm TT}\text{ is a fixed NP-complete language},\\
 D\text{ decides }L_{\rm TT}\text{ in P}
 \Longrightarrow\text{all pin queries and witness search are polynomial},\\
 \text{canonical pin queries}\Longleftrightarrow
       \text{source list-coloring with two independent hub masks}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
To derive a contradiction, one still must exclude every polynomial decider
for that semantic problem, not just an expansion-based implementation. This
can be qualitative; no explicit superpolynomial rate is required. The current
construction does not supply that exclusion.

The separate UWI benchmark
$H(F)=\min_{|p|\le b_0+\lfloor\log_2(|F|+2)\rfloor}(t_p(F)+q(|F|)+1)$
and the sufficient growing-factor condition remain conditional. Source
recovery and finite mask changes provide no established inequality
$H(F_{j+1})\ge H(F_j)r(s_j)/B$ with unbounded net gain. A direct fixed-language
non-P proof needs no further broadening or mandatory universal compiler.
