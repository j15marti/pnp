\section*{Exact model and clean core}
Let $\langle F\rangle$ be a self-delimiting encoding of a 3-CNF $F$, with
$N=|\langle F\rangle|$.  A uniform deterministic exact-clock decider $A$ has a
polynomial $Q_A$ and realized mutable-state trace
\[
  \operatorname{Tr}_A(F)=(\mu_0,\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_{Q_A(N)}),
  \qquad Q_A(N)\le N^{k_A}.
\]
Padding after halting is included in the realization, so every checkpoint has
an unambiguous integer index.  For a normalized schedule
$C\subseteq\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]$, define
\[
  \operatorname{Obs}_{A,C}(F)
   =\bigl(\mu_{\lfloor cQ_A(N)\rfloor}:c\in C\bigr).
\]
The semantic compatibility object remains
\[
  H(F,\alpha)=\{\beta:\alpha\cup\beta\models F\},
\]
or a project-specified quotient of it.  A valid universal transfer must prove,
with no hidden access to $F$ outside the realized observation, that one
polynomial-time extractor $E_A$ satisfies
\[
  E_A(\operatorname{Obs}_{A,C_A}(F))=H(F,\alpha)
  \quad\text{for every encoded }F\text{ and relevant }\alpha,
\]
with all schedule-generation, observation, extraction, and reconstruction
costs charged.  No such theorem is known.

\section*{Theorem: OBSERVATION-INTERFACE-QUADRILEMMA1}
\textbf{Claim (\EXACT).}  For exact-clock uniform deterministic polynomial-time
deciders, any proposed checkpoint or challenge interface falls into at least
one of the following branches.

\begin{enumerate}
\item A fixed normalized cut is presentation-sensitive and can be moved into
an input-oblivious delay without changing the computed language or polynomial
class.
\item A fixed implementation-independent polynomial-cardinality normalized
schedule is likewise defeated by a polynomial wrapper that places all selected
cuts in input-oblivious regions.
\item If the schedule may depend on $A$ and have polynomial cardinality, then
\[
 C^{\mathrm{all}}_A(N)=
 \left\{\frac{t}{Q_A(N)}:0\le t\le Q_A(N)\right\}
\]
is legal, has $Q_A(N)+1$ entries of $O(\log Q_A(N))$ bits, is uniformly
generable in polynomial time, and satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Obs}_{A,C^{\mathrm{all}}_A}(F)
   =\operatorname{Tr}_A(F).
\]
Thus the schedule-selection problem collapses exactly to the full-trace
factorization problem; selecting checkpoints supplies no additional bridge.
\item For exogenous challenges, exact correctness for every challenge stream
permits fixing a uniformly constructible canonical stream.  Bounded-error
correctness yields a randomized endpoint.  A privileged nonuniform or
uncomputable stream yields an advice/oracle/environment endpoint.  None alone
is a lower bound against ordinary uniform deterministic SAT deciders.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Proof.}  Items 1 and 2 are the verified wrapper theorems in the current
LSPV-4 and LSPV-1 reports.  For item 3, exact-clock normalization maps every
integer $t$ to $t/Q_A(N)$; flooring returns $t$, so the observation tuple is the
trace itself.  Its explicit encoding costs
$O(Q_A(N)\log Q_A(N))$, polynomial for fixed $A$.  For item 4, choose the same
canonical challenge symbol or uniformly generated stream on every input.  An
algorithm correct for every stream remains correct on that one.  If correctness
is only probabilistic, the quantifier change is precisely a randomized
algorithm; if the stream is not uniformly generated, the machine receives an
external computational resource.  \hfill$\square$

\paragraph{What the theorem does not say.}
The full trace may contain many states while the decision output is one bit.
There is no inference from the number of semantic states, compatibility
classes, trace positions, or observed bits to running time, space, or circuit
size.  The theorem neither shows that $H$ factors through a trace nor that a
trace-dependent representation is expensive for arbitrary computation.

\section*{SAME\_REALIZATION and all-cost audit}
The surviving factorization must use the exact trace of the same execution that
decides $F$.  It may not splice a witness, proof, reduction transcript, or
alternate solver execution into the state.  Required costs are: parsing
$\langle F\rangle$; constructing $C_A$ or the challenge stream; serializing
every observed state; running $E_A$; reconstructing any compatibility object;
and translating that object into the model to which the lower bound applies.
For the all-step schedule, serialization alone can be
$\Theta(Q_A(N)S_A(N))$ bits.  This remains polynomial but is not free.

\section*{Barrier audits}
\paragraph{Relativization.}
Delay wrappers and canonical fixing relativize.  Therefore they expose why a
relativizing bridge cannot by itself settle P versus NP.  A successful final
arrow must state where it uses nonrelativizing structure.

\paragraph{Algebrization.}
No algebraic extension of a trace is supplied here.  Any algebraic invariant
must first be shown to arise from the same realized trace of every solver; the
current manuscripts do not provide that transfer.

\paragraph{Natural Proofs.}
The theorem is not a circuit lower bound and makes no largeness/usefulness
claim.  If the surviving trace property is converted into a circuit property,
constructivity, largeness, usefulness, and the applicable circuit class must
be audited before invoking it.

\paragraph{Uniformity and polynomial exponents.}
Quantifiers are $\forall A\,\exists k_A,Q_A$; no common exponent is assumed.
All schedules or challenge generators must be uniform relative to the fixed
program $A$, and program-specific finite constants are permitted only when
explicitly encoded in that fixed machine.
