\section{Models, mechanisms, and quantifiers}
The domain is $\mathbb Z_3$. An instance has variables $V$, affine records
\[
x_v=x_u+b_e\pmod 3
\]
and $p$ exceptional records carrying arbitrary explicit binary relations
$R_e\subseteq\mathbb Z_3^2$. Records use $w=\Theta(\log N)$-bit endpoint
fields and constant nine-bit relation tables. The deterministic word RAM has
word width $\Theta(\log N)$ and random source access. Parsing, relation-table
reads, affine contraction, failed branches, compatibility tests, replay,
output, and peak live space are charged.

Three falsifiable mechanisms were tested. (A) Contract the affine bulk and
kernelize only the exceptional relations; falsify by an instance whose
quotient needs more than $2p$ active roots. (B) Use the $p$ exception fields
as a query amplification; falsify superlinear amplification by a charged
source scan. (C) Identify a tractable-to-NP-complete ladder by increasing
$p$; falsify mandatory $3^p$ work by any polynomial shortcut on a proposed
family. Mechanism A survives as an upper bound; B is input-order only; C
gives a parameter boundary, not a lower bound.

\section{Exact contraction and kernel}
\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT: exception kernel]
Let $I$ contain $a$ affine records and $p$ arbitrary relation records.
There is an exact deterministic algorithm with
\[
T(I)=O\!\left(|V|+a+p+3^c p\right),\qquad c\le p,
\]
word operations and $O(|V|+a+p)$ peak words. After charged normalization,
the nontrivial kernel has at most $2p$ three-valued variables and $p$
binary relations. Its one-hot CNF uses at most $6p$ Boolean variables and
$23p$ clauses of width at most three. A constant-overhead gadget conversion
reaches ordinary proper 3CNF.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each affine connected component, a spanning forest assigns a potential
$\phi_v$ from a chosen root. Every nonforest affine record is checked against
the potentials. A failed check proves UNSAT; otherwise every source variable
has the unique form $x_v=y_C+\phi_v$.

Translate an exceptional relation on $(u,v)$ to a relation on their component
roots by subtracting the two potentials. If both endpoints lie in one
component, intersect the resulting diagonal with that root's three-element
domain. An empty table or domain proves UNSAT. All affine components
incident to no remaining exceptional record are satisfiable independently
and may be discarded for decision. Since every remaining relation has two
endpoints, at most $2p$ roots remain.

Choose one endpoint of every quotient relation and let $C$ be their union.
Then $C$ is a vertex cover and $c=|C|\le p$. Enumerate its $3^c$
assignments. Reject assignments violating a root domain or an internal
relation. The roots outside $C$ form an independent set, so each can be
tested independently by trying its three colors against incident relations.
This proves the time and space bounds.

For the CNF count, give each of $J\le2p$ roots three one-hot variables.
Exactly-one costs four clauses, a root domain costs at most three units, and a
binary table costs at most nine forbidden-pair clauses. Hence there are
$3J\le6p$ variables and at most $7J+9p\le23p$ clauses.
\end{proof}

This is a source-complete normalization: it reads and checks all affine and
exception records. If $p=O(\log N)$, the displayed algorithm is polynomial.
Unrestricted $p$ includes graph 3-coloring by using the inequality relation
$R_{\ne}=\{(a,b):a\ne b\}$ and no affine records. Graph 3-colorability is
NP-complete \cite{gjs}; the endpoint supplies no unconditional time lower
bound. The general $H$-coloring dichotomy likewise separates easy fixed
bipartite targets from NP-complete non-bipartite targets
\cite{hellnesetril}.

\section{Exact restricted lower bound and transport}
\begin{lemma}[PROVED/EXACT: $p$ exception-field queries]
There is an equal-metadata family with $p$ disjoint exceptional edges such
that every exact deterministic field-query algorithm reads all $p$ designated
relation bits on the all-one input.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For edge $i$, let its relation be $\{(0,0)\}$ when $b_i=1$ and empty when
$b_i=0$; all endpoint fields, table positions, and input lengths are fixed.
The conjunction is satisfiable iff every $b_i=1$. If an algorithm omits
field $j$ on the all-one input, flipping only $b_j$ gives an equal-metadata
UNSAT input with the same transcript. This is impossible for an exact
algorithm.
\end{proof}

The same family is decided by a single $O(p)$ relation-table scan using
$O(1)$ additional working words. Its fixed-width encoding has
$N=\Theta(p\log p)$ bits, so the lemma gives only
$\Omega(p)=\Omega(N/\log N)$ aligned-word acquisition. Under a paid
normalization or reduction,
\[
L_{\rm old}\le R+H+Q\tau T_{\rm new}^{\le}(g).
\]
Here $L_{\rm old}=p$, while reading or constructing the $p$ relation records
already permits $R+H=\Theta(p)$. The numerator need not be positive.
Direct inclusion preserves the input-order query bound but cannot amplify it.

\section{Cross-role reconciliation}
Role 1's CUTVERTEX68 exact critical-clause count gives another input-order
sensitive-field family, but affine parity recognition remains polynomial.
Role 2's WIDTH-CERT52 supplies matched large-cutset instances with constant
certified elimination width, showing that cutset size alone is not
continuation hardness. Role 3's QUANTIFIER-CERTIFICATE-BARRIER60 makes
all-completion validation $\Pi_2^P$-complete, but a one-input SAT decider is
not required to validate counterfactual completions. These results agree
with the present theorem: rich restricted certificates and large syntactic
parameters do not yield all-decider time lower bounds. Freuder's
bounded-width CSP algorithm is the relevant structural upper-bound precedent
\cite{freuder}.

\section{Proof spine and next lemma}
\begin{align*}
\underbrace{\text{nonfunctional count }p}_{\text{defined}}
&\longrightarrow
\underbrace{\text{charged }O(N+3^pp)\text{ algorithm}}_{\text{PROVED upper bound}},\\
&\not\Longrightarrow
\underbrace{3^{\Omega(p)}\text{ necessary}}_{\text{OPEN}}
\longrightarrow
\underbrace{\text{all-decider coverage}}_{\text{OPEN}},\\
&\hspace{7em}\longrightarrow
\underbrace{\text{superpolynomial contradiction}}_{\text{OPEN}}.
\end{align*}
The highest-value missing lemma is a source-aware theorem for one fixed
NP-complete relation language showing that every exact uniform deterministic
decider either constructs and validates a globally sufficient compression at
superpolynomial cost or performs superpolynomial continuation. It must
survive affine contraction, bounded-width/cutset dynamic programming, source
recognition, caching, alternate representations, and decide-first
normalization. No such theorem is proved here.
