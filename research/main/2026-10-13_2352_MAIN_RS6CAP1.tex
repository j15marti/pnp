\section{Fixed language and inherited premise}
Let $L_{22}$ consist of satisfiable encodings of nonempty proper,
distinct-clause signed exact-$(2,2)$ 3-CNF. Invalid and out-of-domain strings
are rejected. Encoded bit length is $N$, not variable count. Existing
NP-completeness and the two historically distinct compilers are preserved.
For this RS5 branch the compiler is the older selector/duplication map,
not the transparent graph-colouring map used by the other PC22 report.

Proof size $S$ means explicit line count. Depth $D$ counts dependency edges,
so $D<S$. In semantic Cutting Planes each line is an integer-affine inequality
entailed on Boolean assignments by at most two earlier lines, or an axiom.
Resolution embeds with the same line count by replacing clauses with their
Boolean inequalities; no algorithmic cost is inferred from this embedding.

For each fixed rational $p>1$ and $1<\beta<1+1/p$, retain the RS5 premise:
there are UNSAT $F_t$ in the valid exact-$(2,2)$ domain, $N_t\to\infty$, $r_t\ge1$,
and $a>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $t$,
\[
 \exists\pi_t\in\mathsf{Res}(F_t): S(\pi_t)\le r_t,
 \qquad
 \forall\tau\in\sCP(F_t):
 S(\tau)\le r_t^\beta\ \Longrightarrow\ D(\tau)\ge aN_t^p.       \tag{H}
\]
Constants and family may depend on the fixed pair. The source trade-off
and normalized spread inputs retain primary attribution \cite{tradeoff,spread}.

\section{The new exact implication}
\begin{lemma}[Cap removal]
Let $\mathcal P$ be a nonempty family of finite DAG proofs with size $S\ge1$ and $0\le D\le S$.
If some proof $\pi_0$ satisfies $S(\pi_0)\le R\le B$, and every proof of
size at most $B$ has depth at least $H\ge0$, then $B\ge H$ and every proof
in $\mathcal P$ has size at least $H$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$H\le D(\pi_0)\le S(\pi_0)\le R\le B$ calibrates the cap. A proof below
the cap has $S\ge D\ge H$; a proof above it has $S>B\ge H$.
Equivalently, a minimum-size proof lies below $R$ and hence below the cap.
\end{proof}
A same-model in-cap witness is essential. Without one, a system with a
shortest proof of size two and depth one satisfies the statement ``every
proof of size at most one has depth at least ten'' vacuously. A short proof
in a different system is not the required witness.

\begin{corollary}[Uncapped size conclusion]
Under (H), every semantic-CP refutation of $F_t$ has $S\ge aN_t^p$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use the Resolution upper proof as a semantic-CP witness, and set
$R=r_t$, $B=r_t^\beta$, $H=aN_t^p$. Since $r_t\ge1$, $R\le B$.
\end{proof}
Thus the bound is no longer restricted to proofs below the cap. This is
an elementary consequence, not a new general proof-complexity method.

If (H) holds for every admissible fixed pair, there is no one polynomial
bound on semantic-CP refutation length over the valid UNSAT domain of
$L_{22}$. For any proposed exponent $K$ choose an integer $p>\max(1,K)$
and $\beta=1+1/(2p)$; then $aN_t^p>CN_t^K$ for sufficiently large $t$.
The family is selected after the exponent, within one fixed language. This
preserves DIAGTRANSFER1's quantifier order without demanding one family for
all exponents.

\section{What it proves about algorithms, and what it does not}
\begin{theorem}[Explicit certifiers and a sufficient separation condition]
Assuming (H) for every admissible pair, no polynomial-time ordinary bit-machine
procedure can output valid explicit semantic-CP refutations on every valid
UNSAT $L_{22}$ input. Consequently, if every hypothetical polynomial $L_{22}$
decider $A$ admitted a correct external refutation generator $E_A$ with
\[
 T_{E_A}(N)=O_A((N+T_A(N)+1)^{q_A}),                         \tag{1}
\]
for finite $q_A$ independent of the later family parameter, then $L_{22}$
would not be in P.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Explicit output has at least one bit per line, so polynomial running time
would bound proof length by a fixed polynomial, contrary to the corollary.
If $A$ were polynomial, (1) would give precisely such a polynomial generator.
All source construction, calls, coefficients, decoding, output and replay
must be included in (1).
\end{proof}
This excludes a stronger output task, not a decision-only algorithm. The
remaining implication (1) is \textbf{OPEN}. A one-bit answer or a
polynomially checkable decider-indexed trace is not a proof in a particular
lower-bounded system \cite{cr}. No original-run exposure requirement is
imposed. For a purely existential contradiction, even a genuinely derived
uniform polynomial upper bound on semantic-CP proof existence would suffice;
that bound also is not obtained from decision correctness alone.

Compared with DIAGTRANSFER1, a separate size-cap guarantee, depth guarantee,
and favorable fixed exponent are unnecessary if total explicit extraction
is polynomial. This is the precise simplification, not closure of the
remaining separation-bearing premise.

polynomially many charged decision/witness queries. A proposed shortcut is
to use this critical-deletion table as the refutation. It fails, even on

Reuse the six-variable balanced component with edge sets
\[
 E=\{123,145,246,356\},\qquad
 J=\bigwedge_{e\in E}
 ((\bigvee_{i\in e}x_i)\land(\bigvee_{i\in e}\neg x_i)).
\]
It is proper, clause-distinct and exactly balanced. It is SAT, for example

The unchanged W1 control from CP22LIFT1 is UNSAT and also has a critical

\section{Why two other inexpensive-looking repairs do not supply (1)}
\paragraph{Bounded proof search.}
Given $(F,1^B)$, finding a semantic-CP proof of at most $B$ encoded bits is
polynomial in $N+B$ under $P=NP$: supplied-proof validity is in coNP, since
an invalid semantic inference has a short Boolean counterassignment; under
the hypothesis it is in P, and bounded existence/prefix search is then in NP=P.
For each fixed RS5 family, a Resolution upper proof has a polynomial bit
bound, but its exponent depends on the chosen family. Polynomial in $N+B$
is not a uniform polynomial in $N$. Binary encoding of $B$ does not preserve
the preceding polynomial-witness-length argument. No hypothetical oracle
was executed in the audit.

\paragraph{Compact descriptions.}
Polynomial encoding followed by polynomial \emph{total} expansion into the
explicit proof would also be excluded. Polynomial access to a requested
line is weaker. Prepending $R$ repetitions of an axiom to a fixed proof,
and shifting parent indices, gives a descriptor of length $O(\log R)$ plus
the fixed proof, while expansion costs at least $R$ output steps. This is
an easy control, not a claim that hard RS5 proofs are succinctly represented.
