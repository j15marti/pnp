\section{Claim card and falsifiable mechanisms}
\textbf{Desired unrestricted conclusion:} for every total uniform deterministic exact decider $A$ of one fixed canonically encoded NP-complete SAT language and every $c>0,N_0$, some input $F$ of bit length $N\ge N_0$ has $T_A(F)>N^c$.  The machine is a word RAM with word width $w=\Theta(\log N)$ and charged addressed-word reads/writes, preprocessing, failed searches, reconstruction, verification, output and peak live space.

Three mechanisms were preregistered.  (I) amplify Role~4's one-variable strong-2CNF backdoor by disjoint products, testing whether minimum backdoor size becomes a universal continuation obstruction; (II) combine Role~3's pointwise differential coverage with the product; (III) transport the original implication-chain query bound through an explicitly materialized proper-3CNF reduction and inspect the positive remainder.  Mechanism~I is falsified as a lower-bound proposal, II gives only input-order coverage, and III has nonpositive remainder.  These are exact counterexamples to proposed amplifications, not evidence for $P=NP$.

\section{Exact backdoor characterization}
For a proper 3-CNF $F$, let $\mathcal S(F)$ be the set system whose members are the three source-variable supports of its clauses, and let $h(F)=\tau(\mathcal S(F))$ be its transversal number.

\begin{lemma}[Syntactic strong-backdoor identity; PROVED/EXACT]
A variable set $B$ is a strong backdoor of $F$ into syntactic 2-CNF iff $B$ meets every ternary clause support.  Consequently the minimum strong-2CNF backdoor size is exactly $h(F)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $B$ meets every support, any total assignment to $B$ either satisfies a clause or removes at least one literal, leaving width at most two.  Conversely, if some ternary support misses $B$, choose any assignment to $B$; that untouched proper clause remains ternary, so the residual is not syntactic 2-CNF.
\end{proof}
Thus $h$ yields a charged algorithmic upper bound: ternary search discovers a size-$h$ transversal in $O(3^hN)$ time, and $2^h$ assignments followed by linear 2-SAT decide.  This is not a matching lower bound.

\section{The disjoint-product firewall}
For $p\ge1,q\ge3$, take $p$ variable-disjoint copies $H_{q,\sigma^{(1)}},\ldots,H_{q,\sigma^{(p)}}$ and let
\[
P_{p,q,\Sigma}=\bigwedge_{a=1}^p H_{q,\sigma^{(a)}}.
\]
This is a proper 3-CNF with
\[
n=p(q+4),\qquad m=k=p(2q+4),\qquad
N=(8pq+16p+2)w,
\]
where the fixed-record encoding uses two header words and four words per clause and
$w=\lceil\log_2(64(n+m+1))\rceil$.

\begin{theorem}[Linear backdoor, linear exact decision; PROVED/EXACT]
For every $p,q,\Sigma$,
\[
\#\mathrm{SAT}(P_{p,q,\Sigma})=
\begin{cases}
16^p,&\bigoplus_i\sigma^{(a)}_i=0\text{ for every }a,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad h(P_{p,q,\Sigma})=p.
\]
Nevertheless a uniform exact recognizer and parity scan decides the entire family in $O(pq)$ word operations and $O(p+q)$ streamed workspace, including grammar validation and output.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Model counts multiply over disjoint components.  Choosing each private switch $s_a$ hits every clause, so $h\le p$.  Because the source-variable sets of the $p$ nonempty clause systems are disjoint, every hitting set contains at least one variable from each, hence $h\ge p$.  The recognizer groups clauses by their private switch, validates all literal identities and signs, XORs each component's $q$ orientation bits, and accepts iff all component parities are even.  Every input record is charged once; no witness enumeration occurs.
\end{proof}

Taking fixed $q=3$ gives $h=p=n/7=\Theta(N/\log N)$ while exact decision remains $O(N/\log N)$ word operations.  Hence even a linear \emph{minimum} strong-2CNF backdoor does not force $2^{\Omega(h)}$ work by arbitrary deciders.  Large $k$, clause criticality, absence of unit/pure/tautological clauses and large syntactic residual are likewise insufficient: Role~4 already supplies all of them for one component.

\section{All-algorithm coverage and exact transport failure}
On an all-even product member, flipping any one of the $pq$ orientations changes SAT to UNSAT while changing a disjoint pair of signed-literal fields.  Role~3's pointwise differential theorem therefore forces every deterministic exact decider, on that member, to read at least one field from every pair: $pq=\Omega(N/\log N)$ addressed input words.  The direct parity scan matches this order.  This is an unconditional all-decider \emph{input-observation} lower bound in the declared encoding, not a post-acquisition time lower bound.

For a paid materializing reduction from $pq$ independent chain-orientation fields to $P_{p,q,\Sigma}$, even the weak record-write charge is
\[
R(p,q)\ge m=p(2q+4)>pq=L_{\rm old}(p,q).
\]
Thus the compatible-model transport inequality
\[
L_{\rm old}\le R+H+Q\tau T_{\rm new}^{\le}(g)
\]
has nonpositive remainder before target simulation:
$(L_{\rm old}-R-H)/(Q\tau)<0$.  It transfers no lower bound.  Direct inclusion into full proper-3CNF SAT preserves the $pq$ input-query bound without materialization, but cannot increase its order.

\section{Status and single next lemma}
\textbf{PROVED/EXACT:} the backdoor/hitting-set identity, product model count, $h=p$, linear recognizer, pointwise $pq$ field coverage, and negative materialization margin.  \textbf{KNOWN THEOREM:} linear 2-SAT, FPT strong-backdoor discovery, NP-completeness of unrestricted 3-SAT and exact-$(2,2)$ monotone 3-SAT.  \textbf{RETIRED:} $k$, $h$, clause criticality or disjoint-product amplification as universal SAT-work lower bounds.  \textbf{OPEN:} every unrestricted deterministic post-acquisition lower-bound arrow and $P\ne NP$.

The highest-value missing lemma is now narrower: exhibit an independently specified, non-product semantic obstruction $r(F)$ and prove, for \emph{every} uniform exact decider on one fixed NP-complete encoding, that paid acquisition of a representation eliminating the obstruction or residual continuation costs $2^{\Omega(r(F))}/N^{O(1)}$, while $r(F)/\log N$ is unbounded on suitable inputs.  The lemma must survive this product family, 2-SAT, affine/Horn shortcuts, separators, backdoors, learned constraints, caching, source-aware recognition and decide-first normalization.  No such lemma is known here.
