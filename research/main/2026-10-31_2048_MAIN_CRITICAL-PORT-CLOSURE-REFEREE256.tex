\section{Exact obstruction and claim cards}

The canonical language is D\"ocker's duplicate-free proper Monotone
3-SAT-$(2,2)$: every clause has three distinct variables of one sign, clauses
are unique, each variable occurs exactly twice per polarity, and malformed
strings reject.  The exact obstruction in this run is:

\begin{quote}
Can a known critical-clause enforcer, whose three missing port literals are
forced false, close the signed occurrence deficits of a one-variable raw
cofactor and return a smaller, pointwise equivalent valid target child?
\end{quote}

The answer is negative for the minimum one- and two-copy architecture defined
below.  It is not a no-go for larger gadgets, source-specific correlations,
or global re-encodings.

\paragraph{F1.} A minimum one-copy critical-port closure has exactly the raw
two-binary free-boundary relation.  \textbf{FALSIFIED} by all 78 labelled
proper slot layouts.

\paragraph{F2.} Two critical copies repair the one-copy defect without
changing the free-boundary relation.  \textbf{FALSIFIED} by all 2,580 labelled
proper slot layouts.

\paragraph{F3.} A closed six-variable target formula can force a unary or
binary pin.  \textbf{FALSIFIED/COMPUTATIONAL} by the complete $75^2=5,625$
catalogue; every one- and two-pin valuation extends.

\paragraph{F4.} Small logical XOR or endpoint delta supplies an
arbitrary-machine runtime lower bound.  \textbf{FALSIFIED}: lazy flags,
changed-then-restored cells, source rereads, DELAY and omitted tracing are
explicit controls.

\section{The critical source and signed deficit equation}

Let $U_{198}$ be the published 198-variable, 264-clause target-valid UNSAT
formula.  Its zero-based clause~1 is
\[
 C=(x_{194}\lor x_{196}\lor x_{197}).
\]
Let $D=U_{198}\setminus\{C\}$.  An independently replayed 198-bit witness
satisfies all 263 clauses of $D$ and falsifies all three literals of $C$.
Because $U_{198}=D\land C$ is UNSAT,
\[
 D\models \neg x_{194}\land\neg x_{196}\land\neg x_{197}.
\tag{1}
\]
Thus $D$ is an exact three-port critical gadget: each missing positive port
literal is forced false, and the polarity-reversed copy supplies the analogous
negative gadget.

Start from a one-variable raw cofactor.  It has four missing occurrences of
the selected polarity and two binary clauses of the opposite polarity.  Add
$k$ copies of $D$, oriented so their $3k$ missing ports have the binary-clause
polarity, and add $q$ fresh balanced variables and $c$ new ternary clauses.
Padding the two binaries contributes two slots.  Exact target balance is
therefore
\[
 2+3c=4+3k+4q,
 \qquad \boxed{3c-4q=2+3k}.                         \tag{2}
\]
For both $k=1$ and $k=2$, the smallest nonnegative solution has $q=1$;
respectively $(q,c)=(1,3)$ and $(1,4)$.

\section{Free-boundary closure theorem}

Use four independent truth bits $o_1,\ldots,o_4$ for the selected-polarity
deficit literals and four independent bits $b_1,\ldots,b_4$ for the two raw
binary clauses.  The raw local relation is
\[
 R_{\rm raw}(o,b)=(b_1\lor b_2)\land(b_3\lor b_4),
\qquad |R_{\rm raw}|=16\cdot 3^2=144.              \tag{3}
\]
At the minimum, the fresh variable $z$ appears twice in each polarity.  The
selected-polarity slots form two proper clauses from
$\{o_1,o_2,o_3,o_4,z,z\}$.  The opposite-polarity slots consist of $3k$
forced-false ports and two occurrences of $\neg z$; two labelled slots pad the
raw binaries, and every remaining slot is partitioned into proper monotone
triples.  Internal gadget variables are existentially quantified.

\begin{theorem}[Minimum critical-port closure firewall]
For every proper labelled slot layout in the preceding architecture:
\begin{enumerate}
\item if $k=1$, the projected closure relation is either empty or has 108
boundary models, with 63 raw-only and 27 closure-only models;
\item if $k=2$, the projected closure relation is either empty or is a strict
81-model subset of $R_{\rm raw}$, missing 63 raw models;
\item consequently no minimum one- or two-copy layout is pointwise equal to
$R_{\rm raw}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $k=1$, if both $z$ occurrences pad the binaries, the remaining negative
clause consists of three forced-false ports and the projection is empty.  If
one $z$ and one port pad the binaries, the remaining negative clause contains
$\neg z$ and two false ports, forcing $\neg z$.  One raw binary is then made
vacuous, while the two positive clauses reduce to a pairing of the four $o_i$;
this both admits boundary rows excluded by (3) and rejects rows admitted by
(3).  If neither $z$ occurrence pads a binary, the remaining negative triple
repeats $z$ and is not proper.

For $k=2$, using either $z$ occurrence as a pad leaves at least one negative
clause made solely of forced-false ports, so the projection is empty.  If both
binaries are padded by forced-false ports, properness places one $\neg z$ in
each remaining negative clause.  Hence $\neg z$ is forced, both positive
clauses reduce to a pairing of $o_1,\ldots,o_4$, and the projection is (3)
conjoined with two extra binary constraints.  It is a strict subset.

The executable enumeration independently checks every labelled occurrence
placement and every one of the 256 free-boundary rows.  It finds 78 labelled
$k=1$ layouts: 6 empty and 72 of the 108-model type.  It finds 2,580 labelled
$k=2$ layouts: 1,500 empty and 1,080 of the 81-model type.  Zero relations are
equal to (3).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope.}
Free and distinct boundary bits are the correct test for a
source-independent local replacement identity.  Formula-specific equalities
could make a nonidentity layout preserve a particular SAT label.  The theorem
therefore does not rule out source-aware closures, $k\ge3$, different gadgets,
or global re-encoding.  None of those escapes is presently proved hereditary,
power-short and fully charged.

\section{XOR adjunct and physical-model red team}

At each recorded assignment checkpoint, the experiment compares the complete
declared source-indexed logical state: assignment bits plus two aligned clause
count bits and one SAT-status bit per original clause.  It records every
changed cell ID, adjacent $d_{\rm logic}$, cumulative adjacent delta and
endpoint delta under binary and Gray count encodings.  Repeated flips give a
changed-then-restored control: cumulative activity is positive although the
restored fields have zero endpoint delta.

No native fixed-tape SAT machine was executed.  Therefore actual
$d_{\rm phys}$, including head positions, finite control, index tapes, shadow
states and variable-length records, is reported as \emph{NOT MEASURED}.  The
eager fixed-cell logical vector and lazy assignment-only vector are offline
declared realizations, not universal machine traces.  A 100-step DELAY loop
and 1,000 source rereads have zero data-bit changes but positive work.  Thus
neither $d_{\rm logic}$ nor an endpoint XOR proves universal hardness.  If a
future fixed $k$-tape native trace is supplied, the exact interval inequality
$T\ge\sum d_{\rm phys}/k$ applies only after all physical cells and disjoint
checkpoint intervals are declared.
