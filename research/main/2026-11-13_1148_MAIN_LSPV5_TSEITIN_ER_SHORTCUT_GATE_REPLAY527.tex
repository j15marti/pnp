\section{Theorem 527.1 (native /386 XOR-chain extension refutations)}
Let $G$ be a connected cubic graph with $V$ vertices and $E=3V/2$ edges and let the charge be odd.
Write $\mathrm{xor}(a,b)=\neg g$ with paid gates $u=a\wedge\neg b$, $w=\neg a\wedge b$,
$g=\neg u\wedge\neg w$. Order the vertices $v_1,\dots,v_V$ and let $C_j$ be the cut of
$\{v_1,\dots,v_j\}$. Let $c_j$ be the last literal of the sorted XOR chain over $C_j$ (gates memoised,
so equal prefixes share gates). Then /386 admits a refutation as follows:
(i) resolve each of the $4V$ guarded source clauses with the guard unit;
(ii) for each $j$ and edge position $x$, derive the clauses of
$B_x\oplus A_x\oplus D_x=0$ (new prefix, old prefix, edges of $v_j$ at positions $\le x$) from the
previous invariant and the true DEF views of at most two XOR steps;
(iii) combine the final invariant, the unit $c_{j-1}=Q_{j-1}$ and the four clauses of $v_j$ into
the unit $c_j=Q_j$;
(iv) at $j=V$ the cut is empty and $Q_V=1$, so step (iii) yields the empty clause.

\textbf{Proof.} Each derived clause set is a semantic consequence of its premises: the DEF views are
the defining clauses of the gates, the invariant is a GF(2) identity, and the vertex clauses state
the vertex parity. Each step involves $O(1)$ variables (at most four chain literals, two gate
internals per XOR, the new edge and at most three vertex edges). By implicational completeness of
tree Resolution, a DPLL tree over these variables under the assignment falsifying a target clause
compiles into strict non-tautological RES steps deriving a subclause of the target, which is
sufficient for every later step. Hence each of the $O(V\cdot E)$ steps costs $O(1)$ RES, and at
most $3E$ new gates are created per vertex. Every gate is declared once and charged once.
Therefore $W_{386}(\mathrm{Ts}(G,\text{odd}))=O(V\cdot E)=O(V^2)$. \hfill$\square$

This is the native form of a \textbf{KNOWN THEOREM}: Tseitin formulas have polynomial Frege
(hence Extended Resolution) refutations, while Resolution needs $2^{\Omega(n)}$ on bounded-degree
expanders (Urquhart 1987; Ben-Sasson--Wigderson 2001). The increment here is the executable
compiler to the \emph{original} /386 accepted-byte grammar and its consequence for the project's
hard-family ledger.

\subsection*{Consequences and scope}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED (restricted):} odd cubic(-expander) Tseitin as a B1 hard family for
the /386 calculus, or for any R\_mix341 reconstruction that contains /386-style nested DEF gates
(or GF(2)/XOR rules). Resolution-only hardness does not transfer.
\item The DPLL compiler of LSPV-2/604 is a correct \emph{upper} bound $W_R\le 1+I_{\mathrm{DPLL}}$
for DPLL-shaped algorithms; on Tseitin its exponential growth reflects the algorithm, not
$\min_\pi\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi)$. Free optimal proof choice (the user's minimisation) selects the
XOR-chain proof.
\item Any candidate hard family for B1 in /386 must defeat Extended Resolution; no superpolynomial
ER lower bound is known for any family. This places B1-for-/386 at the open frontier of proof
complexity, not at a missing routine lemma.
\item Not a SAT runtime bound, not B2/B3, not a statement about the unrecovered full R\_mix341
dispatcher. Byte length (up to 2.58\,MB), checker time and memory are separate ledgers.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Bridge audit (adopted, not new)}
The corpus already records that any universal B3 schema for a class containing ordinary Resolution
is logically equivalent to P$\ne$NP (unconditional Resolution lower bounds make the bridge carry the
entire separation), and that pointwise transfer $W_R(F)\le\mathrm{poly}(|F|+T_A(F))$ is refuted by
recognise-then-solve deciders. Theorem 527.1 adds the converse pressure: for a calculus strong
enough to make the bridge plausible (nested extension gates), known hard families evaporate. The
seesaw is the Cook--Reckhow programme in miniature; neither side is closed.
