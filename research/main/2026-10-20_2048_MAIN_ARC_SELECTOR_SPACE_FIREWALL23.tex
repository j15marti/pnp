\section*{Exact restricted theorem}
Fix the complete basis of all 16 binary Boolean gates.  For $n\geq 7$, let
$q=2^n$ and $s=\lfloor q/(16n)\rfloor$.

\paragraph{Counting lemma.}
There exists $y\in\{0,1\}^q$ not computed by any $n$-input, one-output circuit
with at most $s$ gates.

\paragraph{Proof.}
A circuit with at most $s$ gates has at most
\[
(s+1)(n+s)\bigl(16(n+s)^2\bigr)^s
\]
encodings: choose the gate count, two ordered predecessors and one of 16 gates
at each position, then the output.  Since $n+s\leq q$ and $s+1\leq q$, the
base-two logarithm is at most
\[
2n+s(4+2n)\leq 2n+\frac{q(4+2n)}{16n}<q.
\]
The final inequality is $32n^2<q(14n-4)$; it holds at $n=7$ and is preserved
by induction.  Hence fewer than $2^q$ truth tables are computed. \hfill$\Box$

For such a $y$, create variables $x_i,a_i,b_i$ for $i\in\{0,1\}^n$.  If
$y_i=1$, put $\ell_i=x_i$; otherwise put $\ell_i=\neg x_i$.  Add the four
proper clauses
\[
(\ell_i\vee \sigma a_i\vee \tau b_i),
\qquad \sigma,\tau\in\{+,-\}.
\]
Their conjunction is satisfiable exactly when $x_i=y_i$; once that holds,
$a_i,b_i$ are free.  Thus every satisfying assignment has designated vector
$x=y$.

Apply the peer Horn/XOR map to every source clause
$C=(\ell_1\vee\ell_2\vee\ell_3)$: introduce $z_j$ with
\[
z_j=x_{|\ell_j|}\oplus[\ell_j\text{ is positive}]
\]
and the negative-Horn clause
$\neg z_1\vee\neg z_2\vee\neg z_3$.  The Horn factor is satisfied by all
$z=0$; the XOR factor is satisfied by $x=0$ and the corresponding sign bits;
their conjunction is equivalent to $C$.  Therefore both factors are separately
satisfiable, their conjunction is exact, and every circuit selecting the
designated shared variable $x_i$ from index $i$ computes $y$ and has more than
$s$ gates.

Use a fixed binary proper-3CNF encoding and pad by duplicating existing clauses.
Set
\[
N_n=2^{\left\lceil\frac53(n+\log_2 n+10)\right\rceil}.
\]
The unpadded formula and both factors have $O(qn)$ bits, safely below $N_n$,
while
\[
s=\Omega(q/n)=\Omega(N_n^{3/5}/n^2)>N_n^{1/2}
\]
for all sufficiently large $n$.  Consequently, a repair that \emph{must
materialize an ordinary selector circuit} for the shared designated assignment
cannot in general meet the square-root live-space budget.

\paragraph{Strongest falsifier and limitation.}
The hard $y$ is explicitly present in this source, and the forcing instances
are easy.  A random-access machine can read $y_i$ directly; a streaming machine
can emit it; an algorithm need not materialize any circuit.  Thus the theorem
refutes only the inference
\[
(\forall i\ \exists w_i)\Longrightarrow
(\exists\text{ square-root-size materialized selector }W\ \forall i).
\]
It gives no unrestricted SAT runtime lower bound, no total ordinary $R_k$, and
no all-input ARC contractor.  Communication, circuit-counting, and compactness
language is therefore only a structural analogy unless source access and
machine simulation are separately charged.
