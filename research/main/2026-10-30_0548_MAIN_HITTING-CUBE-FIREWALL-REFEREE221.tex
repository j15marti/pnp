\section{Falsifiable target and answer}
The missing lemma tested in this run was: \emph{can the source-clause extreme
compatibility graph itself support a polynomially recognizable complete shortcut,
and, if so, where exactly does that shortcut stop?}  We tested four mechanisms:
(i) pairwise disjoint falsifying cubes under a complete opposite-sign conflict
graph; (ii) bounded-middle/conflict-cover promotion; (iii) endpoint and complement
survival signatures; and (iv) recognized affine, 2SAT and Horn controls.

The answer is exact.  Mechanism (i) is the classical hitting-formula theorem and
does give polynomial decision and model counting on that recognized class.
Mechanisms (ii)--(iii) do not extend it: a seven-clause complete conflict graph can
have both $0^n$ and $1^n$ fail and still have a unique model.  Mechanism (iv)
confirms that XOR/Tseitin and 2SAT shortcuts survive independently of the hitting
representation.

\section{Original clause-indexed extreme experiment}
Let original clause $C_j=(\ell_{1j}\vee\ell_{2j}\vee\ell_{3j})$ have distinct
variables.  The signed occurrence $x_{i,j}$ denotes the occurrence of the one
global source variable $x_i$, not a fresh Boolean variable.  Its $S_j$ target is
the value making its literal true; its $U_j$ target is the value making its literal
false.  Thus inversion swaps $S_j$ and $U_j$.  Ordinary clause satisfaction is
$t_j\ge1$, whereas $S_j$ means $t_j=3$.

For each distinct source pair $(C_j,C_k)$, the full matrix was constructed and
checked by coherent enumeration:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 &S_k&U_k\\\hline
S_j&\text{feasible iff no opposite-polarity shared variable}
    &\text{feasible iff no same-polarity shared variable}\\
U_j&\text{feasible iff no same-polarity shared variable}
    &\text{feasible iff no opposite-polarity shared variable}.
\end{array}
\]
Every raw occurrence record contains the literal sign, $S_j$, inverted $S_j=U_j$,
$U_j$, and inverted $U_j=S_j$.  For each source we also recorded coherent $0^n$,
$1^n$, complement pairs, forward and reverse single/multiple-flip trails, exact
per-clause truth vectors and $N_0,N_1,N_2,N_3$.  The checker replays

t_j(\bar a)=3-t_j(a),\qquad
t_j(a\oplus H)-t_j(a)=|H\cap L_j^0(a)|-|H\cap L_j^1(a)|.

\section{Exact restricted theorem and constructive algorithm}
Call a CNF \emph{hitting} when every two distinct clauses clash: they contain some
global variable with opposite signs.  Let $U_C$ be the complete-assignment cube
falsifying $C$.

\begin{theorem}[Iwama model count; independently rederived]
For a hitting CNF $F$ on $n$ variables,
\[
\#\mathrm{SAT}(F)=2^n\left(1-\sum_{C\in F}2^{-|C|}\right).
\]
Consequently $F$ is satisfiable exactly when the displayed Kraft sum is below one.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If a complete assignment falsified distinct $C,D$, it would have to falsify both
opposite literals of a clash variable, impossible.  Hence the cubes $U_C$ are
pairwise disjoint and have sizes $2^{n-|C|}$.  Their disjoint union is exactly the
set of assignments rejected by $F$, giving the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Proper width-three hitting firewall]
A hitting proper 3CNF has at most eight clauses and is UNSAT exactly when it has
eight clauses.  Recognition, exact model counting and decision take polynomial
time in the explicit source size.  A witness can also be constructed in polynomial
time by self-reduction.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every clause contributes $1/8$ to the Kraft sum.  Nonnegative model count gives
$m\le8$, and the theorem gives UNSAT iff $m=8$.  Hitting is checked by scanning
all clause pairs.  Under restriction, any two surviving clauses retain an
unassigned clash: assigning a clash variable satisfies and removes one parent.
Thus the restricted source is hitting.  At each variable, compute the exact model
count in both restrictions and keep a nonzero branch.  Rational arithmetic has
polynomial bit length because all denominators are powers of two bounded by the
number of remaining variables.
\end{proof}
This proof charges recognition and self-reduction.  It does not claim that a
generic solver is forced to construct a conflict graph, nor that a non-hitting
formula is hard.

\section{Sharp hostile controls}
On variables $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ take the eight clauses, one falsifying each of the
eight assignments.  The resulting $H_8$ is hitting and UNSAT.  Remove only the
clause falsified by $001$.  The resulting $H_7$ remains hitting, its conflict
graph is $K_7$, both $000$ and $111$ fail, yet $001$ is its unique model.  At that
model
\[
(N_0,N_1,N_2,N_3)=(0,3,3,1),
\]
so six clauses are in middle states.  This attains the $K_7$ vertex-cover lower
bound and proves simultaneously:
\begin{itemize}
\item complete source conflict and failure of both endpoints do not imply UNSAT;
\item a bounded-middle controller with $k\le5$ returns NO on a SAT source;
\item the eighth disjoint false cube, not clique size alone, is the decisive fact.
\end{itemize}
A duplicate-clause copy of $H_7$ is not hitting (the duplicate pair does not
clash) and stays SAT, explicitly testing the distinct-source-clause hypothesis.

The even three-variable XOR encoding is a hitting $K_4$ with four models.  The
odd-charge $K_4$ Tseitin source has 16 proper clauses and is rejected by the
hitting shortcut; Gaussian elimination refutes it immediately, so algebraic
structure remains an independent easy control.  The padded contradictory 2SAT
source happens to be hitting with eight clauses and is also detected by the exact
count.  Horn, dense planted SAT, strict22 W1 UNSAT and strict22 SWAP6 SAT exercise
the complete fallback.
