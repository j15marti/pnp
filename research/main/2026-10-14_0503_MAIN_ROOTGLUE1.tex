\section{Claim card and exact input model}
The single candidate attacked is the purported inference from polynomial-time
selection of every individual univariate root to polynomial-time solution of
their shared-variable conjunction. We replace it by an exact normal form and
an explicit positive/negative control.

An instance has Boolean unknowns $z\in\F_2^n$, optional affine equations
$Ez=e$, and constraints
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general}
 p_i(M_i z+a_i)=0\quad\text{in }K_i=\F_2[T]/(f_i),\qquad 1\le i\le t.
\end{equation}
Here $f_i$ is an explicit irreducible polynomial of degree $d_i\ge1$;
$p_i$ is a dense univariate polynomial over $K_i$ of declared degree at most
$m_i$; and $M_i\in\F_2^{d_i\times n}$, $a_i\in\F_2^{d_i}$ are explicit.
All variables, including variables left free by the affine part, are charged.
The machine is a single deterministic uniform bit machine with polynomial
workspace and full read access. There are no root, SAT, nonresidue, or advice
oracles.

For definiteness one total bit encoding writes nonnegative integer headers
as $1^k0$: $n$, the number of rows of $E$, those rows and right-hand sides,
then $t$. For each constraint it writes $d_i$, the $d_i+1$ modulus
coefficients, $m_i$, $(m_i+1)d_i$ polynomial coefficient bits, $d_i n$ matrix
bits and $d_i$ offset bits, all in declared increasing-coordinate order.
Require degree-$d_i$ monic irreducible moduli, legal lengths, and no trailing
bits. Invalid records reject. Unary $n$ prevents uncharged exponentially many
unused variables. This is a precise polynomial-size encoding, not a claim
that the general record parser was implemented in the experiment.

The quantifier is: one algorithm works for every such valid record in the
stated tractable subclass. The shared realization is the same parsed record,
root lists, Boolean vector $z$, affine solution, and reconstructed edge
witness. A selector may choose any valid witness, not the author's original
root or trace.

\cite{shoup}. Its trace/separating-set mechanism is directly model-matched.
Schaefer's affine constraint tractability, as discussed and refined by
Allender et al.\ \cite{allender}, is the relevant compatibility comparison.
Graph 3-colourability is NP-complete; Gasarch gives a primary reduction paper
and identifies the earlier Stockmeyer/Lovasz attribution \cite{gasarch}.
No cryptographic, ETH, or unproved general circuit premise is used.

TRACEISO1 was read as an actual source report, together with its root-isolation
implementation. Here is the independent proof check. For nonzero $p$, compute
\[
 g=\gcd(p,T^{2^d}-T)
\]
by $d$ modular squarings. The polynomial $g$ is squarefree and its roots are
exactly the field roots of $p$. For every basis element $\beta_j$ compute
\[
 h_j(T)=\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}(\beta_j T)^{2^k}\pmod g.
\]
At a root $r$, this evaluates to $\operatorname{Tr}(\beta_jr)\in\F_2$.
Retain $\gcd(g,h_j)$ if nonconstant, otherwise retain $g$. The retained root
set stays nonempty and agrees on each processed trace coordinate.
The trace pairing is nondegenerate: the nonzero trace polynomial has degree
less than $2^d$ and cannot vanish everywhere; multiplication by a nonzero
field element permutes the field. Consequently two distinct roots cannot
agree on every basis trace coordinate. After at most $d$ coordinates one
linear factor remains. Return its root and verify it against the original
polynomial. The zero polynomial returns zero and a nonzero constant rejects.
All polynomial operations have polynomial degree and coefficient length.

This verifies the mathematical root-isolation theorem independently of
TRACEISO1's author. Status: \EXACT{}. We do not promote every parser claim,
measured implementation cost, hidden-ANF recognition claim, or the particular
coarse bit-time exponent in that report. The new implementation below is a
separate synthesis implementation; the old 26,359-assertion suite was read as

\section{Complete root lists and affine compatibility}
\begin{theorem}[Exact root-list normal form]\label{thm:lists}
For every instance of \eqref{eq:general}, one can compute in deterministic
polynomial time its equivalent constraints
\[
 Ez=e,\qquad M_i z+a_i\in S_i,
\]
where $S_i$ is the complete list of distinct roots of $p_i$ in $K_i$.
A zero polynomial is represented by an unconstrained-domain flag instead of
an exponentially long field list. A nonzero polynomial with no roots makes
the conjunction unsatisfiable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For nonzero $p_i$, its field-root factor $g_i$ above has at most $m_i$ roots.
Isolate one root, divide $g_i$ exactly by its linear factor, and repeat.
Squarefreeness ensures that this enumerates each root once and stops after
at most $m_i$ iterations. Each iteration has polynomial cost in $m_i,d_i$.
Thus the complete lists, not just one selected root, have polynomial total
size. Their membership conditions are pointwise equivalent to the original
polynomial equations for the same $z$. No joint root choices have been made.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Affine-root-domain solver]\label{thm:affine}
If every nonempty constrained root set $S_i$ is an affine coset over
$\F_2$, the entire instance has a uniform deterministic polynomial-time
decision and witness algorithm. In particular this holds whenever every
nonzero $p_i$ has at most two field roots.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $s_i\in S_i$ and compute
$D_i=\operatorname{span}_{\F_2}\{r+s_i:r\in S_i\}$ by Gaussian elimination.
The root set is a coset exactly when $|S_i|=2^{\dim D_i}$, because
$S_i\subseteq s_i+D_i$ and the elements are distinct. Let the rows of $W_i$
be a basis of $D_i^\perp$. Then
\[
 M_i z+a_i\in S_i
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 W_iM_i z=W_i(s_i+a_i).
\]
Combine these equations with $Ez=e$ and solve one binary linear system.
Return any solution, checking the original constraints. Empty root sets
reject and zero polynomials contribute no equation. Parsing, irreducibility,
root listing, coset recognition, orthogonal complements, matrix composition,
linear solution, verification and output are polynomial in the declared bit
length. Singleton sets and two-element sets are respectively affine cosets
of dimension zero and one, proving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

Theorems~\ref{thm:lists}--\ref{thm:affine} have complete proofs here and remain
\CAND{} for project integration pending independent peer review. They are
not a claim that every two-valued constraint language is affine: the affine
maps and binary-field representation in \eqref{eq:general} are essential.
They also do not classify every larger root set. Many non-affine subtypes
can still be easy.

\section{Three known roots already encode NP-complete compatibility}
Let $K=\F_4=\F_2[\omega]/(\omega^2+\omega+1)$. For Boolean $a,b$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:norm}
 (a+b\omega)^3=a+b+ab\in\F_2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\omega^3=1$, $\omega+\omega^2=1$, and Boolean powers simplify.
Hence $w^3=1$ exactly when $w\ne0$; its three roots are explicitly
$1,\omega,1+\omega$.

For a simple graph $\Gamma=([n],E_\Gamma)$, $n\ge1$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:colour}
 C_\Gamma(w)=
 \bigwedge_{v=1}^n[w_v^3=1]\ \land\!
 \bigwedge_{\{u,v\}\in E_\Gamma}[(w_u+w_v)^3=1].
\end{equation}
Every equation uses the identical degree-three polynomial $T^3+1$ after an
explicit linear map, over the identical public field. The Boolean algebraic
degree of every constraint is at most two.

\begin{theorem}[Three-root compatibility]\label{thm:colour}
The solutions of \eqref{eq:colour} are in bijection with labelled proper
3-colourings of $\Gamma$. Deciding these instances is NP-complete. This
holds despite constant-size known local root sets and constant-time local
root selection.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The vertex equations restrict every $w_v$ to the three nonzero field
elements. An edge equation holds exactly when $w_u+w_v\ne0$, equivalently
$w_u\ne w_v$. Thus assigning the three nonzero elements as three named
colours is a witness-preserving bijection. Constructing all matrices and
constraints is polynomial. A vector of $2n$ bits is a polynomial certificate;
verification checks the displayed equations. NP-hardness is the ordinary
3-colouring theorem under this exact translation. No new foundational
NP-completeness theorem is claimed.
\end{proof}

One fixed total bit language may encode $\Gamma$ by the eight-bit header
\texttt{01011010}, followed by $1^n0$ and its $\binom n2$ upper-triangular
adjacency bits in lexicographic pair order. Every other string rejects.
The input length is $9+n+\binom n2$; the verifier uses $2n$ witness bits.
This is an infinite family containing YES and NO instances, not one fixed
formula or an all-NO benchmark.

The smallest complete-graph control is $K_4$: all local equations have three
roots, but the conjunction has none. $K_3$ has six solutions. Ordinary arc
consistency on the three-colour domains removes nothing on $K_4$, because
every colour has a different compatible neighbour colour. This falsifies
local-domain nonemptiness as a feasibility certificate. It does not prove
that all polynomial algorithms fail: $K_4$ itself is trivial to recognize.
The new source-matched theorem is \CAND{} pending independent review;
3-colouring NP-completeness is \KNOWN{}.

\section{An explicit affine Tseitin carrier and the exact lift}
The coupled family can use a connected cactus $X_n$ with $2n$ triangles.
For triangle $j=0,\ldots,2n-1$, put
$a_j=3j$, $b_j=3j+1$, $c_j=3j+2$ and include its three edges. Add the bridge
$\{b_j,a_{j+1}\}$ for every consecutive pair. Then
\[
 |V(X_n)|=6n,\qquad |E(X_n)|=8n-1,\qquad
 \dim\ker B_{X_n}=2n.
\]
The maximum degree is three and the carrier has treewidth two: triangle
bags of size three joined through their size-two bridge bags give a tree
decomposition. This is a property of the affine carrier, not of the full
coupled constraint graph.

Choose as spanning tree the first two edges of each triangle and all
bridges; choose the third edge of each triangle as the ordered cotree $C$.
For zero charge set $a=0$. Column $L_j$ is one on the three edges of triangle
$j$ and zero elsewhere. Thus
\[
 B_{X_n}a=0,\qquad B_{X_n}L=0,\qquad \pi_CL=I_{2n}.
\]
The columns are independent and their count is the entire kernel dimension,
so every zero-charge solution is uniquely $x=Lz$. Pair successive cotree
bits to form the $w_v$ in \eqref{eq:colour}, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:carrier}
 G_\Gamma(x)=[B_{X_n}x=0]\land C_\Gamma(\pi_Cx).
\end{equation}
Projection and the displayed lift give exact inverse witness maps between
\eqref{eq:colour} and \eqref{eq:carrier}. The bridge values are zero; no
extra free variables are hidden. For a consistent nonzero charge one can
instead compute $a$ with zero cotree coordinates by a forest solve, retaining
$B_Xa=c$, $B_XL=0$, and $\pi_CL=I$.

\paragraph{What this does not prove.}
This carrier exposes that local root selection and affine lifting need not
resolve their shared nonlinear compatibility. It merely transports graph
colouring into cotree coordinates. It supplies no resource amplification,
no factor $r(s)$, no lower bound for arbitrary computation, and no transfer
of graph geometry to the full coupled formula. It does not claim that this
new source automatically inherits an old exact-$(2,2)$ minimality or
PC/PCR/Resolution projection theorem.
