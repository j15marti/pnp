\section{CONSTRUCTION OR PROOF ATTEMPT}
Let $\mu_0,\mu_1$ be distributions on $X$, let $K$ be a stochastic quotient into $Z$, and let $f:X\to[0,1]$.
\begin{theorem}[Erasure-factor inequality]
For $h:Z\to[0,1]$ and $\delta_i=\mathbb E|f(X)-h(Z)|$ under $X\sim\mu_i$, $Z\sim K(X)$,
\[
|\mathbb E_{\mu_1}f-\mathbb E_{\mu_0}f|
\le \delta_0+\delta_1+d_{\rm TV}(K_\#\mu_0,K_\#\mu_1).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}Insert the two expectations of $h(Z)$, apply the triangle inequality, and use the total-variation bound for $[0,1]$-valued tests.\end{proof}
If a uniform label $B$ selects $\mu_B$, Pinsker gives the further bound $\delta_0+\delta_1+\sqrt{2I(B;Z)}$. Thus earlier erasure work can help only after proving circuit-to-quotient factorization; no solver-state obligation is inferred.

For order-$r$ moment vector $\Phi_r(F)$ and target $u_r$:
\begin{theorem}[Moment-cone alternative]
Either $u_r\in\operatorname{conv}\Phi_r(\Omega_+)$ and an UNSAT distribution matches the moments, or a linear functional strictly separates every UNSAT moment vector from $u_r$. If the Euclidean distance is $\Delta$, a unit separator has gap at least $\Delta$.
\end{theorem}
This follows from compact convex separation and nearest-point projection. Moreover, if any distribution matches $D$ bounded features, Hoeffding plus a union bound gives an $\varepsilon$-matching uniform mixture supported on $O(\varepsilon^{-2}\log(D/\delta))$ UNSAT formulas. This supplies the stochastic high-dimensional-vector formulation. JL remains a computational accelerator only.

Let $C\le\mathbb F_2^m$ have dual distance $d^\perp$.
\begin{theorem}[Coset local invisibility]
The uniform distribution on every coset $a+C$ projects uniformly to every coordinate set of size less than $d^\perp$. Hence all cosets have identical such marginals.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}A nonsurjective coordinate projection would yield a nonzero dual codeword supported inside those coordinates.\end{proof}
For a connected graph $G$, take the cut space $C=\{\delta x\}$. Its dual is the cycle space, so $d^\perp$ is the girth. Labels in $C$ are satisfiable parity constraints; every nontrivial coset is UNSAT. Therefore satisfiable and unsatisfiable signed systems are exactly $(g-1)$-wise indistinguishable. An acyclic orientation makes this a signed DAG while the obstruction remains on undirected cycles. The missing bridge is a locally simulatable exact-density proper-3CNF encoding without constant parity bundles.

Let circuit size be $S=M^K$ and certified per-gate error be $\varepsilon(M,\theta)=M^{-E(\theta)+o(1)}$.
\begin{theorem}[Exponent capacity]
With $\Lambda=\sup_\theta E(\theta)$, a gate-union-bound architecture can defeat every fixed polynomial exponent iff $\Lambda=\infty$. If $\Lambda<\infty$, the estimate handles only $K<\Lambda$.
\end{theorem}
The present local planting bound $O(rk/M)$ has certified capacity at most one: for $k=q^\alpha$, $r=q^\rho$, $M=\Theta(q^3)$, its exponent is $1-(\alpha+\rho)/3$. Hence local TV alone cannot reach arbitrary polynomial circuit sizes. A width-amplified bound such as $M^{-cw+o(1)}$ is mandatory.

If admissible parameters form a polyhedron and all error exponents are affine, unbounded capacity holds iff the recession cone contains a direction that strictly increases every error exponent. Otherwise LP duality supplies a finite ceiling certificate. This removes the need to calculate one exact parameter tuple.
