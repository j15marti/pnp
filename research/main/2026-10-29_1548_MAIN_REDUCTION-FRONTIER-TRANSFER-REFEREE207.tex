\section{PROPOSE: one missing lemma and four falsifiers}

The concrete missing lemma is the following.

\begin{quote}
\emph{When does a target solver with resource exponents $(t,s)$ actually induce a source solver
with a favorable simultaneous pair, after charging the physical encoding of the reduced instance,
reducer discovery, access, failed children, reconstruction, and peak storage?}
\end{quote}

The candidate answer was tested against four mechanisms.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{M1, explicit materialization:} write the complete child before invoking its solver.
\item \textbf{M2, on-demand access:} generate each requested child bit without retaining the child.
\item \textbf{M3, many children:} distinguish sequential reuse of storage from retaining all children.
\item \textbf{M4, parameter-only shrink:} falsify the claim that eliminating one variable implies
encoded-size contraction, using exact selector elimination.
\end{enumerate}

\section{BUILD I: a charged reduction-frontier transfer theorem}

All exponents below are relative to the same source bit length $N$.  The machine model has a fixed
finite alphabet and counts all writable cells in peak space.  Let a uniform deterministic reducer on
source input $x$ of length $N$ produce one child $y$ of length
\[
 M=N^{\alpha+o(1)}.
\]
Its time and additional peak space are $N^{r+o(1)}$ and $N^{u+o(1)}$.  A fixed target decider uses
$M^{t+o(1)}$ time and $M^{s+o(1)}$ peak space.  Reconstruction uses $N^{h+o(1)}$ time and
$N^{v+o(1)}$ extra peak space.  Correctness and representation closure are separate hypotheses.

\begin{theorem}[Explicit materialization]
\label{thm:materialized}
If the complete child is written to counted writable storage before the target decider runs, then the
composed source algorithm has
\[
 \tau=\max\{r,\alpha t,h\},\qquad
 \sigma=\max\{u,\alpha,\alpha s,v\}.
\]
Moreover $r\geq\alpha$ for a sequential bit-output reducer, and every such architecture has the
representation floor $\sigma\geq\alpha$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The three phases are sequential, so their times add and their exponents take the maximum.  The
materialized child, reducer workspace, target workspace, and reconstruction workspace may overlap in
time, so a safe simultaneous peak bound is their maximum exponent; the child alone occupies
$M=N^{\alpha+o(1)}$ cells.  A fixed-transition finite-alphabet machine writes only $O(1)$ bits per
step, so producing $M$ explicit bits requires $\Omega(M)$ time.  The formulas are an upper
composition for this implementation plus architecture-specific output and storage floors.  They are
not lower bounds against algorithms that never materialize this child.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[On-demand random access]
\label{thm:ondemand}
Suppose instead that, after $N^{r+o(1)}$ preprocessing with $N^{u+o(1)}$ peak space, a target-input
bit requested during a simulation can be generated in $N^{g+o(1)}$ time and $N^{w+o(1)}$ space.
If each of the target's $M^{t+o(1)}$ steps triggers at most $O(1)$ such requests and no stronger
caching theorem is assumed, then
\[
 \tau=\max\{r,g+\alpha t,h\},\qquad
 \sigma=\max\{u,w,\alpha s,v\}.
\]
There is no automatic $alpha$ storage floor, but there is an access-time tax $g$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiply the number of simulated target steps by the worst-case generator time.  Peak storage is the
maximum simultaneous live storage.  The theorem assumes a genuine random-access generator in the
same source machine model; a succinct description with no access implementation does not satisfy the
hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Polynomially many mandatory children]
\label{cor:many}
If $p=N^{\mu+o(1)}$ children of maximum length $N^{\alpha+o(1)}$ must all be solved, sequential reuse
gives
\[
 \tau=\max\{r,\mu+\alpha t,h\},\qquad
 \sigma=\max\{u,\alpha,\alpha s,v\}.
\]
Retaining every explicit child changes the space bound to include $mu+\alpha$.  On a SAT-side OR,
early success is an input-dependent improvement; on the all-UNSAT side every child is mandatory.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Derivative and Pareto interpretation.}
For a differentiable size map $alpha=\alpha(\theta)$ and fixed target exponent $t$, the
solver-dominated branch has $d\tau/d\theta=t\alpha'(\theta)$.  Reducer- or reconstruction-dominated
branches have slope zero with respect to that parameter.  The upper envelope is piecewise smooth and
has kinks at equality points such as $r=\alpha t$.  A plotted target curve cannot be compared with a
source curve until both axes are normalized to $N$ and all floors are included.  The simultaneous
Pareto set consists of nondominated pairs from one actual composed implementation, not the separate
best time and best space over different runs or machines.

\Status{PROVED/EXACT} for Theorems~\ref{thm:materialized}--\ref{thm:ondemand} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:many} under their explicit model hypotheses.

\section{BUILD II: representation blowup falsifies parameter-only contraction}

For pairwise disjoint variables, define
\[
 F_t=\bigwedge_{i=1}^t(z\vee a_i\vee b_i)\ \wedge\
     \bigwedge_{j=1}^t(\neg z\vee c_j\vee d_j).
\]
Existential elimination of $z$ gives
\[
 \exists z\,F_t\equiv
 \bigwedge_{i,j=1}^t(a_i\vee b_i\vee c_j\vee d_j)
 \equiv
 \left(\bigwedge_i(a_i\vee b_i)\right)\vee
 \left(\bigwedge_j(c_j\vee d_j)\right).
\]

\begin{proposition}[One fewer variable, quadratic explicit CNF]
\label{prop:selector}
Under a fixed explicit literal-index encoding, $F_t$ has
$N=\Theta(t\log t)$ bits while its auxiliary-free projected CNF has
$M=\Theta(t^2\log t)$ bits.  Hence
\[
 \frac{\log M}{\log N}\longrightarrow 2.
\]
A linear-time target solver invoked after explicit materialization therefore yields source time and
peak-space exponents approaching $2$, despite eliminating one variable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
There are $2t$ source clauses and $t^2$ non-tautological pairwise resolvents, with $\Theta(\log t)$
bits per literal identifier.  Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:materialized} with target $t=s=1$ and
$\alpha\to2$.  No hardness is asserted: the displayed circuit has $O(t)$ gates and decides this
projection easily.  It changes representation, so repeated use requires a separate closure and
access theorem.
\end{proof}

The finite control used $t=2^k$ for $k=2,4,\ldots,512$.  The measured effective size exponent rises
monotonically from $1.1825$ to $1.9767$.  This is \textbf{COMPUTATIONAL} confirmation of the exact
asymptotic count.  Proposition~\ref{prop:selector} is a specific falsifier of parameter-only
contraction; it is not an inherent-expansion theorem and not a SAT lower bound.

\section{Static boundary proxy normalized to canonical input size}

The structural inequality
\[
 n\leq 3b+12bq
\]
is accepted here only for the specified connected, proper, strict, exact-$(2,2)$ static modular
hypothesis with $b>0$.  It implies the relaxed permitted curve
\[
 q(b)\geq\max\left\{0,\frac{n}{12b}-\frac14\right\}.
\]
For the illustrative enumeration proxy, set
\[
 E_T(b)=b+q(b),\qquad E_S(b)=b.
\]
On the positive branch,
\[
 E_T'(b)=1-\frac{n}{12b^2},\qquad
 E_T''(b)=\frac{n}{6b^3}>0,
\]
so the unique continuous minimum is $b^*=\sqrt{n/12}$ with
$E_T(b^*)=\sqrt{n/3}-1/4$.  The log-proxy curves satisfy $E_T=E_S$ exactly when $q=0$; the earliest
permitted integer point is $b\geq\lceil n/3\rceil$.  These are permitted points, not existence claims.

For $n=1200$, exact integer rounding gives minimum exponent $20$ at $(b,q)=(9,11),(10,10),(11,9)$.
The Pareto points under coordinates $(E_T,E_S)$ run from $(101,1)$ through $(20,9)$; points with
$b>9$ are dominated by $(20,9)$ even though two tie for minimum time.  The first $q=0$ point is
$(b,q)=(400,0)$.

Now impose canonical bit length $N=\Theta(n\log n)$.  Ignoring a fixed polynomial multiplier of
degree $a$, the proxy's additive polynomial-time exponent is
\[
 \frac{E_T}{\log_2 N}.
\]
At the continuous optimum this is
\[
 \Theta\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{N}}{(\log N)^{3/2}}\right)\longrightarrow\infty,
\]
and at $q=0$ it is
\[
 \Theta\!\left(\frac{N}{(\log N)^2}\right)\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Thus neither apparent crossing becomes a finite polynomial exponent pair.  In the report's explicit
convention $N=n\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$, $n=1200$ yields additive exponents $1.4428$ at the minimum
and $29.2221$ at $q=0$; those numbers depend on encoding constants and do not carry asymptotic
complexity-theoretic meaning.
