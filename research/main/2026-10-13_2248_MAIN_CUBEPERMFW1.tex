\section{Gold-cube residual firewall}
\begin{theorem}[zero-kernel cube permutation]
Let $d\ge3$ be odd and $K=\mathbb F_{2^d}$.  Then $Q(z)=z^3$ has all of the
following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $Q$ is a quadratic permutation of the $d$-dimensional $\F$-space $K$.
\item Its inverse is
\[
 Q^{-1}(b)=b^e,\qquad e=\frac{2^{d+1}-1}{3}.            \tag{5}
\]
\item The polar-output kernel (4) is zero.
\item Given an encoded irreducible polynomial for $K$, both maps and the
test $K_Q=0$ are computable in time polynomial in the explicit record length.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Frobenius map $z\mapsto z^2$ is $\F$-linear and field multiplication is
bilinear, so $z^3=z\,z^2$ is a Boolean polynomial map of degree at most two.
Because $d$ is odd,
\[
 2^d-1\equiv1\pmod3,
\]
and therefore $\gcd(3,2^d-1)=1$.  Thus cubing permutes $K^\times$ and fixes
zero.  Moreover
\[
 3e=2^{d+1}-1=2(2^d-1)+1,
\]
which proves (5).

Identify $K^*$ with $K$ via the nondegenerate trace pairing.  For
$\lambda\in K$, the scalar component has polar form
\[
 B_\lambda(x,y)
 =\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\F}\!\left(
   \lambda(x^2y+xy^2)\right).                           \tag{6}
\]
Assume $B_\lambda$ is identically zero.  For fixed $y$, use
$\operatorname{Tr}(a x^2)=\operatorname{Tr}(a^{2^{d-1}}x)$ to obtain
\[
 (\lambda y)^{2^{d-1}}+\lambda y^2=0
 \qquad\hbox{for every }y\in K.                        \tag{7}
\]
If $\lambda\ne0$, setting $y=1$ shows
$\lambda^{2^{d-1}}=\lambda$.  The fixed field has size
$2^{\gcd(d,d-1)}=2$, hence $\lambda=1$.  Equation (7) would then say that
the Frobenius automorphisms $y\mapsto y^{2^{d-1}}$ and $y\mapsto y^2$ are
equal.  This requires $d\mid(d-2)$, hence $d\mid2$, impossible for odd
$d\ge3$.  Therefore $\lambda=0$ and $K_Q=0$.

Rabin-style irreducibility testing, repeated squaring, field multiplication,
exponentiation by the $O(d)$-bit exponent (5), construction of the quadratic
coefficient matrix, and Gaussian rank computation are polynomial in the
explicit input length.  No inverse table or witness is advice.
\end{proof}
