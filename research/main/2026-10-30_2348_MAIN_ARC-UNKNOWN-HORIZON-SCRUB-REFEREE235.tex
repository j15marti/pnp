\section{The reset seam and a conditional composition}
Let $y=y(x,j)$ be a complete ordinary SAT encoding of length $m$ and let
$B(x,j,i)$ be a deterministic total reentrant accessor returning exactly $y_i$
for $0\le i<m$ and BLANK otherwise.  Each invocation, including source access,
address work, cleanup and scratch restoration, costs at most $b$ time and $s_B$
simultaneous counted space.  The parent retains $R$ bits.  Let $A$ be one fixed
deterministic total guest.  Its actual run on $y$ halts after $T\ge1$ guest
steps, uses work/index space $S_A(T)$ and reaches input-index-tape high-water
$H_A(T)$.  Neither $T$ nor a polynomial exponent for $A$ is supplied.

For an integer budget $h$, use the independently proved LSPV-4 budgeted lift:
it either reproduces the guest's halt within $h$ steps or returns a distinguished
TIMEOUT after exactly $h$ guest steps.  It uses horizon-local index epochs and
FAR saturation, costs
\[
 O\!\left(h\,[b+\log^2(m+h+2)]\right),
\]
and never turns TIMEOUT into a SAT label.  The simulator is a fixed uniform
machine; $h$ is ordinary binary state generated by its outer loop, not advice.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional unknown-horizon scrub lift]\label{th:scrub}
Assume in addition that, after every $h$-step TIMEOUT, the complete simulator
state can be restored to its initial blank guest-tape state in
$O(h[b+\log^2(m+h+2)])$ time without increasing that phase's peak space by more
than a constant factor.  Then the following wrapper is exact.
Run the budgeted lift sequentially for $h=1,2,4,\ldots$.  After every TIMEOUT,
move every simulated guest work/index head back through the physically visited
prefix, blank that phase's writable cells and horizon tags, reset the finite
control and logical input-head shadow, and double $h$.  Keep the read-only source,
child descriptor and parent state unchanged.  Stop only when the simulated guest
halts.  This fixed deterministic machine returns exactly $A(y)$ and satisfies
\[
 T_{\rm sim}=O\!\left(T[b+\log^2(m+T+2)]\right),                         \tag{1}
\]
\[
 S_{\rm sim}=O\!\left(S_A(T)+H_A(T)\log(m+T+2)+s_B+R+\log(m+T+2)\right). \tag{2}
\]
Every failed phase and the stipulated scrub tariff are included.  No bound on
the polynomial degree of $A$ and no answer bit is supplied.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $h_*$ be the first power of two at least $T$.  Then $h_*<2T$ and
\[
 \sum_{h<h_*}h+h_*<2h_*<4T.                                            \tag{3}
\]
Every failed execution is the exact length-$h$ prefix of the same deterministic
guest computation because the hypothesis reconstructs its source, descriptor,
virtual child and initial blank tape state.  Monotonicity of the logarithm, the
stipulated phase tariff and (3) give (1), including every failed phase and scrub.

The first phase that reaches $T$ observes exactly the physical guest's symbols
and returns its label; earlier TIMEOUT phases return no semantic label and export
no derived state.  Sequential scrub reuses the same physical prefixes, so peak
space is the maximum phase footprint rather than their sum.  Since
$h_*<2T$, its tag/address widths are $O(\log(m+T+2))$, and no prefix of the guest
uses more actual work or input-index high-water than its complete $T$-step run.
Adding the simultaneously live accessor, parent and counters proves (2).
The loop is uniform and total because the fixed total guest eventually halts.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Far-index scrub counterexample]\label{lem:far}
Let $D$ be any fixed total exact SAT decider and let $p(N)\ge2$ be any uniform
integer function whose binary representation is constructible in
$O(\log p(N))$ steps.  There is a total exact SAT decider $D_p$ that constructs
$p(N)$ on an index tape, indexes a work tape to cell $p(N)$, writes one bit, and
then returns $D(F)$.  It has time
$T_D(N)+O(\log p(N))$, the same label as $D$, and FLMV work space at least
$p(N)$.  Any reset that returns by sequentially traversing and blanking the
entire physical prefix through that cell costs $\Omega(p(N))$ time.  Taking
$p(N)=N^q$ with $q$ larger than a known time exponent for $D$ makes scrub exceed
the wrapped guest time by a polynomial factor; taking $p(N)=2^N$ gives an
exponential separation while the wrapper itself adds only $O(N)$ time.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
FLMV Section~2.1 permits an indexed jump in one transition after the address has
been written and defines a non-index work tape's space contribution as its
largest position ever read or indexed.  The remote write is label-independent,
so exactness and totality are inherited from $D$.  Sequentially crossing from
position $p(N)$ to the left end takes at least $p(N)$ ordinary head moves.  The
claimed separations follow by substitution. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Time--space-coupled unknown-horizon lift]\label{th:coupled}
Let $W_A(t)$ be the sum, over the fixed finite simulated guest work/index tapes,
of the largest physical prefix positions that must be restored after the exact
$t$-step budgeted simulation, including horizon tags.  Track each high-water
mark with paid address comparisons and a boundary marker.  After each TIMEOUT,
jump to every recorded boundary, sweep left to its immutable origin while
blanking every cell, reset finite control, and double the budget.  If the guest
halts in $T$ steps, the exact total simulation has
\[
 T_{\rm total}=O\!\left(
 T[b+\log^2(m+T+2)]+W_A(T)\log(T+2)\right),                            \tag{5}
\]
\[
 S_{\rm total}=O\!\left(W_A(T)+s_B+R+\log(T+2)\right).                 \tag{6}
\]
No running-time or space exponent is supplied, and TIMEOUT commits no label.
For the LSPV-4 block layout one may take conservatively
$W_A(T)=O(S_A(T)+H_A(T)\log(m+T+2))$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every phase is an exact deterministic prefix, and its high-water marks do not
exceed those of the complete $T$-step run.  There are $O(\log(T+2))$ failed
phases, so sweeping all used prefixes costs at most
$O(W_A(T)\log(T+2))$.  FLMV erases an index tape after its indexed jump.  The
guest must therefore redeliver every address used by a later jump; the simulator
copies and compares those paid bits while maintaining the relevant maximum and
boundary marker.  This adds only the explicit address arithmetic already charged
by the budgeted lift.  The geometric sum bounds all repeated guest-prefix work
by the first term of (5).  Only one phase is live, and scrub restores it before
the next, proving (6).  The first halting phase returns the physical label;
earlier phases return only TIMEOUT. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why this corrects rather than erases the reset seam.}
The earlier head-only geometric replay correctly paid repeated guest steps but
left general tape reset separate.  ARC-EPOCH-TAPES173 avoids wiping polynomially
many sequential calls, but its fixed tag width uses a declared polynomial call
bound.  LSPV-4 supplies that width from a valid horizon.  Lemma~\ref{lem:far}
falsifies the time-only scrub estimate; Theorem~\ref{th:coupled} repairs it by
charging the exact high-water toll rather than assuming it away.

\paragraph{FLMV consequence and nonconsequence.}
For $q=N^{\gamma+o(1)}$ guests of length $m=N^{\lambda+o(1)}$, guest time
$m^{K+o(1)}$, high-water space $m^{L+o(1)}$, accessor exponent $\beta$ and local
exponent $\alpha$, Theorem~\ref{th:coupled} gives
\[
 K'\le\max\{\alpha,\gamma+\beta+\lambda K,\gamma+\lambda L\}.          \tag{7}
\]
On the project diagonal $K\ge L$, with
$(\alpha,\gamma,\lambda)=(11/10,3/5,1/2)$ and $\beta\ge0$, the new term is
absorbed by the accessor/guest-time term.  The fixed point remains
$K_*=6/5+2\beta$, so the $c=241/200$ test still requires $\beta<1/400$.
Off diagonal, dropping $\gamma+\lambda L$ is unsound.  Neither theorem constructs
the accessor, the $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ child, or hereditary closure.

\section{Independent affine-small-bias referee audit}
I independently reconstructed the load-bearing inequalities rather than relying
on the project summary.  Let $D$ be $\varepsilon$-biased on $\{0,1\}^N$, let
$R(x)=Ax\oplus b$, and let $C\subseteq\{0,1\}^M$ have $|C|\le B$.  For each
$y$, a consistent fiber $Ax=y\oplus b$ is an affine subspace described by
$r=\operatorname{rank}(A)$ independent characters.  Fourier expansion of its
indicator gives point-probability difference at most
$(1-2^{-r})\varepsilon\le\varepsilon$ between $D$ and uniform input; inconsistent
fibers contribute zero.  Summing over $C$ gives
\[
 \left|\Pr_D[R(x)\in C]-\Pr_U[R(x)\in C]\right|\le B\varepsilon.       \tag{4}
\]
This remains valid for singular/rectangular $A$ and a source-independent random
mixture by conditioning on the seed.

For target circuit threshold $u_N\le N^\nu$ and polynomial target truth-table
length, circuit-description counting gives
$\log_2 B_N=N^{\nu+o(1)}$.  Choose $\nu<\rho<\mu$ and the explicit easy
$\varepsilon_N=2^{-\lceil N^\rho\rceil}$-biased source ensemble used in
AFFINE-SMALLBIAS-DESCENT-GATE2; every support truth table has circuit size
$N^{\rho+o(1)}<N^\mu$.  Hence $B_N\varepsilon_N=o(1)$.  The fraction of uniform
$N$-bit strings of circuit size at most $N^\mu$ is
$\eta_N\le2^{N^{\mu+o(1)}-N}=o(1)$.  Pointwise completeness on every support
point of $D$ and pointwise soundness on every NO string imply
\[
 1-e_N\le p_D\le p_U+o(1)
 \le e_N+(1-e_N)\eta_N+o(1),
\]
so $e_N\ge1/2-o(1)$.  The source is chosen before the reduction seed; there is
no source-after-seed quantifier swap.

\textbf{Referee verdict: certified only in its declared scope.}  The proof is a
semantic no-go for source-independent randomized maps whose final dependence on
the source truth table is affine and whose guarantee is pointwise for both
labels.  A nonlinear membership map can distinguish a small-bias support from
uniform almost perfectly; a source-dependent preprocessing rule need not be
affine; and none of (4), circuit counting, or the easy source ensemble lower-bounds
the running time of an arbitrary SAT machine.  Promotion to universal SAT
hardness or to an ARC child/accessor is therefore \textbf{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}.
