\section{Question, falsifiers, and result}

The concrete missing lemma was whether the balanced signed-dual condition in
\path{research/agents/lspv-1/2026-10-29_2357_LSPV-1_SIGNED_NAE_DUAL_MATCH242_PUBLIC.tex}
is a genuine coverage boundary for complement-paired strict22 formulas.  Four
falsifiable mechanisms were tested:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an unbalanced signed cycle may nevertheless admit a direct ordinary-SAT
assignment;
\item replacing signed occurrences by literal-value vertices may reduce the
problem to degree-two hypergraph two-colouring;
\item the only degree-two obstruction may lie in a binary odd cycle, which the
paired width-three construction cannot create;
\item endpoint failure and dense local extreme conflicts may still provide no
UNSAT information.
\end{enumerate}
Mechanisms 1--3 hold and yield the theorem below.  Mechanism 4 is confirmed by
the complete clause-indexed controls.

\section{Exact reduction and coverage theorem}

Let
\[
 F=\bigwedge_{p=1}^{m}\bigl(D_p\wedge\overline{D_p}\bigr),
\]
where every $D_p$ is a proper signed 3-clause and
$\overline{D_p}$ negates all three literals.  Designate one clause $D_p$ from
each pair.  A Boolean assignment satisfies both members of pair $p$ exactly
when $D_p$ has one or two true literals.  Hence $F$ is satisfiable exactly
when the designated signed clauses have a not-all-equal assignment.

\begin{lemma}[signed-to-positive split]
Suppose each source variable occurs in at most two designated clauses.  The
signed NAE instance is equivalent to a positive hypergraph two-colouring
instance with ternary hyperedges and a matching of binary hyperedges.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each source occurrence use a vertex whose colour is the truth value of its
literal.  If a variable has two occurrences of the same sign, identify the
two vertices.  If the signs differ, keep two vertices and add a binary NAE
edge, forcing the two literal truth values to differ.  A once-occurring
variable needs no consistency edge.  Each designated clause becomes a
positive ternary NAE hyperedge.  Because no variable has more than two
occurrences, distinct opposite-sign variables give vertex-disjoint binary
edges; they form a matching.  The correspondence with source assignments is
bijective after arbitrary choices for unused variables.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[paired occurrence-two coverage]
Every proper complement-paired width-three formula in which each variable
belongs to at most two clause pairs is satisfiable.  A satisfying assignment
is constructible in linear time by the known NAE-SAT(2) algorithm.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Porschen, Randerath and Speckenmeyer give a linear-time algorithm for NAE-SAT
when each variable occurs at most twice.  The underlying obstruction
characterisation can also be applied after the preceding split: in a
maximum-degree-two hypergraph with all edges of size at least two, a minimal
non-two-colourable component must be a non-bipartite component of the graph
formed by its size-two edges.  One counting proof is that a minimal
non-two-colourable hypergraph has at least as many edges as vertices, whereas
maximum degree two and minimum edge size two give the reverse inequality;
equality forces every edge to have size two and every vertex degree two.
Thus the obstruction is an odd binary cycle.  Here the binary-edge graph is a
matching, hence bipartite.  The positive instance is two-colourable, and the
split lemma maps its colouring back to a satisfying assignment of $F$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scope firewall.}
In $F$, a source variable may occur four times in the ordinary CNF because
each designated occurrence is copied into its complementary mate.  The
``at most two'' hypothesis concerns designated clause pairs, not raw CNF
occurrences.  Conversely, an arbitrary strict exact-$(2,2)$ formula need not
admit any complementary pairing.  The theorem therefore does not decide that
class and does not imply a general SAT algorithm.

\section{Clause-indexed $S/U$ experiment}

For every original clause $C_j$ and signed occurrence $x_{i,j}$, the raw
output records the sign, the local all-literals-true target $S_j$, its inverted
$U_j$ target, the local all-literals-false target $U_j$, and its inverted
$S_j$ target.  Occurrence targets are never treated as independent source
variables.  For every source-clause pair it records $SS,SU,US,UU$ feasibility
and verifies it by exact assignment enumeration.  It also records bounded
multi-clause modes, $0^n$, $1^n$, complement identities, single- and
multi-flip trails, non-tautological opposite-pivot resolvents, prohibited
partial cubes and proof parents.

For a proper 3-clause and full assignment $a$, the implementation independently
checks
\[
 t_j(\bar a)=3-t_j(a),\qquad
 t_j(a\oplus S)=t_j(a)+|S\cap F_j(a)|-|S\cap T_j(a)|.
\]
Thus disjunctive satisfaction ($t_j\ge1$) remains distinct from the local
extreme $S_j$ ($t_j=3$).  The pair matrix is exactly:
\[
 SS,UU\text{ feasible}\iff\text{no opposite-sign shared variable},
\quad
 SU,US\text{ feasible}\iff\text{no same-sign shared variable}.
\]
If both kinds of shared variable occur, no extreme pair is feasible, so every
full assignment leaves at least one parent in a middle state.  The experiment
uses this only as a local invariant, never as an SAT or UNSAT oracle.

\section{Literature and implementation search}

\KNOWN.  Porschen, Randerath and Speckenmeyer, ``Linear Time Algorithms for
Some Not-All-Equal Satisfiability Problems,'' SAT 2003,
\url{https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-540-24605-3_14}, supplies the NAE-SAT(2)
algorithm.  Antunes Filho's thesis gives the degree-two hypergraph obstruction
proof,
\url{https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/144581}.  Kullmann's conflict-graph
and hitting-clause-set programme is relevant to the local extreme graph but
does not turn its incompatibilities into formula UNSAT without a source-level
bridge.  Döcker's broader monotone exact-$(2,2)$ NP-completeness result,
\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08032}, prevents extending the paired theorem
by silently dropping its pairing hypothesis.

A GitHub search for an exact NAE-SAT(2) implementation found general SAT/NAE
packages but no implementation whose checked hypotheses and certificate
format exactly matched this theorem.  That failed search is not evidence of
impossibility.  The present reproducer implements the reduction certificate
and exhaustive controls, but does not claim to reimplement the published
linear-time constructor.
