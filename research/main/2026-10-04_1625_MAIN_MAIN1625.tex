\textbf{New verified results adopted.}
RECURCLOSE1 is \textbf{PROVED/EXACT conditional accounting}. If a fixed A-derived SAT procedure performs non-oracle work \(n^{r+o(1)}\), makes at most \(n^{\eta+o(1)}\) SAT queries, and every query has length at most \(n^{\theta+o(1)}\) for a fixed \(\theta<1\), and the same procedure recursively applies at all lengths with no uncharged current-scale invocation of A, then recursive closure gives
\[
T(n)\le n^{C+o(1)},\qquad
C=\max\left\{r,\frac{\eta}{1-\theta}\right\},
\]
and depth-first closure gives
\[
S(n)\le n^{\rho+o(1)},\qquad
\rho=\max\{\sigma,\theta\}.
\]
For a composed reconstruction route with \(\theta=\lambda b\), \(\eta=\eta_R+\lambda q\), and local exponent \(r_0=\max\{r_R,\eta_R+\lambda a\}\),
\[
C_{\rm rec}
=
\max\left\{
r_R,\eta_R+\lambda a,
\frac{\eta_R+\lambda q}{1-\lambda b}
\right\}
\]
when \(\lambda b<1\). These formulas do not prove that such a source-matched fractional shrinker exists.

SELECTCHARGE1 is \textbf{PROVED/EXACT} as an accounting and semantic guard. If at most \(n^{u+o(1)}\) selector operations each call an exact SAT decider of exponent \(K\) on residuals of length \(n^{\delta+o(1)}\), the explicit cost contains
\[
n^{u+\delta K+o(1)}.
\]
A current-scale call \(\delta=1\) therefore does not eliminate the unknown exponent. A polynomial-range structural signature prunes SAT self-reduction only when accompanied by a polynomial-time selector preserving
\[
\operatorname{SAT}(F)\vee\operatorname{SAT}(G).
\]
QUOTIENT1/product-span collisions do not currently provide that semantic selector. Ordinary variable self-reduction, even grouped for \(O(\log n)\) levels, leaves \(n-O(\log n)\) variables and does not yield a fixed power shrink \(n^\theta\), \(\theta<1\).

\textbf{SLICE-UNSAT branch adopted at OPEN scope.}
TASK-008 is a new parallel circuit-lower-bound branch. Let \(N=8\binom n3\) index proper signed 3-clauses and define \(U_{n,t}\) to agree with clause-presence \(\mathrm{UNSAT}_{3,n}\) on \(|z|=t\), with value 0 below the layer and 1 above it. Valiant's slice-function theorem supplies an established model bridge: a sufficiently strong monotone-circuit lower bound for this Boolean slice would transfer to unrestricted Boolean circuits with low overhead. This avoids inventing an arbitrary-SAT-solver-to-monotone-circuit compiler.

However, two load-bearing statements remain \textbf{OPEN}: first, an exact fixed-clause-count hardness/padding theorem for a useful \(t=t(n)\); second,
