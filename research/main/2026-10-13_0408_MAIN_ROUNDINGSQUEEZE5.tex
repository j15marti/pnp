\section{The theorem being formalized and its role}
Fix the total language
\[
 L_{22}=\{\operatorname{enc}(G):G\in\Cdom,\ G\text{ is satisfiable}\},
\]
where $\Cdom$ consists of nonempty proper, distinct-clause \emph{signed}
3-CNFs with exactly two positive and two negative occurrences of every
active variable. Malformed and out-of-domain strings are rejected. The
existing fixed-language compiler/completeness record is retained; this
report does not replace it with an all-NO benchmark. An input is encoded by
self-delimiting counts followed by indexed literal triples. For $v$ active
variables in this domain, $M=4v/3$ clauses and encoded length is
$L=\Theta(v\log(v+2))$.

Our immediate goal is to formalize the RS4 probabilistic repair, not assume
that a SAT decider produces a proof in a fixed system. The actual result
being proposed is the following: for any fixed rational $p>1$ and
\[
                      1<\beta<1+1/p,                    \tag{1}
\]
there is a family $H_t\in\Cdom$ and a named budget $r_t$, polynomial in its
encoded length $L_t$, such that $H_t$ has a Resolution refutation of at most
$r_t$ lines, while every semantic Cutting Planes refutation with at most
$r_t^\beta$ lines has depth $\Omega(L_t^p)$. Section~\ref{sec:parameters}
gives constructive parameter choices. This is a size--depth trade-off for
\emph{proofs}, not a time lower bound for all deciders of $L_{22}$.

No literature-novelty claim is made. The new interfaces in this project are a no-weakening subclause invariant, an explicit
$wd$ depth bound, a finite extracted-proof-size bound, and a verified
linear-line Resolution lift through the exact selector compiler. The
source width--depth construction is due to de Rezende et al.~\cite{tradeoff};
the normalized spread theorem is due to the prior spread literature in the
form stated by Mossel et al.~\cite{spread}. RS4's proposed replacement of the
probabilistic estimate is made explicit below; it is not attributed to the
source authors as an already published strengthening.

\section{Definitions and external dependencies}
All logarithms except an explicitly written $\ln$ are base two. For integer
$b\ge3$ and $\nu\ge1$, put
\[
 X_0=[b]^\nu,\qquad Y_0=\{0,1\}^{b\nu},\qquad
 \IND(x,y)_i=y_{i,x_i}.
\]
A pointer $\alpha$ fixes a subset $I\subseteq[\nu]$ to elements of $[b]$.
A nonempty row set $D$ is $\alpha$-dense if all rows agree with $\alpha$
and, on the free coordinates, uniform $x\in D$ satisfies
\[
 \Pr[x_J=a]\le b^{-\delta|J|}
 \quad(\varnothing\ne J\subseteq[\nu]\setminus I).        \tag{2}
\]
A row set is $\alpha$-predense if it contains an $\alpha$-dense subset.
For nonempty columns $Y$, let
\[
 \operatorname{def}(Y)=b\nu-\log|Y|.
\]
The clause $C_{I,\eta}$ is falsified exactly when $z_I=\eta$:
\[
 C_{I,\eta}=\bigvee_{i\in I}\begin{cases}z_i,&\eta_i=0,\\
                                     \neg z_i,&\eta_i=1.\end{cases}
\]
In particular $C_{\varnothing,\varnothing}$ is empty.

A triangle is a relation whose row neighborhoods are nested by inclusion.
A binary triangle DAG for $\operatorname{Search}(F)\circ\IND$ has full root
$X_0\times Y_0$, each internal node contained in the union of its at most
two children, and every leaf contained in the pairs falsifying one specified
clause of $F$. Unary nodes may repeat a child. Size is number of original
DAG nodes; depth counts edges. This agrees with the source shape-DAG
interface~\cite[Section 2.6]{tradeoff}.

Resolution clauses have distinct underlying variables and no tautologies.
The only inference in our certificate format is binary Resolution. An input
clause has depth zero. Semantic Cutting Planes uses integer-affine
inequalities; each noninitial line is entailed, on all Boolean assignments,
by at most two previous lines. Its size here is line count. \emph{Neither}
line count nor expanded monomial count is identified with binary proof size
or arbitrary machine time.

\paragraph{External theorem S: normalized spread.}
There is an absolute $C\ge1$ such that any probability measure on sets of
size at most $\nu$, satisfying
$\Pr[B\subseteq A]\le R^{-|B|}$ for every fixed $B$, is hit with probability
at least $0.9$ by an independent Bernoulli-$q$ set whenever
$q\ge C\log(2\nu)/R$. This is Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2 of
\cite{spread}, with the constant enlarged for the logarithm convention and
for $\nu=1$. The constant remains symbolic.

\paragraph{External theorem W: source width--depth family.}
For $3\le c\le k-1<t/(2\ln t)$, Theorem 2.3 of \cite{tradeoff} gives a
linear-size 4-CNF $F_{t,k,c}$ with
\[
 2k^2t^{c+1}\le\nu\le40k^2(2t)^{c+1},                    \tag{3}
\]
a Resolution refutation of width $k+3$ and length $O(k^2(4t)^k)$, while any
Resolution refutation of width at most $k+c$ has depth $\Omega(t^k)$.
We use fixed $k,c$ below. Hidden constants are therefore fixed before $t$
grows; no growing-parameter uniformity is inferred from them.

\section{Robust full image: complete amplification argument}
Set $\ell=\ceil{\log(2\nu)}$. Suppose a nonempty $X\subseteq[b]^\nu$ obeys
\[
 \Pr[x_J=a]\le2b^{-\delta|J|}
 \quad(\varnothing\ne J\subseteq[\nu]).                  \tag{4}
\]
Define
\[
             T=\floor{\frac{b^\delta}{4C\ell}}.
\]
\begin{lemma}[Robust full image]\label{lem:image}
If $T\ge1$ and $\operatorname{def}(Y)<T$, one row $x\in X$ satisfies
$\IND(\{x\}\times Y)=\{0,1\}^\nu$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Associate $x$ with $A_x=\{(i,x_i):i\in[\nu]\}$. The induced probability
measure is $R$-spread for $R=b^\delta/2$: a consistent set of $j\ge1$
positions has containment probability at most $2b^{-\delta j}\le R^{-j}$;
an inconsistent set has probability zero. Let $q_0=2C\ell/b^\delta$.
Then $q_0\le1/2$ and $Tq_0\le1/2$. Each Bernoulli-$q_0$ sample misses all
supported $A_x$ with probability at most $1/10$. The union of $T$
independent samples misses them only if every sample misses them, with
probability at most $10^{-T}$. Its coordinates are independent with
parameter $q=1-(1-q_0)^T\le Tq_0\le1/2$. Coupling with a fair Bernoulli set
shows that the latter's miss probability is at most $10^{-T}$.

If the conclusion failed, choose for every row $x$ one missing output
$z_x$. Every column in $Y$ satisfies the clause forbidding pattern $z_x$
at positions $A_x$. Replace both signs of each underlying bit by the
negative sign, one bit at a time. For a fixed assignment to all other bits,
the number of permitted values changes from
$\mathbf1[P]+\mathbf1[N]$ to $1+\mathbf1[P\wedge N]$, unless a clause
independent of this bit is already false, in which case both counts are
zero. The count never decreases. After all replacements the CNF says
that the one-bit set contains no $A_x$. Its density is at most
$10^{-T}<2^{-T}$, whereas $Y$ has density strictly greater than $2^{-T}$.
This is impossible.
\end{proof}

The clause-sign argument is the monotonization mechanism in Appendix B of
\cite{tradeoff}; the normalized spread amplification changes its quantitative
bound. After fixing a pointer, apply the lemma to free blocks. Projection
of $Y$ to free blocks cannot increase deficiency. A subset of at least half
of a dense row set satisfies (4), and a subset of at least half the columns
increases deficiency by at most one. If no free blocks remain, the required
image is the singleton and follows from nonemptiness.

\section{A cover with an explicit global error budget}
Fix a cleaned triangle $U\subseteq X\times Y$, with rows ordered from
largest to smallest neighborhoods, and an integer threshold $\Delta>0$.
For each pointer $\alpha$ fixing at most $w$ coordinates whose matching row
set $S_\alpha$ is predense, choose the earliest prefix of $S_\alpha$
containing a dense witness $D_\alpha$. Write $x_\alpha$ for its last row.
All matching rows at or below this row are called secured by this strip.
For each output pattern $\eta$ on $I=\operatorname{fix}(\alpha)$ form
\[
 Y_{\alpha,\eta}=U[x_\alpha]\cap
       \{y:y_{i,\alpha_i}=\eta_i\ \text{for }i\in I\}.      \tag{5}
\]
Accept a nonempty cell if $|Y_{\alpha,\eta}|\ge2^{b\nu-\Delta}$;
otherwise put its columns into this node's error set. The inner rectangle
$D_\alpha\times Y_{\alpha,\eta}$ of an accepted cell lies in $U$. The
neighborhood intersection in (5) is indispensable.

\begin{lemma}[Cover and density bounds]\label{lem:cover}
The unsecured rows are fewer than $b^{\nu-(1-\delta)w}$. There are at most
\[
 M_w=\sum_{j=0}^w\binom\nu j(2b)^j\le(2b\nu)^{w+1}        \tag{6}
\]
pointer/output cells, and the column-error density is at most
$M_w2^{-\Delta}$. For a secured row and any strip securing it, every
neighbor not in the column-error set lies in an accepted cell of that strip.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The last statement follows by partitioning $U[x_\alpha]$ into output cells
and using neighborhood inclusion. Summing the densities of rejected cells
gives the column bound.

Let $E$ be the unsecured rows and suppose
$|E|\ge b^{\nu-(1-\delta)w}$. If uniform $E$ is dense, its empty-pointer
strip exists. Otherwise choose an inclusion-maximal nonempty coordinate
set $I$ with a fiber $E_{I=a}$ whose probability exceeds
$b^{-\delta|I|}$. Its cardinality is at most $b^{\nu-|I|}$, so
\[
 b^{\nu-(1-\delta)w-\delta|I|}
 < |E_{I=a}|\le b^{\nu-|I|}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |I|<w.
\]
Maximality implies that the conditional fiber is dense on free coordinates:
a violating free-coordinate fiber would give a violating strict superset
of $I$. In either case a dense fiber of $E$ is a witness for an allowed
strip. That strip's threshold is no later than the fiber's last row, so
that last row is secured. This contradicts its membership in $E$.
\end{proof}

\section{The no-weakening inference invariant}
The following lemma is the new local formalization. It works even when
some child clauses have already been replaced by strictly stronger
subclauses. The code emits ordinary Resolution only.

\begin{lemma}[Cube-subclause derivation]\label{lem:subclause}
Let $I,J\subseteq[\nu]$ and let $\eta\in\{0,1\}^I$. For every
$\sigma\in\{0,1\}^J$ agreeing with $\eta$ on $I\cap J$, suppose a clause
$E_\sigma\subseteq C_{J,\sigma}$ has already been derived. There is a
Resolution derivation from these clauses of a clause
\[
                E\subseteq C_{I\cap J,\eta}\subseteq C_{I,\eta}
\]
with additional depth at most $|J\setminus I|$, width at most $|J|$, and
at most $2^{|J\setminus I|}-1$ new Resolution inferences. No weakening is used.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Order the free variables $J\setminus I$ and construct the full binary tree
of their assignments. At a leaf use $E_\sigma$. At a branch on variable
$v$, the two recursively returned clauses are subclauses of the appropriate
common prefix clause with $v$ and $\neg v$, respectively. If the first
clause lacks $v$, return it unchanged. Otherwise, if the second lacks
$\neg v$, return that clause unchanged. If both pivots are present, resolve
them. Nonpivot literals agree with the common prefix, so no tautological
resolvent is introduced. Induction on the remaining variables proves the
subclause invariant. Each root-to-leaf path adds at most one inference per
free variable; width never exceeds $|J|$. The full binary tree has at most
$2^{|J\setminus I|}-1$ internal vertices, and skipped branches only reduce
that count. Returning a previous line is not a new inference.
\end{proof}

This is a constructive subclause-pruning argument, not the invalid operation
of deleting a weakening line while leaving all its later inferences
unchanged. The invariant permits the later pivots to disappear and prunes
those inferences as well.

\section{Formal spread-budget lifting theorem}\label{sec:lift}
\begin{theorem}[No-weakening spread lifting]\label{thm:lift}
Fix $C$ from theorem S. Let $F$ be an unsatisfiable CNF on $\nu\ge1$
variables; let $b\ge3$, $1\le w\le\nu$ be integers and
$0<\delta<1-1/\log b$. Put
\[
 \ell=\ceil{\log(2\nu)},\qquad h=\ceil{\log(2b\nu)}.
\]
If
\[
             b^\delta\ge64C(w+1)\ell h,                 \tag{7}
\]
then any binary triangle DAG of size $s\le\tfrac12b^{(1-\delta)w}$ and
depth $d$ solving $\operatorname{Search}(F)\circ\IND_b^\nu$ gives a
strict Resolution refutation of $F$ with
\[
 W\le w,\qquad D\le wd,\qquad S\le s M_w2^w.            \tag{8}
\]
These are proof-existence bounds. No polynomial-time discovery procedure for
the strips or the full-image row is asserted.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $\Delta=2(w+1)h+2$. Equation (7) gives
\[
 T=\floor{b^\delta/(4C\ell)}\ge16(w+1)h\ge\Delta+2.
\]
Process the original DAG once in a topological order from leaves to root.
Before processing a node remove all rows and columns placed in error sets
by earlier nodes. Construct and save its strips, then add its own errors
to the global sets. Removing whole rows and columns preserves nested
neighborhoods. There are at most $s$ row-error contributions, so their
union has density less than $1/2$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:cover}. The column union
has density at most
\[
 sM_w2^{-\Delta}
 \le 2^{wh-1+(w+1)h-2(w+1)h-2}=2^{-h-3}<1/2.            \tag{9}
\]
The estimate uses original nodes, not occurrences in an unfolded tree.

For each accepted cell indexed by $(I,\eta)$ we maintain a \emph{derived
subclause} of $C_{I,\eta}$. At a leaf, the full-image lemma on the inner
rectangle realizes the entire cube $z_I=\eta$. Every such assignment
falsifies the source clause labelling that leaf. This source clause must be
a subclause of $C_{I,\eta}$, and is used directly as an axiom of depth zero.

At an internal node, fix an accepted inner rectangle $D\times Y'$. It is
covered by the two original children and, because of the topological
cleaning, by the children as they existed when their own strips were saved.
There is a half-by-half subrectangle in one child: order rows by the first
child's neighborhoods and choose a median. If its neighborhood contains at
least half the columns, use the earlier half of rows and those columns;
otherwise use the later half and its column complement in the other child.
Include the median on both sides so odd cardinalities are covered.

The free-coordinate row density loses at most one bit, and column
deficiency is at most $\Delta+1<T$. Lemma~\ref{lem:image} therefore supplies
\emph{one row} $x$ of that subrectangle realizing the whole parent cube.
It avoids the child's own error rows, so some child strip secures it. Let
$J$ be that one strip's pointer support. All columns in the chosen
subrectangle avoid the child's errors. By security, for every $J$-pattern
compatible with the parent cube the corresponding child cell was accepted.
The inductive hypothesis already supplies a subclause of each such child's
clause. Lemma~\ref{lem:subclause} produces a subclause of the parent clause
with at most $w$ added depth and $2^w-1$ new inferences. The row $x$ is fixed
before varying the output assignment. Exchanging these quantifiers would
invalidate this step.

At the root the cleaned row and column sets each have density at least
$1/2$. For a nonempty coordinate set $J$,
\[
 \Pr[x_J=a]\le2b^{-|J|}\le b^{-\delta|J|},
\]
using $b^{1-\delta}>2$. Hence the whole remaining row set is dense. Root
neighborhoods all equal the remaining columns, of deficiency at most one.
Its empty-pointer cell is accepted. The maintained subclause of its empty
clause is itself empty. Along any original path at most $w$ new depth is
added per edge, proving $D\le wd$. Each node has at most $M_w$ cells; a leaf
cell requires one input line and an internal cell at most $2^w-1$ new lines.
Sharing all previously derived child clauses gives the line bound in (8).
\end{proof}

\section{The exact compiler interfaces, fully separated}
The fixed primary $L_{22}$ graph-colouring record uses transparent
seven-clause padding at the 00:35 PC22\_ALLFIELD1 path. The present transfer
uses the \emph{different} 00:10 selector-and-duplication compiler, denoted
$\Tcomp$, as implemented in CP22LIFT1. Both targets lie in $\Cdom$; this does
not transfer graph-colouring statements to the cylinder source. Neither
minimal unsatisfiability nor clause-monotonicity is asserted here.

For a nonempty proper 3-CNF occurrence list $K$ with $m$ clauses,
$\Tcomp(K)$ has $72m$ variables and $96m$ distinct clauses. It first splits
each $(a\vee b\vee c)$ into
\[
 (s\vee a\vee y),\ (\neg y\vee b\vee c),\
 (\neg s\vee\neg a\vee z),\ (\neg z\vee\neg b\vee\neg c),
\]
duplicates the occurrence list, pairs opposite occurrences into $R=12m$
clones, equates each clone group by a directed cycle, and pads triples of
implications with copies of the fixed forced-zero-port gadget $Q$.
Intermediate duplication is intentional and must not be deduplicated.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit linear-line Resolution lift]\label{lem:upper}
Given a strict Resolution refutation $\pi$ of $K$ with $S$ lines, the actual
selector compiler has a strict Resolution refutation with
\[
                 S'\le342m+2S+1,\qquad
                 W'\le\max\{4,W(\pi)+1\}.              \tag{10}
\]
The translation is uniform and has polynomial bit cost in its explicit input
and output. It uses the known source/compiler map, not anonymous inversion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The fixed width-three $Q$ port proof has 69 ancestor nodes and is explicitly
reconstructed and checked in the supplied program. There are $4m$ copies,
costing $276m$ lines. At most $2R=24m$ lines introduce and unpad the $R$
implications. Prefix and suffix paths around the cycles derive both
implication directions to the representative in at most $2R=24m$ additional
Resolution lines. Recovering the $4m$ representative split clauses costs
at most $16m$ lines, including their input clauses. Resolving each split pair
costs another $2m$ lines and gives the two selector-guarded versions of
each source clause. Simulate $\pi$ twice, once with $s$ appended and once
with all source variables complemented and $\neg s$ appended. The selector
is fresh, so every source pivot remains a legal pivot. The branches derive
$s$ and $\neg s$, followed by one final Resolution step. This costs at most
$2S+1$ lines. Summing gives (10). The extra guard raises source width by one;
all preparatory clauses have width at most four. Binary variable indices and
parent references are charged, so (10) is a line-count bound, not a linear
bit-cost assertion.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Semantic projection with the one-line allowance.}
Under clone identification, $s=0$, $y=\neg a$, $z=0$, and all gadget
variables zero, every target clause becomes a source clause or an inequality
valid on the Boolean cube. A valid projected inequality follows semantically
from any one source axiom. One shared helper axiom suffices, so the projected
semantic-CP proof has size at most $s+1$ and depth at most $D+1$. Literal
substitution preserves the Boolean entailment of every inference. The same
argument composes with properization by directly substituting its fresh
variables, instead of adding a helper once per stage. This is the previously
proved RS2 interface, restated to fix its cost convention. It is \emph{not}
a cheap ordinary-CP proof-search step.

The separated index encoding of a width-four source introduces extension
variables assigned by functions of only the row side or only the column
side. Pulling back a linear inequality therefore gives a triangle. Source
\cite[Section 2.6]{tradeoff} gives the exact search-relation reduction.
Combining it with Theorem~\ref{thm:lift} yields, for
$H=\Tcomp(P(\widetilde{\IND}_b(F)))$,
\[
 s+1\le\tfrac12b^{(1-\delta)w}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 D\ge\frac{d_F(w)}{w}-1.                                \tag{11}
\]
This sharpens RS4's conservative $((d_F(w)-1)/(w+1))-1$ bound.

\section{A constructive region of fixed-parameter trade-offs}\label{sec:parameters}
For fixed $k,c,a,\delta$ and $b=t^a$, the source bounds and index encoding,
properization, and Lemma~\ref{lem:upper} give
\[
 L_t=O(t^q\log t),\quad S_{\Res}(H_t)=O(t^u),\quad
 q=4a+c+1,\quad u=a(k+4)+k,\quad z=a(1-\delta)(k+c).       \tag{12}
\]
Here $\tfrac12t^z$ is the admissible lifting cap. The number of variables is
still the actual $\nu=\Theta_{k,c}(t^{c+1})$; only its logarithm occurs in
(7). For any fixed $a\delta>0$, (7) holds for all sufficiently large $t$.
No numerical threshold is asserted for the external constant $C$.

\begin{theorem}[Formal fixed-parameter region]\label{thm:region}
Subject to the external theorems S and W and the stated index-encoding
interface, for every fixed rational $p>1$ and $1<\beta<1+1/p$ there are
fixed rational/integer parameters producing a family $H_t\in\Cdom$ and
$r_t=t^{u+1}$ with a Resolution proof of size at most $r_t$ and
\[
 \forall\tau:\ H_t\vdash_{\sCP}\bot,
 \quad S(\tau)\le r_t^\beta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad D(\tau)=\Omega(L_t^p).        \tag{13}
\]
The bound is nonvacuous and $r_t$ is polynomially bounded in $L_t$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a rational $\rho$ strictly between $\beta-1$ and $1/p$. Put
\[
 g=1+\rho-\beta>0,\qquad
 \delta=\frac{g}{2(1+\rho)},\qquad
 a=\ceil{4\beta/g},\qquad H_0=ag/2-\beta>0.
\]
Choose an integer $k$ strictly larger than
\[
 \max\left\{
 \frac{p(4a+1)}{1-p\rho},\quad
 \frac{a(1-\delta)+\beta(4a+1)}{H_0},\quad
 \frac4\rho,\quad4\right\},\qquad c=\floor{\rho k}.       \tag{14}
\]
Then $3\le c<k$ and
\[
 k-pq\ge(1-p\rho)k-p(4a+1)>0,
\]
\[
 z-\beta(u+1)
 \ge H_0 k-a(1-\delta)-\beta(4a+1)>0.                    \tag{15}
\]
All these choices are made once, before $t$ grows. For sufficiently large
$t$, the source graph hypotheses, the root-density condition, and (7) hold.
The upper proof is $O(t^u)\le r_t$. By the second margin in (15), every
proof of size at most $r_t^\beta$ satisfies the cap in (11), including the
$+1$ and the factor $1/2$. The source width--depth lower bound gives depth
$\Omega(t^k)$, and the first strict margin absorbs $(\log t)^p$ to imply
$\Omega(L_t^p)$. Finally $L_t\ge\nu\ge2k^2t^{c+1}$ for the explicit
construction, so $r_t$ is bounded by a fixed power of $L_t$.
\end{proof}

Equation (1) is a sufficient feasible region for this fixed-parameter ledger,
not a universal boundary for all constructions. It also follows as a
necessary asymptotic ratio condition within this particular ledger: writing
$c/k\to\rho$, depth requires $\rho<1/p$, while the size-cap condition in
the ideal $a\to\infty,\delta\to0$ limit requires $\rho>\beta-1$.
No impossibility claim outside the displayed sufficient-bound framework is made.

\paragraph{Two concrete choices.}
The earlier RS4 constants $k=400000,c=164000,a=500,\delta=1/200$ satisfy
\[
 k-\tfrac{12}{5}q=\tfrac{7988}{5},\qquad
 z-\tfrac75(u+1)=\tfrac{135993}{5}.
\]
The automatic rational recipe for $(p,\beta)=(12/5,7/5)$ instead gives
$\rho=49/120$, $\delta=1/338$, $a=672$, $k=322681$, $c=131761$.
Its depth margin is exactly one and its size-cap margin is
$378319354/845$. Both choices use the true dimension. These are asymptotic
parameter specifications, not practical SAT instances.

\paragraph{The quantifier limit.}
The theorem is $\forall(p,\beta)\ \exists(H_t)$ in the stated region. It is
not $\exists(H_t)\ \forall p$, and does not supply a single-family
superpolynomial lower bound by allowing $p$ to grow. It certainly does not
replace the separate quantifier over every total uniform $L_{22}$ decider.
