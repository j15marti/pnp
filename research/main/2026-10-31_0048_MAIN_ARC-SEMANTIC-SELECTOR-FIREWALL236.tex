\section{Exact semantic-selector theorem}
Let $L\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$ be a nontrivial decision language and choose fixed
strings $y_1\in L$, $y_0\notin L$.  Define
\[
 \Psi_L(x)=\begin{cases}y_1,&x\in L,\\y_0,&x\notin L.\end{cases}
 \tag{1}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Semantic contraction versus construction]
Equation~(1) is a total deterministic, constant-output, exactly
label-preserving map.  It is hereditary in the semantic sense.  Given any
total decider $A$ for $L$, the direct implementation ``run $A(x)$, then emit
$y_{A(x)}$'' has
\[
 T_{\Psi_A}(n)\ge T_A(n),\qquad
 S_{\Psi_A}(n)\ge S_A(n)-O(1)
 \tag{2}
\]
on the same execution, up to the chosen conventions for the output buffer.
Conversely, any implementation of this \emph{particular two-output map} decides
$L$ by comparing its output with $y_1$.  Thus a fast constructor for (1) is
already a fast decider; semantic existence alone supplies no speedup.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The definition gives $x\in L$ iff $\Psi_L(x)=y_1\in L$, and the output length
is the fixed maximum of $|y_0|,|y_1|$.  Applying the map again preserves the
same label.  In the declared direct implementation, every transition and live
cell used by $A(x)$ occurs before emission, so the execution's time and peak
space cannot be smaller than those of the completed parent call, proving (2).
For the converse, the two fixed outputs are syntactically distinguishable; run
the constructor and accept exactly when it emits $y_1$.  No hardness or lower
bound is used. \qed
\end{proof}

For ordinary SAT take $y_1$ to be the proper clause
$(x_1\vee x_2\vee x_3)$ and $y_0$ to be all eight signed proper clauses on
the same three variables.  The former has seven models and the latter none.
This gives an exact ordinary-SAT-to-ordinary-SAT constant child on both labels,
but it spends the SAT answer bit in its construction.

\begin{corollary}[DELAY firewall for the ARC recurrence]
Suppose the desired uniform recurrence has constants
$\alpha,\gamma\ge0$, $0\le\lambda<1$ independent of the guest and claims
\[
 K'\le \max\{\alpha,\gamma+\lambda K\}. \tag{3}
\]
If $\Phi(A)$ must complete $A(F)$ at parent length, then (3) fails for every
fixed guest exponent
\[
 K>\max\left\{\alpha,\frac{\gamma}{1-\lambda}\right\}. \tag{4}
\]
In particular, at $(\alpha,\gamma,\lambda)=(11/10,3/5,1/2)$ the threshold is
$6/5$; every DELAY-wrapped exact guest with $K>6/5$ defeats the claimed bound.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The completed parent call gives $K'\ge K$.  Inequality (4) implies both
$K>\alpha$ and $K>\gamma+\lambda K$, contradicting (3).  A DELAY wrapper can
add a prescribed polynomial delay before invoking any fixed exact decider,
without changing either SAT or UNSAT correctness. \qed
\end{proof}

This is a firewall for the specified architecture, not a general SAT time
lower bound.  A successful compiler must avoid, preempt, or replace the
parent-size call and prove its own charged source-relative child construction.
