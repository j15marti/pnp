\section{LITERATURE CHECK AND REVISIONS}
Ellis and Linial prove that for fixed $r,d$ there are $r$-uniform $d$-regular hypergraphs of logarithmic Berge girth; their Corollary 6 gives
\[
g_{r,d}(n)>\frac{\log n-\log4}{\log(d-1)+\log(r-1)}-1.
\]
For girth greater than two these hypergraphs are linear. Taking $r=3,d=16$ supplies exactly the support object needed by Candidate A. Degree 16 is the smallest integer degree for which the elementary random-signing first moment below is strictly below one.

Cavalar--Fabris--Mukhopadhyay--Srinivasan--Yehudayoff, ECCC TR26-220, prove robust-sunflower derandomization for $O(w)$-wise independent distributions and also for certain locally-independent approximately-identically-distributed marginals. Their theorem makes Candidate C plausible, but the abstract alone does not certify that the planted distribution satisfies their exact local-independence definition. Candidate C therefore remains OPEN rather than being silently imported.

Bazzi's DNF-fooling theorem requires polylogarithmic-wise independence on the full cube. The planted distribution proved below has strong fixed-coordinate closeness but is not shown to be globally $k$-wise independent, so Bazzi does not directly close the bridge.

\section{CONSTRUCTION OR PROOF ATTEMPT}
\subsection{High-girth bounded-occurrence UNSAT cores}
\begin{theorem}[High-girth UNSAT core]
There exists an infinite sequence of proper distinct-clause 3-CNFs $K_j$ such that, writing $v_j$ for the number of variables and $k_j$ for the number of clauses:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $k_j=16v_j/3$;
\item every variable occurs in exactly 16 clauses;
\item the 3-uniform clause-support hypergraph is linear;
\item its Berge girth is $\Omega(\log v_j)$; and
\item $K_j$ is UNSAT.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply Ellis--Linial with $r=3,d=16$ and an increasing size parameter. Their girth lower bound tends to infinity and is at least $(\log v-\log4)/\log30-1$ for the resulting hypergraph because its actual vertex count $v$ is at most the size parameter. Hence the resulting vertex counts are unbounded, and girth greater than two implies linearity. Regularity gives $k=16v/3$ support edges.

For every support edge independently choose one of its eight signed proper clauses uniformly. Fix an assignment $a$. Exactly one of the eight signings on each 3-edge is falsified by $a$, so the probability that $a$ satisfies every clause is $(7/8)^k$. Therefore the expected number of satisfying assignments is
\[
2^v(7/8)^{16v/3}=\left(2(7/8)^{16/3}\right)^v.
\]
The base is strictly below one: cubing the inequality is equivalent to $7^{16}<8^{15}$, and
\[7^{16}=33232930569601<35184372088832=8^{15}.\]
Thus the expectation is below one for every positive $v$, while the number of satisfying assignments is a nonnegative integer. Some signing has zero satisfying assignments. Choose such a signing for each support hypergraph.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Minimal constant-degree check.}
For a 3-uniform $d$-regular support the same first moment has base $2(7/8)^{d/3}$. The threshold is
\[d>\frac{3\log2}{\log(8/7)}=15.572679\ldots,\]
so $d=16$ is the least integer degree for which this particular argument forces an UNSAT signing.

\subsection{Dilution into the exact density-3 slice}
Fix a core $K$ with $k$ clauses. Randomly inject its variables into the $q$ target variables and independently complement target variables; this sends each core clause marginally uniformly over the $M=8\binom q3$ clause coordinates and preserves UNSAT. Let the resulting random core clause set be $\mathbf K$. Conditional on $\mathbf K$, choose $\mathbf R$ uniformly among all $(t-k)$-subsets of the remaining $M-k$ coordinates, where $t=3q$, and output
\[\mathbf Y=\mathbf K\cup\mathbf R.\]
Then $\mathbf Y$ is always an exact-$t$ UNSAT formula.

\begin{lemma}[Exact one-point marginals]
For every clause coordinate $C$, $\Pr[C\in\mathbf Y]=t/M$, exactly the same as the uniform exact-$t$ slice.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Random signed embedding gives $\Pr[C\in\mathbf K]=k/M$. Hence
\[
\Pr[C\in\mathbf Y]=\frac{k}{M}+\left(1-\frac{k}{M}\right)\frac{t-k}{M-k}=\frac{t}{M}.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local planting bound]
Let $\mathbf N$ be a uniformly random $t$-subset of the $M$ clause coordinates. For every fixed coordinate set $S$ of size $r$ with $k+r<M$,
\[
d_{\rm TV}(\mathbf Y|_S,\mathbf N|_S)
\le \frac{rk}{M}+\frac{rk}{M-k-r}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the union bound and uniform one-point marginals of the embedded core,
\[\Pr[\mathbf K\cap S\ne\varnothing]\le rk/M.\]
Condition on $\mathbf K\cap S=\varnothing$. The restriction of the filler to $S$ is sampling without replacement with population $M-k$ and success budget $t-k$, whereas $\mathbf N|_S$ uses population $M$ and budget $t$. Reveal the $r$ coordinates sequentially. After $j<r$ revelations and $x$ selected coordinates, the two next-coordinate probabilities differ by
\[
\left|\frac{t-k-x}{M-k-j}-\frac{t-x}{M-j}\right|
\le \frac{k}{M-k-r}.
\]
A maximal coupling over the $r$ reveals therefore fails with probability at most $rk/(M-k-r)$. Add the probability that the core directly hits $S$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Polylogarithmic local invisibility]
If $k=O(q^\alpha)$ for a fixed $\alpha<1$ and $r=\operatorname{polylog}q$, then with $M=8\binom q3=\Theta(q^3)$ the local total-variation bound is
\[O(rq^{\alpha-3})=o(1).\]
In particular one may obtain such cores by applying Ellis--Linial at a size parameter $q^\alpha$ whenever the resulting core has $k<3q$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Why high girth blocks the previous local-fingerprint escape}
If $\ell$ core edges satisfy $\ell<g$, their support hypergraph contains no Berge cycle. For a 3-uniform linear hyperforest with $\ell$ edges and $c$ connected components, its incidence graph is a forest, so it uses exactly
\[v=2\ell+c\]
variables. The number of embeddings of a fixed such forest shape into $q$ variables is $\Theta(q^{2\ell+c})$. A fixed signed $\ell$-clause pattern is contained in a uniform exact-$3q$ formula with probability
\[\frac{(3q)_\ell}{(M)_\ell}=\Theta(q^{-2\ell}).\]
Hence the expected number of copies of a fixed constant forest type in the negative distribution is $\Theta(q^c)$, rather than $o(1)$. Thus the earlier local-fingerprint union-bound mechanism that defeated four-clause Tseitin bundles cannot separate the planted high-girth positives using any fixed forest motif. This is an expectation statement, not yet a whp occurrence theorem.

\section{BARRIER AUDIT}
This is not yet a monotone-circuit lower bound. A polynomial-size monotone circuit can combine polynomially many locally innocuous events, and local total-variation closeness alone does not fool such a circuit. The robust-sunflower theorem must be matched to the exact planted distribution, including its local-independence hypothesis and the positive-spread conditions of the circuit approximation argument. No such transfer is proved here.

The high-girth theorem is representation-level and relativizes; it is not the nonrelativizing hinge. No cryptographic assumption is used. Natural-Proofs concerns must be revisited only if the eventual monotone lower bound is phrased as a large constructive property.
