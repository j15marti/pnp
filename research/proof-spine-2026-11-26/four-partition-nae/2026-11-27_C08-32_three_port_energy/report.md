## A: exact local energy and closed 3-port messages
First contract pair parity guards with signed union-find, rejecting inconsistent parity cycles. Substitute original IDs with representative plus XOR offset. Keep lift map. With m phase owners, sum equal signed-literal pairs E=m+2u (u violations); each pair factor has two integer costs indexed by XOR. Constants, loops and opposing parallel interactions combined exactly; count original owners m unchanged. Min E>m is sound NO.
For a region sharing at most three variables S with its outside, define f_S(b)=min over all region interiors of the sum of region factors at boundary b. No count multiplicities are assumed. All factors are complement invariant, hence f_S(b)=f_S(~b). There are at most four relative patterns. For three ports 0,1,2 let y=x0 XOR x1,z=x0 XOR x2. Writing f00=A,f10=B,f01=C,f11=D, the exact identity is
f=A + ((B+D-C-A)/2)y + ((C+D-B-A)/2)z + ((B+C-D-A)/2)(y XOR z).
For zero or one port the function is constant; for two it is one pair table. Three-port summary is exactly pairwise, not a relaxation. Keep each message as an atomic clique-supported table until a terminal expands it; this prevents fractional entries being split across subproblems and preserves integer nonnegative costs. No higher-degree term is introduced.

## B: paid recursive acquisition and matching terminal
Construct graph on phase variables, making each factor support a clique (retain zero structural edges). Include parent boundary S as a virtual clique. If the graph is planar, or has at most L vertices, or satisfies the uniform-clique terminal below, compute the full parent table. Otherwise enumerate vertex sets T of size 0..3, test components after deleting T. If a separator exists, choose a component B avoiding S outside T; this exists since S is a clique and all of S outside T lies in one component. Set A to remaining vertices including T; B child is B union T. Partition factors exactly, assigning factors touching B to the B child and all others to A. No factor can touch both exclusive sides. Recurse on B child with boundary T; import its entire minimum table as ONE factor into A, and recurse on A with the original S. Equality of optimum follows by independent minimization of the B interior for every fixed A word. Complete separator T to clique in each child (explicit or virtual). If no separator and no admitted terminal, UNKNOWN. This is a deterministic greedy algorithm-defined admission class; not a theorem that any possible decomposition is discovered by this particular choice.

For each planar torso, expand its atomic messages into pair costs using A; virtual edges ensure the clique expansion preserves planarity. For a requested parent relative pattern, add penalties on at most two parent-clique edges, M=1+sum_e |c_e(0)-c_e(1)|, to all violating parities. A valid boundary assignment always exists on the free spin domain; its raw cost is at most sum of edge maxima plus constants. Any violating assignment has cost at least sum of minima plus constants plus M. Thus every optimum obeys the boundary pattern and pays no penalty. Multiply all pair costs by 2 if necessary to clear halves (atomic tables are integer). For each edge choose preferred parity p_e of smaller cost, w_e=absolute difference. The remaining optimization is minimum sum w_e over error edge sets X for which p XOR 1_X is a primal cut. In each connected planar component, primal cuts are exactly edge vectors of even parity on every face boundary (face walks count bridges twice); equivalently the dual edges X have odd-degree set T_f consisting of faces whose preferred parity sum is odd. A minimum-weight dual T_f-join is obtained by shortest paths and minimum-weight perfect matching of terminals in each dual connected component. Proof: any T-join decomposes into terminal-pair paths plus cycles, so matching cost <= its cost; XOR the shortest paths of a minimum matching to get a T-join of cost <= matching cost (weights nonnegative). Dual loops from bridges need not be selected. This yields equality and a witness via propagating p XOR 1_X on the primal graph. This is the known planar MaxCut/spin-glass dual matching method (Hadlock 1975 original abstract; Maynard-Rammal 1980 original abstract); signed-cost and pin adapters proved here, no new matching theorem. For the small-torso terminal enumerate 2^L assignments once, compute all parent states jointly. This run executed small-torso terminal, not planar matching implementation. For optional witness reconstruction store one minimizer per table state and recompose on matching shared relative patterns, complementing one entire child if needed. NO requires minimum strictly above m, not failure to acquire tables.
