## A. Triple-group hardness even for explicit star chord diagrams
Let X3C universe U have 3q elements, sets S_i each of size three. Standard NP-completeness follows from 3-dimensional matching on three disjoint q-element shores (take triples as 3-sets), Karp 1972. Introduce mandatory matrix index e per element and occurrence index o_{i,e} for each e in S_i. Matrix A over GF(2) has A[e,o_{i,e}]=A[o_{i,e},e]=1 and all other entries zero. The free indices partition into groups G_i={o_{i,e}:e in S_i}. All mandatory indices selected, groups selected in their entirety or not at all. Explicit matrix dimension |U|+3m, O((|U|+m)^2) bits.

For a group selection K, let r_e be the number of selected triples containing e. Reordering the principal submatrix by element gives a direct sum of adjacency matrices of K_{1,r_e}. For r_e=0, nullity=1; for r_e>=1, rank=2 (two distinct independent rows, all other rows duplicate), nullity=r_e-1. Thus nullity exactly sum_e |r_e-1|. Zero iff every element occurs once. This is an exact polynomial many-one reduction, and membership in NP follows by selection and Gaussian elimination. Explicit circle-graph restriction: for each e, place endpoints in a disjoint circular interval as e,o_1,...,o_r,e,o_r,...,o_1. Every leaf crosses only its center; no inter-element crossings. Concatenate these intervals and connect the circle. Thus even supplied chord geometry and star-local rank do not make joint triple decision polynomial by the known pair theorem. This is NP-hardness of an exact decision problem, not an exponential lower bound. The all-or-none relation fails the delta-matroid symmetric exchange axiom as above; that alone does not rule out arbitrary gadgets with auxiliary variables and pair constraints. If a uniform polynomial such gadget existed for all these instances, Geelen-Iwata-Murota parity would solve X3C. No claim that these particular star diagrams are all in the image of the C8 adapter.
