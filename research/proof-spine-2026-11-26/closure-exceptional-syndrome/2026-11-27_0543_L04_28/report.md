## 2. General permutation-distance certificate (constructive and contradiction)
Let D be any finite directed graph. Let tau be a permutation of original tail labels, and define D' by moving every outgoing list at u to tau(u). Every original cover p (a bijective successor map) corresponds bijectively to p'=p composed with tau^{-1}. This is a coordinate permutation of the bipartite matching problem, not a decision-equivalent HC transformation by itself. If tau is a product of s transpositions, c(p)>=c(p')-s: each successor swap either merges two cycles or splits one into two. Proof: remove the two outgoing arcs, then reconnect their heads exchanged; distinct cycles merge, same cycle splits. This holds even when intermediate digraphs lack original arcs, since permutations exist abstractly. If D' has disjoint components V_i, and every attained local cover has at least l_i cycles, then c(p)>=sum_i l_i-s. A verified sum_i l_i>s+1 refutes D. The cost s can be as low as n-c(tau), the exact transposition length, from its disjoint cycle decomposition, but no need for optimization. Contradiction version: a Hamiltonian p could split into at most 1+s cycles after rewiring, incompatible with verified local bounds. Unlike L04-26's one bridge, cyclic interfaces are permitted; no additive cost lower bound on arbitrary executions inferred.

For a bipartite cover graph whose components are six-cycles (3 left tails, 3 right heads), each admits precisely two matchings, whose relative successor permutations are 3-cycles, hence even. Any reference cover's parity is invariant under all block choices. After rewiring, if local reference cycle count even, l_i=2, else l_i=1. No enumeration of global covers. In any partition of original vertices into modules of whole head groups, each tail's permitted heads all belong to one module. If cross tails between every pair of modules balance, pair opposite tails arbitrarily and exchange lists to restore internal edges. Verify this balance and no remaining cross arcs. More general balanced flows can use a permutation of cross rows, but not implemented.
