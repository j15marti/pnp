## 2. Same source, exact adapters, and unconditional closure

Empty row/column is NO; a singleton forces its edge and excludes competing row/column arcs. Iterate these forced deletions to fixed point initially and within every guard, and after every import. No conditional inference exported except discharge by impossibility of e. Forced proper subtours are another valid closure (not separately implemented in A; connectivity B catches them). This is limited unconditional degree closure, not a free complete forced-information oracle.

Track A (local): exact cycle-cover support through perfect matching. Bipartite left and right copies of vertices, edges corresponding to H. A Hamilton cycle is a perfect matching, so absent perfect matching certifies NO. Given perfect matching pi, orient an auxiliary graph on left vertices with arc a->pi^{-1}(b) for every actual edge (a,b). Edge (a,b) lies in some perfect matching iff pi^{-1}(b) reaches a: for unmatched edge, a directed cycle containing that arc rotates the matching; conversely symmetric difference of two perfect matchings is disjoint alternating cycles. Matched edge supported by pi. Thus exact matching-support projection removes all and only edges that are not in any cycle cover (not exact Hamiltonian support). Its calculation is polynomial by one maximum matching and reachability/SCC. If any Hamilton cycle existed before, all of its edges survive; reverse inclusion immediate. If initial matching absent, a Hall-deficient set or bipartite vertex cover smaller than M certifies NO. No counting of matchings.

Track B (global): separator-chain obstruction. For S subset of vertices with 1<=|S|<M, let Q be SCC condensation of H-S, with l vertices. Let B_Q be bipartite graph of strict reachability between SCCs, not merely immediate arcs. Removing S from any Hamilton cycle partitions its remaining vertices into at most |S| consecutive path segments. Each projects to a chain in Q, possibly repeated SCCs across different segments. Assign each SCC to just one segment in which it occurs; deleting duplicates from other chains preserves strict reachability. If a Hamilton cycle exists, Q has a disjoint chain cover of at most |S| chains. Join consecutive SCCs of each chain into matching edges in B_Q: matching size at least l-|S|. Consequently if nu(B_Q)<l-|S|, NO. A cover of B_Q of size <l-|S| gives a directly checkable negative certificate, obtained via standard alternating reachability. Equivalently width=l-nu exceeds |S| by Dilworth; proof of necessary direction above independent of theorem. S empty is separately checked for strong connectivity. For k fixed, enumerate all S of size <=k, no exponential set search. For k=1, width>1 iff two surviving vertices lie in incomparable SCCs (neither reaches other); audit uses this shortcut. ## 4. Infinite NO family defeating every fixed-radius test in this hybrid
For any fixed k, take s>=max(6,k+3), b>=6. Augmented symmetric directed graph D(s,b) consists of universal clique U of size s (including dummy 0) and s+1 disjoint bidirected cliques of b vertices; each vertex of U adjacent in both directions to every other vertex, no interlobe arcs. Remove dummy to produce canonical native original graph; n=s+(s+1)b-1. With full clock T, this is NO: removing U from any hypothetical Hamilton cycle would leave at most s path segments, but s+1 nonempty lobes are mutually incomparable. Genuine original encoding O(n² log n) bits, no stronger clock substitution. Connected, every initial row/column degree>=2.

Prove fixed-k singleton-arc-conditioned closure stays UNKNOWN, not just finite experiment. Every arc e lies in a cycle cover (take its 2-cycle and cycle remaining vertices within each clique). More strongly every pair e,f of compatible arcs (different tails and heads, or identical) lies in one cycle cover: if disjoint close each by reverse; if already 2-cycle use it; if they form a directed length-two path, close it by endpoint edge when available, else route endpoint to a spare universal vertex and back to start. In every case at most four vertices consumed; each clique has >=6 vertices so each remaining clique has >=2 and can be covered by a directed cycle. Hence support projection in any pin removes nothing beyond row/column conflicts; degree closure no further changes (all unpinned rows/cols originally at least b+s-1 neighbors, lose at most one). For any S with |S|<=k, at least one universal vertex outside S and the two pinned endpoints remains. All other surviving vertices apart from these endpoints form a strongly connected core. If both endpoints remain, u->v plus core->u and v->core joins them into the core; if only u remains it is a sink reachable from core; if only v remains it is a source reaching core. In each case reachability on SCCs is a total chain, width1, so no nonempty S test fails; S empty strongly connected. Unpinned case immediate. Thus no guard disproved and hybrid stabilizes without deletion. This is a rigorous scoped negative-coverage obstruction for every fixed k, even if all tested arcs and cuts acquired. Not a lower bound against arbitrary deciders, not even arbitrary separator acquisition.
