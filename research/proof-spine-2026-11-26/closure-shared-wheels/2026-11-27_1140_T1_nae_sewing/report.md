## Theorem
For every assignment to the surviving variables, B4 with one private port p has an extension iff the remaining three signed literals are not all equal: their integer sum must be 1 or 2. The required p is uniquely 2 minus that sum (adjust sign). Deduplicating identical B tuples up to complete complement is exact. For nonempty signed literal lists A,B not involving p, existential p [NAE(A,p) AND NAE(B,not p)] iff NAE(A concatenated B). If A is mixed, it imposes no p restriction; otherwise all A=a forces p=1-a. If B is mixed, it imposes none; otherwise all B=b forces p=b. These requirements conflict iff both lists are constant with the same value. They may share surviving original carriers arbitrarily; the proof holds pointwise for the SAME assignment, including repeated or opposite signed literals. If pivot occurs elsewhere (including a guard), the bucket must be completed or the rewrite is not licensed. Whole-factor sign reversal leaves NAE invariant and orients the pivot. Reverse records reconstruct every removed bit; global complement gauge is equisatisfiable, not an unconditional bit deduction. Every accepted local step decreases carrier count and never increases factor count. Fixed width <=4 as implemented; no assertion about arbitrary NAE list growth. Native parsing, signed equality closure, local matching by scans, constant truth tables, at most O(N) eliminations, explicit IDs and reverse records give polynomial preprocessing and replay (conservative O(N^4) bit-work with deterministic scan data structures). Final branching is not proved polynomial. No free semantic oracle; integer arithmetic for B occupancy, finite bit IDs, deterministic search.
