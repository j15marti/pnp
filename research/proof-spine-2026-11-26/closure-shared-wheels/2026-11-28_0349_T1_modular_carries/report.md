## 2. Modular carry theorem
User literal value v=x-neg, squared truth t=v² in {0,1}. Let Q=sum_occ t, including repeated occurrences if present. Elementary symmetric e_j(t)=sum_{|I|=j} product_{i in I} t_i. On each Boolean assignment e_j(t)=binom(Q,j) mod p over GF(p), and Lucas gives e_{p^a}(t) equal digit a of Q in base p. Self-contained p=2 proof: (1+z)^Q in GF2[z] factors into product of (1+z^{2^a}) over set bits of Q; coefficient of z^{2^a} is precisely bit a. Thus Q mod4 uses e1 and e2; e2 is the carry of parity, not an unrelated checksum. For B4, Q=2 iff e1=0,e2=1 since Q in [0,4]. For NAE3, Q in {1,2} iff 1+e1+e2=0; for NAE4, Q in {1,2,3} iff 1+e1+e2+e3=0 (the indicator of all-zero or all-one is product_i(1+t_i)+product_i t_i, highest term cancels). All identities remain valid on signed literals t= x or 1+x in GF2. Higher digits have degree p^a; keeping fixed degree polynomial, not a free arbitrary-modulus algebraic expansion. Prior exact weighted equations and domains retained. No claim novelty for Lucas identities or polynomial calculus.
