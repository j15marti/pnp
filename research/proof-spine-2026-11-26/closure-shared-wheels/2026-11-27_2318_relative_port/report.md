## Exact lemmas
1. Normalize signed transport by parity UF (or BFS). Equal/complement duplicate B rows are identical integer occupancy relations. A private carrier occurring once among deduplicated B rows can be eliminated: sum of other three in {1,2} iff a unique slack can complete sum=2. Signed NAE of three literals follows. This is an exact projection with reconstructible slack, not XOR approximation.
2. For any consistent signed quotient, if a NAE3 row has two signed literals known equal, the third must differ. If any known pair is opposite, that row is already satisfied. For guard x_u XOR x_v=b, saturate these implications with parity UF; conflict certifies opposite parity as necessary for all feasible assignments. Each independent union decreases components, so a complete guarded scan costs O(m(r+1)) row inspections plus logarithmic-depth UF, for m rows r carriers. Candidate pair dictionary can be enumerated in O(r^2), all failed guards charged. Repeating only after genuine contractions gives a scoped polynomial procedure; it does not guarantee a useful contraction or terminal on all NO inputs. A contradictory equation is a terminal, not an independent rank increase. If the current closed quotient has every surviving NAE3 row on three distinct components, and a tested pair of distinct components does not co-occur in any row, joining them cannot initiate this propagation. This is a scoped exact refusal for this rule, not for arbitrary RUP/PC.
3. A native projected chart is invariant under simultaneous complement, as are relative parity constraints. If F AND x_u=0 AND x_v=b has an UP conflict, complement gives the mirrored UP conflict under x_u=1,x_v=1-b. The two binary clauses excluding these two tuples entail x_u XOR x_v=1-b, each RUP-verifiable directly on the unpinned chart (even when a different gauge was used previously, provided the exact complement-invariant chart is checked). No general claim that a gauged nonlinear proof row has the same width after discharge. For a general checked gauged proof P in F|a=0, append a OR P and its mirror !a OR mirror(P), then empty; at most 2t+1 rows, width<=w+1. Exact projection and symmetry must both hold. Check direct RUP at root, do not assume anchor is pinned for free. 
4. Signed matching terminal scope: set d_uv=x_u XOR x_v; each NAE triangle has exactly one signed equal pair, so the three indicators of equality are exact-one. With at most two triangle occurrences per edge, identical edge phase at the two incidences is represented by a matching edge covering both required triangle vertices; opposite phases use a required intermediary adjacent to both (exactly one is covered). Single occurrence connects to optional dummy. Require all triangle vertices covered. This is a polynomial mandatory-vertex matching problem. If triangle boundaries span the carrier graph cycle space, each admissible exact-one selection obeys all cycle parity equations (the triangle has even XOR of the three d values); integrate d along a spanning forest, verify all edges. Otherwise additional cycle admission is an unresolved obligation; no free parity-constrained matching. Guards fixing ports or observations need separate consistency check. Neither raw common chart satisfies both hypotheses.
5. Linear NAE3 hypergraphs (any two triples intersect in at most one carrier) with distinct original variables and no prior contractions: a single pair guard activates at most one triple. If it activates, only that triple's three variables are merged; every other row meets the component in <=1 variable, so closure stops without conflict. A pair outside all triples activates none. The Fano plane is UNSAT: every same-color pair lies in exactly one line; without a monochromatic line each of seven lines contributes one same-color pair, but any two-color split of seven points has at least C(3,2)+C(4,2)=9. Gluing copies at one common hub preserves linearity and UNSAT, giving an infinite scoped refusal to single-pair parity closure, with a cheap local Fano bypass.
