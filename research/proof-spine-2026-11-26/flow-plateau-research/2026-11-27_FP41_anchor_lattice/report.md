## Connecting theorem
Unsigned incidence B over F3. A proper coloring is equivalent to By=0 and nonzero difference l_v between first two indexed incident edges at every vertex. For any anchor edge h set u=y-y_h*1. Let p0,p+,p- be incidence vectors of its three color classes relative to u. Each is a perfect matching, p0 contains h, p+,p- avoid h. Componentwise p+-p-=u, p++p-=u^2, p0=1-u^2. Acquire exact affine hull H_in p=b_in of matchings containing h and H_out p=b_out of matchings avoiding h (F3). Therefore every coloring satisfies
H_out(y-y_h*1)=0;
H_out (y-y_h*1)^2=2 b_out;
H_in (y-y_h*1)^2=H_in 1-b_in.
First is LINEAR, not merely a moment constraint; second and third are necessary quadratics. Empty either matching class refutes. No converse from hull membership or independent matching witnesses.

Polynomial acquisition dependency: Lovasz, Matching structure and the matching lattice, JCTB 43 (1987) 187-222, DOI 10.1016/0095-8956(87)90021-9; explicitly stated for EVERY finite field in El Maalouly, Steiner, Wulf, arXiv:2207.09797, Theorem 5.2, Section 5 proof of CPM. A deterministic polynomial algorithm computes the linear span of perfect matching vectors. For a nonempty graph with a matching, incidence of a fixed vertex star evaluates to 1 on every matching, so the affine hull equals its linear span intersected with star-sum=1. For required and forbidden edges, delete endpoints of required disjoint edges, delete forbidden edges, compute on residual graph and lift fixed coordinates; empty residual unique matching, empty family handled by ordinary matching feasibility. Thus every affine row is polynomially acquirable by the known theorem, not a counting or SAT oracle. Original Lovasz full paper not accessible via DOI; theorem statement and proof of its parity corollary read in arXiv original paper; production algorithm not implemented here.
