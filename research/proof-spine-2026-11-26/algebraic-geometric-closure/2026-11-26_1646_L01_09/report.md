## Source, protocol, quantifiers and falsifiers

Exact fingerprint (n,1^{n(n-1)}), empty guard; all masks are ordinary directed Hamiltonian-cycle (HC) decision inputs. A tour is a single-cycle successor permutation, counted once, not n starting vertices. H_n(X) is its formal generating polynomial. Initial certified unconditional closure: for n>=3 every edge occurs in some tour and is absent from another by permuting labels of a fixed tour; n=2 both are forced. Under any mask, absent edges remain absent; exact-tour masks have all their edges forced; deleting an outgoing edge from an exact tour gives an empty row and a certified NO. Do not assume general HC support closure is free. No narrower NP-hardness promise.

A chart R is ANY fixed subset of adjacency masks closed under coordinatewise ternary majority, and sound for ordinary HC: all masks in R contain a tour. It need not be monotone, efficiently represented, or efficiently discoverable. A static portfolio of q such charts must cover all YES masks, including exact-tour masks. Fixed means depends on n (nonuniform allowed) but not on the input mask. Guards are part of R; arbitrary non-bijunctive guards are not automatically admitted. Target: bound how many exact tours a chart can cover; use this to test replacement of L01-08's signed projection by a support-faithful portfolio of label-availability/min-cut adapters. Falsifiers: (1) majority of three chart tours accepted but not a tour; (2) a majority-closed set of tours exceeding claimed capacity; (3) a free implication SCC pair with no one-pair flip; (4) a purported affine image of a small fixed optimal-face portfolio containing all tours without spurious support. The first is a certificate of chart unsoundness, not a counterexample to the theorem's premises.

## A. Constructive exact capacity attainment
Partition the ordered vertices into k=floor(n/3) consecutive triples (a_i,b_i,c_i) and a remaining tail of r=n-3k vertices. Anchor a_i is the entry of triple i. Let t_i be the next anchor, or the first tail vertex if present, or vertex 0 at the end. The two local edge sets are E_i0={a_i->b_i,b_i->c_i,c_i->t_i} and E_i1={a_i->c_i,c_i->b_i,b_i->t_i}. Tail edges are fixed in their natural order, ending at 0 (for n=2 just the two tail edges). Choices use disjoint outgoing rows. Every tuple of k choices gives one distinct normalized tour, and every graph that contains a whole option per triple and the tail contains such a tour. Chart R_* is the conjunction over triples of ((AND_{e in E_i0} x_e) OR (AND_{e in E_i1} x_e)) and tail units. Distributing yields at most 9k+r positive binary/unit clauses. It is a sound monotone 2CNF over original edges. On an exact-tour input it accepts exactly these 2^k tours: any selected witness has n distinct edges, hence equals the input edge set. This proves the lower construction for every n>=2, with no target-supplied tour. Conditional guard: if exactly one local option is available, force that option in this chart; otherwise both options are supported by independent completion. No unconditional inference for ordinary HC outside the chart. Every lift uses original IDs. O(n log n) list bits, polynomial indexed construction/scan, O(n) local option checks. This is not a general HC algorithm.

## B. Global majority/SCC upper bound
Theorem: every sound majority-closed chart contains at most 2^{floor(n/3)} exact-tour masks. Thus the capacity is sharp.

Proof. Let T be its set of exact tours; if empty the claim is trivial. The majority of three exact-tour masks has at most one outgoing and at most one incoming selected edge per vertex. If it is in R, soundness forces it to contain a Hamiltonian cycle, which uses n edges. The majority mask therefore equals that tour. Hence T itself is majority closed.

If three distinct outgoing destinations of a vertex appear among tours in T, choose the three witnessing tours. Their coordinatewise majority has zero outgoing edges at that vertex, an impossible accepted mask. Hence each row uses at most two distinct edges over T. In particular at most 2n original coordinates vary (all edges outside the row supports are constant zero; a row with one possible destination is entirely fixed). The same two-choice assertion holds for incoming columns. The following elementary median-code lemma strengthens the immediate matching-component bound.

Lemma. Any nonempty majority-closed subset S of {0,1}^m with f varying coordinates and pairwise Hamming distance at least d between distinct words has |S|<=2^{floor(f/d)}. Remove constant coordinates first. S is the model set of the 2CNF consisting of all unary and binary clauses valid throughout S. To prove sufficiency, a word satisfying these clauses has each one- or two-coordinate restriction extendable to S. Induct on the number of coordinates to be matched. For a set of size >=3, take three extendable restrictions obtained by dropping three different coordinates; the majority of their extensions agrees on the entire set. Thus the full word is in S. This is an existence proof, not free acquisition of clauses from implicit S.

For this 2CNF, form its implication graph with the two complementary arcs per clause, units included. SCCs come in complementary pairs; satisfiability excludes a self-complementary SCC. Each solution selects exactly one SCC from each pair, and the selected SCCs are forward-closed. For any two models A,B, choose a sink C in the DAG induced by SCCs selected by B but not A. Switch C to true and its complement to false. Every outgoing arc from C leads to an already true SCC or C itself, because B is forward-closed and C is a sink in the difference. No arc from a still-true SCC E to the now-false complement of C can exist: contraposition would yield C->complement(E) to a still-false SCC. The switched assignment is therefore another model. Repeat until B. Every free complementary pair can consequently be flipped individually in some model pair (take A,B disagreeing on it). Such a flip changes exactly the original coordinates belonging to that pair. Every pair's block therefore has size >=d. Blocks disjointly partition the f varying coordinates, so at most floor(f/d) pairs exist, and each model is determined by one bit per pair. This proves the lemma, including the cases f=0 and |S|=1.

Two different n-cycle permutations cannot differ in just one row (bijectivity) or in just two rows (then their relative permutation is one transposition, contradicting equal permutation signs (-1)^(n-1)). Each differing row contributes two differing adjacency bits. Distinct tours thus have Hamming distance at least six. Apply the lemma with f<=2n and d=6. QED.

Consequence: there are (n-1)! normalized tours, so q >= ceil((n-1)!/2^{floor(n/3)}) = 2^{Omega(n log n)}. This is a static explicit sound-cover lower bound, independent of the computational power used to build each chart and even of nonuniformity. In original dense input length N it is 2^{Omega(sqrt(N) log N)}. A polynomial-size OR-of-charts and a polynomial explicit union with O(log N) extra Boolean selector bits cannot suffice. More generally if q charts each split into <=2^h branches whose *full accepted mask relations* including guards are bijunctive, q 2^h >= (n-1)!/2^{floor(n/3)}. It follows h+log2 q >= log2((n-1)!)-floor(n/3). This is not a lower bound for arbitrary adaptive preprocessing, unrestricted DAG sharing or a polynomial decider. A compact description of exponentially many charts is not an explicit evaluation schedule.

## C. Adversarial support projection and optimum faces
Reconstruct L01-08 B: fixed bipartite independent set graph, dual labels per vertex. Substitute every label weight by zero or a nonzero field scalar times a monomial with nonnegative integer exponents in original HC variables. For a Boolean original mask, a label is available exactly when all variables in its monomial support are present. Exponent size does not matter for feasibility. An independent-set assignment with all labels available gives a nonzero individual term over any field, even if its coefficient later cancels. The relation of accepted masks (there exists an available independent set) is a projection of 2CNF: independent-set constraints, fixed pins, and implications from each selected label to every required original edge bit. Existential projection preserves majority by lifting each of three masks to witnesses and applying majority to the entire joint assignments. Equivalently L01-08's explicit availability formula distributes into 2CNF. The same holds for any fixed bijunctive target relation and these label maps.

At stagnation tried a stronger filter: allow a fixed optimal face of the bipartite independent-set problem with arbitrary signed integer unary weights, plus pins, before applying availability. Complement the right-side labels, so each edge is an implication z_l<=z_r. The feasible family is closed under componentwise min and max, including pins. The unary objective is modular: c(u)+c(v)=c(min(u,v))+c(max(u,v)). If u,v are minimizers both right terms must achieve the same minimum. Hence the fixed optimum face is a lattice and majority-closed; projection with label requirements is still bijunctive. This is an unconditional result for fixed faces, not for recomputed objective thresholds depending on the input mask.

Consequently any such support-faithful cover of HC requires the factorial-scale number of charts above, regardless of signs or field. If every individual nonzero projected term contains an HC and every exact source tour is covered, each chart is sound and cover complete. Cancellations among genuine tours may occur; that does not repair missing support. L01-08's signed construction is not in the model: it has nonzero terms on NO graphs that cancel. The exact n=2 control independently enumerates this fact (see results below). No free weighted count or oracle. A polynomial many fixed min-cut terminal variants cannot fix the support mismatch in this adapter class.

## D. Complementary geometric obstruction, broader than bijunctive charts
KNOWN THEOREM: Rothvoss arXiv:1311.2369v4 Corollary 2 gives xc(P_TSP(n))=2^{Omega(n)} for the undirected complete-graph tour polytope. The directed tour polytope projects onto it by x_ij+x_ji for i<j, n>=3, so has at least the same extension complexity. Grochow 2017 Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 4.2 relate cancellation-free monomial projections to Newton polytope extensions; the following union variant is an explicit application, not a new external lower bound.

Suppose Q_i are nonempty bounded polytopes described by f_i inequalities (equalities free), and affine maps L_i z+d_i yield polytopes whose convex hull of the union is the directed tour polytope. Homogenize: lambda_i>=0, sum lambda_i=1, A_i z_i<=lambda_i b_i, with all equalities similarly scaled, and x=sum_i(L_i z_i+lambda_i d_i). For positive lambda_i divide by it to obtain a point of Q_i; for zero lambda_i boundedness implies z_i=0. Conversely any convex combination lifts. Thus the union hull has an extension with sum_i(f_i+1) inequalities. It follows sum_i(f_i+1)>=2^{Omega(n)}. In particular the conclusion holds if each chart's vertices map to actual tours, jointly covering all tours. It also holds when every nonnegative integer exponent image contains a tour in its support and every exact tour exponent occurs: intersect the union hull with sum_e x_e=n. Each generator has total degree>=n; equality generators are precisely actual tour vectors, so this is a face. This strengthens the affine obstruction to allow redundant edge factors.

The bipartite independent-set polytope Q={0<=s<=1,s_u+s_v<=1 for all uv} has 2b+|E| inequalities and is integral. Direct proof: complement right-side coordinates, obtaining order constraints z_l<=z_r. Thresholding at a common t in (0,1] preserves every inequality, and integrating threshold vectors gives exactly z as a convex combination. Pins and fixed linear optimal faces retain integrality. Under the dual-rail monomial projection the exponent vector is affine in s: sum_v[(1-s_v) alpha_v0+s_v alpha_v1]. Thus every exact support-faithful portfolio of these affine charts has total polyhedral size exponential, even with arbitrary affine coefficients or alternate integral low-EF target charts (e.g. bipartite matching). This proof does not require the November arithmetic claims; it does not apply to a nonlinear decoder or cancellation in the image.

Mandatory adversarial controls. General perfect matching is polynomial-time solvable although its polytope has exponential extension complexity by Rothvoss Theorem 1. Bipartite matching has small integral polytope, and its permutation masks have 2^{floor(n/2)} exact witnesses in the analogous block chart, since swapping two rows is legal; the HC sign step is source-sensitive. Determinant=permanent in characteristic 2 is an arithmetic bypass, not a symbolic HC bound. An equality block compactly has two choices; the even parity affine relation {000,011,101,110} is not majority closed, so affine/Gaussian charts are not automatically covered by the factorial bound. The geometric bound only covers affine output images with small EF. A nonlinear polynomial-size surjection from a Boolean cube onto all tours is trivial: give vertices 1..n-1 independent ceil(log2(n-1)) bit priorities, sort by priority and vertex ID, prepend 0 and close the tour. Every permutation occurs by assigning distinct ranks. All outputs valid; checking a supplied output against a graph mask is easy, but finding an accepted priority tuple is exactly ordinary HC. This falsifies a generic extension-complexity inference from arbitrary compact witness decoding, and identifies the missing *existential* work.
