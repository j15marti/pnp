## A. Constructive replica: exact contraction and padding
For 2<=k<=n and weighted k-vertex matrix X (diagonal zero), define E_(k,n)(X) by replacing vertex 1 with the path 1 -> k+1 -> ... -> n, with all path edge weights 1, incoming edges to 1 retaining their labels, outgoing edges formerly 1->j now n->j with weight x_1j, and edges between unchanged vertices retaining weight. All other edges involving the path are zero. For k=n use identity. In the polynomial specialization each nonzero monomial must traverse this path consecutively. Collapsing the path is a bijection to original normalized cycles, and preserves each monomial weight exactly. Algebraically H_n(E_(k,n)(X))=H_k(X). The same proof applies to any zero edge mask; do not infer SAT from a vanishing weighted evaluation in characteristic p.

For k>=3 and j!=1, let K_j(X) be the (k-1)-vertex matrix obtained by deleting j, keeping merged label 1, assigning outgoing weights from that label x_jb for b!=1,j, incoming weights x_a1 for a!=1,j, and retaining all other weights. Set diagonal zero. Every k-tour using edge 1->j corresponds bijectively to a (k-1)-tour, preserving all factors except x_1j. Conversely insert j immediately after 1. Every k-tour uses exactly one such edge; these witness classes are disjoint, so the arithmetic sum counts each once, not an OR of overlapping track votes.
H_k(X)=sum_(j=2..k) x_1j H_(k-1)(K_j(X)); H_2=x_12*x_21.
Padding and contraction work as formal polynomial identities over any commutative ring; no cancellation assumption. Conditional masks are retained as actual zero substitutions. Proof is explicit, no target model or oracle.

## B. Global algebraic replica: untyped circuit certification
Given one arbitrary candidate C of size s for H_n over fixed F_p, define C_k(X)=C(E_(k,n)(X)). Define discrepancies
D_2=C_2-x_12*x_21;
D_k=C_k-sum_(j=2..k) x_1j C_(k-1)(K_j(X)), for k>=3.
The candidate is correct iff ALL D_k are identically zero. Forward from A; reverse by induction from base, hence C=C_n=H_n. This argument treats signs and mixed-variable products exactly, and imposes no syntactic degree, homogeneity or multilinearity restriction. It adapts the known permanent checkability proof of Aaronson and van Melkebeek 2011, Claim 3.2 (see sources); not a claim of priority for the general method. Material extension to this project's HC transfer is the explicit single-candidate vertex-subdivision padding, exact directed-cycle adapter and fixed-field certificate.

One PIT instance can combine the discrepancies as sum_(k=2..n) t^(k-2) D_k with a fresh variable t. Distinct t exponents ensure no cross-discrepancy cancellation. C copies O(n²); constructing each substitution in O(n²+s) elementary indexing operations gives polynomial construction and gate bound O(n²(s+1)) with input/constant gates included and t powers shared. All projections explicit; no free circuit substitution. Alternatively use n-1 separate PIT queries. Use separate tests below to bound degree sharply.

Every candidate gate has degree at most 2^s, even when cancellation occurs. Thus D_k has degree <=2^s+1. A nonzero D_k admits a nonzero evaluation in extension F_(p^d), d=s+3, because field cardinality exceeds total degree (standard root bound by induction on variables). Certificate: k, monic irreducible degree-d polynomial f over F_p, and values of all k(k-1) variables in F_p[t]/(f), encoded as d base-field coefficients each. Irreducibility checked deterministically: x^(p^d)=x mod f and gcd(f,x^(p^(d/l))-x)=1 for every prime divisor l of d. Factor d by trial division; repeated modular Frobenius powers take polynomial bit time for fixed p. Existence of finite field of every positive degree known. Evaluate all C copies at the provided weights and test discrepancy !=0. This verifies an incorrect candidate without evaluating the target HC polynomial, counting tours or extracting multilinearization. Certificate bits O(n²(s+1)log p), arithmetic field multiplications polynomial in d and log p. Even exponential formal degrees are harmless here because only logarithm of field cardinality is stored. Correctness problem in coNP unconditionally over each fixed prime field. No independent PIT implementation or general irreducibility search executed this run.

Assume ordinary directed HC decision in P, hence P=NP. Then the above NP discrepancy predicate can be deterministically decided and witnessed by polynomial-size reductions and bit-prefix queries, and correctness becomes deterministic polynomial. For s<=N^c, c fixed, choose a padded canonical explicit gate encoding of length L=O(s log(s+n²)+s log p) (include n unary), with unused gates or padding and output gate specified. Existence of a completion with a specified bit prefix that satisfies the exact verifier is an NP predicate under the assumed P algorithm. Query it for each next bit; O(L) prefix queries, one initial existence query, optional final verification. If a correct capped circuit exists this produces it, otherwise correctly reports NONE at this cap. Thus small general circuit existence and acquisition are distinct; existence itself lies in Sigma_2^P without the assumption. The same algorithm works for the permanent by ordinary Laplace expansion and identity block padding. This extends L01-06's conditional acquisition from typed set-multilinear circuits to unrestricted signed division-free arithmetic circuits over fixed F_p. No universal small-circuit existence follows from P=NP.

## C. Adversarial replica: PIT converse and invisible defects
For every fixed prime p, arbitrary multivariate finite-field PIT reduces to correctness of an HC candidate at order 2. Let Q be an s-gate polynomial in m variables, with individual degree <=2^s. Choose integer B=2^s+1 and map the i-th variable to t^(B^(i-1)). Distinct monomials map to distinct exponents by base-B digits, hence resulting univariate q vanishes identically iff Q does. Exponent bit lengths O(ms), computed by integer multiplications and compiled by repeated squaring with O(ms) gates per substitution; all shared computations explicitly costed, no enormous exponent expansion. Use t=x_12 in C=x_12*x_21+q(x_12). Then C=H_2 iff Q=0. This is a many-one reduction in the unrestricted candidate input length, not a statement that an order-2 graph requires growing resources; the latter has constant N and is easy. Combined with B, exact correctness and PIT are polynomial-time interreducible. This identifies the actual verification problem, independent of HC decision hardness. The PIT-to-verification reduction is not a lower bound on PIT.

Base field tests cannot substitute for formal PIT. For n=p+1, perturb H_n by Q=x_12^p*x_21-x_12*x_21^p. It is nonzero, homogeneous degree n, yet vanishes on EVERY F_p assignment, in particular Boolean inputs. It is not row-set-multilinear, exactly the permitted general circuit defect. This remains a counterexample for any larger n after multiplying by a monomial of degree n-p-1. At n=p+1 the lower projections E_(k,n), k<n, set x_12=0, so the perturbation disappears there. Thus all smaller identities hold, but D_n=Q. Choose x_12=alpha in a quadratic extension outside F_p, x_21=1; alpha^p-alpha!=0. This is an explicit first failing identity certificate, not an approximation argument. Even knowing all base-field weighted values and the correct total degree cannot verify a general formal circuit. The fixed-field certificate above detects it. No diagonal-variable shortcut used.

Mandatory controls: bipartite matching feasibility is in P while its permanent symbolically counts matchings and is externally claimed hard outside char2. Over F2 permanent=determinant; a graph with even positive count has scalar zero. Complete 3-vertex digraph has two directed cycles, so H_3(1)=0 in F2 even though decision YES. Exact-witness verification (one successor per row, permutation, single cycle) is P. Equality products and affine parity are cheap; bounded-width independent components can be multiplied without global witness enumeration. These controls refute decision-to-count, base-field-value-to-formal-identity, and general rank-to-circuit claims. They do not refute the conditional acquisition theorem.
