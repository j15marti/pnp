## A. Exact regional decomposition, without hull replacement
Let W be the span of all observation normals in a fixed chart. Partition owners into regions R_i, local observation spaces W_i. Put I_i=W_i intersect sum_{j!=i}W_j, I=sum_i I_i. Choose complements K_i with W_i=I_i directsum K_i by Gaussian elimination. Claim W=I directsum (directsum_i K_i). Spanning immediate. For any relation sum_i k_i + sum_i h_i=0 with k_i in K_i, h_i in I_i, isolate k_i+h_i in W_i intersect sum_{j!=i}W_j=I_i. Thus every k_i=0. This also proves independence modulo I and establishes exact functional gluing.

For each region let S_i be actual locally allowed functionals on W_i, and T_i its attained projection to I_i*. Then global feasibility iff there exists b in I* with b restricted to each I_i in T_i. Necessity by restriction; sufficiency choose for each i an actual local extension (not a point in a hull) and independently assign its K_i values, using the direct decomposition. Coordinates outside W free, source chart lifts. In particular if dim I_i<=2 all local boundary tables have at most four words. This is exact decision, not a count obtained from a support table. Overlapping rather than partitioned regions can still give sound necessary nogoods by local refutation, but independent extension/completeness then not automatic.

Acquire T_i by enumerating W_i* if dim W_i<=c log N, checking actual defects and guards. Alternatively use a verified explicit local factor chart of treewidth w_i<=c log N. Each target boundary word b imposes at most two linear equations A_i x=b. Character filtering gives exact integer mass

## B. Common coordinate basis theorem, acquired deterministically
Input a family of distinct subspaces W_i'<=I over F2 of ranks 1 or 2 (here I_i after exact projection; remove rank0 regions if nonempty, reject empty). A simultaneous coordinate basis B of I is a basis with each W_i' spanned by a subset of B. Rank<=2 means at most three nonzero vectors per distinct space. Construct mandatory set M of all nonzero rank-one scope vectors and all nonzero pairwise intersections of distinct rank-two scopes. A common basis must contain M: coordinate subspace intersection is spanned by common coordinate axes, and any nonzero rank-one intersection has its unique vector over F2. Duplicate scopes removed geometrically, signs/tables remain separate.

Check (1) M independent; (2) for each scope W_i', dim(W_i' intersect span M)=|W_i' intersect M|. These necessary conditions ensure a local basis extending its mandatory vectors can be chosen. Select any such basis greedily within each W_i', and collect its optional axes. No optional axis can occur in two distinct scopes, because their intersection vector would have been mandatory. Thus the total number of required axes is q=|M|+sum_i(dim W_i'-|W_i' intersect M|). Their span equals the span of all scopes; hence (3) rank(sum_i W_i') must equal q. This rank is independent of which local complements were selected. If equal, the assembled axes are independent, extend by standard coordinate vectors to basis of I. If lower, any common basis would contain q distinct axes in a smaller-dimensional span, impossible. This proves iff and constructive polynomial recognition, not a heuristic basis search. With arbitrary rank this pairwise-intersection argument is not established. All explicit ranks/intersections computable by Gaussian elimination, e.g. conservative poly(N) (O(N^6) bit time when m,d<=N). For rank2 projection tables use coordinates B and one ordinary 2-CNF clause per forbidden word; rank1 gives unary literals. Ordinary implication SCC now COMPLETE, since the basis observation values are mutually independent and each constraint sees at most two axes. Affine guards absorbed before basis test; transport offsets, not just normals. If basis test fails, return UNKNOWN for this terminal, not UNSAT. On raw mixed affine-bijunctive systems the theorem recognizes one sufficient geometric presentation, not all semantically equivalent 2-CNF functions.
