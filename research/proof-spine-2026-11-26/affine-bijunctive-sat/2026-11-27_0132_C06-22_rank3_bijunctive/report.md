## Theorem A: geometric basis in polynomial time for logarithmic scope rank
For distinct observation subspaces V_i, W=sum V_i, ground set E=union of nonzero vectors of V_i. Give v weight number of scopes containing it. The maximum-weight linear-matroid basis found by greedy sorting and Gaussian independence has weight <=sum_i dim V_i, with equality exactly when |B intersect V_i|=dim V_i for every i. Therefore this is an iff geometric adaptation test, polynomial when maximum scope rank <=c log N for fixed c. Weight counts use deduplicated geometries, not discarded semantic tables. Geometric basis alone may not give bijunctivity.

## Theorem B: exact semantic common-basis recognition at rank <=3
Input actual tables T_i on V_i*, with dim V_i<=3. There is a polynomial iff test for a basis of W adapting to all scopes and making EVERY given T_i ordinary bijunctive. Handle empty table as immediate refutation, rank0 constants directly; repeated identical scopes require one local basis satisfying all their tables.

First acquire a geometric basis by A. Let P be all distinct rank2 scopes and rank2 intersections of distinct scopes. Let L be all rank1 scopes, rank1 scope intersections, and rank1 intersections of distinct planes in P (record unique nonzero vectors). Intersections of coordinate subspaces are coordinate, so all these axes mandatory. Each plane has 3 unordered bases; those containing its mandatory vectors give 3,2,1 choices for 0,1,2 mandatory axes; 3 mandatory axes incompatible with A.

For each distinct scope S enumerate all unordered bases (at most 28 at rank3), requiring mandatory axes and adaptation to every P contained in S. For each actual table on S, evaluate in candidate coordinates, retaining basis iff table equals intersection of pullbacks of its unary and binary projections. This is exactly ordinary 2-CNF definability: forbidden projected words give clauses. Record a successful local basis for each allowed assignment of its variable plane choices. If none, UNKNOWN.

There are at most three coordinate planes in a rank3 scope. Two present planes share a mandatory axis, so each has <=2 choices. Three present planes have three distinct pairwise intersection axes, each plane contains two, all are fixed. Thus local option constraints have at most two Boolean plane variables, or just one ternary choice (a plane with no mandatory axis cannot coexist with another plane in a containing scope). For rank2 only its own plane; rank1 none. Intersect unary ternary domains, solve the Boolean choice constraints by SCC 2-SAT, encoding forbidden combinations. A successful choice recovers recorded local bases. A meta-level failure is not source UNSAT.

Gluing proof: for any distinct scopes i,j their intersection rank0,1,2 has exactly the same basis induced by B_i and B_j, by mandatory axes or plane choices. Repeated scopes use one choice. For a nonempty subfamily J, fix i in J: every V_i intersect V_j is coordinate in B_i; the common intersection is spanned by the intersection of their axis sets, which by pairwise consistency equals intersection_{j in J} B_j. Its cardinality equals dimension of the common subspace, as for the original geometric basis's restrictions. Finite inclusion-exclusion proves the union of all selected B_i has the same cardinality as that original basis, dim W. It spans W and is therefore independent.
