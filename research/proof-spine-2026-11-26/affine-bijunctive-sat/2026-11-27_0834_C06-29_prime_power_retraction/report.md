## Track A: constructive exact modular elimination
For an integer system Ax=b, fixed prime p, modulus M=p^t, normalize entries to residues 0,...,M-1. If any active coefficient a_ij is a unit mod p, swap that row into pivot position, invert a_ij modulo M, eliminate its column from all other rows, record the solved equation and remove that row/column. This is bijective reconstruction of the eliminated variable modulo M. If no coefficient is a unit, all active coefficients are divisible by p. Any nondivisible RHS is inconsistent; otherwise divide ALL active rows and RHS by p and reduce modulus to M/p. This is exact because p z=0 mod M iff z=0 mod M/p, not division at the unchanged modulus. The higher base-p digits of remaining variables are then free relative to the reduced equations. When modulus reaches 1 or all rows exhausted, the remaining coordinates are free.

For p=2 the recorded pivot equations reduced mod 2 describe exactly the projection of modular solutions onto their original parity bits. Proof: at a unit step any solution of the remaining system has a unique pivot residue, whose parity is the recorded affine row; at a divide step every solution modulo M/p lifts to a solution modulo M by freely choosing a higher digit, preserving parity provided M/p>=2; at M/p=1 all remaining parity bits are free. Back-substitution through earlier units then gives a modular witness of each parity word. Triangular recorded equations independent (distinct pivots, only later uneliminated variables). Same reasoning for mod p projection generally. A division to modulus 1 retains no constraint on uneliminated residues. Thus exact projection, not just necessary local marginal equations.

## Track B: independent global Smith form proof, native precision bound
Smith form D=UAV with unimodular integer U,V; r=rank_Q A and nonzero invariant factors d_1,...,d_r. Put c=Ub, w=V^(-1)1_n. Native A1=3b implies 3c=Dw. Zero rows of D have c_i=0. For nonzero d_i, all prime-power factors except possibly one factor of 3 divide c_i. Therefore integer feasibility is equivalent to checking the 3-primary divisibilities. Hadamard: an r-minor has magnitude <=3^(r/2), and product of nonzero invariant factors equals the gcd of all r-minors up to sign. Consequently every v_p(d_i)<n+1. Set t=n+1. Then Ax=b mod 3^t is feasible iff all d_i divide c_i, iff Ax=b has an integer solution. A mod-3 check alone can miss deeper obstructions.

For p=2, 3 is a unit; Ax=b mod 2^t always has solution 3^(-1)1_n. In Smith coordinates D z=c, for each i<=r the solution fixes z_i modulo 2^(t-v_2(d_i)), hence fixes its parity; all n-r free coordinates unrestricted. Thus exact parity projection P_2 of this modular system is an affine space of dimension n-r, regardless of the rank of A modulo 2. It contains 1_n. It is contained in the ordinary parity chart P_0={x:Ax=b mod2}. When the integer system is feasible, P_2 is EXACTLY the parity image of all integer solutions: choose any integer particular solution x*, the kernel is the Z-span of the last n-r columns of V, whose reductions remain independent, giving the same affine chart. Indeed 1-3x* is in the integer kernel and x*+(1-3x*)=1-2x* has all-ones parity. When the integer system is infeasible, P_2 must NOT be described as the parity image of integer solutions; the latter is empty. This distinction matters.

Thus literal identity witness map, both directions, preserves actual joint support, not merely an affine hull. If no integer solution, H AND P_2 is also necessarily UNSAT (same implication).
