## New scoped transport lemma

Let F be a CNF, C be RUP relative to F, and g be a consistent partial assignment. If g falsifies every literal of C, then UP(F,g) already conflicts, since it starts with all assumptions used to check C and unit conflict is monotone under strengthening the consistent assumptions. Thus directly filtering a table row that falsifies a RUP clause does not give any new row exclusion relative to complete original-formula row probing. Nevertheless adding C can strictly increase the set of other guards refutable by UP: it can cause a new unit propagation even when the guard does not initially falsify all of C. UP conflicts remain monotone under clause addition, so before/after count differences are exact numbers of newly refutable frozen rows (not counts of all logically impossible rows). Counted row occurrences are separate per factor, not distinct partial assignments.

A fixed chart with R explicit allowed rows admits a polynomially bounded row-exclusion closure: for each original row g test UP(F,g); append the clause negating g when conflict is verified, permanently delete that row from its factor, repeat scans. Each row is deleted at most once; at most R+1 sweeps of R probes, with at most R appended clauses and total added literal count at most the original sum of row widths. A contradiction of all rows in one factor is sound by exact factor semantics; an empty clause is sound. A fixed point is not a SAT certificate. For polynomially acquired explicit chart length L, repeated full-scan UP is polynomial in L and variables, even charging failed probes and independent replay. Scope: fixed coordinate/factor registry; new factors, branches and chart acquisition require separate credits. No universal availability or progress. Direct row filtering by RUP is redundant under complete row probes, indirect activation is not.
