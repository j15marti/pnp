## The connecting lemma
Given an explicit q-by-q bipartite assignment support and a signing epsilon_ij in {+1,-1} such that all nonzero determinant terms have the same sign, let P(z)=sum_M z^(cost(M)). Then det(epsilon_ij z^w_ij)=+/- P(z) as an identity, not just an evaluation. For simple graphs every coefficient is at most q!. Choose B=q!+1, form integer matrix epsilon_ij B^w_ij (zeros at absent edges), and compute D=abs(det). Each base-B digit of D is the exact number of perfect matchings of that cost. In particular floor(D/B^d) mod B=0 certifies NO. No cancellation or carry is possible. A missing ordinary perfect matching is immediately NO. RST 1999 pp929-930 explicitly gives polynomial recognition and construction of a Polya signing of the 0-1 pattern. Its unit-weight equality implies all terms have the same sign (triangle equality for the finite sum of unit terms), so the signing transfers to all positive weights and formal monomials. This is a known matching/Pfaffian terminal reconstructed with a charged integer-digit extraction, not a new general exact matching algorithm. Kenyon 2009 theorem 2 gives the planar case and proof.
