## Exact theorem: balanced two-port planar substitution
For every explicit weighted bipartite assignment graph formed from a supplied planar bipartite core K and edge-disjoint attachments H_i with disjoint private vertices, each attachment meeting K at one row u and one column v, with equal numbers of private rows and columns, define A_i(z), B_i(z) as the exact local matching polynomials covering all private vertices and respectively neither or both of u,v. The only other masks have zero coefficient by bipartite balance. Assume each attachment is fixed size or admits a verified polynomially paid local minor algorithm (e.g. supplied O(log N) planar edge exceptions, fresh signings), numeric target d <= N^C for fixed C, nonnegative integer costs, and a supplied verified simultaneous planar routing of the replacement paths, e.g. each (u,v) is a core edge and the paths lie in disjoint narrow strips beside those edges. Original edge IDs partitioned, no shared private vertices. Then the exact number of perfect matchings of target cost d is obtainable in deterministic polynomial bit time, even if the union of all adhesion vertices on K is linear in N and the total local exception budget is linear.

Proof: truncate all local polynomials at d. Replace H_i with path u-x-y-v, x a private column, y a private row, and edge weights B_i(z), A_i(z), 1 respectively. If neither port is used, the middle edge must be selected; if both, the two outer edges must be selected. No other local completion exists. These states have exactly the original local generating polynomials, including zero weights. Matching gluing on distinct physical ports is a bijection between reduced matchings plus local choices and original matchings. Thus weighted permanent of the substituted planar graph gives the exact original coefficients through d. Acquire fresh planar bipartite coherent signing, evaluate polynomial weights at integer base t!+1, where t is the original number of rows (simple graph; parallel edges must be separately counted and bound enlarged to (E+1)^t). Absolute signed determinant has coefficients bounded by original matching count, hence no carry. Local truncation may drop higher-weight original matchings but cannot affect coefficients <=d. Digit d is exact. Matrix dimension O(N), polynomial degree O(Nd), coefficient bit length O(N log N), intermediate determinant bit length polynomial by minor/Hadamard bounds, fraction-free elimination polynomial. Can use self-reduction on the reduced graph with polynomial coefficient selection, then local reconstruction, when witnesses requested; NO uses only zero coefficient. General nonlocal guards not supported. Pinned original edges inside attachments/core require revalidation of residual structure, not unconditional transfer. All applications here have empty source guard, canonical anchor symmetry only. Verify core embedding and simultaneous routing, attachment partition, local balances and acquisition conditions; no arbitrary decomposition oracle. Local budgets summed, not multiplied. Full cost includes scanning, all local minor calls, signs, integer arithmetic, reconstruction, verification, failed work, memory. Peak <= polynomial time plus input; cumulative <= same-execution time times peak. No uniform coverage claim.

Four-port falsifier: cyclic boundary order 1,2,3,4, first two row vertices, last two columns. Attachment with just crossing edges (1,3),(2,4), each weight 1. Its occupancy signature has F_empty=F_13=F_24=F_1234=1 and all other entries zero. Planar matchgate identity F_empty F_1234=F_12 F_34-F_13 F_24+F_14 F_23 would read 1=-1. Thus over Q (also Z[z]) even constant-size four-port bipartite attachments cannot always be planarized preserving exact boundary signature and cyclic order. Cai-Gorenstein Matchgates Revisited (2014) Theorem 2.1 gives the necessary identity; this is a scoped explicit instance, not new general matchgate theory. Not an unrestricted lower bound, nor exclusion of alternate decomposition, cancellations with extra state, or a decision-only terminal.
