## Scoped theorem and repair: incidence-linear scoring
For canonical clause database F, total L literal occurrences, n free variables, m rows, let current exact true count k_j and signed sum s_j. For a flip of variable v appearing once in row j with sign e in {-1,+1}, delta=+1 if the literal was false, -1 if true; k'_j=k_j+delta and s'_j=s_j+e*delta. Define B_j=[k_j=0], Z_j=[k_j>0 and s_j=0 mod3]. Then for every candidate variable accumulate delta B_j and delta Z_j over its incident rows. Rows without v contribute0. Thus all candidate scores and prescribed lexicographic tie-break can be computed by one initial row scan and one incidence scan, O(L+n) arithmetic/index updates per iteration instead of O(nL) full rescoring, assuming canonicalized clauses (otherwise aggregate all same-variable occurrence deltas per row). Integers require O(log(L+n+2)) bits, array indexing standard; polynomial bit simulation on standard model by charging addresses. Strict improvement implies at most m flips per fixed database and O((m+1)(L+n)) updates incl final failed scan, peak O(L+n+m) logical records. Across a fixed polynomial number of phases with polynomial clause caps total remains polynomial; NO universal bound on number of phases, search, useful learned clauses or complete refutation. Residue collision: (a OR b OR c) all-false and all-true both k mod3=0, distinct k; signed sum likewise0 mod3 for all-positive all-true. Hence no sound rejection based solely on residues. Rank selection heuristic does not assert any learned clause without exact resolution proof.
