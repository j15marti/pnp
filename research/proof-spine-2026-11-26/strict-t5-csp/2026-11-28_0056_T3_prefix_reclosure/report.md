## Exact bridge theorem (explicit Boolean quotient)
For any signed literal u, let t(u) be its 0/1 truth polynomial over F2, t(-u)=1+t(u). An implication u->v is exact vanishing polynomial p=t(u)(1+t(v)). For u->v->w, let q=t(v)(1+t(w)). Then
  (1+t(w))p + t(u)q = t(u)(1+t(w)).
This is a polynomial-calculus derivation of degree <=3 after Boolean reduction. Multiplication by a signed literal or its complement is implemented by variable multiplication plus addition (degree <=3). Induct on path length: transitive binary consequences all have explicit degree-three derivations, with O(length) multiplications/additions and constant-sized intermediate supports. If paths u->-u and -u->u exist, their endpoint polynomials are t(u) and 1+t(u); sum is 1. Thus any explicitly supplied, verified inconsistent binary subdatabase has a degree-three square-free refutation, regardless of degree of the other source equations. A graph path certificate avoids recomputing full coefficient-space saturation, and its search by reachability is polynomial in the supplied graph; verifying parent deductions, charts, guards and any gauge discharge still required. No claim that inconsistent binary subdatabase can always be acquired.
