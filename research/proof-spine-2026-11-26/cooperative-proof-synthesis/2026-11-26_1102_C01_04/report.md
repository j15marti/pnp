## 2. Exact classification (PROVED)
Let boundary ports a,b have color A; c has color B; d has color C. A local cover must cover each private owner exactly once and each port at most once, obeying every original local tuple guard. Encode its occupancy mask by m=a+2b+4c+8d. If p_A,p_B,p_C are private owner counts, every feasible mask obeys
p_A+a+b = p_B+c = p_C+d.
If c,d vary over the support then both vary together, hence p_B=p_C and c=d. The only two possible variable-level cases are a+b=c (lower support L={0,13,14}) and a+b=1+c (upper support U={1,2,15}). If c,d are fixed then a+b is fixed. For a+b=0 or 2 there is a unique mask; for a+b=1 the only possible freedom is exchange of a and b. Consequently every nonempty support is a subset of L or U, a singleton, or one of the two additional pairs (fixed unequal c,d). There are exactly 26 candidate nonempty relations: 16 singletons, 8 pairs and 2 triples. This is a complete necessary classification. The gadgets below show sufficiency as a disk-embeddable guarded-free local occupancy relation, with possibly different private populations. It is not a claim that each arbitrary given region attains every color-feasible mask.

## 3. Explicit native templates, including disk order (PROVED WITH SCOPE)
Use labels a=A0, b=A1, c=B0, d=C0. All other color IDs are private. List tuples as (A-index,B-index,C-index); tuple IDs start at zero. Counts n include both boundary and private owners. Up to rotating/reflecting the disk and swapping same-color labels, there are two orders: adjacent a,b,c,d; alternating a,c,b,d. Both orders are covered.
- L-adj: n=(3,2,2); T=[(1,0,1),(2,1,0),(2,1,1),(0,1,1),(2,0,0)]. Witness IDs: 0:[2], 13:[3,4], 14:[0,1].
- L-alt: n=(3,2,2); T=[(0,0,1),(2,1,1),(1,0,1),(2,1,0)]. Witness IDs: 0:[1], 13:[0,3], 14:[2,3].
- U-adj: n=(3,3,3); T=[(1,1,2),(2,0,0),(2,2,1),(2,1,2),(0,2,1)]. Witness IDs: 1:[3,4], 2:[0,2], 15:[0,1,4].
