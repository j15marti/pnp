## 2. Rainbow-boundary contraction theorem (PROVED scoped)
Let R be a connected component of the live incidence graph after removal of a set B of owner vertices, with private owners P and actual tuple IDs J. Its actual boundary K is the set of B-owners incident to J; K contains at most one owner of each of the three colors. All private owners must be covered by local tuples. For a boundary mask b in {0,1}^K let R(b) be TRUE iff some subset of J covers exactly P union the selected boundary owners once, and obeys every local original zero/one guard. Outside tuples never touch P, and no local tuple touches another component's private owners. These are exact local support tables, not marginals. If p_c counts private owners of color c and b_c is the selected boundary bit (0 if no such owner), every local cover uses the same number of triples as covered owners of each color:
p_0+b_0=p_1+b_1=p_2+b_2.
Thus if two distinct masks b,b' are feasible, their coordinate differences b_c-b'_c must all be equal. They must differ by +1 or -1 in each color, so K has exactly three owners, all p_c equal, and the two masks are 000 and 111. There are no other possible masks. If |K|<3 or p_c unequal, there is at most one feasible mask. In particular unequal counts can force PARTIAL corner coverage, not just none/all.

Construct local tables by exact enumeration of at most eight masks (constant, or at most two via color count). Store one actual covering witness for each admitted mask. Empty table gives exact NO. Unique mask: remove private owners and their tuples, mark its selected boundary owners as covered, delete other incident tuples and reject if a required external pin is thereby contradicted. Nonselected boundary owners remain obligations. Two masks: contract all private vertices and local tuple nodes, replace by one synthetic triple on the three boundary owners. Choosing the triple means use stored 111 witness; not choosing it means use stored 000 witness. In a planar connected incidence graph, this contraction is a graph minor before parallel edge suppression, so produces a planar incidence star with inherited embedding. Mandatory modes delete edges/owners; their actual witness covers an equal count of all colors. Optional mode removes balanced private counts; remaining owner populations stay equal. Preserve original IDs as reverse history. If duplicate synthetic triples (or a synthetic tuple and an outside identical triple) arise, for pure decision merge interchangeable options with same owner triple by choosing an allowed representative and fixing other optional modes to their stored zero-mode. Guarded external original one-pins must be honored before merging or kept as distinct parallel choices until discharged. No preservation of future arbitrary original-ID guards after decision-only merging, counts or arbitrary weights is asserted.

Forward: any source cover restricts to exactly one admitted mask per region. A mandatory mask must occur, and a synthetic tuple is selected precisely when its region covers its three boundary owners. Distinct regions cannot claim the same owner. Retaining actual external choices yields a cover of the reduced source (before duplicate merging); identical optional tuples can be interchanged without changing decision. Reverse: use the stored witness per region according to its chosen mode, and retain external chosen tuples. Private owner sets are disjoint, all retained owner obligations are met exactly once, and every original guard is checked. Thus equivalence, not merely forward existence. A simultaneous separator set B may have arbitrarily many owners; the theorem applies independently to each connected component when each actual local K is rainbow. One can also contract a single eligible component, leaving wider components untouched. The executed batch implementation declines on any wide component (safe OPEN).

Uniform acquisition without geometric metadata (theorem-level general method, not fully executed): enumerate at most (n+1)^3 choices B of zero or one owner per color (where n is owners/color), find actual components and their boundaries, admit a component with 2<=|J|<=floor(c log2 N) for fixed c, or any other independently certified exact local backend. Test every mask by enumerating at most 2^|J| subsets of indexed tuples, verify guards and occupancy; no free SAT/equivalence oracle. A productive step replaces >=2 tuples by <=1, or rejects, with mu=m active tuple count strictly decreasing. The separator enumeration and all failed tests cost a fixed polynomial (O(N^3) sets, O(N) components per set, O(N^c) local subsets, plus polynomial indexing/verification); repeat <=m times. A globally disconnected component with no boundary is separately solved/rejected, not silently dropped. Reapply unit/PM/other certified closure after reductions and at stagnation. Arbitrary residual coverage not claimed: if all small rainbow components are singleton or huge, this lane has no productive operation.

## 3. Guarded factor-13 macroface theorem
Use C01-02 full recognized octahedral source F_{13b}, not a guessed geometry. Acquire original full face list by six-pole BFS and exact equality of generated face set, then partition into frequency-13 triangular macrofaces by the known barycentric maps. Retain original tuple IDs, original guards, and actual incidence. A macroface has 169 elementary triangles and 105 lattice vertices, constant independent of N. Coarse corners are exactly original vertices incident to >=3 macrofaces; every noncorner is strictly inside one macroface or on an edge of exactly two. For each noncorner owner v, inspect its live actual incident triangles. If none, reject. If all lie in one macroface, its region is determined. If two remain possible, v is ambiguous. Every source cover assigns each noncorner to exactly one such region. For a fixed ownership assignment, all noncorners become private region owners, and every patch boundary consists of the three distinct-color corners. Disallow local tuples covering nonassigned noncorners. Apply integer color balance and exact local enumeration, keeping even unequal-color-count singleton modes; selected positive guards retained locally. Collapse singleton modes, reject overlap, remove covered corners, and use one coarse rainbow tuple per double mode. Each residual is an actual subhypergraph of coarse face incidence, planar and balanced if feasible. Forward and reverse follow from ownership completeness plus RBC. No need to carry fine weights/counts or future arbitrary guards.

If a ambiguous noncorners, enumerate all 2^a assignments, only when a<=floor(log2 m) (m=original tuples). Process ownership choices exactly and retain their distinct guard histories; if any exact coarse branch yields YES, lift; NO only if all admitted branches exactly reject; otherwise OPEN. This is a polynomially sized OR of smaller P3DMs, not a free recursive polynomial decider. With one level and coarse base <=200 tuples, all coarse exact searches have a fixed constant worst-case bound. For larger bases the exact contracted instances can be handed to the cooperative dispatcher. Repeated logarithmic branching at every scale is NOT asserted polynomial; C02 must bound aggregate branching. With a=0 the strict tuple-count reduction is nonbranching, at most m/169 coarse tuples. Raw experiments deliberately omit initial closure to reveal nontrivial coarse systems; separate graph-native execution applies closure on the same original guards. This guard ownership certificate is an additional premise, NOT deduced merely from source planarity.

## 4. Adversarial reverse projection and intact-prime obstruction
Prior C01-02 proved a one-way lift, not arbitrary fine-to-coarse equivalence. Counterexample to the stronger claim that every fine witness can project to an all-or-none macroface corner selection: on full frequency13 octahedron (1352 indexed triples), use numerical MILP only to find a candidate, imposing exact-one original owner rows and <=2 selected corners per macroface. Exact independent verification of the resulting 226-tuple witness confirms its corner ownership counts [1,0,0,2,1,0,1,1]. No macroface covers three corners, though all six coarse corners are covered. The witness itself suffices as a finite counterexample; no reliance on MILP optimality. Guard all other original triangles false. The guarded source is YES and its only cover is that witness, so an all-or-none guarded projection would incorrectly reject it. New singleton partial mode rule solves it exactly (64 local table queries including 53 color-infeasible control queries; 290 search nodes; initial native closure 3 PM calls, 226 live). Not a counterexample to existence of some coarse cover of the unguarded geometry.

Rainbow cuts are not universal on intact octahedral family. Its primal graph is a simple maximal planar triangulation. Any edge joins vertices whose nonzero coordinate signs do not conflict. Three pairwise adjacent vertices therefore belong to a common closed octant; every 3-cycle in that triangular lattice is elementary and facial. By the maximal-planar 4-connectivity theorem (de Fraysseix and Ossona de Mendez, Lemmas 3.2-3.3), primal graph is 4-connected. Delete at most three owners. Surviving primal adjacency is realized by a length-two path through an original tuple center; surviving owners remain connected. All original tuple centers with a surviving owner are in this one component; at most one fully removed-owner face is isolated because triples are distinct. Hence no nontrivial proper small component is separated by a rainbow owner cut on intact family, for m sufficiently larger than c log N. Actual full closure was previously shown to leave all tuples at k4,5; no new claim of full postclosure asymptotic survival. Executed connectivity checks k1,4,5 give 4, all 8/128/200 primal triangles exactly the facial tuples. The earlier geometric lift is a legal bypass on its recognized unguarded subclass. This falsifies a universal raw structural cut claim, not an unrestricted lower bound or the expanded solver's full coverage.
