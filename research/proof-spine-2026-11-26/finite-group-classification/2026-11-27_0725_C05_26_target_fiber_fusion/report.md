## Theorem: exact target-fiber fusion
Let F_1,...,F_k be finite explicitly acquired nonempty sets in F_p^d, disjoint original choice blocks with recorded witnesses, target t. For a pair I={i,j}, let a+D be the affine hull of the exact sum of all outside factors. Compute a by selecting actual outside anchors and D by spanning all their differences; no outside sumset enumeration. Define C=(F_i+F_j) intersect (t-a-D).
Then t belongs to the full sum iff t belongs to C+sum(outside factors). Every original realization satisfies the outside affine test; conversely every row of C is an actual sum of two child rows and an attained outside sum completing it yields an original realization. Empty C proves NO. A nonempty C does not prove feasibility. The operation is target-specific, not an equality of unrestricted sumsets.
Acquisition: let q be a linear quotient map with kernel D. A pair (u,v) survives iff q(u)=q(t-a)-q(v). Build two sorted key tables and join matching keys. The number of surviving row pairs J=sum_l |L_l| |R_l| is exactly known before expansion; emit their ORIGINAL syndrome sums, deduplicate and retain a real witness. Costs poly(N,log p) times (|F_i|+|F_j|+J) after outside hull acquisition, with polynomial-bit keys and counts. J counts compatible row pairs, not distinct sums or native covers. If J exceeds a chosen budget, do not declare NO. With fixed explicit row budget M, J<=M^2. For each output row, original-ID union is obtained by unioning the two child ID unions over all emitting pairs; one representative suffices to reconstruct a witness, while the union gives exact existential per-ID support for that row. It does not preserve arbitrary joint provenance queries or counts. Re-acquire after guard changes; never infer an exclusion from missing representative alone. Unary variant F_i intersect (t-a-D) applied to fixed point. An ID absent from all surviving rows in its block is safely deleted from native residual and native closure rerun; no such deletion occurred on q22.

## Nonexpansive width-compatible contraction

Across one lane at most initial M productive rounds, at most k^2 candidates per full scan, <=M^2 row-pair visits per candidate, plus basis construction, sorting, width scoring, provenance, closure and verification, all polynomial in original encoded N. No exponential discovery hidden in a successful merge. Transient candidate tables at most M^2 rows. A polynomial cap failure returns UNKNOWN. Can also accept expansion under an explicit global row cap and factor-count potential, but not tested here.
For adjacent fusion in a fixed factor order, each retained new cut corresponds to an original cut; the fused factor's difference space is contained in the sum of the two original difference spaces. Unary restriction shrinks a factor's difference space. Consequently each side's new span is a subspace of the corresponding old span, so new intersection dimension cannot increase at any retained cut. Internal cut removed by fusion. Thus maximum inherited cut width cannot increase. Previous C05-25 exact subgroup saturation on the new factors preserves this bound too, by its split-kernel proof. An exact suffix trellis with supplied width w costs poly(N,log p)*p^w, binary 2^w; polynomial for p^w<=N^c or when actual attained state/transition cap suffices. This is decision and optional reverse witness reconstruction, not counting. For nonadjacent merges the inherited-width guarantee is FALSE; recompute paid width or restrict to adjacent merges. q22 native X and Z show strict increases.
