## Scoped theorem
For a vector of Boolean responses f_i on a common b-bit exact interface, put S_i=support of the exact Walsh transform of (-1)^f_i. H is the annihilator of span(union S_i); L is the annihilator of span(all a+a_i for a in S_i, fixed a_i in S_i). Translation multiplies each Fourier coefficient at a by (-1)^(a dot h). Fourier invertibility proves the two claims, and on L the constant derivative vector has coordinates a_i dot h. Thus choose basis B of L, complement C, write x=Cs+By, and f(Cs+By)=f(Cs)+Ay with A_{ij}=a_i dot B_j. An exact target response vector can be matched by eliminating y via a left-nullspace matrix, with Gaussian reconstruction. This is paid polynomial in explicit truth table size, not arbitrary succinct response size. Exact FWHT takes b*2^b additions/subtractions with O(b+1)-bit integers, plus polynomial in b and 2^b for rank; separate exhaustive translation replay here is O(4^b). If R is nonempty and |R|=2^dim H it is a single coset, equivalently an affine system, and substitution into ALL external constraints is safe. A local H does not justify independently choosing an orbit representative for a consumer: A={0,1}, B={1} on two bits has nonempty join but representative 0 fails. Odd support forces H=0 by free translation orbits; unbalanced response forces L=H. Eight nonlinear opposite/equal-label controls (b=3..10, response y plus parity of adjacent products) and all 276 truth tables for b=1..3 passed exhaustive kernel checks. This is not a universal decomposition theorem. No unrestricted lower bound, no claimed resolution of any general barrier.
