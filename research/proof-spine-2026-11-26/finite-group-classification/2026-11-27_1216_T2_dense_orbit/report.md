## Theorem 1: syndrome acquisition of sparse affected-event directions
An explicit affine forbidden-event chart is F2^d minus union E_i, E_i={x:A_i x=b_i}; remove empty events, reapply rank-one forced opposite equations. Stack all normals into A (M rows). A direction h affects event i iff A_i h is nonzero. Global kernel ker A consists of certified translation symmetries. Gauge free coordinates of A to zero: any assignment can be translated by an element of ker A to a unique pivot-coordinate representative. Retain the original lift via Gaussian or via the chosen representative (existential feasibility; not exact model counting). Restrict event normals to pivot coordinates; A now injective on remaining active coordinates. Let H have rows spanning ker A^T, so im A=ker H. For each event block i and each nonzero block evaluation vector v supported there, compute signature H v. A nonzero direction touching one, two or three events exists iff respectively a single signature vanishes, two distinct-block signatures agree, or three distinct-block signatures XOR to zero (zero touch already removed). Enumerate local patterns and use a dictionary for singles, then pair XOR lookup. Lift a zero signature sum to an actual direction by solving Ah=v. This is exact, not marginal. For four touch, after excluding smaller supports, compare pair signatures for a collision with four distinct blocks. Any collision of distinct pairs that overlap would yield a smaller nonzero supported word; these were already excluded. Thus pair hash gives complete four-touch test if fully executed. In general high-rank event tables may give exponential pattern enumeration, so cap the accelerated method and fall back to exact deletion-of-up-to-k-blocks GE for fixed k: a nonzero kernel of the complement is necessary and sufficient. The latter is polynomial in written chart size for fixed k. For the accelerated method, if L=sum_i(2^rank(A_i)-1) is polynomial in N, O(L^2 poly(N)) work/space; the implemented caps are explicit, cap failure UNKNOWN. No assumption that every chart has small rank per event.

## Theorem 2: disjoint spanning-family refusal
Let G_1,...,G_q be pairwise disjoint sets of actual event indices, each with stacked normal rank equal to active dimension d. For every nonzero h, each G_j contains an event with A_i h != 0, since otherwise h would lie in its trivial kernel. Therefore at least q distinct events are affected by EVERY nonzero direction, in EVERY basis. This is a short checkable obstruction for all <=q-1 touch single-orbit directions. Greedy acquire disjoint full-rank groups by adding events that increase span, removing selected indices and repeating; success is a sufficient certificate, failure is not evidence of an eligible direction. Group indivisibility matters; do not substitute ordinary row matroid base packing. More general: any r-dimensional direction subspace H0 has sum_i rank(A_i|H0)>=q*r since each group's stacked restriction is injective; this bounds total affected rank, not decision complexity.

## Theorem 3: exact contraction and costs
For a direction h, affected nonempty flats meet each binary orbit at at most one point. A quotient orbit is forbidden iff an invariant flat covers it or two affected flats cover opposite orientations. Thus replace affected flats by projections of E_i intersect (E_j+h), i<j. Test both orientations by exact affine intersections and rank cardinalities. One surviving point gives an actual lift, and both original guard and old chart are retained in reverse replay. For fixed k>=2, one elimination touches at most k events, introduces at most binom(k,2), and may grow m by at most B=max(0,binom(k,2)-k). Potential (B+1)d+m strictly decreases; zero-touch gauge and unit substitutions likewise decrease (or decrease m with no new events). So fixed-k acquisition plus contractions has polynomial aggregate work in original N for an initial polynomial-size chart: at most d0 contractions, m<=m0+B*d0, each flat at most d0 independent rows, all GE, subset probes, rejected trials, sorting, reverse-map retention and verification polynomial.
