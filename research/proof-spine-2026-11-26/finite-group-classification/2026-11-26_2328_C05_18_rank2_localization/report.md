## Theorem A: localization of every binary rank-two refutation
Let nonempty S_i subsets of F2^n be independent attained response sets; S=sum_i S_i (Minkowski XOR sum). Choose actual a_i in S_i, a=sum a_i, D_i=span(S_i+a_i), W_j=sum_{i!=j}D_i. For target t, a map H:F2^n->F2^2 with H(t) outside H(S) exists iff either (1) t outside aff(S)=a+sum_i D_i, or (2) some j has a rank-at-most-two linear separator of (S_j+a_j) modulo W_j from (t+a) modulo W_j. Empty factor separately certifies NO. This is a statement about separators, not exact feasibility or cardinality of all S.

Proof: any separator H has nonempty H(S) of size <=3. If size 1 or 2, it is an affine point or line missing H(t); a single linear functional separates, giving (1). Otherwise size3. If some H(S_i) has size3 and another has size>=2, their sum is the entire F2^2, since translates of a triple by two distinct shifts have union full. If every nonsingleton is a pair, each is a coset of a line. Two distinct pair directions sum to full; all parallel pairs sum to a pair, never three. Hence exactly one factor has image size3, all others singletons. H annihilates W_j, so descends to its quotient, proving (2). Conversely compose any local separator with the quotient map; every other factor's deviations vanish, so the original target is excluded. Case (1) by Gaussian separation. No independent marginal feasibility substituted for joint support. Theorem remains valid in any exact quotient of the global response space.

## Theorem C: higher-rank active-factor bound
For any fixed field size p and rank budget r, suppose a separator H into Fp^r exists. Quotient its output by stabilizer of the attained sum H(S). The new group G has size <=p^r, final attained sum A is nonempty proper, with trivial translation stabilizer. Translate each factor image to include 0. Whenever a nontrivial factor image B is added, the partial sum strictly enlarges: equality P+B=P implies each b in B is a period of P, hence of the final sum, impossible for nonzero b. Therefore at most |A|-1 <=p^r-2 factors have nonconstant images; the separator annihilates the span of all remaining factor differences. Conversely a separator on an active subset quotient lifts. For fixed binary r, enumerate all subsets up to 2^r-2 (or one factor for r=2 by A), quotient the other differences, and count exactly via product of local character evaluations in coordinates of the surviving summed affine span. Complete polynomial acquisition IF every such quotient has rank <=c log N for fixed c. This generalization is symbolic (rank3 finite controls only), not implemented on native instances. Large p encoded in log p: p^r is not polynomial in log p, no claim of efficient enumeration for unbounded prime. Rank-zero hull acquisition is field-linear but does not give this complexity guarantee. Fixed necessary nonlocal parity H can couple actual factors; do not apply A to separately conditioned marginals. Combined observation separator may be localized, but preserving prescribed H as a mandatory row space needs a separate proof.

## Barrier D: critical-number acquisition even with explicit supports
For an arbitrary explicit binary defect set D={e_u+e_v:uv in E(G)}, target0 never belongs to D. Two-row separator exists iff G admits four colors: the 2-bit column for each vertex is its color, and every edge must map to a nonzero XOR. NP-complete in general, by graph 4-colorability (3-colorability reduced by a universal new vertex); certificate checked directly. Add a disjoint triangle to ensure 0 in affine hull and rule out rank1, without altering four-colorability. This is the known graphic-matroid critical-number connection, independently derived here; rank-two acquisition is not generic polynomial linear algebra even when the complete response list and negative answer are supplied. NOT a native P3DM reduction: the planarity, label and occupancy constraints may severely restrict attained sets. No arbitrary-decider transfer. See Geelen-Nelson, JCTB 116 (2016), DOI 10.1016/j.jctb.2015.08.003, introduction defining critical number and graphic chromatic relation.
