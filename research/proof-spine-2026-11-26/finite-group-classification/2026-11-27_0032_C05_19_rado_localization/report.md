## A. Strict Rado saturation
Choose actual a_i in nonempty attained response sets S_i in F2^n; A_i=S_i+a_i contains zero, D_i=span A_i. Omit constant factors. If a nonempty I satisfies dim(sum_I D_i)<=|I|, choose an inclusion-minimal such J. Singleton J has dimension 1. Otherwise every proper nonempty subset has dimension >=size+1; deleting one index implies dim W>=|J| for W=sum_J D_i. Thus dim W=|J|. Every subset K of J satisfies dim(sum_K D_i)>=|K|. Rado independent-transversal theorem on ACTUAL A_i yields independent b_i in A_i, one per factor, forming a basis of W. Since 0 and b_i are actual choices, their independent sum contains all binary combinations, exactly W. Consequently sum_J S_i=xor_J a_i+W, an attained affine set, and W is a true translation subgroup of the global sum. Unlike arbitrary affine hull substitution, this has a rank-tightness proof. For odd prime p, p-2 factors {0,1} in F_p sum to {0,...,p-2}, aperiodic, rank-one separator with p-2 active factors: the binary proof does not transfer.

## B. Linear active-factor localization
Let H be a minimum-rank r linear separator of a nonempty total attained sum S from t. H(S) must equal F2^r minus H(t): if a second point y is absent, quotient by span(y+H(t)), whose target fiber consists precisely of the two absent points; contradict minimality. Hence the image has trivial stabilizer. By A, every nonempty subset J of nonconstant H(S_i) satisfies dim(sum_J H(D_i))>=|J|+1, else creates a nonzero period. Therefore <=r-1 factors can be active, strengthening C05-18's 2^r-2 bound; rank3 needs at most TWO, not six. More generally quotient any separator by its image stabilizer and apply this bound. Sharp abstract example: r-1 path-edge factors {0,e_i,e_(i+1)} on r vertices. Every proper vertex subset can be matched injectively to path edges by orienting toward a missing vertex; all-ones impossible since each edge chooses at most one endpoint. Sum is all binary words except all-ones, so min rank r and r-1 active factors. No arbitrary-r native adapter asserted.
