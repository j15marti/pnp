## B. Attained subgroup contraction and width monotonicity
For explicit anchored factors A_i, maintain H = {0} initially, together with disjoint removed blocks. Invariant: the exact sum of removed factors is H, a linear subspace, not just its affine hull. For a singleton or pair I of remaining factors, compute its image support Q = sum_{i in I} pi_H(A_i) exactly (enumerate actual row products, deduplicate; pair cap in executed prototype also requires rank <=2). Let D=span(Q). If |Q|=2^dim D, then Q=D since Q subset D. Remove I and replace H by pi_H^(-1)(D). Proof: sum of old removed support and new block equals H+sum A_i = pi_H^(-1)(Q), exactly. Thus after every step, t in sum all A_i iff pi_H(t) in sum remaining projected factors. On any explicit finite prime field replace 2^dim D by p^dim D; size comparison uses O(dim D log p) bits, no p^dim D enumeration beyond actual polynomial block size. Lift by reversing the contraction sequence: for each removed block choose a stored original tuple-row combination with matching required quotient syndrome, subtract its original contribution and continue; final remainder zero. Duplicate projected values retain at least one original ID combination. This is a decision and witness-lift result, not multiplicity preservation or #P counting. Store all original rows if later guards will require re-acquisition; never reuse a saturation certificate under a narrowed domain without replay.

For any original factor order and a cut (L,R), let U,V be spans of all original factors on each side, and U',V' spans of surviving projected factors on those sides. Because H equals the sum of spans of all removed factors, H=H_L+H_R with H_L subset U, H_R subset V (even when a removed block straddles the cut). Therefore pi_H(U)=U', pi_H(V)=V'. For any q in U' intersect V' choose lifts u in U,v in V; u-v=h_L+h_R. Then u-h_L=v+h_R belongs to U intersect V, so q is its image. Hence U' intersect V'=pi_H(U intersect V) and dim(U' intersect V')<=dim(U intersect V). This proves contraction never increases the algebraic cut-width bound for the inherited order. It does NOT prove that each actual frontier count or runtime decreases; recomputing order can behave worse.
