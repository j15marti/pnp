## A: Exact occupancy and quotient join
Group by one color, choose one original-ID tuple per group. Let z_i have a 1 at each of its two other owners. For S groups, use actual occupancy mask s of the two other shores, retain only genuinely disjoint partial covers. Any full cover satisfies this restriction, and two child partial covers can be combined iff their masks a,b satisfy a AND b=0. Their union equals a XOR b. Equivalently each shore of a XOR b must have exactly |S| bits, since child popcounts add to |S| and overlap costs two bits. Thus a nonnegative exact XOR convolution on disjoint-child counts can be filtered by this parent popcount after convolution; it does not need an exponential inclusion-exclusion oracle or subtraction of unknown counts. For complement baseline offset o and difference span W, keep s only if target XOR s XOR o belongs to W. Key by the quotient modulo W before pairing, not after. Every realization of retained parent with disjoint occupancy induces disjoint child masks and passes child span tests (sibling plus outside offset). Hence induction gives exact counts of all disjoint partial covers for retained keys. Root exact native count. This strictly strengthens earlier parity-only intermediate states, while preserving the same global count. Sparse join implemented; post-convolution popcount version proved, not implemented here. Counts polynomial bits since each group chooses one of <=m labels.

## B: Complement-matching conditioned exact join
At node S and mask s, delete all triples touching ANY used owner, including group owners S. Require perfect matching independently in all three two-shore projections of the resulting complement. This is necessary, not sufficient, for native completion. It is stronger than the span test in some cases and may be used with it. Crucial exact-count inheritance: if a parent mask passes all three tests, take any pair of disjoint child realizations of that mask. For each projected pair of shores, combine a perfect matching of the parent complement with the corresponding projection of the other child's actual tuples. They cover exactly the first child's complement; disjointness ensures no collisions. Thus all realizations of a retained parent mask pass both child tests. The same argument and prior span inheritance prove the exact partial-count invariant. No marginal matching witness is used as a global solution. Source-specific exact-one constraints remain intact. ## C: Portable Hall no-good theorem
For an exact partial cover P, let U be its used-owner set, across all three colors. A failed complement projection onto colors a,b yields H subset of unused color a and Y its remaining neighbor set, |Y|<|H|. For every allowed original tuple incident to H whose b-owner is outside Y, choose one owner of that tuple in U (it exists because the tuple is absent from complement). Let B be the union of these chosen blockers. Store source/guard, a,b,H,Y,B. For ANY other disjoint partial cover P' of the same source, in any decomposition or owner projection, if B subset U' and H intersect U' is empty, then P' has no full extension. Proof: every H-owner remains uncovered; each possible outgoing tuple to outside Y is still blocked by a used owner, while available neighbors inside Y number at most |Y|. Hall deficit precludes completion. This depends only on owner occupancy, not on chosen tuples, search path, chart or particular matching algorithm. Verify by scanning original allowed tuples and checking each incident tuple is blocked or ends in Y; no matching oracle required for reuse. Extract H,Y by alternating reachability from an unmatched left vertex in a failed augment; all reached right vertices matched, H contains one more left vertex. Keep both guard polarities. Generalization to guard strengthening is sound only when all retained tuples are a subset of those certified and original owner universe remains exact; do not reuse after deletion relaxation without checking. Learned-only pruning preserves root count (no full cover lost); it need not preserve all intermediate partial realization counts because the selected no-good subset is not necessarily inherited from parent to child. Full matching+cache does preserve intermediate count invariant since cache answers are actual complement matching failures. A 2x2x2 falsifier proves that dropping either used or unused condition, or reintroducing a blocked tuple, is unsound: guarded source {(0,0,0),(1,1,1),(0,1,1)}, bad partial {(0,1,1)}, H={X1},Y=empty,B={Y1}. Good partial {(1,1,1)} satisfies B but not H-unused; empty partial satisfies H-unused but not B. Adding (1,0,0) makes bad partial extendible. Hostile guard check executed, not a SAT research track.
