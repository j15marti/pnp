## Exact theorem: paired binary cancellation
For any explicitly acquired non-tautological Boolean clause set with width at most two, normalized binary clauses on distinct variables, work in the square-free Boolean ring over F2. Let f_C be the product of literal-falsity factors, f_empty=1. In the linear span of these polynomials, the affine subspace is exactly generated by unit/empty polynomials and differences (sums in F2) of each binary polynomial with a fixed representative of its unsigned variable pair. Proof: the degree-two term of each binary row is exactly x_i*x_j, coefficient 1. In any affine combination the coefficient sum in every unsigned pair group is zero, and its even-parity coefficient space is generated by representative pairs. Conversely every such difference is affine. Hence affine constant-one detection is exact for this linear span via grouping and Gaussian elimination, no quadratic dictionary needed. For t explicitly acquired rows and m indexed variables, deterministic sorting and dense elimination are polynomial in t,m and original ID bit lengths; conservative O((t+m+N)^4) bit work for this restricted subroutine, not a discovery bound for t or the chart. Sparse implementation logs toggles. Soundness is independent of whether other proof lanes find the same rows.

Control: six clauses (-1,2),(-2,3),(-3,-1),(1,4),(-4,5),(-5,1) have all different unsigned pairs and no unit, so affine intersection is zero; implication cycles certify UNSAT and exhaustive 32 assignments check zero solutions. Removing final clause yields 3 solutions. This falsifies universal completeness of paired cancellation, not SCC/2-SAT or arbitrary algorithms. It is a standalone abstract control, not a registry native instance.
