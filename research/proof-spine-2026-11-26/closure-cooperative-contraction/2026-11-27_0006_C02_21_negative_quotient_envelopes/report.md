## A. Constructive theorem
Partition one owner shore into disjoint blocks of at most two owners. Enumerate original labeled choices, reject internal collisions and any failed complementary matching projection. Every cover survives. All-ones XOR of a row per block is equivalent to exact cover: each other shore has q total selected incidences and q odd positive occupancies, so every count is one. Local rows need not individually extend.

For ANY acquired linear projection L of the original physical occupancy vector into r bits, group original histories by projected key z. Store mandatory mask M(z) (intersection of physical occupied sets) and possible mask U(z) (union); more generally any conservative lower/upper masks suffice. Projection/alternative aggregation takes intersection of lower masks and union of upper masks. At a join, reject a pair if M(x) intersects M(y); otherwise produce key x+y and candidate masks (M(x) union M(y), U(x) union U(y)), then aggregate alternatives. Reject root if target L(all-ones) missing or its upper mask omits a physical owner. Empty root proves native NO. Proof by induction: restriction of any genuine exact cover has its key present and physical occupancy between masks; no collision or omission test can delete it. Contextual affine filters and even arbitrary further quotient maps preserve that invariant. This is negative-only: nonempty result UNKNOWN, not witness or actual support. Physical masks are original labels, not XORed. With full identity and original disjoint blocks the DP is exact because every retained key equals the disjoint physical union, and M=U=key. No counting inference.

Factor closure: mandatory mask of whole factor = intersection of row lowers; possible mask = union of row uppers. Delete a row if it must overlap another factor's mandatory mask or its possible mask together with all exterior possible masks omits an owner. Two overlapping factor mandatory masks or an empty factor refute. Sound by same cover restriction argument; strict explicit row deletion pays closure.
