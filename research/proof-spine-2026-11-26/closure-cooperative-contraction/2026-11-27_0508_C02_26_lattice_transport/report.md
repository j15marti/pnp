## Theorem
For each distinct pair of groups i,j, let I contain ALL their row indices. Form lattice L_ij = A_complement(I) Z^(R-|I|). For selected rows a in i and c in j retain edge (a,c) if physical masks disjoint and b-A_a-A_c belongs to L_ij. Exact integer extension of the two whole-group pins follows by splitting Ax=b. A genuine cover cannot use an omitted edge. Acquire HNF or another certified containing lattice once per group pair; membership of target residual in its independent basis is rational coordinate equality and integrality. Containment H Z=A_complement with Z integral suffices for negative tests, even without equality proof; correct HNF provides exact signed extension.

Introduce nonnegative transport variables y_ij(a,c) only on retained edges. Require Ax=b, x>=0 and for each pair:
sum_c y_ij(a,c)=x_a for every a in i;
sum_a y_ij(a,c)=x_c for every c in j.
Any genuine cover lifts by putting unit mass on its chosen edge per pair. Hence exact Farkas infeasibility implies native NO. Nonempty relaxation UNKNOWN, not SAT or exact global support. This is a genuine algebra-to-LP hybrid: lattice exclusions change the domain of the LP, even when every row has at least one compatible neighbor. It uses a local marginal relaxation, not a new general LP completeness theorem.

Independent global method: by maxflow/mincut each pair has such a transport iff for every subset S of left rows x(S)<=x(N(S)), because both group totals are 1. A violating Hall inequality is acquirable by a paid rational maxflow (source-left capacities x_a, right-sink x_c, cross-edge capacity 2). No need to enumerate the exponentially many S. Extended LP avoids reliance on a cutting-plane iteration bound; polynomial separation alone needs a polynomial ellipsoid algorithm, not an arbitrary repeated cut loop. A Farkas vector on extended equations can be directly verified by checking each retained edge column and original row column. This theorem does not claim pair distributions share a global joint distribution.
