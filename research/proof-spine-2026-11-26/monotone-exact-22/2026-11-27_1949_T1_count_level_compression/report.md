## Theorem (elementary, not a novelty claim)
For a Boolean factor on distinct coordinates, let p be a fixed sign mask and K a set of allowed integer occupancies. The factor is exactly {x : popcount(x XOR p) in K} iff every word in each selected Hamming shell belongs to the table and every word in other shells is absent. For t fixed signed ones and u free coordinates the attainable occupancies are exactly all integers in [t,t+u]. The factor is infeasible iff K has empty intersection with this interval. A free signed bit has 0-support iff K intersects [t,t+u-1], 1-support iff K intersects [t+1,t+u]. These tests give exactly the same local generalized arc consistency (GAC) as explicit truth-table scanning; repeated fair application yields the same closure or contradiction. The result applies to disjoint occupancy levels, not merely intervals or parity. A polarity mask, level mask and exact scope suffice; no lost joint state. For fixed arity k, brute recognition of 2^k signs with at most 2^k explicit word tests per sign (and linear search in allowed rows in this implementation) is polynomial in original N for polynomially many bounded-width factors. Verify full truth tables before importing. With all fixed-width factors, at most v additional pins or conflict in each propagation epoch, so a polynomial guard bank and all replay work give polynomial PARTIAL procedure. No theorem guarantees a useful guard or contraction for all NO. General unbounded arity recognition or compressed join may be expensive. Coefficients with repeated variables or unequal weights need exact integer rows/DP, not this unit-weight argument.

Sparse exact-one/exact-two signed rows can be summed or subtracted across different scopes, then interval bounds give sound exclusions. If no table has singleton occupancy, this exact-equality generator emits zero. Projected clause factors in both cases were all signed count-level representable but none had singleton occupancy. Prior positive exact-one recognition used conjunctions of multiple clause factors; this new local recognizer does not supersede it. Next bridge: recognize signed cardinality conjunctions across scopes (e.g. an at-least-one row plus pairwise exclusions), then export sparse conservation consequences into the row-shadow lane.
