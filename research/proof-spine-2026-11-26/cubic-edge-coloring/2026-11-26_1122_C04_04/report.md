## 2. Exact bidirectional matching/flow adapter (proved)
Let f:E→F2² be conserved with exactly one zero z=uv. Use nonzero labels 1,2,3 with XOR addition. At u and v the other two edges have equal nonzero labels r and s respectively. Choose a label a not in {r,s}. The set M={e:f(e)=a} ∪ {z} is a perfect matching: every other cubic vertex has all three nonzero labels, and neither u nor v has another incident edge with label a. In F=G−M the other two labels alternate except at u,v. In a cycle C, the number of changes between labels is even, so |C| mod 2 equals the number of repeated-label vertices on C mod 2. Consequently either both endpoints lie on one even cycle, in which case every cycle is even and a proper coloring is obtained by recoloring the factor, or the endpoints lie on two distinct odd cycles, and these are the only odd cycles. In the latter case z is an actual connecting matching edge. This requires only incidence scans and cycle traversal, no target coloring. Conversely, for any admitted M,z assign 3 to all matching edges except z=0. Alternate 1,2 on the complementary cycles, choosing the start of each odd cycle at its endpoint of z; the first and last colors agree there (choose 1 at u and 2 at v, if desired). Each other vertex receives distinct nonzero colors; endpoints satisfy XOR 0, so this is exactly a singleton flow. Thus C03's singleton task and this matching task agree constructively up to an immediate polynomial YES terminal. If any proper coloring exists, its color class containing z is an even-factor matching retaining z, so this output pin costs no existence. This pin is a chosen color-label gauge, NOT a claim that z belongs to all proper matchings. Bridge discovery supplies a sound initial NO terminal (every conserved flow vanishes on a bridge); if the two-odd-cycle input is bridgeless, z cannot lie in a 2-edge cut: that cut has an even shore, both crossing edges would belong to M by matching parity, the factor stays inside shores, and one odd cycle in each even shore is impossible.

## 5. Infinite pinned single-flip obstruction via exact expansion (proved)
Replace each vertex by Heawood 3-pole above with its three boundary endpoints on one bipartition side; identify original seam edge IDs. Any perfect matching selects exactly one seam by integer bipartition balance. Its complement crosses the other two, with one internal path between same-side endpoints (even internal length) plus even internal cycles. Contraction to vertex preserves parity of every external cycle and loses only even internal cycles. Conversely every base matching lifts by choosing the appropriate offset-color matching at each pole, preserving its complementary parity and z. For two matchings, changed seam selection gives exactly two incident symmetric-difference seams per changed patch, none otherwise. A single expanded alternating cycle either remains internal and projects to no difference, or traverses each changed patch once between its two changed seams and projects to one base alternating cycle. Therefore a pinned single-flip repair in any expanded graph would give one in the base graph, contradiction. The chosen graph's pinned obstruction persists at all scales; actual matching lifts executed on 20,260,3380 vertices, oddness 2 vs0 and delta exactly two cycles for chosen targets at all scales.
