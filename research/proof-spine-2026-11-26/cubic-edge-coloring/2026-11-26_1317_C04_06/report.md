## A. Exact local construction: TWO-WIRE-LOG-CAP
Let a connected induced cubic region R have four distinct indexed boundary edges, with no internal constraints apart from cubic coloring. Order its seam incidences. Acquire its exact proper boundary relation by exhaustive local color search, storing one actual completion per word. If relation exactly {(p,p,q,q): p,q in F2^2 minus {0}} after a permutation of the four indices, remove R and join each exterior pair by one replacement edge. Admit a reduction only if seam identities and guards are retained and resulting graph is valid (our implementation requires simple loopless cubic). No paired exterior edges are identified just because they are marginally feasible.

Projection: every proper original coloring has equal paired seam colors; contracted coloring satisfies all exterior vertices. Lift: for each actual reduced pair of colors read its single joint local table row, restore original seam and internal edges; all cubic incidences verified. Thus both YES and NO preserved. For disjoint nonadjacent regions, tables compose independently even when exterior components are separated by the replacements. In a general sequential contraction retain reverse chronological maps, including original edge-ID lists. If any guard refers to deleted internals, reject unless its exact guarded relation and witness lift have separately been proved. No claim of preserving an arbitrary pinned flow path.

Polynomial acquisition theorem: fix constants a,b>0. Enumerate connected induced vertex sets of size k<=floor(a log2 N). ## B. Global algebraic obstruction and exact two-chart lift
Any homogeneous F2-linear lift from (p,q) in F2^4 to edge colors that is proper on all nine nonzero p,q assignments is impossible on a connected region with independent equal-pair ports. Proof: for each internal or boundary edge map L_e:F2^4->F2^2, kernel dimension >=2, kernel avoids all (p,q) with p,q nonzero. A subspace contained in the union of the two coordinate axes is contained in one axis (sum of vectors on opposite axes contradicts containment). Hence kernel equals one full axis, rank=2, and L_e is an invertible linear function solely of p or solely of q. If incident edges have differing types, independent p,q choices can make their colors equal, violating all-different. All three incident types must coincide at each vertex. Connectivity implies a common type at every edge and port, contradicting independent port types. FALSIFIED general single homogeneous-linear section, not the exact decision contraction. This includes maps mixing the two coordinates of each color, not merely scalar-linear maps. Do not conflate affine maps with homogeneous maps: rank-one affine maps may be nonzero everywhere. For the specific Petersen four-pole we additionally exhausted 1024 affine maps: 105 are nonzero on all nine assignments, 12 homogeneous; local XOR arc-consistency with fixed seam maps p,p,q,q proves no affine section. The finite certificate is in both JS and Python audits; no general all-affine claim for arbitrary connected poles.

Nevertheless two linear charts suffice on every such colorable pole given exact witnesses for words 1111 and 1122. On p!=q, map reference colors 1,2,3 to p,q,p+q (unique invertible map). On p=q, map them to p,Jp,p+Jp, with fixed order-three invertible J cycling nonzero vectors 1->2->3->1. Both maps are homogeneous linear in boundary input on the respective guarded chart and may be extended linearly off that guard without claiming validity there. Each restores a proper witness by color permutation. Guard acquisition is one equality test on an already acquired reduced coloring, not a search over combinations of charts. Thus at most two charts, and one impossible; no proof of path preservation across chart changes. Petersen internal edge order Q in results.js: 1111 representative (3,2,2,1,3,3,2,3,2,1); 1122 (3,1,2,1,3,3,2,3,1,2). Nine rows, 18 actual local completions. Necessity of equal pairs can also be proved by XOR conservation and reinserting the two removed adjacent Petersen vertices: unequal colors on one pair force a second unequal pair with same XOR, so their common restored edge would have the third color, contradicting Petersen uncolorability. No free assumption of that theorem for arbitrary regions; finite table independently verified.
