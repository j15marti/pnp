## 1. Balance and the precise failure of separate matchings
Let d_L(c),d_R(c) count stubs of color c. Necessity: d_L(c)-d_R(c)=|L|-|R| for each c; sum gives d_L-d_R=3(|L|-|R|). On six ports, possible distributions are 3+3 or 6+0 (or reversed). The former has an even count of each color; the latter exactly two of each. For a color a, reject duplicate selected stubs at one vertex, delete their endpoint set D, and find an ordinary bipartite PM of H-D. This is exactly feasibility of the single color-a matching with all other boundary colors excluded. Lifting its complement alternates the two remaining colors on cycles and paths. All cycles are even, but the prescribed end colors must agree for an odd-length path including its two semiedges (end vertices on opposite classes), and differ for an even-length path (same class). When at most two residual stubs, balance and the local collisions give a complete polynomial test, since there is at most one path. If no PM for any color, reject. If a found PM lifts, return actual coloring. Neither a failed lift of one arbitrary PM nor all three positive matching tests is a decision for the 2+2+2 case.

Explicit falsifier C6: internal vertices 0..5 in cyclic order, one stub each, L even. Boundary word (0,0,1,1,2,2). Each color individually extends: indexed internal matching for color 0 is edges (2,3),(4,5); for 1 (0,1),(4,5); for 2 (0,1),(2,3). No joint coloring: edge 1-2 is forced 2, edge 3-4 forced 0, edge 5-0 forced 1, hence vertices 0 and 1 require edge 0-1 to be 2 and 1 respectively. Neighbor word (0,0,1,2,2,1) admits cyclic internal edge colors (1,2,0,1,0,2). Both pass balance, and all three colors individually feasible. This is a guard-relative NO, not a closed graph NO. It also has an elementary local-propagation bypass. Stretch an internal edge by a chain of K3,3-minus-edge equality 2-poles. Every pair of equal seam colors extends, only equal colors extend by parity. Exact original word support preserved in both directions; each single-color matching lifts (take cap coloring with seam color a iff original edge selected; otherwise another color). Thus marginal false positives persist at arbitrary scale but are structurally easy, not arbitrary-decider hardness.

## 2. Width-four matching condensation (new exact lemma)
Fix any color a in a 2+2+2 word, with D its two distinct pinned endpoints. If H-D has no PM the word is impossible. Otherwise acquire one PM M using deterministic augmenting paths. H-D has balanced vertex classes. Its new boundary consists of remaining original non-a stubs not touching D, plus incidences of edges from H-D into D. There are at most 4+4=8 such incidences, hence at most four per side by balance. Edges between two deleted vertices are handled separately. At each deleted vertex its other two incident colors must be the two non-a colors b,c in either order: enumerate at most four assignments jointly, enforcing any original pinned stubs and shared-edge equality, and fix the induced boundary words at H-D. No choice of target coloring is assumed.

Contract each matched edge of M to one pair-node. Direct every remaining internal edge from the pair containing its L endpoint to the pair containing its R endpoint. Compute SCCs, retaining original edge IDs; every pair belongs to a balanced block. An edge belongs to some PM iff it is in M or its directed arc lies on a directed cycle (same SCC). Proof: symmetric difference between M and another PM is alternating cycles; conversely flip a directed simple cycle. Thus no inter-SCC edge can have color a under this boundary guard. Every PM is the disjoint union of internal PMs of the blocks, and conversely any such union is a PM. This is unconditional forced-information closure within the pinned chart; no inference to arbitrary source matchings without that guard. All inter-block edges can only be b,c. This rederives the matching-support principle of Tassa 2012.

Take any topological order of the condensation. Let U be a prefix union of blocks, and let q(U) count edges to unprocessed blocks. Every crossing edge goes from an L vertex in U to an R vertex outside U. Count total degree 3 at every vertex of U in H-D INCLUDING external stubs. Since |L_U|=|R_U|, internal edges cancel and
  q(U) = d_R^ext(U)-d_L^ext(U) <= d_R^ext(H-D) <= 4.
There are no incoming arcs at a prefix. For a single next block, its R-side ports consist of its incoming edges (subset of preceding frontier) plus its original external R stubs. The same identity gives at most four in total: q(previous)<=d_R^ext(previous), and the external stubs of the new block are disjoint from previous ones. Balance gives at most four L ports, hence at most eight ports per block. This is a combinatorial bound on actual indexed wires, independent of graph size and SCC internal size. It is not a bound on graph treewidth.

Exact join: every frontier is a subset of at most four ORIGINAL edges; state is the b/c assignment on these edges (at most 16). For each block acquire attainable b/c boundary words (at most 2^8=256) and actual local 3-color lifts. Join only equal colors on the shared edge IDs; remove processed edges from the frontier and retain backpointer. Store one witness per exact frontier state. Every global proper coloring restricts to this join since its color-a matching cannot use inter-SCC edges. Conversely any accepted join glues complete local colorings and the chosen deleted-vertex assignments; all incidences properly colored, so selecting all edges of a named color and alternating its complementary even cycles reconstructs original source witness. If exact tables show empty reachable state set, NO for that pinned word. If a table row is unacquired, it can be dropped only for positive search; a negative answer requires either certified irrelevance via overapproximation or complete exact rows. Implemented conservative UNKNOWN policy if any relevant-attempt row unacquired. Do not conflate marginal local feasibility with simultaneous join.

## 3. Exact acquisition scope and cost
All blocks are balanced and have only b,c boundary colors. With <=6 ports, each color count is even, and a most frequent color leaves <=2 residual stubs. Section 1's ordinary matching+path reconstruction is therefore complete, including zero-port blocks. For an eight-port block, counts 8+0 or 6+2 are similarly complete. Only 4+4 can require four residual stubs. The unresolved local row is precisely compatible path pairing in a balanced eight-pole, not unbounded frontier enumeration. A single arbitrary PM with valid lift certifies YES; a missing color matching certifies NO. Otherwise use an exact enumeration if the block has <=K log2(N) vertices for any one fixed K (at most 3^(3|V|/2) candidates, hence N^(1.5K log2 3)), or report UNKNOWN. The executed finite fallback cap is 12 vertices, not a universal size assumption. For each original word try any of the three colors as first matching; a definitive answer is valid. For all words of a six-pole, run <=729 choices, at most 3 first-color charts and 4 boundary lifts per chart, <=n blocks and 256 local rows each. If all needed rows acquired, this is an exact constant-size relation on the original six stubs, with stored actual witnesses. This is a polynomial-time terminal for the acquired condition that all required balanced 4+4 eight-port rows are solved by matching or logarithmic kernels (or exact overapproximate rejection); special sufficient condition: all blocks <=6 ports or small eight-port blocks.
