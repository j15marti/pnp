## Exact theorem
A good matching means a perfect matching whose complement is all even cycles; exactly equivalent to proper cubic 3-edge-coloring. For each positive guard S subset E, |S|<=2, maintain a bit D(S) that if true certifies no good matching contains S. Initially ordinary matching infeasibility (delete endpoints of required disjoint edges and any fixed forbidden edges) is a sound ban; any guard containing a banned subset is banned. For S, define X(S)={e in E: ordinary PM cannot contain S union {e}, or some already banned T subset S union {e}}. Every good matching containing S avoids every edge of X(S). If X(S) contains an entire vertex star, such a matching cannot cover that vertex. If X(S) contains an odd cycle, that cycle would be a component of the complementary 2-factor, since every vertex on it already has two complement edges. Thus either case implies D(S). Constructive proof: compute X(S) with cached exact matching queries on at most three edges, run bipartite BFS on graph (V,X(S)), recover odd cycle from same-parity BFS conflict. Contradiction proof: any good extension of S supplies one edge in every vertex star and odd cycle, yet all edges of the acquired obstruction are excluded. No enumeration of cycles needed. Empty guard banned implies NO; saturation without it UNKNOWN. Monotonicity allows in-place scans, and each added bit is permanent; dependencies point to earlier bits. For fixed k guards of size<=k, replace triple by k+1, analogous proof; no growing k guarantee.
