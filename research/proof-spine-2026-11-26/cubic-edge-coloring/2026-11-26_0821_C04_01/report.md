## 1. Simultaneous partition and matching contraction (main theorem)
Write F_M=G-M, o(M)=number of odd cycles in F_M, and phi_G(x,M)=number of F_M edges with equal endpoint bits under binary vertex partition x. For any PM M, min_x phi_G(x,M)=o(M) by cycle alternation. G colorable iff some PM has o=0. For any pair M,P define a directed best-cut transition M=>P iff there exists x with phi(x,M)=o(M) and phi(x,P)<=o(M). Equality at the old objective is the neutral case; our transition definition also permits strict improvement. This is not the C03 two-flow shared-zero objective.

Let S be a connected bipartite shore of a two- or three-edge cut, with connected other shore (sufficient, not necessary), distinct inside and outside seam endpoints, and simple reduced cubic graph. Require |S|>1. No fixed internal partition or matching pins on S. Outside pins/seam selection pins can be retained exactly; no arbitrary internal or cross-chart guard transfer. Cap graph is obtained by joining two inside endpoints in the 2-cut case, or adding a new vertex adjacent to three inside endpoints in the 3-cut case. For simple source and distinct endpoints the 2-cap may have a parallel edge; decline such caps in the simple algorithm, or keep edge IDs with a bipartite multigraph matching routine. For both cap graphs degree sums 3(|L|-|R|)=d_L-d_R, where d_L,d_R count seam incidences: for two seams d_L=d_R=1 and |S| even; for three all seams are on the same bipartition side and |S| odd. Thus cap graph is bipartite cubic. Hall from degree regularity gives a perfect matching and, by repeated removal, three disjoint PMs. Every cap seam edge belongs to some cap PM and is absent from another. Store actual internal matching witness for every needed seam status; at most three polynomial ordinary PM probes plus verification (or compute bipartite 1-factorization). No target supplied.

2-cut contraction: replace S by one edge joining outside endpoints u,v. Every PM crosses the cut 0 or2 times by parity; map the latter to the new edge selected. Conversely each reduced PM has actual cap witness for its corresponding status, deleting cap edge and adding seams when selected. If F_M crosses the cut, it crosses twice, and the inside path connecting seam endpoints has odd length by bipartiteness; including seams it is still odd length, so substitution by one edge preserves cycle parity. All inside cycles even. Thus o(M)=o(project M) for EVERY PM, not just even ones. For any original partition x keep exterior bits. If F_M traverses the side, a path of odd length with equal endpoint bits has at least one monochromatic edge. Therefore replacement edge's defect indicator <= original path defect count. If cap edge in projected PM, no local defect in reduced cost. For lift of ANY reduced x assign the inside bipartition so the first seam crosses x; all internal edges cross, and the second seam is noncrossing iff the outside endpoints equal. This yields exactly the replacement edge cost for EVERY lifted PM simultaneously, including any fixed original M with the same projection.

3-cut contraction: replace S by one vertex v incident to the three seams. Any PM crosses an odd number of seams, 1 or3. The case3 is impossible: the complementary factor would be entirely internal on S, a union of even cycles in a bipartite graph of odd order. Thus every PM chooses exactly one seam, and correspondence to projected PM is onto using cap witnesses as above. F_M traverses the two unselected seams by a path of EVEN length between their outside endpoints (inside endpoints same bipartition class), with all internal cycles even. Substitution by a two-edge path through v preserves o(M) exactly. For simultaneous projection, retain original x outside and set x_v opposite the majority bit among three outside seam endpoints. For ANY unselected pair i,j the new two-edge path has zero defects if x_i=x_j, one otherwise. This is the minimum possible for an even-length path between those endpoints. Hence for EVERY PM M, phi_H(proj x,proj M)<=phi_G(x,M), with the SAME x projection independent of M. For lift of any x on H, assign all inside vertices of the boundary bipartition class x_v and all others 1-x_v. Internal edges cross, seam bits agree with original seam incidence cost; this lift gives exact equality for EVERY lifted PM simultaneously. Old M can remain the original pinned matching rather than being replaced by a canonical internal witness.

Thus for each finite list of matchings M_1,...,M_l and any componentwise upper budget vector, feasible partition cost bounds are identical after projecting the matchings. In proof, projection is coordinatewise nonincreasing, reverse lift exact for every matching in list. It even works simultaneously for all matchings, without enumerating them. This is equality of downward-constraint feasibility (not equality of exact cost distributions or symbolic counts). All unrestricted source decisions preserved: even target PM exists iff one exists in reduced graph; lifts construct actual coloring by alternating factor cycles. Guard restriction explicit above.

Corollary: define nu_G(M)=min {phi_G(x,P): phi_G(x,M)=o(M), o(P)=0}, infinity if no even target. Projection sends an old optimal partition to an old optimal partition since o preserved and reduced phi cannot fall below o. Reverse lift attains original optimum and all target costs. Therefore nu_G(M)=nu_H(proj M). More generally the transition M=>P iff proj M=>proj P, for any two fixed original matchings, and every reduced PM has a lift. Consequently entire directed best-cut transition graph is an exact quotient: reachability to an even PM and minimum number of nonincreasing best-cut transitions are preserved (project path, lift path, preserve starting original M, erase any repeated projected states). No target matching or enumeration necessary for contracting. Exact equality of neutral-only transition labels is NOT asserted for general projection since projected cost may be smaller. This is a new structural theorem distinct from CEC13, which requires ALL edges incident to two-cut region cross a supplied fixed partition. Here x is not supplied internally, and every matching's costs and oddness are handled jointly. This does not solve reachability on arbitrary prime graphs.

## 2. Complementary parity method, and a direct-exit falsifier
For supplied M with o(M)=2, a candidate even P can be tested for a direct transition without enumerating 2^q phases. Enumerate one old defect edge per odd F_M cycle (<=n² choices), and either no target defect or a pair of target F_P edges (<=1+n² choices; even cycles imply even defect parity). For both factors, impose x_u XOR x_v=0 on designated defects, 1 elsewhere. Duplicate constraints must agree; graph parity consistency by BFS/XOR union-find, construct x when feasible. Both directions exact because old optimum has exactly one defect per odd cycle and none on even, target <=2 and even. O(n^4) linear-time parity probes on explicit edges, fixed polynomial including failed cases. This is a target-pair test, not a method to find unknown even P.
