## A. Kempe support graph and native caps
At a 5-cut each shore has odd order; each color has odd port multiplicity, hence 3,1,1. Encode boundary type by unordered pair of singleton-color ports, an edge of K5. For unguarded multipole R let S_R be set of attainable pairs. Color symmetry ensures G colorable iff S_A intersects S_B. Every nonisolated vertex in S_R has degree at least two: in a coloring with singleton ports a,b and majority color on the other three, the alternating majority/colour-of-a chain from a ends at one of the majority ports c. Swapping the chain changes singleton pair to {c,b}. Independently using majority and colour-of-b gives {a,d} with d different from a,b. Thus no degree-one vertices. On five vertices such a graph has no bridge: deleting a bridge would yield two components each with at least three vertices (otherwise one vertex had degree one in original). Therefore S_R is a union of simple cycles of lengths 3,4,5, including empty union.

Every simple cycle in K5 is exactly the type support of a native cap of at most five vertices: triangle = one cubic vertex on the triangle's ports plus direct edge joining the other two ports (these must have same majority color); square = three-vertex path, central vertex on unused port, other four ports assigned to endpoints alternately around square (each endpoint port pair distinct, so singleton pair crosses the two groups); pentagon = cubic pentagonal region with ordered ports around it (singleton ports adjacent, as in Mazak/Rajnik/Skoviera 2022, section 3; also all ten assignments checked directly). Counts 10 triangles,15 squares,12 pentagons=37. Given any shore R and cycle C, cap R with corresponding gadget K_C, retaining port order: capped graph colorable iff S_R intersects C. A loop introduced by cap is legitimately NO; parallel edges retain IDs. Witness lift by matching original actual color words under simultaneous permutation. Cap equivalence is decision not equality of ordinary matching sets.

## B. Complete negative cap cover theorem on an actual five-cut
For S subset of K5 define U(S)=E(K5) minus union of all cycles C with C disjoint from S. Then S subset U(S). Equivalently U(S) contains S and bridges of complementary graph K5-S. For two support graphs A,B with no degree-one vertices, A cap B incompatible iff U(A) intersect U(B) empty.
Proof: if A intersects B their common edge survives both. If disjoint, every edge in A lies on a cycle contained in A (no bridges); that cycle is disjoint B, hence that edge is deleted from U(B), and symmetrically. Suppose surviving common edge uv is in neither. Then uv is a bridge of both complement graphs. For each complement choose a cut separating u,v with uv the only complementary cross edge (other disconnected components assigned arbitrarily). Orient both bipartitions u=0,v=1. Every other cross edge of the first cut belongs to A, of second to B. Disjointness means no cross edge in both except uv. For each other vertex w its pair of bits cannot be 00 or 11, as vw or uw would cross both. Nor can both 01 and 10 appear among the three other vertices, since their connecting edge crosses both. Thus all three have the same mixed type, and the cuts isolate u and v respectively (or vice versa). A contains all three spokes from one isolated endpoint to W, B all three spokes from the other. Disjointness excludes each other's spokes. As no degree-one vertices, both A and B must use edges inside the three-vertex set W to cover all three vertices. Each needs at least two of the three edges of K3, impossible disjointly. Contradiction. Handles empty supports automatically. This is an exact negative-only interface rule: collect NO cap results on each shore, delete corresponding cycles from its upper support. Once upper supports disjoint, source NO; unknown caps never deleted. With all correct cap outcomes this rule is complete at this particular cut, without needing to decide individual port patterns. It is not a free coloring oracle.
