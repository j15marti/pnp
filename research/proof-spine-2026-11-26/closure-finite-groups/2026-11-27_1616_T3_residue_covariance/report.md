## 4. PROVED: gauge-averaged signed-residue identity
For a canonically deduplicated nontautological clause with distinct variables, fix any assignment and let k be the exact number of true literals. Reencode each variable independently by x'_v=x_v XOR g_v, reversing its literal polarity when g_v=1. Transform the assignment and all clauses together. Satisfaction and exact k remain unchanged. For a fixed true occurrence its signed value in the new representation is s'_v=(-1)^{g_v}s_v. Under uniform independent gauge bits, the k signs are independent symmetric signs, even though rows need not be independent. Let a_k be the number of sign strings with sum divisible by3. By symmetry the two nonzero residue counts are equal, and appending either sign gives a_{k+1}=2^k-a_k, a_0=1. Induction gives a_k=(2^k+2(-1)^k)/3. For k>0 the probability of satisfied signed-zero is therefore (1+2(-1/2)^k)/3. Exclude k=0 from this score. Exact controls k1..4:0/2,2/4,2/8,6/16.

Linearity of expectation gives an exact gauge-invariant averaged score over any clause table, no joint-independence assumption and no exponential enumeration. For width<=3, four times the mean signed-zero satisfied-row count is 2 times the number of exact-two rows plus the number of exact-three rows. For width<=w use common denominator2^w and integer numerator sum of a_k*2^(w-k) over satisfied rows. Bit length O(w+log m); exact evaluation from k counts alone, polynomial even for variable width. Can use this as a future deterministic invariant tie-break, not tested as a solver arm in this run. Finite precision, no semantic oracle; candidate flips can be scored from incident lists or polynomial full rescans.

Counterexample to covariance of raw tie-break: clauses (x),(y),(x OR z),(y OR w) at x=y=0,z=w=1 with x lower ID than y. Flipping either x or y repairs exactly one defect and creates no signed-zero satisfied row, so raw tie picks x. Reencode x'=1-x, transforming its clauses and assignment. Flipping x' now creates a satisfied mixed-sign row (-x' OR z) with signed sum zero, whereas flipping y does not. Raw residue tie chooses y. Hence the raw score and even selected move depend on chart gauge; it is a permissible discovery heuristic but not an intrinsic semantic invariant or UNSAT certificate. Averaging removes exactly this source of dependence but leaves ordinary occupancy information, not a new universal progress guarantee.
