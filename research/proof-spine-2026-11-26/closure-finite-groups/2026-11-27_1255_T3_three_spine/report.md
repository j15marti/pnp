## Theorem: symmetry pin and elimination commute
In a consistent signed E quotient, every port equals its root bit XOR a fixed phase. Complement all root bits. Every E equality and signed edge remains true; a balance sum s becomes 4-s, so weight two is invariant. Thus for any existing root g, a global complement orbit has a representative g=0. This is an existential symmetry reduction of feasibility, not a forced bit. For independent connected components of the factor hypergraph the argument holds separately, provided guards are invariant under the corresponding flips or absent. Do not insert gauge pins under arbitrary noninvariant guards.

Let J be the conjunction of factors incident to a distinct variable v. For g != v, restriction R_g commutes with existential projection: R_g(exists v J)=exists v R_g(J), by evaluating the two possible v values at each fixed remaining assignment. Restriction cannot enlarge any factor scope; a bucket containing g loses that coordinate. If an elimination ordering avoids g, gauge-first gives no larger bucket scopes than the same order without restriction (allowing vacuous steps for disappeared variables). Actual adaptive orders may differ; no universal width drop is implied. One can check support complement closure of every local table, choose maximum incidence root deterministically, restrict tables, then acquire an exact bounded-scope projection. This changes the representation before the expensive search and uses no original CNF symbol advice.

For a fixed cap k, a complete elimination order whose incident union has size <=k gives exact decision in polynomial work. On a quotient with n variables and m factors, each pivot replaces at least one incident factor with at most one new table, enumerating <=2^k words and testing all incident factors. At most n pivots, all candidate scopes can be recomputed by scanning m tables for each variable at each stage. A coarse bound O(n^2 (m+n) 2^k poly(k,log N)) indexed bit operations plus normalization and reconstruction, with polynomial bit overhead for sorting/comparing IDs and storage. Scope-cap greedy discovery may decline, even if a different order exists. Reverse choices reconstruct a witness; complete UNSAT can instead be certified by empty support or checked RUP. Polynomially many gauge candidates may be dovetailed with all computation charged; no universal coverage theorem follows. Finite cap bounds in the tests are not the fixed all-input proof bound.
