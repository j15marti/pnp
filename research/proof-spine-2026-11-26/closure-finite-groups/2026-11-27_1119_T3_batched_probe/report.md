## Model and theorem
For a CNF C on n bits, a finite list of k explicit guard assignments, encode for each signed literal l a k-bit mask M_l of lanes deriving l, initialized by the guard. Let D be the mask of contradictory lanes. For each synchronous round take a snapshot and compute
M'_l = M_l OR (OR_{clauses A containing l} AND_{u in A minus {l}} M_{-u});
D' = D OR (OR_A AND_{u in A} M_{-u}) OR (OR_l (M'_l AND M'_{-l})).
An empty AND is all k ones. Empty clauses mark all lanes dead. Use exact finite k-bit operations, no approximate softmax or rounding. This is simultaneous Horn forward closure of the unit-resolution rules with signed propositions. Every live lane coincides with its independent scalar UP closure; every dead lane has a unit-resolution contradiction, because every propagated literal is implied by the current lane assumptions and C up to contradiction. Conversely ordinary unit propagation is a subset of this monotone rule set, and a UP conflict is eventually flagged. Induction on rounds and on scalar derivation steps proves both directions. At most 2n literal additions per lane are possible; a lane with no change in a full round is fixed. Thus at most 2n+2 rounds suffice globally (all lanes advance in each round). This bound is in rounds, not work.

Use prefix/suffix conjunctions per clause to compute all omitted-literal products with O(sum clause lengths + number of clauses + n) word-vector operations per round. A k-bit mask costs O(k) Boolean work, not one free operation, and peak masks/state cost O(nk + explicit clause encoding + max clause length * k) bits, aside from indexing. Generating candidate guards, checking deduplication, scalar replays, scanning candidate implications and source normalization are additional. For a fixed width w, enumerate at most sum_{i<=w} 2^i binom(n,i) guards (polynomial in n for fixed w). Negate a dead guard to get a RUP clause. A live singleton lane g deriving l yields (-g OR l), also RUP: under g and -l, the same unit derivation contradicts. Retain only sound clauses, check individually, and reapply root closure. Every new distinct clause of width <=w decreases the integer count of not-yet-added such clauses (bounded by the same polynomial). Therefore this restricted closure procedure can be completed in fixed polynomial total bit cost, including polynomial candidate generation, all lanes, all rounds, and replays, for explicit polynomial-size input and fixed w. It may stop without deciding feasibility. This is not the sought universal decrease: a saturated residual may be unresolved. If w grows with input, neither enumeration nor credit remains uniformly polynomial without an additional bound. Dense attention is optional: exact indexing with ordinary bit vectors suffices. On a single tape, a serial scan implementation can access the polynomial-size serialized arrays at polynomial overhead per indexed operation; cumulative memory <= total time times peak space, all replicas counted. No fixed-power transport result yields an unrestricted superpolynomial lower bound. No bit-level runtime or cumulative memory was measured in this run.

Counterexample to pure width-two probe completeness: the 16 width-four clauses excluding every assignment to four variables. No guard of width at most two produces a unit or conflict; all clauses retain at least two unassigned literals unless satisfied. Hence no nontrivial new unit/binary clause is acquired by these rules. This UNSAT CNF is a counterexample for the named closure rules alone, not for the full native preprocessing (a small exact join solves it). No universal post-projection obstruction or P!=NP conclusion.
