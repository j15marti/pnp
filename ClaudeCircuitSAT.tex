\section{Method}
Build circuits step by step: fix basis $B_2$ (fan-in 2, free negation); track for each $n$ the
exact minimum gate count $g(f)$ (wires $=2g$ plus output) over all $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$, then pass to the
decision problem $\mathrm{CSAT}_{n,m}$: given a circuit with $n$ inputs and $m$ gates, is it satisfiable?
Lower bounds are tracked as a function of the parameters $(n,m)$ (parametrized ledger).

\section{Parametrized view}
For $\mathrm{CSAT}_{n,m}$ the natural parameters are $n$ (search space), $m$ (circuit size), and $k=m-n$ (excess).
Trivial facts: $m<n-1$ implies some input is irrelevant. Open: any unconditional bound of form $T(n,m)\ge 2^{\omega(1)\cdot\ldots}$.
Candidate directions: (i) bounds for restricted models (monotone, bounded-depth, read-once branching programs) as a staircase of provable ledger rows;
(ii) the step-by-step gate-elimination count extended to $\mathrm{SAT}$-hard families (planned: track which $n\le5$ families force $>n-1$ gates).
