\section{Model and ledger}
A circuit $C$ has $n$ inputs, $g$ binary gates, hence $w=2g$ gate-input wires.
$\mathrm{SAT}(n,w)$: decide $\exists x\,C(x)=1$. Track the triple
$(n,w,\text{parameter }\pi)$ with $\pi\in\{\text{treewidth},\text{pathwidth},\text{\#solutions},\dots\}$
and look for bounds $T(n,w,\pi)$; a parametrised bound such as $2^{\Omega(\pi)}$ is the target.
Step plan: (1) smallest circuits per $n$ and per solution count (this file),
(2) which wires are \emph{critical} (flipping them flips SAT), (3) width-parametrised
bounds, (4) compare with repository work.

\section{Step 1: structural lemmas}
\begin{lemma}[Connectivity, PROVED]\label{l:conn}
If $f$ depends on all $n$ inputs and is computed by a fan-in-2 circuit then $g\ge n-1$, so $w\ge 2(n-1)$.
\end{lemma}
\emph{Proof.} The inputs must lie in the output's cone; a DAG with one sink reaching $n$ sources with in-degree $\le2$ has $\ge n-1$ internal nodes (tree counting). \qed

\begin{lemma}[Reading bound, PROVED, query model]\label{l:read}
Let $C_0=\bigwedge_i x_i$ built as a chain ($n-1$ AND gates). Replacing any one gate-input wire
by the negation of its source (or by a constant 0) yields an UNSAT circuit; $C_0$ is SAT. Hence any algorithm
that is correct on all circuits of this shape must read all $w$ wires: $\Omega(w)$ time. (Trivial; separates nothing.)
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Black-box, KNOWN]
If the solver may only evaluate $C$, $\Theta(2^n)$ evaluations are needed in the worst case (adversary hides one satisfying point).
The white-box question is exactly whether wire structure can beat this, and the parameter $\pi$ measures how much structure there is:
for treewidth $k$ the time is $2^{O(k)}\mathrm{poly}$ (KNOWN upper bound), and SETH-type lower bounds (CONDITIONAL, KNOWN) say $2^{o(k)}$ is unlikely.
\end{proposition}
