\paragraph{Status.} Unconditional super-linear lower bounds for Circuit-SAT in the
Turing/RAM model are \textbf{OPEN}; P versus NP remains \textbf{OPEN}. Everything below is
either a proved bound in an explicitly restricted \emph{access model} or a labelled conjecture.
Labels: PROVED, COMPUTATIONAL, CONDITIONAL, OPEN.

\section{Setup and parameters}
A circuit $C$ has $n$ inputs, $s$ gates of fan-in $\le 2$ over $\{\wedge,\vee,\neg\}$, and
$w\le 2s$ wires (connections). Circuit-SAT: $\exists x\,C(x)=1$. Lower bounds are
parametrized by $(n,s)$ (or $(n,w)$). Two access models:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{WIRE}: algorithm sees $C$ only via queries ``what feeds gate $g$?''; cost = wire reads.
\item \textbf{BLACK}: algorithm only evaluates $C(x)$; cost = evaluations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Step ledger (build-up)}
Step $n$: smallest circuit with unique model on $n$ inputs is a conjunction of $n$ literals,
$n-1$ gates, $2(n-1)$ wires; exact synthesis over $\{\wedge,\vee\}$ with free negation confirms
this for $n=2,3$ (min gates $1,2$; min wires $2,4$) \textsc{(COMPUTATIONAL, script
\texttt{research/agents/claude/circuitsat/wire\_adversary.py})}. For $n\ge 4$ the
exhaustive check is not yet run; the formula $n-1$ is an upper bound (PROVED) and
the matching lower bound ($n$ essential inputs need $\ge n-1$ binary gates to be connected) is PROVED
by connectivity of the underlying DAG.

$n$ & min gates (unique model) & min wires\\\hline
1 & 0 & 0\\ 2 & 1 & 2\\ 3 & 2 & 4\\ $n$ & $n-1$ & $2(n-1)$

\section{Proved lower bounds in restricted models}
\begin{theorem}[WIRE, PROVED]\label{thm:wire}
Every deterministic algorithm deciding Circuit-SAT in the WIRE model reads $\Omega(w)$ wires
on some instance, even with $n=1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Family: $a_i=x_1\wedge y_i$ for $i\le m$, output $\bigwedge_i a_i$. Base instance $y_i=x_1$ for all
$i$ is satisfiable ($x_1=1$). Instance $B_b$ sets $y_b=\neg x_1$ and is unsatisfiable. $B_b$ and base
differ in exactly one wire. An algorithm that leaves wire $b$ unread cannot distinguish base from
$B_b$; so it must read all $m$ wires $y_i$ (checked exhaustively for $m\le 8$).
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[BLACK, PROVED]\label{thm:black}
Deterministic BLACK algorithms need $2^n$ evaluations in the worst case.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Instances $C_a=[x=a]$ ($n-1$ gates) and the constant-0 circuit; the all-zero answers of an algorithm
that skipped point $a$ are consistent with $C_a$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Parametrized form] In a hybrid model charging 1 per wire read and $1$ per
evaluation, the two families give $\Omega(\max(w,2^n))$ only as a maximum of separate bounds; a combined
bound of type $w\cdot 2^{n}$ is \textbf{OPEN} and is not implied by the above.
\end{remark}

\section{Why this does not reach P vs NP}
Both bounds are artifacts of restricted access: WIRE forbids reading the circuit sequentially at
cost $O(w)$ (linear is the trivial upper bound for reading), and BLACK forbids inspecting the circuit.
A white-box algorithm pays $\Omega(w)$ simply to read the input, so Theorem~\ref{thm:wire} is
only the trivial input-size bound. Relativization-type barriers apply to BLACK arguments.
Any Turing-model statement of the form $\mathrm{time}\ge w^{1+\epsilon}$ would be new.
