\section{Model and ledger}
A circuit has $n$ inputs, $g$ binary gates over the full basis $B_2$ (negation free), one output. \emph{Wires} $=2g$. For each non-output node let $d^+(v)\ge1$ be its fan-out (every node of a minimal circuit lies on a path to the output). The \emph{fan-out excess} is $r=\sum_{v\neq out}(d^+(v)-1)$ and $q=|\{v:d^+(v)\ge2\}|\le r$ is the number of \emph{shared nodes}.

\begin{prop}[Wire identity, PROVED]\label{p:id}
$g=(n-1)+r$ and wires $=2(n-1)+2r$.
\end{prop}
\emph{Proof.} Counting edges two ways: $2g=\sum_{v\ne out}d^+(v)=(n+g-1)+r$, so $g=n-1+r$. \qed

So every connection beyond the tree-minimum $2(n-1)$ is paid for by fan-out excess; a gate lower bound $g(f)\ge cn$ is the same as $r\ge (c-1)n+1$.

\begin{lem}[Dependence bound, PROVED]
If $f$ depends on all $n$ inputs then $g(f)\ge n-1$ and wires $\ge 2(n-1)$, tight for $\mathrm{AND}_n$ ($r=0$).
\end{lem}
\emph{Proof.} The inputs and gates form a connected DAG with $n$ sources; Prop.~\ref{p:id} with $r\ge0$ (every input has fan-out $\ge1$). \qed

\paragraph{Known explicit bounds (KNOWN, not re-verified).} Gate elimination gives $\approx 3n$ for $B_2$ (Blum), and the repository's \texttt{GROKPROOF} records $3.5n-O(\log n)$ (ECCC TR26-260, cited there; not checked by me). Via Prop.~\ref{p:id}, $r\gtrsim 2.5n$ for such functions. No super-linear explicit bound is known; Circuit-SAT itself has no unconditional super-linear bound.

\section{Parameterised statements}
\begin{prop}[Fan-out parameterised upper bound, PROVED]\label{p:fpt}
Circuit-SAT is solvable in time $O(2^{q}\cdot s)$, where $q$ is the number of nodes (inputs or gates) of fan-out $\ge2$ and $s$ the circuit size; hence in $O(2^{\min(q,n)}s)$ (guessing only inputs also suffices) and $2^{\min(r,n)}\mathrm{poly}(s)$.
\end{prop}
\emph{Proof.} Guess a bit $b_v$ for each shared node. Cut the out-edges of shared nodes and feed the constant $b_v$ instead; require the original computation of each shared gate to evaluate to $b_v$ (shared inputs just take value $b_v$). The result is a forest of read-once formulas whose roots carry target values. For a read-once formula, compute bottom-up the set $\subseteq\{0,1\}$ of attainable values (leaves: $\{0,1\}$; gate: image of the product set), in linear time; the trees have disjoint leaves, so satisfiability is the conjunction over trees. \qed

\textbf{Consequence.} Any family on which Circuit-SAT needs superpolynomial time must have $q=\omega(\log s)$: super-logarithmically many nodes whose values are reused. Lower-bound effort should be concentrated on this reuse budget, i.e.\ on $r$ rather than on $n$ or $s$ separately.

\begin{prop}[Black-box bound, PROVED, relativizing, no bearing on P vs NP]
A deterministic algorithm that accesses $C$ only through evaluation queries needs $2^n-1$ queries on the family ``at most one satisfying point''.
\end{prop}
\emph{Proof.} Adversary answers $0$ to every query; after $2^n-2$ queries two points remain consistent with ``unsatisfiable'' and ``satisfied at the unqueried point''. \qed

\begin{prop}[Reading bound, PROVED, trivial]
Deciding Circuit-SAT on AND-of-literals circuits (UNSAT iff some variable occurs in both polarities) requires reading $\Omega(n)$ wires: an adversary answers distinct fresh variables until all but one literal slot is read. This is an input-size bound, not hardness.
\end{prop}

\paragraph{Conditional tightness (CONDITIONAL).} CNF-SAT embeds in Circuit-SAT with $q\le n$, so under SETH no $2^{(1-\varepsilon)q}\mathrm{poly}(s)$ algorithm exists for all $q$; Prop.~\ref{p:fpt} is then tight in the exponent.
