\section{Setup}
A circuit $C$ has $n$ inputs, gates of fan-in $2$ (any of the 16 binary functions), and wire count $w(C)=2\cdot\#\text{gates}$ (+1 for output designation, ignored). \CSAT$(n,w)$: given $C$ with $n$ inputs and $\le w$ wires, is there $x$ with $C(x)=1$? Parameters: $n$ (inputs) and $w$ (wires); a parametrized bound is a function $T(n,w)$.

\section{Ladder: build up inputs and wires}
\begin{lemma}[connectivity]\label{lem:conn}
If $C$'s output depends on all $n$ inputs, then $C$ has $\ge n-1$ gates, hence $\ge 2n-2$ wires.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} The underlying graph on inputs and gates must connect every input to the output; a fan-in-2 tree/DAG with $n$ sources reaching one sink needs $\ge n-1$ gates (each gate merges at most two components). \end{proof}

\paragraph{Step family.} For $a\in\{0,1\}^n$ let $A_a=\bigwedge_i (x_i\oplus a_i\oplus 1)$ (negations absorbed into gates). It has exactly $n-1$ gates, meets Lemma~\ref{lem:conn} with equality, and is satisfied only by $a$. Going from $n$ to $n+1$ inputs adds exactly one gate and two wires to $A_a$ (Step rule: $\Delta\text{gates}=1$, $\Delta\text{wires}=2$, minimal by Lemma~\ref{lem:conn}).

\begin{theorem}[evaluation-oracle bound]\label{thm:eval}
Let $\mathcal F_n=\{A_a\}\cup\{\mathbf 0\}$ with the circuit available only as a black box that returns $C(x)$. Any deterministic algorithm deciding satisfiability on $\mathcal F_n$ makes $2^n$ queries in the worst case, though every member has $\le 2n-2$ wires. Moreover $Q(n+1)=2Q(n)$ for this family.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Adversary answers $0$ to every query. After $q<2^n$ queries some $a$ is unqueried; both $\mathbf 0$ (unsat) and $A_a$ (sat) are consistent, so the algorithm cannot yet decide. Hence $2^n$ queries are needed (and suffice). For the recurrence: fixing $x_{n+1}\in\{0,1\}$ restricts to two disjoint copies of $\mathcal F_n$ whose query sets are disjoint; the adversary plays both. \end{proof}
\emph{Caveat.} This is a black-box bound only; an algorithm that reads the wiring (e.g.\ finds the literals of $A_a$ in $O(n)$ time) escapes it. It shows that any lower bound must come from the cost of \emph{exploiting} structure, not from small size. Status: PROVED but model-restricted, hence not evidence about P vs NP.

\section{Parametrized targets}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Known upper bounds} (KNOWN, cited from memory, to verify): \CSAT\ with $cn$ gates has $2^{(1-\delta_c)n}$ algorithms (gate-elimination/branching, Williams-type); so $T(n,cn)\le 2^{(1-\delta_c)n}\mathrm{poly}$.
\item \textbf{Conditional lower bound} (CONDITIONAL): SETH implies no $2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}\mathrm{poly}(w)$ algorithm for general $w$; says nothing for $w=O(n)$.
\item \textbf{Unconditional, restricted} : Buss--Williams style alternation-trading limits time-space for SAT (ledger row L13 in the repo); the repo's TPORT barrier (rows L34/L35) is a candidate bridge to a portfolio of small-space solvers.
\item \textbf{OPEN target}: find a natural restricted algorithm class (e.g.\ branching on inputs with gate-elimination simplification) and prove $T(n,w)\ge 2^{\Omega(n/\log w)}$ for it. Next run: define the class formally and test on the $A_a$ family and on hard $n\le 3$ circuits.
\end{enumerate}
