\section*{Status}
\textbf{P versus NP remains OPEN.} Nothing here is an unconditional super-polynomial
lower bound. Labels: PROVED, COMPUTATIONAL, CONDITIONAL, OPEN.

\section{Program}
Ladder: (i) smallest circuits, track gates $g$ and wires $w=2g$ (fan-in 2);
(ii) raise number of inputs $n$ one at a time, record the exact minimum wire count
$W(n)=\max_f 2\,C_{B_2}(f)$; (iii) relate to Circuit-SAT$(n,m)$ with $n$ inputs and $m$ gates,
seeking a \emph{parametrized} bound $T(n,m)$.

\section{Step 1 -- exact minimum circuits (COMPUTATIONAL)}
Basis $B_2$ (all 2-input gates), inputs and constants free. Exhaustive BFS over sets of
computed truth tables (\texttt{research/agents/claude/circuitsat/mincircuit.py}):
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lcccccc}\toprule
$n$ & \#functions & 0 gates & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4\\\midrule
1 & 4 & 3 & 1 & & & \\
2 & 16 & 4 & 12 & & & \\
3 & 256 & 5 & 33 & 114 & 80 & 24\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
So $\max_f C(f)=0,1,4$ for $n=1,2,3$ (wires $0,2,8$). Hardest 3-variable functions need 4 gates.
Runtime $<10$s (depth-4 for $n=3$); $n=4$ is beyond this naive BFS and needs chunked/SAT-based search.

\section{Step 2 -- probe lower bound for Circuit-SAT (PROVED, trivial model)}
Model: the circuit is given as a wire list (gate $j$ has two pointers/literals); the algorithm
probes entries; cost = probes.
\begin{theorem}
Every deterministic algorithm deciding Circuit-SAT for $m$-gate instances makes $\ge m$ probes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $I_0$ be the AND of literals $x_1,\dots,x_m$ (satisfiable). For $j$ let $I_j$ replace
the $j$-th literal by $\neg x_{j'}$ ($j'\ne j$); $I_j$ contains $x_{j'}$ at slot $j'$ and so is
unsatisfiable. An algorithm that never probes slot $j$ sees the same answers on $I_0$ and $I_j$.
Hence all $m$ slots must be probed on $I_0$. \qedhere
\end{proof}
The same adversary gives $\Omega(m)$ randomized probes (Yao, uniform over $\{I_0,I_j\}$ gives
success $\le 1/2+$ fraction unprobed). This is only an input-reading bound, linear in wires;
it is the $n$-independent floor $T(n,m)\ge m$.

\section{Step 3 -- parametrized targets}
Brute force gives $T(n,m)=O(2^n m)$. \textbf{CONDITIONAL:} under ETH, no $2^{o(n)}\mathrm{poly}(m)$
algorithm exists; under SETH, no $O(2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}\mathrm{poly}(m))$ for CNF-SAT, which
transfers to Circuit-SAT. Unconditionally the best known is the probe floor $m$ above and
known restricted-model bounds; the gap $m$ versus $2^n m$ is OPEN.

\section{Repository cross-check}
Relevant: ledger row L13 (Buss--Williams, alternation trading) and the TPORT/ONETAPE notes in
\texttt{research/agents/claude/} give time--space style restricted bounds; none bound
Circuit-SAT by wire counting. Circuit-size counting ($2^{\Omega(2^n/n)}$ functions vs.\ circuits)
shows hard functions exist but is a non-constructive size bound for functions, not for SAT.

\section{Next steps}

\item Define a SAT-hardness measure: minimum wires of a circuit family whose satisfiability

\end{enumerate}
