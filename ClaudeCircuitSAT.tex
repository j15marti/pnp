\section*{Status}
\textbf{P versus NP remains OPEN.} No unconditional super-linear lower bound for \CSAT{} is claimed. Labels: PROVED, KNOWN, COMPUTATIONAL, CONDITIONAL, OPEN.

\section{Setup and parameters}
A circuit $C$ has $k$ inputs, $m$ fan-in-2 gates (any of the 10 non-degenerate binary operations; negation and constants free), and $w=2m$ gate-input wires. \CSAT: is there $x\in\{0,1\}^k$ with $C(x)=1$? Parameters: $(k,m)$; a bound is \emph{parametrized} if stated as $T(k,m)$.
The wire-by-wire programme: for each $k$, record $\mathrm{size}(f)$ (minimum gates, hence minimum wires $2\,\mathrm{size}(f)$) over all $f:\{0,1\}^k\to\{0,1\}$, then ask what any solver must spend on instances of that size.

\section{Lower bounds on solving \CSAT}
\begin{theorem}[Reading bound, PROVED]\label{thm:read}
In the model where $C$ is given as a gate list probed bit-by-bit, every deterministic \CSAT{} algorithm makes $\ge m$ probes (one per gate description) in the worst case, for every $m$ and $k\ge1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Family: $g_0=x_1$, $g_i=g_{i-1}\wedge g_{i-1}$ for $i\le m$; output $g_m$. It is satisfiable. For $j\le m$ let $C_j$ replace gate $j$ by $g_{j-1}\wedge\neg g_{j-1}$; $C_j$ is unsatisfiable. An algorithm that never probes the description of gate $j$ behaves identically on $C$ and $C_j$, so errs on one. Hence it probes every gate. \qed
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Black-box bound, PROVED]\label{thm:bb}
With only evaluation access to $C$, deciding satisfiability needs $2^k$ queries.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The adversary answers $0$ to every query; any unqueried $x$ could be the unique satisfying input. \qed
\end{proof}
\begin{observation}[Parametrized picture]
Upper bound $O(2^k m)$ (brute force); Thm.~\ref{thm:read} gives $\Omega(m)$ for any $k$; Thm.~\ref{thm:bb} says any improvement must inspect structure. Conditionally (SETH), no $2^{(1-\varepsilon)k}\mathrm{poly}(m)$ algorithm (KNOWN, CONDITIONAL). The gap between $\Omega(m)$ and $2^k m$ unconditionally is OPEN; closing it for $m=\mathrm{poly}(k)$ is P vs NP.
\end{observation}

\section{Link to repository work}
From \texttt{AGENTS.md} and the world ledger (\texttt{research/agents/claude/world-ledger/}): row L13 (Buss--Williams) bounds alternation-trading time--space proofs for SAT; rows L34--L37 (T-PORT) show such methods cannot beat linear time for portfolios of about $N$ programs. These constrain what \emph{technique} can give super-linear \CSAT{} bounds in small space but do not yet use the wire-count parameter; a transfer theorem is missing (CANDIDATE: view wire count $2m$ as the size parameter $N$ in the T-PORT barrier).
Gate-elimination lower bounds (Schnorr, Stockmeyer, Iwama--Morizumi, Find--Golovnev--Hirsch--Kulikov; $\approx 3.1n$ for $B_2$) bound circuit \emph{size of a function}, not the time to \emph{decide} satisfiability of a given circuit; the distinction is the crux and the step table above lives on the size side.
