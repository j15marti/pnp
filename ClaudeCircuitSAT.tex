\paragraph{Honest status.} No super-polynomial (or even super-linear) lower bound for Circuit-SAT is proved here. Statements are labelled \PROVED, \KNOWN, \COMP, \CAND, \OPEN. $\mathsf{P}\neq\mathsf{NP}$ remains \OPEN; this document does not change the status in the repository's root \texttt{main.tex}.

\section{Goal and model}
\textbf{Circuit-SAT}: given a fan-in-2 circuit $C$ over basis $B_2$ (all 16 binary gates; negation free) with inputs $x_1..x_n$, decide $\exists x\,C(x)=1$. Two lower-bound questions are tracked separately:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A)] \emph{Construction cost}: the minimum number of gates $g(f)$ and wires $w(f)=2g(f)$ of a circuit computing a function $f$ on $n$ inputs (the ``wire-by-wire'' ledger).
\item[(B)] \emph{Decision cost}: the time an algorithm needs to decide SAT on a circuit of $s$ gates.
\end{enumerate}
Plan: fix the simplest functions, increase $n$ one step at a time, record the exact minimum wires, and look for the pattern a general argument must explain.

\section{Step ledger: exact small cases}
\begin{lemma}[Connectivity, \PROVED]\label{lem:conn}
A fan-in-2 circuit with one output that depends on all $n$ inputs has $g\ge n-1$ gates and $w=2g\ge 2(n-1)$ wires. Tight for $\mathrm{AND}_n,\mathrm{OR}_n,\mathrm{XOR}_n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The underlying graph restricted to the cone of the output is connected and contains the $n$ inputs. Each gate has in-degree 2, so merging components: a graph on $n+g$ nodes with $2g$ edges, connected, needs $2g\ge n+g-1$, i.e.\ $g\ge n-1$. Tightness: balanced trees.
\end{proof}

\section{Decision-cost lower bound (B)}
\begin{lemma}[Probe lower bound, \PROVED, weak]\label{lem:probe}
Any deterministic algorithm that decides Circuit-SAT on size-$s$ instances, accessing the circuit only by gate/wire queries, makes $\Omega(s)$ queries in the worst case.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take $C_0=$ an AND tree of $s$ gate-slots over distinct inputs, satisfiable. For each slot $i$ let $C_i$ replace gate $i$ by the constant $0$ (unsatisfiable). An algorithm that never queries slot $i$ answers identically on $C_0$ and $C_i$, which have different answers. So it must query every slot.
\end{proof}
This is linear in the input description and says nothing about time beyond reading the input; it is recorded only as the exact base of the ledger.

\section{Known gate-elimination bounds (\KNOWN; cited from memory, to be re-verified against sources next run)}
For explicit functions (affine dispersers) on $B_2$ circuits: $2n-O(1)$ (Schnorr, XOR), $3n-o(n)$ (Blum 1984), $3.011n$ (Find--Golovnev--Hirsch--Kulikov 2016), $\approx 3.1n$ (Li--Yang 2022). Super-linear $B_2$ lower bounds for any explicit function are \OPEN, and Circuit-SAT $\notin$ P/poly would imply $\mathsf{NP}\not\subseteq\mathsf{P/poly}$, which is out of reach of known techniques (relativization, natural proofs, algebrization barriers).

\section{Why the wire ledger cannot yet scale}
Gate elimination: setting one variable removes $\ge c$ gates; the constant $c$ is limited by local structure (a variable with fan-out 1 or XOR-heavy neighbourhoods). The $n=3$ data show the first genuine gap above connectivity ($4$ vs.\ $2$). A \CAND{} research direction: tabulate, for each $n\le 5$, the extremal functions and the fan-out pattern of the minimum circuits, to see whether a wire-based potential (e.g.\ counting fan-out-$\ge2$ inputs) separates hard from easy functions.
