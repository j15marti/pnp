\section{Goal and model}
\begin{definition}[Circuit, wires]
A circuit $C$ on inputs $x_1..x_n$ is a DAG of $s$ gates, each of fan-in $2$ over the basis $B_2$ (all 16 binary gates; negation costs one gate), one designated output. A \emph{wire} is an edge; $w(C)=2s$. $\mathrm{CSAT}(C)=[\exists x: C(x)=1]$.
\end{definition}
Ladder idea (user's plan): fix the family $\mathcal F_{n,s}$ of circuits with $n$ inputs and $s$ gates; for each step $(n,s)\to(n+1,s)$ or $(n,s+1)$ record (i) the minimal wires to realise each function, (ii) the cost of deciding CSAT. Lower bounds may be parametrised by $(n,s,w)$ with $w$ a structural width (pathwidth of the wire graph).

\section{Proved lower bounds in explicit models}
\begin{theorem}[Black-box bound, PROVED]\label{t:bb}
Let $n\ge1$. Any deterministic algorithm that decides CSAT for circuits of size $\ge 2n-1$ using only evaluation queries $C(x)$ needs at least $2^n$ queries (on the zero-answering adversary). Randomised, error $\le 1/3$: $\ge 2^n/3$ queries.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Adversary answers $0$ to every query. After $q<2^n$ queries some $a$ is unqueried; the zero circuit and the minterm circuit $[x=a]$ (size $\le 2n-1$, padded) agree on all answers but differ in CSAT. Randomised: Yao with $D_0$ = zero circuit versus $D_1$ = minterm at uniform $a$; $q$ queries see a $1$ with probability $\le q/2^n$, so error $\ge \tfrac12(1-q/2^n)$, which is $\le1/3$ only if $q\ge 2^n/3$.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Wire-reading bound, PROVED]\label{t:read}
In the model where the algorithm reads wires (``which literal feeds slot $j$?''), CSAT for circuits with $s\ge2$ gates needs $\ge s$ wire reads, even randomised $\Omega(s)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $C_0=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{s}\ell_j$ (a chain of $s-1$ ANDs plus slot structure, with literal-negation free on wires), $\ell_j=x_{a_j}$ all positive, every variable used appearing at least once. Replacing one slot $\ell_j$ by $\neg x_{a}$ where $x_a$ occurs in another slot makes the instance UNSAT, while $C_0$ is SAT. An algorithm that skips slot $j$ cannot distinguish. For randomised, the same sensitivity at a uniformly random slot gives $\Omega(s)$.
\end{proof}
Meaning: the white-box gap between $2^n$ (Thm.~\ref{t:bb}) and $s$ (Thm.~\ref{t:read}) is exactly the open question; any superlinear unconditional bound must exploit wire structure beyond reading it.

\section{Parametrised view}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Upper bound (KNOWN):} dynamic programming over a path decomposition of the wire graph of width $w$ decides CSAT in $2^{O(w)}\mathrm{poly}(s)$; so any lower bound must be $2^{\Omega(\cdot)}$ in a width-type parameter if it is to be tight, and trivially $\le 2^n$.
\item \textbf{Lower bound (CONDITIONAL):} under SETH one expects no $(2-\varepsilon)^{w}\mathrm{poly}(s)$ algorithm (known for CNF-SAT parametrised by primal pathwidth; transfer to circuit wire-width follows by CNF$\to$circuit with width $O(n)$; citation to be re-verified).
\item \textbf{Unconditional, restricted:} Thm.~\ref{t:bb}; time--space tradeoffs for SAT with alternation trading ($n^{2\cos(\pi/7)}\approx n^{1.80}$, Williams; Buss--Williams show this technique cannot go further, ledger row L13 in repo). TPORT/ONETAPE reports (\texttt{research/agents/claude/}) show analogous barriers for the portfolio and one-tape variants.
\end{itemize}
