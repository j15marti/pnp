\textbf{P versus NP remains OPEN.} Nothing here is an unconditional super-polynomial lower bound
for a white-box Circuit-SAT algorithm. Labels: PROVED, KNOWN, COMPUTATIONAL, CANDIDATE, OPEN, RETIRED.

\section{Model and ledger}
A circuit $C$ has $n$ inputs, $g$ gates of fan-in $\le 2$ (basis $\{\wedge,\vee\}$, free negation on wires),
fan-out unbounded, one output. $W$ = number of gate in-wires ($W\le 2g$). $\textsc{CSat}(n,g)$ = decide whether
some $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ has $C(x)=1$. Parameters: $(n,g,W)$; a lower bound may be parameterized, e.g.\ $T(n,g)$.

\section{Step 1: wire lower bounds for the construction (PROVED)}
\begin{lemma}[connectivity] If $C$ depends essentially on all $n$ inputs then $g\ge n-1$ and $W\ge 2n-2$.\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} Each input has a path to the output, so the underlying undirected graph on $n+g$ nodes is connected
(non-output gates not feeding the output are deleted), hence $W\ge n+g-1$. Also $W\le 2g$ gives $g\ge n-1$, so $W\ge 2n-2$.\end{proof}
Tight: the conjunction of $n$ literals uses exactly $n-1$ gates and $2n-2$ wires. It has a unique
satisfying assignment, so the unique-solution family is the cheapest to build.

\section{Step 2: lower bounds for solving}
\begin{theorem}[reading bound, PROVED] Any deterministic algorithm deciding \textsc{CSat} on wire-list encodings
must read $\Omega(W)$ wires, even on the family $\bigwedge_{i\le k}(\ell_i\wedge \ell_i')$ with $n=k$, $g=2k-1$.\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Instance $A$: $\ell_i'=\ell_i$ for all $i$ (SAT). Instance $B_j$: $\ell_j'=\neg\ell_j$ (UNSAT, only wire $j$ differs).
An algorithm that has left some pair $j$ unread answers identically on $A$ and $B_j$.\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[black-box evaluation queries, PROVED, weak model] An algorithm that only evaluates $C$ on chosen points needs
$2^n$ queries in the worst case, already on conjunctions of $n$ literals ($g=n-1$).\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Adversary answers $0$ to every query; the unqueried point could be the unique solution.\end{proof}
\textbf{Barrier (important).} Theorem 2 does \emph{not} bound white-box algorithms: conjunctions of literals are
solved in $O(n)$ time by reading the wires. It shows only that a lower bound must use circuit structure; it is the
relativization-type obstruction. KNOWN conditional: SETH implies no $2^{(1-\epsilon)n}\mathrm{poly}$ algorithm for CNF-SAT, hence
for \textsc{CSat}; this is conditional and not claimed here.
