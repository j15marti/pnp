\section{Goal and method}
Build circuits from the simplest case upward, tracking (i) the minimum number of gates $g$ and
wires $w$ needed at each stage and (ii) the lower bound on the work needed to decide satisfiability
as a function of the parameters $n$ (inputs) and $s$ (gates). Model: fan-in-2 gates over the full
basis $B_2$, unbounded fan-out; the wire count is $w=2g$. The input is the explicit
gate/wire table, accessed by cell probes. The lower bound is allowed to be a function $T(n,s)$.

\section{Proved structural lemmas}
\begin{lemma}[Connectivity bound; PROVED]\label{lem:conn}
A fan-in-2 circuit whose output depends on all $n$ inputs has $g\ge n-1$ gates and
$w\ge 2(n-1)$ wires. The bound is tight (balanced tree for $\mathrm{AND}_n$, $\mathrm{OR}_n$,
$\mathrm{XOR}_n$).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Restrict to the cone of the output. Every essential input has a path to the output, so the cone is
connected. It has $n+g$ nodes and $2g$ edges (every gate has two in-wires). A connected graph has
at least (nodes $-1$) edges, so $2g\ge n+g-1$, giving $g\ge n-1$.
\end{proof}
This matches Stage 2: $\mathrm{AND}_3$ has $g=2=n-1$.

\begin{proposition}[Linear probe lower bound in the gate count $s$; PROVED]\label{prop:probe}
Any deterministic algorithm that decides \SAT{} for circuits with $s$ gates, reading the gate-type
table by cell probes, needs at least $s$ probes in the worst case, already for $n=1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $U_s$ have input $x$, $n_x=\neg x$, $g_0=n_x\wedge x$, and $g_i=g_{i-1}\wedge x$ for
$1\le i\le s$, with output $g_s$. Then every $g_i\equiv 0$, so $U_s$ is unsatisfiable.
Let $U_s^{(i)}$ change only the type of $g_i$ from AND to OR. Then $g_i=0\vee x=x$, each later
gate is $x\wedge x=x$, and the output is $x$, so $U_s^{(i)}$ is satisfiable. Run the algorithm on
$U_s$. If it never probes the type cell of $g_i$, it behaves identically on $U_s^{(i)}$ and errs
on one of the two. Hence all $s$ type cells are probed.
\end{proof}
Verified by brute force for $s\le 40$: \texttt{experiments/claude\_circuitsat/adversary\_check.py}.

\begin{proposition}[Black-box bound in $n$; KNOWN/elementary]\label{prop:bb}
An algorithm that accesses $C$ only through evaluation queries needs $2^n-1$ queries to decide
satisfiability in the worst case.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Adversary: answer 0 to every query. Any unqueried point could be the unique satisfying
assignment, so one of the $2^n$ points is left unqueried unless all but one are queried. The
algorithm must query at least $2^n-1$ points to rule out satisfiability on the all-zero run.
\end{proof}

\section{Parameterized bound ledger}
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lll}\toprule
Statement & Model & Status\\\midrule
$T(n,s)\ge s$ & cell-probe on the circuit table & PROVED (Prop.~\ref{prop:probe})\\
$g\ge n-1$ to depend on $n$ inputs & fan-in 2 & PROVED (Lem.~\ref{lem:conn})\\
$T\ge 2^n-1$ & black-box evaluation only & KNOWN (Prop.~\ref{prop:bb})\\
$T(n,s)\ge s\cdot 2^{\epsilon n}$ for white-box deterministic & RAM / Turing & OPEN; would imply P$\ne$NP\\
SETH-style $T\ge 2^{(1-o(1))n}$ & white-box & CONDITIONAL (hypothesis, not a theorem)\\
Explicit circuit-size lower bound of about $3.1n$ over $B_2$ & general & KNOWN literature
(recalled from memory, not re-verified this run)\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}

The gap is the whole difficulty. Proposition~\ref{prop:probe} gives linear dependence on $s$ and
Proposition~\ref{prop:bb} gives exponential dependence on $n$ but only for black boxes. A white-box
lower bound must show that no algorithm can exploit the wiring to beat enumeration. The ladder
supplies exact minimum wire counts for small $n$ (the data above), but small-$n$ exact sizes do
not extrapolate to an asymptotic bound.
