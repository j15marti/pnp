\section{Proved baseline: linear in the wire count}
\begin{prop}[PROVED] For every $m$ there are circuits with one input, $\Theta(m)$ wires, such that every deterministic decider for Circuit-SAT reads $\Omega(m)$ wire descriptions, and every randomized decider with error $\le 1/3$ reads $\Omega(m)$ in expectation.\end{prop}
\emph{Proof.} Let $U$ be the OR of $k$ blocks $x\wedge\neg x$, so $U$ is unsatisfiable with $m=\Theta(k)$ wires. Let $U_i$ change the single wire $\neg x$ to $x$ in block $i$; then $U_i$ is satisfiable. A deterministic algorithm answering ``unsat'' on $U$ that does not read block $i$'s critical wire behaves identically on $U_i$, so it must read all $k$ critical wires. Randomized: take $U$ or $U_i$ ($i$ uniform) with probability $1/2$ each; an algorithm reading $<k/3$ wires finds $i$ with probability $<1/3$ and errs with probability $>1/3$ on the $U_i$ half (Yao). $\square$

Takeaway: the dependence on $m$ at fixed $n$ is tight up to the input-reading model ($O(m)$ upper bound at fixed $n$ by evaluating). The whole difficulty is the joint $(n,m)$ dependence: brute force is $O(2^n m)$.

\section{Parametrized targets}
\begin{conj}[OPEN] $T(n,m)\ge m\cdot h(n)$ with $h$ superpolynomially growing, for general-model Circuit-SAT.\end{conj}
Known context (not new here): CNF-SAT $\le$ Circuit-SAT preserves $(n,m)$ up to constants, so SETH gives CONDITIONAL $2^{(1-\epsilon)n}\mathrm{poly}(m)$ failure; unconditionally only the time--space tradeoffs of the Fortnow--Williams line are known (SAT time $n^{2\cos(\pi/7)}\approx n^{1.80}$ at $n^{o(1)}$ space; Buss--Williams show alternation trading stops there).
