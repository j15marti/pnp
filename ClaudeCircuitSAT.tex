\section{Setup and parameters}
A circuit has $n$ inputs, gates from $\{\wedge,\vee,\neg\}$ (fan-in $\le2$), and \emph{wire count} $w$ = total fan-in (inputs and constants free). Circuit-SAT: given $C$ (as a wiring list of $m$ wires), decide $\exists x\,C(x)=1$. Lower bounds are sought as functions of $(n,m)$ --- a parametrized bound --- in two models: (R) \emph{wiring-probe}: the algorithm reads wiring entries; (B) \emph{black-box}: only evaluates $C$.

\section{Step 0--1: $n=1$}
All 4 functions of one input: constants and $x$ cost $0$ wires; $\neg x$ costs $1$. 
\begin{prop}[R-bound, PROVED/EXACT] For $n=1$ and every $m$, any (even randomized, zero-error) algorithm for Circuit-SAT needs $\Omega(m)$ wiring probes. 
\end{prop}
\emph{Proof.} Family: $C_j$ = $\bigwedge_{i=1}^m \ell_i$, a chain of $m-1$ AND gates whose $m$ literal slots are $x$, except slot $j$ is $\neg x$ (via one NOT gate) or all slots are $x$. The all-$x$ circuit is SAT; $C_j$ is UNSAT. A deterministic algorithm that misses slot $j$ sees the same answers as on the all-$x$ circuit, so it must probe all slots; randomized: probes $<m/2$ slots err w.p.\ $>1/2$ on a random $j$. $\square$

So with one input the bound is already linear in wires: SAT hardness in model (R) comes from \emph{reading}, not from $n$.

\section{Step 2: monotone growth of wire cost in $n$}
\begin{prop}[PROVED/EXACT] A function depending on $k$ inputs needs $\ge k-1$ binary gates, hence $\ge 2(k-1)$ wires. 
\end{prop}
\emph{Proof.} The gate DAG feeding the output must connect to all $k$ essential inputs; a DAG with $k$ source inputs reaching one sink through fan-in-2 gates has $\ge k-1$ gates. $\square$

\section{Black-box side (model B)}
\begin{prop}[KNOWN/PROVED] In model (B), the family $C_a=[x=a]$, $a\in\{0,1\}^n$ plus the all-zero-output circuit forces $2^n$ evaluations for any deterministic algorithm (adversary answers 0). 
\end{prop}
Combined parametrized statement (COMPUTATIONAL-free, elementary): $T_{\text{probe}}\ge \Omega(m)$ and $T_{\text{blackbox}}\ge 2^n$; a general algorithm may mix both, and the gap between $\max(m,\cdot)$ and $2^n$ is exactly the open region --- bounds there imply superpolynomial separations (cf.\ ETH/SETH-type conditional statements).
