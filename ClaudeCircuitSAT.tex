\section{Goal and plan}
Study $\textsc{CSAT}(n,s)$: given a fan-in-2 circuit with $n$ inputs and $s$ gates, is it satisfiable?
Ladder: (i) fix the simplest circuits, (ii) record the exact minimum gates/wires needed to build each function
as $n$ grows, (iii) ask which of these building costs transfer to a decision lower bound, with the bound allowed
to be parametrized in $(n,s)$ (and later depth, fan-out, treewidth).

\section{Building ladder: exact minimum construction cost}
Model: gates are arbitrary binary Boolean functions, fan-in 2, inputs/negations free. Wires $=2\cdot$gates (fan-in 2 each,
counting each gate's two incoming edges).
\begin{lemma}[connectivity, PROVED/EXACT]\label{l:conn}
A circuit depending on all $n$ inputs has at least $n-1$ gates, hence at least $2(n-1)$ wires; AND$_n$ attains it.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} Each gate merges at most two connected components of the underlying graph ($n$ input components initially);
the output must lie in the single component containing all inputs, so $\ge n-1$ gates. A balanced AND tree attains it.\end{proof}

\begin{thm}[COMPUTATIONAL, exhaustive search \texttt{research/agents/claude/circuitsat/minsize.py}]\label{t:tab}
Number of functions, up to output negation, whose minimum circuit has exactly $g$ gates:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lccccc}\toprule
$n$ & $g=0$ & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4\\\midrule
2 & 3 & 5 & -- & -- & --\\
3 & 4 & 15 & 57 & 40 & 12\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
So $\max_f C(f)=1$ for $n=2$ and $4$ for $n=3$ (matches the known values; counts sum to $2^{2^n-1}$: 8 for $n=2$, 128 for $n=3$).
Hence minimum wires $2,\dots,8$ for $n=3$.
\end{thm}
Interpretation: the hardest 3-input function costs 4 gates (8 wires) versus the connectivity floor $2$ gates (4 wires); the
gap between Lemma~\ref{l:conn} and the true cost already appears at $n=3$ (e.g.\ parity-type functions). Runtime note: $n=3$,
depth 4 took 57\,s in one invocation, above the 30\,s rule; for $n=4$ the search must be chunked by first-gate choice
(next-step task) or replaced by SAT-based synthesis.

\section{From building cost to a decision bound}
\begin{lemma}[linear read bound, KNOWN/easy, PROVED/EXACT]\label{l:read}
Any algorithm deciding $\textsc{CSAT}$ on circuits with $s$ gates given as an adjacency oracle makes $\Omega(s)$ queries.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} Let $C_0=\bigvee_{i\le s/3}(x_1\wedge\neg x_1)$ (unsatisfiable). Changing any single block to $x_1\wedge x_2$ gives a satisfiable circuit,
so an adversary forces a query to every block.\end{proof}
Parametrized picture:
\begin{itemize}
\item Unconditional: $T(n,s)\ge \Omega(s)$ (Lemma~\ref{l:read}); $T\ge\Omega(n)$ to see all relevant inputs. KNOWN. 
\item Upper bound: $O(2^n s)$ by enumeration; $\mathrm{poly}$ for fixed treewidth $w$ ($2^{O(w)}s$). KNOWN.
\item Conditional: ETH (via CNF$\to$circuit) gives $2^{\Omega(n)}$ for circuits with $s=O(n)$; SETH gives $2^{(1-\epsilon)n}$ barrier for CNF
inputs. CONDITIONAL, not a separation.
\item Gap: nothing unconditional between $\Omega(s)$ and $2^n s$ for general algorithms. OPEN.
\end{itemize}
Repository link: the P-vs-NP corpus (AGENTS.md ledger rows L34--L37, \texttt{research/agents/claude/tport/}) shows alternation-trading
proofs cannot beat linear time for UWI-sized portfolios; this explains why time-space methods stall near $\Omega(s)$ for
CSAT-like portfolios and is the closest existing barrier to this ladder. Not used as a theorem here.
