\section{Goal and model}
Goal: lower bounds for CircuitSAT (given a circuit $C$ on $n$ inputs of size $s$, is there $x$ with $C(x)=1$?).
Idea: climb a ladder, starting with the simplest circuits and adding inputs one at a time, recording for each $n$
the exact minimum number of gates and wires needed, then look for structure that extrapolates.
Model: $B_2$ circuits (fan-in 2, any binary gate, negation free, unbounded fan-out); $G(f)$ = minimum gates,
and wires $W(f)=2G(f)$ (gate-input wires; fan-out edges counted per use). Parameters: $n$ inputs, $s$ size,
and possible structural parameters $k$ (treewidth/pathwidth of the circuit graph, depth, number of essential inputs).

\section{Ladder, exact data (COMPUTATIONAL)}
Script \texttt{research/agents/claude/circuitsat/exact\_small.py} exhaustively searches all circuits level by level
(raw data \texttt{exact\_small\_results.json}; runs in a few seconds). Number of functions needing exactly $g$ gates:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lccccc}\toprule
$n$ & $g=0$ & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4\\\midrule
1 & 4 & & & & \\ 2 & 6 & 10 & & & \\ 3 & 8 & 30 & 114 & 80 & 24\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
Hence $L(n)=\max_f G(f)$ is $0,1,4$ for $n=1,2,3$ (wires $0,2,8$). (KNOWN: $L(4)=7$ by Knuth's exhaustive tables;
not recomputed here.) The totals $4,16,256$ match $2^{2^n}$, a sanity check.

\section{Proved lemmas of the ladder}
\begin{lemma}[connectivity, PROVED]\label{l:ess}
If $f$ depends on $e$ inputs then $G(f)\ge e-1$, so $W(f)\ge 2(e-1)$.
\end{lemma}
\emph{Proof.} The circuit restricted to the cone of the output is a connected DAG with fan-in $\le2$ whose leaves include
all $e$ essential inputs; a binary tree/DAG with $e$ distinct leaves has at least $e-1$ internal nodes. \qed

\begin{prop}[unique-SAT instances are cheap to write down, PROVED]
The minterm $[x=a]$ and parity on $n$ inputs both have $G=n-1$ exactly.
\end{prop}
\emph{Proof.} Upper bound: a balanced tree of $n-1$ gates (AND with free negations; XOR). Lower bound: Lemma~\ref{l:ess}. \qed

\noindent Consequence: the \emph{instances} of the hardest black-box CircuitSAT problem (unique satisfying assignment) have
linear size, so any lower bound must come from the cost of \emph{solving}, not from instance size. Data point: at $n=3$
the 24 functions needing 4 gates (e.g.\ majority-type) are the first to exceed $n-1=2$ gates.

\section{Lower bounds on solving CircuitSAT}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Black-box (query) model, KNOWN/PROVED.} Any algorithm that only evaluates $C$ must make $2^n$ queries in the worst case
(adversary answers 0 until one point is left). Quantum: $\Omega(2^{n/2})$ (BBBV). Not a white-box bound, since the algorithm sees $C$.
\item \textbf{Conditional.} SETH $\Rightarrow$ no $(2-\epsilon)^n\mathrm{poly}$ algorithm for CNF-SAT (KNOWN definition; CONDITIONAL).
Parameterised by pathwidth $k$ of the instance, SAT is $2^k\mathrm{poly}$ and SETH-based tightness results are in the literature (recalled).
\item \textbf{Circuit-size lower bounds for explicit functions, KNOWN.} Best $B_2$ bound is about $3.1n$ (Li--Yang, STOC 2022; Blum $3n-o(n)$).
These bound the size of circuits \emph{computing} a function, which is what a CircuitSAT lower bound for a fixed circuit family would need,
but they are linear, far from superpolynomial.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Candidate parameterised target (CANDIDATE)}
Let $T(n,s)$ be the best worst-case time for CircuitSAT on $n$ inputs and size $s$. Targets in increasing strength:
(a) $T\ge 2^{\Omega(n)}$ for $s=\mathrm{poly}(n)$ (implies $\mathrm{P}\ne\mathrm{NP}$, OPEN);
(b) $T\ge (2-\epsilon)^{n}$ for $s=O(n)$ (SETH-like, CONDITIONAL);
(c) restricted-model versions: monotone, bounded-depth, bounded-treewidth, read-once, where unconditional bounds may be reachable.
Next ladder steps: compute exact $G$ for $n=4$ with canonical-form pruning; tabulate depth and treewidth of optimal circuits;
find a quantity (e.g.\ number of distinct sub-functions, cofactor rank) that matches $L(n)$ for $n\le4$ and test whether it
yields a provable restricted bound.
