\paragraph{Status.} \textbf{P versus NP remains OPEN.} No super-linear unconditional lower bound
for Circuit-SAT is claimed. Labels: PROVED, COMPUTATIONAL, KNOWN, CANDIDATE, OPEN.

\section{Model and program}
A circuit $C$ on $n$ inputs has $s$ gates of fan-in $2$ over $\{\wedge,\vee\}$ with free negation on wires
(so $2s$ wires of gate input, plus the output). Circuit-SAT: given $C$, decide $\exists x\,C(x)=1$.
Plan: (i) exact minimum gate/wire counts for small $n$ (the \emph{construction cost});
(ii) lower bounds on \emph{solving} as a function of the parameters $(n,s)$, e.g.\ $T(n,s)$;
(iii) look for parametrized bounds of the form $f(n)\cdot s$ or $s^{g(n)}$.
Known upper bound: $O(2^n s)$ by exhaustion; ETH/SETH-style lower bounds are conditional (KNOWN, not proved here).

Results: unique-solution functions ($|f^{-1}(1)|=1$) need $n-1$ gates ($n=2,3$: 1, 2);
parity needs $3(n-1)$ gates ($n=2,3$: 3, 6; matches the KNOWN Schnorr bound for this basis); the maximum over $n=3$ is $6$.
(Complement-closed counts: each function and its negation have equal cost.)
Gate $\times$ wire accounting: a size-$g$ circuit has $2g$ input wires; the connectivity bound below shows $g\ge n-1$ is forced.

\begin{proposition}[PROVED, connectivity] A fan-in-2 circuit depending on all $n$ inputs has $\ge n-1$ gates
(the underlying DAG restricted to the output cone is connected, an $n$-leaf binary-in-degree structure needs $n-1$ merges).
\end{proposition}
Tight for the unique-solution (AND of literals) family.

\section{Solving cost: an unconditional parametrized bound (PROVED, trivial regime)}
Model: deterministic algorithm with random access to the encoding of $C$ (gate $j$'s two predecessors and polarities),
cost = number of gate descriptions probed.
\begin{proposition}\label{p:read}
For every $s\ge2$, even with $n=1$, any exact Circuit-SAT algorithm probes $\ge s-1$ gate descriptions in the worst case.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} $I_0$: $g_1=x$, $g_j=g_{j-1}\wedge x$ ($j\ge2$); satisfiable ($x=1$). $I_i$ ($2\le i\le s$): same except
$g_i=g_{i-1}\wedge\neg x$; then $g_i\equiv0$ and all later gates are $0$, unsatisfiable. If the algorithm on $I_0$ does not probe gate $i$ it
answers identically on $I_i$, contradiction. \end{proof}
Hence $T(n,s)=\Omega(s)$ for all $n$; combined with $O(2^ns)$, the open question is the exact $n$-dependence:
is $T(n,s)\ge s\cdot 2^{\epsilon n}$ for $s=\mathrm{poly}(n)$? OPEN (that is the substantive statement; implies $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$ only for $s$ polynomially large with $n$ — and would be SETH-flavoured).

\section{Barriers recorded so far}
Circuit-size lower bounds for explicit functions known: $3.1n-o(n)$ (Li--Yang, KNOWN, from literature; not rechecked this run) in full binary basis.
Any argument via counting alone gives only hardness of \emph{some} function, not Circuit-SAT as an algorithmic problem; relativization/natural proofs
barriers apply to circuit-size methods. Gate elimination yields linear bounds only.
Connection to the repository (\texttt{AGENTS.md}): the world ledger rows L13, L34--L40 show alternation-trading/time--space and one-tape routes stall

(CANDIDATE: transfer of the portfolio bounds to the $2^{\epsilon n}$ question; no theorem yet).
