\textbf{P versus NP remains OPEN.} No superpolynomial lower bound for \CSAT{} is
claimed. This document builds a ladder of \emph{exact} small-case facts and
\emph{proved} elementary bounds, and tracks which parametrized bounds are
unconditional, conditional, or open. Status labels: PROVED/EXACT, KNOWN THEOREM,
COMPUTATIONAL, CONDITIONAL, CANDIDATE, OPEN, FALSIFIED/RETIRED.

\section{Setup and parameters}
\begin{definition}[Circuit, wires]
A circuit $C$ on $n$ inputs is a DAG of $g$ fan-in-2 gates over the full binary basis
$B_2$ (negation free), one designated output. Its \emph{wire count} is $w(C)=2g$
(gate-input edges). $\CSAT$: given $C$, is there $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ with $C(x)=1$?
\end{definition}
Two quantities are tracked at each rung $(n,g)$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{construction cost} $w^*(f)=2\cdot\min\{g: \exists C,\ C\equiv f\}$, the minimal wires to
 build a function $f$ (the \emph{wire-by-wire} ladder);
\item \textbf{solving cost} $T(n,w)$: worst-case time to decide \CSAT{} on circuits with $n$ inputs and $w$ wires.
\end{itemize}
Natural parameters: $n$ (inputs), $w$ (wires), $k=w-n$ or $w/n$ (density),
treewidth/pathwidth of $C$, number of unsatisfying-to-satisfying "switch" wires.
A parametrized bound has the form $T(n,w)\ge F(n,w)$ on a hard family.

\section{Proved elementary bounds}
\begin{lemma}[Construction floor; PROVED/EXACT]\label{lem:floor}
If $C$ depends essentially on all $n$ inputs then $g\ge n-1$, so $w\ge 2(n-1)$.
Hence any circuit with exactly one satisfying assignment has $w\ge 2(n-1)$, with equality
attained by the AND tree of literals (matches the table for $n=2,3$).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every input must have a path to the output. A DAG with $n$ sources feeding one sink via
fan-in-2 gates has at least $n-1$ gates (each gate merges at most two components of the
"reachability forest"; inductively, $n$ components need $n-1$ merges). A function with a
unique satisfying point $a$ depends on every variable (flip $a_i$). Equality: $\bigwedge_i \ell_i$. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Description-reading bound; PROVED/EXACT, trivial]\label{prop:read}
Any correct \CSAT{} algorithm that accesses the circuit through a probe model
(reading gate descriptions) must probe $\Omega(w)$ descriptions in the worst case.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Chain $g_0=x_1$, $g_i=g_{i-1}\wedge x_1$ ($1\le i\le m$), output $x_1\wedge\neg g_m$.
Then $g_m\equiv x_1$ and the circuit is unsatisfiable. Rewire one input of a single gate $g_j$
from $x_1$ to $x_2$ ($g_j=g_{j-1}\wedge x_2$): then $g_m\equiv x_1\wedge x_2$ and
$x=(1,0)$ satisfies. The $m$ modified instances differ from the base instance only in one
gate; an algorithm that never probes gate $j$ answers identically on both. \qed
\end{proof}
This is the unavoidable "read the whole circuit" floor; it is the first rung of the
parametrized ladder $T(n,w)\ge w$.

\section{Barriers to keep in view}
Natural proofs (Razborov--Rudich), relativization, algebrization. Circuit-lower-bound
\emph{construction} counts (Lemma~\ref{lem:floor}) concern $w^*(f)$ of explicit functions;
a lower bound on \emph{solving} cost needs a different argument. Known explicit circuit lower
bounds over $B_2$ are only $\approx 3.1n$ gates, so wire-counting alone cannot reach superpolynomial.
