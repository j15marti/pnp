\section{Goal and method}
Instance: a fan-in-2 circuit $C$ with $n$ inputs and $m$ gates (wires $w=2m+$outputs). Question: $\exists x\,C(x)=1$.
Plan: climb a ladder of families $\mathcal F_{n,m}$, tracking (i) the minimum number of gates/wires any circuit in the family needs, (ii) the cost of \emph{deciding} satisfiability of the family, as functions of the parameters $(n,m)$. Two separate quantities must not be conflated: \emph{circuit size} lower bounds (gate elimination, about $c\cdot n$) and \emph{decision time} lower bounds for \CSAT\ (OPEN beyond reading the input, apart from restricted time--space models).

\section{Rung 0: wire accounting (PROVED)}
\begin{lemma}[connectivity] A fan-in-2 circuit depending on all $n$ inputs has $\ge n-1$ gates and $\ge 2(n-1)$ input wires into gates; $\mathrm{AND}_n$ and $\mathrm{XOR}_n$ attain $n-1$ (basis $B_2$). \end{lemma}
\emph{Proof.} The circuit graph restricted to the output cone is connected with $n$ sources and one sink; a fan-in-2 DAG tree-like cover needs $n-1$ merges. Balanced trees attain it. \qed

\begin{lemma}[read every wire] Any deterministic algorithm deciding \CSAT\ on $m$-gate circuits, given random access to the gate list, makes $\ge m$ probes on some input. \end{lemma}
\emph{Proof.} Let $F$: $g_j=g_{j-1}\wedge y_j$ for $j=1..m$ ($g_0=y_0$), output $g_m$; satisfiable. Let $G_j$ replace gate $j$ by $g_j=g_{j-1}\wedge\neg g_{j-1}$; output constant $0$, unsatisfiable, and $G_j$ differs from $F$ only in gate $j$'s description. An algorithm on $F$ that skips gate $j$'s description answers identically on $G_j$, so it errs. Hence all $m$ gates are probed on $F$. \qed

So the trivial parametrized bound is $\Omega(m)$, with $n$ irrelevant. This is the baseline of the ladder.

\section{Rung 1: exact minimum circuits, small $n$ (COMPUTATIONAL)}
Script \texttt{research/agents/claude/circuitsat/mincircuit.py} runs BFS over sets of computed truth tables in $B_2$ (free negation) and reports the exact minimum gate count of every $n$-variable function. Outputs (\texttt{ladder\_n2.txt}, \texttt{ladder\_n3.txt}, each $<$30\,s, no chunking needed):

All $2^{2^n}$ functions are covered ($16$, $256$). Hardest 3-variable functions need $4$ gates; hardest 2-variable need 1. The BFS state count grows $\approx 26\times$ per level ($30,843,21778,569780$ for $s=1..4$ at $n=3$), so $n=4$ needs a smarter method (SAT-based exact synthesis, canonical forms); that is the next rung (CANDIDATE).

\section{Rung 2: counting and parametrized shape (KNOWN / PROVED)}
The number of circuits with $s$ gates over $n$ inputs is $\le (16\,(s+n)^2)^s$, so almost every $f$ needs $s=\Omega(2^n/n)$ (Shannon). For \CSAT\ the relevant consequence: in the \emph{evaluation-only} (black-box) model, deciding $\exists x\,C(x)$ takes $2^n$ queries in the worst case (adversary answers $0$ until the last point). This is not a bound for algorithms that read $C$, and relativization-type barriers apply to promoting it.

Known unconditional circuit-size milestones (KNOWN, cited from literature, not re-proved here): $3n-o(n)$ for explicit functions in $B_2$ (Blum 1984), improved to $3.1n-o(n)$ (Li--Yang, STOC 2022).
Known time--space decision bounds (KNOWN): SAT needs time $n^{2\cos(\pi/7)-o(1)}\approx n^{1.80}$ on random-access machines with $n^{o(1)}$ space (Williams 2008; Buss--Williams: alternation trading cannot exceed that exponent).

\section{Rung 3: parametrized lower-bound ledger}
Write $T(n,m)$ for the decision time of \CSAT. 
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}\toprule
Bound & Status & Model\\\midrule
$T=\Omega(m)$ & PROVED (Lemma 2) & RAM, probe model\\
$T=\Omega(2^n)$ & PROVED & black-box evaluation only\\
$T\ge m^{1.80}$ (when $n\approx m$) & KNOWN & space $m^{o(1)}$\\
$T\ge 2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}\mathrm{poly}(m)$ for all $\varepsilon$ & CONDITIONAL & under SETH\\
$T\ge 2^{o(n)}$ impossible & CONDITIONAL & under ETH\\
$T\ge m^{1+\delta}$, general RAM & OPEN & --\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
The parametrized target: find $g$ with $T(n,m)\ge g(n,m)$ where $g$ grows with $n$ at fixed excess $m-n$ (bounded ``co-size''). Fixed-parameter view: with $k=m-n$ fixed, \CSAT\ may be easier (e.g.\ $2^{O(k)}\mathrm{poly}$-type algorithms for restricted structure), so the interesting regime is $m=n\cdot\mathrm{poly}$.

\section{Link to repository work (j15marti/pnp)}
Relevant: \texttt{research/agents/claude/world-ledger/} row L13 (alternation-trading barrier) bounds the time--space route; TPORT (L34--L37) shows portfolio versions do not beat linear time by alternation trading at the UWI size $\approx N$; ONETAPE (L38--L40) shows crossing-sequence bounds hold for nondeterminism too and so cannot separate P from NP. Takeaway for this ladder: Rung 2's time--space bound is the only unconditional super-linear statement available, and it is capped at exponent $\approx1.80$ for known techniques.

\section{Next steps (CANDIDATE)}

\end{enumerate}
