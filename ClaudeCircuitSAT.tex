\section{Model and parameters}
A circuit $C$ has $n$ inputs, $s$ gates of fan-in 2 (basis $B_2$: all 16 binary Boolean functions,
free negation of outputs), hence exactly $w=2s$ gate-input wires (plus one output wire).
\CSAT: given $C$, decide $\exists x\,C(x)=1$.
Parameters tracked per rung: $n$ (inputs), $s$ or $w$ (gates/wires), $\mathrm{tw}$ (treewidth of the underlying graph).
A \emph{parametrized} bound has the form $T(n,s,\mathrm{tw})$; the classical statements are
$T=O(2^n s)$ and $T=2^{O(\mathrm{tw})}s$ (KNOWN).

\section{Rung ladder: minimal wires needed per step (COMPUTATIONAL, exact)}
Let $C(f)$ be the minimum number of $B_2$ gates computing $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$ (inputs and negations free).
Exhaustive layered search over sets of computed functions (\texttt{synth.py}, finishes in about 10\,s for $n=3$)
gives:

\begin{tabular}{c|ccccc|c|c}
$n$ & \#$f$ with $C=0$ & $C=1$ & $C=2$ & $C=3$ & $C=4$ & $\max C$ & $C(\mathrm{AND}_n)$\\\hline
1 & 4 & -- & -- & -- & -- & 0 & 0\\
2 & 6 & 10 & -- & -- & -- & 1 & 1\\
3 & 8 & 30 & 114 & 80 & 24 & 4 & 2

Counts sum to $2^{2^n}$ (4, 16, 256). Wires $=2C$. So the cheapest circuit realising any $n=3$ function uses at most 8 wires,
and the 24 hardest functions need exactly 4 gates / 8 wires. The first \emph{non-trivial} satisfiability instance
(a function with a unique satisfying input) appears at rung $n=2$ with 1 gate, 2 wires.

\section{Lemmas valid for all $n$}
\begin{lemma}[Connectivity, PROVED/EXACT]\label{lem:conn}
If $f$ depends on all $n$ inputs, any fan-in-2 circuit for $f$ has $s\ge n-1$ gates and $w\ge 2(n-1)$ wires.
$\mathrm{AND}_n$ (one satisfying input) meets this with equality.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every input must have a directed path to the output, so the underlying undirected graph on inputs and gates is connected
on $n+s$ nodes with $2s$ gate-input edges; connectivity needs $2s\ge n+s-1$, i.e.\ $s\ge n-1$. A balanced tree of ANDs attains it.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Description-probe bound, PROVED/EXACT]\label{lem:probe}
Fix $s$. Any deterministic algorithm that decides \CSAT\ on $s$-gate circuits, accessing the circuit only by probing
gate records (type and two sources) at chosen indices, must probe at least $s$ records on some instance.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Take the chain $g_i=\mathrm{AND}(g_{i-1},x_{i \bmod n})$, satisfiable by $x=1^n$. If the algorithm leaves record $j$
unprobed, answer all probes as in the chain; the instance $I'$ where only gate $j$ is replaced by the constant-0 gate
gives identical answers but is unsatisfiable. So the algorithm errs on one of them unless it probes all $s$ records.
\end{proof}
Consequence: the wire count is a genuine lower bound in the probe model, $T=\Omega(w)$ up to the record size
($\Omega(w/\log w)$ bit probes). This is the formal content of ``every wire must be tracked''.

\begin{lemma}[Black-box evaluation bound, PROVED/EXACT but \emph{model-restricted}]\label{lem:bb}
If $C$ is accessible only as an evaluation oracle $x\mapsto C(x)$, deciding satisfiability needs $2^n$ queries in the worst case.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Adversary answers $0$ to every query; after $2^n-1$ queries some input remains untested, and the oracle could accept exactly that input.
\end{proof}
\textbf{Caution (FALSIFIED as a route):} Lemma~\ref{lem:bb} says nothing about the real problem, because the real
algorithm sees the description, and structured descriptions are solved faster (treewidth DP, below).
The gap between $2^n$ (oracle) and $s$ (probe) is exactly the open region.

\section{Known parametrized landscape}
\begin{itemize}
\item KNOWN THEOREM: $\CSAT\in \mathrm{TIME}(2^n\cdot s)$; and $2^{O(\mathrm{tw})}\cdot s$ by dynamic programming on a tree decomposition.
\item CONDITIONAL (SETH): no $(2-\varepsilon)^n\mathrm{poly}(s)$ algorithm, since CNF-SAT embeds in \CSAT\ with the same $n$.
Likewise no $(2-\varepsilon)^{\mathrm{pw}}\mathrm{poly}(s)$ under SETH (known for CNF-SAT parametrized by primal pathwidth).
\item KNOWN THEOREM (transferred): Williams-type time--space bounds. \CSAT\ and 3-SAT are interreducible in linear size
via Tseitin (computable in logarithmic space), so with $N=\Theta(s)$ the bound $T\ge N^{2\cos(\pi/7)-o(1)}\approx N^{1.80}$
for algorithms with $N^{o(1)}$ space applies to \CSAT\ (reduction-transfer argument, to be re-verified in a later run with
exact encodings). The repository row L13 (Buss--Williams) says alternation trading cannot push this exponent further.
\item Gate-elimination gives circuit \emph{size} lower bounds for explicit functions ($3.1n-o(n)$ in $B_2$, Find--Golovnev--Hirsch--Kulikov; $5n-o(n)$ for $U_2$)
but these bound the circuit, not the time of a \CSAT\ algorithm; the ladder in Section~2 is the exact, small-$n$ analogue.
\end{itemize}
