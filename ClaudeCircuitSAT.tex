\section{Goal and method}
Circuit-SAT$(n,s)$: given a Boolean circuit over basis $B_2$ with $n$ inputs and $s$ gates
(wires $w=2s$ counting fan-in), decide whether some $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ makes it output 1.
Plan: (i) for each $n$, tabulate the minimum wires $W_n(f)$ needed to build $f$ (the ``building ledger'');
(ii) turn each ledger level into a lower bound on deciding SAT for circuits of that size;
(iii) look for a bound that is a function of the pair $(n,s)$, i.e.\ parametrized.

\section{Lower bounds that are actually proved}
\begin{prop}[connectivity; PROVED, folklore]
If $f$ depends on all $n$ inputs then every $B_2$ circuit for $f$ has $\ge n-1$ gates, hence $\ge 2(n-1)$ wires.
Tight for $\mathrm{AND}_n,\mathrm{XOR}_n$ (matches the $n=3$ computation).
\end{prop}
\begin{prop}[reading bound; PROVED]
Any deterministic decider for Circuit-SAT that accesses the circuit description by probes needs $\Omega(s)$ probes.
\end{prop}
\emph{Proof.} Take the chain $g_i=g_{i-1}\wedge x_i$, output $g_n$ (satisfiable). Replacing any one gate by
$x_i\wedge\neg x_i$ makes it unsatisfiable, so an algorithm skipping a gate description errs on one of the two instances. \qed
\begin{prop}[black-box bound; PROVED, model-limited]
If the circuit is accessible only by evaluation queries, deciding SAT needs $2^n$ queries (adversary answers 0 until one point remains).
\end{prop}
These are trivial: $\Omega(s)$ and $2^n$ in incomparable models. Closing the gap, i.e.\ a bound like $2^{\epsilon n}$ for
algorithms that may \emph{inspect} the circuit, is the open content of P vs NP.

\section{Parametrized targets}
\begin{conj}[SETH-type; CONDITIONAL, not proved]
For every $\epsilon>0$ no algorithm solves Circuit-SAT$(n,s)$ in time $2^{(1-\epsilon)n}\mathrm{poly}(s)$.
\end{conj}
Unconditional parametrized statements to pursue (all OPEN): (a) time $\ge s^{1+\delta}$ for $n=\omega(\log s)$;
(b) restricted wire models, e.g.\ fan-out~1 (formulas), bounded depth, bounded treewidth of the wire graph,
where Circuit-SAT is FPT in treewidth ($2^{O(\mathrm{tw})}\mathrm{poly}(s)$), so any lower bound must be in
terms of $\mathrm{tw}$ versus $n$; (c) a wire-by-wire potential: track the minimum cut of the wire graph per ledger level.
\paragraph{Repository link.} The project's TPORT/ONETAPE notes
(\texttt{research/agents/claude/2026-10-28\_1326\_CLAUDE\_TPORT-0001.tex}) show alternation-trading style proofs
hit barriers near linear time with random access; the Buss--Williams result limits time--space routes. These bear on (a)
and suggest the wire-cut potential (c) as the independent direction.
