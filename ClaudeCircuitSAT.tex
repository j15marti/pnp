\section{Setup and parameters}
Circuit $C$: $n$ inputs, $m$ fan-in-2 gates over all 16 binary functions (basis $B_2$), $w=2m$ wires, one output.
Circuit-SAT: given $C$, decide $\exists x\,C(x)=1$. Parameters: $(n,m)$, with size $s=n+m$.
Two access models, which give different lower bounds:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Black-box}: only evaluations $C(x)$.
\item \emph{Wire-list}: the algorithm probes the connection list (``gate $g$, input $a$ is wired to $h$'').
\end{itemize}

\section{The ladder (step-by-step construction)}

\section{Proved lower bounds}
\begin{lem}[Connectivity, PROVED/EXACT]\label{lem:conn}
If $f$ depends on all $n$ inputs then $c(f)\ge n-1$ (so $\ge 2(n-1)$ wires).
\end{lem}
\begin{proof} Take the undirected graph on inputs and gates. Each gate has two incoming wires, so adding gates one by one in topological order merges at most two of the current connected components of the input-set into one. The output must be connected to every input it depends on, so $n$ components need $\ge n-1$ merges.\end{proof}
Tight: an AND tree of literals has $n-1$ gates and a unique satisfying assignment. This matches the computed $n=2,3$ rows.

\begin{prop}[Wire-list reading bound, PROVED/EXACT, trivial]\label{prop:read}
Any deterministic Circuit-SAT algorithm in the wire-list model makes $\ge m$ probes (in the worst case), even for $n=1$.
\end{prop}
\begin{proof} Gates $z_0\equiv0$, $z_1\equiv1$; $g_i=\mathrm{OR}(g_{i-1},z_0)$ for $i=1..m-2$, output $g_{m-2}$ ($g_0=z_0$). This $C_0$ is unsatisfiable. If a correct algorithm leaves the wire ``gate $j$, second input'' unprobed, rewiring it to $z_1$ gives $C_j$ with output constantly 1, satisfiable, yet the algorithm's view is identical. So every such wire must be probed: $\Omega(m)$.\end{proof}

\begin{prop}[Black-box bound, KNOWN, folklore]\label{prop:bb}
Black-box Circuit-SAT needs $2^n$ queries worst case (planted unique solution: AND tree $\wedge$ literal pattern $a$; adversary answers 0 until one point is left). By Lemma~\ref{lem:conn} the planted family has size only $\Theta(n)$, so the $2^n$ bound is a statement about access, not about computing with the wiring.
\end{prop}

\section{Parametrized picture and the gap}
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lll}\toprule
Model & Lower bound & Status\\\midrule
black-box, $n$ inputs & $2^n$ queries & KNOWN\\
wire-list, $m$ gates & $\Omega(m)$ probes & PROVED (trivial)\\
wire-list, general RAM/TM & $\omega(m)$ or $2^{\epsilon n}$ unconditional & OPEN\\
conditional (ETH/SETH-type) & $2^{(1-o(1))n}$ & CONDITIONAL\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
Key observation (round 1): the minimal-wire ladder alone cannot yield hardness, since the tight families (AND trees, unique solution) are solved in $O(m)$ by propagating from the output. Any hardness must come from wiring structure that defeats propagation (reconvergent fan-out, XOR-rich parts). The ladder should therefore track for each $(n,m)$ the \emph{hardest} circuits for a fixed propagation/DPLL-type measure, which is a natural parameter: e.g.\ cutwidth/treewidth $\tau$ of the circuit graph. KNOWN (not re-proved here): Circuit-SAT is solvable in $2^{O(\tau)}\,m$ time, so a parametrized lower bound must come with large $\tau$; $\tau\le n+O(1)$ is not forced.
