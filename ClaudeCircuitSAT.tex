\section{Setting and parameters}
An instance is a circuit $C$ over inputs $x_1..x_n$ with $g$ binary gates (negation free), $m=2g$ wires
(fan-in 2). Question: $\exists a\ C(a)=1$? Parameters: $n$ (inputs), $g$ or $m$ (size), and further
parameters to be introduced (fan-out profile, depth, treewidth of the wire graph, ``contradiction depth'').
Two different quantities must not be confused:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Expression cost}: minimum wires needed to \emph{write down} an instance with a given property
(a circuit-size quantity; tracked step by step below).
\item \emph{Solving cost}: time/probes needed to \emph{decide} instances. A lower bound on expression cost
is \textbf{not} a lower bound on solving cost; it only calibrates how large the hard instances must be.
\end{itemize}

\section{Step-by-step ledger of minimal wire counts}
Let $U(n)$ = min gates of a circuit in which every input reaches the output and which is UNSAT;
$S_1(n)$ = same connectivity, exactly one satisfying assignment.
\begin{lem}[PROVED/EXACT] $U(n)=n$ for $n\ge1$ (so $2n$ wires), and $S_1(n)=n-1$ for $n\ge2$.\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
If all $n$ inputs reach the output, the wire graph has $\ge n-1$ gates, with equality iff it is a tree in which
every input has fan-out one, i.e.\ a read-once formula on $n$ distinct variables; such a function depends on
every variable, hence is non-constant: so UNSAT needs $\ge n$ gates. Upper bound: $(x_1\wedge\neg x_1)\wedge x_2\wedge\dots\wedge x_n$
uses $n$ gates; conjunction of $n$ literals gives $S_1\le n-1$, matching the tree bound.
\end{proof}
Each added input costs exactly one gate (two wires) in both families: the increment is $+1$ gate,
$+2$ wires per input. Exhaustive search (\texttt{research/agents/claude/circuitsat/mincircuit.py}) agrees (COMPUTATIONAL):
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{cccc}\toprule
$n$ & $U(n)$ & $S_1(n)$ & max $C(f)$ over all $f$ (B$_2$ basis)\\\midrule
1 & 1 & 0 (input itself) & 0\\ 2 & 2 & 1 & 1\\ 3 & 3 & 2 & 4\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
The $n=3$ gate-count distribution over the 256 functions is $0{:}8,\ 1{:}30,\ 2{:}114,\ 3{:}80,\ 4{:}24$.
(Search covers distinct-node gates to depth 4; $U,S_1$ with repeated-node gates to depth 3.)
Stage $n=4$ is not run: naive search exceeds the 30-second rule; it needs a C/SAT-based chunked search (next step).

\section{Unconditional solving bound: probes}
Access model: adjacency/wire-list oracle; cost = number of wire reads.
\begin{prop}[PROVED/EXACT, trivial] Deterministic and randomized (error $<1/3$) \CSAT\ with $n\ge2$ requires $\Omega(m)$ wire reads.\end{prop}
\begin{proof} Let $C_0$: output $=x_1\wedge y$, $y=\neg^{m'}x_1$ a chain of $m'$ NOT-gates with even $m'$ (UNSAT).
Rewiring any single chain wire to $x_2$ makes the instance SAT (set $x_1=1$ and $x_2$ to the needed parity).
Every wire is critical; a (random) hidden rewired wire is found only by reading it. Yao's principle gives $\Omega(m')$. \end{proof}
This is only linear in $m$; it says the \emph{input must be read}, nothing about the $2^{\Omega(n)}$ regime.

\section{Parametrized picture}
Known upper bounds: $O^*(2^n)$ brute force; $2^{n-\Omega(n/\log m)}$-type improvements for restricted classes.
\begin{itemize}
\item CONDITIONAL (SETH): no $2^{(1-\varepsilon)n}\mathrm{poly}(m)$ algorithm; CNF-SAT reduces to \CSAT\ with $m=\mathrm{poly}(n)$.
\item KNOWN THEOREM direction (Williams): faster-than-$2^n$ \CSAT\ for rich classes implies circuit lower bounds, not conversely.
\item Candidate parameter spine: lower bound $T(n,m)\ge m\cdot f(\text{treewidth of wire graph})$ for low-width circuits is \emph{false} as a
hardness claim (bounded treewidth is FPT: $O(2^{tw}m)$), so hardness must come from \emph{unbounded width}; the real target is a
trade-off $T\ge 2^{\Omega(\mathrm{tw})}$ for \emph{some} family with $\mathrm{tw}=\Theta(n)$ -- CANDIDATE/OPEN, equivalent in spirit to SETH-style claims.
\end{itemize}
