\section{Setup}
A circuit $C$ has $n$ inputs, $m$ two-input gates (basis $B_2$, free negation), hence $2m$ wires into gates plus the output wire. Circuit-SAT$(n,m)$: is there $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ with $C(x)=1$? Parameters: $n$, $m$, and fan-out/depth where noted. A lower bound is a function $T(n,m)$.

\section{Ladder, step by step}
\subsection*{Step $n=1$}
Functions: $0,1,x,\bar x$. Satisfiability of a constant-size circuit costs $O(1)$; the output must read at least one wire to distinguish $x$ from $0$. Min wires for a non-constant output: $0$ gates.
\subsection*{Step $n\ge 2$: connectivity bound (wires needed to build)}
\begin{lem}[PROVED/EXACT; folklore connectivity]
A circuit whose function depends on all $n$ inputs has at least $n-1$ gates, i.e.\ at least $2(n-1)$ gate-input wires.
\end{lem}
\begin{proof}
Treat the circuit as a DAG; the output must be reachable from every relevant input. Each gate merges at most two predecessors, so the undirected graph on $n$ inputs plus $m$ gates being connected needs $m+n-1\ge$ edges $\le 2m$, so $m\ge n-1$.
\end{proof}
Applied to unique-SAT functions (exactly one satisfying assignment, e.g.\ the AND of $n$ literals): $\mathrm{gates}\ge n-1$, and the AND tree meets it. The ladder is tight.

\section{Decision-time lower bound in $m$ (adversary)}
\begin{thm}[PROVED/EXACT, trivial; model: read-only random access to the wire list]
Any deterministic algorithm deciding Circuit-SAT on circuits with $m$ gates must read $\Omega(m)$ wires in the worst case, already for $n=1$.
\end{thm}
\begin{proof}
Let $g_1=x$-buffer, $g_{i}=\mathrm{buf}(g_{i-1})$ for $i\le m-1$, output $=g_{m-1}\wedge\bar x$: unsatisfiable. Redirecting any single buffer wire $g_i\gets \mathbf 1$ makes the output $\bar x\wedge 1$, satisfiable. An algorithm that skips a wire cannot tell the two circuits apart; an adversary answers queries as the unsat circuit. Hence $\ge m-1$ reads.
\end{proof}
So $T(n,m)=\Omega(m)$ unconditionally; this is size of input only and says nothing about hardness in $n$.
