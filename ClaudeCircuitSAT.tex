\textbf{P versus NP remains OPEN.} No unconditional super-polynomial (or even

Status labels: PROVED/EXACT, KNOWN THEOREM, COMPUTATIONAL, CONDITIONAL, CANDIDATE, OPEN, FALSIFIED/RETIRED.

\section{Setup}
A circuit $C$ has $n$ inputs, $g$ binary gates (AND with free edge negation, AIG style),
one output. A \emph{wire} is an edge of the DAG; $w(C)$ counts edges. Circuit-SAT: given $C$, is there $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ with $C(x)=1$?
Parameters tracked: $n$, $g$, $w$, and the structural parameter $k$ (cutwidth/pathwidth of the wiring).
Upper bounds: brute force $O(2^n(g+n))$ (KNOWN); $2^{O(k)}\mathrm{poly}$ by dynamic programming over a width-$k$ layout (KNOWN).

\section{The ladder, exact results}
\begin{lemma}[Connectivity ladder; PROVED/EXACT]\label{lem:conn}
If $C$ depends essentially on all $n$ inputs, then $g\ge n-1$ and $w\ge n+g-1\ge 2n-2$; equality iff the cone is a tree of $n-1$ gates.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every input must have a directed path to the output, so the underlying undirected graph on $n+g$ nodes is connected, hence has $\ge n+g-1$ edges.
Fan-in $2$ gives $w\le 2g$, so $2g\ge n+g-1$, i.e.\ $g\ge n-1$. Trees attain it.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}[Needle ladder; PROVED/EXACT for the bound, COMPUTATIONAL for tightness]
The indicator of a single point of $\{0,1\}^n$ needs $\ge n-1$ gates by Lemma~\ref{lem:conn}; an AND-tree attains $n-1$. Exact search gives $1,2$ for $n=2,3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{theorem}[Exhaustive minimum-size table; COMPUTATIONAL]
Let $s_n(f)$ be the minimum AIG gate count of $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$. Exhaustive iterative-deepening search over all gate sequences
(\texttt{experiments/claude\_circuitsat/aig\_ladder.py}, data \texttt{results/raw/claude\_circuitsat/aig\_ladder.json}) gives the histogram:
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{@{}lccccccc@{}}\toprule
$n$ & $s=0$&1&2&3&4&5&6\\\midrule
1 & 4&&&&&&\\ 2 & 6&8&&2&&&\\ 3 & 8&24&64&30&104&8&18\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
So $\max_f s_1,s_2,s_3 = 0,3,6$ (XOR$_2$ costs 3, XOR$_3$/parity-type costs 6); the corresponding minimal wire counts are $2s+1$ output-inclusive.
The sequence of maxima $0,3,6$ is consistent with $3(n-1)$ for parity but $n=4$ is not yet computed.
\end{theorem}

\section{Black-box (evaluation-oracle) bound}
\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT, restricted model]\label{thm:bb}
Any deterministic algorithm that decides Circuit-SAT for $n$-input circuits given only evaluation access $x\mapsto C(x)$ needs $2^n$ evaluations in the worst case.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Adversary answers $0$ to every query; after $2^n-1$ queries the unqueried point can still be the unique satisfying point or not.
\end{proof}
\textbf{Transfer status: FAILED TRANSFER as a Turing-model bound.} The algorithm in the real problem receives the wiring, which Theorem~\ref{thm:bb} hides;
tree-like wiring is solvable in time $O(g)$ (CANDIDATE-level observation, standard DP), so any parameterized bound must be stated in terms of a wiring parameter $k$.
It also relativizes, and the repository's barrier ledger requires a non-relativizing step.

\section{Parameterized targets}
\begin{conjecture}[OPEN; CONDITIONAL support]
No algorithm decides Circuit-SAT in time $2^{(1-\varepsilon)k}\mathrm{poly}(g)$ for every $\varepsilon>0$, where $k$ is the pathwidth of the circuit's wiring.
\end{conjecture}
Support: SETH-based lower bounds for SAT parameterized by pathwidth (Iwata--Yoshida, ESA 2015; cited from memory, to be re-verified). 
Unconditionally, the best known general lower bounds are time--space tradeoffs for SAT (Williams: $n^{2\cos(\pi/7)-o(1)}\approx n^{1.80}$ with $n^{o(1)}$ space; KNOWN THEOREM, cited from memory)
and explicit circuit-size bounds of order $3.5n-o(n)$ (as recorded in \texttt{GROKPROOF/}); neither bounds the \emph{decision time} of Circuit-SAT as a function of $n,g,k$.
