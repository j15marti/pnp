\section{Goal and method}
Circuit-SAT: given a fan-in-2 circuit $C$ over $\{\wedge,\vee,\neg\}$ with $n$
inputs and $m$ wires, decide $\exists x\,C(x)=1$. We climb a ladder: fix the
simplest circuit families, track the minimum number of gates/wires needed to
build each function, increase $n$, and look for a lower bound on solving
SAT, possibly parameterised by $(n,m,\text{depth},\text{fan-out},\text{treewidth})$.
Convention: negations are free on wires; a gate is an AND; $m=2g$ for $g$ gates.

\section{Rung 1: building-cost lemmas (PROVED)}
\begin{lemma}[Connectivity]\label{l:conn}
If $f$ depends on $n$ inputs, any fan-in-2 circuit for $f$ has $g\ge n-1$
gates, hence $m\ge 2(n-1)$ wires. Equality iff a read-once formula is possible.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} The underlying graph must connect the output to every essential
input; $g$ binary gates add at most $g$ new nodes to the output's cone, so
$n\le g+1$.\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Parity-type cost, $n\le 3$]
With free negation and AND gates, $\mathrm{XOR}_2$ needs 3 gates (6 wires),
$\mathrm{XOR}_3$ needs 6 gates (12 wires).
\end{lemma}
Proof: exhaustive search, see COMPUTATIONAL table below (the unconditional
argument for $n=2$ is the standard one: one or two ANDs give only monotone/unate
functions up to negation).

\section{Rung 3: what this gives for SAT lower bounds}
\begin{theorem}[Read-everything bound, PROVED, trivial]\label{t:read}
Let SAT-instances be circuits given as wire lists with $n$ inputs and $m$
wires. Any deterministic algorithm in the probe model (query a wire's endpoints)
needs $\Omega(m)$ probes, already on AND-of-$n$-literals instances.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Instance: $\bigwedge_i \ell_i$ with $\ell_i$ literals on distinct
variables (satisfiable). If the algorithm never probes slot $j$, replace $\ell_j$
by the negation of some $\ell_k$; the answer flips to UNSAT but all probes are
identical. So all $n$ slots are probed; padding to $m$ wires with dummy gates
gives $\Omega(m)$.\end{proof}
This is parameterised ($\Omega(m)$) but linear, hence far from useful for P vs NP.

\subsection{Parameterised picture (CANDIDATE roadmap)}
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}\toprule
Parameter regime & Known/expected & Status\\\midrule
fan-out-free (formula), read-once & linear time & KNOWN\\
treewidth $w$ & $2^{O(w)}m$ upper bound; ETH-type $2^{o(w)}$ lower bound & KNOWN (conditional)\\
general, depth $d$, $m$ wires & best unconditional is linear probes & OPEN beyond\\
SETH-style: $2^{(1-\epsilon)n}\mathrm{poly}(m)$ & conditional only & CONDITIONAL\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
The wire-count ladder is the building-cost side; the missing step is a
bridge from ``every circuit family for $f$ needs many wires'' (circuit lower
bounds for explicit $f$, currently $\approx 3.1n$ for general $B_2$) to ``deciding
satisfiability of an arbitrary given circuit is hard'', which is not implied: easy
functions can be hard to decide only when the \emph{encoding} hides structure.

\section{Cross-reference to the repository}
Per \texttt{AGENTS.md} and \texttt{research/agents/claude/}: the world ledger
(row L13 Buss--Williams) shows alternation trading cannot give SAT time exponent
$2\cos(\pi/7)\approx1.247$ in $n^{o(1)}$ space; TPORT/ONETAPE reports show portfolio
and one-tape routes yield only low-degree polynomial bounds. These are the
strongest known time--space style \emph{conditional on model} bounds for SAT and
are the natural parameterised targets (exponent as function of space $s$) for
circuit-SAT. Nothing in the corpus claims an unconditional superpolynomial bound.
