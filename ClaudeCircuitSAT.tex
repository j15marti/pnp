\section{Goal and method}
Circuit-SAT: given a fan-in-2 circuit $C$ with $n$ inputs and $s$ gates, decide $\exists x\,C(x)=1$.
Plan: climb a ladder $(n,s,w)$ (inputs, gates, wires, $w=2s$ for fan-in 2), record for each rung the exact minimum
wire/gate budget, and state lower bounds as functions of the parameters, e.g.\ $T(n,s)\ge\ldots$.

\section{Proved baseline bounds}
\begin{lem}[essential-input bound; PROVED] If $f$ depends on $k$ variables, any fan-in-2 circuit has $\ge k-1$ gates and $\ge 2(k-1)$ wires.
\end{lem}
\emph{Proof.} The cone of the output with $g$ gates meets at most $g+1$ inputs (induction on $g$: each gate adds one new fan-in slot). \qed

\begin{thm}[probe lower bound; PROVED] In the model where $C$ is read from a gate-list by queries, any deterministic algorithm deciding Circuit-SAT, even for $n=1$, needs $\ge s/2$ probes.
\end{thm}
\emph{Proof.} Let $g_0=x\wedge\neg x$ and $g_i=g_{i-1}\wedge g_{i-1}$ ($i\le s$), output $g_s$: unsatisfiable. Setting any single $g_i$ to the constant $1$ gives a satisfiable circuit
differing in one gate. An algorithm probing $<s/2$ gates leaves some $g_i$ unprobed and answers identically on both. \qed

This is an input-size bound only; it carries no information about $2^n$-type hardness.
