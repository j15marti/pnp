\section{Program}
Start from the simplest circuit-SAT instances, grow the circuit gate by gate and wire by wire, record the minimum
number of wires/gates each step forces, then grow the number of inputs $n$ and record how the bound scales.
Bounds may be parameterized by $(n,m,k)$: inputs, gates (wires $=2m$ in fan-in-2), and a structural parameter $k$ (e.g.\ treewidth, depth).

\section{Model}
Circuit $C$: DAG over $n$ inputs, $m$ binary gates from $B_2$ (all 16 two-input functions), output negation free.
Wires $w(C)=2m$ (fan-in edges). $\mathrm{CSAT}$: given a description of $C$, decide $\exists x\,C(x)=1$.
Two distinct quantities are tracked and must never be conflated:
(a) $s(f)$, the minimum circuit \emph{size} of a function $f$ (a lower bound on \emph{building} circuits);
(b) the \emph{decision cost} of CSAT on a size-$m$ description (a lower bound on \emph{solving}).

\section{Step ledger: lower bounds for solving CSAT}
\begin{prop}[Description-read bound; PROVED, model-restricted]
In the model where $C$ is given by a read-only table of $m$ gate records and the solver pays one unit per record read,
any deterministic (or randomized with one-sided error $<1/2$) CSAT solver reads $\Omega(m)$ records.
\end{prop}
\emph{Proof sketch.} Let $C_0$ be an unsatisfiable circuit whose output gate is $g\wedge h$ with $h$ computing $\neg g$ through a chain touching all $m$ gate records. If the solver skips record $j$ on the all-run, replace gate $j$ so that the chain is broken and the circuit becomes satisfiable; the solver's transcript is identical. Hence every record is read. $\square$

This is only the \emph{trivial linear} bound: it says nothing about time beyond input size, and it is fully compatible with $\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}$.

\subsection{Known general-circuit frontier (KNOWN THEOREMS, from the literature as recorded in the repo's GROKPROOF notes; not re-verified this run)}
Explicit-function circuit lower bounds over $B_2$ are only linear, $\approx 3.1n$ by gate elimination; no superlinear general bound is known.
Hence a general parameter-free lower bound for CSAT by wire counting is beyond the frontier; the realistic targets are parameterized or restricted models.

\section{Parameterized targets (CANDIDATE / CONDITIONAL)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Treewidth $k$:} CSAT is solvable in $2^{O(k)}\,\mathrm{poly}(m)$; a matching $2^{o(k)}$ lower bound holds \emph{conditionally} on SETH/ETH (CONDITIONAL). An unconditional version is OPEN.
\item \textbf{Monotone / bounded-depth restrictions:} lower bounds on size (e.g.\ AC$^0$ parity) are KNOWN, but are bounds on \emph{building}, not on CSAT decision.
\item \textbf{Restricted algorithm class (CANDIDATE):} prove $\Omega(2^{n/c})$ for DPLL/resolution-style CSAT solvers via wire-by-wire proof-size bounds; transfers a proof-complexity lower bound, not a Turing-machine one.
\end{enumerate}
