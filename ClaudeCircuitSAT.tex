\section{Setup}
A circuit $C$ over basis $B_2$ (all 2-input gates) has $n$ inputs, $m$ gates, $2m$ wires (fan-in 2).
$\mathrm{CSAT}(n,m)$: given $C$, decide $\exists x\in\{0,1\}^n: C(x)=1$.
Parameters: $n$ (inputs), $m$ (gates), $w=2m$ (wires), and optionally fan-out/depth.
Two different quantities are tracked and must not be confused:
\begin{itemize}
\item $c_n(f)$: minimum gates to \emph{build} $f$ (circuit complexity of one function);
\item $T_{\mathrm{CSAT}}(n,m)$: time/probes to \emph{decide} satisfiability of a given circuit.
\end{itemize}

\section{Step 2: a proved decision lower bound in the probe model (PROVED)}
Model: the circuit is given as a gate list; an algorithm learns one gate (type and two predecessor indices)
per probe.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:read}
Every deterministic (even adaptive) algorithm deciding $\mathrm{CSAT}$ on circuits with $m$ gates
needs $\Omega(m)$ probes in the worst case; more precisely $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $k=\lfloor m/2\rfloor$. Gate $u=\neg x_1$; gates $v_i=x_1\wedge u$ ($i\le k$), each $\equiv 0$; an OR-tree
of $v_1,\dots,v_k$ ($k-1$ gates) is the output; total $2k\le m$ gates (pad with dummies). The instance is unsatisfiable.
Replacing the second predecessor of any single $v_i$ by $x_1$ gives $v_i=x_1\wedge x_1$, and setting $x_1=1$ satisfies the circuit.
An algorithm that never probed $v_i$ gets identical answers on both instances, so it must probe all $k$ gates $v_i$. \end{proof}
Remark (honest): this is the trivial input-reading bound; it is linear in $m$ and independent of $n$.
Nothing in it uses non-determinism or inputs, and a superlinear bound would already be a new result.
