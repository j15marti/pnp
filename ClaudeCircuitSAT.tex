\section{Model and the ladder idea}
A circuit $C$ has $n$ inputs, $g$ fan-in-2 gates over the full basis $B_2$ (negation free), one output; \emph{wires} are gate-input edges ($2g$ of them, plus the output wire). \CSAT: given $C$, decide $\exists x\,C(x)=1$. Instance size $m=\Theta((n+g)\log(n+g))$ bits.

\textbf{Program.} Climb a ladder of instance families $\mathcal L_{n}$, $n=1,2,3,\dots$, recording at each rung (i) the exact minimum number of gates/wires needed to build the circuits of that rung, (ii) the resulting lower bound on the resources to decide satisfiability, with the parameter $n$ (inputs), $w$ (treewidth) or $g$ as appropriate. Goal: see which parameter the hardness attaches to, then try to prove it in a restricted-but-meaningful model and test whether it lifts.

\section{Proved rungs-to-all-$n$ statements}
\begin{theorem}[connectivity, PROVED/EXACT]\label{thm:conn}
If $f$ computed by a fan-in-2 circuit depends on all $n$ inputs, the circuit has $\ge n-1$ gates, hence $\ge 2(n-1)$ wires; tight for $\mathrm{AND}_n$ and $\mathrm{XOR}_n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Restrict to ancestors of the output: a connected undirected graph on $n+g'$ vertices with exactly $2g'$ edges. Connectivity gives $2g'\ge n+g'-1$, i.e.\ $g'\ge n-1$. A balanced tree attains it.\end{proof}
Consistency check: the $n=3$ row has the all-relevant functions needing $\ge2$ gates; the table's maximum 4 exceeds $n-1=2$, so size lower bounds above connectivity occur already at $n=3$ (e.g.\ majority needs 4).

\begin{theorem}[description-reading bound, PROVED/EXACT]\label{thm:read}
Any deterministic \CSAT{} solver that accesses the instance by probing gate descriptions must probe $\ge g$ distinct gates on the family below, i.e.\ $\Omega(m/\log m)$ probes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Base instance: chain $y_1=x_1\wedge x_2$, $y_{i}=y_{i-1}\wedge x_{i+1}$, output $y_g$ (satisfiable). Variant $V_i$: gate $i$ replaced by constant $0$ (unsatisfiable). A solver never probing gate $i$ behaves identically on base and $V_i$ but must answer differently.\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[black-box evaluation, PROVED/EXACT]\label{thm:bb}
An algorithm that touches $C$ only through evaluations $x\mapsto C(x)$ needs $2^n$ evaluations in the worst case.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} Adversary answers $0$ to every query until all $2^n$ points are queried; any unqueried point could be the unique satisfying assignment.\end{proof}

\section{What the ladder says so far (the key observation)}
\begin{observation}[size does not see hardness, PROVED/EXACT]
Satisfiable and unsatisfiable instances are indistinguishable by the quantities tracked so far: $\mathrm{AND}_n$ (satisfiable) and $\mathrm{AND}_n\wedge\neg x_1$ (unsatisfiable) both have $\Theta(n)$ gates; on the connectivity count, adding one input costs exactly $+1$ gate, $+2$ wires. So \emph{pure wire counting yields only the linear bound} of Theorem~\ref{thm:read}. Hardness must come from the \emph{interaction pattern} of wires (cycles in the undirected skeleton, i.e.\ reconvergent fan-out), not their number.
\end{observation}
This suggests the parametrization to chase: let $c$ = cyclomatic number of the skeleton ($c=2g-(n+g)+1=g-n+1$ for connected circuits; $c=0$ iff tree, where \CSAT{} is linear time by DP). Upper bound (KNOWN THEOREM, standard DP over a tree decomposition): $2^{O(w)}\cdot m$ with $w\le c+1$ the treewidth. Conversely, CONDITIONAL on SETH, no $(2-\varepsilon)^{w}\mathrm{poly}(m)$ algorithm is expected for \CSAT{}/CNF-SAT parametrized by pathwidth (recalled from the fine-grained literature, Lampis and Cygan et al.; \emph{citation to be re-verified before reliance}).

\paragraph{Target T1 (OPEN).} Find an unconditional lower bound $T(m,c)$ in a restricted-but-nontrivial model (e.g.\ read-once branching programs with bounded space, or the repository's small-space time--space setting) of the form $2^{\Omega(c)}$ or $2^{\Omega(c/\log c)}$, and prove it by induction on the ladder: show that adding one reconvergent wire at most doubles the number of ``distinguishable partial assignments'' (a boundary/cut-rank invariant) and that some families force the doubling.
