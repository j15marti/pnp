\section{Goal and method}
Start from the simplest Circuit-SAT instances; grow the circuit one gate/wire at a time, recording (i) the minimum number of wires needed to build an instance of that class and (ii) a lower bound on the work any decider must do, as a function of parameters $(n,g,w)$: inputs, gates, wires (fan-in $\le2$, so $w=2g$ for binary gates, plus output).

\section{Ladder (rung $\to$ wires $\to$ decider cost)}
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{llll}
Rung & Instance class & Min wires to build & Decider lower bound\\\hline
0 & output $=x_1$ & 1 (input$\to$output) & 1 read (PROVED/EXACT, trivial)\\
1 & $n$ inputs, all essential & $2(n-1)$ & $\Omega(n)$ reads (below)\\
2 & + one negation layer (literal gates) & $2(n-1)+n$ & $\Omega(n)$\\
3 & + $k$ extra gates, fan-out $\le f$ & $2(n-1+k)$ & $\Omega(g)$ reads\\
4 & general $(n,g)$ & $2g$ & OPEN beyond $\Omega(g)$ in explicit models
\end{tabular}\end{center}

\section{Exact results so far}
\begin{lemma}[wire minimum, PROVED/EXACT] A fan-in-2 circuit with a single output in which all $n$ inputs are connected to the output has at least $n-1$ binary gates, hence at least $2(n-1)$ wires; equality holds for a tree.\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} The underlying graph is connected on $n+g$ nodes, so it has $\ge n+g-1$ edges; every gate contributes exactly 2 incoming edges, so $2g\ge n+g-1$, i.e.\ $g\ge n-1$.\end{proof}

\begin{prop}[description-reading lower bound, PROVED/EXACT in the gate-list query model]
Any deterministic algorithm that decides Circuit-SAT from a gate list, reading entries by query, must read $\ge g$ gate descriptions in the worst case on circuits with $g$ gates.\end{prop}
\begin{proof} Let $C$ be the AND-tree over wires $w_1,\dots,w_g$ where each $w_i$ is either the input $x_i$ or the constant-0 gate $x_i\wedge\neg x_i$. $C$ is satisfiable iff every $w_i=x_i$. If the algorithm skips the description of $w_i$ on an all-$x$ instance, flipping $w_i$ to constant 0 gives the same transcript but the opposite answer.\end{proof}
This is a query-model statement; it gives no step bound against algorithms that are handed more structure, and it is linear only. The trivial $2^n$ brute-force upper bound versus this $\Omega(g)$ gap is the whole problem.

\section{Parameterization}
Candidate parameters for a non-uniform-in-parameter bound: $n$ (inputs), $g$, fan-out $f$, circuit depth $d$, treewidth $\tau$ (Circuit-SAT is FPT in $\tau$: time $2^{O(\tau)}\mathrm{poly}$; KNOWN THEOREM), and the wire-cut width $c$ of a linear layout. A parameterized target: time $\ge 2^{\Omega(\tau)}$ under ETH (CONDITIONAL; known from SAT lower bounds in treewidth), and an \textbf{unconditional} bound of this shape is OPEN.
