\section{Program}
Start from the smallest Circuit-SAT instances, grow the number of inputs $n$ one at a time, and at each step
record (a) the exact minimum number of gates $g^*(n)$ and wires $w^*(n)=2g^*(n)$ (fan-in 2, one output wire) needed for the
hardest / for specific instances, and (b) a lower bound on the work of deciding satisfiability, possibly
parametrized by $n$, size $s$, treewidth/pathwidth $t$, or fan-out $d$.
Two notions must be kept apart: \emph{representation cost} (wires needed to \emph{write down} the instance, a
circuit-complexity quantity) versus \emph{solving cost} (time/queries to decide satisfiability). Only the second is a
P vs NP lower bound; the first feeds it only through a transfer theorem that is \OPEN{}.

\section{Wire-level lower bounds that are actually proved}
\begin{lemma}[connectivity, \PROVED{}] Let $f$ depend essentially on $n$ variables. Any fan-in-2 circuit computing $f$ has at least $n-1$ gates and at least $2(n-1)$ wires.\end{lemma}
\emph{Proof.} Every essential input has a directed path to the output, so the circuit's underlying graph restricted to those inputs and their ancestors-of-output is connected on $n+g$ nodes, so has $\ge n+g-1$ edges; fan-in 2 gives wires $=2g$ (counting gate-input wires), and $2g\ge n+g-1$ gives $g\ge n-1$. \qed
This is tight for AND$_n$, and in B2 for parity; in DM parity needs $3(n-1)$ gates (\KNOWN, Schnorr 1974; matches $n=2$ above, $n=3$ value 6 not re-verified here).

\begin{lemma}[reading the instance, \PROVED{}] Any algorithm that decides Circuit-SAT on instances of $s$ wires must read $\Omega(s)$ of the encoding in the worst case.\end{lemma}
\emph{Proof sketch.} Take an AND-chain of $s$ wires with the final literal ($x_1$ or $\neg x_1$ against a forced $x_1$-conjunct) hidden at a position the algorithm never reads: the two instances differing there differ in satisfiability but agree on all read bits. \qed\ (A trivial bound; it shows the relevant bounds are in the \emph{not}-all-read regime only for promise/structured inputs.)

\section{Parametrized picture (what is known vs. open)}
\begin{itemize}
\item Upper bounds: Circuit-SAT is solvable in $2^{n}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(s)$ and in $2^{O(t)}\mathrm{poly}(s)$ for treewidth $t$ (\KNOWN). So any lower bound has the shape $2^{\Omega(\min(n,t))}$ at best.
\item Matching lower bounds exist only \CONDITIONAL{} on ETH/SETH (no $2^{o(n)}$ resp.\ $2^{(1-\epsilon)n}$ for CNF-SAT; analogous pathwidth lower bounds). Unconditionally the best \emph{circuit-size} lower bounds for explicit functions in B2 are linear, roughly $3.1n$ via gate elimination (\KNOWN, recalled from memory; not re-verified this run), far from superpolynomial.
\item \OPEN: an unconditional bound of the form $\mathrm{time}\ge f(n,t)$ with $f$ superpolynomial for general algorithms.
\end{itemize}

\section{Incremental ("add one input") scheme}
Define $W(n)=\min$ wires of a hardest $n$-input instance. Gate elimination gives $W(n+1)\ge W(n)+c$ if setting $x_{n+1}$ to a constant removes $\ge c/2$ gates, which yields only linear $W$. Superlinear growth would need a measure that is not destroyed by restriction, i.e.\ an argument that avoids restrictions; this is the classical natural-proofs / relativization / algebrization obstacle region. \CAND: track instead the fan-out profile $(d_1,\dots,d_n)$ of inputs; wires $=\sum_i d_i + \sum_{\text{gates}}(\text{outdeg})$ and low fan-out forces tree-like (small-treewidth, easy) structure. Whether a fan-out/treewidth tradeoff forces $2^{\Omega(t)}$ solving cost is \OPEN.

\section{Barrier checklist (to be applied to any proposed bound)}
Relativization (black-box search needs $2^n$ queries but the white-box circuit is given); natural proofs; algebrization; and the \emph{representation vs solving} gap above. Any claimed bound must say which of these it avoids.
