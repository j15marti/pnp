\paragraph{Status.} \textbf{P versus NP remains OPEN.} Nothing here is a proof of a superpolynomial lower bound for $\CSAT$. Labels: PROVED/EXACT, KNOWN THEOREM, COMPUTATIONAL, CONDITIONAL, CANDIDATE, OPEN, FALSIFIED/RETIRED.

\section{Program}
Build circuits from the simplest case upward, tracking at each step (i) the number of inputs $n$, (ii) the minimum number of gates $s$ and wires $w$ ($w=2s$ for fan-in-2 gates, plus the output wire), and (iii) what is required to \emph{decide} satisfiability. Lower bounds are parametrized by $(n,s)$ (or $(n,w)$).

\paragraph{Why parametrization is forced.} Brute force decides $\CSAT$ in time $O(2^n s)$. If $s\ge 2^n/n$ this is $\mathrm{poly}(s)$. So a meaningful superpolynomial bound must live in the regime $s=\mathrm{poly}(n)$, i.e.\ it is a bound of the form $T(n,s)\ge f(n)$ with $s\le n^{O(1)}$, not a bound in $s$ alone. (PROVED/EXACT, trivial.)

\section{Model hierarchy}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}\toprule Model & Bound & Status\\\midrule
Black-box (evaluate $C$ at points) & $\Omega(2^n)$ queries & PROVED/EXACT (Thm.~\ref{t:bb})\\
Probe model (read gate descriptors) & $\ge s$ probes & PROVED/EXACT (Thm.~\ref{t:probe})\\
Full white-box, time & super-poly in $n$ for $s=\mathrm{poly}(n)$ & OPEN (= P vs NP)\\
Same, under ETH & $2^{\Omega(n)}$ for $s=O(n)$ & CONDITIONAL\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}

The gap between the first and third rows is exactly the white-box gap: the circuit's \emph{wiring} must be exploited. Every step below tracks which wire-structure an algorithm could exploit.

\begin{theorem}[Black-box, PROVED/EXACT]\label{t:bb} Any deterministic algorithm that accesses $C:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$ only by evaluation needs $2^n$ queries to decide satisfiability in the worst case.\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof.} Adversary answers 0 until one point remains; the unqueried point may be the unique satisfying assignment. \qed

\begin{theorem}[Probe lower bound, PROVED/EXACT]\label{t:probe} Let instances be fan-in-2 circuits given as gate lists with gate types from $B_2$. Any deterministic algorithm needs at least $s$ gate-descriptor probes on some $s$-gate instance.\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof.} Let $C_0$: $g_1=x_1\wedge x_2$, $g_i=g_{i-1}\wedge x_{i+1}$ ($s=n-1$), output $g_s$; satisfiable by all-ones. Let $C_j$ replace the type of $g_j$ by the constant~$0$; then $C_j$ is unsatisfiable. Answer every probe as in $C_0$; an algorithm halting with $g_j$ unprobed gives the same answer on $C_0$ and $C_j$. \qed

This is the trivial linear read bound; it is the $s$-axis baseline, not progress toward P vs NP.

\begin{observation}[PROVED/EXACT] A function depending on all $n$ variables needs $\ge n-1$ gates ($\ge 2(n-1)$ wires): the underlying graph must connect $n$ inputs to one output through fan-in-2 gates. AND/OR/XOR meet it with equality for $n=2,3,4$ (data above).\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[COMPUTATIONAL, small $n$ only] The first $n$ where a symmetric function exceeds $n-1$ gates is $n=3$ (MAJ$_3$: 4 vs.\ 2). EXACT-1, MAJ, THR$_2$ and $\mathrm{MOD}_3$ all exceed 4 gates at $n=4$. No extrapolation to asymptotics is claimed.\end{observation}

\section{Known theorems (KNOWN THEOREM; recalled, primary sources NOT re-verified this run)}
Gate elimination gives explicit-function circuit lower bounds over $B_2$ of the form $c\,n-o(n)$ with $c=3$ (Blum 1984), improved to about $3.011n$ (Find--Golovnev--Hirsch--Kulikov, FOCS 2016) and $3.1n$ (Li--Yang, STOC 2022). \textbf{TODO next run:} verify these citations against primary sources before relying on them.

\textbf{Transfer classification.} These bound the size of a circuit \emph{computing} a function, not the time to decide satisfiability of a given circuit. Relation: FAILED TRANSFER as stated; CANDIDATE only if a gate-elimination argument can be run against an algorithm's partial-assignment/simplification process (a DPLL-style model), where each branch eliminates gates -- this yields lower bounds only for restricted (e.g.\ DPLL/resolution-like) algorithm classes, a known barrier to generalization.
