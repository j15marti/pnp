\section{Goal and method}
Start from the smallest Circuit-SAT instances, add inputs $n$ and wires/gates $m$ one step at a time, record (i) the minimum number of gates/wires needed to build each circuit and (ii) the cost any solver must pay, as a function $T(n,m)$ (a parameterized bound). Each rung must state its computation model.

\section{Rung 0--3: exact minimum circuit size (COMPUTATIONAL)}
Basis: all 16 binary gates (negation costs one gate, as $g(x,x)$); fan-in 2; size = number of gates; wires $\le 2\cdot$size. Exhaustive level-by-level search over sets of computed truth tables
(\texttt{experiments/claude\_circuitsat/2026-10-27\_min\_circuit\_ladder.py}, raw data \texttt{results/raw/claude\_circuitsat\_min\_size.json}, 15\,s).
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrrrr@{}}\toprule
$n$ & #functions & size 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4\\\midrule
1 & 4 & 3 & 1 & & & \\
2 & 16 & 4 & 12 & & & \\
3 & 256 & 5 & 33 & 114 & 80 & 24\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
So $\max_f C(f)=1,1,4$ for $n=1,2,3$. This is a finite fact; it does not extrapolate to a bound (Shannon counting gives $\max_f C(f)=\Theta(2^n/n)$ \textbf{KNOWN}, but not for explicit/SAT-relevant $f$).

\section{Rung A: unique-satisfier circuits need $n-1$ gates (PROVED)}
\begin{lemma} Any fan-in-2 circuit with one output whose function depends on all $n$ inputs has at least $n-1$ gates (hence $\ge n-1$ non-input wires into gates, and $\ge 2(n-1)$ wires if all gates have fan-in 2 and no input is unused).\end{lemma}
\emph{Proof.} Every input must reach the output by a directed path, so the underlying graph restricted to the cone is connected on $n$ inputs plus $g$ gates; a fan-in-2 DAG with $g$ gates has at most $2g$ edges, and a connected graph on $n+g$ vertices needs $\ge n+g-1$ edges, so $2g\ge n+g-1$, i.e.\ $g\ge n-1$. \qed

Exact for AND$_n$ (one satisfying assignment, up to negations): $n-1$ gates, matching the lemma ($n=3$: 2, consistent with the table).

\section{Rung B: description-reading lower bound (PROVED, query model)}
\textbf{Model.} The circuit is given as an array of gates; gate $i$ has a type and two pointer cells. A deterministic algorithm decides satisfiability, paying one unit per cell read.
\begin{theorem} Deciding satisfiability needs $\ge k$ cell reads on instances with $m=\Theta(k)$ gates and $n=k$ inputs.\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof.} Fixed public gates: $\neg x_i$ for each $i$, and an AND-tree over $h_1,\dots,h_k$. Gate $h_i=\mathrm{AND}(x_i,y_i)$ where the secret pointer cell $y_i$ points to $x_i$ (satisfiable) or to $\neg x_i$ ($h_i\equiv0$). On the all-good instance the output is satisfiable; flipping any single $y_j$ makes it unsatisfiable. If the algorithm reads fewer than $k$ pointer cells, some $y_j$ is unread, and it answers identically on the two instances, an error. \qed

This is the trivial ``must read the input'' rung; it is the baseline of the parameterized bound $T(n,m)\ge m$.

\section{Rung C: parameterized shape and what is missing}
\begin{align*}
\text{PROVED:}&\quad T_{\mathrm{query}}(n,m)\ge \Omega(m),\\
\text{KNOWN upper bound:}&\quad T(n,m)\le O(2^n m),\\
\text{CONDITIONAL (SETH/ETH-type):}&\quad T(n,m)\ge 2^{(1-o(1))n}\ \text{for}\ m=\mathrm{poly}(n),\\
\text{OPEN, the target:}&\quad T(n,m)\ge \omega(\mathrm{poly}(n,m))\ \text{unconditionally.}
\end{align*}
The gap between $\Omega(m)$ and $2^{\Omega(n)}$ is exactly the open problem. The ladder's job is to find, rung by rung, an invariant (wire congestion, fan-out, cut width, number of distinct sub-functions at a frontier) that grows with $n$ and is forced on every solver, not only on circuit \emph{builders}. Caution: Rungs 0--3 and A bound the \emph{size of circuits computing $f$}, not the time of solvers for SAT on a circuit; conflating the two is the main failure mode (a lower bound on formulas is not a lower bound on algorithms deciding formula satisfiability).
