\section{Setup and parameters}
A circuit $C$ has $n$ inputs, $s$ gates of fan-in $2$ over the basis $B_2$ (all 2-input gates), hence $m=2s$ wires between nodes (plus output marker). Circuit-SAT: given $C$, is there $x$ with $C(x)=1$?
Parameters: $n$ (inputs), $s$ (gates), $m$ (wires), and structural width $k$ (treewidth/cutwidth/pathwidth of the underlying graph). The target shape of a bound is $T(n,s,k)$, e.g.\ $2^{\epsilon k}\,\mathrm{poly}(s)$.

\section{The ladder}
\subsection*{Rung 0: connections needed for dependence on $n$ inputs}
\begin{lemma}[PROVED/EXACT]\label{l:conn}
If a fan-in-2 circuit's output depends on all $n$ inputs, then $s\ge n-1$ and $m\ge 2(n-1)$; both are tight for $\mathrm{AND}_n,\mathrm{OR}_n,\mathrm{XOR}_n$ (balanced trees).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} Each input has a path to the output, so the underlying undirected graph on inputs and gates is connected with $n+s$ nodes; every gate has two in-wires, and a connected graph on $n+s$ nodes needs $\ge n+s-1$ edges, i.e.\ $2s\ge n+s-1$, so $s\ge n-1$. Tightness: a tree. \end{proof}

\subsection*{Rung 1: reading lower bound in the size parameter}
\begin{proposition}[PROVED/EXACT]\label{p:read}
For every $s$ there is a family of satisfiable circuits $C_s$ with $s$ gates such that changing any single gate's type to constant-$0$ gives an unsatisfiable circuit. Hence any deterministic algorithm for Circuit-SAT with probe access to the gate list makes $\ge s=\Omega(m)$ probes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} Let $C_s$ be the chain $g_1=x_1\wedge x_2$, $g_{i+1}=g_i\wedge x_{(i \bmod n)+1}$, output $g_s$. Only $x=1^n$ satisfies it. An algorithm not probing gate $i$ cannot distinguish $C_s$ from $C_s^{(i)}$ (gate $i$ replaced by $0$), whose answer is UNSAT. \end{proof}

\subsection*{Rung 2: exact minimum sizes for small $n$ (COMPUTATIONAL)}
Exhaustive depth-limited search over all circuits (script \texttt{research/agents/claude/circuitsat/minsize.py}, runtime 27\,s, data \texttt{minsize\_results.json}); the counts include complements, which are free in $B_2$. Each function's row is its minimum gate count; wires are twice that.
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}\toprule
$n$ & size 0 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & total\\\midrule
1 & 4 & 0 & 0&0&0 & 4\\ 2 & 6 & 10 & 0&0&0 & 16\\ 3 & 8 & 30 & 114 & 80 & 24 & 256\\\bottomrule\end{tabular}\end{center}
Totals equal $2^{2^n}$, so the search is exhaustive and $n=3$ needs at most 4 gates (8 wires). The 24 functions of size 4 are those with no cheaper form (parity-/majority-type). This matches the known $B_2$ values (e.g.\ $\mathrm{MAJ}_3$ and $\mathrm{XOR}_3$-like functions are size-4 or less; $\mathrm{XOR}_3$ has size 2).
\emph{Next:} $n=4$ (65\,536 functions, expected max size 7) needs a chunked/meet-in-the-middle search per the 30-second rule.

\subsection*{Rung 3: parameterized time statements (KNOWN / CONDITIONAL)}
\begin{itemize}
\item KNOWN THEOREM: brute force is $2^n\cdot s$; with a tree decomposition of width $k$, dynamic programming gives $2^{O(k)}\,s$.
\item CONDITIONAL: under SETH, no $2^{(1-\epsilon)n}\mathrm{poly}(s)$ algorithm exists for CNF-SAT, and CNF is a circuit family, so the same holds for Circuit-SAT; under SETH treewidth-parameterized CNF-SAT has no $(2-\epsilon)^{k}\mathrm{poly}$ algorithm (Lampis et al.; to be re-verified against primary sources in a later run).
\item KNOWN (repository, ledger row L13): alternation-trading proofs cannot reach SAT time exponent $2\cos(\pi/7)$ with $n^{o(1)}$ space; rows L34--L40 limit portfolio and one-tape variants.
\end{itemize}
