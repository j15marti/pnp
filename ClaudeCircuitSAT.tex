\section{Goal and method}
Circuit-SAT: given a fan-in-2 circuit $C$ with $n$ inputs and $m$ gates (so $2m$
internal wires), decide $\exists x\; C(x)=1$. Plan: start from the smallest circuits,
add inputs one at a time, and record (a) the minimum wires/gates any circuit with a given
property needs, and (b) the cost any algorithm must pay. The bound may be parametrized
in $(n,m)$ or in structural parameters (treewidth, depth).

\section{Wire ledger: how many connections are forced}
\begin{prop}[PROVED] A fan-in-2 circuit whose output depends on all $n$ inputs has $\ge n-1$ gates,
hence $\ge 2(n-1)$ gate-input wires. (The underlying graph must be connected;
a tree on $n$ leaves with fan-in 2 has $n-1$ internal nodes.) Tight for $\mathrm{AND}_n$.
\end{prop}
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{c|c|c}
$n$ & min gates (all inputs relevant) & min wires $2(n-1)$\\\hline
1&0&0\\2&1&2\\3&2&4\\4&3&6\\5&4&8\\$n$&$n-1$&$2n-2$\end{tabular}\end{center}
Known (KNOWN, gate elimination): explicit functions need $3n-o(n)$ gates over $B_2$
(Find--Golovnev--Hirsch--Kulikov); nothing superlinear is known for explicit functions.
Hence ``min wires to build the instance'' is only linear in $n$ for any provable family.

\section{Cost of solving: first rigorous step}
\begin{prop}[PROVED, trivial linear bound]\label{p:read}
Any deterministic algorithm that decides Circuit-SAT on $n$-input circuits in the
read-the-description model must read $\ge n-1$ leaf wires (hence $\Omega(m)$ time for
$m=\Theta(n)$).
\end{prop}
\emph{Proof.} Take $C=\bigwedge_{i=1}^n \ell_i$ with each leaf $\ell_i\in\{x_i,\neg x_i\}$ over a pool of
variables, arranged so a clash is ``same variable, opposite sign'' at two leaves (use $n/2$ variables each
appearing twice, signs equal). Such $C$ is satisfiable. Flipping the sign of one leaf of a pair makes $C$
unsatisfiable. If the algorithm leaves two paired leaves unread (or one leaf of any pair), the adversary
answers consistently with both versions; so it must read all but at most one leaf of each pair, i.e. $\ge n/2$ leaves.\qed

\begin{prop}[PROVED, black-box] An algorithm given only evaluation access to $C$ needs $2^n$ queries in
the worst case: the adversary answers 0 until one point remains. Parametrized form: $2^{n}$ for
$n$ inputs, i.e.\ the brute-force bound is tight for black-box access, which is why any
nontrivial lower bound must use the circuit structure. (Relativization barrier.)
\end{prop}

\section{Candidate directions (OPEN)}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Incremental-input ledger.} Define $W(n)=$ min wires of a circuit family in which
adding input $n$ forces new structure. Goal: find a promise family where every correct algorithm must
effectively enumerate $2^{\Omega(k)}$ partial states for some structural parameter $k$. Currently none.
\item \textbf{Repository link.} The repo's TPORT/UWI work (rows L34--L37, alternation trading) shows
Williams-style arguments cannot beat linear time in small space for polynomial-size portfolios; any
parametrized bound here should be classified with the repo ledger (a statement equivalent to P=NP is
a reformulation, not progress).
\item Restrict to monotone / bounded-depth / bounded-width circuits where lower bounds are provable
(KNOWN: monotone exponential bounds for CLIQUE) and see which parameter (depth, width) makes SAT hard.
\end{enumerate}
