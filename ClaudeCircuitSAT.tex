\section{Model and goal}
A circuit is a DAG over $x_1,\dots,x_n$ with fan-in-2 gates from $B_2$ (all 16 binary ops), output negation free.
Cost $g(C)$ = gates, $w(C)=2g(C)$ = wires into gates. $\mathrm{CSAT}(n,s)$: given $C$ with $n$ inputs and $\le s$ gates, decide $\exists x\,C(x)=1$.
Two distinct quantities are tracked and must not be conflated:
(a) $L_{\text{circ}}(f)$, minimum circuit size of a function $f$ (the ``wires needed to build'' side);
(b) $T_{\text{solve}}(n,s)$, cost of any algorithm for $\mathrm{CSAT}(n,s)$ (the lower bound we ultimately want, possibly parametrized by $n$, $s$, treewidth, etc.).

\section{Step 1: the building ladder (exact, small $n$)}
\begin{prop}[PROVED/EXACT: connectivity bound]
If $f$ depends on all $n$ inputs then every circuit for $f$ has $g\ge n-1$ gates and $\ge 2(n-1)$ wires; tight for $\mathrm{AND}_n$ and every point function $[x=a]$.
\end{prop}
\emph{Proof.} Every input must have a directed path to the output, so the underlying undirected graph on $n+g$ nodes is connected: edges $2g\ge n+g-1$, i.e.\ $g\ge n-1$. A balanced tree of ANDs/literals attains it. \qed

\begin{prop}[PROVED/EXACT: relevant-variable parametrization]
A circuit with $g$ gates depends on at most $g+1$ inputs (same argument), so $\mathrm{CSAT}(n,s)$ is solvable in time $2^{\min(n,s+1)}\mathrm{poly}(s)$. Hence the hard regime is $s\ge n-1$; for $s<n-1$ unused inputs are free.
\end{prop}

\section{Step 2: lower bound on solving, by model}
\begin{prop}[PROVED/EXACT, black-box model only]
Any deterministic algorithm that accesses $C$ only by evaluating $C(x)$ needs $2^n$ evaluations on the family $\{[x=a]\}_{a}$ plus the all-zero circuit (adversary answers 0 until one point is left).
\end{prop}
This is a query bound, \emph{not} a Circuit-SAT lower bound: the solver may read the wiring. Status as a route to P$\neq$NP: FALSIFIED/RETIRED as sufficient (it does not survive white-box access; the point-function circuit exposes $a$ in $n-1$ gates).

\textbf{Wire-reading information accounting (CANDIDATE).} Define the leakage $\lambda(C)$ = bits of $\arg\mathrm{SAT}$ determined by inspecting $k$ wires. Point functions leak $a$ at $n-1$ wires. A hard family must have min-size circuits where any $k$ wires reveal $o(k)$ solution bits, forcing size $\gg n$ while keeping a unique satisfier. Open arrow A1: quantify $\lambda$ and prove size growth.

\section{Step 3: what is known unconditionally for circuit size}
Best explicit general-circuit lower bound recalled: $\approx 3.1n$ gates (Li--Yang, STOC 2022) over $B_2$ \emph{[KNOWN THEOREM, from memory, verify]}. The ladder of Step 1 ($n-1$) is the trivial base; the gap between $n-1$ and superlinear is exactly the open problem. Gate elimination, restrictions, and the Williams ACC route are the live tools; natural-proofs, relativization and algebrization barriers apply to any technique that is not explicitly audited against them.
