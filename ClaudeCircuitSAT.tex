\section{Goal and method}
Build circuits step by step, starting from the simplest unsatisfiable instance,
tracking at each step the minimum number of gates and wires, while raising the
number $n$ of inputs. Record a lower bound on the work to decide satisfiability as a
function of parameters $(n,s,w,\dots)$ (variables, gates, wires, fan-out, depth,
treewidth), since the final bound may be parametrized.

\section{Model}
A circuit has $n$ variables with leaves $x_i,\neg x_i$ ($2n$ literal sources), fan-in-2
AND/OR gates, one output, gates numbered topologically; $g$ gates give $w=2g$ wires.
The \emph{wire-probe model}: an algorithm sees the circuit only by querying where a
given wire comes from.

\begin{definition}[1-minimal UNSAT]
$C$ is \emph{1-minimal unsatisfiable} if $C$ is unsatisfiable and, for every wire $e$,
the circuit $C[e{:=}1]$ (wire $e$ replaced by constant 1, a weakening) is satisfiable.
\end{definition}
This is the circuit analogue of a minimally unsatisfiable CNF, but at wire level.

\section{Rung 1: exact minimum size (PROVED)}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:min}
For $n\ge1$ the minimum number of gates of a 1-minimal UNSAT circuit using $n$ variables
is exactly $g_{\min}(n)=2n-1$, i.e.\ $w_{\min}(n)=4n-2$ wires.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Lower bound.} If variable $i$ occurs in only one polarity, fix $x_i$ to make all its
leaves true; this equals setting those leaf wires to $1$ and keeps $C$ unsatisfiable,
contradicting 1-minimality. So both $x_i,\neg x_i$ feed some gate: at least $2n$
literal wires. A gate other than the output with no outgoing wire would have its in-wires
irrelevant (setting one to 1 leaves $C$ unchanged and unsatisfiable); hence every
non-output gate has an outgoing wire: at least $g-1$ gate-to-gate wires. Then
$2g\ge 2n+(g-1)$, so $g\ge 2n-1$.
\emph{Upper bound.} $C_n=\bigvee_{i=1}^n (x_i\wedge\neg x_i)$ uses $n$ AND and $n-1$ OR gates
$=2n-1$. It is unsatisfiable; setting any literal wire to 1 turns one AND into a literal,
and setting any other wire to 1 makes an OR (or the output) equal 1, so each case is
satisfiable.
\end{proof}
Equality forces every gate fan-out to be exactly 1 (a tree) and each variable to have
exactly two leaf wires.  The implication chain $x_1\wedge\bigwedge_i(\neg x_{i}\vee x_{i+1})\wedge\neg x_n$
is a second extremal family (also $2n-1$ gates), showing extremal circuits are not unique
and have very different depth ($O(\log n)$ vs.\ linear).

\begin{corollary}[linear probe bound]
Any deterministic wire-probe algorithm that correctly decides \SAT{} must probe all
$4n-2$ wires on $C_n$: an unprobed wire $e$ could be replaced by $1$, giving a satisfiable
circuit with the same answers to all other probes. Hence $\Omega(n)$, and in general
$\Omega(s)$ in the circuit size $s$.  (PROVED; it is an acquisition bound, not a time bound
beyond reading the input.)
\end{corollary}

\section{Why Rung 1 is weak, and what could be stronger}
The extremal circuits are easy: $C_n$ is solved in linear time by a local scan.  A genuine
hardness source must be an instance family where \emph{every} wire is irredundant
\emph{and} no local certificate exists.  Candidate directions: (i) force sharing (fan-out
$\ge2$) so wires cannot be probed locally; (ii) require 1-minimality under a stronger
weakening set (set \emph{pairs} of wires to 1, ``$k$-minimal'') and track $g_{\min}(n,k)$ --
conjecture growth in $k$; (iii) parametrize by $k$ and ask whether a probe/time bound of
the form $\Omega(n^{k})$ or $2^{\Omega(k)}$ appears.  Known upper-bound frame: brute force
$2^n s$, treewidth DP $2^{O(t)}s$.  Conditional (not proved here): SETH-type lower bounds for
$n$-variable \SAT.
