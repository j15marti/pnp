\section{Goal, model, and plan}
\CSAT\ instance: a fan-in-2 circuit $C$ over basis $B_2$ with $n$ inputs, $g$ gates and
$m=2g$ internal wires (plus output). Question: $\exists x\; C(x)=1$.
Plan: (i) fix the simplest circuits; (ii) track the minimum number of gates/wires needed to
build each function as $n$ grows; (iii) pair this with lower bounds for \emph{deciding} satisfiability,
in explicit models, with parameters $(n,m)$; (iv) look for parameterized bounds such as
$T(n,m)\ge f(n)\cdot m^{c}$.

\begin{lemma}[Connectivity, PROVED/EXACT]\label{lem:conn}
A fan-in-2 circuit whose output depends on $k$ inputs has at least $k-1$ gates and hence $2(k-1)$ wires.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof} The subcircuit of the output's dependence cone is a connected DAG with $k$ source inputs; each gate merges at most 2 components, so $k-1$ merges are required. \end{proof}
The table meets this for all-input-dependent symmetric functions at $n\le3$ with slack (e.g.\ MAJ$_3$ needs 4 vs.\ bound 2): the gap $C(f)-(n-1)$ is the \emph{non-tree overhead}; tracking it per $n$ is the proposed measure of "wire cost beyond connectivity".

\section{Decision lower bounds in explicit models}
\begin{definition}[Wire-query model] The algorithm sees $n$, $m$ and may query, for any wire index, its source gate/type. It must decide satisfiability.\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Linear wire bound, PROVED/EXACT]\label{thm:wire}
For every $n\ge1$ and $m\ge 2$ even there is a satisfiable $m$-wire, $n$-input circuit $C_{n,m}$ such that every deterministic wire-query algorithm correct on all circuits of this size must query at least $m$ wires on $C_{n,m}$ (or on a one-wire modification of it). So $T\ge m$, independent of $n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof} $C_{n,m}$: an AND chain $g_1=x_1\wedge x_2$, $g_k=g_{k-1}\wedge x_{1+(k\bmod n)}$, $k\le m/2$. Satisfiable by $1^n$. Replacing any single wire by constant $0$ gives an unsatisfiable circuit, because the output is the conjunction of everything on the chain. If the algorithm misses wire $w$ on $C_{n,m}$, its transcript is identical on $C_{n,m}$ and on the circuit with $w\mapsto 0$, which have different answers. \end{proof}
\textit{Honest remark:} this is just input-reading; it shows the true difficulty lies in the $n$-dependence.

\begin{proposition}[Evaluation-oracle model, PROVED/EXACT]
If the algorithm may only \emph{evaluate} $C$ on chosen inputs (black box), $2^n$ evaluations are necessary and sufficient in the worst case (adversary answers 0 until the last untried input).\end{proposition}
Thus the two models give $\Omega(m)$ and $2^n$ with matching trivial upper bound $O(m2^n)$ for the combination. The central OPEN question for this document is whether
\[T(n,m)\;\ge\; m\cdot 2^{\epsilon n}\quad(\text{or } m\cdot n^{\omega(1)})\]
holds for any natural model strictly between wire-reading and black-box evaluation, e.g.\ \emph{structure-reading with $s$ bits of working memory}. This is the parameterized form.
