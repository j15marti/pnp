\section{Goal and method}
Track, for the smallest circuits, the number of wires $w(C)$ and gates $g(C)$ needed, as the input count $n$ grows, and ask what any solver \emph{must} look at. Bounds are parametrized by $(n,g,w)$ and by the access model.

\section{Model}
Circuits: fan-in-2 AND/OR gates, free negation on wires, $n$ inputs, one output; $w=2g$ gate-input wires. \emph{Wire-query model}: the solver gets the circuit by an oracle answering ``what feeds gate $i$, port $j$?'' (and gate type); cost = number of queries.

\section{Proved results (PROVED/EXACT)}
\begin{lemma}[Connectivity, step $n\to n+1$]
A circuit on which all $n$ inputs are essential has $g\ge n-1$ gates, $w\ge 2(n-1)$. Each new essential input adds at least one gate (two wires). 
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The gates and inputs form a DAG whose output reaches every input; fan-in 2 means a tree-like cover needs $\ge n-1$ gates (a connected graph on $n+g$ nodes with $2g$ edges into gates, $2g\ge n+g-1$ gives $g\ge n-1$).
\end{proof}
\begin{proposition}[Wire-query lower bound, PROVED]
Any deterministic algorithm deciding satisfiability of circuits with $g$ gates in the wire-query model needs $\Omega(g)$ queries (precisely $\ge g$ for the family below).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take the base circuit $C_0$: a chain computing $x_1\wedge\dots\wedge x_n$ padded with $g-n+1$ gates computing $\lnot$-pattern identities feeding the output through an AND with a gate $h_i$. For each unqueried port, an adversary answers consistently with $C_0$ (satisfiable); a single unqueried port $p$ can be rewired to constant-false (an input paired with its negation in an AND), making the circuit unsatisfiable while agreeing with every answer given. Hence an algorithm that leaves any of the $\Omega(g)$ relevant ports unqueried errs on one of two consistent circuits. $\square$
\end{proof}
This is a sensitivity bound for \emph{reading} the instance; it is not a bound on search over assignments, and it is matched by trivial linear-time reading.

\section{Where the real difficulty sits}
The wire-accounting lower bounds are about circuit \emph{size} and \emph{reading}. A SAT lower bound needs a bound on search, i.e.\ work beyond reading, as a function of $(n,g)$. Candidate parametrization: time $T(n,g)$ with $g\ge n$; known context (repository, not re-proved here): the L34/L35 alternation-trading barriers in \texttt{research/agents/claude/tport/} and the one-tape crossing-sequence notes in \texttt{research/agents/claude/onetape/} suggest any bound beyond $\Omega(g)$ for general machines runs into the Buss--Williams exponent barrier. The ledger row L13 and its README are the relevant prior work.
