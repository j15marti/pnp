# Exact affine hulls, reduced-product counterexamples, and expansion controls

Status: mathematical proofs and independently executable finite checks. No unrestricted SAT upper or lower bound is claimed. The one-parity result is a rederivation of existing project work, not a new discovery.

## 1. Affine hull of a 2-CNF relation

For a satisfiable 2-CNF H, every linear equation true of all its models is generated by its forced literals and its variable equalities or complemented equalities. There are no additional irreducible linear consequences.

Proof. The model set S of H is closed under coordinatewise majority: if a binary clause fails on the majority of three models, each of its literals fails on at least two models, so one of the three models falsifies both literals, a contradiction. Choose a model a and translate S'=S+a. Translation preserves majority and puts zero in S'. Thus S' is closed under coordinatewise AND, since maj(u,v,0)=u AND v. Its linear span V is also AND-closed because coordinatewise multiplication is bilinear over F_2:

$$
(\sum_i u_i)\mathbin{\&}(\sum_j v_j)
=\sum_{i,j}(u_i\mathbin{\&}v_j).
$$

A linear subspace V of F_2^n closed under coordinatewise AND is a Boolean subalgebra. Coordinates that are identically zero are fixed. Partition the remaining coordinates according to equality of their evaluation maps on V. Each equivalence-block indicator lies in V: first form the union of supports of a basis using u OR v = u+v+(u AND v), obtaining an identity on the active coordinates; then multiply vectors or their complements relative to this identity to separate the desired block from every other block. Hence V consists exactly of independent constant values on these blocks, with zero on inactive coordinates. Translate back by a to obtain forced literals and equality/complement relations. QED.

These consequences can be found by polynomially many 2-SAT tests. Obtain one model a. For each variable test whether its opposite value is possible. For each pair test whether the parity opposite to a_i XOR a_j is possible; that additional parity condition is itself 2-CNF. Gaussian elimination removes redundant equations. SCCs and reachability give a more direct implementation.

## 2. Why affine codimension one is exact

For any nonempty finite relation S, not just 2-CNF, and any affine hyperplane A={x:l(x)=b},

$$
S\cap A\ne\varnothing
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{aff}(S)\cap A\ne\varnothing.
$$

If S misses A, the linear functional l has the constant value 1-b on S and therefore on its entire affine hull. The reverse implication follows by inclusion. Combining this fact with Section 1 yields a polynomial decision procedure for 2-CNF plus one affine equation. This consequence was already present in prior project reports.

## 3. Minimal rank-two failure after full binary affine exchange

Use five variables x_1,...,x_5. Let H be the Horn prefix chain

$$
H=(\neg x_2\lor x_1)\land(\neg x_3\lor x_2)
\land(\neg x_4\lor x_3)\land(\neg x_5\lor x_4).
$$

Its six models, written in variable order, are

$$
00000,10000,11000,11100,11110,11111.
$$

Successive differences are the five unit vectors, so aff(H)=F_2^5. Let A impose

$$
x_2\oplus x_3\oplus x_5=1,
$$

$$
x_1\oplus x_4\oplus x_5=0.
$$

The pairs of left-hand-side values on H's models are, in the same order,

$$
(0,0),(0,1),(1,1),(0,1),(0,0),(1,1).
$$

The required pair (1,0) never occurs. Thus H AND A is UNSAT.

The three nonzero homogeneous row-space vectors of A have Hamming weights 3,3,4. Consequently A has no nontrivial unary or binary consequence: every projection onto at most two coordinates is full. Thus the reduced product that transfers the exact affine hull of H into A, and the complete 2-CNF abstraction of A into H, reaches a fixed point immediately. Both components remain satisfiable.

### Minimality

For affine codimension two, any nonzero affine consequence of width at most two can be added to H as 2-CNF. After this addition the remaining affine requirement has relative codimension at most one, where the affine-hull test is exact by Section 2. Therefore a failed fixed point of this kind requires all three nonzero row-space vectors to have weight at least three.

Each nonzero matrix column contributes exactly two to the sum of those three weights. The sum is therefore at most 2n, and at least 9. This forces n>=5. The example above achieves that lower bound.

This minimality concerns affine codimension two and the specified exact affine-hull/full-binary exchange. It is not a lower bound on arbitrary SAT algorithms. Failed-literal probing can solve this small chain example.

## 4. Disjoint parity blocks defeat bounded-coordinate exchange

Fix k>=1 and set t=k+1. Use disjoint blocks p_1,...,p_t and q_1,...,q_t. Define

$$
H=\bigwedge_{i,j}(\neg p_i\lor\neg q_j),
$$

and let A assert odd parity in both blocks:

$$
\bigoplus_i p_i=1,\qquad\bigoplus_j q_j=1.
$$

H allows nonzero bits in at most one block. Its support contains zero and all 2t unit vectors, so its affine hull is full. A requires at least one nonzero bit in both blocks, so H AND A is UNSAT.

Every projection of A onto at most k coordinates is full: at least one bit remains unassigned in each t-bit parity block, and these two remaining freedoms independently satisfy the two equations. Therefore exchanging aff(H) with all exact A projections of arity at most k makes no refinement. For k=2 this gives a six-variable example with two disjoint ternary XORs and nine Horn clauses.

This does not defeat arbitrary contextual conditioning. For example, probing p_i=1 forces all q bits to zero through H and is then contradicted by A. A failed-literal algorithm resolves these easy examples. The obstruction applies specifically to separate exact affine-H / bounded-coordinate-A exchange.

## 5. Connected easy-SAT exponential monomial expansion

Use one common anchor a and m selector triples (p_i,q_i,r_i). Let H contain the three within-triple pairwise exclusions and each selector's implication to a:

$$
(\neg p_i\lor\neg q_i),\ (\neg p_i\lor\neg r_i),\ (\neg q_i\lor\neg r_i),
$$

$$
(\neg p_i\lor a),\ (\neg q_i\lor a),\ (\neg r_i\lor a).
$$

Let A impose p_i XOR q_i XOR r_i=1 for every i. In every solution a=1 and exactly one selector in each triple is 1, giving 3^m solutions. An explicit witness chooses every p_i and the anchor true and all q_i,r_i false.

This factor graph is connected through a single anchor. Removing a separates the m constant-size components. Its primal graph has a tree decomposition with bags {a,p_i,q_i,r_i}, so treewidth is at most three.

In the Boolean polynomial ring, the odd-parity indicator of a triple is

$$
p_i+q_i+r_i-2(p_iq_i+p_ir_i+q_ir_i)+4p_iq_ir_i.
$$

Modulo H's pairwise exclusions this reduces to p_i+q_i+r_i. Their product has exactly 3^m distinct monomials, choosing one selector from each triple. Horn closure adds the anchor to each nonempty monomial. The resulting 3^m closures remain distinct and all coefficients are +1, so no cancellation reduces this expansion. The same distinct-support argument applies in characteristic two.

Thus eager expansion into a flat map of closed monomials may be exponential on a connected, bounded-treewidth, trivially satisfiable family. This refutes a universal polynomial monomial-count claim for that representation or implementation path. It does not refute factored polynomial circuits, separator-based evaluation, adaptive witness search, or P=NP.

## 6. An UNSAT selector system that survives every singleton probe

The elementary examples above are solved by failed-literal probing. A stronger control uses the triangle-free Mycielski graph of the five-cycle.

### Graph and symbolic UNSAT certificate

Start with cycle vertices v_0,...,v_4. Add vertices u_0,...,u_4, with u_i adjacent to the two cycle neighbors of v_i. Add one vertex w adjacent to every u_i. The resulting graph has eleven vertices and twenty edges and is triangle-free.

Suppose it had a proper three-coloring. Rename colors so w has color 3. Every u_i then has color 1 or 2. In the original five-cycle, recolor any v_i of color 3 with the color of u_i. Adjacent cycle vertices cannot both originally have color 3. If v_i is recolored and v_j is its cycle neighbor, the edge u_i-v_j guarantees the new color differs from v_j's color. Thus this produces a proper two-coloring of the odd five-cycle, impossible. The graph has no three-coloring.

### Selector encoding

For each vertex v use three bits x_(v,1), x_(v,2), x_(v,3). H contains all within-vertex pairwise exclusions and, for each graph edge, the three same-color pairwise exclusions. These are ninety-three Horn 2-clauses. A contains eleven variable-disjoint odd ternary parity equations. Their conjunction requires exactly one color per vertex and a proper coloring, and therefore is UNSAT.

At the unconditioned reduced-product fixed point, H has the zero model and every unit-vector model, so its affine hull is full. A's unary and binary projections are full. No information transfers.

### Why every one-bit conditional fixed point remains consistent

This statement holds for every triangle-free graph in this selector encoding, irrespective of whether it is three-colorable.

First pin x_(v,c)=0. Gaussian/binary exchange gives exactly this fixed zero and the disequality between the two remaining color bits at v. Call this affine relation E. The 2-CNF side restricted by E has affine hull exactly E: either remaining color can be selected at v with every other vertex's bits zero; for any coordinate at another vertex, one can set it to one and choose a compatible remaining color at v. Toggling that external coordinate back to zero produces its unit direction in the affine hull. No additional linear relation survives. A AND E is consistent, and A has no additional nontrivial binary consequence outside E. Hence the reduced product is at a consistent fixed point.

Next pin x_(v,c)=1. Let E fix all three bits at v and, at every neighbor of v, fix color c to zero and assert disequality between its other two color bits. Because the graph is triangle-free, the neighbors of v form an independent set. Every combination of their two available colors, with every other vertex uncolored, is an H-model satisfying E. These models span all of E's local freedoms.

For any color coordinate at a vertex outside the closed neighborhood of v, set that coordinate to one. Every adjacent neighbor of v can independently choose one of its two available colors different from this selected color. Thus an H-model exists with the selected external coordinate one, and with it zero while all other coordinates remain the same. These differences supply every remaining unit direction. Therefore the exact affine hull of the refined H is E, with no further relations. Again A AND E is consistent and its complete binary abstraction is already implied by E. This is a consistent fixed point.

Thus all sixty-six pinned branches survive the full iterated exact affine-H / binary-A reduced product. No failed literal is derived, so iterating singleton exclusion adds no clause at the unconditioned state. This is a specific limitation of that local closure, not of arbitrary contextual reasoning or stronger lookahead.

### Independent computational validation

`verify_singleton_fixedpoint.py` implements implication-graph reachability, the exact 2-CNF affine hull, Gaussian elimination, all unary/binary affine consequences, and iteration to a fixed point. All sixty-six probes reach consistent fixed points after two rounds. A separate graph-coloring backtracking search visits 154 nodes and finds zero three-colorings. The graph proof above independently certifies UNSAT.

The exact mixed instance is saved in `singleton_fixedpoint_instance.json`. `singleton_fixedpoint_validation.json` records every probe and its ranks, clause count, round count, script hash, and runtime. `mycielski_selector_33.cnf` is an equivalent ordinary CNF with 33 variables and 137 clauses, using four 3-clauses for each odd parity condition.
