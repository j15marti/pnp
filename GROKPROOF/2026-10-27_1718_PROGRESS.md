## Literature Review
Reconfirmed Fortnow–Lipton–van Melkebeek–Viglas (JACM 2005): for any c < φ (golden ratio ≈1.618), there exists d > 0 such that SAT ∉ DTISP(N^{c}, N^{d}) on random-access Turing machines; the forbidden region is characterized by s < β(t) with β(t) = ((t+2)/t² - t)/2. Williams (2007/2008) improves the subpolynomial-space endpoint to ≈1.8019 = 2 cos(π/7). Buss–Williams establish optimality of that endpoint within alternation-trading proofs. No literature provides a universal, fully charged, correctness-preserving all-input transformation from an arbitrary polynomial-time SAT solver into a portfolio of ordinary small SAT instances meeting the required budgets.
