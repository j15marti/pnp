\section{Known Unconditional Lower Bounds}
The strongest unconditional circuit lower bounds for explicit functions remain modest relative to the thresholds needed to separate P from NP.

\begin{itemize}
\item For general Boolean circuits, the best known lower bound for an explicit function in NP is $5n - o(n)$ (Iwama--Morizumi--Raz and subsequent constant improvements).
\item Shannon's counting argument yields existence of functions requiring $\Omega(2^n/n)$ gates, but these are non-constructive.
\item For AC$^0$, H{\aa}stad's switching lemma gives $2^{\Omega(n^{1/(d-1)})}$ size lower bounds for parity and majority. Recent work (OpenAI 2026, Chukhin 2026, and follow-ups including short point-list refinements) improved depth-$d$ bounds for explicit polynomial-time functions to $2^{\Omega_d((n\log n)^{1/(d-1)})}$ against depth-$d$ AC$^0$-style circuits, with further refinements for specific functions such as inverse trace, Goldreich--Tal, and polynomial evaluators.
\item For ACC$^0$, Williams (2011/2014) proved NEXP $\not\subseteq$ ACC$^0$; Murray--Williams pushed this to NQP $\not\subseteq$ ACC$^0$. Subsequent refinements (Chen et al.) improved size parameters (including sub-half-exponential for NEXP) and average-case hardness for related constant-depth classes with modular gates. Almost-everywhere strengthenings exist for $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ against ACC$^0$ approximation. Extensions to GC$^0$ and related classes yield matching or improved exponential bounds for majority and separations from quantum circuit classes. $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ requires exponential-size GCC$^0$ circuits under certain parameters.
\item Formula size lower bounds and monotone circuit lower bounds (Razborov, Andreev, Alon--Boppana) exist for specific functions, but do not yet resolve P versus NP. Exponential monotone lower bounds for clique do not transfer to general circuits. Conditional results under NSETH or Orthogonal Vectors conjecture yield stronger monotone or ETHR$\circ$ETHR lower bounds.
\end{itemize}

These results establish that superpolynomial circuit lower bounds for NP would imply P $\neq$ NP, yet current techniques fall short of this threshold for general circuits. Superpolynomial lower bounds remain open even for explicit functions in NP against general circuits. The algorithmic method continues to be the primary source of new ACC$^0$-style separations, including almost-everywhere variants.

\section{Asymptotic Hierarchy Theorems}
Time and circuit hierarchy theorems supply asymptotic separations that do not resolve P versus NP.

\begin{theorem}[Time hierarchy]
For any time-constructible $T(n) \ge n$, there exist languages in TIME$(T(n)^2)$ that are not in TIME$(T(n))$. The diagonalization languages lie outside NP.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Paul--Pippenger--Szemer\'edi--Trotter, 1983]
DTIME$(n) \subsetneq$ NTIME$(n)$ for multi-tape Turing machines. Extensions separate slightly superlinear bounds under additional separator assumptions, but the result does not scale to polynomial time. No superpolynomial separation between DTIME and NTIME at polynomial scales is known.
\end{theorem}

Circuit hierarchy theorems similarly yield asymptotic separations for non-uniform complexity. For every fixed $k$ there exists a language in $\Sigma_2^P \cap \Pi_2^P$ requiring circuits of size $\Omega(n^k)$ (Kannan's theorem). Subsequent refinements place such fixed-polynomial lower bounds in ZPP$^{\rm NP}$ and $\rm S_2^P$. The non-uniform hierarchy and Karp--Lipton collapse implications prevent direct transfer of these bounds to NP without additional assumptions: if NP $\subseteq$ P/poly then the polynomial hierarchy collapses to $\Sigma_2^P$.

Hardness magnification results (Oliveira--Santhanam, Chen--Jin--Williams, and subsequent work) show that modest improvements on existing lower bounds for certain gap versions of MCSP or MKtP would imply superpolynomial lower bounds for NP or EXP. Current magnification thresholds remain above known unconditional bounds for these meta-complexity problems. Recent cryptographic evidence (Mazor--Pass 2024/2026) shows that appropriate Gap-MCSP variants are not NP-hard under Levin reductions assuming iO and subexponentially secure one-way functions. This does not rule out magnification but constrains the form of any hardness that could amplify. Consistency results in bounded arithmetic (Atserias--Buss--M\"uller, Thapen 2025) show that stronger NEXP lower bounds (including NEXP $\not\subseteq$ EXP/poly and NEXP $\not\subseteq$ coNEXP) remain consistent with strong theories of arithmetic such as $V_2^0$ strengthened by true universal number-sort sentences.

\section{Barriers}
Three principal barriers constrain proof techniques.

\begin{theorem}[Baker--Gill--Solovay, 1975]
There exist oracles $A,B$ such that P$^A$ = NP$^A$ and P$^B$ $\neq$ NP$^B$. Consequently, relativizing techniques cannot resolve P versus NP.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Razborov--Rudich, 1997]
Natural proofs cannot establish superpolynomial circuit lower bounds against circuit classes that can compute pseudorandom generators secure against the corresponding circuit class. This applies in particular to P/poly under standard cryptographic assumptions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Aaronson--Wigderson, 2008]
Algebrization further restricts arithmetization-based techniques. Almost all major open problems, including P versus NP and P versus RP, require non-algebrizing methods. Recent work strengthens algebrization barriers for certain MA$_E$ subclasses and PostBPE.
\end{theorem}

Williams' algorithmic method (satisfiability algorithms implying circuit lower bounds) and Mulmuley's geometric complexity theory evade some of these barriers in restricted settings but have not yet produced a superpolynomial lower bound for an NP-complete problem against general circuits. The method has been extended to NQP, almost-everywhere hardness, and improved size-depth tradeoffs for ACC$^0$ and related classes.

\section{Dynamic and Reverse Dynamic Programming Approaches}
Dynamic programming (DP) provides a systematic method for solving satisfiability problems by decomposing them into overlapping subproblems. For Circuit-SAT and its reduction to 3-SAT, the standard many-one reduction introduces one auxiliary variable per gate together with a constant number of 3-clauses enforcing local consistency of the gate function. The resulting 3-CNF has size linear in the circuit size; any asymptotic improvement for 3-SAT therefore transfers (up to the linear blow-up) to Circuit-SAT, and vice versa under the usual polynomial-time reductions.

Known exact algorithms for $k$-SAT improve upon brute-force $2^n$ by factors of the form $2^{n(1-1/O(k))}$ (PPZ, PPSZ and subsequent refinements). Under the Exponential Time Hypothesis these improvements are optimal up to the precise constant in the exponent; a $2^{o(n)}$ algorithm for 3-SAT would already refute ETH and, via the Circuit-SAT reduction, yield a non-trivial algorithm for general Circuit-SAT. The latter, by Williams' algorithmic method, would imply NEXP $\not\subseteq$ P/poly. No such algorithm is known.

Dynamic programming yields polynomial-time algorithms precisely when a structural width parameter is bounded. For a CNF whose incidence graph has treewidth $w$, DP along a tree decomposition solves SAT (and \#SAT) in time $2^{O(w)}\mathrm{poly}(n)$. Analogous results hold for modular treewidth, cliquewidth and related parameters. Williams' algorithm for ACC$^0$-SAT proceeds by first converting the circuit to a quasi-polynomial-size SYM$^+$ circuit and then applying a DP (zeta-transform) algorithm over the subset lattice of the input variables; the resulting time is $2^{n-n^{\Omega(1)}}$ on polynomial-size ACC$^0$ circuits, which is already sufficient for the NEXP $\not\subseteq$ ACC$^0$ separation. The same technique does not extend to general circuits of polynomial size, for which the best known Circuit-SAT algorithms remain of the form $2^{n-o(n)}$ only under additional structural restrictions.

Reverse (or backward) dynamic programming corresponds to reasoning from the output gate toward the inputs, or, in the reversible-computation model, to evaluating the inverse of a bijective circuit. Reversible Circuit-SAT remains NP-complete for any universal reversible gate set (with suitable initialization and finalization alphabets). While reversible synthesis techniques can produce circuits whose forward and backward evaluation are both efficient for special families, no general reverse-DP procedure is known that improves the exponential dependence on the number of free variables for arbitrary Circuit-SAT or 3-SAT instances. In particular, the existence of a polynomial-time reverse-DP algorithm for either problem would place both in P and collapse NP to P; no evidence for such an algorithm appears in the literature, and the standard barriers (relativization, algebrization, natural proofs) continue to apply.

In the P = NP universe such a DP or reverse-DP algorithm must exist, yet the known width-based and ACC$^0$-based procedures remain exponentially bounded in the unrestricted case and therefore supply no contradiction. In the P $\neq$ NP universe the same procedures are consistent with the Exponential Time Hypothesis and with the failure of superpolynomial circuit lower bounds for NP. Thus dynamic and reverse dynamic programming currently furnish useful algorithms for restricted circuit classes and for formulas of small width, but do not yet produce an asymptotic forcing of either resolution of P versus NP.

\section{Consistency Across Universes}
\subsection{P = NP Universe}
If P = NP, then the polynomial hierarchy collapses to P, and BPP = P by the Sipser--G{\'a}cs--Lautemann theorem (BPP $\subseteq$ PH). Cryptographic one-way functions cannot exist, and many average-case hardness assumptions fail. No contradiction with known unconditional theorems is immediate; the principal tension is with the widespread belief in one-way functions and the existence of secure cryptography. The Paul--Pippenger--Szemer{\'e}di--Trotter linear-time separation remains consistent, as does Kannan's fixed-polynomial circuit lower bounds in the second level of the hierarchy (which collapses). Hardness magnification becomes vacuous under collapse. Repository acquisition bounds of order $\Omega(N/\log N)$ remain compatible, as they are model-restricted and do not force superpolynomial unrestricted time. Charged-transport ceilings with nonpositive remainders after linear construction costs are likewise compatible. Dynamic and reverse-DP procedures remain consistent, as any poly-time algorithm would simply be one of the (as yet unknown) algorithms that exist under the collapse.

\subsection{P $\neq$ NP Universe}
This is consistent with all known unconditional results. It permits one-way functions, secure cryptography, and non-collapse of the polynomial hierarchy. Circuit lower bounds for NP remain open even under this assumption. The repository's $\Omega(N/\log N)$ acquisition bounds in restricted models are compatible with either resolution of the unrestricted Turing-machine question. Recent ACC$^0$ lower bounds for NEXP/NQP (including almost-everywhere variants) remain valid. No asymptotic forcing of separation arises from current hierarchy or circuit results. Thapen's consistency results for stronger NEXP separations are compatible. Width-bounded DP and the ACC$^0$-SAT algorithm are likewise compatible.

\subsection{P = BPP Universe}
Most experts conjecture P = BPP. This is implied by the existence of sufficiently hard functions in E (Impagliazzo--Wigderson: if E requires exponential circuits then P = BPP). Polynomial identity testing remains the principal problem known to be in BPP but not known to be in P. Relative to a random oracle, P = BPP holds with probability 1. No contradiction arises. The result is consistent with both P = NP and P $\neq$ NP.

\subsection{RP = P, ZPP = P, UP = P}
These equalities are likewise consistent with current knowledge. UP = P would imply that NP has unambiguous witnesses that can be found in polynomial time, collapsing certain cryptographic constructions based on unique solutions, but no formal contradiction is known. Relative to random oracles, RP = ZPP = P holds.

\subsection{MA = AM}
Under standard assumptions this holds; the classes are believed equal. No known separation or contradiction. Interactive proof characterizations place AM inside the polynomial hierarchy, consistent with both collapses and non-collapses. Recent MA$^{\rm ACC^0}$ simulations do not force equality or separation.

In all examined universes, the principal open questions (superpolynomial circuit lower bounds for NP, explicit pseudorandom generators secure against P/poly, etc.) remain unresolved. No asymptotic contradiction forcing one resolution over the other has been identified from the available literature or the repository's verified results. Conditional hardness under NSETH or Orthogonal Vectors conjecture yields stronger restricted-circuit bounds but does not resolve the unrestricted question. The repository's exact acquisition and charged-transport results supply no residual positive remainder that would force a separation in any of these universes.

\section{Relation to Repository Findings}
The concurrent LSPV/RMV investigations establish exact acquisition bounds of order $\Omega(N/\log N)$ for specific orientation-field encodings and demonstrate that numerous candidate amplifiers fail to produce superlinear transport remainders under charged reductions. These include:

\begin{itemize}
\item Fixed-width chain encodings force $\Omega(q)$ orientation queries on all-one inputs, matched by linear scans.
\item Dense couplings, treewidth, density, padding, and essential constraints do not force superlinear continuation costs.
\item Independent products and guarded aggregation preserve only input-order acquisition.
\item Shared-interface tables, relation-algebra boundaries, exception kernels, and selector graphs yield exact bounds matched by linear or near-linear procedures.
\item Backdoor products (BACKDOOR-PRODUCT-FIREWALL97): linear minimum strong syntactic 2-CNF backdoors ($h = \Theta(N/\log N)$) still admit $O(pq)$ charged recognizers; transport remainders are nonpositive.
\item Cubic affine / Tseitin-like formulas (CUBIC-AFFINE-FIREWALL98): exponential regular-resolution size on high-treewidth instances coexists with linear charge deciders; transport from proof length to arbitrary decision time is falsified absent a new simulation theorem.
\item Degree-boundary and Horn-selector families (DEGREE-BOUNDARY96, HORN-TRANSPORT-CEILING100): direct inclusion preserves all-decider acquisition bounds of order $\Theta(N/\log N)$ while matching linear recognizers; paid materialization yields nonpositive transport numerators.
\item Krom projections and related affine bulk plus exceptions admit linear or near-linear decision procedures despite large syntactic measures.
\end{itemize}

The charged transport inequality requires a positive numerator after subtracting construction and recognition costs. Linear construction or recognition consistently consumes the linear chain/direct-sum bound. Direct inclusion preserves the same query order but supplies no amplification. These results are \PROVED{} in their declared models and are consistent with the broader literature: restricted models admit linear or near-linear decision procedures even when syntactic measures appear large. They do not resolve the unrestricted Turing-machine question. No residual positive-remainder candidate surviving linear construction costs has been identified in the current corpus. All such bounds remain compatible with both P = NP and P $\neq$ NP.

\item Examine whether hardness magnification thresholds for Gap-MCSP or related meta-complexity problems can be reduced below current known bounds, and reconcile with cryptographic non-hardness evidence under Levin reductions (Mazor--Pass).
