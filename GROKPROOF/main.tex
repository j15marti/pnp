\section{Known Unconditional Lower Bounds}
The strongest unconditional circuit lower bounds for explicit functions remain modest relative to the thresholds needed to separate P from NP.

\begin{itemize}
\item For general Boolean circuits over the $B_2$ basis, the best known lower bound for an explicit function in P is $3.5n - O(\log n)$ (Mishra--Santhanam, ECCC TR26-260, October 2026), improving the previous $3.1n-o(n)$ of Li--Yang. The proof uses refined gate elimination with reparameterisation on an underlying independent parameter space, applied to affine dispersers of dimension $O(\log n)$. For the $U_2$ basis the best remains $5n-o(n)$. No subsequent improvements to general-circuit bounds for explicit functions in P have appeared through ECCC TR26-271 (23 October 2026; latest available as of this update) or adjacent reports such as TR26-268 (linear query lower bounds for bounded-degree graph-isomorphism testing).
\item Shannon's counting argument yields existence of functions requiring $\Omega(2^n/n)$ gates, but these are non-constructive.
\item For AC$^0$, H{\aa}stad's switching lemma gives $2^{\Omega(n^{1/(d-1)})}$ size lower bounds for parity and majority. Recent work (OpenAI 2026, Chukhin 2026 ECCC TR26-248, Li--Zhong ECCC TR26-261) improved depth-$d$ bounds for explicit polynomial-time functions to $2^{\Omega_d((n\log n)^{1/(d-1)})}$ against depth-$d$ AC$^0$-style circuits, breaking the classical parity barrier at every constant depth. Valiant's connection implies that a sufficiently strong depth-3 AC$^0$ lower bound of $2^{\omega(n/\log\log n)}$ would yield the first superlinear lower bound for logarithmic-depth general circuits.
\item For ACC$^0$, Williams (2011/2014) proved NEXP $\not\subseteq$ ACC$^0$; Murray--Williams pushed this to NQP $\not\subseteq$ ACC$^0$. Subsequent refinements improved size parameters and almost-everywhere strengthenings exist for $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ against ACC$^0$ approximation. Near-maximum $2^n/n$ bounds hold for $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{prMA}}/1$ (Ren--Williams, arXiv:2607.09963 / ECCC TR26-118, 2026), via iterative win-win, range avoidance, and PCP.
\item Formula size lower bounds and monotone circuit lower bounds (Razborov, Andreev, Alon--Boppana) exist for specific functions, but do not yet resolve P versus NP. Exponential monotone lower bounds for clique do not transfer to general circuits. Explicit formula lower bounds of $n^{3-o(1)}$ (H{\aa}stad) remain below thresholds that would force superpolynomial general-circuit separations via known reductions.
\end{itemize}

These results establish that superpolynomial circuit lower bounds for NP would imply P $\neq$ NP, yet current techniques fall short of this threshold for general circuits. The new $3.5n$ bound remains linear and does not yet force a separation. Superpolynomial lower bounds remain open even for explicit functions in NP against general circuits. The algorithmic method continues to be the primary source of new ACC$^0$-style separations. Recent range-avoidance characterizations (ECCC TR26-270) identify rich fragments of prAM whose derandomization reduces to Avoid, containing prMA, GNI, and certain GapSVP complements, but do not yield new general-circuit lower bounds.

\section{Asymptotic Hierarchy Theorems and Gaps}
Hierarchy theorems provide unconditional asymptotic separations within resource classes but do not resolve cross-resource comparisons such as deterministic versus nondeterministic time.

\begin{itemize}
\item Time hierarchy: If $t$ is time-constructible and $r(n)\log r(n)=o(t(n))$, then $\mathrm{DTIME}(r(n))\subsetneq\mathrm{DTIME}(t(n))$. Consequently P $\subsetneq$ EXP.
\item Nondeterministic time hierarchy: If $t$ is time-constructible and $r(n+1)=o(t(n))$, then $\mathrm{NTIME}(r(n))\subsetneq\mathrm{NTIME}(t(n))$. Consequently NP $\subsetneq$ NEXP.
\item Space hierarchy: If $s$ is space-constructible and $r(n)=o(s(n))$, then $\mathrm{DSPACE}(r(n))\subsetneq\mathrm{DSPACE}(s(n))$. Consequently L $\subsetneq$ PSPACE and PSPACE $\subsetneq$ EXPSPACE.
\item Circuit size hierarchy: SIZE$(s(n))\subsetneq$ SIZE$(s'(n))$ for appropriate $s,s'$ below the Shannon bound.
\end{itemize}

These establish that resource increases yield strictly more power asymptotically, yet leave open whether P = NP, P = PSPACE, or NP = coNP. The gap between known linear circuit lower bounds ($\approx 3.5n$) and the superpolynomial thresholds required for P $\neq$ NP remains vast. Magnification results (Atserias--M\"uller, arXiv:2503.24061) show that slightly superlinear lower bounds (of the form $n^{1+\epsilon}$ or $n^{2\epsilon}$ for $\epsilon>0$) for approximating sufficiently sparse problems in NP would imply fixed-polynomial formula lower bounds for NP (hence NP $\not\subseteq$ FML$[n^{O(1)}]$). 

Formally, for every fixed $\epsilon>0$,
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{3.5n-O(\log n)}{n^{1+\epsilon}}=0.
\]
Thus the best explicit general-circuit bound is asymptotically negligible relative to any fixed magnification threshold. No amplification from linear to $n^{1+\epsilon}$ has been obtained. No asymptotic forcing of a separation has been obtained from the hierarchy theorems alone. Recent work equates the average-case versus worst-case gap for PH with certain meta-complexity implications for gap versions of MINpKT (Goldberg et al., ECCC TR26-253), but this does not close the deterministic time gap. Optimal nondeterministic communication lower bounds for graph connectivity (Zhang, ECCC TR26-269) and improved polynomial approximation bounds for discrete logarithm (Kopparty et al., ECCC TR26-271) likewise do not transfer to general Boolean circuit separations.

The repository's charged positive-remainder transport inequality and adaptive-trajectory analyses further confirm that linear acquisition bounds (e.g., $\Theta(N/\log N)$ orientation-field reads) are consumed by construction costs or preserved only at input order under direct inclusion, yielding no amplification to superlinear or superpolynomial regimes. Quantitative accounting shows that paid construction terms of order $O(q)$ or $\Theta(N\log N)$ strictly dominate residual linear acquisition, keeping the numerator of the transport inequality non-positive for the examined families.

\section{Barriers and Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item Relativization (Baker--Gill--Solovay): There exist oracles $A,B$ with $\mathrm{P}^A=\mathrm{NP}^A$ and $\mathrm{P}^B\neq\mathrm{NP}^B$. Diagonalization alone cannot resolve P versus NP.
\item Algebrization (Aaronson--Wigderson): Extends relativization to algebraic oracles; many proof techniques fail to resolve even NEXP versus P/poly under algebrization.
\item Natural proofs (Razborov--Rudich): If one-way functions exist, natural proofs cannot prove superpolynomial circuit lower bounds for NP.
\item Localization and other barriers constrain magnification and average-case hardness transfers.
\end{itemize}

These barriers do not prove independence but severely restrict the applicability of standard techniques. Non-relativizing, non-algebrizing, non-natural methods (such as the algorithmic method of Williams) have produced the strongest known separations outside NP.

\section{Multi-Universe Consistency Checks}
No known theorem forces a contradiction in any of the following plausible universes, nor does any force the opposite. Asymptotic arguments from hierarchies and known lower bounds remain compatible with each:

\begin{itemize}
\item P = NP: Consistent with current knowledge; would collapse PH to P, place all NP-complete problems in P, and imply derandomization of BPP under standard assumptions, but no absolute contradiction with hierarchy theorems or the $3.5n$ circuit bounds. The repository ceilings on transport and adaptive trajectories do not produce a contradiction under this assumption, as the charged linear costs are compatible with polynomial-time decision. Karp--Lipton implies a collapse of PH, but this is not an unconditional contradiction. Range-avoidance reductions for AM fragments remain compatible.
\item P $\neq$ NP: Consistent; supported by the absence of polynomial algorithms for thousands of NP-complete problems and by the existence of NP-intermediate candidates (Ladner), but not forced by any unconditional lower bound or asymptotic gap closure. The linear-to-superlinear gap remains open.
\item RP = P, BPP = P, ZPP = P: Believed under hardness assumptions (Impagliazzo--Wigderson, etc.); no unconditional separation or equality is known. Hierarchy results for randomized classes exist under certain conditions but do not resolve the deterministic case.
\item P = UP: Open; related to unique solutions and cryptography. No forcing contradiction.
\item MA = AM: Open; related to interactive proofs and derandomization. Consistent with both equality and separation under current bounds. Recent characterizations of AM fragments via range avoidance (TR26-270) and input-oblivious classes (Hirsch--Volkovich--Williams, ECCC TR26-250) provide further containments but no collapse.
\item NEXP $\not\subseteq$ P/poly or stronger: Partially supported by Williams-style results for restricted circuit classes, but open for general circuits. Near-maximum bounds for $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{prMA}}/1$ do not transfer to P/poly separations.
\end{itemize}

In each case the literature provides neither an absolute asymptotic lower-bound contradiction nor a collapse that is forced independently of assumptions. The repository's LSPV/RMV investigations (firewall, transport, degree-boundary ceilings, selector-graph, Horn-transport) likewise identify no load-bearing implication that closes the gap under either assumption. Average-case hardness results and meta-complexity equivalences likewise remain compatible with both resolutions. The asymptotic evidence continues to point toward the persistence of the linear-versus-superpolynomial gap rather than its closure. The ratio of the best explicit linear bound to any fixed superlinear magnification threshold tends to zero, reinforcing the distance to a forcing argument.

\section{Relation to Concurrent Repository Investigations}
The adaptive-trajectory firewall (ADAPTIVE-TRAJECTORY-FIREWALL107), backdoor-product, selector-graph, Horn-transport, and charged-transport analyses in this repository establish ceilings on certain structured reductions and quantifier transports. These results show that initial syntactic measures (e.g., strong 2-CNF backdoors of size $q+1$) collapse under paid adaptive discovery and propagation to zero residual in linear word time, with SAT/UNSAT asymmetry handled by SCC/witness replay or conflict detection. Degree-boundary and charged-transport ceilings similarly remain open arrows: the positive-remainder inequality
\[
T_{\rm new}^{\le}(g(q))\ge\frac{L_{\rm old}(q)-R(q)-H(q)}{Q(q)\tau(q)}
\]
requires a positive numerator, and linear construction costs consume acquisition bounds of order $\Theta(N/\log N)$. These are compatible with both P = NP and P $\neq$ NP; they constrain the form of any potential polynomial algorithm or lower-bound proof but do not yet produce an asymptotic separation.

\section*{Conclusion}
The evidence from hierarchy theorems, circuit lower bounds, magnification thresholds, and barrier results points consistently toward the difficulty of proving either equality or separation by currently available asymptotic techniques. The quantitative gap between the best explicit general-circuit lower bound ($3.5n-O(\log n)$) and the thresholds that would resolve P versus NP (superpolynomial, or even the modest slightly superlinear thresholds of the form $n^{1+\epsilon}$ from magnification) remains substantial and unclosed; the ratio vanishes for every fixed $\epsilon>0$. No contradiction has been identified that forces one resolution in the universes examined. P versus NP remains open.
