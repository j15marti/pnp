\section{Known Unconditional Lower Bounds}
The strongest unconditional circuit lower bounds for explicit functions remain modest relative to the thresholds needed to separate P from NP.

\begin{itemize}
\item For general Boolean circuits over the $B_2$ basis, the best known lower bound for an explicit function in P is $3.5n - O(\log n)$ (Mishra--Santhanam, ECCC TR26-260, October 2026).
\end{itemize}

\section{Time-Space Tradeoffs}
\KNOWN\ (Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas, JACM 2005; Williams, CCC 2007 / TOCT 2013).  
In the indexed read-only-input random-access model, SAT cannot be decided simultaneously in time $N^{t+o(1)}$ and space $N^{s+o(1)}$ whenever
\[
s < \beta(t) := \frac12\Bigl(\frac{t+2}{t^2}-t\Bigr)
\]
for the applicable range of $t$, and in particular cannot be decided in time $N^{2\cos(\pi/7)-o(1)}$ and space $N^{o(1)}$ (Williams). These are the strongest published simultaneous bounds used in the present investigation. Numerical evaluation confirms $2\cos(\pi/7)\approx 1.8019$. Buss--Williams established that this endpoint is optimal within the framework of alternation-trading proofs for the subpolynomial-space case.

\section{Conditional Resource Contraction}
Under the temporary assumption that a fixed uniform deterministic total correct SAT solver $A$ exists with
\[
T_A(N)\le N^{K+o(1)},\qquad S_A(N)\le N^{L+o(1)}
\]
for unknown finite $K,L\ge 2$, the parameters $\lambda=1/(5K)$, $\zeta=0.1$ would, if an all-input transformation of the stated budget existed, yield a simultaneous profile
\[
t'\le 1.3-\frac1{5K},\qquad s'\le 0.2
\]
that lies strictly inside the FLMV forbidden region for every finite $K\ge 2$. Independent numerical verification confirms the membership (e.g., for $K=2$, $t'=1.2$, $\beta(1.2)\approx 0.511 > 0.2$; for $K=5$, $t'\approx 1.260$, $\beta\approx 0.397>0.2$; for large $K$, $t'\to 1.3$ and $\beta(1.3)\approx 0.326 > 0.2$). The recurrence
\[
t'\le\max(1.1,0.6+t/2),\qquad s'\le\max(0.5,s/2)
\]
has fixed point $(1.2,0.5)$ satisfying the tangent certificate $433t+216s<630$.  

\PROVED. Classical self-reducibility of any correct total SAT decider $A$ with $T_A(N)\le N^{K+o(1)}$ issues $\Theta(n)$ parent-sized queries and therefore incurs aggregate time $N^{1+K+o(1)}$. This exceeds the local-work budget $N^{1.1+o(1)}$ for every finite $K\ge 2$. Hence classical self-reducibility cannot realize the required contraction.

\OPEN. The required universal all-input construction of $\le N^{1-\lambda+\zeta+o(1)}$ ordinary SAT children of length $\le N^{\lambda+o(1)}$, with aggregate local work $\le N^{1.1+o(1)}$ and live parent space $\le N^{0.2+o(1)}$, including every discovery, correctness, access, memory and combination cost, remains unproved. Numerical membership alone does not establish a contradiction. Primary literature supplies no such universal fully charged transformation. Exact-(2,2)-CNF-3 is NP-complete, while Horn and 2-SAT are in P and may serve as restricted test cases. Reconfirmed 2026-10-31 22:15 PDT: no new primary source closes the transformation.

\RESTRICTED. On Horn and 2-SAT instances, polynomial-time algorithms exist independently of any hypothetical solver $A$. Variable-block self-reduction (partition into blocks of size $N^\lambda$, propagate, generate residual children) meets the size and work bounds using only these known algorithms; the residual after propagation is empty or of constant size. Thus restricted contractions are feasible in principle without black-box access to $A$, but this does not transfer to the NP-complete case.

\FALSIFIED\ (restricted model). Literal clause partitions fail on expander graphs. After Gaussian elimination of a Tseitin affine shell conjoined with an arbitrary non-affine core $Q(B)$, the surviving core retains an encoding length that can be $\Theta(N)$, forcing parent-sized queries to $A$ that exhaust the local-work budget $N^{1.1+o(1)}$.

\begin{remark}[Black-box Residual Discovery Barrier --- RESTRICTED]
Let $A$ be any correct total SAT decider with $T_A(N)\le N^{K+o(1)}$. On a linear-size expander exact-(2,2)-CNF-3 family, any adaptive controller that produces a nonempty proper residual child of encoding length $\le N^{\lambda+o(1)}$ must either submit at least one parent-sized query (cost $N^{K+o(1)}$) or maintain intermediate configuration or memoization state of size $\Omega(N^\delta)$ for some $\delta>0.2$. In either case the local-work budget $N^{1.1+o(1)}$ and live parent space $N^{0.2+o(1)}$ are exceeded for all finite $K\ge 2$. This holds for literal partitions and for residual cores after Tseitin elimination; the fully general adaptive case remains OPEN.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Source-Normalized Transfer --- CONDITIONAL]
An explicit physical child of size $N^{\alpha+o(1)}$ transfers target exponents $(t,s)$ to the simultaneous source pair $(\max\{r,\alpha t,h\},\max\{u,\alpha,\alpha s,v\})$. Succinct access removes the $\alpha$ storage floor only by paying a native access-time tax; the tax must be charged in the local budget. This accounting is exact for the stated model but does not by itself produce a forbidden profile without an explicit all-input constructor meeting the size and cost bounds.
\end{remark}

\section*{Conclusion}
The evidence from hierarchy theorems, circuit lower bounds, magnification thresholds, and barrier results points consistently toward the difficulty of proving either equality or separation by currently available asymptotic techniques. The quantitative gap between the best explicit general-circuit lower bound ($3.5n-O(\log n)$) and the thresholds that would resolve P versus NP remains substantial and unclosed. Conditional numerical contractions enter known forbidden time-space regions but do not establish the required universal transformation from an arbitrary polynomial-time SAT solver. No contradiction has been identified that forces one resolution in the universes examined. P versus NP remains open.
