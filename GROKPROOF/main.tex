\section{Known Unconditional Lower Bounds}
The strongest unconditional circuit lower bounds for explicit functions remain modest relative to the thresholds needed to separate P from NP.

\begin{itemize}
\item For general Boolean circuits, the best known lower bound for an explicit function in NP is $5n - o(n)$ (Iwama--Morizumi--Raz and subsequent constant improvements).
\item Shannon's counting argument yields existence of functions requiring $\Omega(2^n/n)$ gates, but these are non-constructive.
\item For AC$^0$, H\aa{}stad's switching lemma gives $2^{\Omega(n^{1/(d-1)})}$ size lower bounds for parity and majority.
\item For ACC$^0$, Williams (2011/2014) proved NEXP $\not\subseteq$ ACC$^0$; subsequent refinements strengthened size and depth parameters for related constant-depth classes with modular gates.
\item Formula size lower bounds and monotone circuit lower bounds (Razborov, Andreev, Alon--Boppana) exist for specific functions, but do not yet resolve P versus NP. Exponential monotone lower bounds for clique do not transfer to general circuits.
\end{itemize}

These results establish that superpolynomial circuit lower bounds for NP would imply P $\neq$ NP, yet current techniques fall short of this threshold for general circuits. Superpolynomial lower bounds remain open even for explicit functions in NP against general circuits.

\section{Asymptotic Hierarchy Theorems}
Time and circuit hierarchy theorems supply asymptotic separations that do not resolve P versus NP.

\begin{theorem}[Time hierarchy]
For any time-constructible $T(n) \ge n$, there exist languages in TIME$(T(n)^2)$ that are not in TIME$(T(n))$. The diagonalization languages lie outside NP.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Paul--Pippenger--Szemer\'edi--Trotter, 1983]
DTIME$(n) \subsetneq$ NTIME$(n)$ for multi-tape Turing machines. Extensions separate slightly superlinear bounds under additional separator assumptions, but the result does not scale to polynomial time.
\end{theorem}

Circuit hierarchy theorems similarly yield asymptotic separations for non-uniform complexity. For every fixed $k$ there exists a language in $\Sigma_2^P \cap \Pi_2^P$ requiring circuits of size $\Omega(n^k)$ (Kannan's theorem). Subsequent refinements place such fixed-polynomial lower bounds in ZPP$^{\rm NP}$ and $\rm S_2^P$. The non-uniform hierarchy and Karp--Lipton collapse implications prevent direct transfer of these bounds to NP without additional assumptions: if NP $\subseteq$ P/poly then the polynomial hierarchy collapses to $\Sigma_2^P$.

Hardness magnification results show that modest improvements on existing lower bounds for certain gap versions of MCSP or MKtP would imply superpolynomial lower bounds for NP or EXP. Current magnification thresholds remain above known unconditional bounds for these meta-complexity problems. Recent cryptographic evidence suggests that appropriate Gap-MCSP variants are not NP-hard under Levin reductions under standard obfuscation and one-way function assumptions.

\section{Barriers}
Three principal barriers constrain proof techniques.

\begin{theorem}[Baker--Gill--Solovay, 1975]
There exist oracles $A,B$ such that P$^A$ = NP$^A$ and P$^B$ $\neq$ NP$^B$. Consequently, relativizing techniques cannot resolve P versus NP.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Razborov--Rudich, 1997]
Natural proofs cannot establish superpolynomial circuit lower bounds against circuit classes that can compute pseudorandom generators secure against the corresponding circuit class. This applies in particular to P/poly under standard cryptographic assumptions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Aaronson--Wigderson, 2008]
Algebrization further restricts arithmetization-based techniques. Almost all major open problems, including P versus NP and P versus RP, require non-algebrizing methods.
\end{theorem}

Williams' algorithmic method (satisfiability algorithms implying circuit lower bounds) and Mulmuley's geometric complexity theory evade some of these barriers in restricted settings but have not yet produced a superpolynomial lower bound for an NP-complete problem against general circuits.

\section{Consistency Across Universes}
\subsection{P = NP Universe}
If P = NP, then the polynomial hierarchy collapses to P, and BPP = P by the Sipser--G\'acs--Lautemann theorem (BPP $\subseteq$ PH). Cryptographic one-way functions cannot exist, and many average-case hardness assumptions fail. No contradiction with known unconditional theorems is immediate; the principal tension is with the widespread belief in one-way functions and the existence of secure cryptography. The Paul--Pippenger--Szemer\'edi--Trotter linear-time separation remains consistent, as does Kannan's fixed-polynomial circuit lower bounds in the second level of the hierarchy (which collapses).

\subsection{P $\neq$ NP Universe}
This is consistent with all known unconditional results. It permits one-way functions, secure cryptography, and non-collapse of the polynomial hierarchy. Circuit lower bounds for NP remain open even under this assumption. The repository's $\Omega(N/\log N)$ acquisition bounds in restricted models are compatible with either resolution of the unrestricted Turing-machine question.

\subsection{P = BPP Universe}
Most experts conjecture P = BPP. This is implied by the existence of sufficiently hard functions in E (Impagliazzo--Wigderson: if E requires exponential circuits then P = BPP). Polynomial identity testing remains the principal problem known to be in BPP but not known to be in P. Relative to a random oracle, P = BPP holds with probability 1. No contradiction arises. The result is consistent with both P = NP and P $\neq$ NP.

\subsection{RP = P, ZPP = P, UP = P}
These equalities are likewise consistent with current knowledge. UP = P would imply that NP has unambiguous witnesses that can be found in polynomial time, collapsing certain cryptographic constructions based on unique solutions, but no formal contradiction is known. Relative to random oracles, RP = ZPP = P holds.

\subsection{MA = AM}
Under standard assumptions this holds; the classes are believed equal. No known separation or contradiction. Interactive proof characterizations place AM inside the polynomial hierarchy, consistent with both collapses and non-collapses.

In all examined universes, the principal open questions (superpolynomial circuit lower bounds for NP, explicit pseudorandom generators secure against P/poly, etc.) remain unresolved. No asymptotic contradiction forcing one resolution over the other has been identified from the available literature or the repository's verified results.

\section{Relation to Repository Findings}
The concurrent LSPV/RMV investigations establish exact acquisition bounds of order $\Omega(N/\log N)$ for specific orientation-field encodings and demonstrate that numerous candidate amplifiers fail to produce superlinear transport remainders under charged reductions. These include:

\begin{itemize}
\item Fixed-width chain encodings force $\Omega(q)$ orientation queries on all-one inputs, matched by linear scans.
\item Dense couplings, treewidth, density, padding, and essential constraints do not force superlinear continuation costs.
\item Independent products and guarded aggregation preserve only input-order acquisition.
\item Shared-interface tables, relation-algebra boundaries, exception kernels, and selector graphs yield exact bounds matched by linear or near-linear procedures.
\item Backdoor products, cubic affine (Tseitin-like) formulas, and Krom projections admit linear or near-linear decision procedures despite large syntactic measures (minimum backdoors, exponential resolution size in restricted systems, quadratic projected CNF size).
\end{itemize}

The charged transport inequality requires a positive numerator after subtracting construction and recognition costs. Linear construction or recognition consistently consumes the linear chain/direct-sum bound. Direct inclusion preserves the same query order but supplies no amplification. These results are \PROVED{} in their declared models and are consistent with the broader literature: restricted models admit linear or near-linear decision procedures even when syntactic measures appear large. They do not resolve the unrestricted Turing-machine question. No residual positive-remainder candidate surviving linear construction costs has been identified in the current corpus.

\section{Next Targets}
\begin{itemize}
\item Track incremental improvements in ACC$^0$ and related constant-depth circuit lower bounds for smaller classes, and monitor whether algorithmic methods extend closer to NP.
\item Examine whether hardness magnification thresholds for Gap-MCSP or related meta-complexity problems can be reduced below current known bounds, and reconcile with cryptographic non-hardness evidence under Levin reductions.
\item Audit the repository's transport inequalities for any residual positive-remainder candidates that survive linear construction costs; current evidence indicates none.
\item Maintain a ledger of oracles separating or equating the classes listed above, and monitor extensions of the Paul--Pippenger--Szemer\'edi--Trotter separation.
\item Consider whether almost-everywhere or average-case strengthenings of existing lower bounds interact with the repository's exact all-decider acquisition results.
\end{itemize}
