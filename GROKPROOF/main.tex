\section{Known Unconditional Lower Bounds}
The strongest unconditional circuit lower bounds for explicit functions remain modest relative to the thresholds needed to separate P from NP.

\begin{itemize}
\item For general Boolean circuits over the $B_2$ basis, the best known lower bound for an explicit function in P is $3.5n - O(\log n)$ (Mishra--Santhanam, ECCC TR26-260, October 2026), improving the previous $3.1n-o(n)$ of Li--Yang. The proof uses refined gate elimination with reparameterisation on an underlying independent parameter space, applied to affine dispersers of dimension $O(\log n)$. For the $U_2$ basis the best remains $5n-o(n)$.
\item Shannon's counting argument yields existence of functions requiring $\Omega(2^n/n)$ gates, but these are non-constructive.
\item For AC$^0$, H{\aa}stad's switching lemma gives $2^{\Omega(n^{1/(d-1)})}$ size lower bounds for parity and majority. Recent work (OpenAI 2026, Chukhin 2026 ECCC TR26-248, Li--Zhong ECCC TR26-261) improved depth-$d$ bounds for explicit polynomial-time functions to $2^{\Omega_d((n\log n)^{1/(d-1)})}$ against depth-$d$ AC$^0$-style circuits, breaking the classical parity barrier at every constant depth. Valiant's connection implies that a sufficiently strong depth-3 AC$^0$ lower bound of $2^{\omega(n/\log\log n)}$ would yield the first superlinear lower bound for logarithmic-depth general circuits.
\item For ACC$^0$, Williams (2011/2014) proved NEXP $\not\subseteq$ ACC$^0$; Murray--Williams pushed this to NQP $\not\subseteq$ ACC$^0$. Subsequent refinements improved size parameters and almost-everywhere strengthenings exist for $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ against ACC$^0$ approximation. Near-maximum $2^n/n$ bounds hold for $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{prMA}}/1$ (Ren--Williams, arXiv:2607.09963 / ECCC TR26-118, 2026), via iterative win-win, range avoidance, and PCP.
\item Formula size lower bounds and monotone circuit lower bounds (Razborov, Andreev, Alon--Boppana) exist for specific functions, but do not yet resolve P versus NP. Exponential monotone lower bounds for clique do not transfer to general circuits.
\end{itemize}

These results establish that superpolynomial circuit lower bounds for NP would imply P $\neq$ NP, yet current techniques fall short of this threshold for general circuits. The new $3.5n$ bound remains linear and does not yet force a separation. Superpolynomial lower bounds remain open even for explicit functions in NP against general circuits. The algorithmic method continues to be the primary source of new ACC$^0$-style separations.

\section{Asymptotic Hierarchy Theorems and Gaps}
Hierarchy theorems provide unconditional asymptotic separations within resource classes but do not resolve cross-resource comparisons such as deterministic versus nondeterministic time.

\begin{itemize}
\item Time hierarchy: If $t$ is time-constructible and $r(n)\log r(n)=o(t(n))$, then $\mathrm{DTIME}(r(n))\subsetneq\mathrm{DTIME}(t(n))$. Consequently P $\subsetneq$ EXP.
\item Nondeterministic time hierarchy: If $t$ is time-constructible and $r(n+1)=o(t(n))$, then $\mathrm{NTIME}(r(n))\subsetneq\mathrm{NTIME}(t(n))$. Consequently NP $\subsetneq$ NEXP.
\item Space hierarchy: If $s$ is space-constructible and $r(n)=o(s(n))$, then $\mathrm{DSPACE}(r(n))\subsetneq\mathrm{DSPACE}(s(n))$. Consequently L $\subsetneq$ PSPACE and PSPACE $\subsetneq$ EXPSPACE.
\item Circuit size hierarchy: SIZE$(s(n))\subsetneq$ SIZE$(s'(n))$ for appropriate $s,s'$ below the Shannon bound.
\end{itemize}

These establish that resource increases yield strictly more power asymptotically, yet leave open whether P = NP, P = PSPACE, or NP = coNP. The gap between known linear circuit lower bounds ($\approx 3.5n$) and the superpolynomial thresholds required for P $\neq$ NP remains vast. Magnification results (Atserias--M\"uller) show that slightly superlinear lower bounds for sparse approximation problems in NP would imply fixed-polynomial formula lower bounds for NP, but the current explicit bounds fall short of even these modest magnification thresholds.

\section{Barriers and Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item Relativization (Baker--Gill--Solovay): There exist oracles $A,B$ with $\mathrm{P}^A=\mathrm{NP}^A$ and $\mathrm{P}^B\neq\mathrm{NP}^B$. Diagonalization alone cannot resolve P versus NP.
\item Algebrization (Aaronson--Wigderson): Extends relativization to algebraic oracles; many proof techniques fail to resolve even NEXP versus P/poly under algebrization.
\item Natural proofs (Razborov--Rudich): If one-way functions exist, natural proofs cannot prove superpolynomial circuit lower bounds for NP.
\item Localization and other barriers constrain magnification and average-case hardness transfers.
\end{itemize}

These barriers do not prove independence but severely restrict the applicability of standard techniques. Non-relativizing, non-algebrizing, non-natural methods (such as the algorithmic method of Williams) have produced the strongest known separations outside NP.

\section{Multi-Universe Consistency Checks}
No known theorem forces a contradiction in any of the following plausible universes, nor does any force the opposite:

\begin{itemize}
\item P = NP: Consistent with current knowledge; would collapse PH to P, place all NP-complete problems in P, and imply derandomization of BPP under standard assumptions, but no absolute contradiction with hierarchy theorems or circuit bounds.
\item P $\neq$ NP: Consistent; supported by the absence of polynomial algorithms for thousands of NP-complete problems and by the existence of NP-intermediate candidates (Ladner), but not forced by any unconditional lower bound.
\item RP = P, BPP = P, ZPP = P: Believed under hardness assumptions (Impagliazzo--Wigderson, etc.); no unconditional separation or equality is known. Hierarchy results for randomized classes exist under certain conditions but do not resolve the deterministic case.
\item P = UP: Open; related to unique solutions and cryptography. No forcing contradiction.
\item MA = AM: Open; related to interactive proofs and derandomization. Consistent with both equality and separation under current bounds.
\item NEXP $\not\subseteq$ P/poly or stronger: Partially supported by Williams-style results for restricted circuit classes, but open for general circuits.
\end{itemize}

In each case the literature provides neither an absolute asymptotic lower-bound contradiction nor a collapse that is forced independently of assumptions. The repository's LSPV/RMV investigations (firewall, transport, degree-boundary ceilings) likewise identify no load-bearing implication that closes the gap under either assumption.

\section{Relation to Concurrent Repository Investigations}
The adaptive-trajectory firewall, backdoor-product, selector-graph, and Horn-transport analyses in this repository establish ceilings on certain structured reductions and quantifier transports. These results are compatible with both P = NP and P $\neq$ NP; they constrain the form of any potential polynomial algorithm or lower-bound proof but do not yet produce an asymptotic separation. Degree-boundary and charged-transport ceilings similarly remain open arrows.

\section*{Conclusion}
The evidence from hierarchy theorems, circuit lower bounds, magnification thresholds, and barrier results points consistently toward the difficulty of proving either equality or separation by currently available asymptotic techniques. The quantitative gap between the best explicit general-circuit lower bound ($3.5n-O(\log n)$) and the thresholds that would resolve P versus NP remains superlinear and, in the general case, superpolynomial. No contradiction has been identified that forces one resolution in the universes examined. P versus NP remains open.
