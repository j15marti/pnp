\section{Known Unconditional Lower Bounds}
The strongest unconditional circuit lower bounds for explicit functions remain modest.

\begin{itemize}
\item For general Boolean circuits, the best known lower bound for an explicit function in NP is $5n - o(n)$ (Iwama--Morizumi--Raz and subsequent improvements of the constant).
\item Shannon's counting argument yields existence of functions requiring $\Omega(2^n/n)$ gates, but these are non-constructive.
\item For AC$^0$, H\aa{}stad's switching lemma gives $2^{\Omega(n^{1/(d-1)})}$ size lower bounds for parity and majority.
\item For ACC$^0$, Williams (2011/2014) proved NEXP $\not\subseteq$ ACC$^0$; subsequent work strengthened size and depth parameters and extended to related classes.
\item Formula size lower bounds and monotone circuit lower bounds (Razborov, Andreev, Alon--Boppana) exist for specific functions, but do not yet resolve P versus NP.
\end{itemize}

These results establish that superpolynomial circuit lower bounds for NP would imply P $\neq$ NP, yet current techniques fall short of this threshold for general circuits.

\section{Barriers}
Three principal barriers constrain proof techniques.

\begin{theorem}[Baker--Gill--Solovay, 1975]
There exist oracles $A,B$ such that P$^A$ = NP$^A$ and P$^B$ $\neq$ NP$^B$. Consequently, relativizing techniques cannot resolve P versus NP.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Razborov--Rudich, 1997]
Natural proofs cannot establish superpolynomial circuit lower bounds against circuit classes that can compute pseudorandom generators secure against the corresponding circuit class. This applies in particular to P/poly under standard cryptographic assumptions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Aaronson--Wigderson, 2008]
Algebrization further restricts arithmetization-based techniques. Almost all major open problems, including P versus NP and P versus RP, require non-algebrizing methods.
\end{theorem}

Williams' algorithmic method (satisfiability algorithms implying circuit lower bounds) and Mulmuley's geometric complexity theory evade some of these barriers in restricted settings but have not yet produced a superpolynomial lower bound for an NP-complete problem.

\section{Asymptotic Considerations}
Time hierarchy theorems guarantee that for any time-constructible $T(n)$, there exist languages in TIME$(T(n)^2)$ not in TIME$(T(n))$. These are asymptotic separations, yet they do not separate P from NP because the diagonalization languages lie outside NP.

Circuit hierarchy theorems similarly yield asymptotic separations for non-uniform complexity. The non-uniform hierarchy implies that for every $k$ there is a language in $\Sigma_2^P \cap \Pi_2^P$ requiring circuits of size $\Omega(n^k)$. Kannan's theorem and subsequent refinements place such lower bounds in the polynomial hierarchy, but the collapse implications of NP $\subseteq$ P/poly prevent a direct transfer to NP without additional assumptions.

Hardness magnification results show that modest improvements on existing lower bounds for certain gap versions of MCSP or MKtP would imply superpolynomial lower bounds for NP or EXP. These remain conditional on closing small gaps in current bounds.

\section{Consistency Across Universes}
\subsection{P = NP Universe}
If P = NP, then the polynomial hierarchy collapses to P, and BPP = P by the Sipser--G\'acs--Lautemann theorem (BPP $\subseteq$ PH). Cryptographic one-way functions cannot exist, and many average-case hardness assumptions fail. No contradiction with known theorems is immediate; the principal tension is with the widespread belief in one-way functions and the existence of secure cryptography.

\subsection{P $\neq$ NP Universe}
This is consistent with all known unconditional results. It permits one-way functions, secure cryptography, and non-collapse of the polynomial hierarchy. Circuit lower bounds for NP remain open even under this assumption.

\subsection{P = BPP Universe}
Most experts conjecture P = BPP. This is implied by the existence of sufficiently hard functions in E (Impagliazzo--Wigderson). Polynomial identity testing remains the principal problem known to be in BPP but not known to be in P. Relative to a random oracle, P = BPP holds with probability 1. No contradiction arises.

\subsection{RP = P, ZPP = P, UP = P}
These equalities are likewise consistent with current knowledge. UP = P would imply that NP has unambiguous witnesses that can be found in polynomial time, collapsing certain cryptographic constructions, but no formal contradiction is known.

\subsection{MA = AM}
Under standard assumptions this holds; the classes are believed equal. No known separation or contradiction.

In all examined universes, the principal open questions (superpolynomial circuit lower bounds for NP, explicit pseudorandom generators, etc.) remain unresolved. No asymptotic contradiction forcing one resolution over the other has been identified from the available literature.

\section{Relation to Repository Findings}
The concurrent LSPV/RMV investigations in this repository establish exact acquisition bounds of order $\Omega(N/\log N)$ for specific orientation-field encodings and demonstrate that numerous candidate amplifiers (backdoors, treewidth, density, independent products, shared interfaces, relation widening) fail to produce superlinear transport remainders under charged reductions. These results are consistent with the broader literature: restricted models admit linear or near-linear decision procedures even when syntactic measures appear large. They do not resolve the unrestricted Turing-machine question.
