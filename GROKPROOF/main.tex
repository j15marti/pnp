\section{Known Unconditional Lower Bounds}
The strongest unconditional circuit lower bounds for explicit functions remain modest relative to the thresholds needed to separate P from NP.

\begin{itemize}
\item For general Boolean circuits over the $B_2$ basis, the best known lower bound for an explicit function in P is $3.5n - o(n)$ (Mishra--Santhanam, ECCC TR26-260, October 2026), improving the previous $3.1n-o(n)$ of Li--Yang. The proof uses refined gate elimination with reparameterisation on an underlying independent parameter space. For the $U_2$ basis the best remains $5n-o(n)$.
\item Shannon's counting argument yields existence of functions requiring $\Omega(2^n/n)$ gates, but these are non-constructive.
\item For AC$^0$, H{\aa}stad's switching lemma gives $2^{\Omega(n^{1/(d-1)})}$ size lower bounds for parity and majority. Recent work (OpenAI 2026, Chukhin 2026 ECCC TR26-248, Li--Zhong ECCC TR26-261) improved depth-$d$ bounds for explicit polynomial-time functions to $2^{\Omega_d((n\log n)^{1/(d-1)})}$ against depth-$d$ AC$^0$-style circuits, breaking the classical parity barrier at every constant depth.
\item For ACC$^0$, Williams (2011/2014) proved NEXP $\not\subseteq$ ACC$^0$; Murray--Williams pushed this to NQP $\not\subseteq$ ACC$^0$. Subsequent refinements improved size parameters and almost-everywhere strengthenings exist for $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{NP}}$ against ACC$^0$ approximation. Near-maximum $2^n/n$ bounds hold for $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{prMA}}/1$ (Ren--Williams, 2026).
\item Formula size lower bounds and monotone circuit lower bounds (Razborov, Andreev, Alon--Boppana) exist for specific functions, but do not yet resolve P versus NP. Exponential monotone lower bounds for clique do not transfer to general circuits.
\end{itemize}

These results establish that superpolynomial circuit lower bounds for NP would imply P $\neq$ NP, yet current techniques fall short of this threshold for general circuits. The new $3.5n$ bound remains linear and does not yet force a separation. Superpolynomial lower bounds remain open even for explicit functions in NP against general circuits. The algorithmic method continues to be the primary source of new ACC$^0$-style separations.

\section{Asymptotic Hierarchy Theorems}
Time and circuit hierarchy theorems supply asymptotic separations that do not resolve P versus NP.

\begin{theorem}[Time hierarchy]
For any time-constructible $T(n) \ge n$, there exist languages in TIME$(T(n)^2)$ that are not in TIME$(T(n))$. The diagonalization languages lie outside NP.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Paul--Pippenger--Szemer\'edi--Trotter, 1983]
DTIME$(n) \subsetneq$ NTIME$(n)$ for multi-tape Turing machines. Extensions separate slightly superlinear bounds under additional separator assumptions, but the result does not scale to polynomial time. No superpolynomial separation between DTIME and NTIME at polynomial scales is known.
\end{theorem}

Circuit hierarchy theorems similarly yield asymptotic separations for non-uniform complexity. For every fixed $k$ there exists a language in $\Sigma_2^P \cap \Pi_2^P$ requiring circuits of size $\Omega(n^k)$ (Kannan's theorem). Subsequent refinements place such fixed-polynomial lower bounds in ZPP$^{\rm NP}$ and $\rm S_2^P$. The non-uniform hierarchy and Karp--Lipton collapse implications prevent direct transfer of these bounds to NP without additional assumptions: if NP $\subseteq$ P/poly then the polynomial hierarchy collapses to $\Sigma_2^P$.

Hardness magnification results (Oliveira--Santhanam, Chen--Jin--Williams, and subsequent work) show that modest improvements on existing lower bounds for certain gap versions of MCSP or MKtP would imply superpolynomial lower bounds for NP or EXP. Atserias--M\"uller (arXiv:2503.24061, 2025) introduce distinguishers (sparse matrices retaining properties of error-correcting codes) yielding a simple general magnification: fixed-polynomial formula-size lower bounds for NP are implied by slightly superlinear formula-size lower bounds for approximating any sufficiently sparse problem in NP. The thresholds are sharp, and the approach yields a uniform magnification for MCSP that appears to sidestep the localization barrier. Current magnification thresholds remain above known unconditional bounds for these meta-complexity problems in the relevant regimes. Recent cryptographic evidence (Mazor--Pass) shows that appropriate Gap-MCSP variants are not NP-hard under Levin reductions assuming iO and subexponentially secure one-way functions. Consistency results in bounded arithmetic (Atserias--Buss--M\"uller, Thapen 2025) show that stronger NEXP lower bounds (including NEXP $\not\subseteq$ EXP/poly and NEXP $\not\subseteq$ coNEXP) remain consistent with strong theories of arithmetic such as $V_2^0$ strengthened by true universal number-sort sentences.

\section{Barriers}
Three principal barriers constrain proof techniques.

\begin{theorem}[Baker--Gill--Solovay, 1975]
There exist oracles $A,B$ such that P$^A$ = NP$^A$ and P$^B$ $\neq$ NP$^B$. Consequently, relativizing techniques cannot resolve P versus NP.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Razborov--Rudich, 1997]
Natural proofs cannot establish superpolynomial circuit lower bounds against circuit classes that can compute pseudorandom generators secure against the corresponding circuit class. This applies in particular to P/poly under standard cryptographic assumptions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Aaronson--Wigderson, 2008]
Algebrization further restricts arithmetization-based techniques. Almost all major open problems, including P versus NP and P versus RP, require non-algebrizing methods.
\end{theorem}

Williams' algorithmic method and Mulmuley's geometric complexity theory evade some of these barriers in restricted settings but have not yet produced a superpolynomial lower bound for an NP-complete problem against general circuits.

\section{Repository Findings: Consistent Input-Order Ceilings and Absence of Residual Positive Transport}
The concurrent LSPV/RMV investigations establish a sequence of exact, model-restricted results that systematically close candidate amplification routes. Across fixed-width chain encodings, dense couplings, treewidth, density, padding, essential constraints, independent products, shared-interface tables, relation-algebra boundaries, exception kernels, selector graphs, backdoor products, cubic affine / Tseitin-like formulas, degree boundaries, Horn selectors, and adaptive trajectories, the verified pattern is that any all-decider acquisition bound of order $\Omega(N/\log N)$ is matched by a linear-time recognizer or scan, so that charged transport remainders remain nonpositive after linear construction or recognition costs.

Notable recent closures include:
\begin{itemize}
\item BACKDOOR-PRODUCT-FIREWALL97: a linear minimum strong syntactic 2-CNF backdoor $h=\Theta(N/\log N)$ coexists with an $O(pq)$ charged recognizer; disjoint-product amplification does not force $2^{\Omega(h)}$ work.
\item DEGREE-BOUNDARY96: maximum-degree-three 3-colorability is linear-time (Brooks + $K_4$ detection); the degree-four endpoint is NP-complete, yet a direct-inclusion family forces only $\Theta(N/\log N)$ packed-edge-word queries matched by a linear scan.
\item SELECTOR-GRAPH-BOUNDARY95: domain-two arbitrary binary CSP reduces to 2-SAT; domain-three inequality is graph 3-coloring; supplied feedback vertex sets yield exact $d^{O(c)}$ algorithms, but two-selector tables force only $d^2$ field reads matched by a scan.
\item HORN-TRANSPORT-CEILING100: degree-three sources are satisfiable by Hall matching; degree-four Horn-selector language is NP-complete; padded implication-chain images force $\Theta(N/\log N)$ field reads, but explicit materialization writes more words than the query bound, yielding negative transport numerator.
\item ADAPTIVE-TRAJECTORY-FIREWALL107: initial syntactic parameters $(k,h)=(4q+4,q+1)$ collapse to $(0,0)$ after charged binary discovery and propagation; SAT uses SCC/witness replay while UNSAT uses conflict, showing that neither initial $k$ nor $h$ models every adaptive solver. The missing invariant must cover the full paid state trajectory.
\end{itemize}

These exact model-restricted bounds remain compatible with either resolution of the unrestricted Turing-machine question. No residual positive-remainder candidates that survive linear construction costs have been identified across the firewall and boundary families. The charged transport inequality
\[
T_{\rm new}^{\le}(g(q))\ge
\frac{L_{\rm old}(q)-R(q)-H(q)}{Q(q)\tau(q)}
\]
is justified only with a positive numerator; linear costs consume the numerator.

\subsection{Asymptotic Analysis of Transport Remainders}
Consider a family of reductions or encodings where the old acquisition lower bound $L_{\rm old}(q)=\Omega(q)$ (or $\Omega(N/\log N)$ under standard packing) and the reduction cost $R(q)+H(q)=\Theta(q)$ or larger. The numerator is then $O(q)-\Theta(q)\le 0$ for large $q$. Thus the inequality supplies no superlinear lower bound on the new model. Direct inclusion preserves the query order but supplies no amplification factor. Adaptive policies that discover and propagate structure can collapse initial syntactic parameters, further reducing any potential remainder. This pattern is consistent across all verified families and does not produce an asymptotic forcing of separation or collapse.

\subsection{Adaptive Collapse and State-Trajectory Invariants}
The adaptive-trajectory result demonstrates that syntactic parameters such as clause count or backdoor size can be reduced to zero by a linear-time discovery process that is charged against the acquisition bound. Consequently, any lower-bound argument that relies on the persistence of an initial large parameter must account for the full paid state trajectory, including representation changes and label-dependent (SAT versus UNSAT) strategies. In asymptotic terms, the collapse shows that the effective parameter can drop from $\Theta(q)$ to $O(1)$ after $O(q)$ work, leaving no residual exponential dependence on the original parameter. This reinforces the non-positivity of transport remainders and eliminates candidate amplification routes that treat initial syntactic width as invariant.

\section{Dynamic and Reverse Dynamic Programming Approaches}
Dynamic programming yields polynomial-time algorithms precisely when a structural width parameter is bounded. For a CNF whose incidence graph has treewidth $w$, DP along a tree decomposition solves SAT (and \#SAT) in time $2^{O(w)}\mathrm{poly}(n)$. Williams' algorithm for ACC$^0$-SAT proceeds by converting to a quasi-polynomial-size SYM$^+$ circuit and applying a DP (zeta-transform) algorithm over the subset lattice, yielding time $2^{n-n^{\Omega(1)}}$ on polynomial-size ACC$^0$ circuits, sufficient for the NEXP $\not\subseteq$ ACC$^0$ separation. The same technique does not extend to general circuits of polynomial size.

Reverse dynamic programming corresponds to reasoning from the output gate toward the inputs. Reversible Circuit-SAT remains NP-complete. No general reverse-DP procedure is known that improves the exponential dependence on the number of free variables for arbitrary Circuit-SAT or 3-SAT instances.

\subsection{Parameterized Forward and Reverse Dynamic Programming}
We parameterize both by the size of the active state maintained at each step of a topological (or reverse-topological) traversal. For an arbitrary circuit there exist cuts whose frontier has size $\Theta(n)$; the corresponding parameter is $\Theta(n)$ and the algorithm requires $2^{\Theta(n)}$ time. When a tree decomposition of width $w$ is available the frontier can be chosen of size $O(w)$.

Any algorithm that decides Circuit-SAT by maintaining an explicit state at each of the $m$ steps must, on some instances, encounter a step whose parameter is $\Omega(n)$. This follows from the existence of circuits whose every topological cut has large frontier. The resulting time lower bound of $2^{\Omega(n)}$ holds for this particular algorithmic paradigm; it does not yet transfer to arbitrary Turing machines. The repository's adaptive-trajectory results show that even when initial syntactic parameters are large, paid adaptive discovery can collapse them, reinforcing that fixed-parameter analyses do not capture every solver.

In both the P = NP and the P $\neq$ NP universes the existence of instances forcing an exponential parameter at some step is consistent. No residual positive transport remainder that would force a separation outside the DP model has been identified. The standard barriers continue to apply.

\section{Asymptotic Interaction of Linear Circuit Bounds and Magnification}
The $3.5n-o(n)$ lower bound for an explicit function in P is asymptotically linear. Magnification theorems require a slightly superlinear lower bound (e.g., $n^{1+\varepsilon}$) on a sparse approximation problem in NP to lift to a fixed polynomial lower bound for NP. Because the best known explicit general-circuit bounds remain $O(n)$, and the magnification thresholds of Atserias--M\"uller sit strictly above the linear regime, no asymptotic transfer is currently available. The gap between $3.5n$ and any $n^{1+\varepsilon}$ is itself superlinear; closing it would constitute a qualitative advance rather than a constant-factor improvement. Consistency of NEXP $\not\subseteq$ P/poly with strong bounded arithmetic further indicates that the linear barrier is not an artifact of weak proof systems. The repository's consistent input-order ceilings align with this linear regime: matched linear recognizers prevent the formation of residual superlinear remainders that could seed a magnification step.

\section{Consistency Across Universes}
\subsection{P = NP Universe}
If P = NP, then the polynomial hierarchy collapses to P, and BPP = P. Cryptographic one-way functions cannot exist. The new $3.5n$ lower bound for a function in P is consistent with collapse. Magnification becomes vacuous under collapse. Repository acquisition bounds of order $\Omega(N/\log N)$ and the systematic absence of residual positive transport remainders remain compatible. Adaptive collapses of syntactic parameters are consistent, as efficient discovery procedures exist under collapse. Dynamic and reverse-DP procedures remain consistent. No contradiction arises from the linear nature of known circuit bounds.

\subsection{P $\neq$ NP Universe}
This is consistent with all known unconditional results. It permits one-way functions and non-collapse of the polynomial hierarchy. The $3.5n$ bound supplies only a linear barrier. Recent ACC$^0$ lower bounds for NEXP/NQP remain valid. No asymptotic forcing of separation arises from current hierarchy or circuit results. Width-bounded DP, the ACC$^0$-SAT algorithm, and the repository's input-order ceilings (including adaptive trajectory collapse) are compatible. The non-positivity of transport remainders does not contradict separation, as the ceilings apply only to model-restricted acquisition.

\subsection{P = BPP, RP = P, ZPP = P, UP = P, MA = AM}
These equalities are consistent with current knowledge. Relative to random oracles, RP = ZPP = P holds. No formal contradiction is known. The repository pattern of matched linear recognizers for $\Omega(N/\log N)$ acquisition and adaptive parameter collapse does not produce a contradiction under these collapses either. Impagliazzo--Wigderson implies that P = BPP if E requires exponential circuits; the known $3.5n$ bound is far from this threshold. Near-maximum bounds for $\mathrm{E}^{\mathrm{prMA}}/1$ are likewise compatible.

In all examined universes, the principal open questions remain unresolved. No asymptotic contradiction forcing one resolution over the other has been identified from the available literature or the repository's verified results. The new explicit linear circuit lower bound, improved magnification thresholds, the consistent input-order transport ceilings, and the adaptive collapse of syntactic parameters do not force a resolution.

\section{Open Directions for Asymptotic Forcing}
\begin{itemize}
\item Monitor whether the $3.5n$ bound can be pushed past $4n$ or combined with magnification to reach the superlinear thresholds of Atserias--M\"uller. The asymptotic gap from linear to $n^{1+\varepsilon}$ remains the critical obstacle.
\item Continue searching for residual positive-remainder candidates that survive linear construction costs and adaptive discovery; current evidence indicates none across the firewall and boundary families, including adaptive trajectories, selector graphs, and Horn transports.
\item Maintain a ledger of oracles separating or equating the classes listed above, and monitor extensions of the Paul--Pippenger--Szemer\'edi--Trotter separation beyond slightly superlinear regimes.
\item Consider whether almost-everywhere or average-case strengthenings of existing lower bounds interact with the repository's exact all-decider acquisition results and adaptive collapses.
\item Evaluate conditional lower bounds under NSETH or Orthogonal Vectors conjecture for possible transfer to unrestricted models.
\item Investigate whether further refinements of width-based or reversible DP procedures can be combined with the repository's charged-transport ceilings to produce a residual positive remainder in an unrestricted model.
\item Analyze whether the per-step parameter lower bound for explicit-state forward/reverse DP can be amplified, via charged transport or other means, beyond the DP paradigm itself, accounting for adaptive collapse of syntactic parameters.
\item Examine whether the improved AC$^0$ bounds of Li--Zhong and Chukhin can be lifted or magnified toward unrestricted circuit classes without invoking natural proofs.
\item Develop state-trajectory invariants that survive adaptive discovery and representation changes, and test whether any such invariant yields a positive transport remainder outside the model-restricted regimes.
\end{itemize}
