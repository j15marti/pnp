\section*{OPEN Lemma (All-Input Resource Contraction)}
\textbf{Status: OPEN.}

Assume, for the sake of contradiction, that \(P = NP\). Then there exists a fixed uniform deterministic total correct SAT solver \(A\) and finite exponents \(K, L \ge 2\) such that
\[
T_A(N) \le N^{K+o(1)}, \qquad S_A(N) \le N^{L+o(1)}
\]
on every ordinary encoded input of length \(N\).

Let \(\lambda = 1/(5K)\) and \(\zeta = 0.1\). The following statement remains open: there exists a uniform transformation \(\mathcal{T}\) such that, on every ordinary \(N\)-bit 3-SAT (or exact-(2,2)-CNF-3) instance \(F\),
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\mathcal{T}\) produces at most \(N^{1-\lambda+\zeta+o(1)}\) ordinary SAT children, each of length at most \(N^{\lambda+o(1)}\);
\item the satisfiability status of \(F\) is recoverable from the children's answers by a local procedure whose total work is at most \(N^{1.1+o(1)}\) and whose live parent space is at most \(N^{0.2+o(1)}\);
\item every cost of discovery, correctness verification, failed strategies, repeated access, memory allocation, buffering, and combination is fully charged inside the stated budgets.
\end{enumerate}
If such a \(\mathcal{T}\) existed, the resulting simultaneous resource profile
\[
t' \le 1.3 - \frac{1}{5K}, \qquad s' \le 0.2
\]
would lie strictly inside the FLMV forbidden region \(s < \beta(t)\) with
\[
\beta(t) = \frac12\Bigl(\frac{t+2}{t^2}-t\Bigr),
\]
contradicting the known lower bound of Fortnow–Lipton–van Melkebeek–Viglas. No such transformation has been established, nor has it been rigorously refuted for arbitrary \(A\).

\section*{Proved Numerical Membership}
\textbf{Status: PROVED/EXACT.}

For every finite \(K \ge 2\), the profile \((t', s') = (1.3 - 1/(5K), 0.2)\) satisfies \(s' < \beta(t')\). Explicit verification:
\begin{align*}
K=2 &\implies t'=1.2, \quad \beta(1.2)\approx 0.511 > 0.2,\\
K=5 &\implies t'=1.26, \quad \beta(1.26)\approx 0.397 > 0.2,\\
K=10 &\implies t'=1.28, \quad \beta(1.28)\approx 0.361 > 0.2,\\
K=100 &\implies t'\approx 1.298, \quad \beta\approx 0.330 > 0.2.
\end{align*}
The membership holds for all larger finite \(K\) by continuity of \(\beta\) and the fact that \(t'\) remains bounded above by \(1.3\) while \(\beta(1.3)\approx 0.326 > 0.2\).
