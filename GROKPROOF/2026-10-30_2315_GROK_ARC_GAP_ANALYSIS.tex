\section{Status}
P versus NP remains \textbf{OPEN}. The repository j15marti/pnp (private, TeX-based living document) maintains a rigorous, multi-agent investigation focused on deriving a contradiction from the assumption P=NP via known time-space lower bounds for SAT. \section{Recovered State}
The primary strategy is resource contradiction under the temporary assumption that a fixed, uniform, correct deterministic SAT solver \(A\) exists with
\[
T_A(N) \le N^{K+o(1)}, \qquad S_A(N) \le N^{L+o(1)}
\]
for some finite \(K \ge 2\), \(L \ge 0\).

Known unconditional lower bounds (FLMV 2005, Williams 2007/2008) exclude certain simultaneous pairs \((t,s)\). In particular:
- SAT \(\notin\) DTISP\([N^{2\cos(\pi/7)-o(1)}, N^{o(1)}]\) (Williams),
- more general tradeoffs of the form \(s < \beta(t)\) for the Fortnow--Lipton--van Melkebeek--Viglas family.

The missing step is an \(A\)-dependent, all-input, answer-preserving transformation that, from any such \(A\), produces a solver whose effective \((t',s')\) falls strictly inside a forbidden region, with all discovery, verification, access, memory, switching, and combination costs fully charged.

Conditional numerical targets (e.g., \(\lambda=1/(5K)\), \(\zeta=0.1\), children of length \(N^{\lambda+o(1)}\), total work \(N^{1.1+o(1)}\), live space \(N^{0.2+o(1)}\)) remain unproved. Affine shells, Tseitin, Horn/2-SAT, and one-sided abstractions have been explored and shown insufficient for the unrestricted case.

\section{Precise Open Lemma Required}
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Lemma (Universal Child Generator, OPEN).}
Assume \(A\) is a uniform deterministic total exact ordinary-SAT decider with the resource bounds above. There exist constants \(\lambda\in(0,1)\), \(b\), \(r\), \(u\) (depending only on \(K,L\)) and a uniform algorithm that, on every input formula \(F\) of length \(N\), produces at most \(N^{b+o(1)}\) ordinary-SAT instances \(F_i\) each of length at most \(N^{\lambda+o(1)}\), together with a charged combination procedure, such that:
\begin{enumerate}
\item correctness is preserved for both SAT and UNSAT;
\item selector discovery, failed strategies, repeated access, and live parent space are all charged;
\item total local work excluding the recursive calls on the \(F_i\) is at most \(N^{r+o(1)}\);
\item the effective resources of the composed solver lie strictly inside a published FLMV/Williams forbidden region.
\end{enumerate}
\end{quote}

No such map is currently known. Literal CNF partitions fail on expanders. Semantic decompositions leave an arbitrary NP-complete core. Adaptive certificate schemes (van Melkebeek two-algorithm incompatibility) require additional near-boundary seeds that are not implied by arbitrary polynomial \(K\).

\section{Failed or Restricted Hypotheses}
- Source-independent affine MCSP threshold descent: ruled out after correction of the bias bound.
- Free semantic SAT answers or uncharged guest calls of size \(N^{\eta}\) with \(\eta>\lambda\): incompatible under DELAY/COPY models.
- Compact linear summaries imply cheap construction: falsified (minimum distance of linear codes is NP-hard to compute).
- Restricted PC-degree bounds on bounded-context grammars: do not transfer to unrestricted computation; nonlinear extension variables can evade them.
- Occurrence-disjoint products and port hierarchies: remain linear-size and do not yield sublinear children for arbitrary SAT.

\section{Next Decisive Steps}
1. Formalize an explicit, both-label, hereditary same-machine stage in the exact FLMV random-access model whose every unavoidable ordinary guest query has physical length \(N^{\lambda+o(1)}\).
2. Charge selector discovery, failures, and peak live space rigorously.
3. Test the candidate map on expander Tseitin, minimal UNSAT, exact-(2,2)-CNF, Horn, and XOR-mixed instances.
4. If the map is obtained, verify the composed resources against the strongest applicable published theorem (Williams or full FLMV Master Theorem), never mixing time and space from different machines.

Until the universal implication is established or refuted, P versus NP remains open.
