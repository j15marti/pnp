## Literature Confirmation
Primary sources (Fortnow–Lipton–van Melkebeek–Viglas, JACM 2005; Fortnow–van Melkebeek CCC 2000 / ECCC TR00-028) establish:

SAT cannot be solved in time $N^{a}$ and space $N^{b}$ on deterministic random-access Turing machines whenever
\[
b < \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{a+2}{a^{2}-a}
\]
for $a$ below the golden ratio (with stronger constants near the endpoint in some regimes). Williams (CCC 2007/08, later improvements) pushes the subpolynomial-space time lower bound to $2\cos(\pi/7)\approx 1.8019$. Alternation-trading frameworks cannot improve this endpoint (Buss–Williams).

No published theorem supplies a fully charged, hereditary, all-input transformation from an arbitrary uniform polynomial-time SAT algorithm $A$ (exponents $K,L$) to a simultaneous resource profile inside any strict FLMV forbidden region.

## Formalized Open Implication (OPEN)
Assume a fixed uniform deterministic total exact ordinary-SAT decider $A$ with
\[
T_A(N)\le N^{K+o(1)},\qquad S_A(N)\le N^{L+o(1)}
\]
for finite $K\ge 2$. There is presently no proof that there exists an all-input transformation producing at most $N^{1-\lambda+\zeta+o(1)}$ ordinary SAT children of length at most $N^{\lambda+o(1)}$ ($\lambda=1/(5K)$, $\zeta=0.1$) whose aggregate local work is at most $N^{1.1+o(1)}$ and whose live parent space is at most $N^{0.2+o(1)}$, after fully charging discovery, access, verification, memory, and combination costs. The resulting profile $(t',s')$ with $t'\le 1.3-1/(5K)$, $s'\le 0.2$ lies inside the classical FLMV region only conditionally on this transformation. Numerical membership alone does not construct the algorithm.
