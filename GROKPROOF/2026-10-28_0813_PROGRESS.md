# GROKPROOF Progress Report: 2026-10-28_0813

## Strongest New Result
No unconditional resolution of P versus NP has been achieved. The primary resource-contradiction strategy remains open at the universal transformation step. Under the assumption that a fixed, correct, uniform polynomial-time SAT solver \(A\) exists with
\[
T_A(N) \le N^{K+o(1)}, \qquad S_A(N) \le N^{L+o(1)}
\]
for finite \(K \ge 2\), \(L \ge 0\), no rigorously established, A-dependent, all-input transformation has been proved that produces an impossible simultaneous time-space profile for ordinary 3-SAT or exact-(2,2)-CNF-3 while correctly accounting for discovery, construction, access, memory, and combination costs.

## Proof Impact
- The FLMV / Williams time-space lower bounds (SAT not in \(\mathrm{DTISP}(N^{1.801-o(1)}, N^{o(1)})\) and related trade-offs) remain the strongest published unconditional barriers applicable to random-access models. These exclude certain simultaneous resource profiles but do not yet force a contradiction with an arbitrary polynomial-time algorithm.
- Conditional contractions (e.g., \(\lambda = 1/(5K)\), \(\zeta = 0.1\), yielding \(t' \le 1.3 - 1/(5K)\), \(s' \le 0.2\)) and the recurrence \(t' \le \max(1.1, 0.6 + t/2)\), \(s' \le \max(0.5, s/2)\) are numerically suggestive relative to the forbidden region \(\beta(t) = ((t+2)/t^2 - t)/2\), but the underlying all-input decomposition and resource accounting remain unproved.
- Adaptive UNSAT certificates, one-sided semantic abstractions, circuit decision lower bounds, and proof-complexity obstructions (expander Tseitin, PC/PCR degree bounds) are correctly restricted to their stated models and do not transfer to unrestricted polynomial-time SAT algorithms.
- Semantic decompositions via affine elimination, Horn/2-SAT, or nonlinear extensions correctly reduce certain structured instances but leave an arbitrary NP-complete core that still requires the original solver on large inputs.

## Failed Hypotheses
- Literal CNF partitions on expander graphs fail to yield useful small children.
- Direct transfer of restricted circuit or sequential-head bounds to full RAM/SAT algorithms is falsified by known counterexamples and model mismatches.
- Near-boundary seed assumptions (e.g., \(T_A \le N^{1.802+o(1)}\), \(S_A = N^{o(1)}\)) produce conditional contradictions only under additional hypotheses that are not known to hold for arbitrary polynomial solvers.
- Minimally UNSAT formulas defeat proper-subformula contextual projections without expensive correctness checks.

## Remaining Gap
The missing universal implication is: from the existence of any fixed polynomial-time SAT solver \(A\) (with unknown finite exponents) to the construction, for every input, of a collection of smaller instances whose collective solution (via \(A\) or verification) yields a time-space profile forbidden by an established theorem, with all overheads rigorously charged and no circular reliance on solving parent-sized instances first. Until this implication is proved (or refuted by a concrete counterexample transformation that stays within allowed resources), the resource-contradiction strategy does not establish P ≠ NP.

## Next Decisive Lemma
Prove or refute the existence of an A-dependent, all-input, resource-accounted transformation that, for arbitrary \(K \ge 2\), produces \(\le N^{1-\lambda+\zeta+o(1)}\) SAT children each of length \(\le N^{\lambda+o(1)}\) with total local work \(O(N^{1.1+o(1)})\) and live parent space \(O(N^{0.2+o(1)})\), for \(\lambda = 1/(5K)\), \(\zeta = 0.1\), such that correctness, discovery, and combination costs are included and the resulting profile lies strictly inside a published forbidden region. Alternatively, exhibit a family of hard instances on which every such attempted decomposition either fails soundness or exceeds the resource budget.
