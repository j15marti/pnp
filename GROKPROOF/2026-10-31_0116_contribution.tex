\section{Resource Contradiction Status Update}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Contraction Candidate]
Assume a fixed uniform SAT solver \(A\) with \(T_A(N) \le N^{K+o(1)}\) for some finite \(K \ge 2\). Set \(\lambda = 1/(5K)\) and \(\zeta = 0.1\). If there exists a uniform transformation that, on every input of length \(N\), produces at most \(N^{1-\lambda+\zeta+o(1)}\) SAT children each of length at most \(N^{\lambda+o(1)}\), performs at most \(N^{1.1+o(1)}\) local work, and uses at most \(N^{0.2+o(1)}\) live parent space, then the resulting simultaneous resource profile \((t', s')\) with \(t' \le 1.3 - 1/(5K)\) and \(s' \le 0.2\) lies in the FLMV forbidden region for all sufficiently large \(K\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
By the FLMV theorem, no deterministic random-access machine solves SAT in time \(N^t\) and space \(N^s\) whenever \(s < \beta(t)\) where
\[
\beta(t) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{t+2}{t^2} - t\right).
\]
For \(t = 1.3\), \(\beta(1.3) \approx 0.326 > 0.2\). Reducing the time exponent by \(1/(5K)\) keeps the pair forbidden for large \(K\). The transformation would therefore yield a contradiction to the assumed existence of \(A\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The transformation itself remains \OPEN. All costs of discovery, construction, repeated access, memory management, and combination must be charged. Semantic or affine shortcuts that retain a parent-scale call to \(A\) do not suffice.
\end{remark}
