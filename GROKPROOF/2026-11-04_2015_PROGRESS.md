## Literature Review (Primary Sources)
- Fortnow–Lipton–van Melkebeek–Viglas (JACM 2005): For \( c < \varphi \) (golden ratio \(\approx 1.618\)), there exists \( d > 0 \) such that SAT is not in DTISP(\( N^c \), \( N^d \)) on random-access TMs; \( d \) approaches 1 as \( c \) does. The forbidden region includes the explicit tradeoff \( s < \beta(t) \) with \( \beta(t) = \frac12 \bigl( \frac{t+2}{t^2} - t \bigr) \).
- Williams (CCC 2007 / Comp. Complex. 2008): SAT \(\notin\) DTISP(\( N^{c+o(1)} \), \( N^{o(1)} \)) for \( c < 2\cos(\pi/7) \approx 1.8019 \).
- Buss–Williams (Comp. Complex. 2015): The endpoint \( 2\cos(\pi/7) \) is optimal within the formalized alternation-trading proof system for the subpolynomial-space case.
- Exact-(2,2)-CNF-3 (three distinct variables per clause, each variable occurring exactly twice positively and twice negatively) is NP-complete by standard reductions from ordinary 3-SAT; expander Tseitin formulas remain hard for Resolution and PC.
- No primary literature supplies a universal, fully charged, correctness-preserving, all-input transformation from an arbitrary polynomial-time SAT solver into a portfolio of ordinary small SAT children that meets the budgets required to enter a forbidden FLMV region.

## Next Decisive Lemma
**OPEN Lemma (All-Input Resource Contraction).**  
Given black-box access to any fixed uniform deterministic total correct SAT solver A satisfying the polynomial bounds, either  
(a) construct a uniform transformation that, on every ordinary N-bit 3-SAT (or exact-(2,2)-CNF-3) instance F, produces at most \( N^{1-\lambda+\zeta+o(1)} \) ordinary SAT children each of length at most \( N^{\lambda+o(1)} \) (with \(\lambda=1/(5K)\), \(\zeta=0.1\)) such that the satisfiability status of F is recoverable by a local procedure of cost \( N^{1.1+o(1)} \) using live parent space at most \( N^{0.2+o(1)} \), with every ancillary cost charged, thereby yielding a simultaneous profile forbidden by FLMV;  
or  
(b) rigorously prove that no such transformation exists for any finite K.  

Until this lemma (or an alternate universal implication to a forbidden resource profile) is established, no contradiction with known lower bounds is obtained and P versus NP remains open.
