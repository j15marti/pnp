### Latest Approach: Quadratic Polar-Rank Spectrum (LSPV-1/491)
Theorem 491.1 (PROVED/RESTRICTED): For any quadratic vector map \( Q: \mathrm{GF}(2)^n \to \mathrm{GF}(2)^m \) (arbitrary sharing and cancellations), the maximum agreement with any globally affine prediction is bounded by
\[
U(Q) = 2^{n-m} \sum_{s \in \mathrm{GF}(2)^m} 2^{-r_s/2},
\]
where \( r_s \) is the polar rank of the scalar quadratic \( s \cdot Q \). Consequently, covering all inputs requires at least \( \lceil 2^n / U(Q) \rceil \) affine templates. The bound is invariant under invertible affine input changes and invertible linear output recombinations. It recovers the independent-AND case \( U(Q) = 3^g \) and is verified by independent Python and Node.js controls on small dimensions, including sharing and duplicate falsifiers.

This is a **PROVED/RESTRICTED** application of the standard quadratic Walsh/polar-rank theorem (Carlet et al.) to the vector-agreement setting. It supplies a restricted analytical upper-control / lower-cover metric for quadratic maps. It does **not** transfer to a lower bound on shared-DAG Resolution, mixed \( R_{\mathrm{mix341}} \) certificates, or arbitrary polynomial-time SAT algorithms. Resource accounting for computing the spectrum itself is explicit and offline; no FLMV same-machine claim is made. Restriction to degree \( \le 2 \) is substantial.
