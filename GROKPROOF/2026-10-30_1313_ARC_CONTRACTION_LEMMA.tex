\section*{Candidate Lemma (ARC-contraction, OPEN)}

Assume there exists a uniform deterministic SAT algorithm \(A\) satisfying
\[
T_A(N)\le N^{K+o(1)},\qquad S_A(N)\le N^{L+o(1)}
\]
for fixed finite \(K\ge 2\), \(L\ge 0\).

Set
\[
\lambda=\frac1{5K},\qquad \zeta=0.1.
\]

\textbf{Claim (still OPEN).}
There is a deterministic procedure \(C\) that, on every CNF formula \(F\) of encoded length \(N\), runs in time \(N^{1.1+o(1)}\) and space \(N^{0.2+o(1)}\) and outputs a list of at most
\[
N^{1-\lambda+\zeta+o(1)}
\]
CNF formulas \(F_1,\dots,F_m\), each of length at most \(N^{\lambda+o(1)}\), such that
\[
\mathrm{SAT}(F)\;\iff\;\bigvee_{i=1}^m\mathrm{SAT}(F_i)
\]
and every subsequent call that \(C\) makes to \(A\) is on an instance of length \(\le N^{\lambda+o(1)}\).

If the claim holds, the overall time and space exponents of the resulting SAT algorithm satisfy
\[
t'\le 1.3-\frac1{5K},\qquad s'\le 0.2,
\]
which lies strictly inside the Fortnow–Lipton–van Melkebeek–Viglas forbidden region
\[
s<\beta(t)=\frac12\Bigl(\frac{t+2}{t^2}-t\Bigr)
\]
for every finite \(K\). This would contradict the existence of \(A\).

\textbf{Current obstacles.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Self-reducibility yields only \(O(1)\)-variable decreases per step.
\item Affine elimination on Tseitin shells leaves an arbitrary residual NP-complete core.
\item All resource costs (discovery, verification, indexing, live parent space, failed branches) must be charged; no construction currently meets the simultaneous bounds.
\end{itemize}

The lemma is therefore labelled \textbf{OPEN}.
