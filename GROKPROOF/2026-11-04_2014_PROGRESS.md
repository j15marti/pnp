## Literature Review (Primary Sources)
- Fortnow–Lipton–van Melkebeek–Viglas (JACM 2005): For deterministic random-access Turing machines, SAT is not solvable in time \(N^{t}\) and space \(N^{s}\) whenever \(s < \beta(t)\) with \(\beta(t) = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(\frac{t+2}{t^2}-t\bigr)\). Example: \(t=1.3\) yields \(\beta\approx 0.326\); simultaneous \(N^{1.3}\) time and \(N^{0.2}\) space is excluded.
- Williams (CCC 2007 / Comp. Complex. 2008): SAT \(\notin\) DTISP(\(N^{c+o(1)}, N^{o(1)}\)) for all \(c < 2\cos(\pi/7)\approx 1.8019\).
- Buss–Williams: The \(2\cos(\pi/7)\) endpoint is optimal within the alternation-trading proof framework for the subpolynomial-space regime.
- Exact-(2,2)-CNF-3 is NP-complete. Expander Tseitin formulas remain hard for Resolution and Polynomial Calculus.
- No primary literature supplies a universal, fully charged, correctness-preserving, all-input transformation from an arbitrary polynomial-time SAT solver into a portfolio of ordinary small SAT children meeting the stated local budgets while preserving the forbidden simultaneous profile.
- Recent claims of subexponential algorithms for k-SAT (e.g., purported ETH refutations) were noted but do not affect the polynomial-time simultaneous resource barriers under consideration and remain outside the established literature for this strategy.
