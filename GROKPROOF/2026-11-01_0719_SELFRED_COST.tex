\section*{Lemma: Classical Self-Reducibility Aggregate Cost}
\label{lem:selfred-cost}

Let \( A \) be any correct total decision algorithm for SAT (or for Monotone 3-SAT-(2,2)) with time bound
\[
T_A(N) \le N^{K+o(1)}
\]
for some fixed finite \( K \ge 1 \), where \( N \) is the encoded input length.

The classical self-reduction proceeds by successively fixing one variable at a time.  On an input of length \( N \) with \( n = \Theta(N) \) variables (as holds for the standard encoding of 3-CNF or exact-(2,2)-CNF), the procedure issues \( \Theta(n) \) calls to \( A \).  Each call is made on a formula whose length remains \( \Theta(N) \) until the final constant-size instances.

Consequently the aggregate time is
\[
\Theta(n) \cdot N^{K+o(1)} = N^{1+K+o(1)}.
\]

For the local-work budget \( N^{1.1+o(1)} \) appearing in the conditional ARC contraction (with \( \lambda = 1/(5K) \), \( \zeta = 0.1 \)), we have
\[
1+K > 1.1
\]
for every finite \( K \ge 2 \).  Thus classical self-reduction exceeds the stated local-work bound on every sufficiently large input.

This cost holds uniformly for both SAT and UNSAT inputs and is independent of any adaptive strategy that merely reorders the sequence of single-variable fixings without reducing the total number of full-size queries.

The same aggregate bound applies after any fixed polynomial re-encoding that preserves asymptotic length, including the MODELBRIDGE-0012 reduction that maps ordinary 3-SAT to Monotone 3-SAT-(2,2) at a constant blow-up per clause.
