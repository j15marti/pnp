\section{Work: saturated stalls defeat bounded-width stall feedback}\label{sec:work}
\subsection*{Gap}
T3-06 (\S3) proves that every strict stall offers a \emph{critical} resolvent, possibly a duplicate. T1-05 and T4-05 bound the feedback phase polynomially for a fixed width $w$. T4-05 then names the missing step: show that a strict stall always yields a \emph{novel} paid bounded-width consequence, or find a counterexample in which every width-bounded consequence is already present. We settle the bounded-width version negatively.

\begin{definition}
For a CNF $F$ and $w\ge \max_{C\in F}|C|$, let $S_w(F)$ be the closure of $F$ under resolution steps whose non-tautological resolvent has width $\le w$, where a resolvent subsumed by a clause already present is discarded. A clause $R$ is \emph{novel} for $S$ if no clause of $S$ subsumes $R$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[PROVED/EXACT]\label{lem:sat}
$\square\in S_w(F)$ if and only if $F$ has a resolution refutation of width $\le w$. If $\square\notin S_w(F)$ and $F$ is UNSAT, then:
(i) $S_w(F)$ is UNSAT and closed;
(ii) strict stalls of $S_w(F)$ exist (any assignment minimising the number of violated clauses is one, and it violates $\ge1$ clause);
(iii) at \emph{every} assignment $a$, and in particular at every strict stall, every critical resolvent in the sense of T3-06 (and every other resolvent of two clauses of $S_w(F)$) is either non-novel or has width $>w$.
$S_w(F)$ has at most $\sum_{i\le w}2^i\binom{n}{i}$ clauses and can be computed in time $n^{O(w)}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
($\Leftarrow$) Take a width-$\le w$ refutation and induct on its steps. Each derived clause $R$ is subsumed by some $R'\in S_w(F)$. Suppose $R=\mathrm{Res}_p(C,D)$ with $C'\subseteq C$ and $D'\subseteq D$ in $S_w(F)$. If $p\notin C'$ then $C'\subseteq R$. Similarly if $\neg p\notin D'$. Otherwise $\mathrm{Res}_p(C',D')\subseteq R$ has width $\le w$, so it is in $S_w(F)$ or subsumed there. Applied to $R=\square$ this puts $\square$ in $S_w(F)$. ($\Rightarrow$) holds because every clause of $S_w(F)$ is derived by width-$\le w$ steps.
(i) holds since resolution is sound and $F\subseteq S_w(F)$. (ii) holds because $S_w(F)$ is UNSAT. (iii) is closure: any width-$\le w$ resolvent of two clauses of $S_w(F)$ is subsumed in $S_w(F)$ by construction. The size bound counts clauses of width $\le w$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[FALSIFIED: ``strict stall $\Rightarrow$ novel width-$\le w$ consequence'', every fixed $w$]\label{thm:main}
For every fixed $w\ge3$ there are UNSAT 3-CNF formulas $F_n$ with the following properties. The polynomial-time preprocessed database $S_w(F_n)$ ($n^{O(w)}$ work) is $\square$-free. It has strict stalls. At every one of them no novel resolvent of width $\le w$ exists, so no width-$\le w$ resolution-based feedback can acquire a new row. Examples:
(a) Tseitin formulas on bounded-degree expanders;
(b) random 3-CNF with $m=\Delta n$ clauses, $\Delta$ a large constant, w.h.p.;
(c) the 3-CNF ordering principle $\mathrm{GT}_n$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:sat} it is enough that $F_n$ has no width-$w$ refutation for large $n$. Each family has resolution width $\Omega(n)$ (KNOWN THEOREM): (a) and (b) by Ben-Sasson--Wigderson [JACM 2001], where (b) also uses Chv\'atal--Szemer\'edi expansion; (c) by Bonet--Galesi [Comput.\ Complexity 2001] for their 3-CNF version of GT$_n$. The tournament encoding used in \texttt{code/} is a close variant; for it, width growth is checked only computationally (table below).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[PROVED/EXACT, cubic Tseitin: input is already width-3 saturated]\label{prop:tseitin}
Let $G$ be a simple cubic graph with an odd total charge, and let $T(G)$ be its standard Tseitin 3-CNF (4 clauses per vertex). Then $S_3(T(G))=T(G)$. At every strict stall of $T(G)$, every critical resolvent is novel and has width exactly $4$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Two clauses of the same vertex forbid two wrong-parity words, which differ in an even number $\ge2$ of positions. Their resolvent is therefore tautological, since the clauses clash on $\ge2$ literals. Clauses of adjacent vertices share exactly the edge variable, and $G$ is simple, so the resolvent has width $2+2=4$. Clauses of non-adjacent vertices share no variable. So no resolvent of width $\le3$ exists, and every non-tautological resolvent has width 4. No clause of $T(G)$ has width 4, so none can subsume a width-4 resolvent; such a resolvent is novel.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Consequences for the portfolio}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The T1-05/T4-05 credit cannot be paired with productivity at a fixed width.} The bound flips$+$admissions $\le hm+(h+1)K$ holds, but on the families of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} it is spent on a database that saturates without $\square$. To keep the stall productive, $w$ must grow to the refutation width, which is $\Omega(n)$. Then $K\ge\binom{r}{\Omega(r)}=2^{\Omega(r)}$, so the credit is exponential (PROVED/EXACT given the KNOWN THEOREM width bounds).
\item \textbf{Widening by one does help locally, but that cannot be a polynomial policy in general.} In every witness above each stall did offer a novel critical resolvent of width exactly $w+1$ (COMPUTATIONAL; not proved in general). Iterating ``widen by one at saturation'' amounts to running width-$W$ resolution. The Ben-Sasson--Wigderson size--width tradeoff (KNOWN THEOREM) gives tree-like size $\ge 2^{W-w_0}$ and general size $\ge\exp(\Omega((W-w_0)^2/n))$. On (a) and (b) every resolution-based lane, including the DPLL/guarded proof-DAG lane (tree-like resolution), therefore needs exponential size (Chv\'atal--Szemer\'edi; Urquhart for Tseitin). GT$_n$ is the exception: it has polynomial-size resolution refutations even though its width is $\Omega(n)$. It shows that the failure is in \emph{acquisition order} by width, not in the existence of short proofs.
\item \textbf{Where the ``alternate exact contraction'' half stands.} On (a), Gaussian elimination solves Tseitin in polynomial time, so an affine lane escapes. On (b) and (c) the clause-table affine relaxation is trivial, because a $k$-clause with $k\ge2$ has full affine hull. Random 3-CNF is therefore the natural CANDIDATE for a family on which all five lanes, at \emph{fixed} parameters, stall at once: UP/occupancy, clause-table GE, 2-SAT, width-$w$ elimination, and the tree-like DAG. Only the affine lane with derived rows (a Res($\oplus$)-type system) is not covered by a KNOWN THEOREM for general DAGs. That case is OPEN, and recent lower bounds cover only restricted Res($\oplus$) forms; they are not imported here.
\end{enumerate}

\end{itemize}
