\section{Work: certifying the absence of Hall exports}\label{sec:work}
\begin{definition}[T1 Hall certificate]
Let $F$ be a clause set (for example a UP-closed guarded state). Its \emph{exclusion graph}
$X(F)$ has the literals of $F$ as vertices. $\{\ell,\bar\ell\}$ is an edge, and
$\{\bar a,\bar b\}$ is an edge for every binary clause $(a\vee b)\in F$. A \emph{Hall
certificate} is a family $S$ of clauses of $F$ that are pairwise disjoint as sets of signed
literals, together with a set $\mathcal B$ of cliques of $X(F)$ (bins) covering
$\bigcup S$, such that $|\{B\in\mathcal B: B\cap\bigcup S\neq\emptyset\}|<|S|$. (This is T1's
definition. Partition versus cover makes no difference, because subsets of cliques are cliques.)
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Hall certificates are LP refutations]\label{thm:lp}
If $F$ has a Hall certificate $(S,\mathcal B)$, then the polytope
\[
P(F)=\Bigl\{y\in[0,1]^{\mathrm{lits}}:\ y_\ell+y_{\bar\ell}=1,\ \ \textstyle\sum_{\ell\in C}y_\ell\ge1\ (C\in F),
\ \ \sum_{\ell\in K}y_\ell\le1\ (K \text{ a clique of } X(F))\Bigr\}
\]
is empty. Equivalently, any $y\in P(F)$ certifies that $F$ has no Hall certificate, for every
demand family and every bin choice. \lab{PROVED/EXACT}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $y\in P(F)$. The demands give $\sum_{C\in S}\sum_{\ell\in C}y_\ell\ge|S|$. The literals of
distinct demands are distinct and each lies in a touched bin, and $y\ge0$. So the left side is
at most $\sum_{B\text{ touched}}\sum_{\ell\in B}y_\ell\le\#\text{touched}<|S|$. Contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Half-integral criterion]\label{cor:half}
Suppose every clause of $F$ has at least two literals and $X(F)$ is triangle-free. Then
$y\equiv\tfrac12\in P(F)$, so $F$ has no Hall certificate. The check costs one pass over $F$
plus triangle detection in $X(F)$ ($O(|E(X)|^{3/2})$). \lab{PROVED/EXACT}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each clause sum is $\ge 2\cdot\tfrac12$. In a triangle-free graph every clique has at most two
vertices, so each clique sum is $\le1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
(i) Any CNF without binary clauses whose clauses all have width $\ge2$ is Hall-free at the root.
This covers random 3-CNF, the T3 torus, and Steiner/NAE 3-CNF families.
(ii) The converse of Theorem~\ref{thm:lp} fails. An odd-charge triangle component gives
$P(F)=\emptyset$ but has no Hall certificate (see the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:tseitin}). So
Hall certificates are strictly weaker than refutations of this LP.
(iii) Hall certificates are also simulated by cutting planes with polynomially many lines (sum
the demand inequalities against the clique inequalities, which CP derives from the pairwise
clauses) \lab{KNOWN THEOREM-style; standard argument, not re-proved here}. So no Hall-based lane can beat the known CP lower
bounds where those apply.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Tseitin is Hall-free under every guard]\label{thm:tseitin}
Let $G$ be a finite simple graph with charges $b:V\to\F_2$. Let $T(G,b)$ be the standard
Tseitin CNF: edge variables, and for each vertex all $2^{\deg v-1}$ clauses that forbid the
wrong-parity assignments of its incident edges. Let $\rho$ be any partial assignment such that
unit propagation on $T|_\rho$ ends without conflict, and let $F$ be the resulting UP-closed
state. Then $F$ has no Hall certificate. \lab{PROVED/EXACT}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Call a vertex \emph{live} if it has at least one free incident edge. In $F$, a live vertex with
$f$ free edges contributes clauses of width exactly $f$ over exactly those edges. UP-closure
with no conflict rules out $f=1$, so every clause of $F$ has width $\ge2$. Binary clauses come
only from vertices with $f=2$ (``2-vertices''). Each such vertex with free edges $x,y$
contributes the two clauses encoding $x\oplus y=c$, so its exclusion edges form a perfect
matching between $\{x,\bar x\}$ and $\{y,\bar y\}$.

\emph{Cliques.} Two distinct variables share at most one vertex, because $G$ is simple. So a
literal $\ell_y$ is adjacent to exactly one of $x,\bar x$, and no clique contains a
complementary pair plus a third literal. A clique on three distinct variables $x,y,z$ needs
pairwise-shared 2-vertices $v_{xy},v_{yz},v_{xz}$. These are distinct, since a common vertex
would have three free edges. So $x,y,z$ form a triangle of $G$ whose three vertices are all
2-vertices. That triangle is then a whole connected component of the free subgraph, and $x,y,z$
occur in no other clause of $F$. A clique on four variables would need six distinct shared
2-vertices among four edges, which have only eight endpoint incidences: impossible. So every
clique of size $3$ lies inside an isolated \emph{triangle component}.

\emph{Decomposition.} Demands and bins each lie inside one connected component of the free
subgraph (exclusion edges never cross components). If $|\Gamma(S)|<|S|$ then some component $c$
has $|\Gamma(S_c)|<|S_c|$, so it suffices to treat each component separately.

\emph{Components without a triangle component:} $X$ restricted there is triangle-free and all
clauses have width $\ge2$. Corollary~\ref{cor:half}, applied to the component, excludes a
certificate.

\emph{A triangle component} has 3 variables, 6 literals and 6 binary clauses. If its parity is
consistent, the literal exclusion graph is a 6-cycle: colour each literal by its value under a
solution $\sigma$, and note that both $\sigma$ and $\bar\sigma$ satisfy every clause, so every
edge joins opposite colours. It is then triangle-free, and Corollary~\ref{cor:half} applies. If
the parity is inconsistent, the literal graph is two disjoint triangles $T$ and $\bar T$, which
are the only cliques of size 3. Every demand $(a\vee b)$ has $\{\bar a,\bar b\}$ as an edge, so
$\{a,b\}$ lies inside $T$ or inside $\bar T$. Three disjoint demands would partition the six
literals into pairs inside $T$ or $\bar T$, which is impossible because $|T|=3$ is odd. So
$|S|\le2$. Then a deficiency would need $|S|=2$ with a single bin holding four literals, but
cliques have at most three. So there is no certificate.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Scope for the portfolio]\label{cor:portfolio}
On every Tseitin instance, and in particular on T3's toroidal family for every $k$ and both
labels, the T1 Hall lane applied to any conflict-free UP-closed guarded state returns UNKNOWN.
It can never be the first productive step after UP, and a guard is rejected by Hall only if UP
already rejects it. Together with T3's width bound ($\ge k/8$, any fixed-width RUP fails for
large $k$), the torus defeats UP, fixed-width RUP and Hall simultaneously. Exact parity
extraction plus Gaussian elimination still refutes it in polynomial time, so it is \emph{not}
a simultaneous stall of the named portfolio. \lab{PROVED/EXACT for the Hall part; the rest
cites T3 and is KNOWN THEOREM-style}
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{What is not proved.} (a) The theorem covers UP-closed states only. After width-two
RUP saturation under a guard, learned binaries can create exclusion triangles that are not
triangle components (seen in 68 states). Hall-freeness there is supported only
computationally (below), so it stays OPEN. (b) Nothing here says anything about Hall exports on
mixed nonaffine families. Corollary~\ref{cor:half} is a \emph{test} that a designer can run on
such a family; it is not a theorem about all of them. (c) No unrestricted lower bound follows.
