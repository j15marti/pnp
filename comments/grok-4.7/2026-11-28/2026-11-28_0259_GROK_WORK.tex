\subsection*{Setting}
A \emph{full-or-one-hole factor} is $A=(a+P)\setminus\{u\}$, where $P\le\F^r$ is a subspace given by a basis and the hole $u\in a+P$ is optional. This is the class in the T1 fiber theorem: independent local coordinates and binary linear syndrome maps.

\begin{theorem}[Exact zero-overlap merge; PROVED/EXACT]
Let $P\cap Q=\{0\}$, $A=(a+P)\setminus\{u\}$ and $B=(b+Q)\setminus\{v\}$, where either hole may be absent and the corresponding term is then dropped. Then
\[A+B=(a+b+P\oplus Q)\setminus\big[(u+b+Q)\cup(a+v+P)\big].\]
When both holes exist, the two removed affine subspaces meet exactly in $\{u+v\}$, so $|P|+|Q|-1$ targets are lost.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $x+y=x'+y'$ with $x,x'\in a+P$ and $y,y'\in b+Q$, then $x+x'=y+y'\in P\cap Q=0$. So $(x,y)\mapsto x+y$ is injective on $(a+P)\times(b+Q)$, and its image is $a+b+P\oplus Q$. The set $A\times B$ is the product minus $(\{u\}\times(b+Q))\cup((a+P)\times\{v\})$. An injective image of a set difference is the difference of the images. Finally, if $u+y=x+v$, injectivity forces $x=u$ and $y=v$.
\end{proof}
\noindent Membership in $A+B$ takes polynomial time with Gaussian elimination: a coset test plus two coset-exclusion tests. However, the result leaves the full-or-one-hole class whenever both holes exist and $\max(|P|,|Q|)\ge2$. The next theorem shows this departure cannot be absorbed in general.

\begin{lemma}[PROVED/EXACT]
Any three distinct $w_0,w_1,w_2\in\F^r$ form a 2-dimensional coset minus one hole: $\{w_0,w_1,w_2\}=(w_0+\mathrm{span}(w_0{+}w_1,w_0{+}w_2))\setminus\{w_0{+}w_1{+}w_2\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$w_0+w_1$ and $w_0+w_2$ are nonzero and distinct, hence independent over $\F$. The coset is $\{w_0,w_1,w_2,w_0+w_1+w_2\}$, and its fourth point differs from the other three.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Problem MCS3.} Input: $r$, a target $t\in\F^r$, and $k$ triples of distinct vectors $\{w_{i,0},w_{i,1},w_{i,2}\}$, all written in binary. Question: is $t\in\sum_i\{w_{i,0},w_{i,1},w_{i,2}\}$? By the lemma, this is exactly target membership for a sum of $k$ independent full-or-one-hole factors with $\dim P_i=2$ and one hole each.

\begin{theorem}[NP-completeness of iterated full-or-one-hole merging; PROVED/EXACT, reduction from a KNOWN THEOREM]
MCS3 is NP-complete.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
MCS3 is in NP: a choice vector is a certificate checkable in $O(kr)$.

For hardness, use KNOWN THEOREM: positive 1-in-3-SAT is NP-complete with three distinct variables per clause (Schaefer, STOC 1978; Garey--Johnson [LO4]). The question there is whether some assignment makes exactly one variable true in each clause. Given clauses $C_1,\dots,C_m$, duplicate every clause, which does not change satisfiability. Now every used variable $x$ has an occurrence list $o_1,\dots,o_s$ with $s\ge2$. Create one coordinate (row) $\rho_{x,\ell}$ for each consecutive pair $(o_\ell,o_{\ell+1})$. For clause copy $c$ and position $j\in\{0,1,2\}$, let $w_{c,j}$ be the sum of the unit vectors of the rows incident to occurrence $(c,j)$. Set $t=0$. Then $r\le 6m$ and $k=2m$.

\emph{Distinctness.} Each occurrence lies in at least one row, so $w_{c,j}\neq0$. Different positions of one clause hold different variables, so their row sets are disjoint and nonempty, and the three vectors are distinct.

\emph{Correctness.} A choice $(c\mapsto j_c)$ defines $\delta(o)=1$ iff the clause of $o$ chose $o$'s position. Then $\sum_c w_{c,j_c}=\sum_{x,\ell}(\delta(o_\ell)+\delta(o_{\ell+1}))\rho_{x,\ell}$. This sum is $0$ iff $\delta$ is constant along every variable's chain, that is, iff $\delta(o)=z_x$ for one global assignment $z$. Since each clause copy chooses exactly one position and its variables are distinct, $z$ makes exactly one variable true per clause. Conversely, a 1-in-3 assignment $z$ gives each clause copy the position of its unique true variable, and the sum is $0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[CONDITIONAL on P$\neq$NP]
Unless P$=$NP, no polynomial-time procedure decides target membership for arbitrary finite sums of independent full-or-one-hole factors given by explicit binary syndrome maps, even with $\dim P_i=2$ and one hole each. It follows that the T1 fiber theorem cannot be iterated into a polynomial exact merge for arbitrary orders and overlaps. Any proof of the T1 ``first unresolved arrow'' must use structure of \emph{reachable STRICT-EB4 residuals} beyond this factor interface. Examples of such structure: a merge tree in which every merge has both operands in the class and $d\ge1$ (polynomial by the T1 theorem plus closure), or explicit hole lists with $2^d>h_A+h_B$ at every merge.
\end{corollary}
