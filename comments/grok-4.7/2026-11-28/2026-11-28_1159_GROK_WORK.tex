\section{Work: NAE on Steiner triple systems}\label{sec:work}
\begin{definition}
A Steiner triple system STS$(v)$ is a set $\mathcal L$ of 3-subsets (lines) of $[v]$ such
that every pair of points lies in exactly one line. It exists iff $v\equiv1,3\pmod 6$
(\lab{KNOWN THEOREM}, Kirkman 1847). Then $|\mathcal L|=b=v(v-1)/6$, and every point lies
on $r=(v-1)/2$ lines. $F_v$ is the CNF with clauses $(x\vee y\vee z)$ and
$(\bar x\vee\bar y\vee\bar z)$ for each line $\{x,y,z\}$: $v$ variables, $2b$ clauses of
width 3.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\lab{PROVED/EXACT}]\label{thm:mono}
For every STS$(v)$ and every $x\in\{0,1\}^v$ with $s=\sum_ix_i$,
\[\#\{L\in\mathcal L: L\text{ monochromatic under }x\}=\tfrac12\bigl(s-\tfrac v2\bigr)^2+\tfrac{v(v-4)}{24}.\]
Hence $F_v$ is UNSAT for every admissible $v\ge7$, and every assignment violates at least
$\lceil v(v-4)/24\rceil$ lines, about a quarter of all $b$ lines.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For Boolean $x,y,z$, let $A_L=(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)+xyz=1-e_1(L)+e_2(L)$. It equals $1$ if $L$
is monochromatic and $0$ otherwise (check the weights $0,1,2,3$: $1,0,0,1$). Every pair lies
on exactly one line, so $\sum_Le_2(L)=e_2([v])=\binom s2$. Every point lies on $r$ lines,
so $\sum_Le_1(L)=rs$. Therefore
\[\textstyle\sum_LA_L=b-\frac{v-1}2s+\frac{s(s-1)}2=\frac12\bigl(s^2-vs\bigr)+\frac{v(v-1)}6
=\frac12\bigl(s-\frac v2\bigr)^2+\frac{v(v-1)}6-\frac{v^2}8,\]
and $\frac{v(v-1)}6-\frac{v^2}8=\frac{v(v-4)}{24}>0$ for $v>4$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
Non-2-colourability of STS$(v)$, $v\ge7$, is folklore (\lab{KNOWN THEOREM}; e.g.\ via the
same pair count). I have not found the exact closed form above stated in the literature,
but it is elementary, and I make \emph{no novelty claim}. The same computation for any
$\lambda$-fold triple system gives $\lambda$ times the same right-hand side. That case is
\lab{PROVED}, but those systems are not linear, so Theorem~\ref{thm:stall}(a) does not
apply to them.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[\lab{PROVED/EXACT}: explicit static degree-3 refutation]\label{thm:cert}
Let $C^+_L=(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)$ and $C^-_L=xyz$ be the standard clause polynomials (each is
$0$ exactly on the assignments satisfying its clause). In $\mathbb Q[x]/(x_i^2-x_i)$,
\[\sum_{L\in\mathcal L}\bigl(C^+_L+C^-_L\bigr)\;=\;\tfrac12\bigl(s-\tfrac v2\bigr)^2+\tfrac{v(v-4)}{24}.\]
All axiom multipliers are the constant $1$, the cubic terms cancel inside each line, and
the right side is a square plus a positive constant. This is a degree-3 Positivstellensatz
(SOS) refutation of $F_v$ for $v\ge7$ with $2b+O(v^2)$ monomials. It is checkable in
$O(v^2)$ exact rational operations and is produced uniformly from $\mathcal L$ with no
search.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The identity is the polynomial form of the computation in Theorem~\ref{thm:mono}, which
used only $x_i^2=x_i$ (to write $s^2=s+2e_2$). At a satisfying assignment every
$C^\pm_L=0$, so the left side is $0$ while the right side is $\ge v(v-4)/24>0$.
Code part (3) verifies the identity coefficient by coefficient for 14 systems with
$v\le63$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\lab{PROVED}: simultaneous root stall of four bounded rules]\label{thm:stall}
For every STS$(v)$, $v\ge7$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] (Width-$\le2$ RUP; this extends T1's Fano argument to the whole family.) No
guard of $\le2$ literals produces a UP conflict. Hence there is no first new RUP clause
of width $\le2$, under any selection policy and any amount of scanning, and the fixed
point is the input itself.
\item[(b)] (Bounded-boundary elimination.) Every pair of variables co-occurs in a clause,
so the primal graph is $K_v$ and every variable's bucket has boundary $v-1$. No
complete-bucket elimination step with boundary cap $w\le v-2$ is admissible. For example,
T1's width-6 compression is inert for every $v\ge9$.
\item[(c)] (Local affine hull.) Each line's NAE support consists of the six nonconstant
words of $\{0,1\}^3$, whose affine hull is all of $\mathbb F_2^3$. Local GF(2) elimination
therefore has no linear relation to start from.
\item[(d)] (Pair-join binary shadows.) Take two lines, which meet in at most one point.
Every two-variable projection of their joint NAE support is all of $\{0,1\}^2$, so pair
joins yield no binary exclusion.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a) No clause is unit or binary, and a single pin leaves two free literals in every
clause. With two pins on points $p\ne q$, only the unique line $\{p,q,t\}$ contains both.
If the colours differ, nothing is forced. If they are equal, $t$ is forced to the opposite
colour. Every other line contains at most one of $p,q,t$, because two of them already
determine the line $\{p,q,t\}$. So UP stops after one forced bit without conflict.
(b) holds by the STS pair property. (c) is immediate.
(d): let the lines be $L_1,L_2$, sharing at most one point $a$, and fix target values for
two variables $u\ne w$ in $L_1\cup L_2$. If $u,w\in L_1$, choose the third point of $L_1$
to make $L_1$ nonconstant. $L_2$ then has at most one assigned point, and its two free points
can be chosen to make it nonconstant. If $u\in L_1\setminus L_2$ and $w\in L_2\setminus L_1$,
give $a$ (if it exists) any value. Each line then has at most two assigned points and a
free point that makes it nonconstant. If $u=a$, the line containing both $u$ and $w$ has one
free point and the other line has two, and both can be completed. Code part (7) checks this
exhaustively for 21 to 378 line pairs per system.
\end{proof}
