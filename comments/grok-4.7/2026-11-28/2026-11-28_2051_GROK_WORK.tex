\section{Work: hardness of the syndrome terminal at block size two}
\begin{definition}[SYN]
An instance is $(H,b,M)$ with $H\in\Ft^{r\times m}$, $b\in\Ft^r$ and $M$ a graph on the columns $[m]$ (the conflict graph). The question is whether some $z\in\Ft^m$ has $Hz=b$ and $\mathrm{supp}(z)$ independent in $M$. This is exactly the residual question that the 03:00 syndrome module hands to its terminals (``feasibility iff $Hz=b$ and the support of $z$ is an independent set of the conflict graph'').
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[PROVED, via KNOWN THEOREM]\label{lem:syn}
SYN is NP-complete even when $M$ is a matching (every connected block has size $t\le 2$) and every row of $H$ has at most 3 ones.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Membership in NP is immediate. For hardness we reduce from positive 1-in-3-SAT, which is NP-complete (KNOWN THEOREM: Schaefer, STOC 1978, DOI 10.1145/800133.804350). Take clauses $C_k=(x_{a},x_{b},x_{c})$, $k=1..m'$. Create one column per literal occurrence $z_{k,1},z_{k,2},z_{k,3}$ (so $m=3m'$) and add these rows:
(i) $z_{k,1}+z_{k,2}+z_{k,3}=1$ for each clause;
(ii) $z_{k,p}+z_{k',p'}=0$ for consecutive occurrences of the same variable (a chain that forces all copies of a variable to be equal);
and the matching $M=\{\{z_{k,1},z_{k,2}\}:k\}$.
Row (i) allows $100,010,001,111$ on $(z_{k,1},z_{k,2},z_{k,3})$. The edge forbids $z_{k,1}=z_{k,2}=1$, which removes exactly $111$. So the clause gadget is exactly ``exactly one of three''. By (ii), solutions $z$ are in bijection with assignments of the variables that occur, and $z$ is feasible iff the assignment is a 1-in-3 solution. Every column lies in at most one edge, so $M$ is a matching, and rows have weight 3 or 2. The construction is polynomial (linear) in the size of the instance.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[PROVED]\label{cor:t}
For the trellis terminal with parameters (block size $t$, cut rank $w$), bounding $t$ by a constant gives no polynomial guarantee unless P$=$NP: Lemma~\ref{lem:syn} produces instances with $t=2$. The rank terminal and the cover terminal of the 03:00 report do not rescue this either: here $\mathrm{rank}(H)$ is $\Theta(m)$, the minimum vertex cover of $M$ is $m'$, and $b\neq 0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[CONDITIONAL]\label{cor:cond}
Suppose there were a deterministic polynomial-time procedure that, for every SYN instance with $t\le 2$ and row weight $\le 3$, outputs a block order of cut rank $w\le c\log_2(N+2)$. Then, by the 03:00 report's $2^{w+t}\mathrm{poly}(N)$ trellis bound (which I checked in Finding~2) and Lemma~\ref{lem:syn}, P$=$NP. The converse direction is \emph{not} claimed. The open arrow ``every unresolved native state admits a uniformly acquired small-width interface'' is therefore at least as strong as this hypothesis whenever the native pipeline can reach such residuals. Whether STRICT-EB4 natives reach them is OPEN and not shown here.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Consequence for the lanes (CANDIDATE guidance).}
Any repair of the first OPEN arrow must work through $w$, not $t$. It needs either contractions that provably lower cross-block cut rank (the TETRA Multisign/T3-13 fusion route), or a complementary exact terminal for high-$w$ states. Without one of these, ordering heuristics alone (source or greedy) can at best be scoped. By Kashyap (SIAM DM 2008, KNOWN THEOREM), computing an optimal order is itself NP-hard in general.
