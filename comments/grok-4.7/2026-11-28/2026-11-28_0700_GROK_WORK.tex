\subsection{A sharp codimension dichotomy}
\begin{theorem}[PROVED WITH SCOPE]\label{thm:dich}
Let $\mathsf{AffCover}_c$ be the problem: given $n$ and flats $G_1,\dots,G_k\subseteq\F^n$ (as equation lists, $k$ unbounded) with $\mathrm{codim}\,G_i\le c$, decide whether $\bigcup_iG_i=\F^n$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathsf{AffCover}_1$ is in P (one Gaussian elimination).
\item $\mathsf{AffCover}_2$ is coNP-complete, already for \emph{linear} guards of the form $\{x_a=x_b=x_c\}$.
\item For coordinate-subcube guards (each equation fixes one variable), codimension $\le 2$ is in P (2-SAT), and codimension 3 is coNP-complete (3-SAT).
\end{enumerate}
The same holds relative to any explicitly given carrier $A$ (rewrite in free coordinates; codimensions do not increase).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Membership in coNP: an uncovered point is a polynomially checkable witness of non-coverage.
(1) Discard inconsistent guards. If some guard has codimension 0, it covers everything. Otherwise each $G_i=\{a_i\cdot x=b_i\}$, and $x$ is uncovered iff $a_i\cdot x=1+b_i$ for all $i$. This is a linear system, and coverage holds iff it is inconsistent.
(2) Positive NAE-3SAT is NP-complete (Schaefer 1978; KNOWN THEOREM). For a clause $\mathrm{NAE}(x_a,x_b,x_c)$ set $G=\{x_a+x_b=0,\ x_b+x_c=0\}=\{x_a=x_b=x_c\}$, a linear flat of codimension 2. An assignment violates the clause iff it lies in $G$. Hence the guards cover $\F^n$ iff the NAE instance is unsatisfiable, so the complement of $\mathsf{AffCover}_2$ is NP-hard.
(3) The complement of a codim-$c$ subcube is a width-$c$ clause, so coverage is the unsatisfiability of a width-$c$ CNF.
\end{proof}
\noindent\textbf{Consequences (PROVED WITH SCOPE).} (a) An exact polynomial-time ``symbolic coverage'' or ``compression'' routine for arbitrary polynomial-size families of codim-2 affine guards would give P$=$NP. Polynomial-size coverage certificates for all covered families would give NP$=$coNP. Compression therefore has to come from the \emph{acquisition} side: few guards ($k=O(\log N)$, where inclusion--exclusion is polynomial) or special structure such as codimension 1. (b) The hardness lives entirely in guards of codimension 2. Codimension does not separate easy from hard beyond~1.

\begin{proposition}[PROVED WITH SCOPE; Kraft-type count]\label{prop:kraft}
If $G_1,\dots,G_k$ cover a carrier $A$ then $\sum_i2^{-\mathrm{codim}_A G_i}\ge 1$. In particular, a cover using only guards of codimension $\ge c$ needs $k\ge 2^c$ guards. If the cover is irredundant, Brouwer's theorem (KNOWN THEOREM as cited in \texttt{1cab6e6}; spot-checked in code \S1) also gives joint normal rank $r\le k-1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$|A|\le\sum_i|G_i|=\sum_i 2^{-\mathrm{codim}\,G_i}|A|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[PROVED WITH SCOPE; batching does not compose]\label{prop:batch}
On $\F^{2t}$ take the $t$ pairwise disjoint codim-2 guards $G_i=\{x_{2i}=0,x_{2i+1}=0\}$, $i<t$. The uncovered region $U$ is a product of $t$ copies of $\{01,10,11\}$. So $|U|=3^t$, $\mathrm{aff}(U)=\F^{2t}$, and every flat contained in $U$ has at most $2^t$ points. Hence $U$ needs at least $(3/2)^t$ affine pieces.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A flat $L\subseteq U$ lies in the product of its projections to the coordinate pairs. Each projection is a flat inside a 3-point set, so it has size $\le 2$, and $|L|\le 2^t$. The affine hull is full because $U$ contains $1^{2t}$ and every $1^{2t}+e_j$ (flipping one bit of a pair $11$ gives $01$ or $10$), and these differences span $\F^{2t}$.
\end{proof}
For $t=5$ (one five-guard batch): $|U|=243$, $\mathrm{aff}(U)=\F^{10}$, and the code finds 32 disjoint pieces by disjointification while the lower bound is 8. So after one batch the next batch has \emph{no} affine carrier smaller than the whole space. Pieces must be branched on, and the number of pieces can multiply from batch to batch. This is consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:dich}, and it means ``fixed-size batches'' is not by itself a polynomial procedure.

\subsection{Transfer of guard covers to resolution over parities}
Guards in this project are affine (relative port pins and XOR pins), so the natural host system is $\Resx$ (Itsykson--Sokolov, APAL 171 (2020) 102722). Its lines are disjunctions of $\F$-linear equations. Tree-like $\Resx$ refutations correspond, with constant-factor size loss, to parity decision trees (PDTs) for the false-clause search problem (same reference).

\begin{theorem}[PROVED WITH SCOPE]\label{thm:resx}
Let $F$ be an unsatisfiable CNF on $n$ variables. Let flats $G_1,\dots,G_k$ cover $\F^n$, and let $r$ be the rank of all their defining normals together (so $r\le k-1$ for an irredundant cover, by Brouwer). Suppose each $F\wedge G_i$ has a $\Resx$ refutation $\pi_i$ of size $s_i$, where $G_i$'s equations are allowed as axioms.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{DAG:} $F$ has a $\Resx$ refutation of size $\le\sum_i(s_i+1)+2^{r+1}$.
\item \emph{Tree-like:} if every $\pi_i$ is tree-like, i.e.\ a PDT of size $s_i$ solving false-clause search for $F$ on inputs in $G_i$, then $F$ has a tree-like $\Resx$ refutation (a PDT) of size $\le 2^r\max_is_i$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a basis $\ell_1,\dots,\ell_r$ of the span of all normals. Each $G_i$ is a union of cells $\{\ell=v\}$, $v\in\F^r$, and since the $G_i$ cover, each cell lies inside some $G_{i(v)}$.
(2) Query $\ell_1,\dots,\ell_r$ (a complete tree with $2^r$ leaves). At leaf $v$, run the PDT of $G_{i(v)}$. It answers correctly on every input of the cell, because the cell is contained in $G_{i(v)}$. The size is $\le 2^r\max s_i$.
(1) Add the linear clause $\neg G_i$ (the disjunction of the negated equations of $G_i$) to every line of $\pi_i$. Axiom lines from $G_i$ become tautologies, which are introduced by semantic weakening (Itsykson--Sokolov show that semantic $\Resx$ is p-equivalent to syntactic $\Resx$). The result derives $\neg G_i$ from $F$ in $s_i+1$ lines. The $k$ linear clauses $\neg G_i$ mention only linear forms in $\mathrm{span}(\ell)$ and are jointly unsatisfiable, so the complete parity tree on $\ell$ refutes them with $<2^{r+1}$ nodes.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[CONDITIONAL on the cited lower bound; scoped]\label{cor:php}
Itsykson and Sokolov (2020) prove that tree-like $\Resx$ refutations of the weak pigeonhole principle $\mathrm{PHP}^m_n$ have size $2^{\Omega(n)}$ (KNOWN THEOREM; generalised to extensible formulas by Gryaznov--Ovcharov--Riazanov, ToCT 16(3), 2024, Thm.~3.1). Then any cover of $\F^{\mathrm{vars}}$ by $k$ affine guards whose conditional refutations are tree-like $\Resx$ of size $\le s$ (this includes DPLL/failed-literal trees and tree-like resolution) satisfies $(k-1)+\log_2 s\ge\Omega(n)$. With $k=5$ fixed, some lane must spend $2^{\Omega(n)}$. With $k=O(\log n)$ guards and polynomial lane trees, a guard cover of $\mathrm{PHP}^m_n$ cannot exist.
\end{corollary}
\noindent The precise constant (Gryaznov et al.\ state $2^{m'}$ for $m'$-extensible formulas) is quoted from the paper's statement and was not re-derived here.

\noindent\textbf{Scope and limits.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The transfer is at CNF level. For native E/B instances, compose with the compiler-locality transfer of the 04:04 KST log.
\item DAG lanes (RUP, GAC, Gaussian elimination) fall under part~(1). Superpolynomial lower bounds for \emph{general} DAG-like $\Resx$ are OPEN. \item The transfer says nothing about lanes outside $\Resx$ (Cutting Planes; cardinality/Hall reasoning beyond the HALL-SIM hypothesis of the 06:00 log).
\item It does not bound guard \emph{acquisition} cost or prove that any lane is incomplete on the project's sources.
\end{itemize}
