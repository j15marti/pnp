\subsection{Setting}
Let $F$ be a CNF over variables $V$. For a literal $\ell$ write $\ell(x)=x_v$ if
$\ell=v$ and $1-x_v$ if $\ell=\neg v$. The LP relaxation $\mathrm{LP}(F)$ consists of
$I_C:\ \sum_{\ell\in C}\ell(x)\ge1$ for every $C\in F$, together with $0\le x\le1$.
A \emph{guard} $\rho$ is a consistent set of literals.

\begin{definition}[Weighted positive-support rule, linear form]
A WPS certificate for $\neg\rho$ is a nonnegative rational combination of the $I_C$
and the box constraints yielding $\alpha\cdot x\ge\beta$, together with a set $E$
of literals forced by $\rho$ via binary clauses or, more generally, via unit
propagation (UP) from $\rho$, such that $\max\{\alpha\cdot x:\ x\in[0,1]^V,\ x
\text{ agrees with }\rho\cup E\}<\beta$. The rule then learns the clause $\neg\rho$.
\end{definition}
The T1/FP55 sparse-support step is the special case where the positive-coefficient
literals all lie in $E$ and the LHS is $\le0<\beta$, except that T1 uses
exact-one \emph{equalities}, which are not in $\mathrm{LP}(F)$ (see Lemma~2).

\begin{theorem}[\lab{PROVED}; equivalent to a \lab{KNOWN THEOREM}]
For every CNF $F$ and guard $\rho$, a WPS certificate for $\neg\rho$ exists iff
UP on $F\wedge\rho$ reaches a conflict.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
($\Leftarrow$) Suppose UP derives a conflict, and let $E$ be the UP-closure. Some
clause $C$ has every literal false under $\rho\cup E$. Then $I_C$ itself has LHS
$0<1$ on the restricted box.
($\Rightarrow$) Suppose UP from $\rho$ gives no conflict, and let $\tau\supseteq\rho\cup E$
be its closure. Define $x^*_v$ by $\tau$ on assigned variables and $x^*_v=\tfrac12$
otherwise. Take any clause $C$. If $\tau$ satisfies $C$, some literal has value 1.
Otherwise there is no conflict and $\tau$ is UP-closed, so $C$ has at least two
unassigned literals, contributing $\ge\tfrac12+\tfrac12$. Hence $x^*$ satisfies every
$I_C$ and the box, and therefore every nonnegative combination: $\alpha\cdot x^*\ge\beta$.
Since $x^*$ agrees with $\rho\cup E$, the maximum in the definition is $\ge\beta$.
So no certificate exists.
\end{proof}
This is the Blair--Jeroslow--Lowe observation that the LP relaxation of a clause set is
infeasible iff unit resolution refutes it \lab{KNOWN THEOREM}: C.~E.~Blair,
R.~G.~Jeroslow, J.~K.~Lowe, \emph{Comput.\ Oper.\ Res.} 13(5) (1986) 633--645; see also
Chandru--Hooker, \emph{Optimization Methods for Logical Inference}, Wiley 1999. The proof
above is self-contained, so nothing depends on the attribution.

\begin{corollary}[\lab{PROVED}]
If every clause of $F$ has width $\ge g+2$, then no guard with $|\rho|\le g$ has a WPS
certificate. In particular, on a CNF all of whose clauses have width $\ge3$, the linear
weighted-support rule with single-literal guards acquires \textbf{zero} units, whatever
the weights, number of rows or budget. Its closure is exactly failed-literal probing,
which is empty there.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Fixing $\le g$ literals shortens each clause by at most $g$ literals, so each clause
keeps $\ge2$ free literals or is satisfied. UP therefore never fires, and Theorem~1
applies.
\end{proof}
\textbf{Application.} The RANDOM companion is a pure 3-CNF (40 variables, 184
clauses). Every report states that root UP gives zero pins and there are no exact-one rows.
The native RANDOM chart is reported with 184 rows and 552 literals, consistent with
width 3 throughout, but I did not verify widths row by row. So the proposed target
cannot yield anything on RANDOM in the linear single-guard form. The obvious linear
generalization is \lab{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}. A productive variant must use at least one of:
(i) integer rounding (Chv\'atal--Gomory/clique/cardinality cuts), (ii) guards of size
$\ge2$, i.e.\ pair probing and learned binaries, or (iii) projection or extension
variables. With the mandatory early forced-information step (AGENTS.md, \texttt{bcb1ce4}),
failed-literal probing \emph{is} the full linear forced-information content for single
guards. No LP-weighted refinement adds to it.

\begin{lemma}[\lab{PROVED}; why JOINT works]
For an exact-one group $S$ with $|S|\ge3$ encoded by one ALO clause and the pairwise AMO
clauses, the equality $\sum_{v\in S}x_v=1$ is not implied by $\mathrm{LP}(F)$:
$x\equiv\tfrac12$ satisfies all those clauses but gives $\sum=|S|/2>1$. For $|S|=2$ the
equality is LP-implied. The upper half $\sum_S x_v\le1$ is the clique cut, obtained by
integer rounding (rank-1 Chv\'atal--Gomory for $|S|=3$).
\end{lemma}
So the T1/FP55/T2 exact-one lanes owe their power on JOINT to integer rounding (clique
equalities) plus GF(2) and Hall structure, not to LP weights. RANDOM, with no binary
clauses and hence no cliques, offers none of this at the root.

\emph{finite-size} observation only. Each learned clause has a polynomial-size resolution
(RUP) derivation, so a refutation in polynomially many rounds would give a
polynomial-size resolution refutation. For large $n$, random 3-CNF at constant density
w.h.p.\ has no such refutation (Chv\'atal--Szemer\'edi 1988 \lab{KNOWN THEOREM}). Hence
fixed-$k$ probing with polynomially many rounds cannot refute random 3-CNF
asymptotically (\lab{PROVED}, conditional only on that known theorem). The $n\le40$ success
does not scale.
\end{itemize}
