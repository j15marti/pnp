\section{Work: gadget-hidden Tseitin versus the current lanes}\label{sec:work}
\begin{definition}[Composed Tseitin, local encodings]
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite graph with maximum degree $\Delta$, and $\sigma:V\to\F_2$ with
$\sum_v\sigma_v=1$. Let $g:\{0,1\}^k\to\{0,1\}$. Each edge $a$ carries a private block
$y_a\in\{0,1\}^k$, and $X_v=\bigcup_{a\ni v}y_a$. A \emph{local encoding} is a CNF
$F=\bigsqcup_vF_v$ with $\mathrm{vars}(F_v)\subseteq X_v$ and
$F_v(x)=1\iff\bigoplus_{a\ni v}g(y_a)=\sigma_v$. It is \emph{canonical} if $F_v$ has one
blocking clause per violating word of $X_v$. (This is LSPV-3's $F_{G,g,\sigma}$.) Work in
$R=\F_2[x]/(x_i^2+x_i)$. For a clause $C$ let $p_C=\prod_{\ell\in C}\bar\ell$, with
$\bar\ell=1+x$ for $\ell=x$ and $\bar\ell=x$ for $\ell=\neg x$. So $p_C(x)=1$ iff $C$ is
falsified.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Low-degree $\F_2$ certificate]\label{thm:anf}
\lab{PROVED/EXACT} For every local encoding, in $R$:
\begin{align}
\textstyle\sum_{v\in V}\Bigl(1+\prod_{C\in F_v}(1+p_C)\Bigr)&=1, \label{eq:gen}\\
\textstyle\sum_{v}\sum_{j}p_{C_{v,j}}\prod_{i<j}(1+p_{C_{v,i}})&=1, \label{eq:ns}
\end{align}
where $C_{v,1},C_{v,2},\dots$ lists $F_v$. Thus $F$ has an $\F_2$ Nullstellensatz refutation
whose multipliers are multilinear in $X_v$, with degree $\le k\Delta$. If the clauses of each
$F_v$ have pairwise disjoint falsifying sets (for example, canonical), then
$\sum_{C\in F}p_C=1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$R$ is isomorphic to the ring of functions $\{0,1\}^n\to\F_2$, because multilinear
representations are unique. So it suffices to check identities pointwise.
$\prod_{C\in F_v}(1+p_C)(x)=1$ iff every clause of $F_v$ is satisfied, iff $F_v(x)=1$. Hence
$1+\prod_{C\in F_v}(1+p_C)$ is the violation indicator of $v$. That indicator equals
$\sigma_v+\sum_{a\ni v}g(y_a)\pmod 2$, where $g(y_a)$ denotes the function. Summing over $v$
counts each edge term twice, so the left side of \eqref{eq:gen} is $\sum_v\sigma_v=1$.
\eqref{eq:ns} follows from the telescoping identity
$1+\prod_{j=1}^t(1+u_j)=\sum_{j=1}^tu_j\prod_{i<j}(1+u_i)$, proved by induction on $t$. Every
factor uses only variables of $X_v$, and $|X_v|\le k\Delta$. With disjoint falsifying sets,
$p_Cp_{C'}=0$ as functions for $C\neq C'$ in $F_v$. So
$\prod_{C\in F_v}(1+p_C)=1+\sum_{C\in F_v}p_C$, and \eqref{eq:gen} becomes
$\sum_Cp_C=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Cost and what it says about the lanes]
Computing each group ANF by Möbius transform costs $O(k\Delta\,2^{k\Delta})$, so the whole
check costs $O(|V|k\Delta2^{k\Delta})$. This is polynomial in $N$ whenever
$k\Delta=O(\log N)$ (for instance, constant $k$ on bounded-degree graphs, as in every
instance below). For a balanced $g$, the canonical CNF already has $|V|2^{k\Delta-1}$
clauses, so the check is then polynomial in $N$. For heavily biased gadgets that is not
automatic. No extension variable, oracle or search is used. T3-09's
indicator span (``all clause indicators XOR to $1$'') therefore covers \emph{every} gadget,
not only parity. T2's degree-$d$ saturation (\texttt{2692fdf}) covers every local encoding,
once $d\ge k\Delta$. This does not show that a bounded-degree lane is complete. The degree is
the family's local width.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[What the composition does hide]\label{prop:stall}
Let $F$ be canonical, with $G$ cubic, $\sigma$ odd and $g$ nonconstant.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \lab{PROVED/EXACT} If $3k\ge4$ (any $k\ge2$), no clause of width $\le2$ is RUP
with respect to $F$. There are no unit or binary clauses. Hence width-two RUP saturation and
binary-implication/SCC closure are at their initial fixed point, for every $|V|$.
\item[(ii)] \lab{PROVED/EXACT} For $g=\mathrm{AND}_2$ and every vertex of degree $d\ge2$, the
affine hull of the satisfying set of $F_v$ is all of $\F_2^{2d}$. So grouped equal-scope
affine extraction (T3-09's targeted bypass) yields no equation, and GE sees nothing.
\item[(iii)] \lab{PROVED, from KNOWN THEOREMS} For cubic expander families and every fixed
$w$, width-$w$ RUP saturation fails to refute $F$ once $|V|$ is large.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(i) Every clause has width $3k\ge4$. With $\le2$ literals pinned and nothing yet propagated,
a clause becomes unit only if $\ge3k-1\ge3$ of its literals are false. That is impossible, so
unit propagation never fires and never conflicts.

(ii) Write each block as $(y_i,z_i)$. If $\sigma_v=0$, the satisfying set contains $0$ and
every unit vector (one bit set leaves all ANDs $0$), so the hull is everything. If
$\sigma_v=1$, take $p$ with $y_1=z_1=1$ and all else $0$. Then $p+e_j$ is satisfying for each
of the $2d-2$ coordinates $j$ outside block 1. The points
$r_1=p+e_{z_1}+e_{y_2}+e_{z_2}$ (that is, $y_1=1,z_1=0,y_2=z_2=1$) and $r_2$ (the same with
$y_1$ and $z_1$ swapped) are satisfying, because exactly one AND is $1$. Together with the
directions $e_{y_2},e_{z_2}$, they give $e_{z_1}$ and $e_{y_1}$. The directions span
$\F_2^{2d}$.

(iii) $g$ is nonconstant, so it has a sensitive input: some $b\in\{0,1\}^k$ and coordinate
$i$ with $g(b)\ne g(b\oplus e_i)$. In every block, fix all bits except coordinate $i$ to $b$.
Then $g(y_a)=x_a\oplus\beta_a$, where $x_a$ is the free bit. Vertex $v$ now demands
$\bigoplus_{a\ni v}x_a=\sigma'_v:=\sigma_v\oplus\bigoplus_{a\ni v}\beta_a$, and
$\sum\sigma'_v=\sum\sigma_v=1$ because each $\beta_a$ appears twice. Restricting a canonical
CNF gives the canonical CNF of the restricted function. Clauses of violating words that are
inconsistent with the restriction are satisfied, and the rest shrink to blocking clauses. So
$F|_\rho$ is the standard Tseitin CNF $T(G,\sigma')$. Restriction does not increase
resolution size (KNOWN). For cubic expanders, $T(G,\sigma')$ needs resolution size
$2^{\Omega(|V|)}$ (Urquhart 1987; also width $\Omega(|V|)$ via Ben-Sasson--Wigderson 2001)
(KNOWN THEOREM). Now suppose width-$w$ RUP saturation succeeded. Each admitted clause has an
input-resolution derivation of length $\le n+1$ from the current set. There are at most
$\sum_{i\le w}2^i\binom ni$ admissions. So we would get a resolution refutation of size
$\mathrm{poly}(n^w)\cdot|F|$, which is polynomial for fixed $w$ and $k$. That contradicts the
exponential bound once $|V|$ is large.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Answer to the two named gaps]\label{cor:ans}
(a) Gadget-hidden Tseitin hides the odd XOR core from width-two RUP, binary implication and
grouped affine GE. It does not hide it from the $\F_2$ indicator span or from degree-$k\Delta$
packet/PC saturation. As a hostile family for the six-lane portfolio it is
\lab{FALSIFIED/RETIRED}. (b) On this family NONAFFINE-TARGETED-CORRECTION holds
\lab{PROVED/EXACT, family-restricted}. The paid correction is the exact group ANF

the groups gives the terminal $1$. (c) The general NONAFFINE-TARGETED-CORRECTION lemma and the
UCAC load-bearing target remain \lab{OPEN}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Where hostile families must come from; limits]
A family hostile to the $\F_2$ algebraic lanes needs large $\F_2$ polynomial-calculus degree.
KNOWN THEOREMS supply such families: random 3-CNF of suitable constant density
(Ben-Sasson--Impagliazzo, \emph{Comput.\ Complexity} 2010; Alekhnovich--Razborov 2001/03),
Tseitin mod $q$ for odd prime $q$ on expanders over $\F_2$ (Buss--Grigoriev--Impagliazzo--Pitassi,

some other lane (for example, $\F_q$ elimination for mod-$q$ Tseitin). A family hostile to
\emph{every} polynomial-time lane would itself be a lower-bound statement, which is open. The
theorem says nothing about Walsh/Fourier lanes on this family, which I did not test, or about
non-local encodings where one clause mixes blocks of non-adjacent vertices. The merged
encoding (\S\ref{sec:comp}) shows that the constant-coefficient span alone is
encoding-fragile. Only the product form \eqref{eq:ns} is encoding-robust.
\end{remark}
