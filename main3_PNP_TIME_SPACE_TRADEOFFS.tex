\paragraph{Named candidate and falsifiers.}
\textsf{C3-T5-FOREST-CYCLE-DUAL068} is a pair of uniform exact
algorithms selected after charged graph preprocessing. For every
finite digraph with $n$ explicitly named vertices, $m$ distinct

\paragraph{Source and baseline.}

unary declarations of $n,m$ and fixed-width binary arc endpoints,

\begin{theorem}[Binary forest and independent component shifts]
If $G$ has no self-loop and no pair of opposite arcs, then
\[
 \#\mathrm{Hom}(G,T_5)
 =5^c\sum_{\beta\in\{0,1\}^{n-c}}
 [\,\text{the forest assignment from }\beta
       \text{ satisfies all non-tree arcs}\,].
\]
One uniform blank-start deterministic locally headed implementation
computes this count and YES/NO in
$O((N+1)^6(1+2^{n-c}))$ bit time and
$O((N+1)^3)$ simultaneous writable bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose a spanning forest of the undirected support. Anchor each
component root at colour zero. Each parent-child edge permits
exactly two child colours, obtained by adding $1$ or $3$ if the
source edge points toward the child and subtracting these increments
otherwise. Every choice of the $n-c$ binary decisions produces one
unique rooted forest labelling. Checking every remaining arc
establishes a bijection with rooted homomorphisms. Independently
adding any of five constants to every label of each component
preserves all constraints and changes each root. This action is
free, proving the factor $5^c$. The full locally headed machine
pays for reading and validating its source, an $O(n^2)$-bit
adjacency representation, forest discovery, each real recursive
branch and incident-edge check, binary counting, head movements,
and output. Its search tree has $O(n2^{n-c})$ nodes.
The conservative sixth-degree polynomial bounds those operations
on a fixed finite-tape implementation; all live objects fit the
stated polynomial space.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact cycle-space and polynomial-space Fourier counter]
Orient the $m$ arcs as given and let
$C\in\{-1,0,1\}^{r\times m}$ be the signed fundamental-cycle
matrix of a source-constructed spanning forest, interpreted modulo
five. Then
\[
 \#\mathrm{Hom}(G,T_5)
 =5^c\sum_{d\in\{1,3\}^m}[Cd=0\bmod 5].
\]
Writing $\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/5)$, the same integer equals
\[
 \frac{5^c}{5^r}\!
 \sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb Z_5^r}
 \prod_{e=1}^m
 \bigl(\zeta^{\langle\lambda,C_e\rangle}
       +\zeta^{3\langle\lambda,C_e\rangle}\bigr).
\]
An exact streaming realization has
$O((N+1)^8(1+5^r))$ time and
$O((N+1)^3)$ simultaneous writable bits.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every actual vertex assignment induces edge differences
$d_e=x_{v(e)}-x_{u(e)}$ and these telescope to zero on
fundamental cycles. Conversely, any $d\in\{1,3\}^m$ with
$Cd=0$ integrates uniquely on the forest after fixing component
roots to zero; the cycle equations verify every chord.
There are exactly $5^c$ original labellings per integrated $d$.

Character orthogonality over $\mathbb Z_5^r$ gives
$[Cd=0]=5^{-r}\sum_\lambda\zeta^{\langle\lambda,Cd\rangle}$.
Interchanging two finite sums produces the product formula.
Instead of storing or approximating complex numbers, evaluate it
using four signed integer coefficients in
$\mathbb Z[z]/(z^4+z^3+z^2+z+1)$, streaming one
$\lambda$ at a time. The final sum is a scalar divisible by $5^r$.
Each intermediate coordinate has $O(m+r)$ bits, because there are
at most $2^m$ terms per product and $5^r$ accumulated products;
fixed-degree reduction increases bit lengths by only $O(m)$.
Retain the source-derived $O(mr)$ cycle entries, one radix-five
address counter, four-coordinate intermediates, exact accumulator,
and the source/working heads. This proves the stated joint
polynomial-space ledger and polynomial-per-term time, with all
construction and failed arithmetic operations charged.
\end{proof}

 \boxed{T(N)=(N+1)^{O(1)}
       (1+\min\{2^{n-c},5^r\}),\qquad
       S_{\rm peak}(N)=(N+1)^{O(1)}.}
\]

$5^{w+1}\operatorname{poly}(N)$ time and

\[
  \sum_{k=0}^a\sum_{\ell=0}^b
  {a\choose k}{b\choose\ell}

tree vertex. In particular, a directed triangle has 15, a
transitive triangle has 5, and a directed five-cycle has 10

\section{New exact T5 reduction: degree-two cyclic weighted suppression (/069)}

\noindent\textbf{Status: PROVED/EXACT constructive upper bound;
independent mathematical review OPEN; finite tests COMPUTATIONAL.}
The named dependency \textsf{C3-T5-DEG2-SUPPRESSION069}
strengthens the source-authenticated /068 forest/cycle-rank solver.
Fix any explicit original directed graph $G$ on $n$ vertices, with
binary source length $N\ge n+m$. Reject malformed sources; a
self-loop or opposed arc pair yields exact count zero, while
duplicate copies of the same directed arc are redundant.
Define the undirected support 2-core $H_2$ by leaf peeling, and
let $h$ count its vertices with degree at least three.

\begin{theorem}[Five-coordinate degree-two suppression]
There is one uniform blank-start locally headed deterministic
algorithm that outputs the exact original-label count
$Z(G)=|\operatorname{Hom}(G,T_5)|$ and its zero/nonzero decision
with same-realization resource guarantees
\[
 T(N)=O((N+1)^8(1+5^h)),\qquad
 S_{\rm peak}(N)=O((N+1)^3)\ {\rm writable\ bits}.
\]
After the stated elimination the residual weighted support has
$k\le h$ vertices and $q$ nonempty components, and at most
$5^{k-q}$ anchored candidate assignments are tested.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Represent every real directed edge between $a<b$ by an integer
vector $W_{ab}(d)$, $d\in\mathbb Z_5$, where the target constraint
is $W_{ab}(x_b-x_a)$. For edge $a\to b$ it is the indicator of
$d\in\{1,3\}$ and for $b\to a$ it indicates
$-d\in\{1,3\}$. Every intermediate state maintains an
integer scalar $z$ and this \emph{exact} original count identity:
\[
 Z(G)=z\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z_5^{V_{\rm active}}}
               \prod_{\{a,b\}}W_{ab}(x_b-x_a).
\]
A degree-zero vertex multiplies $z$ by five.
For a degree-one vertex $v$ adjacent only to $u$,
sum over its colour, multiply $z$ by
$\sum_{d=0}^4W_{uv}(d)$, and remove that factor.
These sums are independent of $x_u$ by translation symmetry.
For a degree-two vertex $v$ with two distinct neighbours
$a<b$, orient its adjacent factors as $F(x_v-x_a)$ and
$J(x_b-x_v)$ and replace the two by the exact cyclic convolution
\[
 H(d)=\sum_{s=0}^{4}F(s)J(d-s\bmod5).
\]
If an edge factor already exists between $a,b$, combine it
with $H$ \emph{pointwise}, not by a second convolution.
The displayed identity follows by exchanging finite summations,
at each elimination, including all disconnected components.
Each remaining factor is translation-invariant.
The residual assignment sum fixes one colour to zero per
connected component and multiplies by $5^q$; the other
$k-q$ colours are exhaustively evaluated, retaining their
exact integer multiplicities.

Eliminate degree at most one until the ordinary 2-core remains;
then suppress degree at most two, always processing newly
created lower-degree vertices first. Deleting degree-two $v$
and adding an edge between its neighbours never increases either
neighbour's simple support degree. Hence a final vertex of
degree at least three had degree at least three in $H_2$:
$k\le h$. Every factor entry is a nonnegative count of some
eliminated original-variable completions with boundary colours
fixed, and is at most $5^n$. Thus all coefficients, scalars,
addresses and output integers have $O(n+1)$ bits.
At most $n(n-1)/2$ factors of five such integers, a source
buffer, an active-degree table, counters and final assignments
fit in $O((N+1)^3)$ simultaneous writable bits.
There are at most $n$ eliminations, each doing at most 25
products and polynomial sequential source/factor tape scans.
Each of at most $5^{k-q}$ final assignments scans only
polynomially many bounded-precision factors. Conservative
$O((N+1)^8)$ bit steps per iteration and for all preprocessing,
head travel, address arithmetic, multiplication, cache retention,
malformed-input handling and exact output suffice.
No implicit Oracle, shared free buffer or preloaded weighted
graph is assumed. The Python reference is not a native physical
microcode trace.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Chosen dependency and falsifiers.}
The new lemma closes a specific instance of
\textsf{C3-T5-CORE-QUOTIENT}: construct an exact source-derived
quotient for dense, nontrivial minimum-degree-three cores.
The intended counterchecks are (i) that in/out identical-neighborhood
classes might not be independent or might have nonuniform external arcs,
(ii) a forbidden complete $2$-by-$2$ biclique could exist in $T_5$,
(iii) counting a two-color support by $2^{m_i}-2$ could lose or
double-count original vertex assignments, and (iv) discovering supports,
enumerating states, integer precision or materialized source
might invalidate the same-machine ledger. All four are resolved in
the declared model; no arbitrary-solver lower bound is asserted.

\begin{theorem}[Exact directed independent-twin count]
Take every finite source-authenticated digraph $G=(V,E)$
with $n$ named vertices, and normalize repeated identical arcs.
A self-loop or a pair of opposite arcs has exact count zero.
For the other graphs, identify $u\sim v$ iff both their \emph{complete}
out-neighbor and in-neighbor sets are identical. Let $n_0$ be the
number of isolated original vertices, and let
$C_1,\ldots,C_q$ be the remaining equivalence classes,
with sizes $m_i=|C_i|$. Form the quotient oriented graph $Q$
in which $i\to j$ means that \emph{all} arcs $C_i\to C_j$
are present. Define
\[
{\cal S}=\{S\subseteq\mathbb Z_5:1\le|S|\le2\},
\qquad
a_i(S)=\begin{cases}
1,&|S|=1,\\
2^{m_i}-2,&|S|=2.
\end{cases}
\]
For every $G$, the exact homomorphism count is
\[
\boxed{
Z(G)=5^{n_0}
\sum_{(S_1,\ldots,S_q)\in{\cal S}^q}
\left(\prod_{i=1}^q a_i(S_i)\right)
\prod_{i\to j\in E(Q)}
   [\,S_i\times S_j\subseteq R\,].
}
\]
One uniform deterministic blank-start locally headed machine
constructs and evaluates this sum, correctly rejects malformed source
words, and computes both the count and its zero/nonzero answer
using the \emph{same complete execution} with
\[
 \boxed{
 T(N)=O((N+1)^8(1+15^q)),\qquad
 S_{\mathrm{peak}}(N)=O((N+1)^3)\ \text{writable bits},
 }
\]
for a canonical unary-count binary graph encoding of length
$N\ge n+m$. For $q=O(\log N)$ the time is polynomial.
No claim about a mandatory representation for arbitrary algorithms
is included.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equality of both complete in/out neighborhood signatures is
an equivalence relation. Distinct equivalent vertices cannot
be adjacent: if $u\to v$, then $v\in N^+(u)$ but
$v\notin N^+(v)$ because the source has no loops.
Suppose $u\in C_i$ and $v\in C_j$ with $u\to v$.
All $u'\in C_i$ have the same outgoing neighborhood, hence
$u'\to v$, while all $v'\in C_j$ have the same incoming
neighborhood, hence $u'\to v'$. Thus every distinct
pair of classes has either no arcs or exactly one orientation of
all possible bipartite arcs.

Every color has precisely two outneighbors and two inneighbors in
$T_5$. If $C_i$ is incident with any quotient arc and
$S_i$ is its set of actually used colors in a satisfying original
assignment, then $S_i$ lies in the two-element
in- or out-neighbor set of some color at the opposite class.
Therefore $1\le |S_i|\le2$.
For two colors $a\ne a'$ the intersection
$N^+(a)\cap N^+(a')$ has at most one member:
$N^+(a)=\{a+1,a+3\}$ modulo five.
Consequently two adjacent quotient classes cannot
\emph{both} use two colors. This is a valid optional pruning
condition, not a needed premise of the formula.

A fixed nonempty support $S_i$ of size one admits exactly one
assignment of the $m_i$ labeled original vertices.
For a fixed support of size two there are $2^{m_i}$ maps
and exactly two fail to use both colors, leaving $2^{m_i}-2$.
All original assignments partition disjointly by their support
tuple. As each quotient arc is complete bipartite, all its
individual vertex arcs hold if and only if
$S_i\times S_j\subseteq R$. Isolated vertices contribute
five independent choices apiece. Multiplying the exact
onto-multiplicities and summing proves the identity.

To realize it physically, validate unary counts and every endpoint,
deduplicate arcs, reject loops/opposed arcs as zero and malformed
words as INVALID, build explicit in/out bit matrices, and compare
all pairs of signature vectors. Store exact class memberships,
the quotient graph, up to $q$ five-bit masks, at most $15q$
integer weights, physical addresses/counters and the output.
There are at most $15^q$ mask tuples and each checks at most
$q^2$ quotient arcs. Every nonnegative summand represents
a disjoint family of original assignments; consequently every
intermediate partial sum is at most $5^n$ and has
$O(n+1)$ bits. All retained buffers and work-head positions fit
$O((N+1)^3)$ writable bits. Conservatively charging locally
headed scans, membership tests, failed tuples, bit arithmetic
including exponentiation, multiplication, source acquisition,
authentication, discovery and final output gives
$O((N+1)^8)$ steps per tuple and for all preprocessing.
The same deterministic run meets both stated bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Five-state pair-intersection rigidity]
Write $N^+(a)=\{a+1,a+3\}\subseteq\mathbb Z_5$.
For $a\ne b$,
\[
|N^+(a)\cap N^+(b)|=
\begin{cases}1,&b-a\equiv2\text{ or }3\pmod5,\\
0,&b-a\equiv1\text{ or }4\pmod5.
\end{cases}
\]
No three distinct source colors have a common target successor.
Exactly five unordered distinct-color pairs are compatible.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The two elements of $N^+(a)$ differ by two modulo five.
Intersection is nonempty exactly when the second pair is a shift
by two or minus two, and then it contains one element.
Every target symbol has precisely two incoming neighbors;
therefore three distinct colors cannot all point to one symbol.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Nearcomplete pair coverage and palette rigidity]
Fix $k\ge4$ hubs and any set $F\subseteq\binom{[k]}3$
of missing triples. Let
$U=\bigcup_{S\in F}S$, $s=|U|$, and assume $s<k$.
Put one private sink leaf $L_S$ for each $S\notin F$,
with incoming arcs from precisely the three hubs in $S$.
Every pair of hubs lies in a \emph{present} triple.
Every valid hub labeling uses at most two different
$\mathbb Z_5$ colors, and a two-color labeling
must use one of the five unordered pairs $\{a,a+2\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For two hubs in $U$, choose a third hub outside $U$;
its triple is present because every missing triple lies inside $U$.
For a pair containing an outside hub, choose any distinct third
hub; its triple is again present. These choices are available
because $k\ge4$.
A triple sink needs a common successor of its three colors.
Each target color has only two predecessors, and two
distinct hub colors have a common successor precisely when
they differ by $+2$ or $-2$ modulo five.
Thus every pair of distinct hub colors is adjacent in the
undirected five-cycle with steps $\pm2$.
Since that cycle has no triangle, a legal hub labeling uses
at most two distinct colors.
\end{proof}

\[

       \ \text{or}\ S\subseteq U\setminus B\}|,
\qquad
e_F(B,p)=\binom{u+p}{3}+\binom{k-u-p}{3}-f_F(B).

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle

5\left[
  \sum_{B\subseteq U}\ \sum_{p=0}^{r}
  \binom rp\,2^{e_F(B,p)}-2^m
 \right].}
\]
There is one deterministic complete locally headed
source-to-integer-output computation with, on recognized instances,
\[

\qquad S_{\mathrm{peak}}(N)=O((N+1)^3)\ {\rm bits}.

\begin{proof}

assign its first color to exactly those hubs, and choose
$p$ among the $r$ unaffected hubs to get the first color.

exactly two legal extensions; a present mixed two-color
triple has exactly one. The number of
\emph{present} monochromatic triples equals $e_F(B,p)$:
start with all $\binom{u+p}{3}+\binom{k-u-p}{3}$
monochromatic triples and remove the $f_F(B)$ missing
ones. Thus the complete leaf-extension multiplicity is

\section{Authenticated Circuit Value source}
For $n\geq1$, the binary source has unary $1^n0$, followed by $n$ gate
records. A record contains a two-bit opcode for constant zero, constant
one, AND or OR and two $n$-bit predecessor masks. Constants have zero
masks. For other gates each mask has exactly one bit set, naming a
strictly earlier gate. Repeated predecessors are allowed. The last gate
is the output. Thus the valid source length is
\[
N=2n^2+3n+1.
\]
Define $f(x)=0$ on malformed strings. A fixed total locally headed
multitape evaluator $A$ first validates the unary header and total length,
before allocating the VALUE prefix. It then rescans real source records,
reads predecessor values by a physical VALUE-tape scan, and writes each
new value. Induction over gate order proves both-label correctness.
Valid-source time is $O(nN\log(N+2)+N\log(N+2))$; a loose all-input
envelope is $O(N^2\log(N+2))$. Valid-source writable space is
$O(n+\log(N+2))$. Invalid length claims reject before large allocation.
Each individual tape head can be kept below $N+d$ for a sufficiently
large fixed constant $d$; separate fixed counter tapes hold addresses.
No input-dependent advice or graph is preloaded.

This grammar is P-complete under logspace reductions. Starting from
Circuit Value \cite{Ladner1975}, replace each gate with positive and
negative monotone rails. NOT swaps rails; AND and OR use their De Morgan
dual on the negative rail. Append an output-copy AND gate. A logspace
transducer prints the unary header and one-hot masks by counters and
rescans. The increase in length is polynomial, and the final bit is
unchanged. Membership follows by the actual evaluator $A$.

\section{A fully local public clock}
Choose an even fixed integer $C\geq2$ exceeding the cubic-time constant
of $A$, including the initial source-head return. Choose a fixed $d\geq8$
so that, for every $u\geq d$,
\[
2^u\geq(C+4)u^3.
\]
Such a $d$ is computable by finite search because $2^u/u^3$ increases
for $u\geq8$ and is unbounded. Put $b(N)=N+d$.

\begin{lemma}[Three bounded bouncing clocks]
There is a fixed finite-control, three-tape clock which, after an exact
$b$-step bootstrap on an $N$-bit source, runs for exactly $Cb^3$ steps.
Throughout, each clock head remains in cells $0,\ldots,b-1$.
At the end of the epoch all three heads are at cell $b-1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
During bootstrap, sweep the source and then $d$ blank positions. On each
clock tape write left and right endpoint symbols at cells $0$ and $b-1$.
The first $b-1$ steps move right; the final step stays while writing the
right endpoint. A fixed counter of $d$ states after the first source
blank locates this endpoint; no free length oracle is used.

An enabled clock head moves one cell in its current direction whenever
that move remains inside the endpoints. At an outward-facing endpoint,
it stays for one step, reverses its direction, and emits a pulse.
A half-sweep therefore takes exactly $b$ enabled steps. Enable the first
clock on every physical transition, the second only on first-clock
pulses, and the third only on second-clock pulses. The third clock emits
one pulse per $b^3$ transitions. Count $C$ such pulses in finite control.
Since $C$ is even, the numbers of half-sweeps on the three tapes are
$Cb^2$, $Cb$, and $C$, respectively, all even. Every head returns to its
initial right endpoint. Endpoint symbols and direction bits suffice for
each local transition. All initialization, moves, and endpoint holds are
paid transitions.
\end{proof}

\section{Complete block-respecting realization}
Construct one fixed machine $M_*$ with $A$'s tapes, the three clock tapes,
and a fresh MARK tape. All work tapes start blank and all heads at zero.
The source tape is never modified. The only nonblank initial tape is the
original binary input.

\paragraph{Bootstrap.}
Use the exact $b$-step procedure above. The original source head ends at
$b-1$, still inside its first tape block. MARK remains at zero. The
VALUE and counter tapes remain blank.

\paragraph{Synchronous evaluation.}
For the next $Cb^3$ global transitions run $A$ and the clock as a finite
multitape product. This includes $A$ returning to the start of the source,
parsing, validation, rereads, gate evaluation, and storing its Boolean
answer. After $A$ finishes, freeze its tape contents and head locations
while retaining $f(x)$ in control. The chosen constant $C$ guarantees
completion before the clock expires. This is one machine, not a network:
each product step reads and updates a fixed number of local heads.

\paragraph{Alignment and marker.}
At the end of the epoch all clock heads are at the right endpoint.
Freeze $A$ and clocks 2 and 3. Allow clock 1 one $b$-step half-sweep left.
MARK stays at zero. On the next rightward half-sweep, move MARK right on
each of the first $b-1$ steps. At the last endpoint-hold step, move MARK
right exactly if $f(x)=1$. Hence its final position is $b-1+f(x)$.
One additional paid read/output transition then halts.

The only possible tape-block crossing is the last MARK move, at absolute
time $Cb^3+3b$, an integer multiple of $b$. Every other head always stays
inside its first $b$-cell block. Thus authentication and initialization,
not merely the final suffix, obey the block-respecting condition.

\begin{theorem}[P-complete authentic first-visit query at legal width]
For the fixed machine $M_*$, let
\[
t(N)=Cb(N)^3+3b(N)+1,\qquad j(N)=Cb(N)^2+4.
\]
Then $b(N)\geq\lceil\log_2t(N)\rceil$, $b(N)=\Theta(t(N)^{1/3})$,
and the predicate
\[
E(x)=\bigl[((\mathrm{MARK},0,2),(1,j(|x|)))
           \in E(G_{M_*,x})\bigr]
\]
is P-complete under logspace reductions. It is exactly $f(x)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The partitioned elapsed time is
\[
b+Cb^3+b+b+1=t(N).
\]
The dummy read begins time block $j(N)$, since
$(j(N)-1)b+1=t(N)$. Before that read, MARK has only read cells in
block 1. At the read it is in block 2 if and only if $f(x)=1$.
Williams's first-visit edge definition therefore gives exactly $E(x)=f(x)$.
The specified source node denotes the initially blank second MARK block;
chronological edges are distinct from that source edge.

Since $t\leq(C+4)b^3\leq2^b$, the required logarithmic condition holds.
The cubic relation yields $b=\Theta(t^{1/3})$.
Both $b(N)$ and the binary index $j(N)$ can be calculated in $O(\log N)$
workspace with charged input-length acquisition and binary arithmetic.
Combining these with the logspace Circuit Value compiler proves hardness.
Actual simulation, or the original evaluator $A$, proves membership in P.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact two-terminal transfer for the five-symbol tournament}
\label{sec:tmr3-t5-two-terminal091}
Let $R(x,y)$ mean $y-x\in\{1,3\}\pmod5$. For a directed
two-terminal source $H$ with distinct terminals $s,t$, define
$F_H(d)$ to be its number of original-vertex homomorphisms with
$x_s=0,x_t=d\in\mathbb Z_5$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact two-terminal composition]
For two directed sources $H,J$ with disjoint internal vertices
and terminals identified in parallel or in series, respectively,
\[
F_{H\parallel J}(d)=F_H(d)F_J(d),\qquad
F_{H\circ J}(d)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z_5}F_H(a)F_J(d-a).
\]
For $k$ otherwise disjoint modules sharing precisely the same
terminal pair $s,t$, their union has exact count
\[
 Z(G)=5\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z_5}\prod_{i=1}^{k}F_{H_i}(d).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Translation by any $c\in\mathbb Z_5$ on all source colors preserves
each edge constraint, bijectively. Thus a conditional count with
endpoint values $a,b$ equals $F_H(b-a)$. For fixed endpoints,
parallel modules have independent interiors, giving multiplication.
In series, sum once over the color of the shared vertex, which is
not double-counted. For common-terminal parallel modules, additionally
sum over the five possible global colors of $s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Complete-biclique terminal signature]
Orient every arc of $K_{p,p}$, $p\ge2$, from left to right,
and mark two distinct left-side terminals. Then
\[
(F_p(0),F_p(1),F_p(2),F_p(3),F_p(4))=
(3\cdot2^{p-1}-2,\ 0,\ 2^{p-2},\ 2^{p-2},\ 0).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The common out-neighbor sets of two terminal colors $(0,d)$ are
$\{1,3\},\emptyset,\{3\},\{1\},\emptyset$ for $d=0,\ldots,4$.
For $d=2,3$ each right-side vertex is forced to the unique
common out-neighbor and each of $p-2$ private left vertices has two
choices. For $d=0$, configurations using exactly one of the two
available right colors contribute $2\cdot 2^{p-2}$; configurations
using both contribute $2^p-2$, with each private left vertex forced
to the common predecessor color. The sum is $3\cdot 2^{p-1}-2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Biconnected high-width easy family]

|E(B_{k,p})|&=kp^2,\\

\tau_{\mathrm{miss}}(B_{k,p})&=k(p-2),\\

\end{aligned}

bipartite complement of missing cross-edges.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

all modules. Deleting a private vertex preserves each module's
connectivity. The $K_{p,p}$ subgraph gives the treewidth bound,
and private vertices have degree $p$. All $k(p-2)$ private left
vertices cover the missing cross-edges. Conversely, match each
private left vertex of module $i$ to a distinct right vertex
of module $i+1$ cyclically. This matching has size $k(p-2)$;
K\"onig's theorem gives equality. Substitute the preceding
five-entry vector in the common-terminal product formula.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact three-terminal transfer and a 3-connected high-width easy family}
\label{sec:tmr3-three-terminal-092}
Throughout this section the fixed tournament satisfies
$R(a,b)\iff b-a\in\{1,3\}\pmod5$. For a three-terminal source
$H=(V,E;s,t,u)$ define
\[
F_H(d,e)=\#\{x:V\to\mathbb Z_5:
 x_s=0,\ x_t=d,\ x_u=e,\ \forall(v,w)\in E\ R(x_v,x_w)\}.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Exact three-terminal gluing]
If sources $H_1,\ldots,H_k$ share exactly the three named terminals
$s,t,u$, all other vertices are pairwise disjoint, and the union has
no further arcs, then
\[
Z\Bigl(\bigcup_{i=1}^k H_i\Bigr)=
5\sum_{d=0}^4\sum_{e=0}^4\prod_{i=1}^kF_{H_i}(d,e).
\]
The interface has at most $25$ relative-color entries, regardless of
the number of modules, but computing the entries may remain hard.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Simultaneously adding any $c\in\mathbb Z_5$ to every color
preserves every arc predicate. Fixing $x_s=0$ picks exactly one of
the five translates of each full assignment. Conditional on
$(x_s,x_t,x_u)=(0,d,e)$ the private assignments of the modules
are independent, so their exact multiplicities multiply.
Summing over $d,e$ and the five translates gives the identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[All-forward complete bipartite three-terminal vector]
For $p\ge3$, orient every arc of $K_{p,p}$ from left to right and
designate three distinct left-side terminals. Its $25$-entry
relative-color vector is zero except for
\[
F_p(0,0)=a_p:=2^p+2^{p-2}-2
\]
and the six entries
\[
F_p(0,2)=F_p(0,3)=F_p(2,0)=F_p(3,0)
=F_p(2,2)=F_p(3,3)=b_p:=2^{p-3}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Any color on the right must be dominated by every used left
color. Every color of the fixed T5 tournament has exactly two
in-neighbors, so at most two distinct left colors may occur.
Among pairs containing $0$, only $\{0,2\}$ and $\{0,3\}$ share
a common out-neighbor. For three equally colored terminals,
all private left colors equal to $0$ contribute $2^p$ assignments.
Each of the two eligible second colors contributes
$2^{p-3}-1$ additional choices for the private left side,
with a forced right-side color. This totals $a_p$.
For each of the six nonconstant compatible terminal tuples,
all $p-3$ private left vertices have two permitted choices,
while each right vertex is forced; the count is $b_p$.
Other tuples have no legal right color.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Three-vertex-connected high-width tractable family]
For $k\ge2$ and $p\ge4$, construct $C_{k,p}$ from $k$ all-forward
$K_{p,p}$ modules by identifying the same three left-side
terminals across all modules and no other vertices. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
|V(C_{k,p})|&=3+k(2p-3),&
|E(C_{k,p})|&=kp^2,\\
\kappa(C_{k,p})&=3,&
\delta(C_{k,p})&=p,\\
\operatorname{tw}(C_{k,p})&\ge p,&
\tau_{\mathrm{miss}}(C_{k,p})&=k(p-3),\\
Z(C_{k,p})&=5\left[
 (2^p+2^{p-2}-2)^k+6(2^{p-3})^k
 \right].
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $\kappa$ is undirected vertex-connectivity, and
$\tau_{\mathrm{miss}}$ is minimum vertex cover in the bipartite
missing-cross-edge complement.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Deleting any two vertices leaves at least one of the three
common terminals, adjacent to every remaining right vertex;
each private left vertex retains at least $p-2$ right
neighbors. Thus the graph remains connected. Deleting all
three common terminals separates its $k$ modules, proving
$\kappa=3$. Every nonterminal has degree $p$.
Each module contains an intact $K_{p,p}$ subgraph, so
$\operatorname{tw}\ge p$. All missing cross-edges are incident
on the $k(p-3)$ private left vertices, giving a complement
vertex cover of that size. Conversely, cyclically match each
private left vertex of module $i$ to a distinct right vertex
of module $i+1$; these pairs are missing cross-edges and
form a matching of size $k(p-3)$, proving equality by
K\"onig's theorem. The counting formula follows from the
preceding lemma and three-terminal gluing.
\end{proof}


\begin{theorem}[Unbounded-connectivity exact orbit compression]

\[
\begin{aligned}
|V|&=q+k(2p-q),& |E|&=kp^2,\\
\kappa&=q,&\delta&=p,\\
\operatorname{tw}&\ge p,&
\tau_{\mathrm{miss}}&=k(p-q),\\
Z(C_{q,k,p})&=5\left[
(2^p+2^{p-q+1}-2)^k+
(2^q-2)(2^{p-q})^k
\right].
\end{aligned}
\]
This is an exact polynomial-time counting family even if the

\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

\end{proof}

\section{Sharp terminal connection rank and an easy full-rank interface family}

$(x_{t_0},\ldots,x_{t_{q-1}})=(0,\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{q-1})$.

\[
Z(H\cup_T K)=5\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb Z_5^{q-1}}
f_H(\alpha)f_K(\alpha).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Sharp $q$-terminal connection rank]
The rational connection matrix indexed by all $q$-boundaried
original $T_5$ sources, with entry
$M_q(H,K)=Z(H\cup_T K)$, has rank exactly $5^{q-1}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The displayed gluing factorization proves the upper bound.

with original arcs $u\to v$, $u\to w$ and $w\to v$.

\[
R(a,b)\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z_5}R(a,z)R(z,b)
=\mathbf1[b-a=1\pmod5].
\]
Indeed, when $b=a+1$, the only possible internal colour

$d=0$. Let $B_\alpha$ consist of one private such path from
$t_0$ to $t_i$ for each $i>0$, enforcing difference $\alpha_i$.
Then $f_{B_\alpha}(\beta)=\mathbf1[\alpha=\beta]$.
Hence the explicit $5^{q-1}$-by-$5^{q-1}$ submatrix
$(M_q(B_\alpha,B_\beta))_{\alpha,\beta}$ equals
$5I_{5^{q-1}}$, proving the lower bound.
\end{proof}

lower bound: any rational linear separator encoding followed by

\begin{theorem}[Exact degree-two branch-core witness construction]
Let $b>0$ be the number of vertices of undirected degree at least
three in the edge-bearing connected critical digraph $H$.

a T5 witness for $H-e$ with at most $5^{b-1}$ branch-color candidates.

\[

unique internal path; an internal edge traversal in the forward

adds $2$ or $4$. Thus for a length-$L$ thread with $f$ forward arcs,
the possible endpoint differences are exactly
\[
D_{L,f}=\{2L-f+2j\bmod5:0\le j\le L\}.

For $L\ge4$ the endpoint relation is universal. Since such a
four-edge subpath cannot be essential to edge-minimal UNSAT,
every critical thread has length at most three. Preserve its
exact $D_{L,f}$ relation on its branching endpoints, except for
the thread containing $e$: deletion of $e$ splits it into two
free-ended paths, each extendible independently from its branch
endpoint, so its entire endpoint relation is removed.

thread relation, and reconstruct all thread interiors using
five-state path dynamic programming or one-ended propagation
on the severed thread. Verify all original arcs other than $e$
before output. Input scans, branch discovery, every rejected
candidate, addresses, storage, reconstruction, and output are
included in the conservative simultaneous resource bounds.

they claim a lower bound. The degree-two-only exceptional case
uses the ordinary cycle/path solver.
\end{proof}

\section{TMR-3/095: Minimum-product branch-tree witnesses and a physical criticality barrier}
\textbf{Scope.} We study the promised search task $(H,e)$, where $H$ is a
connected edge-minimal T5-unsatisfiable oriented source and $e$ is a
designated arc. The output is a complete T5 coloring of $H-e$.
No promise recognizer, witness advice, or UNSAT refutation is supplied.

\begin{theorem}[Binary spanning-tree witness enumeration]
Every connected oriented T5 source with $n\geq1$ vertices has an exact
witness-finding algorithm enumerating at most $2^{n-1}$ rooted
assignments. Every satisfying coloring occurs once modulo the fivefold
global color translation.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose an undirected spanning tree rooted at $r$ and normalize
$x_r=0$. Every tree edge, traversed from parent to child, permits
exactly two offsets, $(1,3)$ in the direction of its source arc and
$(-1,-3)$ otherwise. Enumerate these binary choices and test every
source arc. Each satisfying rooted coloring uniquely determines the
tree-edge choices, while each choice determines a complete vertex
coloring. Non-tree arcs are checked without any inference from the
number of candidate branches to necessary work.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Minimum-product branch-core exact search]
Let $n'$ be the number of edge-bearing vertices of $H$, let $b$ be the
number of vertices of undirected degree at least three, and suppress
the degree-two threads of $H$. For each pair $u<v$ of branching
vertices, intersect the exact endpoint supports of all intact
threads connecting that pair into $S_{uv}\subseteq\mathbb Z_5$.
Omit the support of the thread containing $e$. Let $T^*$ be a
minimum-product spanning tree of the resulting branch graph and set
$W=\prod_{uv\in T^*}|S_{uv}|$. Then a complete source-valid witness
of $H-e$ can be found after at most
\[
Q(H,e)\leq \min\{2^{n'-1},W\}\leq
\min\{2^{n'-1},4^{b-1}\}
\]
complete rooted tree assignments. Each assigned core coloring is
extended by exact five-state path dynamic programming.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The supplied and independently checked short-path lemma implies
every intact thread of an intrinsically minimal UNSAT source has
length at most three, and therefore at most four endpoint differences.
The source is bridgeless, so deleting one thread support cannot
disconnect the remaining branch graph. Every solution satisfies the
intersected pair supports and therefore one of the at most $W$
root-normalized assignments induced by $T^*$. Conversely, checking
the remaining pair constraints and reconstructing each internally
disjoint thread by five-state dynamic programming yields a complete
valid source assignment. The severed thread has two independently
extendible anchored pieces. Exhaustive search over an original
spanning tree supplies the alternative $2^{n'-1}$ bound.
Sorting positive edge weights $|S_{uv}|$ for Kruskal minimizes their
product because the logarithm is strictly increasing.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Rigid-pin modular cycles are not physically arc-minimal]
For $k\geq3$ let $H_k$ have terminals $v_i$ on a cyclic sequence and
private vertices $w_i$, with each module having arcs
$v_i\to v_{i+1}$, $v_i\to w_i$, and $w_i\to v_{i+1}$.
Then $H_k$ is T5-UNSAT if and only if $k\not\equiv0\pmod5$, but in every
UNSAT case $H_k$ is not edge-minimal UNSAT.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each three-arc module forces endpoint difference $1$ modulo five.
Their cyclic sum is $k$, so the complete source is UNSAT exactly when
$k\not\equiv0$. Deleting a direct terminal arc relaxes that module
to endpoint support $\{1,2,4\}$, while deleting either private-side
arc relaxes it to $\{1,3\}$. When all other modules remain intact, the
altered module must realize difference $1-k\not\equiv1$. Because the
two deletion supports intersect only at $1$, there is always at least
one physical arc whose removal still leaves the full source UNSAT.
The contradiction is minimal only at the compressed-module level,
not the original physical-arc level.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Same-realization status.}
With explicit source bit length $N$, a deliberately conservative
direct deterministic random-access realization uses
$O((N+1)^4(1+Q))$ time and $O((N+1)^2)$ simultaneous peak work bits.
A repeated-scan deterministic sequential-tape realization admits the
conservative upper bounds $O((N+1)^8(1+Q))$ time and $O((N+1)^3)$
peak work bits. Full witness output alone needs $\Omega(n')$ steps.
No larger lower bound follows. These are constructive upper bounds,

\section{TMR-3/096: Bipartite high-girth intrinsic-critical T5 obstructions}
\noindent\textbf{Disposition: PROVED (structural existence and girth extremal order);
COMPUTATIONAL (finite complete witness and independent MILP audit);
OPEN (all-algorithm intrinsic-critical witness lower bounds).}
For every integer $g\ge7$, there is a connected oriented graph $H$ whose
underlying graph is bipartite with girth greater than $g$, such that $H$ is
T5-UNSAT while $H-e$ is T5-SAT for \emph{every physical arc} $e$.
Furthermore $|V(H)|=2^{O(g)}$ is achievable.

\begin{proof}
Take a five-regular graph of girth greater than $g$ with $2^{O(g)}$ vertices
from regular-cage existence and upper bounds (Sachs 1963; Lazebnik,
Ustimenko and Woldar 1997). A connected component $B$ of its bipartite
double cover is five-regular, bipartite and has girth greater than $g$.
Let $n=|V(B)|$, $m=5n/2$, and orient each edge independently uniformly.
For a proper five-coloring of the underlying graph, the tournament
condition permits exactly one physical orientation for each edge.
Improper colorings permit none. Consequently
\[
\mathbb E_{\vec B}\, Z_{T5}(\vec B)
=\frac{P_B(5)}{2^m}
\le \left(\frac5{2^{5/2}}\right)^n<1.
\]
Some orientation is UNSAT. Deleting a maximal collection of redundant
\emph{original physical arcs} leaves an arc-minimal UNSAT source.
Deleting isolates produces a connected source, since multiple nontrivial
components would contradict minimality. Bipartiteness and girth survive
edge deletion. This existence proof does not give polynomial
critical-instance generation: the pruning step charges exact SAT calls.
\end{proof}
Let $f_{\mathrm{bip}}(g)$ be the smallest nonisolated vertex count of such
critical sources. Each degree-two thread of length at least four has
unrestricted endpoint support in $\mathbb Z_5$ and so cannot occur in
a physical-edge-minimal UNSAT graph. The suppressed minimum-degree-three
core is simple for $g\ge7$; each core edge expands to at most three
physical edges. Put $r=\lfloor(g-3)/6\rfloor$. Its radius-$r$ BFS ball is
a tree with at least $3\cdot2^r-2$ branching vertices. Hence
\[
3\cdot2^{\lfloor(g-3)/6\rfloor}-2
\le f_{\mathrm{bip}}(g)\le 2^{O(g)},
\qquad f_{\mathrm{bip}}(g)=2^{\Theta(g)}.
\]
This is a size-versus-girth theorem, \emph{not} a lower bound on SAT
witness time in terms of source input length.

\paragraph{Computational witness control.}
The $80$-vertex, degree-four, girth-eight incidence graph of the
symplectic generalized quadrangle $W(3,3)$ was acyclically oriented
and minimized by $160$ charged exact SAT calls. The resulting
$bipartite$, \emph{acyclic}, biconnected and physically arc-critical
graph has $n=31$, $m=42$, girth $8$, $19$ branching vertices, and
cycle rank $12$. All $42$ designated physical arc deletions admitted
complete independently verified T5 witnesses. The five-bit-domain
backtracker used $8$--$36$ recursion nodes per deletion (total $707$).
A separate Boolean $0$--$1$ ILP implementation using SciPy/HiGHS
confirmed source UNSAT and all $42$ deletion witnesses SAT. Its
success is a finite easy-family control, not a general worst-case bound.
For code, exact edges and audit see
\path{research/agents/tmr-3/2026-11-18_TMR3_T5_BIPARTITE_HIGH_GIRTH096.md}
and \path{experiments/tmr/tmr3_t5_bipartite_critical096_smoke.py}.
The conservative same-realization binary spanning-tree upper bound of
the previous section still applies; no FLMV SAT-recognition lower bound
has been transported to this SAT-only promise witness search.
All four verification gates remain non-separating.
