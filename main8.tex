\begin{abstract}
This run tried to build the last source-checkable link between the existing
WRAPPER machinery and the Williams time-space endpoint.  It recovered the
earlier removal of the unknown solver exponent, corrected the global cost of
SourceLocalization, proved a more general max-affine acceptance theorem,
implemented a combined exact partial-wrapper portfolio, and tested that
portfolio against hostile controls.  The build is semantically exact on every
completed plan, but it does not have favorable construction work and live
space on every CNF.  The decisive open item is no longer recursion,
combination, hashing, or selector scheduling.  It is an all-input,
same-realization successor whose discovery, access, replay, and workspace
already satisfy the endpoint ledger.  No such theorem was recovered from the
project corpus or the primary literature.  Therefore P versus NP remains
\Open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Executive result and claim card}

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.91\textwidth}
\textbf{Current result.}  The project has the exact downstream recurrence,
finite-depth elimination of the unknown exponent $K$, exact AND/OR and
zero-child composition, fixed finite portfolio scheduling, several sound
merge rules, source replay ledgers, and a model-matched lower-bound endpoint.
The present build adds a disjoint clause-first cover and an executable hybrid.
It does not prove an all-CNF favorable successor.  That single load-bearing
arrow remains \Open.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

The formal target is the following.  Let $A$ be one fixed uniform exact SAT
machine, supplied under the contradiction hypothesis $P=NP$.  Construct from
the finite code of $A$ another uniform exact SAT machine with a strictly
better charged joint exponent pair.  Every construction, source read, child
access, failed portfolio member, replay, retained map, and live buffer must
belong to the same execution.  The transformer may depend on $A$ but may not
first run $A$ on the parent at its unknown exponent or materialize an
$M^{K+o(1)}$ computation trace.

The strongest convenient explicit target remains
\begin{equation*}
 q\le M^{1/3+o(1)},\qquad
 |G_i|\le M^{2/3+o(1)},\qquad
 T_{\rm all}\le M^{1+o(1)},
 \tag{CS}
\end{equation*}
with an exact streamed combiner and compatible live space.  This cell is
sufficient, not necessary.  Section~\ref{sec:maxaffine} gives the weaker and
more useful acceptance region.

The quantifier distinction is essential.  A source-only rule satisfying
(CS) on every CNF would recursively give a near-linear SAT algorithm without
assuming $P=NP$.  For the intended contradiction route, at least one
worst-case portfolio member may instead be derived non-black-boxly from the
code of $A$.  That dependence does not make acquisition free.

\section{The weakest reusable acceptance theorem}
\label{sec:maxaffine}

Let $(t,s)\in\R$ mean that one uniform exact machine simultaneously solves
SAT in time $N^{t+o(1)}$ and space $N^{s+o(1)}$.  Set
\[
 \gamma=2\cos(\pi/7)=1.8019377358\ldots.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Charged max-affine successor]
Suppose a fixed finite compiler maps every achievable $(t,s)$ to an
achievable $(t',s')$ satisfying
\begin{align*}
 t'&\le \max\{r,\max_{j\in J}(w_j+\lambda_jt)\},\\
 s'&\le \max\{p,\max_{j\in J}\lambda_js\},
\end{align*}
where $J$ is finite and $0\le\lambda_j<1$.  Put
\[
 C_*:=\max\left\{r,\max_j\frac{w_j}{1-\lambda_j}\right\}.
\]
If $C_*+p<\gamma$, then every finite starting pair reaches
$t+s<\gamma$ after finitely many compiler returns.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every channel, $w_j+\lambda_jC_*\le C_*$ and $r\le C_*$.  Hence $C_*$
is a common pre-fixed point.  With
$\lambda_{\max}=\max_j\lambda_j$, monotonicity gives
\[
 t_m\le C_*+\lambda_{\max}^m(t_0-C_*)_+,
 \qquad
 s_m\le p+\lambda_{\max}^m(s_0-p)_+.
\]
The strict margin $\gamma-(C_*+p)>0$ absorbs both transient terms after a
finite number of returns.  Finite inlining yields one uniform machine, so the
Williams endpoint applies.
\end{proof}

For one recursive channel with fanout exponent $a$, child scale $b<1$,
per-child interface exponent $\beta$, access or regeneration exponent $g$,
nonrecursive time floor $r$, and live-space floor $p$, the criterion becomes
\begin{equation*}
 \boxed{
 \max\left\{r,\frac{a+\beta+g}{1-b}\right\}+p<\gamma.}
 \tag{A}
\end{equation*}
For ordinary explicit children, direct materialization requires
$r\ge a+b$ and retaining one child normally requires $p\ge b$.  With
$(a,b,r,p)=(1/3,2/3,1,2/3)$ and $\beta=g=0$, the landing pair is
$(C_*,p)=(1,2/3)$ and $5/3<\gamma$.  Other boundary cells work too; for
example $(a,b)=(1/2,1/2)$ has sum $3/2$ under the same ideal linear ledger.

This is the strongest conceptual narrowing from the run.  The final link
need not hit exactly the $1/3,2/3$ cell.  It may use several channels, a
finite portfolio, or a typed contracting cycle, provided its least charged
budget lies strictly under the endpoint.

\section{Correction to the recovered SourceLocalization route}

The older half-scale entry computed the cost of filling one already localized
child table:
\[
 e_{\rm one}=\beta+\lambda k.
\]
The recovered PRF3-0079 entry explicitly corrects the whole-algorithm cost.
If exact coverage needs $M^{a+o(1)}$ children, the cost is
\begin{equation*}
 e_{\rm global}=a+\beta+\lambda k.
 \tag{B}
\end{equation*}
For the all-block schedule $a=1-\lambda$.  At $k=2$ and target $C=4/3$,
\[
 1-\lambda+\beta+2\lambda<\frac43
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \boxed{\lambda+\beta<\frac13.}
\]
Thus $\lambda=1/2$ fails for every $\beta\ge0$ in this schedule.  Near
$\beta=1/6$, one instead needs $\lambda<1/6$; even as $\beta\to0$, one needs
$\lambda<1/3$.

The same route tested directly against Williams has recurrence
\[
 t'\le\max\{r,1-\lambda+\beta+g+\lambda t\},
 \qquad s'\le\max\{p,\lambda s\}.
\]
At $\lambda=p=1/2$, ordinary explicit children require
\begin{equation*}
 \boxed{\beta+g<\frac{\gamma-3/2}{2}
 =0.1509688679\ldots.}
 \tag{C}
\end{equation*}
Consequently the old bound $\beta<1/6$ is not sufficient for this endpoint.
The conditional point $\beta=1/7$, $g=0$ gives
$C_*+p=25/14=1.785714\ldots<\gamma$.

A shared object $H$ can remove the repeated table term only if it actually
performs the semantic batch work.  It must support exact counterfactual
recovery, aggregate child access, recursive compose/project, and compatible
workspace without a parent reread.  A single stored source plus short handles
does not prove these properties.

\section{New exact partial construction}

\begin{lemma}[CLAUSE-FIRST-COVER1]
Let $F$ contain a normalized non-tautological clause
$C=(\ell_1\vee\cdots\vee\ell_w)$ with distinct literals.  Define
\[
 \tau_i=\{\ell_1=0,\ldots,\ell_{i-1}=0,\ell_i=1\}
 \qquad(1\le i\le w).
\]
Then
\[
 \SAT(F)=\bigvee_{i=1}^{w}\SAT(F\upharpoonright\tau_i),
\]
the terms are disjoint on complete assignments, and every residual witness
lifts to a witness of $F$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Every model of $F$ satisfies $C$ and therefore has a unique first true
literal in the displayed order.  It extends exactly one $\tau_i$.  Conversely,
a model of a residual combined with its defining partial assignment satisfies
the original formula.
\end{proof}

This improves a width-$w$ Shannon cover from $2^w$ assignments to $w$
disjoint routes and needs no SAT answer to choose a route.  It is not a size
theorem: a residual may still have $\Theta(M)$ encoded length, and repeated
use may build a large tree before small leaves appear.

The executable hybrid combines this rule with unit propagation, tautology and
subsumption deletion, component AND, Horn and 2-CNF VALUE procedures,
bounded-frontier dynamic programming, fixed strong backdoors, backdoor DNFs,
exact residual equality, clause dominance, and source replay.  Every completed
CONST, VALUE, LEAF, AND, OR, and LIFT plan was independently replayed.  Every
reported SAT plan also supplied a source-valid lifted witness.

\section{Exact restricted gates}

Two symbolic controls retire overly strong versions of the target without
claiming a general SAT lower bound.

\begin{proposition}[Solution-set restriction gate]
For
$F_m=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}(x_i\vee y_i\vee z_i)$ on disjoint triples, any
restriction cover preserving the complete solution set and leaving at most
$r$ clauses nonempty needs
\[
 q\ge(7/4)^{m-r}.
\]
\end{proposition}

One residual covers at most $4^{m-r}7^r$ of the $7^m$ satisfying assignments:
every eliminated block must already have a true literal.  The claim rules out
a small universal witness-cover by restriction cubes on this family.  It does
not rule out a decision-only VALUE procedure, which easily answers this
monotone formula.

\begin{proposition}[2CNF-backdoor-DNF gate]
Take a width-three clause on every variable triple of an $n$-variable source.
Every term whose restriction leaves a 2CNF must assign at least $n-2$
variables.  A tautological 2CNF-backdoor DNF therefore has at least
$2^{n-2}$ terms.
\end{proposition}

The complement of a term's assigned variables may contain no triple, so it
has size at most two.  Each term covers at most four complete assignments,
and tautological coverage needs all $2^n$.  This is compatible with the
positive backdoor-DNF literature, which gives fixed-parameter detection for
specified base classes but not a favorable cover for every CNF
\cite{ordyniak2021bdnf,dreier2022}.

\section{Literature frontier}

The primary literature provides useful boundaries and structured positive
cases, not the missing all-input compiler.

\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{Dense multi-query boundary.}
Dell and van Melkebeek rule out, under their stated noncollapse assumption, a
polynomial-time reduction from $n$-variable $d$-SAT using $O(n^b)$ queries of
$O(n^c)$ bits when $b+c<d$ \cite{dellvanmelkebeek2014}.  For dense 3-CNF,
$M=n^{3+o(1)}$, and (CS) becomes $q=n^{1+o(1)}$ with
$n^{2+o(1)}$-bit children.  It lies exactly at $b+c=3$.  The theorem neither
excludes nor constructs the equality point.

\item[] \textbf{Sparsification.}
The IPZ lemma yields $2^{\varepsilon n}$ sparse fixed-width CNFs
\cite{ipz2001}.  Quantitative bounds deteriorate when $\varepsilon$ is made
small enough for polynomial fanout \cite{calabro2010}.  It does not
interpolate to the required polynomial-fanout power shrink.

\item[] \textbf{Backdoors and knowledge compilation.}
Backdoor DNFs are the closest existing syntax for heterogeneous exact forward
covers.  Bounded-width and compiled representations can make merging and
queries cheap after a favorable structure is supplied.  No cited theorem
guarantees that structure, its power-size residuals, and near-linear
discovery on all CNFs \cite{ordyniak2021bdnf,darwiche2002}.

\item[] \textbf{Compression barriers.}
Fortnow-Santhanam and Drucker show strong consequences of generic instance
compression \cite{fortnowsanthanam2011,drucker2015}.  The target here has
aggregate output $M^{1/3}M^{2/3}=M$, so these results do not directly exclude
it.  Their usual collapse consequences are also already compatible with the
contradiction hypothesis $P=NP$.

\item[] \textbf{Hashing and isolation.}
Valiant-Vazirani hashing can isolate a witness with randomized probability,
but the constrained instance still contains essentially the full source
formula \cite{valiantvazirani1986}.  Increasing or decreasing the hash output
length changes buckets and collisions, not the encoded source scale.

\item[] \textbf{Self-improvement.}
Williams gives a genuine self-improvement theorem for circuit-analysis
problems, while explicitly identifying the polynomial regime as a remaining
question \cite{williams2024}.  The architecture supports finite
algorithm-to-algorithm bootstrapping; it does not provide the ordinary-CNF
cover or virtual-source decoder required here.
\end{description}

Sparse CNFs are a particularly important warning.  If $M=n^{1+o(1)}$, a
child of $M^{2/3+o(1)}$ bits cannot even name all $n$ source variables in an
ordinary explicit encoding.  A general theorem must perform real variable
elimination, factor the source, or prove a semantic quotient.  Clause
sparsification and short pointers alone do not meet that requirement.

\section{Alternative routes that remain logically viable}

\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{Route I: ordinary explicit cover.}
Construct an exact all-input family satisfying (A), stream its children, and
recurse on ordinary SAT inputs.  The $1/3,2/3$ cell is one favorable point.

\item[] \textbf{Route II: shared BatchAcquire.}
Build one object $H(A,F)$ whose acquisition, exact counterfactual recovery,
aggregate access, recursive projection, and retained state are all charged.
For an all-block boundary cover, $H$ must remove genuine repeated semantic
work, not only duplicate storage.

\item[] \textbf{Route III: virtual children.}
Return a short handle plus a proved bit-level random-access decoder for the
logical child.  Charge every decoder invocation and all retained parent state,
and prove a model-preserving simulation for the endpoint.  Otherwise the
construction has changed ordinary SAT into a succinct-oracle problem.

\item[] \textbf{Route IV: typed double wrapper.}
Use a finite graph of interface types.  A same-scale outer edge is permitted
only when every reachable return cycle has scale product below one and the
least Bellman time-space budgets land below $\gamma$.

\item[] \textbf{Route V: $A$-dependent certified portfolio.}
Combine zero-child, component, backdoor, bounded-width, implication, and
disjunctive members.  Every reported SUCCESS is exact, every failure is
charged, and at least one member must meet a common favorable bound on every
CNF.  At least one worst-case member may use the finite code of the assumed
machine $A$, but cannot pay $A$'s parent-scale running time.
\end{description}

These routes can coexist.  The max-affine theorem evaluates their joint
channels without requiring one universal $1/3,2/3$ rule.

\section{The exact remaining theorem}

The strongest current statement is therefore:
\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.91\textwidth}
\textbf{FINAL-LINK1, OPEN.}  From the finite code of any assumed uniform
polynomial-time SAT machine $A$, construct a fixed finite exact wrapper
system which, on every ordinary CNF, returns a certified VALUE or a
well-founded recursively closed Boolean plan.  Charge discovery, all failed
members, fanout, generation, child access, replay, source rereads, transport,
combination, and live state on one realization.  Prove that its max-affine or
typed-cycle budget satisfies $C_*+S_*<2\cos(\pi/7)$, with no parent-scale call
to $A$, no $M^K$ trace, and no undeclared succinct-SAT oracle.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

The source-only special case (CS) is a clean sufficient lemma and a valuable
experimental target.  It is also essentially a near-linear SAT algorithm:
\[
 T(M)\le M^{1/3+o(1)}T(M^{2/3+o(1)})+M^{1+o(1)}
      =M^{1+o(1)}.
\]
This explains why no routine merge rule in the literature supplies it.

The next experiment should hard-cap every portfolio attempt by the proposed
time and space envelopes and return FAIL once a cap is reached.  Search for
the smallest CNF on which all current members fail.  In parallel, the next
theorem attempt should target a shared $H(A,F)$ with explicit
Acquire--Recover--Aggregate--Covary--Close contracts.  Additional heuristic
wrappers are useful only when they supply a new proved promise domain or a
strictly better charged channel.

\section{Conclusion}

The final link was not built in the theorem-strength sense.  The run did
build the strongest honest partial implementation, repair two exponent
ledgers, identify a broader feasible region, and isolate the exact remaining
quantifier.  The prior work was not wasted: it solved most of the semantic and
accounting scaffolding around the link.  What it did not solve is favorable
all-input acquisition on one realization.  Until FINAL-LINK1 is proved, the
proof spine is conditional and P versus NP remains open.
