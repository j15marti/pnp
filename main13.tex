\begin{abstract}
ARC seeks a uniform transformation of an arbitrary correct polynomial-time SAT
decider into another decider with better simultaneous time and space.
A finite iteration of the proposed exponent recurrence would enter a known
forbidden time--space region. The arithmetic is not the missing theorem:
the all-input, hereditary, semantically correct and fully charged contractor
has not been constructed. Exact boundary-assignment joins work on supplied
low-width scoped inputs; the latest hereditary claim remains confined to a
family already easy by dynamic programming. This summary separates elementary
derivations, published endpoints, source-reported restricted constructions
and open implications. Neither $P\ne NP$ nor an unrestricted SAT lower bound
is proved here.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Quantifiers.}
Under $P=NP$ there would be some fixed SAT decider $A_0$ and finite exponents
$t_0,s_0$ such that, for every encoded formula $F$ of length $N$,
\[
 A_0(F)=\SAT(F),\qquad
 (T_{A_0}(N),S_{A_0}(N))
 \le (N^{t_0+o(1)},N^{s_0+o(1)}).
\]
Both bounds must describe the \emph{same} machine.
ARC needs a uniform source-level transformation $A\mapsto\Phi(A)$,
applicable to arbitrary such $A$, correct on every input and with a
reusable resource guarantee. Code constants may depend on the fixed $A$,
and a fixed iteration depth may depend on its fixed exponents.
An existential contradiction need not compute the optimal exponent,
but cannot assume a free common exponent bound.

\section{The conditional chain and its endpoint}
The chain is
\[
\begin{gathered}
P=NP\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{a fixed polynomial SAT decider }A_0,\\
\underbrace{\text{all-input hereditary charged contractor}}_{\OPEN}
\ \Longrightarrow\ A_r\text{ with forbidden simultaneous resources}\\
\Longrightarrow\ \text{FLMV contradiction}.
\end{gathered}
\]
A compact witness, separate child satisfiability statuses, or a numerical
recurrence cannot replace the missing contractor.

\paragraph{Published endpoint (\KNOWN).}
FLMV uses deterministic random-access Turing machines, counted work/index
tapes, a read-only indexed input and a one-way write-only output.
Corollary 5.8 states, for constant $c>1$,
\[
 \NTIME[n]\not\subseteq\DTISP[n^c,o(n^{d(c)})],
 \qquad d(c)=\frac12\left(\frac{c+2}{c^2}-c\right).
\]
Theorem 3.1 transfers SAT algorithms to nondeterministic linear time with
polylogarithmic time/length overhead and logarithmic extra space; Corollary
3.2 permits strict exponent margins \cite{flmv}. A free output tape is
not a reusable child-input buffer.

\begin{proposition}[Elementary numerical margin]\label{prop:margin}
At $c_\star=241/200$,
\[
 d(c_\star)=\frac{11642479}{23232400}
 =\frac12+\frac{26279}{23232400}>\frac12.
\]
An all-input SAT algorithm in the matched model with time
$N^{a+o(1)}$, $a<c_\star$, and space $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ would contradict
the cited endpoint after its SAT transfer.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $241/200$ into $d$ and subtract $1/2$. Strict positive
gaps in time and space absorb the polylogarithmic transfer factors.
The argument uses one simultaneous algorithm.
\end{proof}
The convenient center $(6/5,1/2)$ also has
$d(6/5)=23/45>1/2$. The larger $c_\star$ leaves time slack;
a limiting exponent is not silently treated as a finite-step bound.

\section{The same-realization resource ledger}
An actual contractor must charge discovery and validation, failed candidates,
ordinary child bits and headers, variable renaming, auxiliaries, source
accesses, recomputation, solver steps, query answers, caches, indexes,
generator workspace, child buffers and live ancestors.
It must preserve both SAT and UNSAT, and every recursive child must be
eligible. A fallback that runs $A$ outside a promise may restore correctness
while losing the claimed worst-case resource improvement.

\begin{proposition}[Eager versus fully lazy simulation]\label{prop:ledger}
Suppose a \emph{correct} parent procedure makes at most $N^{a+o(1)}$
sequential calls on ordinary children of length at most
$N^{\lambda+o(1)}$. The child solver has joint exponents $(t,s)$.
Other parent work costs $N^{r+o(1)}$. A requested child bit can be
generated in time $N^{g+o(1)}$ and space $N^{\eta+o(1)}$.
Retained parent/ancestor state uses $N^{\kappa+o(1)}$ space.
Eager generation of one child at a time has ledger
\[
 t'\le\max\{r,a+\lambda+g,a+\lambda t\},\quad
 s'\le\max\{\lambda,\lambda s,\eta,\kappa\}.
\]
Fully lazy regeneration on every solver probe instead has ledger
\[
 t'\le\max\{r,a+\lambda t+g\},\quad
 s'\le\max\{\lambda s,\eta,\kappa\}.
\]
These are conditional simulation bounds, not constructions of the semantic
procedure in their hypothesis.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Eager generation emits at most $N^{a+\lambda+o(1)}$ bits, paying the
per-bit cost once for each emitted bit. The solver calls take
$N^{a+\lambda t+o(1)}$ time altogether. One child is live alongside
the declared generator, solver and retained state. In lazy mode,
upper-bound input probes by solver steps and pay on every probe.
A fixed sum of polynomial terms has the maximum exponent.
\end{proof}
This is the useful arithmetic of \texttt{EAGER-LAZY-FRONTIER34}
\cite{eager}, with its semantic premise explicit.
The lazy expression is a worst-case simulation upper bound, not a lower
bound against all caching or semantic algorithms.

At $(a,\lambda,t,s)=(3/5,1/2,6/5,1/2)$ the child-solver time exponent
is already $6/5$. Eager generation contributes $11/10+g$, so permits
$g\le1/10$ at this center. Lazy access adds $g$ to $6/5$ and has
no corresponding slack. This compares implementations; it does not prove
SAT hardness.

\section{Finite contraction: arithmetic versus construction}
\begin{proposition}[Conditional finite-entry calculation]
Assume a correct all-input hereditary transformation realizes, at each
fixed iteration,
\[
 t_{j+1}\le\max\{b,3/5+t_j/2\},\qquad
 s_{j+1}\le\max\{1/2,s_j/2\},\qquad b<c_\star.
\]
Every finite initial pair reaches, after a fixed finite number of steps,
$t_r<c_\star$ and $s_r\le1/2$ at exponent scale.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Put $q=\max\{b,6/5\}<c_\star$. The time update is monotone,
$1/2$-Lipschitz, and fixes $q$, so
$t_j\le q+2^{-j}(t_0-q)_+$.
Also $s_j\le\max\{1/2,2^{-j}s_0\}$. Choose finite $r$ to make the
time excess smaller than $c_\star-q$ and the space bound at most $1/2$.
For finitely many compositions, subpolynomial overheads remain
subpolynomial. The endpoint retains strict margins.
\end{proof}
For eager construction with $a=3/5,\lambda=1/2$, one may take
$b=\max\{r,11/10+g\}$, so sufficient conditions are
\[
 r<c_\star,\quad g<21/200,\quad \eta,\kappa\le1/2.
\]
Fully lazy access instead has fixed point $6/5+2g$ when that term dominates,
giving the stronger sufficient condition $g<1/400$.
Neither is a necessary condition for all algorithms.
An actual amortized generator of total time $N^{\gamma+o(1)}$ may replace
the bitwise generation term by $\gamma$, but must include all reads,
retries and metadata.

\section{Actual restricted semantic progress}
\subsection{Exact boundary join}
\texttt{INTERFACE-PROJECTION8} \cite{interface} uses a supplied valid
event stream with \texttt{OPEN}, \texttt{CLAUSE} and \texttt{CLOSE}.
Each variable has one contiguous live interval; clauses mention live
variables only. Let $S_j$ be the variables live at block cut $j$, and set
\[
 R_j(u,v)=
 [u|_{S_{j-1}\cap S_j}=v|_{S_{j-1}\cap S_j}]
 \land\SAT(\text{block }j\text{ pinned to }u,v).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact join, with an elementary proof]
For a valid stream with empty exterior boundaries,
\[
 \SAT(F)\ \Longleftrightarrow\
 \exists u_0,\ldots,u_q\ \bigwedge_{j=1}^{q}R_j(u_{j-1},u_j).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A global satisfying assignment restricts to compatible boundaries and
local witnesses. Conversely, choose a witness for each true relation.
A variable appearing in separated blocks remains live at every intervening
cut. Adjacent boundary agreement makes all its local values agree.
Each clause is satisfied in its block, so the glued assignment satisfies $F$.
\end{proof}
The common consistent boundary assignment is essential: unrelated witnesses
for individually satisfiable pieces do not suffice.

\subsection{Source-reported resource and heredity claims}
The \texttt{INTERFACE-PROJECTION8} status report claims ordinary pinned
proper-3CNF children of length $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ and at most
$N^{1/2+o(1)}2^{2w}$ calls. For $w\le(\log_2N)/20$, this is
$N^{3/5+o(1)}$, yielding the restricted ledger
\[
 t'\le\max\{1,3/5+t/2\},\qquad
 s'\le\max\{1/2,s/2\}.
\]
Its full reported finite audit was not rerun for this summary.

The later fallback \texttt{HEREDITARY-SCOPED10} entry claims a recursively
iterable version for $w(N)=o(\log N)$, with temporary auxiliaries adding
at most two live variables per level. For fixed depth $r$, $w+2r$
remains sublogarithmic relative to the intended square-root descendant
scales. It reports the improved restricted recurrence
\[
 t'\le\max\{1,1/2+t/2\},\qquad
 s'\le\max\{1/2,s/2\},
\]
and 6,143 generated child streams with inherited-width checks and no
reported failures \cite{fallback}. These are \emph{source-reported
implementation and accounting claims}; an accessible complete formal
artifact was not available in that fallback entry.

\paragraph{Why this is not a separation.}
A valid low-width event stream already admits direct dynamic programming:
retain the satisfying assignment set on the live frontier; duplicate on
opening, filter on clauses, and existentially project on closing.
The time is $N2^w\operatorname{polylog}N$ and space is
$2^w\operatorname{polylog}N$. For $w=o(\log N)$ these are
$N^{1+o(1)}$ and $N^{o(1)}$. A contractor restricted to this easy
representation supplies no unrestricted SAT lower bound.

The older fixed promise $w\le(\log N)/20$ is not automatically hereditary:
if width persists when length becomes $N^{1/2}$, the permitted width
halves. The finite-depth margin report requires a smaller root width
and a separate bound on auxiliary-variable growth \cite{width}.
The later sublogarithmic promise addresses finite-depth eligibility,
not arbitrary-input normalization.

\section{Counterexamples the chain must retain}
\paragraph{Separate SAT statuses do not glue (\RET).}
Put each proper three-literal clause in $H$ if it has at most one positive
literal and in $D$ otherwise. All-zero satisfies $H$; all-one satisfies $D$.
Both parts of every proper 3CNF are therefore satisfiable, but their
conjunction need not be: use all eight sign patterns on three variables.
Every assignment falsifies its complementary clause.
This elementary counterexample explains why a shared boundary relation is
substantive \cite{fallback}.

\paragraph{Branching need not shorten ordinary children (\RET).}
For selector $x_i$ and fresh private variables, append to a disjoint core $H$
\[
 (x_i\vee a_{ij}\vee b_{ij})\land
 (\neg x_i\vee c_{ij}\vee d_{ij}).
\]
Restricting $x_i$ deletes one clause and leaves a two-clause.
Replacing $(u\vee v)$ by
$(u\vee v\vee z)\land(u\vee v\vee\neg z)$ restores two clauses.
With $bm$ gadgets, every canonical residual has $|H|+2bm$ clauses
and standard serialized length $\Theta(N)$, while remaining satisfiable
exactly when $H$ is \cite{restriction}. This refutes the specific
branch/restrict/properize shrinkage claim, not every semantic compressor.
A compiler may recognize and discard these independent gadgets.

\paragraph{A parent pointer is not free ordinary input.}
A short descriptor naming a parent and restriction can denote a long
residual. An arbitrary solver may reread it. Materialize and count the child,
or charge each accessor and its state as in Proposition \ref{prop:ledger}.
Treating write-only output as a readable tape changes the machine model.

\paragraph{Compact histories are not the contractor.}
A short program can run for a long time. One circuit correct for every
indexed tableau clause is stronger than separate local witnesses.
Affine histories may be fast-forwardable. Proof-certified solver races
cover their certified class, not every correct solver. These are
safeguards against shortcuts, not additional proof routes.

\section{The exact missing link}
\OPEN: one uniform transformation for ordinary SAT inputs and arbitrary
fixed correct base deciders must provide all of:
\begin{enumerate}
\item at most $N^{3/5+o(1)}$ sequential ordinary children, each of length
      at most $N^{1/2+o(1)}$;
\item exact global gluing for both SAT and UNSAT without first solving the source;
\item generation/discovery time below $N^{c_\star}$ with strict exponent slack;
\item same-machine peak auxiliary space $N^{1/2+o(1)}$, including readable
      children and live ancestors;
\item hereditary eligibility for the fixed number of iterations needed
      for arbitrary fixed initial exponents.
\end{enumerate}
No reviewed source constructs this object.
A cheap all-input normalizer into the supplied sublog-width class would
combine with direct dynamic programming to enter the forbidden endpoint;
it cannot be assumed as preprocessing.

A useful next acceptance test is a connected high-width nonaffine family,
including minimally unsatisfiable controls, with two explicit recursion
levels and a delayed correct base solver. Record ordinary child bytes,
reads, failed branches and peak live space. Finite success would remain
\textbf{COMPUTATIONAL}, not the all-input theorem.
