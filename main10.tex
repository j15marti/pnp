\begin{abstract}
This manuscript states the strongest defensible form of the WRAPPER program.
It corrects a material endpoint error: the published Williams theorem gives a
universal time--space product exponent equal to the golden ratio, not
$2\cos(\pi/7)$.  The earlier universal $2\cos(\pi/7)$ claim was retracted in
the published full version.  The concrete conditional landing point
$(t,s)=(1,2/3)$ nevertheless remains excluded, now by Fortnow's direct
near-linear-time, sublinear-space SAT lower bound.

Two composition theorems survive the correction.  A recursively closed
ordinary successor of encoded-size exponent $2/3$, constructed in
near-linear time and $M^{2/3+o(1)}$ space, can be applied a fixed number of
times and then handed to any fixed polynomial SAT solver.  A finite typed
WRAPPER whose charged least budgets satisfy $C+\rho<\phi$ similarly reaches
Williams's valid product region.  A compact closed generator is proved
equivalent, up to polylogarithmic factors, to an ordinary shrinker by a
quasilinear canonical-CNF verifier, removing the former condition
$2b\le h$.  None of the wrapper, hashing, parallel, disjunctive, backdoor, or
semantic-merge experiments constructs the required successor on every CNF
within the charged bounds.  Therefore P versus NP remains \Open{}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Status, scope, and quantifiers}

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.93\textwidth}
\textbf{Current conclusion.}  This project does not prove $P\ne NP$.  It
proves exact conditional composition theorems and identifies one all-input
compiler statement whose proof would complete the direct contradiction.
That compiler statement is \Open{}.  Every claim below is classified as
\Exact{}, \Comp{}, \Open{}, or \Retired{}.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

The contradiction hypothesis has the following order of quantifiers.  If
$P=NP$, select once and for all one total uniform deterministic SAT machine
$A$ and constants $K,L<\infty$ such that, on length $m$,
\begin{equation}
 T_A(m)\le m^{K+o(1)},\qquad S_A(m)\le m^{L+o(1)}.
 \label{eq:seed}
\end{equation}
The finite code of $A$ may be hardwired into a uniform constructor.  No
input-dependent or length-dependent table is thereby free.  The missing
assertion has the form
\[
 (P=NP)\Longrightarrow
 \exists A\,\exists W\,\exists M_0\,\forall F
 \quad \mathcal C(A,W,F),
\]
where $A,W,M_0$ are fixed before the input $F$ is seen.  This is weaker than
requiring one transformation to work relative to every possible SAT
implementation, but it still requires a worst-case all-input theorem.

\subsection{Exact input and machine model}

Let $M=L(F)$ be the actual bit length of a canonical self-delimiting CNF
encoding.  It includes headers, delimiters, literal signs, binary variable
identifiers, padding, embedded constants, certificates, circuit topology,
and every explicitly returned bit.  A legal identifier is bounded by the
declared variable count.  Malformed strings are rejected within the same
resource bounds.

All endpoint algorithms are one total uniform deterministic random-access
implementation with $O(\log M)$-bit words.  The source input is read-only.
Every other writable bit is workspace, including stacks, route tables,
parent state, hash tables, prime encodings, collision records, random seeds,
materialized child instances, and recursive queries.  A write-only stream
cannot later be reread for free.  If a child is represented virtually, the
decoder state and every access to it are charged.  Parallel branches are
serialized for time accounting, while simultaneously live states are
charged to space.  Randomization is usable at a deterministic endpoint only
after a uniform worst-case derandomization or a fixed-seed theorem has been
proved.  Representation changes require bit-local near-linear simulation or
an explicitly charged conversion.  Generic polynomial equivalence of
machine models is not enough at an exponent-sensitive endpoint
\cite[Section~2.1]{fortnowetal2005}.

\section{The corrected external endpoints}

Set
\[
 \phi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}=1.6180339887\ldots,
 \qquad
 \gamma_0=2\cos(\pi/7)=1.8019377358\ldots.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Williams product endpoint, \Exact{}]
For polynomially bounded simultaneous time and space in the stated
random-access setting, the published full version establishes, for every
fixed $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 T(M)S(M)=\Omega_{\mathrm{i.o.}}
       \bigl(M^{\phi-\varepsilon}\bigr)
\]
for SAT.  Consequently, no one uniform exact SAT algorithm has upper
exponents $t,s$ with $t+s<\phi$ \cite[Theorem~4.11]{williams2010}.
\end{theorem}

The STACS extended abstract had claimed the larger universal product
exponent $\gamma_0$.  The published full version explicitly retracts that
claim \cite[Section~4.5, footnote~9]{williams2010}.  The
$2\cos(\pi/7)$ constant is established in the subpolynomial-space regime;
it must not be used as a universal product half-plane
\cite{williams2008mod,busswilliams2015}.  Every earlier inference of the form
$t+s<1.8019\ldots\Rightarrow\bot$ at polynomial space is \Retired{}.

\begin{theorem}[Fortnow direct endpoint, \Exact{}]
For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, SAT has no uniform exact algorithm using
simultaneously $M^{1+o(1)}$ time and $M^{1-\varepsilon}$ space on the
model-matched random-access machine \cite{fortnow2000}.
\end{theorem}

Thus $(t,s)=(1,2/3)$ remains a valid forbidden point.  For example, take
$\varepsilon=1/4$; an $M^{2/3+o(1)}$ space upper bound is eventually at most
$M^{3/4}$.  Its exponent sum $5/3$ is greater than $\phi$, so this point is
excluded by Fortnow, not by Williams's universal product theorem.

The endpoint discipline used below is therefore:
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{Product route:} prove one simultaneous realization with a
strict margin $t+s<\phi$ and invoke Williams.
\item[] \textbf{Direct route:} prove $M^{1+o(1)}$ time together with
$M^{1-\varepsilon}$ space for one fixed $\varepsilon>0$ and invoke Fortnow.
\item[] \textbf{Small-space route:} invoke the $\gamma_0$ constant only
after proving the separately required $M^{o(1)}$-space hypothesis.
\end{description}

\section{The strongest direct proof spine}

\subsection{The one remaining theorem}

\begin{hypothesis}[Charged strict successor, CSS]
\label{hyp:css}
Assume $P=NP$.  There exist one fixed seed solver $A$, one total uniform
deterministic constructor $W=W_A$, and a threshold $M_0$ such that for every
well-formed canonical CNF $F$ of encoded length $M\ge M_0$, $W(F)$ returns a
well-formed ordinary canonical CNF $G$ satisfying
\begin{align}
 \SAT(G)&=\SAT(F),                                      \label{eq:css-sem}\\
 L(G)&\le M^{2/3+o(1)},                                \label{eq:css-size}\\
 T_W(F)&\le M^{1+o(1)},                                \label{eq:css-time}\\
 S_W(F)&\le M^{2/3+o(1)}.                              \label{eq:css-space}
\end{align}
The $o(1)$ functions are uniform over inputs of the same length.  The output
lies in the same domain, so $W$ can be reapplied.  The time and space bounds
charge parsing, source scans, route discovery, failed alternatives, hashing,
prime or label computation, collision resolution, equivalence tests,
backdoor discovery, all calls to $A$ or any other solver, recursive calls,
certificate generation and checking, replay, random-bit generation or
derandomization, output production, all materialized children, retained
parents, decoder state, and combination.  The correctness proof is uniform
and source-checkable and uses neither per-length advice nor an uncharged
oracle, source pointer, ROM, external object, or unbounded word.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{center}
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.93\textwidth}
\textbf{Remaining theorem, CSS (\Open{}).}  Under $P=NP$, one fixed total
uniform deterministic $W_A$ must map every sufficiently large canonical
$M$-bit CNF to an equivalent ordinary canonical CNF of length
$M^{2/3+o(1)}$, in $M^{1+o(1)}$ time and $M^{2/3+o(1)}$ live space, and its
output must lie in the same domain for reapplication.  These bounds include
parsing and malformed rejection; discovery and failed routes; all source
scans, solver or recursive calls, and replay; hashing, primes, collisions,
equivalence and backdoor tests; randomness or derandomization; certificates,
decoders, retained parents, materialized queries, stacks, and every output
bit.  Correctness must be uniform and source-checkable, with no advice,
uncharged oracle, source pointer, ROM, external object, or unbounded word.
This is precisely Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:css}.  It is not established by the
current experiments or literature audit.
\end{minipage}}
\end{center}

The numeric value $2/3$ is a convenient sufficient point, not a claim of
necessity.  More generally, any charged recursively closed successor whose
finite composition reaches a verified endpoint would suffice.

\subsection{Finite-depth seed elimination}

\begin{theorem}[Direct WRAPPER composition, \Exact{}]
\label{thm:direct-compose}
Let $A$ satisfy \eqref{eq:seed}.  Suppose a total uniform constructor maps
each length-$m$ CNF to one equivalent ordinary CNF of length
$m^{h+o(1)}$, using time $m^{r+o(1)}$ and live space
$m^{p+o(1)}$, where $0<h<1$.  The constructor is recursively closed and all
charges are as in Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:css}.  For every fixed depth $d$, apply
the constructor $d$ times and then run $A$.  The resulting SAT algorithm has
exponents
\begin{align}
 t_d&\le \max\{r,Kh^d\},\label{eq:direct-t}\\
 s_d&\le \max\{p,h,Lh^d\}.\label{eq:direct-s}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After $i$ stages the current formula has length
$M^{h^i+o(1)}$.  Because $d$ is fixed, the sum of construction times is
bounded by the largest exponent, $M^{r+o(1)}$, and the seed call takes
$M^{Kh^d+o(1)}$ time.  At any stage, discard an obsolete writable parent
after its child has been produced.  The largest materialized successor has
size $M^{h+o(1)}$, construction workspace is $M^{p+o(1)}$, and the seed call
uses $M^{Lh^d+o(1)}$ space.  Taking the maxima gives
\eqref{eq:direct-t}--\eqref{eq:direct-s}.  Each stage preserves
satisfiability, so the final answer is exact.  The fixed composition is a
single total uniform terminating program.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[CSS reaches Fortnow, \Exact{} conditional implication]
Under CSS, choose one fixed $d$ large enough that
\[
 K(2/3)^d\le1,
 \qquad
 L(2/3)^d\le2/3.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:direct-compose} gives one exact SAT algorithm with
time $M^{1+o(1)}$ and space $M^{2/3+o(1)}$.  Fortnow's theorem gives a
contradiction.  Therefore
\[
 (P=NP)\wedge\mathrm{CSS}\Longrightarrow\bot.
\]
It is not presently valid to remove ``$\wedge\mathrm{CSS}$''.
\end{corollary}

The original ``divide the $2^K$ contribution by repeated $2^x$'' argument
belongs exactly here.  Each $2/3$ size contraction multiplies the seed
exponents by $2/3$; fixed repetition removes the unknown $K,L$.  That
arithmetic starts only after the first charged successor has been built.  It
does not construct CSS.

\section{Alternative finite typed WRAPPER spine}

A direct single-successor compiler is sufficient but not logically
necessary.  A finite epoch may have multiple typed recursive exits, direct
VALUE exits, and administrative work.  The safe formulation is finite-depth
composition ending in total seed solvers.  An unbounded recursive procedure
is not justified merely because a Bellman inequality has a finite solution.

Let $I$ be a fixed finite set of types and $I_0\subseteq I$ the possible
entry types.  Refine types until every history-dependent feasibility fact is
encoded.  For an edge $e:i\to j$, let
$\theta_e\in[0,1]$ be child scale, $w_e$ the charged recursive time weight,
and $h_e$ the retained-state space exponent.  Let $R_i$ include all
nonrecursive time, including classification, discovery, failed trials,
VALUE work, child generation, replay, and combination.  Let $P_i$ include
all live nonrecursive storage and materialized or virtual-child state.  All
parameters must be realized by the same implementation.

Start at depth zero with total seed solvers of exponents $K_i,L_i$.  Define
\begin{align}
 C_i^{(0)}&=K_i,&
 C_i^{(d+1)}&=\max\{R_i,\max_{e:i\to j}(w_e+\theta_e C_j^{(d)})\},
 \label{eq:typed-time}\\
 \rho_i^{(0)}&=L_i,&
 \rho_i^{(d+1)}&=\max\{P_i,\max_{e:i\to j}h_e,
                         \max_{e:i\to j}\theta_e\rho_j^{(d)}\}.
 \label{eq:typed-space}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-depth typed seed elimination, \Exact{}]
\label{thm:typed}
Assume every directed cycle reachable from $I_0$ has scale product strictly
below one.  Let $C_i^*,\rho_i^*$ be the least solutions of the corresponding
Bellman inequalities.  Then the finite-depth budgets in
\eqref{eq:typed-time}--\eqref{eq:typed-space} converge at the roots to those
least budgets.  If
\begin{equation}
 \max_{i\in I_0}C_i^*+\max_{i\in I_0}\rho_i^*<\phi,
 \label{eq:typed-landing}
\end{equation}
one fixed depth yields a total uniform exact SAT algorithm in Williams's
valid product-forbidden region.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the finite recurrences gives maxima over typed paths plus a seed
boundary term multiplied by the path's scale product.  Since the graph is
finite and every reachable cycle contracts, there are constants
$a<\infty$ and $\lambda<1$ such that every block of $a$ edges contributes
scale at most $\lambda$.  Hence the maximum length-$d$ path product tends to
zero geometrically.  The root budgets converge to the Bellman path maxima.
The strict margin in \eqref{eq:typed-landing} survives at some fixed depth;
hardwire that depth and terminate every leaf with its total seed solver.
This gives a terminating uniform program with simultaneous product exponent
below $\phi$.
\end{proof}

This theorem repairs a real flaw in an unbounded-recursion reading of the
WRAPPER: a scale-one self-loop can satisfy a displayed local inequality yet
never halt.  Finite seed depth makes termination explicit.  The remaining
\Open{} typed assertion is the all-input construction of an epoch whose
fully charged least budgets satisfy \eqref{eq:typed-landing}.  A numerically
favorable ledger without an implemented constructor is not such a theorem.

\section{Closed generated covers are ordinary shrinkers}

Generated outputs were proposed as a way to avoid materializing all members
of a disjunctive cover.  Under a closed explicit representation this is not a
weaker final object than the ordinary shrinker already sought.

\begin{lemma}[Canonical verifier, CANONICAL-VERIFY1, \Exact{}]
\label{lem:canonical}
For a canonical padded CNF encoding of length $m$ with
$O(\log m)$-bit literal records, there is a uniform bounded-fan-in circuit
$V_m(x,y)$ of description length $\widetilde O(m)$ such that
\[
 \exists y\ V_m(x,y)=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 x\text{ is a well-formed satisfiable CNF encoding}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The circuit parses and range-checks the encoding, guesses one value for each
active literal occurrence, and checks each clause in its original order.
It sends records $(\mathrm{active},\mathrm{id},\mathrm{value})$ through a
uniform Batcher sorting network and rejects adjacent active records with the
same identifier but different values.  The sorting and record comparisons
use $\widetilde O(m)$ gates \cite{batcher1968sorting}.  Consistency plus all
clause checks is equivalent to a single satisfying assignment.  Malformed
headers, delimiters, identifiers, and padding are rejected.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[GENERATOR-SHRINKER-EQUIVALENCE1, \Exact{}]
\label{thm:generator}
For every $M$-bit CNF $F$, suppose a deterministic uniform constructor emits
a closed topologically encoded circuit $E_F$ over a fixed finite basis.  Its
only free terminals are selector bits.  Count every selector and output
terminal, gate, constant, wire-description bit, and embedded source bit, and
write $e=L(E_F)\le M^{h+o(1)}$ for some $h<1$.  If
\begin{equation}
 \SAT(F)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists z,y\ V_m(E_F(z),y)=1,
 \label{eq:generated-cover}
\end{equation}
where malformed and unused selector outputs are rejected, then one can emit
an equivalent ordinary 3-CNF $Q_F$ with
\begin{align*}
 L(Q_F)&\le M^{h+o(1)},\\
 T(Q_F)&\le M^{\max\{r_E,h\}+o(1)},\\
 S(Q_F)&\le M^{\max\{p_E,h\}+o(1)},
\end{align*}
where $r_E,p_E$ are the fully charged construction exponents of $E_F$.
Conversely, a charged ordinary shrinker gives a constant-output generator
with the same exponents up to polylogarithmic factors.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every output bit has an explicit terminal, so $m\le e$.  Compose $E_F$ with
the circuit from Lemma~\ref{lem:canonical}, constrain its acceptance bit to
one, and add full biconditional Tseitin clauses for every gate.  Existential
selector, witness, and gate variables satisfy the resulting formula exactly
when \eqref{eq:generated-cover} holds.  The verifier and Tseitin encoding add
only $\widetilde O(e)$ bits.  Materializing the result gives the stated time
and space maxima.  For the converse, after constructing an ordinary
successor, encode its bits as constants in a closed constant-output circuit.
\end{proof}

This replaces the earlier quadratic-verifier analysis and removes the old
condition $2b\le h$.  A compact closed generated cover and an ordinary
shrinker are equivalent up to polylogarithmic factors.  A source pointer,
ROM lookup, shared external object, compressed bus, random oracle, or
unpriced decoder is not a closed generator.  OR-MUX circuitization can share
the evaluation of an explicit family, but an equality-volume family still
has parent-scale total description; the multiplexer does not create a
contraction \cite{pippengerfischer1979}.

\section{Auditable proof spine}

The strongest direct route is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 P=NP
 &\Longrightarrow \text{choose one fixed clocked polynomial SAT solver }A\\
 &\mathrel{\mathop{\Longrightarrow}^{\Open}}
       \text{construct CSS at }(h,r,p)=(2/3,1,2/3)\\
 &\Longrightarrow \text{apply it a fixed depth and then run }A\\
 &\Longrightarrow (t,s)=(1,2/3)
 \Longrightarrow \bot\quad\text{(Fortnow).}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:direct-spine}
\end{equation}

The alternative typed route is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 P=NP
 &\Longrightarrow \text{choose fixed typed seed solvers}\\
 &\mathrel{\mathop{\Longrightarrow}^{\Open}}
       \text{construct one fully charged cycle-contracting typed epoch}\\
 &\Longrightarrow
       \max_{i\in I_0}C_i^*+\max_{i\in I_0}\rho_i^*<\phi\\
 &\Longrightarrow \text{one fixed finite-depth uniform algorithm}
 \Longrightarrow \bot\quad\text{(Williams).}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:typed-spine}
\end{equation}

Generator--shrinker equivalence says that replacing ``ordinary successor''
in \eqref{eq:direct-spine} by ``closed generated cover'' does not bypass its
open arrow.  The wrapper portfolio may still discover CSS or the typed epoch,
but its discovery and replay costs are part of the theorem to be proved.

\section{Conclusion}

The WRAPPER program now has a defensible conditional spine and a sharply
localized open theorem.  The endpoint correction makes the manuscript
stronger, not weaker: it removes a retracted universal claim while preserving
the principal $(1,2/3)$ route through the correct Fortnow theorem.  Finite
depth repairs recursive halting, and the canonical verifier closes the
generated-output bookkeeping without the old $2b\le h$ loss.  What remains
is substantive rather than notational: construct, on every canonical CNF,
the first strictly smaller equivalent object within the complete charged
envelope.  That theorem has not yet been proved, so P versus NP remains open.
