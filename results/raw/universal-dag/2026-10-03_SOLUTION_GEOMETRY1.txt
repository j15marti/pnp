SOLUTION-GEOMETRY1
Date: 2026-10-03
Status: COMPUTATIONAL finite experiment + KNOWN-THEOREM literature mapping.
P versus NP remains OPEN.

Target:
Test whether sparse SAT solution sets exhibit a curvature/distortion-like geometric invariant that tracks computational hardness more robustly than solution count.

Literature anchors:
- Gopalan, Kolaitis, Maneva, Papadimitriou (SIAM J. Comput. 2009): Boolean solution graphs under single-variable flips have structural/computational dichotomies; 3-CNF solution-graph connectivity is PSPACE-complete and components can have exponential diameter, whereas tractable-side classes have linear component diameter.
- Achlioptas and Ricci-Tersenghi (SIAM J. Comput. 2009): random CSP solution clusters develop frozen variables.
- Gamarnik (PNAS 2021): overlap-gap geometry can rigorously obstruct stable/insensitive algorithm classes, but is not an unrestricted NP-hardness theorem.
- Bresler and Huang (FOCS 2021): many-way OGP yields lower bounds for low-degree polynomial algorithms on random k-SAT.
- Kizildag (APPROX/RANDOM 2025): sharp-threshold OGP results for random k-SAT/p-spin in the relevant asymptotic large-k/random setting.
- Zhan (arXiv:2602.23411, 2026 preprint): proposes a Boolean-hypercube discrete-geometric explanation of random 3-SAT clustering/freezing/easy-hard-easy; useful current analogy, not an established unrestricted lower-bound theorem.
- Ollivier and Villani (SIAM J. Discrete Math. 2012): discrete hypercube supports rigorous Ricci-curvature notions, but this run did not use Ricci curvature as the primary metric.

Experiment:
Exact exhaustive assignment enumeration for n=20,22,24.
Random simple genuine 3-SAT at alpha in {2.5,3,3.5,4,4.25,4.5}.
Seed base 20261003.
Repetitions: n20=20 per alpha, n22=10 per alpha, n24=5 per alpha.

Hostile controls:
- planted 2-SAT, density 4;
- satisfiable Horn 3-CNF, density 4;
- planted local-chain 3-CNF whose primal graph has constant pathwidth/treewidth, density 4;
- planted consecutive 3-XOR equations converted exactly to four 3-CNF clauses each, with n-4 equations and 16 satisfying assignments; effective CNF density is 3.2, 3.2727, 3.3333 at n=20,22,24.

Metrics:
- number of solutions;
- number of single-bit-flip connected components;
- largest-component fraction;
- global backbone coordinates;
- frozen variables in largest component;
- local flexibility = average solution-graph degree / n;
- fraction of solution pairs in different components;
- intrinsic/Hamming stretch d_solution/d_Hamming, sampled from up to 64 exact BFS sources in largest component;
- sampled/exact missing Hamming-distance-band size;
- minimum Hamming distance between distinct components;
- basic DPLL node count on the same formula.

Main finite random-3SAT signal:
At n=24 median values:
alpha 2.5: solutions 3644, components 3, largest fraction .964, frozen 1, disconnected-pair fraction .071, local flex .307, max sampled stretch 4.33.
alpha 3.0: solutions 481, components 4, largest fraction .606, frozen 6, disconnected fraction .568, local flex .208, max stretch 2.6.
alpha 3.5: solutions 114, components 5, largest fraction .421, frozen 16, disconnected fraction .708, local flex .145, max stretch 1.
alpha 4.0: solutions 6, components 2, frozen 18, local flex .097.
alpha 4.25: solutions 21, components 3, largest fraction .571, frozen 18, disconnected fraction .610, local flex .087.
These are very small finite sizes and not asymptotic threshold estimates.

Key observation 1: fragmentation/freezing, not geodesic stretch, is the visible finite signal.
Max intrinsic/Hamming stretch is largest at lower density, then often returns to 1 because surviving clusters become tiny or geodesically convex.
The naive pairwise Hamming-gap diagnostic is zero on all tested random-3SAT medians; it does not recover asymptotic/many-way OGP at these finite sizes.

Key observation 2: raw geometry badly fails the universal-hardness hostile controls.
At n=24:
- planted bounded-treewidth chain: median 77 solutions, 10 components, largest fraction .333, 18 frozen variables, disconnected-pair fraction .826.
- planted affine/XOR control: exactly 16 solutions, 4 components, largest fraction .25, 22 frozen variables in largest component, disconnected-pair fraction .80, missing Hamming band 11, minimum intercomponent Hamming distance 14.
Yet the chain is solvable by constant-width dynamic programming and XOR by Gaussian elimination.
Therefore shattering, freezing, disconnectedness, and overlap gaps can all be large in polynomial-time families.

Key observation 3:
Planted 2-SAT can be extremely sparse (median 4 solutions at n=24) while remaining one connected component and trivially easy.
Thus sparsity alone is also not the resource.

Instance-wise correlation:
Across satisfiable random-3SAT instances pooled over n=20,22,24, Spearman correlations between log2(DPLL nodes+1) and tested geometry metrics were weak:
solutions -0.151; components -0.132; frozen-largest +0.118; local flexibility -0.120; disconnected-pair fraction -0.064; mean stretch -0.157; max stretch -0.153; min intercomponent distance +0.009.
This basic DPLL is only one solver and small-n finite data, so the correlation null result is COMPUTATIONAL only.

Falsification:
RAW-GEOMETRY-HARDNESS is FALSIFIED/RETIRED as a universal SAT hardness invariant.
Sparse/clustered/frozen/distorted solution spaces can be algorithmically easy when an affine or bounded-treewidth coordinate system exists.

Surviving candidate:
Define distortion only after an efficient-structure quotient/atlas:
Dist*(F) = minimum residual geometric obstruction after applying a declared tractable atlas such as affine elimination, Horn/2-SAT closure, bounded-treewidth decomposition, and certified semantic wrappers.
Acquisition of the atlas must be charged; existence alone is not free.

Possible resource:
For each tractable representation R in atlas A, map solutions to quotient states and measure:
- number/entropy of connected primitive components,
- intrinsic-vs-ambient stretch after quotient,
- multi-overlap gaps after quotient,
- frozen directions not explained by the tractable structure.
Then minimize over R that are efficiently constructible from the SAME_REALIZATION.

Barrier:
No theorem presently maps large quotient-normalized distortion to arbitrary SAT runtime.
OGP results exclude specified stable/local/low-degree algorithm classes, not all polynomial-time algorithms.
Gopalan et al. concern reconfiguration complexity, not ordinary SAT decision complexity.
Thus the arbitrary-algorithm bridge remains OPEN.

Next target:
Build an "atlas-normalized distortion" experiment:
1. exactly eliminate the planted XOR controls by Gaussian coordinates;
2. collapse bounded-treewidth chain controls by separator states;
3. compare the residual primitive/continuation geometry with random 3-SAT;
4. test whether any geometry remains large after these known polynomial-time normalizations.
