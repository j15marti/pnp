Date: 2026-10-03
Status: COMPUTATIONAL upper-bound curve + PROVED model-validity of each constructed BP.
P versus NP remains OPEN.

Target
------
Measure the STORE-versus-RECOMP tradeoff between FBDD/read-once and read-twice on the Q4 edge-cover representation of full four-variable 3-SAT clause presence.

Model
-----
Fix the 16 cube vertices in order. To verify the vertex-edge-cover CNF, inspect incident edge variables at each vertex.

For a chosen set K of edge variables:
- e in K may be queried independently at both endpoints, so e can occur twice on a path;
- e notin K may be queried at most once; if queried at its earlier endpoint, its Boolean value is retained in the branching-program state until the later endpoint.

Thus every constructed program is syntactic (1,+|K|):
only variables in K are permitted a second read.
DAG states with identical future behavior are hash-consed exactly.

The reported curve is a GREEDY VALID UPPER BOUND, not the minimum possible (1,+k) size.

Interpretation
--------------
Within this vertex-sequential STORE/RECOMP architecture:
- one reread-enabled variable nearly halves size: 14328 -> 7284;
- three reread-enabled variables give 2317 nodes, already below the previously constructed 2432-node general FBDD upper bound (the baselines are different architectures, so this is not a lower-bound separation);
- twelve reread-enabled variables give 215 nodes;
- sixteen give 128;
- twenty-two give 81;
- twenty-nine are enough for the 64-node fully recomputing architecture.

This is a direct finite demonstration that a small amount of legal recomputation can substitute for a large amount of remembered frontier state.

Literature
----------
Borodin-Razborov-Smolensky (Computational Complexity 1993) established strong lower bounds for syntactic read-k programs and explicitly noted functions easy for read-twice but hard for read-once variants.
Jukna (1995) emphasized the syntactic/semantic distinction.
Savicky-Zak (TCS 2000) established a strict (1,+k) hierarchy for explicit pointer functions.
Earlier (1,+k) lower-bound work gives exponential/superpolynomial bounds for explicit functions when only a sublinear number of variables can be reread.
Razgon's recent bounded-repetition CNF work identifies the "splitting power" of read-twice programs as a central mechanism and gives upper/lower bounds under structural restrictions.

What this does NOT prove
------------------------
1. The k=0 value 14328 is only this vertex-sequential architecture; a separate adaptive FBDD construction of size 2432 is already known.
2. The curve is greedy, not globally optimized for K or vertex ordering.
3. 64 is an upper bound, not the global minimum read-twice size.
4. The dramatic collapse is special to n=4 because each genuine 3-clause is falsified by only two assignments. For n-variable 3-SAT the analogous assignment-cover occurrence is 2^(n-3).
5. No arbitrary-algorithm lower bound follows.

Research consequence
--------------------
READ-ONCE STATE is not robust under even small recomputation budgets in this laboratory.
Any candidate lower-bound potential must therefore charge at least one of:
- reread/recomputation count,
- retained frontier information,
- total query work,
- or a combined time-space-recomputation product.

A promising finite quantity is the Pareto frontier
  (number of reread-enabled variables k,
   DAG nodes,
   worst total queries,
   retained information bits).
The next run should compute retained-state entropy/frontier width along this exact curve and test whether a product such as
  state_bits * recomputation
or
  space * time
is substantially more stable than node count alone.
