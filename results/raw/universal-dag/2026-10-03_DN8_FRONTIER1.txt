DN8-FRONTIER1: characterization of D(n,8)
Date: 2026-10-03
Status: PROVED/EXACT for the elementary reductions below; COMPUTATIONAL for enumerated core counts and finite certificates. P versus NP remains OPEN.

Definition
D(n,8) is the family of simple genuine 3-CNFs on an ambient set of n variables,
with exactly 8 distinct clauses. Each clause contains exactly 3 distinct variables.
Input encoding has M(n)=8*C(n,3) clause-presence bits and the promise that exactly 8 are 1.

1. Eight clauses are the minimum UNSAT count
Each genuine 3-clause falsifies exactly 2^(n-3) assignments.
Seven clauses can cover at most 7*2^(n-3) < 2^n assignments.
Therefore every UNSAT D(n,8) formula is minimally unsatisfiable.

2. Support cutoff
Tarsi's lemma for minimally unsatisfiable CNFs gives number of used variables <= number of clauses - 1.
Hence every UNSAT member of D(n,8) uses at most 7 variables.
There are no genuinely new full-support UNSAT cores for n>=8.

4. Certified all-pairs clause-query lower bound
For n=4,5,6,7, exhaustive exact-cover enumeration gives, for every clause input i,
intersection of all UNSAT covers containing i = {i}.
Thus for every unordered input pair {i,j}, some UNSAT exact cover contains i but not j.
Replacing i by j yields a legal weight-eight SAT input: removing i leaves exactly its
falsifying subcube uncovered, and a distinct genuine clause has a distinct falsifying
codimension-3 subcube and cannot replace it exactly.

Therefore every input pair has an opposite-output swap witness.
If a clause-query DAG had at most M(n)-2 internal nodes, it could query at most M(n)-2
distinct inputs globally, leaving at least two inputs unqueried. Their certified witness
pair would be indistinguishable. Hence:
B(n,8) >= M(n)-1 = 8*C(n,3)-1 for n=4,5,6,7.

For every n>=7, any pair of 3-clauses uses at most six variables. Map that pair into the
seven-variable certificate by a signed variable permutation, take its D(7,8) witness,
and embed the at-most-seven-variable witness back into the ambient n variables.
Therefore:
B(n,8) >= 8*C(n,3)-1 for every n>=4.

5. Declared fan-in-two circuit lower bound
The all-pairs witness property implies a correct circuit on the promise domain must depend
on at least M(n)-1 clause inputs. A connected circuit combining r distinct source inputs
using fan-in-two binary gates requires at least r-1 binary combining gates. Thus on the
declared AND/OR plus unary-NOT basis:
G(n,8) >= 8*C(n,3)-2 for every n>=4.
This is a model-specific finite/promise-domain circuit lower bound, not an unrestricted SAT-time lower bound.

6. Exact pair-conflict representation
Under the exact-eight promise, UNSAT iff the eight falsifying subcubes are pairwise disjoint.
Therefore SAT iff there exists a selected pair of clauses with overlapping falsifying subcubes.
This gives an exact quadratic DNF over the clause-presence bits.

For a fixed clause, its conflict-graph degree is
d(n)=8*C(n-3,3)+12*C(n-3,2)+6*(n-3).
With M(n)=8*C(n,3), the conflict graph has
E(n)=M(n)*d(n)/2
edges.
A naive fan-in-two implementation gives SAT circuit size <= 2E(n)-1, which is O(n^6).
Thus fixed-m=8 under this presence-bit encoding has a polynomial-size circuit family; this
finite frontier cannot by itself witness unrestricted 3-SAT hardness.

The direct assignment-coverage circuit gives the independent upper bound
G(n,8) <= 2^n*C(n,3).
For small n this is often smaller than the naive pair-conflict DNF; structural factoring can improve both, as in D(7,8) <= 2285.
