Literature/model identification:
- An acyclic switching-and-rectifier network is a directed acyclic graph with literal-labelled switching edges and free rectifier edges; acceptance is existence of a live source-sink path.
- This is the classical nondeterministic branching-program / NROBP viewpoint. Modern papers explicitly call NROBPs acyclic read-once switching-and-rectifier networks.
- Rectifier networks are DAGs whose complexity is measured by routing edges and depth.
- Read-k and semantic branching-program literature shows that allowing repeated reads can make restricted lower bounds much harder; the theorem below already permits arbitrary repetition of positive clause labels globally.

Declared model for the theorem:
1. Directed or undirected passive monotone switching-and-rectifier network.
2. Controlled contacts are labelled only by positive clause-presence variables x_c.
3. A label may occur arbitrarily many times.
4. Free rectifier/wiring edges and arbitrary graph sharing are allowed.
5. Correctness is required only on the exact-weight-eight promise D(n,8).
6. Resource C is the total number of controlled contact occurrences.
7. Sequential relays, complement/normally-closed contacts, and active memory are outside the model.

Exact structural fact:
Let M=8*C(n,3) and H_n be the clause-conflict graph.
A set of pairwise nonconflicting clauses corresponds to pairwise disjoint falsifying subcubes.
Each such subcube has size 2^(n-3), hence alpha(H_n)<=8.
All eight sign patterns on one variable triple form an independent set of size 8, so alpha(H_n)=8.

Cut-hitting lemma:
Let K be any finite controlled-contact s-t cut and L(K) its set of distinct clause labels.
Put R=V(H_n)\L(K).
Every promised input using only labels in R disables all contacts of K and is rejected.

If |R|>8, then because alpha(H_n)=8, R contains a conflict pair {u,v}.
Choose any six further labels in R.
The resulting exact-weight-eight formula contains a conflict pair and is therefore SAT.
It avoids the cut labels, contradiction.

Hence every controlled-contact cut contains at least:
M-8
distinct clause labels, and therefore at least M-8 controlled edges.

Contact-capacitated Menger/max-flow:
Give every controlled contact capacity 1 and every free rectifier edge infinite capacity.
The minimum finite s-t cut has capacity at least M-8.
Therefore there exist at least M-8 s-t paths that are pairwise disjoint in controlled contacts.

Every such path contains at least two controlled contacts.
Reason: if a path had exactly one controlled contact x_c, then any promised formula containing c would activate that path. But c belongs to an UNSAT exact cover, namely the eight sign patterns on its underlying variable triple.

Therefore every correct passive monotone switch network satisfies:
C >= 2(M-8)=2M-16.

Exact upper construction:
Choose two distinct variable triples.
Let A be the eight sign patterns on the first triple and B the eight sign patterns on the second.
A and B are disjoint independent sets.
Give A source-side contacts only, B sink-side contacts only, and every remaining clause one source and one sink contact.
Orient every conflict edge from a source-capable label to a sink-capable label and connect the corresponding contact endpoints by free rectifier routing.
This is an acyclic switching-and-rectifier DAG.
It uses:
|A| + |B| + 2(M-16) = 2M-16
controlled contacts and computes the conflict function, hence D(n,8) SAT on the promise.

Thus, after independent review, the target theorem is:
C_passive-DAG(n,8) = 2M-16 = 16*C(n,3)-16.

Its oriented conflict relation contains 96 source-sink pairs.
Maximal bicliques of this oriented 24x24 relation: 72.
The fractional rectangle-cover LP and the integer cover both equal 20.
The LP dual is integral and gives a 20-pair fooling set:
(0,16),(1,28),(2,25),(3,17),(4,30),(5,19),(6,18),(7,27),

Therefore every rectifier realization of THIS oriented routing relation has cut rectangle/biclique breadth at least 20.
This is a finite orientation-specific routing lower bound, not a theorem about all optimal 48-contact switch-DAG orientations.

New interpretation:
The user's contact-as-DAG model is not merely analogous to branching programs; it lands directly in switching-and-rectifier / nondeterministic branching-program complexity.
The fixed-m=8 family is especially revealing because DAG sharing and arbitrary positive-label repetition do not reduce controlled-contact count below 2M-16, although free routing still carries O(n^6) structure in the direct construction.

Major limitation:
Polynomial-size switching-and-rectifier / nondeterministic branching programs are an NL/poly-type model, not all polynomial time.
Known unrestricted NBP/switching-network lower bounds for explicit efficiently computable functions are weak compared with what a P-vs-NP proof would require.
Therefore this is a strong restricted-model calibration, not an arbitrary SAT lower bound.
