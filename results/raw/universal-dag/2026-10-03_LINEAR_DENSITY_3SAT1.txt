Interpretation:
The declared DPLL implementation exhibits an easy-hard-easy density pattern.
At alpha<=3, all sampled formulas were SAT and node growth was weak.
Near alpha=4.25..4.5, SAT/UNSAT balance changes and median DPLL nodes grow much faster.
Over this finite range an exponential fit near 1.058^n fits the median-node data better than
a simple power law, but this is finite algorithm-specific evidence only.
It is not evidence that all algorithms require exponential time.

Known model-specific certified input-dependence theorem remains separate:
for n>=4 and 8<=m<=7*C(n,3),
B(n,m)>=8*C(n,3)-1 in the global clause-query DAG model.
Thus for m=alpha*n at any fixed positive alpha and sufficiently large n, the certified
clause-query lower bound is Theta(n^3). This is polynomial and does not explain the DPLL
near-threshold search growth.

Literature context:
Random 3-SAT is known empirically/theoretically to have a transition near clause density
around 4.26; this experiment is consistent with the classical hard-band phenomenon but is
not a threshold proof.

P versus NP remains OPEN.
