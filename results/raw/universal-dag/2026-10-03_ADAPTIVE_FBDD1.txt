ADAPTIVE-FBDD1
Date: 2026-10-03
Status: COMPUTATIONAL finite construction and verification. P versus NP remains OPEN.

Target:
Attack the fixed-order residual-state barrier by allowing a free read-once branching program (FBDD): variable choice may depend on the path, but no input variable may be queried more than once on any root-to-sink path.

Literature:
1. Wegener: FBDD = read-once branching program; variable ordering may differ along paths.
2. Guenther-Drechsler 2002: exact FBDD minimization exists but is NP-hard / exact methods apply only to small Boolean functions; heuristics are standard for larger inputs.
3. Borodin-Razborov-Smolensky 1993: read-twice branching programs can be polynomial where read-once programs are not, so RECOMP/repeated tests are a mandatory hostile control.
4. Dinesh-Koroth-Sarma 2016: bitwise decomposable projective dimension characterizes deterministic branching-program size up to polynomial factors; ordinary projective dimension may be exponentially smaller.
5. Beame-Li-Roy-Suciu: decision-DNNFs convert to FBDDs with quasipolynomial blowup in general, so FBDD lower bounds can transfer to that restricted compilation family.

Comparison:
The current root MAIN records a 720-node fixed-order D(4,8) upper bound.
The adaptive read-once construction reduces this tested upper bound to 301 nodes and reduces worst-case path length to 17.
The result demonstrates that fixed-order residual-width measurements are not invariant under adaptive querying.

Promise-aware interpretation:
The FBDD construction uses the exact-weight-eight promise. A state is terminal UNSAT when every legal remaining completion is an exact cover and terminal SAT when no exact-cover completion remains.
This is a partial-function representation result, not a total-function FBDD theorem.

Hostile controls:
- Exact FBDD minimization was not completed; 301 and 2248 are upper bounds only.
- A direct exact minimax decision-depth computation was attempted for D(4,8) but did not finish within the run ceiling; no optimum depth claim is made.
- Read-twice/general branching programs can be much smaller than FBDDs in known explicit examples, so repeated queries remain a serious escape route.
- Fixed m=8 has an O(n^6) conflict circuit and a one-pass algorithm that can store the at most eight selected clause identities; therefore no superpolynomial hardness inference is available.
- Parity remains an easy control with deep querying but tiny state.
