\section{Proof spine}
All routes from an arbitrary polynomial-time decider to a superpolynomial lower bound remain \OPEN{}.

The fixed-width implication chain still gives the exact base rung: its formula
is UNSAT iff every orientation field is one, and every deterministic exact
field-query decider reads all $q$ orientations on the all-one input.  This is
a restricted input-access lower bound, not an unrestricted time theorem.

The next rung is now exact.  Write
\[
F(X,Y)=T(X)\wedge B(X,Y),
\]
where $B$ is 2-CNF, $T$ has at most $k$ clauses of width at most three, and
$X$ is the set of variables occurring in $T$.  Then $s=|X|\le3k$.
Existentially eliminating $Y$ preserves 2-CNF.  The conjunction $Q_B(X)$ of
all entailed unit/binary clauses is exactly $\exists Y\,B$, has at most
$2s^2$ clauses, and yields
\[
F\in SAT\iff T(X)\wedge Q_B(X)\in SAT.
\]
Thus the kernel has at most $3k$ variables and $k+18k^2$ clauses.  In the
declared word-RAM encoding it is constructible in $O(k^2N/\log N)$ word time
and $O(N+k^2\log N)$ peak live bits.

This is an upper bound and a transport barrier.  The source chain supplies
only input-order work, while projection construction already costs input
order, so the positive-remainder inequality produces no target lower bound.
Connectedness, essential ternary clauses, and minimum syntactic backdoor $k$
also fail to force $2^k$ work.  Arbitrary binary coupling of exactly-one
ternary blocks reaches the NP-complete graph-coloring endpoint, but
classification does not supply a superpolynomial time lower bound.
