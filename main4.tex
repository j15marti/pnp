\begin{abstract}
This manuscript records an existence-level route toward a contradiction from
$P=NP$. Its central premise is an unproved SAT-specific improvement theorem
for joint deterministic time-space bounds. We prove the scalar and matrix
iteration implications, state the exact endpoint and uniformity obligations,
and identify what a concrete WRAPPER could contribute. This is a conditional
research framework, not a proof of $P\ne NP$. Numerical checks verify the
arithmetic and demonstrate failures of weakened hypotheses; they do not
establish the open SAT-specific premise.
\end{abstract}

\section{Status, provenance, and the logical target}
The internal source PROOF-SYNTHESIS-0013 explicitly replaces a universal
source-code compiler requirement by language-level achievable-region
contraction \cite{arc0013}. PRF2-0155 supplies the affine iteration component
\cite{prf20155}. The concrete recursive WRAPPER route is developed separately
in \texttt{main5.tex}; its conditional composition source is PRF3-0018
\cite{prf30018}. The present route may be justified by such a construction,
but does not require rewriting every implementation of a SAT solver.

We distinguish four statuses throughout. \textsc{Established endpoint} means
a cited external theorem with its model restrictions. \textsc{Proved
conditional lemma} means a mathematical implication proved here, with its
premises retained. \textsc{Open hypothesis} means the needed SAT theorem has
not been established. \textsc{Computational check} means a finite executable
sanity check, which cannot establish a universal asymptotic claim.

The proposed implication is
\[
 (P=NP)\ \land\ H_{\mathrm{ARC}}\ \Longrightarrow\ \bot.
\]
Until $H_{\mathrm{ARC}}$ is proved, this is not a separation. Proving
$P=NP\Rightarrow H_{\mathrm{ARC}}$ would also suffice; an unconditional
construction is not logically required.

\section{The joint achievable region}
Fix a deterministic random-access model compatible with the endpoint below,
an ordinary explicit SAT encoding, and input length $N$. Space and time are
measured simultaneously on one implementation. Any simulations or encoding
changes must be charged before applying the endpoint.
\begin{definition}
The finite polynomial achievable region is
\[
\R=\left\{(t,s)\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2:
\begin{array}{l}
\exists\text{ one uniform exact SAT machine }A,\\
T_A(N)\le N^{t+o(1)},\quad S_A(N)\le N^{s+o(1)}
\end{array}\right\}.
\]
All asymptotic upper bounds are worst-case over inputs of the given length.
\end{definition}
The word ``uniform'' excludes separate advice or an independently selected
program at each input length. One fixed finite program must decide the whole
language. The two coordinates may not be borrowed from different machines.

\begin{lemma}
The region $\R$ is upward closed. If $P=NP$, then $\R$ contains a finite point.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A witness for $(t,s)$ also witnesses any componentwise larger bounds.
Under $P=NP$, SAT has a fixed polynomial-time deterministic decider. Its
ordinary workspace is polynomially bounded, and conversion to the fixed
reasonable model costs at most a polynomial. Thus some finite pair is
achievable.
\end{proof}
Adding artificial delay or copying instructions to a witness does not remove
faster achievable points. This neutralizes objections based solely on the
syntax of a deliberately slowed implementation. It does not produce any
new fast witness.

\section{The corrected external forbidden regions}
Set
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \gamma_0&=2\cos(\pi/7)=1.8019377358\ldots,\\
 \phi&=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}=1.6180339887\ldots,
 &\B&=\{(t,s):t+s<\phi\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The published full version of Williams's paper proves, for polynomially
bounded simultaneous time and space on the declared random-access model,
\[
 T(N)S(N)=\Omega_{\mathrm{i.o.}}(N^{\phi-\epsilon})
 \quad\text{for every }\epsilon>0 \cite[Theorem~4.11]{williams2010}.
\]
It also explicitly retracts the extended abstract's earlier claim that the
universal product exponent was $\gamma_0$ \cite[Section~4.5,
footnote~9]{williams2010}.  Separately, the established subpolynomial-space
time lower bound uses $\gamma_0$ in the $N^{o(1)}$-workspace regime;
$\gamma_0$ is not a general product half-plane
\cite[Theorem~1.2]{williams2008mod}\cite[Theorem~3.25]{busswilliams2015}.

There is a second endpoint important to the concrete WRAPPER route:
Fortnow proves that, for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, SAT cannot be solved
simultaneously in $N^{1+o(1)}$ time and $N^{1-\varepsilon}$ space on the
model-matched random-access machine \cite{fortnow2000}.  Hence the verified
forbidden set used in this manuscript is the union of
\[
 t+s<\phi,
 \qquad
 t\le1\ \text{ and }\ s<1,
 \qquad
 s=0\ \text{ and }\ t<\gamma_0,
 \tag{END}
\]
where each clause is invoked only with its cited model hypotheses.  In the
middle clause, $s<1$ means that some fixed
$\varepsilon>0$ has $s\le1-\varepsilon$; in the last clause, $s=0$ denotes
$N^{o(1)}$ workspace.  In particular, $(1,2/3)$ is forbidden by Fortnow,
although
$1+2/3>\phi$.

Here $N$ is actual encoded bit length, including headers, delimiters, binary
identifiers, padding, and embedded constants.  The endpoint machine is one
total uniform deterministic implementation; malformed encodings are rejected
within the charged bounds \cite[Section~2.1]{fortnowetal2005}.  Input is read-only, every writable store and
materialized recursive query is counted, and words contain $O(\log N)$ bits.
Parallel branches are serialized and charged by total work.  Random streams,
per-length seeds, advice, source pointers, and unbounded-word prime or hash
operations are not free.  A representation change is admissible only after a
bit-local $N^{1+o(1)}$ model simulation or a fully charged explicit
conversion; generic polynomial equivalence is insufficient.

For the product branch, if $t+s=\phi-\delta$ with $\delta>0$, the eventual
upper product is at most $N^{\phi-3\delta/4}$.  Taking
$\epsilon=\delta/2$ contradicts the infinitely-often lower bound.  No
assertion at equality is used.

\section{Scalar achievable-region contraction}
\begin{hypothesis}[SAT-specific ARC, scalar sufficient form]
Under the standing hypothesis $P=NP$, there exist fixed constants
$0<\lambda<1$ and $c\ge0$ with
\[
 v_*:=\frac{c}{1-\lambda}<\phi
\]
such that every $z=(t,s)\in\R\setminus\B$ has some
$z'=(t',s')\in\R$ satisfying
\[
 t'+s'\le\lambda(t+s)+c.
 \tag{ARC}
\]
\end{hypothesis}
The constants are independent of the point being improved and of input
length. The witness for $z'$ can differ from the witness for $z$. ARC is an
existence implication between uniform algorithms. It is not a rule selecting
a convenient algorithm separately for each input. No recovered construction
has established this hypothesis for general SAT.

\begin{theorem}[Finite crossing, conditional on ARC]
If ARC holds and $\R$ contains a finite point, then
$\R\cap\B\ne\varnothing$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start with $z_0\in\R$ and let $v_0=t_0+s_0$. If $z_0\in\B$, we are done.
Otherwise choose the next witness by ARC, stopping if it enters $\B$.
For every constructed stage,
\[
 v_r\le\lambda^r v_0+c\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\lambda^j
      =v_*+\lambda^r(v_0-v_*).
\]
If no previous stage has entered, choose a finite integer $r$ such that
\[
 \lambda^r(v_0-v_*)<\phi-v_*.
\]
Such an integer exists because the right side is positive. The inequality
forces $v_r<\phi$, completing the proof.
\end{proof}
When $v_0>\phi$, any integer strictly greater than
\[
\frac{\log((v_0-v_*)/(\phi-v_*))}{\log(1/\lambda)}
\]
is sufficient. This bound is for proof analysis; the contradiction does not
require an algorithm that first discovers the unknown starting exponents.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional separation spine]
The model-compatible endpoint, $P=NP$, and ARC imply a contradiction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The assumption supplies a finite point. The preceding theorem supplies a
point in $\B$. The endpoint prohibits that point.
\end{proof}

\section{More general potentials and matrix contraction}
For a different potential $V$, require an explicit threshold $\theta$ with
$\{z:V(z)<\theta\}\subseteq\B$, not merely a claim that smaller potential
``suggests'' improved resources. An affine contraction with fixed point
below $\theta$ then gives the same finite-crossing argument.

\begin{hypothesis}[Matrix sufficient form]
There are an entrywise nonnegative matrix $M\in\mathbb R^{2\times2}$ and
vector $d\ge0$ with spectral radius $\rho(M)<1$ such that
\[
 z_*=(I-M)^{-1}d\in\B,
\]
and for every $z\in\R\setminus\B$ some $z'\in\R$ satisfies
$z'\le Mz+d$ componentwise.
\end{hypothesis}
\begin{lemma}[Matrix envelope]
This matrix hypothesis and a finite achievable starting point force finite
entry into $\B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Define the upper envelope $u_0=z_0$ and $u_{r+1}=Mu_r+d$.
Nonnegativity of $M$ implies inductively $z_r\le u_r$ until entry occurs.
Also
\[
 u_r=z_*+M^r(z_0-z_*)\longrightarrow z_*.
\]
Since $\B$ is open relative to the nonnegative quadrant, $u_r\in\B$ for
all sufficiently large $r$. Since $\B$ is downward closed,
$0\le z_r\le u_r$ implies $z_r\in\B$.
\end{proof}
The actual witness sequence need not converge; convergence of its envelope
is enough. Spectral radius less than one does not imply that $t+s$ decreases
at each individual step. Non-normal matrices can exhibit transient growth.
For a general proposed forbidden set, downward closure or another valid
order-to-membership argument must be established. An upper bound near an
arbitrary open set does not suffice.

A multi-stage improvement cycle is another option. Its composite affine map
must satisfy the required contraction and endpoint conditions, and every
intermediate witness must have justified joint resource bounds. Contraction
of only one selected stage cannot cancel uncharged expansion elsewhere.

\subsection{Finite existential return cycles}
The preceding multi-stage option admits an exact existence-level formulation
that is weaker than requiring every phase to contract. It is useful when one
transformation improves time while worsening space and a later transformation
reverses that trade.

\begin{definition}[Finite return witness]
Fix an entrywise nonnegative matrix $P$ and vector $q\ge0$. A finite
$(P,q)$-return witness for $z\in\R$ is a finite list
\[
 z=z^{(0)},z^{(1)},\ldots,z^{(m)}=w
 \quad\text{with every }z^{(i)}\in\R
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 w\le Pz+q.
\]
The intermediate points may expand and the length $m$ need not be uniform.
Each point must nevertheless be witnessed by one uniform exact SAT machine
with joint time and space bounds.
\end{definition}

\begin{hypothesis}[SAT-specific finite return closure]
There are fixed $P\ge0$ and $q\ge0$ with $\rho(P)<1$ and
\[
 z_*=(I-P)^{-1}q\in\B
\]
such that every $z\in\R\setminus\B$ has a finite $(P,q)$-return witness.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{theorem}[Finite return-cycle crossing]
If the finite return closure holds and $\R$ contains a finite point, then
$\R\cap\B\ne\varnothing$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Start from a finite $z_0\in\R$. If it is outside $\B$, choose the endpoint
$z_1$ of one finite return witness; continue only while the selected endpoint
is outside $\B$. Define $u_0=z_0$ and $u_{r+1}=Pu_r+q$. Nonnegativity of
$P$ gives $z_r\le u_r$ by induction. Moreover
\[
 u_r=z_*+P^r(z_0-z_*)\longrightarrow z_*.
\]
The open, downward-closed set $\B$ therefore contains $u_r$, and hence
$z_r$, after finitely many outer returns. Only finitely many finite witness
chains have then been selected, so their concatenation is finite.
\end{proof}

A checkable sufficient form uses a fixed sequence of phase bounds
\[
 z^{(i+1)}\le M_i z^{(i)}+d_i,\qquad i=0,\ldots,m-1,
\]
with $M_i,d_i$ entrywise nonnegative. The composite parameters are
\[
 P=M_{m-1}\cdots M_0,
 \qquad
 q=d_{m-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}M_{m-1}\cdots M_{j+1}d_j.
\]
Thus it is enough that this composite has $\rho(P)<1$ and its fixed point lies
strictly in $\B$; no individual $M_i$ must be stable. For example,
\[
 M_0=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&1/5\end{pmatrix},\quad
 M_1=\begin{pmatrix}1/5&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\quad
 d_0=d_1=\binom{1/20}{1/20}
\]
give $P=(2/5)I$, $q=(3/50,3/20)^T$, and
$z_*=(1/10,1/4)^T\in\B$, although each phase matrix has spectral radius two.
This is an arithmetic model, not a SAT transformation.

The quantifiers are important. Under $P=NP$ one first chooses one finite
achievable pair. The return hypothesis then supplies finitely many further
uniform-machine witnesses, possibly different at every stage. It does not
require a universal procedure that accepts arbitrary source code and emits
the next machine. Williams's staged Circuit-SAT self-improvement gives a
concrete precedent for finite algorithm-to-algorithm bootstrapping, but its
theorems do not establish the return hypothesis for ordinary SAT in the
$P=NP$ regime; the paper lists extension to that regime as open
\cite{williams2024}.

This theorem also does not cover a single solver that performs
$J(N)\to\infty$ transformations as input length grows. That is a growing-depth
uniform algorithm and still needs an effective schedule, uniform error rates,
and charged access as in PRF3-0018. In contrast, the number of returns above
is a finite constant after the starting exponent pair has been fixed. A
strictly decreasing sequence alone remains insufficient: the values
$\phi+2^{-r}$ never enter $\B$.

Common Lyapunov functions for arbitrarily switched linear systems provide a
useful control-theory analogy, including the fact that polynomial Lyapunov
degree need not be uniformly bounded \cite{mason2004}. This is not a theorem
transfer: arbitrary physical switching is a different quantifier from the
existential choice of uniform SAT witnesses, and it supplies no SAT-specific
successor relation.

\subsection{Cofinal seed hitting: a weaker sufficient hypothesis}
The global closure hypotheses above are stronger than the contradiction
argument needs.  Upward closure lets one replace an unknown achievable point
by one member of any componentwise cofinal family, after which only one finite
route from that fixed seed is required.

\begin{definition}[Cofinal seed family]
A set $H\subseteq\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2$ is componentwise cofinal if, for every
finite $z\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^2$, some $h\in H$ satisfies $z\le h$.  A finite
$H$-route from $h$ is a list
\[
 h=z^{(0)},z^{(1)},\ldots,z^{(m)}
\]
such that every $z^{(i)}\in\R$ and $z^{(m)}\in\B$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[COFINAL-SEED-HITTING1]\label{thm:cofinal-seed-hitting}
Assume the endpoint theorem applies in the fixed model.  If there is a
componentwise cofinal set $H$ such that every $h\in H\cap\R$ has a finite
$H$-route into $\B$, then $P\ne NP$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Suppose instead that $P=NP$.  Then some finite $z\in\R$.  Choose $h\in H$
with $z\le h$.  Upward closure gives $h\in\R$, so the premise supplies a
finite route whose last point belongs to $\R\cap\B$, contradicting the
endpoint theorem.
\end{proof}

In particular, it suffices to use
\[
 H=\{(K,K):K\in S\}
\]
for any unbounded $S\subseteq\mathbb N$, including the powers of two.  The
route and its length may depend on the fixed cap $K$; no common affine map,
common contraction factor, or effective source-code compiler is required.
Every point on the selected route must still be witnessed by one uniform exact
SAT machine with both resource coordinates.  Thus the new load-bearing
hypothesis is the existence of such SAT-specific routes for a cofinal seed
family.  That hypothesis is \textsc{open}.

The weakening is strict at the level of arithmetic models.  For powers of two
$K$, start at $(K,K)$ and repeatedly multiply both coordinates by
$\lambda_K=(K-1)/K$.  Each fixed $K$ reaches $t+s<1$, hence enters $\B$, after
finitely many steps, although $\sup_K\lambda_K=1$ and the route lengths are
unbounded over $K$.  Exact-rational checks for $K=2,4,\ldots,256$ give route
lengths $3,8,21,54,131,309,708,1594$.  This is not a SAT construction.

There is also no growing-depth promotion.  Under $P=NP$, first one finite
achievable point is fixed, then one fixed $K$ dominating it is chosen, and
finally one fixed finite route is selected.  A prescription $K=K(N)$ or a
route depth tending to infinity with the input length would instead require a
single uniform scheduling and error-control argument and is outside
Theorem~\ref{thm:cofinal-seed-hitting}.

\section{Errors, finite iteration, and uniformity}
Exponent coordinates are constants, whereas $o(1)$ is a function of input
length. Expressions such as $z'\le Mz+d+o(1)$ must therefore be interpreted
through actual machine resource bounds, or replaced by precise constant
slack statements. They are not inequalities between fixed points and an
unidentified error term.

For example, a scalar guarantee with a fixed additive error $\eta$ yields
limiting envelope $(c+\eta)/(1-\lambda)$. It is sufficient that
\[
 0\le\eta<(1-\lambda)\phi-c.
\]
If the theorem permits arbitrarily small positive exponent slack, select
one satisfying this strict inequality. After a finite number of stages,
finitely many $o(1)$ bounds can be absorbed into a common eventual threshold.

A finite chain of existential implications does not require a computable
selector that outputs the next machine from the previous machine. Each
selected witness must nevertheless be a uniform finite algorithm, and its
existence must be proved without assuming an uncomputable runtime oracle
inside that algorithm. Finite logical selection is different from an
unbounded input-length-dependent family. If the method instead requires
$J(N)\to\infty$ transformations inside one solver, it must establish a
uniform scheduling and effective error-control theorem such as the
conditional machinery in PRF3-0018 \cite{prf30018}.

\subsection{A same-realization two-stage target}
One recovered guard is now expressible as a precise sufficient inequality.
Suppose a model-matched first phase maps an achievable time exponent $t$ to
one joint witness
\[
 (t_1,s_1)\le(qt,\alpha t),\qquad q\ge1,\quad\alpha\ge0,
\]
and a second SAT-specific phase maps that same witness to
\[
 (t_2,s_2)\le(a+bt_1+cs_1,\ d+s_1),
 \qquad a,b,c,d\ge0.
\]
Both displays are hypotheses about complete algorithms, including conversion
and access costs.  Let $\theta=bq+c\alpha$.

\begin{theorem}[ARC-JOINT-TWO-STAGE1]
If both phases exist for every point encountered, $\theta<1$, and
\[
 \frac{(1+\alpha)a}{1-bq-c\alpha}+d<\phi,
 \tag{J}
\]
then finitely many complete returns enter the forbidden region.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The return envelope is
\[
 t_{r+1}=a+\theta t_r,\qquad s_{r+1}=d+\alpha t_r.
\]
It converges to
\[
 t_*=\frac{a}{1-\theta},\qquad s_*=d+\alpha t_*.
\]
Condition (J) says $t_*+s_*<\phi$.  Strictness gives finite
crossing, and componentwise domination transfers the envelope bound to the
actual witnesses.
\end{proof}
A space simulation by itself leaves the time coordinate unchanged and cannot
erase an arbitrary starting exponent.  The theorem therefore identifies a
genuine two-phase target: one phase must improve time, and all space inherited
by the second phase must belong to the same realization.  An exact-arithmetic
audit performed 1,602 checks over 108 parameter cases; it validates the
recurrence algebra only.  Neither phase is currently known for general SAT.
The complete audit is recorded in
\url{https://github.com/j15marti/pnp/blob/main/research/agents/lead/2026-10-07_162204_LEAD_LEAD-0031.tex},
and the polynomial-regime bridge is developed in \texttt{main6.tex}.

\section{What WRAPPER mechanisms could supply}
A concrete construction proving an achievable resource map can establish
ARC. It need not act on every syntactic representative of a resource point.
However, it must establish the resulting machine's exact SAT correctness and
worst-case joint bounds. The following candidate mechanisms are proposals,
not additional proved closures of $\R$.
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{Forward-only plus recursive wrappers.} Construct justified
children before determining which child is satisfiable; charge all child
construction, solving, combination, and state retention. Deferring answers
removes one possible circular dependency, not the quantitative burden.
\item[] \textbf{Parallel portfolios.} A fixed finite portfolio can exploit
its fastest correct member under time-sharing with bounded time overhead.
A favorable inputwise coverage theorem and aggregate space bound are still
necessary. Large input-dependent portfolios require their own scheduling,
discovery, and validation charges.
\item[] \textbf{Disjunctive and backdoor decompositions.} Exact OR identities
are useful, but UNSAT may require all children. Backdoor discovery and all
residual solving must be counted.
\item[] \textbf{Merged histories.} Common SAT answers, a shared terminal
node, prime labels, and compact route descriptions do not themselves imply
small encoded residual instances or cheap semantic merges.
\end{description}
The latest compiler audit records these distinctions and the unresolved
pre-contraction reduction requirement \cite{lead0027}.

\section{Open obligations and hostile controls}
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{ARC-O1: SAT-specific closure.} Prove existence of improved
uniform witnesses; arithmetic convergence does not create them.
\item[] \textbf{ARC-O2: strict quantitative margin.} Prove fixed constants
and a limiting envelope strictly inside the forbidden region. A fixed point
on its boundary is insufficient.
\item[] \textbf{ARC-O3: same realization.} Attach both time and space bounds
to the same algorithm at every stage.
\item[] \textbf{ARC-O4: full costs.} Include preprocessing, code generation
when performed at runtime, SAT queries, recomputation, combination,
representation lengths, and peak live workspace.
\item[] \textbf{ARC-O5: scope and iterability.} Establish correctness on all
inputs in scope and ensure successor witnesses remain in the domain of the
improvement implication until crossing.
\item[] \textbf{ARC-O6: model compatibility.} Charge changes of machine,
encoding, or SAT variant before comparing to the endpoint.
\end{description}
A strictly decreasing sequence is insufficient without a finite-crossing
argument: $v_r=\phi+2^{-r}$ never crosses. Similarly, removing
$r=O(\log n)$ variables by ordinary exhaustive branching keeps polynomially
many children but leaves $n-O(\log n)$ variables. This does not give a fixed
power reduction $n^b$ with $b<1$, and variable count alone is not encoded
formula length.

The aggregate compression identity from PRF2-0145 is
\[
 D_r=\frac{2^k}{\prod_iG_i}=2^{k-x},\qquad
 x=\sum_i\log_2G_i,\qquad
 D_r\le N^d\Rightarrow x\ge k-d\log_2N.
\]
It is exact accounting for a specified count \cite{prf20145}. To constrain
$\R$, a further theorem must connect that count to unavoidable machine
resources. Merely counting assignments does not provide that theorem.

\section{Final-link refinement: charged max-affine return maps}

The fixed $(a,b)=(1/3,2/3)$ WRAPPER cell is sufficient but not necessary for
the ARC spine.  The weakest reusable target exposed by the final-link audit is
a charged successor envelope.  Suppose a fixed finite compiler maps an
achievable pair $(t,s)$ to another achievable pair satisfying
\begin{align}
 t'&\le \max\{r,\max_{j\in J}(w_j+\lambda_jt)\},\label{eq:final-link-time}\\
 s'&\le \max\{p,\max_{j\in J}\lambda_js\},\label{eq:final-link-space}
\end{align}
where $J$ is finite and every $0\le\lambda_j<1$.  All discovery, failed
portfolio attempts, fanout, child generation and access, replay, source
rereads, combination, and retained state must occur on the same realization
and be included in $r,p,w_j$.

\begin{theorem}[Charged max-affine ARC return]
Define
\[
 C_*:=\max\left\{r,\max_{j\in J}\frac{w_j}{1-\lambda_j}\right\},
 \qquad \lambda_{\max}:=\max_j\lambda_j.
\]
Repeated finite returns satisfy
\[
 t_m\le C_*+\lambda_{\max}^m(t_0-C_*)_+,
 \qquad
 s_m\le p+\lambda_{\max}^m(s_0-p)_+.
\]
Consequently, if $C_*+p<\phi$, every finite starting pair enters the
valid product-forbidden region after finitely many returns.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every channel, $w_j+\lambda_jC_*\le C_*$ and $r\le C_*$.  Thus $C_*$ is
a common pre-fixed point of the time map.  The map is monotone and
$\lambda_{\max}$-Lipschitz above that point, giving the first displayed
bound by iteration.  The identical argument for space gives the second.  A
finite return count makes the two transient terms smaller than the strict
margin $\phi-(C_*+p)$.  Finite composition preserves uniformity.
\end{proof}

For one channel with fanout exponent $a$, child scale $b<1$, interface
multiplicity $\beta$, access or replay exponent $g$, time floor $r$, and live
space floor $p$, the acceptance condition is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \max\left\{r,\frac{a+\beta+g}{1-b}\right\}+p<\phi.}
 \label{eq:final-link-cell}
\end{equation}
Ordinary explicit children normally force $r\ge a+b$ for output traffic and
$p\ge b$ for one live child.  The $(1/3,2/3)$ cell with linear construction
does not satisfy the golden-ratio product inequality, since $5/3>\phi$.
It is nevertheless a valid conditional endpoint point by Fortnow because its
closed algorithm has time exponent exactly one and space exponent below one.
The $(1/2,1/2)$ cell and sufficiently cheap nearby cells fit the product
branch.  A virtual child may avoid the explicit
$p\ge b$ floor only after a bit-level decoder, every access cost, retained
parent state, and endpoint-model simulation are proved and charged.

\]

\[

\]

 \beta+g<\frac{\phi-3/2}{2}=0.0590169943\ldots.
\]
The conditional choice $\beta=1/20$, $g=0$ gives

three residual clause sets pairwise distinct and incomparable, but its product
splits into constant-size components and is directly easy.  Thus neither tree
count is a machine lower bound.  The theorem retires only the inference from

falsify the combined hybrid or any semantic quotient.

\section{FINAL-LINK2: typed epochs, layout accounting, and generator equivalence}

The second final-link pass preserves the max-affine ARC deduction but
changes the most useful formulation of its missing premise.  A successful
step need not be a source-only restriction cover at every raw operation.  It
may be a finite charged epoch containing direct VALUE exits, typed recursive
exits, and same-scale administrative transitions.  After the entire epoch is
collapsed, however, its least time and space budgets must be computed on one
realization, and every feasible recurrent macro-cycle must contract.

For a finite type set, let an edge $e:i\to j$ have physical child scale
$\theta_e\in[0,1]$, fanout exponent $f_e$, one-time per-child
generation/acquisition exponent $g_e$, per-unit recursive simulation/access
overhead $u_e$, recursive time weight $w_e=f_e+u_e$, and retained-parent-state
exponent $h_e$.  Let $R_i$ and $P_i$ contain all nonrecursive time and
live-space charges of type $i$.  In particular, $R_i$ contains $f_e+g_e$ in
addition to shared discovery, failed trials, VALUE work, and combination;
$P_i$ contains either an explicit child buffer of exponent $\theta_e$ or the
state of its virtual decoder/generator.  The exact Bellman ledger is
\begin{align}
 C_i&\ge R_i,& C_i&\ge w_e+\theta_e C_j,\label{eq:fl2-bellman-time}\\
 \rho_i&\ge P_i,& \rho_i&\ge h_e,&
 \rho_i&\ge\theta_e\rho_j.\label{eq:fl2-bellman-space}
\end{align}
Source rereads and one-time replay belong in $R_i$; repeated decoder or replay
work belongs in $u_e$.  A parent-scale VALUE query is a scale-one solver edge,
not a local constant.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-depth typed seed elimination]
Suppose one fixed deterministic uniform constructor gives every CNF a
source-checkable finite epoch whose terminal channels are correct VALUE exits
or typed recursive exits.  The type set is fixed and finite, every child is
an ordinary CNF or a charged random-access view of one, and the same joint
time/space implementation realizes all parameters above.  Let $I_0$ be the
fixed set of possible entry types, with root classification included in the
root floors.

At outer depth zero run fixed polynomial seed solvers with exponents
$K_i,L_i$.  At depth $d+1$ apply one epoch and then the depth-$d$ solver to
each recursive exit.  Thus the exponent budgets obey the finite recurrences
\begin{align*}
 C_i^{(0)}&=K_i,&
 C_i^{(d+1)}&=\max\{R_i,\max_{e:i\to j}(w_e+\theta_eC_j^{(d)})\},\\
 \rho_i^{(0)}&=L_i,&
 \rho_i^{(d+1)}&=\max\{P_i,\max_{e:i\to j}h_e,
                         \max_{e:i\to j}\theta_e\rho_j^{(d)}\}.
\end{align*}
Refine the types until history-dependent feasibility is represented.  If
every root-reachable directed cycle of this graph has scale product below
one, put
\[
 q_d=\max_{\substack{|P|=d\\\operatorname{start}(P)\in I_0}}
       \prod_{e\in P}\theta_e.
\]
Set $q_d=0$ if there is no such length-$d$ path.
Then $q_d\to0$, and the finite-depth budgets converge to the least Bellman
budgets $C_i^*,\rho_i^*$ in
\eqref{eq:fl2-bellman-time}--\eqref{eq:fl2-bellman-space}.  In particular,
if
\[
 \max_{i\in I_0}C_i^*+\max_{i\in I_0}\rho_i^*<\phi,
 \tag{TE}
\]
one fixed hardwired depth gives a uniform exact SAT algorithm in the valid
product-forbidden region.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the finite recurrences along typed paths gives the Bellman path
formulas plus inherited boundary terms multiplied by the path-scale product.
Because the graph is finite and every reachable cycle contracts, a constant
number of edges contains either no cycle or a cycle with product at most one
fixed $\lambda<1$; hence $q_d$ decays geometrically.  With
$K=\max_iK_i$ and $L=\max_iL_i$, the root budgets are bounded by
$\max_iC_i^*+q_dK$ and
$\max\{\max_i\rho_i^*,q_dL\}$, up to the finitely many common $o(1)$ terms.
The strict margin in (TE) therefore holds at some fixed depth.  That finite
composition is uniform and terminates at the total seed solvers.
\end{proof}

This is \textsc{Proved/Exact} conditional composition.  It replaces the
overstrong claim that Bellman feasibility alone makes an unbounded recursive
machine halt: a contracting self-edge can still loop forever at input length
one if no base rule is given.  The all-input epoch constructor remains
\textsc{Open}.  Same-scale raw work must be collapsed into one charged epoch
by a proved source-checkable potential, or its finite control state must be
part of the type.  Average-over-input amortization is insufficient.

For a numerical check of the theorem rather than a SAT construction, take
two types with one edge in each direction, and put
\[
 R_A=R_B=1,\qquad P_A=P_B=h_e=\tfrac12,\qquad
 w_e=\tfrac14,\qquad\theta_e=\tfrac12.
\]
The only directed cycle has scale product $1/4$, the least budgets are
$C_A^*=C_B^*=1$ and $\rho_A^*=\rho_B^*=1/2$, and their sum is
$3/2<\phi$.  Thus every finite polynomial seed is eliminated at some fixed
hardwired depth.  This arithmetic example lies in the valid product region;
it does not supply the open all-input epoch constructor.

\subsection{A block-layout gate at the aggregate-linear boundary}

The equality $qL=M^{1+o(1)}$ shows that source size alone does not obstruct a
streamed cover.  It does expose a scheduling distinction.  Let
\[
 q=M^{a+o(1)},\qquad L=M^{b+o(1)},\qquad
 B=M^{\delta+o(1)}
\]
with $a\ge0$, $0\le b<1$, and $0\le\delta\le a$, where $B$ children are
buffered together.  In the optimistic block-transpose schedule, blocks are
sequential, the source is read-only random access and scanned once per block,
and routing inside the selected block is output-sensitive.  Assume no live
state beyond the buffer and active recursive call, and recursive closure of
this same schedule at every child scale.  The fully charged recurrence is
\[
 C\ge\max\{1+a-\delta,a+b,a+bC\},\qquad
 S\ge\max\{b+\delta,bS\}.
\]
Its least exponent ledger is
\[
 C(\delta)=\max\left\{1+a-\delta,\frac{a}{1-b}\right\},
 \qquad S(\delta)=b+\delta.
\]

\begin{theorem}[BATCH-LAYOUT-GATE1]
For the preceding parameter range,
\[
 \min_\delta(C(\delta)+S(\delta))=
 \begin{cases}
  1+a+b,&b\le 1/(1+a),\\
  b+a/(1-b),&b>1/(1+a).
 \end{cases}
\]
In particular, $a+b=1$ with $b<1$ implies
\[
 \min_\delta(C(\delta)+S(\delta))=2>
 \phi.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $A=1+a$ and $D=a/(1-b)$.  The objective is
$\max\{A-\delta,D\}+\delta+b$.  It is the constant $A+b$ while
$A-\delta\ge D$ and is $D+\delta+b$ afterward.  If $D>A$, its minimum is
$D+b$ at $\delta=0$; if $D\le A$, its minimum is $A+b$ (the formulas agree
when $D=A$).  The inequality
$D>A$ is equivalent to $b>1/(1+a)$.  At equality both branches agree and the
statement places the boundary in the first branch.  The omitted $a+b$ floor
is redundant: the scan floor dominates it when $a+b\le1$, while
$a/(1-b)\ge a+b$ when $a+b\ge1$.  On $a+b=1$ with $b<1$, the first branch
applies and equals two.
\end{proof}

This is an exact obstruction only for the declared source-major block
schedule.  A child-major online generator with one source pass would instead
have the optimistic ledger
\[
 C=\max\{1,a/(1-b)\},\qquad S=b,
\]
which gives $C+S=1+b$ when $a+b=1$.  Thus the theorem does not rule out the
desired cell.  It proves that aggregate-linear output volume alone is not
enough: the missing construction must provide child-major semantic routing,
a stronger shared representation, or a different typed epoch.

\subsection{One-copy OR compilation and its correction}

Fix one assumed polynomial SAT decider $A$ with certified time exponent $K$.
For $q$ explicit $m$-bit CNFs $G_1,\ldots,G_q$, a selector multiplexer and one
unrolled circuit for $A$ give an ordinary Tseitin CNF $Q$ satisfying
\[
 \SAT(Q)=\bigvee_{i=1}^q\SAT(G_i),\qquad
 |Q|=m^{K+o(1)}+O(qm)\operatorname{polylog}(qm).
\]
This OR-MUX identity is \textsc{Proved/Exact}; circuitization of a fixed
time-$T$ computation has $T\operatorname{polylog}T$ size in the relevant
upper-bound construction \cite{pippengerfischer1979}.  It replaces $q$
copies of the solver circuit by one.  At the equality-volume boundary
$qm=M^{1+o(1)}$, however, the returned CNF is still parent-scale.
Moreover, for explicit input CNFs, a direct guarded OR construction has
total length $\widetilde O(qm)$ without invoking $A$.  Thus OR-MUX is an
exact identity but supplies no favorable size exponent here.

Suppose instead that a source-specific circuit $E_F(z)$ of size
$M^{h+o(1)}$ outputs an entire legal $M^{b+o(1)}$-bit child and satisfies
\[
 \SAT(F)\iff\exists z\;\SAT(E_F(z)).\tag{GC}
\]
The initial fixed-machine analysis composed $E_F$ with $A$.  A red-team
correction shows that this gives no machine-specific advantage.

\begin{lemma}[CANONICAL-VERIFY1]
Fix a canonical padded CNF encoding with $O(\log m)$-bit literal records.
There is a uniform bounded-fan-in circuit $V_m(x,y)$ of description length
$\widetilde O(m)$ such that
\[
 \exists y\;V_m(x,y)=1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 x\text{ is a well-formed satisfiable CNF encoding}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Guess one truth value per active literal occurrence and evaluate clauses in
their original order.  In parallel, send the records
$(\mathrm{active},\mathrm{identifier},\mathrm{value})$ through a uniform
Batcher sorting network and reject adjacent active records with equal
identifiers and unequal values.  There are $O(m\log^2m)$ compare-exchanges,
and each $O(\log m)$-bit compare/swap has polylogarithmic circuit size
\cite{batcher1968sorting}.  The parser rejects malformed headers, delimiters,
identifiers, and padding.  Consistency plus clause acceptance is equivalent
to one assignment satisfying the decoded formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[GENERATOR-SHRINKER-EQUIVALENCE1]
Let a deterministic uniform constructor emit, for every $M$-bit CNF $F$, a
closed topologically encoded circuit $E_F$ over a fixed finite basis.  Its
only free inputs are selector bits.  Its total bit description includes every
input and output terminal, gate type, constant, wire endpoint and embedded
source bit; no source pointer, implicit bus, ROM, or external object is
available outside that description.  Assume
\[
 e(F):=L(E_F)\le M^{h+o(1)},\qquad 0<h<1.
\]
Interpret its $m$ output bits only through CANONICAL-VERIFY1, so malformed or
unused selector outputs are rejected, and assume
\[
 \SAT(F)\iff\exists z,y\;V_m(E_F(z),y)=1.\tag{GC$'$}
\]
If the constructor uses time $M^{r_E+o(1)}$ and space
$M^{p_E+o(1)}$, with every subcall and failed route charged, then it emits an
ordinary equivalent 3-CNF $Q_F$ satisfying
\[
 L(Q_F)\le M^{h+o(1)},\quad
 T\le M^{\max\{r_E,h\}+o(1)},\quad
 S\le M^{\max\{p_E,h\}+o(1)}.
\]
Conversely, a charged ordinary shrinker yields such a constant-output
generator with the same exponents up to polylogarithmic factors.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every selector terminal and output terminal is explicit, so their counts and
in particular $m$ are at most $e(F)$.  Compose $E_F$ with
$V_m$, constrain the verifier output to one, and introduce full
biconditional Tseitin clauses for every gate.  For each $(z,y)$ an extension
to gate variables exists exactly when the composed circuit accepts.  By
(GC$'$), $Q_F$ is satisfiable exactly when $F$ is.  CANONICAL-VERIFY1 and the
Tseitin expansion have length $\widetilde O(e(F))$, giving the stated size
and construction time.  Retaining the materialized successor in writable
storage gives the stated space maximum.  For the converse, topologically
encode a constant-output circuit whose output is the already constructed
successor.
\end{proof}

This \textsc{Proved/Exact} theorem eliminates both the quadratic verifier and
any former auxiliary inequality relating child width to generator size.
Under a fully closed explicit encoding,
compact generated covers and ordinary shrinkers are equivalent up to
polylogarithmic factors.  A source pointer, ROM lookup, compressed bus,
external shared object, or random oracle is not a closed generator unless its
expansion and every access are charged.
OR-MUX remains useful for sharing explicit evaluations, but it does not
convert the equality-volume cover into a contraction.

\subsection{Current ARC status}

The exact recurrence recovered from PRF2-0155 and RECURCLOSE1 still removes
the unknown starting exponents once a strict recursively closed shrinker
exists.  For a map with output scale $h<1$, root construction exponent $r$,
and live-space exponent $p$, fixed-depth reapplication of a seed
$(K,L)$ gives
\[
 t_d\le\max\{r,Kh^d\},\qquad
 s_d\le\max\{p,h,Lh^d\}.
\]
At $(h,r,p)=(2/3,1,2/3)$, a fixed $d$ yields the conditional point
$(1,2/3)$.  This point is not in the golden-ratio product region, since
$5/3>\phi$; it is forbidden instead by Fortnow's theorem, for example with
the fixed choice $\varepsilon=1/4$.  This is exactly where the original
``divide the $2^K$ contribution by repeated $2^x$ shrinkage'' reasoning
belongs.  It eliminates the leaf exponent after contraction has been
acquired; it does not construct or cheaply acquire the first contraction.

The executable audits add 9,598 typed-ledger checks, 1,020 red-team checks,
709 exact shared-trie residual recoveries, 8,064 semantic-antichain checks,
5,904 OR-multiplexer identity checks, 372 tableau calculations, and 12,443
layout-grid checks, all with zero reported semantic or arithmetic errors.
These are \textsc{Computational} checks of the stated finite models.  They do
not establish the universal epoch or shrinker.

The present ARC conclusion is unchanged: no recovered result proves the
all-input charged successor, and P versus NP remains \textsc{Open}.  The
advance is a sharper dichotomy.  Source-major block scheduling is outside the
product endpoint at equality volume, while any compact exact legal-child
generator satisfying the closed explicit accounting above already entails an
ordinary shrinker of the same exponent up to polylogarithmic factors.  Any
genuinely new $A$-dependent route must therefore derive a favorable epoch
from the code of
one selected $A$ without simply assuming a sublinear exact existential
circuit for the source.

The strongest current proof arrow is therefore
\[
\begin{gathered}
 P=NP
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\substack{
   \text{\textsc{Open}: an all-input charged shrinker or typed epoch}\\
   \text{with strict endpoint-compatible parameters}}}\\
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{\textsc{Proved/Exact}: fixed-depth seed elimination}}
 \Longrightarrow
 \boxed{\text{one clause of (END) holds for }(t,s)}\\
 \Longrightarrow\bot.
\end{gathered}
\]
The first boxed implication is the only SAT-specific construction gap in
this arrow; neither the generator equivalence nor the computational audits
establish it.
