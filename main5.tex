\begin{abstract}
This manuscript records a conditional route from a hypothetical polynomial-time SAT algorithm to a forbidden joint time-space bound. It separates an exact recursive composition argument from the unproved existence of a sufficiently cheap source-dependent SAT transformation. No separation of P and NP is claimed. Prime labels, reconvergence, disjunctions, portfolios, and backdoors are candidate implementation mechanisms, not substitutes for the missing transformation theorem.
\end{abstract}
\section{Status and assumptions}
The computational model, input encoding, and charge for random access must be fixed before applying a lower bound. Let $N$ denote encoded input length, not variable count. Assume $P=NP$, and fix one uniform deterministic SAT decider $A$ with $T_A(N)\le N^{K+o(1)}$ and $S_A(N)\le N^{L+o(1)}$, where $K,L$ are finite constants. Time and space bounds below always belong to the same algorithm.

The endpoint used here is the model-matched time-space product obstruction from Williams: polynomially bounded SAT time and space cannot have product $O(N^{\gamma-\varepsilon})$ for a fixed positive $\varepsilon$, where $\gamma=2\cos(\pi/7)$ \cite{williams2010}. Consequently, producing exponents $C,\sigma$ with $C+\sigma<\gamma$ would contradict the assumption. This is not a lower bound against arbitrary polynomial time with arbitrary polynomial space.

\section{The missing inner interface}
\begin{definition}[Charged recursive interface]
A procedure $R_A$ on a formula $F$ of length $M$ generates standard SAT instances $G_1,\ldots,G_q$ and an exact combination procedure, such that
\[
\SAT(F)=\operatorname{Combine}_F(\SAT(G_1),\ldots,\SAT(G_q)).
\]
For fixed $a\ge0$, $0<b<1$, and $c\ge0$, require $q\le M^{a+o(1)}$, $|G_i|\le M^{b+o(1)}$, and total local work $M^{c+o(1)}$. Local work includes discovery, certificates, child generation, all combination operations, and any rereading or regeneration not separately charged elsewhere. The guarantee holds uniformly for every input and remains valid on every child.
\end{definition}
This is a contract, not a construction. The exact combiner may be a disjunction, but must be evaluable in the claimed time and space. A general Boolean circuit on child answers cannot simply be assumed to admit streaming evaluation with negligible storage.

The procedure may depend on the fixed code of $A$. A call to $A$ on a query of length $M^{\delta+o(1)}$ costs $M^{\delta K+o(1)}$ before multiplicity. In particular, a current-scale call retains the unknown exponent $K$. Any such call must be charged honestly; renaming it as a route-selection or certificate operation changes no cost \cite{lead0027}. The bounds $a,b,c$ used for exponent elimination must not hide uncontrolled dependence on $K$.

\section{Composition algebra}
First use idealized exact powers. With depth $d$, the leaf count has exponent
\[
A_d=a\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}b^i=\frac{a(1-b^d)}{1-b}.
\]
The hypothetical decider at the leaves contributes exponent $A_d+Kb^d$. Local work at level $j<d$ has exponent
\[
E_j=\frac{a(1-b^j)}{1-b}+cb^j.
\]
Writing $g=a/(1-b)$ and $C=\max\{c,g\}$, every $E_j\le C$, and $A_d+Kb^d\le C+Kb^d$.
\begin{lemma}[Fixed-depth composition]
For each fixed integer $d\ge1$, the charged interface yields time
\[
T_d(N)\le N^{\max\{\max_{0\le j<d}E_j,A_d+Kb^d\}+o(1)}.
\]
For a sequential explicit-child implementation with local workspace $M^{s+o(1)}$, retained parent-state and resumable sequential generation costs included, and a combiner obeying that workspace contract,
\[
S_d(N)\le N^{\max\{s,b,Lb^d\}+o(1)}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At level $j$, multiply the number of calls by each call's local work. At depth $d$, multiply the leaf count by the leaf running time. A sum of finitely many terms has the largest exponent. For space, process one child at a time. Stored child instances have sizes bounded by $N^{b},N^{b^2},\ldots$; the input at the root is read-only. Local states have exponents at most $s$, and the leaf decider uses exponent $Lb^d$. A fixed-depth sum has the largest exponent. The $o(1)$ errors remain $o(1)$ after a fixed number of compositions. If processing the combiner requires storing all child answers, that storage must already fit $s$; otherwise the stated space bound is unavailable.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional fixed-depth contradiction]
Suppose the charged interface and explicit-child implementation exist under $P=NP$, and
\[
\Delta=\gamma-\left(\max\{c,a/(1-b)\}+\max\{s,b\}\right)>0.
\]
Then $P\ne NP$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Under $P=NP$, choose $A$ and its finite $K,L$. Choose a fixed finite $d$ so that $(K+L)b^d<\Delta/2$. The preceding lemma gives a single uniform algorithm with joint exponent sum at most $C+\max\{s,b\}+(K+L)b^d+o(1)<\gamma$ for sufficiently large inputs. Finitely many smaller inputs may be handled by a fixed finite table without changing asymptotic exponents. Apply the model-matched endpoint. The contradiction discharges $P=NP$, provided the hypothesized interface has actually been proved.
\end{proof}
This finite-depth theorem is enough for a strict endpoint margin. The integer $d$ need not be computable from an arbitrary supplied solver: its mathematical existence allows it to be hardcoded in one finite algorithm. This observation does not supply $R_A$.

\section{Growing depth and regenerated inputs}
A stronger asymptotic statement aims for exactly $N^{C+o(1)}$ time, removing the leaf exponent rather than merely making it smaller than a fixed margin. The rate-controlled growing-depth composition of PRF3-0018 addresses this under explicit hypotheses \cite{prf30018}. One must choose an effective schedule $J(N)\to\infty$, control all errors along the levels, and keep the smallest scales above the thresholds at which each promised estimate is valid. A pointwise $o(1)$ at each fixed depth does not automatically yield a uniform $o(1)$ at growing depth.

Regenerating a child instead of storing it may reduce workspace. It does not make child access free. If a child algorithm makes $Q$ bit requests and each request triggers a cost $R$, their product must be charged; nested virtual input interfaces can multiply these costs again. The claim $S=N^{o(1)}$ requires a proved small-live-state implementation and a charged time bound for all such accesses. The explicit-child alternative often needs fewer access assumptions and can still suffice because a positive space exponent is allowed by the product endpoint.

\section{Architecture alternatives}
\subsection{Double wrapper}
The forward-only inner layer emits justified alternatives before knowing their SAT answers. The outer layer resolves these answers recursively. This removes an architectural dependence on choosing the successful route first. It leaves open the cost of enumerating alternatives, their encoded sizes, and the exact combination rule. SAT-correctness is a separate proof from resource improvement.

\subsection{Disjunction and charged backdoors}
A decomposition $\SAT(F)=\bigvee_i\SAT(G_i)$ gives a streaming OR combiner. SAT instances may terminate early, whereas UNSAT instances require all children in the worst case. A backdoor of size $h$ may induce $2^h$ branches; finding the backdoor and solving residual instances must also be charged. An existence theorem for a small backdoor without cheap acquisition does not meet the inner contract. Nor does a small backdoor theorem for a restricted class establish coverage of arbitrary SAT.

\subsection{Parallel portfolios}
A fixed finite portfolio of exact solvers can be time-shared to exploit its fastest member with a constant time overhead, assuming ordinary suspendable simulations. The simulator must count all retained states. A slow competitor may consume too much memory before the winner finishes. Memory caps or staged restarts therefore require their own analysis. For a growing portfolio, scheduling and description costs are no longer automatically constant. The required coverage statement is that every input has a favorable member, with uniform correctness and a joint resource guarantee. Testing different winners on different instances is not a coverage proof.

\subsection{Prime lineage, hashes, and merging}
In Boolean semantics, TRUE and NOT FALSE agree, as do FALSE and NOT TRUE. Assigning different primes preserves syntactic provenance, not four distinct Boolean truth values. Any prime product is charged by bit length and arithmetic cost. An ordinary hash needs collision handling for exact correctness. A common terminal node or equal SAT bits does not justify identifying histories under future operations. A merge needs a semantic congruence appropriate to the remaining computation. Compact macro-route indices can save lineage space after contraction; they do not establish contraction. A finite DAG contains no directed cycles: recursion must be described by a well-founded call graph or an unfolded DAG, not a literal cyclic DAG.

\section{Where aggregate compression belongs}
For an explicitly defined quantity $D_0=2^k$, exact reduction factors $G_i$ give
\[
D_r=\frac{2^k}{\prod_iG_i}=2^{k-x},\qquad x=\sum_i\log_2G_i.
\]
Thus $D_r\le N^d$ implies $x\ge k-d\log_2N$ \cite{prf20145}. This is exact bookkeeping. To use it in this manuscript, one must connect $D_r$ to actual encoded children or charged computation. Raw assignments do not by themselves force enumeration. Branching into $2^x$ subproblems each of nominal size $2^{k-x}$ preserves a total of $2^k$ possibilities rather than providing a net saving.

A restricted hostile control is useful: a formula-independent family of $q$ partial assignments, each leaving at most $\ell$ variables unassigned, must satisfy $q2^\ell\ge2^n$ if it must cover every possible unique satisfying assignment. This rules out that specific oblivious construction. It does not exclude source-dependent semantic reductions or arbitrary algorithms \cite{lead0027}.

\section{Open obligations and proof spine}
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{W1: semantic reduction.} Prove exactness of the candidate inner operation on the entire claimed domain.
\item[] \textbf{W2: encoded shrinkage.} Prove a fixed exponent $b<1$ for actual standard SAT children, including every source reference needed to interpret them.
\item[] \textbf{W3: full construction charge.} Bound discovery, certification, generation, and combination before any hidden expensive semantic query.
\item[] \textbf{W4: total branching.} Bound all necessary alternatives, including adversarial UNSAT inputs.
\item[] \textbf{W5: recursive closure.} Prove that every produced child lies in a domain with the same contract.
\item[] \textbf{W6: joint resources.} Give one implementation with charged input access, live state, answer storage, and any portfolio scheduling.
\item[] \textbf{W7: endpoint compatibility.} Match the machine model and encoding and preserve a strict numerical margin.
\end{description}
The proof spine is
\[
\begin{aligned}
P=NP&\Rightarrow \text{one polynomial SAT decider }A\\
&\xRightarrow{\text{OPEN: W1--W6}}\text{favorable recursive SAT interface}\\
&\xRightarrow{\text{conditional composition}}\text{one algorithm with improved joint bounds}\\
&\xRightarrow{\text{W7 and strict margin}}\text{contradiction to the endpoint}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Until the open interface is proved, the valid logical statement is $(P=NP)\land H\Rightarrow\bot$, where $H$ denotes its missing guarantees. It does not follow that $P\ne NP$ without establishing $H$ independently or under the contradiction hypothesis. The alternative achievable-region route in main4.tex can prove existence of improved bounds without a universal syntactic compiler \cite{arc0013,prf20155}.

\section{Reproducible initial model and run discipline}
The companion \texttt{wrapper\_test.py} checks finite-depth exponent calculations, exact Shannon disjunction on small random CNFs against brute force, a negative example for merging equal-answer histories, and the restricted covering count. These are bounded computational checks, not verification of W1--W7 for general SAT. Each Python process has an internal 25-second deadline; larger jobs must be checkpointed and divided into separate sub-30-second batches. The JSON result records seed, counts, timing, scope, and status.

Each automated run must review current repository state and teammate logs, consult relevant primary literature, build or revise a mathematical or executable model, perform a hostile test, and record exact results and limitations. Manuscript revisions must distinguish proved lemmas, conditional statements, computational observations, conjectures, and falsified claims. A stalled run may diagnose and repair a teammate's blocker, but must preserve evidence and explain the change. A timestamped local log and pending-write record must be saved before attempting GitHub writes; another session must not be assumed to share ephemeral local storage.
