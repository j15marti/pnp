\begin{abstract}
This manuscript records a conditional route from a hypothetical polynomial-time SAT algorithm to a forbidden joint time-space bound. It separates an exact recursive composition argument from the unproved existence of a sufficiently cheap source-dependent SAT transformation. No separation of P and NP is claimed. Prime labels, reconvergence, disjunctions, portfolios, and backdoors are candidate implementation mechanisms, not substitutes for the missing transformation theorem.
\end{abstract}
\section{Status and assumptions}
The computational model, input encoding, and charge for random access must be fixed before applying a lower bound. Let $N$ denote encoded input length, not variable count. Assume $P=NP$, and fix one uniform deterministic SAT decider $A$ with $T_A(N)\le N^{K+o(1)}$ and $S_A(N)\le N^{L+o(1)}$, where $K,L$ are finite constants. Time and space bounds below always belong to the same algorithm.

The endpoint used here is the model-matched time-space product obstruction from Williams: polynomially bounded SAT time and space cannot have product $O(N^{\gamma-\varepsilon})$ for a fixed positive $\varepsilon$, where $\gamma=2\cos(\pi/7)$ \cite{williams2010}. Consequently, producing exponents $C,\sigma$ with $C+\sigma<\gamma$ would contradict the assumption. This is not a lower bound against arbitrary polynomial time with arbitrary polynomial space.

\section{The missing inner interface}
\begin{definition}[Charged recursive interface]
A procedure $R_A$ on a formula $F$ of length $M$ generates standard SAT instances $G_1,\ldots,G_q$ and an exact combination procedure, such that
\[
\SAT(F)=\operatorname{Combine}_F(\SAT(G_1),\ldots,\SAT(G_q)).
\]
For fixed $a\ge0$, $0<b<1$, and $c\ge0$, require $q\le M^{a+o(1)}$, $|G_i|\le M^{b+o(1)}$, and total local work $M^{c+o(1)}$. Local work includes discovery, certificates, child generation, all combination operations, and any rereading or regeneration not separately charged elsewhere. The guarantee holds uniformly for every input and remains valid on every child.
\end{definition}
This is a contract, not a construction. The exact combiner may be a disjunction, but must be evaluable in the claimed time and space. A general Boolean circuit on child answers cannot simply be assumed to admit streaming evaluation with negligible storage.

The procedure may depend on the fixed code of $A$. A call to $A$ on a query of length $M^{\delta+o(1)}$ costs $M^{\delta K+o(1)}$ before multiplicity. In particular, a current-scale call retains the unknown exponent $K$. Any such call must be charged honestly; renaming it as a route-selection or certificate operation changes no cost \cite{lead0027}. The bounds $a,b,c$ used for exponent elimination must not hide uncontrolled dependence on $K$.

\section{Composition algebra}
First use idealized exact powers. With depth $d$, the leaf count has exponent
\[
A_d=a\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}b^i=\frac{a(1-b^d)}{1-b}.
\]
The hypothetical decider at the leaves contributes exponent $A_d+Kb^d$. Local work at level $j<d$ has exponent
\[
E_j=\frac{a(1-b^j)}{1-b}+cb^j.
\]
Writing $g=a/(1-b)$ and $C=\max\{c,g\}$, every $E_j\le C$, and $A_d+Kb^d\le C+Kb^d$.
\begin{lemma}[Fixed-depth composition]
For each fixed integer $d\ge1$, the charged interface yields time
\[
T_d(N)\le N^{\max\{\max_{0\le j<d}E_j,A_d+Kb^d\}+o(1)}.
\]
For a sequential explicit-child implementation with local workspace $M^{s+o(1)}$, retained parent-state and resumable sequential generation costs included, and a combiner obeying that workspace contract,
\[
S_d(N)\le N^{\max\{s,b,Lb^d\}+o(1)}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At level $j$, multiply the number of calls by each call's local work. At depth $d$, multiply the leaf count by the leaf running time. A sum of finitely many terms has the largest exponent. For space, process one child at a time. Stored child instances have sizes bounded by $N^{b},N^{b^2},\ldots$; the input at the root is read-only. Local states have exponents at most $s$, and the leaf decider uses exponent $Lb^d$. A fixed-depth sum has the largest exponent. The $o(1)$ errors remain $o(1)$ after a fixed number of compositions. If processing the combiner requires storing all child answers, that storage must already fit $s$; otherwise the stated space bound is unavailable.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional fixed-depth contradiction]
Suppose the charged interface and explicit-child implementation exist under $P=NP$, and
\[
\Delta=\gamma-\left(\max\{c,a/(1-b)\}+\max\{s,b\}\right)>0.
\]
Then $P\ne NP$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Under $P=NP$, choose $A$ and its finite $K,L$. Choose a fixed finite $d$ so that $(K+L)b^d<\Delta/2$. The preceding lemma gives a single uniform algorithm with joint exponent sum at most $C+\max\{s,b\}+(K+L)b^d+o(1)<\gamma$ for sufficiently large inputs. Finitely many smaller inputs may be handled by a fixed finite table without changing asymptotic exponents. Apply the model-matched endpoint. The contradiction discharges $P=NP$, provided the hypothesized interface has actually been proved.
\end{proof}
This finite-depth theorem is enough for a strict endpoint margin. The integer $d$ need not be computable from an arbitrary supplied solver: its mathematical existence allows it to be hardcoded in one finite algorithm. This observation does not supply $R_A$.

\section{Growing depth and regenerated inputs}
A stronger asymptotic statement aims for exactly $N^{C+o(1)}$ time, removing the leaf exponent rather than merely making it smaller than a fixed margin. The rate-controlled growing-depth composition of PRF3-0018 addresses this under explicit hypotheses \cite{prf30018}. One must choose an effective schedule $J(N)\to\infty$, control all errors along the levels, and keep the smallest scales above the thresholds at which each promised estimate is valid. A pointwise $o(1)$ at each fixed depth does not automatically yield a uniform $o(1)$ at growing depth.

Regenerating a child instead of storing it may reduce workspace. It does not make child access free. If a child algorithm makes $Q$ bit requests and each request triggers a cost $R$, their product must be charged; nested virtual input interfaces can multiply these costs again. The claim $S=N^{o(1)}$ requires a proved small-live-state implementation and a charged time bound for all such accesses. The explicit-child alternative often needs fewer access assumptions and can still suffice because a positive space exponent is allowed by the product endpoint.

\section{Architecture alternatives}
\subsection{Shannon exposure followed by an exact quotient}
One natural two-phase proposal first exposes a frontier by restricting
variables and then merges equal residuals before recursion.  The semantic
identity is exact and forward-only:
\[
 \SAT(F)=\bigvee_{\alpha\in\{0,1\}^{H}}\SAT(F\upharpoonright\alpha).
\]
Equality of residual Boolean functions is a safe quotient for this OR, but
computing or representing that quotient is a separate charged operation.
Knowledge-compilation canonicity results concern declared target
representations and do not by themselves give small ordinary-CNF children
or cheap acquisition \cite{darwiche2002}.

The following hostile family closes one tempting universal implementation.
Let
\[
 F_m=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}(x_i\vee y_i),
\]
whose ordinary indexed-CNF length is $M_m=\Theta(m\log m)$.  Restrict
$x_1,\ldots,x_h$ in all $2^h$ ways and quotient the resulting ordinary CNFs
by syntactic equality, or even by equality of their residual Boolean
functions.
\begin{lemma}[SHANNON-QUOTIENT-GATE1]
The quotient has exactly $2^h$ classes.  If the frontier is polynomial in
$M_m$, then every residual retains length $M_m^{1-o(1)}$ in the worst case;
in particular no fixed power bound $M_m^{b+o(1)}$ with $b<1$ follows.  If
$h=m-o(m)$ is chosen to leave a fixed-power residual by raw restriction, the
frontier remains $2^{m-o(m)}$, hence superpolynomial in $M_m$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For an assignment $\alpha$, the $i$th restricted clause is deleted when
$\alpha_i=1$ and becomes the unit clause $y_i$ when $\alpha_i=0$; all
unrestricted clauses remain.  Two different assignments therefore give
different residual CNFs.  They also give different Boolean functions: at a
coordinate where the assignments differ, set the corresponding $y_i$ to
zero, satisfy every other resulting unit, and set every untouched $x_j$ to
one.  This separates the two residual functions.  Thus quotienting does not
reduce the $2^h$ frontier.  Polynomial fanout implies
$h=O(\log M_m)$, while the untouched $m-h$ clauses still occupy
$\Theta(m\log m)=M_m^{1-o(1)}$ bits.  Conversely a fixed-power residual
requires $m-h=o(m)$, leaving $2^{m-o(m)}$ distinct classes.
\end{proof}
Thus this specific Shannon-plus-equality-quotient mechanism is
\textsc{Falsified/Retired} as a universal power shrinker, even on a monotone
2-CNF family.  This is not a representation-independent lower bound: a new
semantic representation or source-dependent transformation can escape, but
must return to ordinary SAT with all acquisition, decoding, and access costs
charged.  Quotienting merely by the SAT answer would use the bit being
computed; treating that operation as free is an oracle-model change.

\subsection{Sparsification as a disjunctive wrapper}
Impagliazzo--Paturi--Zane Corollary~1 gives, for every fixed $k$ and
$\varepsilon>0$, an exact disjunction of at most $2^{\varepsilon n}$
$k$-CNFs, each with $O(n)$ clauses \cite{ipz2001}.  The later quantitative
formulation of Calabro--Impagliazzo--Paturi Lemma~1 is uniform in
$\varepsilon\in(0,1]$, has $n^{O(k)}$ delay, and bounds every child by
\[
 O\!\left((k/\varepsilon)^{3k}n\right)
 \quad\text{clauses}\cite{calabro2010}.
\]
Thus the fixed-$\varepsilon$ quantifier by itself is not the decisive
obstruction.

Let $M$ be the ordinary indexed-CNF bit length.  To certify polynomial
fanout $M^{a+O(1)}$ on inputs with $n\gg\log M$, this transfer must take
$\varepsilon=O(\log M/n)$.  The quantitative child guarantee then becomes
\[
 O\!\left(n\left(\frac{kn}{\log M}\right)^{3k}\right)
 \quad\text{clauses}.
\]
Even on the densest duplicate-free fixed-width scale
$M=n^{k+o(1)}$, this upper bound has nominal $M$-power
$3+1/k-o(1)$, and capping it by returning the original formula certifies
only exponent one.  On the sparse scale $M=\Theta(n\log n)$, the original
formula already has linear clause count, so the sparsity conclusion again
certifies no fixed $b<1$.

The quantitative calculation establishes
\textbf{IPZ-POLYFANOUT-TRANSFER-GATE1}: the cited sparsification theorems are
\textsc{Known Theorem}, while their published bounds do not prove the present
power-shrinking ordinary-CNF interface.  The following red-team guard is
stronger on the declared solution-set-preserving model.

\begin{lemma}[PROJECTED-SOLUTION-COVER-GATE1]
Fix constant width $k$.  Let $X=\{x_i,y_i:1\leq i\leq m\}$ and
\[
 F_m=\bigwedge_{i=1}^{m}(x_i\vee y_i),
\]
and suppose each width-$k$ CNF $G_j(X,Z_j)$ may use its own auxiliary
variables and satisfies the exact projected solution-cover identity
\[
 \operatorname{sol}_X(F_m)=
 \bigcup_{j=1}^{q}\pi_X\operatorname{sol}(G_j).
\]
Every satisfiable $G_j$ contains at least one original-variable occurrence
from each block $\{x_i,y_i\}$.  Hence it has at least
$\lceil m/k\rceil$ clauses and at least $m$ original-variable occurrences.
Consequently its ordinary globally indexed CNF length is
$\Omega(m\log m)=\Omega(|F_m|)$.  The same bound applies to a locally
reindexed child when the source-coordinate projection map is included in the
charged interface.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Union equality implies
$\pi_X\operatorname{sol}(G_j)\subseteq\operatorname{sol}_X(F_m)$.  If a
satisfiable $G_j$ mentioned neither $x_i$ nor $y_i$ for some block $i$, take
a satisfying assignment to $X\cup Z_j$ and reset both original variables to
zero, leaving every auxiliary value fixed.  No clause of $G_j$ changes, but
the projected $X$-assignment falsifies $F_m$, a contradiction.  Thus every
block contributes an original-variable occurrence.  A width-$k$ clause can
contain occurrences from at most $k$ blocks, proving the clause and occurrence
bounds.  Global indexing charges $\Theta(\log m)$ bits per original-variable
reference.  Local indexing merely moves the same source identification into
the explicit projection map required to interpret the child.
\end{proof}

This strictly extends SPARSIFICATION-SOLUTION-COVER-GATE1: auxiliary variables
do not repair an exact bounded-width projected solution cover.  The
existential-projection convention is the standard CNF-encoding semantics
formalized, for example, by Emdin et al.\ in their Section~2.3
\cite{emdin2022}; their parity-specific Theorem~1.1 is not used or transferred.
Hence IPZ-style exact solution-set sparsification, even with existential
auxiliaries, is \textsc{Falsified/Retired} as a universal W2 power shrinker,
independently of fanout and the quantitative sparsity rate.  This is an actual
child-size lower bound in that restricted explicit representation, not an
unrestricted SAT lower bound.  Decision-only OR reductions require only
equality of SAT bits, not union equality of projected solution sets, and
therefore escape the lemma.  Circuits, unbounded clauses, compressed
non-CNF representations, and other semantic transformations also remain open.
The sparsifier is forward-only and avoids current-scale SAT calls; those
positive properties do not repair W2 for this family.

\subsection{Double wrapper}
The implemented prototype in \texttt{experiments/DOUBLE\_WRAPPER\_2026-10-07}
already supplies this architecture. Its iterative inner layer uses unit propagation,
restrictions, variable-disjoint component splitting, and exact normalized formula
keys. It emits constants or ordinary CNF leaves with measured encoding length at
most $\max\{2,\lfloor M^{2/3}\rfloor\}$, without a SAT query. Restriction branches
combine by OR; variable-disjoint components combine by AND. The outer layer
recursively applies the same construction to unresolved leaves, with strictly
decreasing encoded size.

Thus decision exactness, the enforced per-leaf cutoff, and semantic recursive
closure are addressed for this prototype. It is inaccurate to list its combination
rule or per-leaf encoded size as wholly unknown. The missing bounds concern
\emph{total} construction work, total alternatives/internal gates, and simultaneous
time and workspace. Branching until every leaf is small can do exponential work.
The code retains full formulas and caches, and its literal-visit counters omit
sorting, hashing, and other costs. Its local normalization is decision-preserving;
it does not promise preservation of original variable identities or prime lineage.

\subsection{Disjunction and charged backdoors}
A decomposition $\SAT(F)=\bigvee_i\SAT(G_i)$ gives a streaming OR combiner. SAT instances may terminate early, whereas UNSAT instances require all children in the worst case. A backdoor of size $h$ may induce $2^h$ branches; finding the backdoor and solving residual instances must also be charged. An existence theorem for a small backdoor without cheap acquisition does not meet the inner contract. Nor does a small backdoor theorem for a restricted class establish coverage of arbitrary SAT.

\subsection{Backdoor DNFs: exact cover, but not automatic power shrinkage}
For a self-reducible tractable CNF class $\mathcal C$, a
$\mathcal C$-backdoor DNF is a finite set $\mathcal G$ of partial
assignments such that the corresponding DNF is a tautology and
$F\upharpoonright\tau\in\mathcal C$ for every $\tau\in\mathcal G$.
Consequently
\[
 \SAT(F)=\bigvee_{\tau\in\mathcal G}
 \SAT(F\upharpoonright\tau).
\]
This is an exact forward OR plan.  Ordyniak, Schidler, and Szeider prove that
the variables used by a backdoor DNF form a strong backdoor (Observation~1),
and that detecting a $k$-term backdoor DNF is fixed-parameter tractable for
$\mathcal C\in\{\mathrm{2CNF},\mathrm{Horn},\mathrm{AntiHorn},
\mathrm{2CNF}\cup\mathrm{Horn},
\mathrm{2CNF}\cup\mathrm{AntiHorn}\}$ (Theorem~3)
\cite{ordyniak2021bdnf}.  Thus the construction compresses a branching
cover; it does not imply that an unrelated general CNF has such a cover.
The FPT statement also does not by itself provide the fixed exponents and
live-space bound required by the present interface when $k$ grows.

\begin{lemma}[BDNF-WRAPPER-GATE1]\label{lem:bdnf-gate}
There is an explicit Horn-backdoor-DNF family with $M^{o(1)}$ terms for
which emitting the residual CNFs certifies no child exponent $b<1$.
This is \textsc{Proved/Exact} for ordinary explicit indexed-CNF residuals.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the Horn family in the proof sketch of Theorem~2 of
\cite{ordyniak2021bdnf}.  For $s\ge1$, put $r=2^s-s$ and take variables
$p,a_1,\ldots,a_s,q_1,\ldots,q_r$ with clauses
\[
 (a_i\vee p)\quad(1\le i\le s),
 \qquad
 (a_1\vee\cdots\vee a_s\vee q_j\vee\neg p)
 \quad(1\le j\le r).
\]
The $s+2$ partial assignments
\[
 p=0;\qquad p=1,\ a_1=\cdots=a_s=0;\qquad
 p=1,\ a_i=1\quad(1\le i\le s)
\]
form a tautological DNF, and every residual is Horn.  Hence their fanout is
$s+2=M_s^{o(1)}$.  In an ordinary globally indexed clause-list encoding,
the parent length is
\[
 M_s=\Theta(2^s s^2):
\]
there are $\Theta(2^s)$ long clauses with $\Theta(s)$ occurrences, and each
index uses $\Theta(s)$ bits.  Under the second partial assignment above,
the residual is precisely the $r$ unit clauses $(q_j)$.  Its encoded length
is
\[
 L_s=\Theta(2^s s)=M_s^{1-o(1)},
\]
because $\log L_s/\log M_s\to1$.  Therefore this direct explicit-child
transfer violates every fixed $L_s\le M_s^{b+o(1)}$ with $b<1$.
\end{proof}

Local reindexing does not change the conclusion: the displayed residual has
$r$ distinct unit clauses, so an ordinary explicit local-index encoding
still requires $\Theta(r\log r)=\Theta(2^s s)$ bits.  An explicit map back
to source variables only adds representation cost.  This is a syntactic
restriction-emission statement, not a decision lower bound.

\begin{lemma}[BDNF-VALUE-ESCAPE1]\label{lem:bdnf-value-escape}
The same family admits a uniform exact partial procedure using $O(M)$ time,
$O(\log M)$ work space, no recursive children, and no calls to the
hypothetical SAT decider.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the fixed ordinary variable name $p=1$.  In one read-only-input scan,
test whether setting $p=0$ satisfies a clause or leaves a nonempty clause
containing only positive literals.  If every clause passes, return the
constant value TRUE; otherwise return \textsc{fail}.  The accepted case is
sound because setting every remaining variable to one satisfies the residual.
The test stores only the variable name, input addresses, and clause flags.
Every formula in the displayed family passes: the long clauses contain
$\neg p$, and each binary clause becomes the positive unit $(a_i)$.
\end{proof}

Thus the gate does not refute backdoor-DNF algorithms and is not an obstacle
on this easy source family to a decision-only VALUE return.  More generally,
a procedure may solve base-class residuals directly, stream them implicitly,
or combine this member with other exact partial mechanisms.  Such a
realization must charge DNF acquisition, tautology/coverage certification,
residual access, base-class solving, and live state.  The paper's family
still shows why the mechanism can enlarge the finite portfolio: its DNF has
$s+2$ terms whereas complete enumeration of the displayed size-$s$ strong
backdoor has $2^s$ branches.  No worst-case general-SAT coverage or favorable
joint exponent for arbitrary supplied backdoor DNFs follows.

\subsection{Parallel portfolios}
A fixed finite portfolio of exact solvers can be time-shared to exploit its fastest member with a constant time overhead, assuming ordinary suspendable simulations. The simulator must count all retained states. A slow competitor may consume too much memory before the winner finishes. Memory caps or staged restarts therefore require their own analysis. For a growing portfolio, scheduling and description costs are no longer automatically constant. The required coverage statement is that every input has a favorable member, with uniform correctness and a joint resource guarantee. Testing different winners on different instances is not a coverage proof.

\begin{theorem}[CERTIFIED-PARTIAL-PORTFOLIO1]\label{thm:partial-portfolio}
Fix finitely many uniform forward inner procedures $W_1,\ldots,W_k$.  On an
ordinary SAT instance $F$ of encoded length $M$, each $W_j$ halts with either
\textsc{fail} or a finite plan $(C_j;G_{j,1},\ldots,G_{j,q_j})$.  Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
\item whenever $W_j$ succeeds, its plan is exact:
\[
 \SAT(F)=C_j\bigl(\SAT(G_{j,1}),\ldots,\SAT(G_{j,q_j})\bigr);
\]
\item every $F$ is covered, meaning at least one $W_j$ succeeds;
\item success and failure, including all discarded attempts, are decided
without a current-scale SAT oracle and within
$M^{c_j+o(1)}$ time and $M^{s_j+o(1)}$ live space;
\item every successful plan has $q_j\le M^{a_j+o(1)}$ and children of
length at most $M^{b_j+o(1)}$, where $b_j<1$, and its actual explicit or
implicit representation, generation, access, and combiner costs are included
in those bounds.
\end{enumerate}
Then sequentially running $W_1,\ldots,W_k$ until the first success is one
uniform exact inner procedure with
\[
 a=\max_j a_j,\qquad b=\max_j b_j<1,\qquad
 c=\max_j c_j,\qquad s=\max_j s_j.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Coverage guarantees termination with a successful plan, and the first
hypothesis gives exactness independently of which member wins.  Because $k$
is fixed, the sum of the attempted running times has exponent
$\max_j c_j$.  Attempts are sequential and failed state is discarded, so
peak live space has exponent $\max_j s_j$.  The retained plan is the winning
member's plan, giving the stated fanout and child-size maxima.  The maximum of
finitely many $o(1)$ terms is still $o(1)$.  All output writing or generator
state is already charged by hypothesis.
\end{proof}

This is a partial-generator specialization of the recovered finite-witness
portfolio-absorption lemma, not a new source of contraction.  It removes the
need for a separate classifier among a fixed collection of syntactically safe
inner mechanisms.  The load-bearing premise is now exact favorable
\emph{coverage} with common charged bounds.  Merely having one correct member
is insufficient: every reported success must be exact, including on UNSAT
instances.  A growing portfolio, an unsafe first finisher, retained failed
states, or an input-dependent certificate requires a separate ledger.

The theorem includes the case $q_j=0$: a certified constant TRUE or FALSE
combiner is a zero-child plan.  Such a VALUE member must establish its
constant answer before reporting success; otherwise an unsafe first finisher
invalidates the portfolio.  Vacuous child bounds may be recorded with
$a_j=b_j=0$.

Recursive and depth-based backdoors provide exact partial mechanisms in their
stated structural domains \cite{maehlmann2021,dreier2022}.  They do not prove
bounded depth or cheap acquisition on every CNF.  SATzilla demonstrates the
practical value of per-instance portfolios \cite{xu2008}, but its empirical
selection objective is not a worst-case coverage theorem and does not transfer
to the present resource contract.

\subsection{Prime lineage, hashes, and merging}
In Boolean semantics, TRUE and NOT FALSE agree, as do FALSE and NOT TRUE. Assigning different primes preserves syntactic provenance, not four distinct Boolean truth values. Any prime product is charged by bit length and arithmetic cost. An ordinary hash needs collision handling for exact correctness. A common terminal node or equal SAT bits does not justify identifying histories under future operations. A merge needs a semantic congruence appropriate to the remaining computation. Compact macro-route indices can save lineage space after contraction; they do not establish contraction. A finite DAG contains no directed cycles: recursion must be described by a well-founded call graph or an unfolded DAG, not a literal cyclic DAG.

\section{Where aggregate compression belongs}
For an explicitly defined quantity $D_0=2^k$, exact reduction factors $G_i$ give
\[
D_r=\frac{2^k}{\prod_iG_i}=2^{k-x},\qquad x=\sum_i\log_2G_i.
\]
Thus $D_r\le N^d$ implies $x\ge k-d\log_2N$ \cite{prf20145}. This is exact bookkeeping. To use it in this manuscript, one must connect $D_r$ to actual encoded children or charged computation. Raw assignments do not by themselves force enumeration. Branching into $2^x$ subproblems each of nominal size $2^{k-x}$ preserves a total of $2^k$ possibilities rather than providing a net saving.

A restricted hostile control is useful: a formula-independent family of $q$ partial assignments, each leaving at most $\ell$ variables unassigned, must satisfy $q2^\ell\ge2^n$ if it must cover every possible unique satisfying assignment. This rules out that specific oblivious construction. It does not exclude source-dependent semantic reductions or arbitrary algorithms \cite{lead0027}.

\section{Interface obligations and candidate-specific status}
\begin{description}
\item[] \textbf{W1: semantic reduction.} Prove exactness of the candidate inner operation on the entire claimed domain.
\item[] \textbf{W2: encoded shrinkage.} Prove a fixed exponent $b<1$ for actual standard SAT children, including every source reference needed to interpret them.
\item[] \textbf{W3: full construction charge.} Bound discovery, certification, generation, and combination before any hidden expensive semantic query.
\item[] \textbf{W4: total branching.} Bound all necessary alternatives, including adversarial UNSAT inputs.
\item[] \textbf{W5: recursive closure.} Prove that every produced child lies in a domain with the same contract.
\item[] \textbf{W6: joint resources.} Give one implementation with charged input access, live state, answer storage, and any portfolio scheduling.
\item[] \textbf{W7: endpoint compatibility.} Match the machine model and encoding and preserve a strict numerical margin.
\end{description}
These are interface requirements, not seven uniformly unresolved claims.
For the explicit double-wrapper prototype, W1, the per-leaf part of W2,
and semantic W5 are addressed. W3, W4, W6 and the favorable quantitative
closure needed by W5 are not established at the target exponents.
Other candidates require their own status ledger.

The proof spine is
\[
\begin{aligned}
P=NP&\Rightarrow \text{one polynomial SAT decider }A\\
&\xRightarrow{\text{OPEN: jointly favorable interface bounds}}\text{favorable recursive SAT interface}\\
&\xRightarrow{\text{conditional composition}}\text{one algorithm with improved joint bounds}\\
&\xRightarrow{\text{W7 and strict margin}}\text{contradiction to the endpoint}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Until the open interface is proved, the valid logical statement is $(P=NP)\land H\Rightarrow\bot$, where $H$ denotes its missing guarantees. It does not follow that $P\ne NP$ without establishing $H$ independently or under the contradiction hypothesis. The alternative achievable-region route in main4.tex can prove existence of improved bounds without a universal syntactic compiler \cite{arc0013,prf20155}.

\section{Forward-tree and structured-DAG bridge}
The nonrecursive inner WRAPPER can be stated without hiding the unknown
polynomial exponent.  Let $Q(N)$ be the number of nodes actually evaluated
in its unfolded AND/OR tree, $G(N)$ its total charged generation and
combination time, $D(N)$ its depth, $R(N)$ its generator workspace, and
$M(N)$ the largest explicit CNF leaf.  All unsuccessful routes needed in the
worst case are included.

\begin{theorem}[FORWARD-TREE-BRIDGE1]
Assume $P=NP$.  If one exact source-queryable realization satisfies
\[
 Q=N^{q+o(1)},\quad G=N^{g+o(1)},\quad
 D,R,M=N^{o(1)},
\]
with $N^{o(1)}$ bits per depth-first frame, then SAT has one deterministic
algorithm with
\[
 T=N^{\max\{q,g\}+o(1)},\qquad S=N^{o(1)}.
\]
Consequently $\max\{q,g\}<2\cos(\pi/7)$ contradicts the endpoint.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix the polynomial SAT decider supplied by $P=NP$, with finite time and space
exponents $K,L$.  Every explicit leaf has length $N^{o(1)}$, so its time and
space costs are $N^{o(1)}$.  Depth-first evaluation therefore costs
$Q(N)N^{o(1)}+G(N)$ time.  Only one leaf, one generator state, and one
root-to-current-node stack are live.  The stated depth and frame bounds make
their total $N^{o(1)}$ bits.
\end{proof}
If generation is bounded only per node, its exponent must be added to $q$.
A succinct generator descriptor is not an ordinary short CNF, and every
generated-bit access must be charged.  A shared recursive DAG may replace the
tree only with a model-matched evaluator bound or with its unfolded size
charged.  Bounded treewidth and bounded query-DAG separator number are
promising structural certificates, but the published generic simulations do
not by themselves establish a time exponent below the endpoint.  A merge
that literally returns one of two satisfiable candidates is a p-selector;
its qualitative existence under $P=NP$ likewise supplies no controlled
exponent or low-space implementation.  These literature connections and
their exact hypotheses are documented in \texttt{main6.tex}.

The earlier aggregate identity
\[
 2^k/2^x=2^{k-x}
\]
fits here only through a proved source map showing that $x$ bits of reduction
make $M=N^{o(1)}$, or make $Q$ satisfy the displayed exponent.  An injective
prime or hash label for $2^d$ histories itself needs at least $d$ bits;
shorter deterministic fingerprints require exact collision resolution.

The fixed-power recursive route has a clean numerical construction target.
The values
\[
 b=s=\frac23,\qquad a=\frac13,\qquad c=1
\]
give $C+\sigma=5/3<2\cos(\pi/7)$.  With $b=s=2/3$, the separate sufficient
thresholds are $a<0.378424\ldots$ and $c<1.135271\ldots$.  No recovered
general-input WRAPPER proves these bounds.
