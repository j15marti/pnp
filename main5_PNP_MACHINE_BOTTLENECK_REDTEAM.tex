\section{Selected end-to-end target}
The audited pipeline is: an encoded UNSAT 3-CNF $F$ with $|\langle F\rangle|\le N$; its multisign DAG hypergraph; a fixed sound rule set $R$; a minimum-cost derivation $\pi:F\vdash_R\bot$ chosen freely; the worst-case quantity
\[
\Wbar_R(N)=\max_{F\in\mathrm{UNSAT},\,|\langle F\rangle|\le N}\ \min_{\pi:F\vdash_R\bot}\mathrm{cost}_R(\pi);
\]
the bridge $\mathrm{Bridge}(R)$: $\mathrm{SAT}\in\mathrm{P}\Rightarrow\Wbar_R(N)\le\mathrm{poly}(N)$; and the consequence P $\ne$ NP from a superpolynomial $\Wbar_R$. $R$ ranges over Cook--Reckhow systems (polynomial-time verifier, sound and complete for UNSAT) with cost polynomially related to proof length. The first unclosed arrow is the bridge.

\section{Results on the bridge}
\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT, elementary; folklore attribution not verified; peer review OPEN]
Assume P $=$ NP. For every Cook--Reckhow system $R$ the following are equivalent: $R$ is p-bounded; $R$ is p-optimal; $R$ p-simulates $Q_A$ for some polynomial-time UNSAT decider $A$, where $Q_A$ accepts the empty proof of $F$ iff $A$ rejects $F$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
p-optimality implies simulation of $Q_A$ by definition, and translating length-$|F|$ $Q_A$-proofs gives polynomial $R$-proofs. If every UNSAT $F$ has an $R$-proof of length at most $b(|F|)$, the prefix-extension language $\{(F,w):\exists\pi\sqsupseteq w,\ |\pi|\le b(|F|),\ V_R(F,\pi)=1\}$ lies in NP $=$ P, so prefix search produces an $R$-proof in polynomial time, ignoring any given proof from another system; this p-simulates every system.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[PROVED/EXACT]
$\mathrm{Bridge}(R)\iff(\mathrm{P}=\mathrm{NP}\Rightarrow R\text{ is p-optimal})$. Hence the bridge is exactly the claim that in a P $=$ NP world $R$ efficiently simulates every sound refutation procedure, including Gaussian elimination, Horn and 2-SAT propagation, algebraic and circuit-compression methods and adaptive switching. Granting the optimal derivation sequence for free is sound and also neutral: under P $=$ NP a p-bounded system's optimal derivations are found in polynomial time, so the difficulty lies entirely in the simulation, not the search.
\end{corollary}
\begin{theorem}[PROVED/EXACT in one direction; converse OPEN]
Let $T\supseteq\mathrm{PV}$ be $\Pi_1$-sound with polynomial-time proof checking. Let $R_T$ accept $(i,p,1^t)$ as a proof of $F$ when $p$ is a $T$-proof of $\sigma_i$: ``$M_i(G)=0$ implies $G$ unsatisfiable for all $G$'', and $M_i$ outputs $0$ on $F$ within $t$ steps. Then $R_T$ is a Cook--Reckhow system, and if some polynomial-time UNSAT decider has a $T$-provable $\sigma_i$ then $\Wbar_{R_T}$ is polynomial. Consequently ``P $=$ NP implies a $T$-provably sound polynomial-time decider'' implies $\mathrm{Bridge}(R_T)$. The converse would need uniform reflection for $T$ and is not claimed.
\end{theorem}
For $T=\mathrm{PV}$ this is Cook's 1975 theorem through Extended Frege. For strong $T$ the bridge becomes a provability statement, not a complexity lower bound.
