\begin{abstract}
We formalize the projection-cost direction suggested by the $2^k$ compression and
WRAPPER investigations. Polynomial-time unrestricted Boolean existential
projection is equivalent to $P=NP$. An all-variable projection compiler with
simultaneous resources in a known forbidden SAT time--space region is impossible.
A charged partial-projection trilemma and a finite-step contraction principle
are established conditionally on explicit source-level implementations.
Neither compact representations nor Shannon counting yields a universal
SAT decision-time lower bound. The required solver-to-low-resource projection
transfer remains OPEN; no proof of $P\ne NP$ is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definitions and model}
Let $C(x,y)$ be a bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit encoded in $N$ bits,
where $x$ and $y$ are disjoint lists of input variables. Define
$Q_y C(x)=\bigvee_{b\in\{0,1\}^{|y|}}C(x,b)$.
A \emph{uniform unrestricted projection compiler} is a deterministic
algorithm that, on $(C,x,y)$, outputs a bounded-fan-in circuit $G(x)$
satisfying $G(a)=Q_yC(a)$ for every $a$. Output length, compiler time,
working space, and subsequent evaluation time are separate resources.
The compiler may not silently use an oracle, advice, or an uncharged
equivalence test. The circuit input encoding, random-access word size,
source reads, output bits, virtual accessors, query multiplicity, and
simultaneously live memory must be included in every endpoint claim.

\begin{definition}[Charged projection tuple]
For compiler $\Pi$, input $C$, and output $G$, write
$\mathcal R_\Pi(C)=(D_\Pi,W_\Pi,L_\Pi,E_\Pi)$, where $D_\Pi$ is
construction time, $W_\Pi$ is peak writable workspace, $L_\Pi=|G|$
is output encoding length, and $E_\Pi$ is the cost of the specified
consumer of $G$. These quantities are not interchangeable.
\end{definition}

\section{Unrestricted projection equivalence}
\begin{theorem}[Projection completeness; PROVED/EXACT]
There exists a uniform polynomial-time unrestricted projection compiler
for all encoded Boolean circuits and all partitions $(x,y)$ if and only
if $P=NP$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For the forward implication, take $x$ empty. The compiler produces a
zero-input circuit whose value equals $\exists y\,C(y)$; constructing
and evaluating it decides Circuit-SAT in polynomial time. Circuit-SAT
is NP-complete.

Conversely assume $P=NP$. Fix a deterministic polynomial-time
Circuit-SAT decider $A$. On input $(C,x,y)$, construct a bounded-fan-in
circuit $G$ that, on assignment $a$ to $x$, forms the encoding of
$C(a,y)$ and simulates $A$ on that encoding. Uniform polynomial-time
Turing computations have uniform polynomial-size Boolean circuit
simulations constructible in polynomial time. Hardwire the input
description $C$ and the partition into that simulation. Then
$G(a)=1$ exactly when $\exists b\,C(a,b)=1$. Both construction
time and $|G|$ are polynomial in $N$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The converse does not promise a minimum-size output, an efficient
structured-d-DNNF output, near-linear construction time, or low
working space. It also does not prove an exponent improvement over $A$.
\end{remark}

\section{Unconditional projection resource obstruction}
Let $\mathcal F$ denote a \emph{source-verified}, model-matched forbidden
region of simultaneous deterministic SAT time and space exponents.
For example, the published Williams product endpoint rules out
$t+s<\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$ in its stated random-access model
\cite{williams}. The larger $2\cos(\pi/7)$ threshold must NOT be
used as a universal polynomial-space product frontier; the published
full version retracts that unrestricted claim. The classical
small-space threshold has separate hypotheses.

\begin{theorem}[Complete-projection impossibility; PROVED/EXACT
conditional on the cited model-matched endpoint]
There is no uniform all-variable projection compiler whose complete
construction and zero-input output evaluation together yield a SAT
algorithm with simultaneous time-space exponents $(t,s)\in\mathcal F$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Given a circuit $C$, project all its input variables and evaluate the
resulting constant. By exact projection semantics this decides
Circuit-SAT. After charging the circuit-to-SAT encoding adapter and
machine simulation in the endpoint model, a compiler with the
claimed resources would violate the forbidden-region theorem.
No additional SAT hypothesis is needed.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The endpoint must be checked for the \emph{same language encoding and
machine model}; an unspecified polynomial-time reduction can destroy
exponent-sensitive bounds. This theorem prohibits a low-resource
compiler, not all polynomial-time compilers.
\end{remark}

\section{Partial projection and the cost trilemma}
\begin{proposition}[Charged composition; PROVED/EXACT]
Suppose $\Pi$ constructs $G(x)=Q_yC(x)$ in time $D$ and working space
$W$, with $|G|=L$. Suppose a total algorithm decides satisfiability
of $G$ in time $T_G(L)$ and space $S_G(L)$. If the complete $G$ is
materialized in writable memory, the sequential composition has
\[
T_{\rm all}\le D+T_G(L)+O(N+L),\qquad
S_{\rm all}\le W+O(L)+S_G(L)
\]
up to implementation-dependent logarithmic bookkeeping.
If $G$ is written to an external output tape and later reread,
the appropriate access and storage charges must instead be included.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Construct $G$, retain its representation, then run the residual
solver. The displayed bounds count the sum of sequential time and
a conservative bound on simultaneously retained state.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Projection-cost trilemma; PROVED/EXACT as a logical
disjunction]
Fix a forbidden resource region $\mathcal F$ and a particular
all-input, exact, model-matched partial-projection-plus-residual-solver
scheme. At least one of its fully charged construction, retained
workspace/output access, or residual-solving costs prevents the
\emph{combined} algorithm from lying in $\mathcal F$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If no charged component prevented membership, the preceding
composition would produce an all-input SAT decider in $\mathcal F$,
contradicting the endpoint. The disjunction does not give an
exponential lower bound for any individual component.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
An algorithm need not perform a separate per-input equivalence
verification when correctness of the compiler is proved once.
Charging an unexecuted verifier is invalid.
\end{remark}

\section{Finite-step exponent transfer}
Suppose a uniform, recursively applicable reduction makes at most
$N^{a+o(1)}$ ordinary SAT queries of encoded length at most
$N^{\gamma+o(1)}$, with $0<\gamma<1$, and has construction/combination
time $N^{g+o(1)}$. Under sequential streaming and fully charged
memory, suppose its workspace is $N^{b+o(1)}$ and one live child
requires the scaled seed space. For a seed algorithm with exponents
$(c,s)$, the claimed transfer is
\[
c'\le\max\{g,a+\gamma c\},\qquad
s'\le\max\{b,\gamma s\}.
\]
These are accounting consequences of a real reduction, NOT existence
statements.

\begin{lemma}[Finite contraction; PROVED/EXACT]
Let $0<\gamma<1$, $a,g,b\ge0$ and
$c_{j+1}\le\max\{g,a+\gamma c_j\}$,
$s_{j+1}\le\max\{b,\gamma s_j\}$. Set
$c_*=\max\{g,a/(1-\gamma)\}$. Then
\[
c_j\le c_*+\gamma^j(c_0-c_*)_+,\qquad
s_j\le\max\{b,\gamma^js_0\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Both bounds follow by induction using $g\le c_*$ and
$a+\gamma c_*\le c_*$. For space use
$\max\{b,\gamma\max\{b,z\}\}\le\max\{b,\gamma z\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional forbidden-region transfer; CONDITIONAL]
Suppose a source-level uniform transformation works for every
polynomial-time SAT decider, is closed under any fixed finite number
of iterations, obeys the above simultaneous resource bounds with
constants independent of the seed algorithm, and the fixed point
$(c_*,b)$ lies strictly inside a source-verified open forbidden region
$\mathcal F$. Then $P\ne NP$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume $P=NP$ and fix one polynomial-time SAT machine with finite
exponents $(c_0,s_0)$. Choose a sufficiently large \emph{fixed}
iteration count so the finite-step bounds enter $\mathcal F$.
The resulting single uniform SAT machine contradicts the endpoint.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The transformation premise is OPEN and carries the central
separation-strength obligation. A limit $s_j\to0$ for fixed iteration
indices does not establish $N^{o(1)}$ space. A positive-space
forbidden region is preferable when a finite iteration is required.
\end{remark}

\section{Assumption and hostile-control ledger}
\begin{itemize}
\item[] \textbf{PROVED/EXACT:} General polynomial-time existential
projection is equivalent to $P=NP$; conditional recurrence algebra;
model-matched projection impossibility from a known endpoint.
\item[] \textbf{KNOWN THEOREM:} Restricted knowledge-compilation
languages can incur existential-quantification or intermediate
representation blowups \cite{capelli,vinall,decolnet}. These do not
give unrestricted SAT time lower bounds.
\item[] \textbf{FALSIFIED GENERALIZATION:} Eliminating $r$ Boolean
variables always costs $2^{\Omega(r)}$. For
$C(x,y)=x\oplus y$, $\exists y\,C=1$; affine systems permit
polynomial-time Gaussian elimination.
\item[] \textbf{FALSIFIED GENERALIZATION:} $2^k$ assignments force
$2^k$ computational steps. Parity, Horn, 2-SAT, affine, and
bounded-treewidth examples defeat this inference.
\item[] \textbf{OPEN:} A solver-independent, uniform, all-input
projection/successor transformation with simultaneous time-space
improvement and complete discovery, query, replay, encoding, and
workspace charges.
\item[] \textbf{OPEN:} Any transfer from restricted representation
lower bounds, Shannon's almost-all-functions circuit counting, or
semantic continuation width to arbitrary SAT decider runtime.
\end{itemize}

\section{Precise next obligation}
Construct or refute a theorem with quantifiers
\[
(P=NP)\Longrightarrow
\exists A\,\exists\Pi\,\forall C:
\quad \Pi_A(C)\equiv Q_yC
\quad\land\quad
\mathcal R_{\Pi_A}(C)\text{ yields a model-matched forbidden SAT bound}.
\]
The source-level compiler must be explicit and its construction,
output, access, recursive calls, and live state charged on one
realization. A statement asserting the forbidden bound without an
independent mechanism merely renames the separation problem.
